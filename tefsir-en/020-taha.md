# 20. Sūrat Ṭāhā

**135 verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the letters **طه** in its first verse.

The body of the sūra is **the story of Moses (Mūsā)**: from verse 9 to verse 98, ninety verses. This is the longest and most uninterrupted telling of the story of Moses anywhere in the Qurʾān within a single sūra. **But the sūra is not a story sūra:** the story is set inside a sentence built in the first eight verses, and the last twenty verses return to that sentence.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What ends the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-8 | Opening: not *li-tashqā* but *tadhkira* | *Lahu'l-asmāʾu'l-ḥusnā* (8) |
| **II** | 9-48 | **The calling of Moses** — the fire, the staff, the prayer, the commission | *Anna'l-ʿadhāba ʿalā man kadhdhaba wa tawallā* (48) |
| **III** | 49-79 | The encounter with Pharaoh, the sorcerers, the crossing of the sea | *Wa aḍalla Firʿawnu qawmahu wa mā hadā* (79) |
| **IV** | 80-98 | The Children of Israel, the Sāmirī and the calf | *Wasiʿa kulla shayʾin ʿilmā* (98) |
| **V** | 99-114 | Why the story was told, the resurrection, not hastening | *Wa qul rabbi zidnī ʿilmā* (114) |
| **VI** | 115-127 | **Adam and Iblīs** — forgetting, *tashqā*, *dankan* | *Wa la-ʿadhābu'l-ākhirati ashaddu wa abqā* (127) |
| **VII** | 128-135 | Patience, prayer, the straying of the gaze, waiting | *Fa-sa-taʿlamūna man aṣḥābu'ṣ-ṣirāṭi's-sawiyy* (135) |

### The spine of the sūra: ش-ق-و

**The sūra opens with one root and closes with the same root, and this is a datum that can be counted from the text. The root occurs three times in the sūra and in no other place in it:**

| Verse | Expression | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | *Mā anzalnā ʿalayka'l-Qurʾāna **li-tashqā*** | **To the Prophet** — not that you should fall into distress |
| **117** | *Fa-lā yukhrijannakumā mina'l-jannati **fa-tashqā*** | **To Adam** — if you are driven out you will fall into distress |
| **123** | *Fa-mani'ttabaʿa hudāya fa-lā yaḍillu **wa lā yashqā*** | **To everyone** — whoever follows does not fall into distress |

**What is negated in the second verse is named as a danger in the hundred and seventeenth, and made conditional in the hundred and twenty-third.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the three verses use the same root in the same pattern (a verb, with *alif maqṣūra*):** the sūra opens with "the Qurʾān was not sent down on you to be a burden to you"; **it says where the real burden lies in the story of Adam** — in turning away. And at the close the two join: **whoever follows the guidance does not become *shaqī*.**

### The second spine: ذ-ك-ر

**The root occurs in eight places in the sūra, with eight distinct functions:**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | *Illā **tadhkiratan** li-man yakhshā* | **The work of the Book** |
| 14 | *Wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta **li-dhikrī*** | **The ground of the prayer** |
| 34 | *Wa **nadhkuraka** kathīrā* | **What Moses asks for** |
| 42 | *Wa lā taniyā **fī dhikrī*** | **The measure given with the commission** |
| 44 | *Laʿallahu **yatadhakkaru*** | **What is hoped for from Pharaoh** |
| 99 | *Wa qad ātaynāka min ladunnā **dhikrā*** | **The name of the Qurʾān** |
| 113 | *Aw yuḥdithu lahum **dhikrā*** | **The purpose of the sending down** |
| 124 | *Wa man aʿraḍa **ʿan dhikrī*** | **What is turned away from** |

**So the name of the Book, the ground of the prayer, the measure given to the two messengers, what is hoped for from Pharaoh and the subject of turning away — all of it is one root.** This is a distribution that can be verified from the text.

### The third spine: أبقى

***Abqā* (more lasting) occurs four times in the sūra, and the first of them is in the mouth of Pharaoh:**

| Verse | Who says it | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **71** | **Pharaoh** | *Wa la-taʿlamunna ayyunā ashaddu ʿadhāban **wa abqā*** |
| **73** | **The sorcerers** | *Wa'llāhu khayrun **wa abqā*** |
| **127** | **The text** | *Wa la-ʿadhābu'l-ākhirati ashaddu **wa abqā*** |
| **131** | **The text** | *Wa rizqu rabbika khayrun **wa abqā*** |

**Pharaoh asks "which of us is more severe in punishment and more lasting"; the sorcerers answer with the same word; and the sūra uses the same word twice more, once of punishment and once of provision.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra does not dispute the measure the other side has chosen — **it takes that measure out of his hand and puts it in its place.** This technique was recorded at `085-buruc.md` and `026-suara.md` as well.

## 20/1 — طه

*Ṭāhā*

"Ṭā-hā."

### A note on the disconnected letters

**The discussion of the disconnected letters (*al-ḥurūf al-muqaṭṭaʿa*) has been set out many times in the series** (`002-bakara.md`, `050-kaf.md`, `038-sad.md`, `036-yasin.md`, `026-suara.md`, `019-meryem.md`) **and is not repeated here.** In summary: **no certain knowledge about the meaning of these letters has been transmitted; the most honest position is to say that it is not known.**

**What belongs here is a disagreement peculiar to *ṭāhā*, and it must not be concealed.** These two letters, unlike the other disconnected letters, **can also be read as a word**, and for that reason they have been dwelt on separately.

| Reading | What it says | Its status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Disconnected letters** — like the other sūra openings; the meaning is unknown | Its position at the head of a sūra and its standing in the same place as the other disconnected letters support this reading |
| 2 | **A form of address** — it is reported that in some Arab dialects it is a call meaning "O man / O fellow" | **It is a transmitted explanation; it is not certain knowledge.** The claim about the dialect is disputed |
| 3 | **One of the names of the Prophet** | **It is transmitted; there is no evidence for it in the text.** That it is the name of the sūra does not show that the word is a name |
| 4 | **An imperative** — from the verb *ṭaʾ*, "tread, set your foot down" | This has been found forced on linguistic grounds |

**None of these is preferred in this commentary, and the second and third readings are not given as certain knowledge.** **The record is this: there is no certain knowledge.**

**And an observation about the word order may be entered:** on whichever reading these letters are taken, **the second verse turns directly to the one addressed:** *mā anzalnā **ʿalayka***. **That is, the first sentence of the sūra is built not with a voice whose identity is disputed, but with a sentence whose addressee is plain.**

## 20/2-3 — مَآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْكَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ لِتَشْقَىٰٓ · إِلَّا تَذْكِرَةً لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ

*Mā anzalnā ʿalayka'l-Qurʾāna li-tashqā · Illā tadhkiratan li-man yakhshā*

"**We did not send the Qurʾān down on you that you should fall into distress. · Only as a reminder to whoever fears.**"

### The root ش-ق-و

**The root was treated in the series at `092-leyl.md`, `091-sems.md` and `087-ala.md` in connection with the word *ashqā*** — there it was discussed that the word is an elative (*ism al-tafḍīl*) on the *afʿal* pattern and stands opposed to *atqā*. **I rely on that treatment; I do not repeat the discussion of the pattern here.**

**What belongs here is the verbal form of the root, and its concrete sense.**

The core the lexicographers give for the root **ش-ق-و / ش-ق-ي** is: **to undergo hardship, to grow weary, to struggle with difficulty.** Two branches issue from this:

| Branch | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **This-worldly** | **To be wearied, to toil, to suffer hardship** | *Shaqiya fī ʿamalihi* — he wore himself out at his work |
| **Otherworldly** | **To be wretched, to meet a bad end** | *Ashqā* — the most wretched |

**And its opposite is not *saʿāda* (happiness, the ease of one's affairs) but simply the absence of *shaqāwa*.** The Qurʾān names the two together: *fa-minhum shaqiyyun wa saʿīd* (Hūd 11/105).

**Which of the two branches is meant in this verse has been disputed, and the disagreement must not be concealed:**

| Reading | What *li-tashqā* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Bodily weariness** — the Qurʾān was not sent down to load on you a burden you cannot carry |
| 2 | **Grief** — it was not sent down that you should consume yourself because they do not believe |
| 3 | **General** — hardship of every kind |

**I do not impose a preference.** **But I record one indication, not as a preference:** the answer given in the third verse is *tadhkira* — that is, **the work of the Book.** The sentence is speaking about **why the Book came down**; and so what is negated stands nearer to **what the Book lays on the one addressed**.

### The finest point in the sentence: where the negation falls

**A point of word order must be recorded, and the whole weight of this verse lies here.**

**The sentence is built thus:** *mā anzalnā* (we did not send down) + *ʿalayka'l-Qurʾāna* (on you the Qurʾān) + *li-tashqā* (that you should fall into distress).

**The particle of negation (*mā*) enters upon the verb, but the real target of the negation is the purpose clause with *lām*.** In Arabic this construction negates **not the verb but the ground of the verb**. That is, the sentence does not say "we did not send the Qurʾān down" — it says **it was sent down, but not for this purpose**.

**This is what is most often lost in translation.** The sentence "We did not send the Qurʾān down on you that you should suffer" grants that the sending down took place, and **excludes one purpose only.**

**And the type of the *lām* has also been disputed:**

| Type | What it conveys | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| ***Lām al-taʿlīl*** | **Purpose** | "we did not send it down so that…" — the intention is negated |
| ***Lām al-ʿāqiba*** | **Outcome** | "we did not send it down so as to result in…" — the outcome is negated |

**Both explanations are transmitted; both bring the sense of the sentence to the same place:** *shaqā* is neither the aim nor the outcome of this Book.

### إِلَّا تَذْكِرَةً — a disjunctive exception

**The exception *illā tadhkiratan* is not made from the *li-tashqā* that precedes it** — because "distress" and "reminder" are not of the same genus. **The lexicographers count this a *istithnāʾ munqaṭiʿ* (disjunctive exception):** its sense is **"rather, only"**.

**So the sentence has two steps: one purpose is rejected, and another is put in its place.**

| Rejected | Put in its place |
|---|---|
| *Li-tashqā* — **your distress** | *Tadhkiratan li-man yakhshā* — **another's remembering** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pronouns on the two sides:** the subject of the rejected purpose is **the one addressed himself** (*tashqā* — you); the object of the purpose put in its place is **someone else** (*li-man yakhshā*). **That is, the centre of gravity of the Book is shifted from the one who carries it to the one who hears it.**

### The other links of the same boundary

**This sentence is the plainest expression of a boundary treated in many places in the series. The same line was treated at `026-suara.md` on 26/3 and at `035-fatir.md` on 35/7-9, and I rely on those treatments:**

| Place | Expression | How it says it |
|---|---|---|
| al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/3 | *Laʿallaka **bākhiʿun nafsaka** allā yakūnū muʾminīn* | **Diagnosis** — "you will all but destroy yourself" |
| Fāṭir 35/8 | *Fa-lā tadhhab nafsuka ʿalayhim **ḥasarāt*** | **Prohibition** — "do not waste away in grief" |
| al-Kahf 18/6 | *Fa-laʿallaka **bākhiʿun nafsaka*** | **Diagnosis** — again |
| **Ṭāhā 20/2** | *Mā anzalnā ʿalayka'l-Qurʾāna **li-tashqā*** | **Negation** — the purpose itself is removed |

**It was recorded at al-Shuʿarāʾ: the root ب-خ-ع is to take the knife in slaughtering beyond what is required, down to the *bikhaʿ* point at the base of the neck vertebrae — that is, "to the very end of the limit".** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the difference is worth recording.** al-Shuʿarāʾ and al-Kahf **describe a state that exists**; Fāṭir **lays down a prohibition**; **Ṭāhā goes down to the source of the matter: it says that this state is not the purpose of the Book.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verb forms of the three verses:** the others take the **behaviour** of the one addressed as their subject, **Ṭāhā takes the revelation itself.** That is, the consolation is given not as a piece of advice but **as a definition of the Book.**

### لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ — to whom the reminder is addressed

The root **خ-ش-ي**: **to hold back because one knows the greatness of a thing.** The lexicographers record a difference between *khashya* and *khawf*: **khawf** is fear in general, while **khashya** is a holding back **grounded in knowledge**. Indeed *innamā yakhsha'llāha min ʿibādihi'l-ʿulamāʾ* (Fāṭir 35/28) supports this distinction.

**And it must be recorded that the sentence draws a boundary: the Book comes down to everyone, but its working as a *tadhkira* is made conditional.** **This distinction was treated at `080-abese.md` (*ammā mani'staghnā* / *wa ammā man jāʾaka yasʿā*) and at `050-kaf.md` on 50/37 (*li-man kāna lahu qalb*);** I rely on those treatments.

**And a link within the sūra is worth recording:** the same root will be used in the forty-fourth verse **of Pharaoh**: *laʿallahu yatadhakkaru **aw yakhshā***. **That is, the condition of the third verse is left open even for the hardest addressee in the sūra.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

## 20/4-6 — تَنزِيلًا مِّمَّنْ خَلَقَ ٱلْأَرْضَ وَٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ ٱلْعُلَى · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱسْتَوَىٰ · لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا وَمَا تَحْتَ ٱلثَّرَىٰ

*Tanzīlan mimman khalaqa'l-arḍa wa's-samāwāti'l-ʿulā · Ar-Raḥmānu ʿala'l-ʿarshi'stawā · Lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍi wa mā baynahumā wa mā taḥta'th-tharā*

"**A sending down from the One who created the earth and the high heavens. · The Merciful has mounted the throne. · To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens, whatever is on the earth, whatever is between the two, and whatever is beneath the soil.**"

### The reversal of the order

**A point of word order is worth recording: the verse names *al-arḍ* (the earth) before *al-samāwāt* (the heavens).**

**The usual order in the Qurʾān generally runs from the heavens down to the earth** (*khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*). **Here it is reversed.**

**I record this as an observation and make no claim about intent:** the sentence begins from the ground under the feet of the one addressed and climbs upward; **and in the sixth verse it goes further down still:** *wa mā taḥta'th-tharā* — beneath the soil.

**ٱلثَّرَىٰ** — root **ث-ر-ي**: **moist earth, damp ground.** The lexicographers record that the word is used not of dry earth (*turāb*) but of **earth holding moisture in it**. The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.

**And four layers are counted:** the heavens, the earth, what is between the two, **what is beneath the soil.** **The same technique of "covering every direction" was treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/1-6 and at `019-meryem.md` on 19/64;** I rely on those treatments. **The difference here is that a fourth layer has been added** — the scope is widened not only upward but **downward** as well.

### ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱسْتَوَىٰ

**On this expression I enter a note per the method (`USLUP.md`) and do not open the discussion.**

**The same expression was treated at `032-secde.md` on 32/4, and two positions were tabulated there** — **tafwīḍ** (the wording is accepted as it stands and its modality is committed to God) and **taʾwīl** (it is interpreted as the establishing of sovereignty). **Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference. I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.** The common principle is al-Shūrā 42/11.

**The only thing that belongs here is the choice of subject, and this is a datum of the text: the subject of the sentence is *al-Raḥmān*.**

**This expression occurs in seven places in the Qurʾān** (al-Aʿrāf 7/54, Yūnus 10/3, al-Raʿd 13/2, Ṭāhā 20/5, al-Furqān 25/59, al-Sajda 32/4, al-Ḥadīd 57/4) **and in most of them the subject is *Allāh* or a pronoun. There are two places where it is built with the name *al-Raḥmān*: here and al-Furqān 25/59.** This is a datum that can be counted.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the distribution above:** the subject of the sentence that declares sovereignty has been made **the name that declares mercy**. **And it was treated at `019-meryem.md` how this name forms the spine of a sūra;** in Ṭāhā the name occurs **four times** (5, 90, 108, 109) and **all four are in a scene of power:** the throne, Aaron's reminder before the calf, the day the voices fall silent, the moment intercession is made conditional on permission. **This distribution can be verified from the text.**

## 20/7-8 — وَإِن تَجْهَرْ بِٱلْقَوْلِ فَإِنَّهُۥ يَعْلَمُ ٱلسِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى · ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ لَهُ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ

*Wa in tajhar bi'l-qawli fa-innahu yaʿlamu's-sirra wa akhfā · Allāhu lā ilāha illā huw, lahu'l-asmāʾu'l-ḥusnā*

"**Though you speak the word aloud, He knows the secret and what is more hidden than it. · God — there is no god but He. To Him belong the most beautiful names.**"

### ٱلسِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى — two steps

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: three degrees are counted but only two are named.**

| Degree | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Tajhar bi'l-qawl* | **What is said aloud** |
| 2 | *Al-sirr* | **The secret** — unsaid, held within |
| 3 | *Wa **akhfā*** | **What is more hidden than it** — not named |

***Akhfā* is an elative: "more hidden".** And what is meant by it has been disputed:

| Reading | What *akhfā* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **What is hidden even from the person himself** — what he has not yet become aware of |
| 2 | **What has not yet been born** — the thought that will occur to him in the future |
| 3 | **The furthest limit of hiddenness, without specifying a degree** |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the elative is left without an object:** the sentence **does not define** the third step. **Had it defined it, that too would have been a limit.** That it leaves off naming shows that the limit has been removed.

### The reversal of the conditional sentence

**A point of word order is worth recording: the sentence does not say *"even if you speak in secret He knows"* — it says *"even if you speak aloud"*.**

**The expected construction is this: "even if you conceal it, He knows".** The verse does the opposite: **it makes openness the condition and hiddenness the answer.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the direction of the condition:** the sentence is saying that **raising the voice is unnecessary**. **And this is on the same line as the second verse of the sūra:** there it was said that the Book is not a burden on the one addressed, **here that the one addressed need not raise his voice.** Both remove a **surplus**.

**And a link within the sūra must be recorded:** in the hundred and eighth verse all voices will be hushed (*fa-lā tasmaʿu illā hamsā*), and in the ninety-sixth the Sāmirī's secret will come out into the open. **Sound and hiddenness are two axes at work throughout the sūra.**

### لَهُ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ

**The expression was treated at `059-hasr.md` on 59/24, and it was recorded there: *lahu* has been brought forward, and in Arabic the fronting of the predicate conveys *ḥaṣr* (restriction) — "the most beautiful names belong to Him alone"; *ḥusnā* is the feminine elative of *aḥsan*, so not "beautiful names" but "the most beautiful names".** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**It was also recorded there: the expression occurs in four places in the Qurʾān (al-Aʿrāf 7/180, al-Isrāʾ 17/110, Ṭāhā 20/8, al-Ḥashr 59/24).** **This verse is one of those four.**

**And there is something that belongs here: where the sentence stands.** The eighth verse is the sentence that **closes the opening block of the sūra**; and in the ninth verse the text passes straight into the story of Moses.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence:** the story begins after **a speaker whose identity has been declared**. **And in the fourteenth verse of the story the same sentence will come once more, this time said to Moses:** *innanī ana'llāhu lā ilāha illā ana.* **The eighth verse in the third person, the fourteenth in the first.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

## 20/9-12 — وَهَلْ أَتَىٰكَ حَدِيثُ مُوسَىٰٓ · إِذْ رَءَا نَارًا فَقَالَ لِأَهْلِهِ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ نَارًا لَّعَلِّىٓ ءَاتِيكُم مِّنْهَا بِقَبَسٍ أَوْ أَجِدُ عَلَى ٱلنَّارِ هُدًى · فَلَمَّآ أَتَىٰهَا نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰ · إِنِّىٓ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكَ فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ إِنَّكَ بِٱلْوَادِ ٱلْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى

*Wa hal atāka ḥadīthu Mūsā · Idh raʾā nāran fa-qāla li-ahlihi'mkuthū innī ānastu nāran laʿallī ātīkum minhā bi-qabasin aw ajidu ʿala'n-nāri hudā · Fa-lammā atāhā nūdiya yā Mūsā · Innī ana rabbuka fakhlaʿ naʿlayka innaka bi'l-wādi'l-muqaddasi Ṭuwā*

"**Has the report of Moses come to you? · When he saw a fire and said to his family, 'Stay here; I have made out a fire. Perhaps I shall bring you a brand from it, or find a guide at the fire.' · When he reached it he was called: 'O Moses! · I, indeed I am your Lord. Take off your sandals; you are in the sacred valley, Ṭuwā.'**"

### Three tellings — the scene compared

**This scene has been treated twice in the series: `028-kasas.md` on 28/29-35 and `027-neml.md` on 27/7-8.** **The analysis of the root أ-ن-س was made at al-Qaṣaṣ** (to see and make out, to grow warm; from the same root *ins* and *uns*; the lexicographers record the sense "to sense a light from afar") **and the two tellings were compared in a table at al-Naml.** **I do not repeat the root analysis; I rely on those treatments.**

**What belongs here is the comparison of all three at once. The whole of the table can be verified from the text:**

| | **Ṭāhā 20/10-12** | al-Naml 27/7-8 | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/29-30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Word to the family | ***Imkuthū*** — "stay" | **None** | **None** |
| Making it out | *Innī **ānastu** nārā* | *Innī **ānastu** nārā* | *Ānasa min jānibi'ṭ-Ṭūri nārā* |
| Statement of place | Given afterwards: *al-wādi'l-muqaddasi **Ṭuwā*** | **None** | *Min jānibi'ṭ-Ṭūr* |
| What is to be brought | *Bi-**qabasin*** | *Bi-**shihābin qabasin*** | *Bi-**jadhwatin** mina'n-nār* |
| The second possibility | ***Aw ajidu ʿala'n-nāri hudā*** | *Bi-khabarin* | *Bi-khabarin* |
| Mention of warming | **None** | *Laʿallakum taṣṭalūn* | *Laʿallakum taṣṭalūn* |
| The first call | ***Yā Mūsā*** — the name | *Būrika man fi'n-nār* — blessing | *Innī ana'llāhu rabbu'l-ʿālamīn* — identity |
| Then | *Innī ana rabbuka **fakhlaʿ naʿlayk*** | Identity a verse later (27/9) | — |

**Four differences are worth recording, and all four are countable data.**

**One — only in Ṭāhā is there a command spoken to the family: *imkuthū* (stay, remain).** The root **م-ك-ث**: **to halt in a place and wait, to tarry.** **That is, Ṭāhā opens the scene with Moses being left alone.**

**Two — only in Ṭāhā is the second thing sought *hudā*.** In al-Naml and al-Qaṣaṣ what is expected from the fire is **news** and **a brand**; in Ṭāhā **a brand** and **a guide.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the distribution of words in the sūra:** the root ه-د-ي occurs in eight verses of this sūra (10, 50, 79, 82, 122, 123, 128, 135) and **comes out of Moses's own mouth in the first sentence of the story, before anything has yet happened.** **That is, the sūra places what is sought at the head of the story, and closes with the same word in its last verse:** *wa mani'htadā* (135).

**Three — the expression *ʿala'n-nāri hudā* is worth recording.** A fire seen at night in the desert is the sign **that someone is there**; a man who has lost his way goes to that fire and asks the road. **That is, the sentence describes an everyday custom of the desert.**

**And the observation recorded at `028-kasas.md` holds here too:** the call comes not in a place that was sought — **but on the road, at a point turned aside to for an everyday need.** **What is peculiar to Ṭāhā is this, and I give it as my own reading:** Moses was looking for **someone to ask the road of**; **what he found was the one who gives the road itself.** The word is the same: *hudā*.

### إِنِّىٓ أَنَا۠ رَبُّكَ — the repetition of the pronoun

**A point of word order must be recorded: *innī* already conveys "I"; and then *ana* (I) comes as well.**

**In Arabic this is the *ḍamīr al-faṣl*, the pronoun of separation or emphasis, and it conveys that the predicate is bound to the subject definitively.** In English it can be given only by stress: **"I, indeed I am your Lord."**

**And the same pattern will come once more in the fourteenth verse, denser still:** *innanī ana'llāhu lā ilāha illā ana.* **Three separate pronouns across two verses.**

### فَٱخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ

The root **خ-ل-ع**: **to pull off and cast away something worn on top.** The lexicographers explain the root through **a garment's or a covering's being parted from the body**; from the same root come *khilʿa* (the robe of honour bestowed) and *khulʿ* (the dissolving of a marriage). **The root ن-ع-ل occurs here for the first time in the series:** *naʿl* — **sandal, shoe; what is placed under the foot.**

**The ground of the command is given in the verse: *innaka bi'l-wādi'l-muqaddasi Ṭuwā*.**

**In the classical commentaries different explanations of the reason for the command are transmitted, and the disagreement must not be concealed:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The sanctity of the place** — taking off the sandal is reverence for the place |
| 2 | **Contact** — the bare foot's touching the blessed ground |
| 3 | **The quality of the sandal** — that certain hides were not held fit by the custom of the day |
| 4 | **Symbolic** — the laying aside of worldly preoccupations |

**The fourth explanation is widespread in the ṣūfī literature; I transmit it too as a transmitted explanation, not as a datum the text supplies.** **I do not impose a preference.**

**The one thing that can be verified from the text is this, and I record it:** the command comes **immediately after the declaring of identity and before the giving of the commission.** **That is, the first thing commanded is not a task but a preparation.**

**طُوًى** — given as the name of the valley. **Whether the word is a proper name or an attribute meaning "twice, folded over" has been disputed;** among the variant readings both a nunated and an unnunated pronunciation are transmitted. **I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

## 20/13-16 — وَأَنَا ٱخْتَرْتُكَ فَٱسْتَمِعْ لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · إِنَّنِىٓ أَنَا ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنَا۠ فَٱعْبُدْنِى وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِذِكْرِىٓ · إِنَّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ ءَاتِيَةٌ أَكَادُ أُخْفِيهَا لِتُجْزَىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍۭ بِمَا تَسْعَىٰ · فَلَا يَصُدَّنَّكَ عَنْهَا مَن لَّا يُؤْمِنُ بِهَا وَٱتَّبَعَ هَوَىٰهُ فَتَرْدَىٰ

*Wa ana'khtartuka fa'stamiʿ limā yūḥā · Innanī ana'llāhu lā ilāha illā ana faʿbudnī wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta li-dhikrī · Inna's-sāʿata ātiyatun akādu ukhfīhā li-tujzā kullu nafsin bimā tasʿā · Fa-lā yaṣuddannaka ʿanhā man lā yuʾminu bihā wattabaʿa hawāhu fa-tardā*

"'**I have chosen you; so listen to what is revealed. · Truly I, indeed I am God; there is no god but I. So serve Me, and establish the prayer for My remembrance. · The Hour is surely coming; I all but keep it hidden, so that every soul may be repaid for what it strives after. · Let no one who does not believe in it and follows his own whim turn you away from it, or you will perish.**'"

### فَٱسْتَمِعْ لِمَا يُوحَىٰ — choosing and listening

**A point of word order must be recorded: the verb of choosing is in the perfect (*ikhtartuka* — I have chosen), while the command to listen is joined to it by the causal *fāʾ*.** **That is, listening is set down as the consequence of having been chosen.**

**And the verb is not *ismaʿ* but *istamiʿ* — Form VIII.** In Arabic this pattern conveys **deliberate listening, listening with attention.** **The same pattern of the same root occurred in a sentence of reassurance at `026-suara.md` on 26/15:** *innā maʿakum **mustamiʿūn***. **I rely on that treatment.**

**I record this as an observation:** the same root, in al-Shuʿarāʾ **God's listening**, here **the listening commanded of Moses.**

**And *limā yūḥā* — a passive verb, its agent not named.** The sentence does not say "what I reveal to you"; **it says "what is revealed".** This will be repeated in the thirty-eighth verse of the same sūra: *idh awḥaynā ilā ummika **mā yūḥā***.

## 20/14 — وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِذِكْرِىٓ

*Wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta li-dhikrī*

"**And establish the prayer for My remembrance.**"

### One of the densest sentences in the sūra

**The verse is the last sentence of the fourteenth verse and deserves separate treatment. The sequence must be recorded:**

| Order | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Innanī ana'llāh* | **Who** |
| 2 | *Lā ilāha illā ana* | **The note of uniqueness** |
| 3 | *Faʿbudnī* | **The general command** — service |
| 4 | *Wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta* | **The particular command** — the prayer |
| 5 | *Li-dhikrī* | **The ground** |

**That is, the sentence descends from identity to service, and within service to a concrete act, and then states the ground of that act.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the prayer comes **not as an instance of the command to serve but as an item named separately** — the separate commanding of a single act after a general command is called in Arabic *ʿaṭf al-khāṣṣ ʿalā'l-ʿāmm* (the coupling of the particular to the general), and it conveys **that the act has been brought forward.**

### لِذِكْرِى — three readings

**More than one reading of the expression is transmitted in the classical commentaries, and the disagreement must not be concealed.** **One reason for this is that the verbal noun *dhikr* can be annexed either to its agent or to its object.**

| Reading | What *li-dhikrī* means | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"So as to remember Me"** — the verbal noun annexed to its object | The content of the prayer is remembrance; the most widespread reading |
| 2 | **"So that I may remember"** — the verbal noun annexed to its agent | Consistent with *fa'dhkurūnī adhkurkum* (al-Baqara 2/152) |
| 3 | **"When you remember Me"** — the *lām* conveys time | The time of the prayer for one who forgets and then remembers |

**All three readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record one indication, not as a preference, and its ground is the sūra's own weave of words:** the root ذ-ك-ر is one of the spines of this sūra (tabulated in the section on structure) **and in the hundred and twenty-fourth verse the subject of turning away is precisely *dhikrī*:** *wa man aʿraḍa **ʿan dhikrī***.

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | *Wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta **li-dhikrī*** | **The prayer — for remembering** |
| **124** | *Wa man aʿraḍa **ʿan dhikrī*** | **Turning away — from remembering** |

**The same expression, at the two ends of the sūra: one a command, the other an abandonment.** **This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra** and an indication supporting the first reading — but it is not a proof.

### What this says to the present

**The verse gives a ground for the prayer, and the ground is built not on benefit but on relationship.** The sentence does not say "so that you may find peace" or "so that you may gain discipline". **These may be results of the prayer; but they are not the ground the verse gives.**

**And a measure of time emerges:** remembering is something that cannot be sustained continuously; **forgetting is the ordinary state of a human being.** Indeed the same sūra will name the first ancestor of humankind in the hundred and fifteenth verse in connection with **forgetting**. **Read together with this verse, the binding of the prayer to fixed times appears as an acceptance of forgetting and the building of an order against it.** **I record this as my own reading; its ground is the verse's clause of purpose together with the sūra's axis of *nisyān* (forgetting).**

## 20/15-16 — إِنَّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ ءَاتِيَةٌ أَكَادُ أُخْفِيهَا

### أَكَادُ أُخْفِيهَا — the sentence that has been dwelt on

**The sentence is among the expressions most debated by the lexicographers and the exegetes, and the disagreement must not be concealed.** ***Akādu* means "I all but"; *ukhfīhā* means "I keep it hidden".** A literal rendering: **"I all but keep it hidden."** The difficulty is this: the Hour is hidden already; what does "I all but keep it hidden" mean?

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | ***Akādu* here is for emphasis** and carries no sense of "all but": "I keep it hidden" |
| 2 | **The furthest limit of hiddenness:** "I hide it as though I hid it even from Myself" — an intensification |
| 3 | **That the verb *akhfā* is among the *aḍdād*:** the root means both to hide and to bring out into the open; "I shall all but bring it out" |

**The ground of the third reading is a fact of the language: the root خ-ف-ي is among those the lexicographers count under the heading *aḍdād* (a word's bearing two opposite senses).** **I transmit this as the lexicographers record it. All three readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**What can be verified from the text is this: the ground of the hiddenness is given in the verse.** *Li-tujzā kullu nafsin bimā **tasʿā*** — **"so that every soul may be repaid for what it strives after."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the purpose clause:** the not-knowing of the time is given not as a lack but as **the condition on which the repayment can be just**. **A test whose time is known cannot measure what it measures.**

The root **س-ع-ي**: **to walk fast, to hurry, to exert oneself for a task.** **The word occurs three times in this sūra** and its distribution is worth recording:

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | *Bimā **tasʿā*** | **A person's striving** |
| 20 | *Fa-idhā hiya ḥayyatun **tasʿā*** | **The movement of the serpent** |
| 66 | *Annahā **tasʿā*** | **The ropes of the sorcerers** |

**The same verb: one of them the subject of the reckoning, two of them a movement the eye sees.** This is a distribution that can be verified from the text.

**فَتَرْدَىٰ** (16) — root **ر-د-ي**: **to fall from a height, to tumble into a chasm.** *Tardā* — you will perish; the concrete picture of the root is **a fall.** **The same root was treated at `092-leyl.md` on 92/11 (*idhā taraddā*);** I rely on that treatment.

## 20/17-21 — وَمَا تِلْكَ بِيَمِينِكَ يَٰمُوسَىٰ · قَالَ هِىَ عَصَاىَ أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ عَلَيْهَا وَأَهُشُّ بِهَا عَلَىٰ غَنَمِى وَلِىَ فِيهَا مَـَٔارِبُ أُخْرَىٰ · قَالَ أَلْقِهَا يَٰمُوسَىٰ · فَأَلْقَىٰهَا فَإِذَا هِىَ حَيَّةٌ تَسْعَىٰ · قَالَ خُذْهَا وَلَا تَخَفْ سَنُعِيدُهَا سِيرَتَهَا ٱلْأُولَىٰ

*Wa mā tilka bi-yamīnika yā Mūsā · Qāla hiya ʿaṣāya atawakkaʾu ʿalayhā wa ahushshu bihā ʿalā ghanamī wa liya fīhā maʾāribu ukhrā · Qāla alqihā yā Mūsā · Fa-alqāhā fa-idhā hiya ḥayyatun tasʿā · Qāla khudhhā wa lā takhaf, sa-nuʿīduhā sīratahā'l-ūlā*

"'**And what is that in your right hand, O Moses?**' · He said: '**It is my staff. I lean on it; with it I beat down leaves for my sheep; and I have other uses for it.**' · '**Cast it down, O Moses!**' He said. · **He cast it down, and there it was, a serpent moving swiftly!** · '**Take hold of it and do not be afraid. We shall return it to its former state**,' He said."

### Why the question is asked

**This is the question most dwelt on in the sūra, and the reason it is asked is not stated in the text.** In the classical commentaries different explanations are transmitted: to turn the attention onto that object, to open the conversation, or to recall the familiar first so as to soften the shock of the miracle. **I transmit these as transmitted explanations.**

**Now I record my own reading, showing its ground. The ground is not the question itself — it is the length of the answer.**

**The question is three words: *wa mā tilka bi-yamīnika*. The answer is three items, and none of them was asked for:**

| Part of the answer | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| *Hiya ʿaṣāya* | **The name** — this is what was asked |
| *Atawakkaʾu ʿalayhā* | **The first use** — leaning |
| *Wa ahushshu bihā ʿalā ghanamī* | **The second use** — beating leaves down for his animals |
| *Wa liya fīhā maʾāribu ukhrā* | **The rest** — not even enumerated |

**The question asks "what is it"; the answer answers "what is it good for".** **And the answer is three times as long as what was asked.**

**My own reading is this, and it is not binding:** the work of the question is to make Moses count out, in his own words, **how familiar** the thing in his hand is. **Because the next command (*alqihā*) will break precisely that familiarity.** The object does not change — **the certainty about the object changes.**

**There is a second datum of the text supporting this reading: in the twenty-first verse the staff is returned to *sīratahā'l-ūlā* (its former state).** **That is, the scene does not transform the object permanently; it suspends its former state once and gives it back.** **What is taught is not the object but to whom the ruling over the object belongs.**

**And a third datum: the same hand and the same object will be named once more in the sixty-ninth verse:** *wa alqi **mā fī yamīnika***. **There too the staff is not called by name — it is called "what is in your right hand".** **This overlap between the two verses can be verified from the text.**

### أَهُشُّ — the concreteness of the word

The root **ه-ش-ش** — **occurs here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to beat the branches of a tree with a stick so that its leaves fall** (for an animal to eat). *Hashsha'l-waraq* — he beat the leaves down. A sense of **softness, slackness** is also recorded in the root (*hashshun* — brittle, easily broken).

**The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse names the shepherd's daily work **by its technical name.** **And at `019-meryem.md` on 19/25 the same family of verbs was commanded to Mary (Maryam)** (*wa huzzī ilayki bi-jidhʿi'n-nakhla* — shake the trunk of the palm towards you). **Both scenes involve the shaking of a tree; one for a livelihood, the other for help.**

**مَـَٔارِب** — root **أ-ر-ب**: **need, requirement, business.** The plural of *maʾraba*. From the same root comes *arīb* (one who knows his need, resourceful). **The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

### حَيَّةٌ تَسْعَىٰ — three sūras, three words

**What the staff turns into is named in the Qurʾān by three different words, and this is a datum that can be counted:**

| Place | Word | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **Ṭāhā 20/20** | ***Ḥayya* tasʿā** | **A serpent** — the name of the kind, and **its movement** |
| al-Naml 27/10 / al-Qaṣaṣ 28/31 | *Ka-annahā **jānn*** | **A small, quick snake** — with a particle of comparison |
| al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/32 / al-Aʿrāf 7/107 | ***Thuʿbān** mubīn* | **A great serpent** — manifest |

**These scenes were treated at `026-suara.md` and `027-neml.md`; I do not repeat the word analyses.**

**The difference that belongs here is this, and it can be verified from the text:** in al-Naml and al-Qaṣaṣ the scene is given **through Moses's eyes** (*ka-annahā* — "as though") and his reaction is to flee; **in Ṭāhā there is no particle of comparison** — it says *ḥayya* outright, **and the fleeing is not mentioned either.** All that Ṭāhā gives is the command *lā takhaf*.

**I record this as an observation, not as a contradiction but as a choice of detail:** the three tellings cut into the same event at three different points — **one the sight, one the fear, one the object itself.**

## 20/22-24 — وَٱضْمُمْ يَدَكَ إِلَىٰ جَنَاحِكَ تَخْرُجْ بَيْضَآءَ مِنْ غَيْرِ سُوٓءٍ ءَايَةً أُخْرَىٰ · لِنُرِيَكَ مِنْ ءَايَٰتِنَا ٱلْكُبْرَى · ٱذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ

*Waḍmum yadaka ilā janāḥika takhruj bayḍāʾa min ghayri sūʾin āyatan ukhrā · Li-nuriyaka min āyātina'l-kubrā · Idhhab ilā Firʿawna innahu ṭaghā*

"'**Draw your hand to your side; it will come out white, without any ill — another sign. · That We may show you some of Our greatest signs. · Go to Pharaoh; he has transgressed.**'"

**مِنْ غَيْرِ سُوٓءٍ** — "**without any ill**". **The note itself is worth recording: the verse states separately that the whiteness is not a disease.** For a man of that day a hand gone white in the desert was a direct sign of disease; **the sentence closes off that possibility.**

**جَنَاح** — when the word *janāḥ* is used of a human being it means **"arm, side, armpit"**; **it was treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/32 (*waḍmum ilayka janāḥaka mina'r-rahb*), and the explanation the lexicographers ground in the image of a bird was transmitted there.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the difference is worth recording:** in al-Qaṣaṣ the drawing in of the *janāḥ* was against **fear**; **here the putting of the hand into the *janāḥ* is for the producing of a sign.** The same word, in two different offices.

### إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ — the single word of the ground

The root **ط-غ-ي** was treated in many places in the series (`079-naziat.md`, `050-kaf.md`, `089-fecr.md`, `068-kalem.md`) **and is not repeated here.** The description given there: **water's overflowing its vessel; going past the measure and out beyond it.**

**What belongs here is the distribution of the root in this sūra, and this is a datum of the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **24** | *Idhhab ilā Firʿawna innahu **ṭaghā*** | **Pharaoh** — the ground of the commission |
| **43** | *Idhhabā ilā Firʿawna innahu **ṭaghā*** | **Pharaoh** — the same sentence, in the dual |
| **81** | *Wa lā **taṭghaw** fīh* | **The Children of Israel** — after being delivered |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the three verses above:** the sūra warns the delivered party **with the very root that characterises the one they were delivered from.** **That is, *ṭughyān* is given not as a risk attached to an identity but as one attached to a position** — and indeed in the eighty-first verse the subject of the prohibition is **the favour itself.**

## 20/25-28 — قَالَ رَبِّ ٱشْرَحْ لِى صَدْرِى · وَيَسِّرْ لِىٓ أَمْرِى · وَٱحْلُلْ عُقْدَةً مِّن لِّسَانِى · يَفْقَهُوا۟ قَوْلِى

*Qāla rabbi'shraḥ lī ṣadrī · Wa yassir lī amrī · Waḥlul ʿuqdatan min lisānī · Yafqahū qawlī*

"He said: '**My Lord! Open my breast for me. · Make my task easy for me. · Loosen a knot from my tongue · that they may understand my speech.**'"

### Comparison with al-Inshirāḥ

**The root ش-ر-ح was analysed in detail at `094-insirah.md` on 94/1**, and the description recorded there is this: **the concrete sense of the root is "to spread out and open flesh, to widen it by cutting it into thin slices"** (*sharaḥtu'l-laḥm*); from this arise the senses **to open and widen a thing** and **to open up a saying** (whence *sharḥ*, exposition). **The root ص-د-ر was treated there as well:** what comes forward, the place of issuing; from the same root *maṣdar*. **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat the root analysis.**

**What belongs here is the comparison of the two texts, and the whole of it can be verified:**

| | **Ṭāhā 20/25** | al-Inshirāḥ 94/1 |
|---|---|---|
| Mood | ***Imperative*** — *ishraḥ* | **Question** — *alam nashraḥ* |
| Who says it | **Moses** — a servant | **The text** — God |
| Time | **Future** — not yet done | **Past** — done |
| Function | **Request** | **Reminder** |

**The same verb, the same object (*ṣadr*), two different directions.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above:** al-Inshirāḥ reminds one prophet of **what has already been given him**; **Ṭāhā records another prophet's asking for the same thing.** **That is, the same gift stands in one place as a request and in the other as an answer.**

**And a second overlap recorded at `094-insirah.md` can be carried over here:** in al-Inshirāḥ, *sharḥ* was followed by *yusr* (ease) — *inna maʿa'l-ʿusri yusrā*. **The same order is in Ṭāhā too: *ishraḥ* … *wa yassir*.** **Two sūras use the same two words in the same order.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

### The structure of the prayer

**Four sentences, four steps, and the last sentence a consequence:**

| Order | Request | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ishraḥ lī **ṣadrī*** | **The inward** — the capacity to carry |
| 2 | *Wa yassir lī **amrī*** | **The work** — the commission itself |
| 3 | *Waḥlul ʿuqdatan min **lisānī*** | **The instrument** — speech |
| 4 | *Yafqahū **qawlī*** | **The result** — the other side's understanding |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence:** the requests widen **from the inside outward**, and **the fourth is no longer a request but the ground of the third.** Indeed *yafqahū* is in the jussive — that is, it is the answer to the imperative, not a separate petition.

**And what is not asked for in the whole of the prayer is worth recording: Moses does not ask that the one he addresses should believe.** **He asks to be understood.** *Yafqahū qawlī* — "that they may understand my speech". Not the outcome, but **the soundness of the transmission.**

**This is on the same line as the boundary treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/56** (*innaka lā tahdī man aḥbabta*) — where six separate expressions of that boundary from six sūras were tabulated. **What Ṭāhā adds is that the boundary appears in a prophet's own prayer.** I rely on that treatment.

### عُقْدَةً مِّن لِّسَانِى

The root **ع-ق-د**: **to knot, to tie, to bring two ends together and draw them tight.** **The root was treated at `113-felak.md` (*al-naffāthāti fi'l-ʿuqad*);** I rely on that treatment. From the same root come *ʿaqd* (a contract — the binding of two parties) and *iʿtiqād* (the heart's binding itself to a judgement).

**A point of word order must be recorded, and it is easily missed: *ʿuqdatan* is indefinite and it is *min lisānī* — "a knot from my tongue".**

**That is, what is asked is not the loosening of the whole tongue.** **One knot is asked for** — and this **agrees with Moses's own words at `028-kasas.md` on 28/34:** *wa akhī Hārūnu huwa **afṣaḥu minnī lisānā*** — "my brother Aaron (Hārūn) is **more fluent of tongue** than I." **There too it was not an absolute incapacity but a comparison.**

**I record this as an observation:** both sūras put the matter as a **degree**, not as a **defect**.

The root **ف-ق-ه** **was treated at `048-fetih.md`, `059-hasr.md` and `063-munafikun.md`** — **to understand in depth, to grasp what lies under a saying.** **I do not repeat it here.** **And the choice of verb is worth recording:** it does not say *yasmaʿū* (that they may hear); **understanding itself is what is asked for.**

## 20/29-35 — وَٱجْعَل لِّى وَزِيرًا مِّنْ أَهْلِى · هَٰرُونَ أَخِى · ٱشْدُدْ بِهِۦٓ أَزْرِى · وَأَشْرِكْهُ فِىٓ أَمْرِى · كَىْ نُسَبِّحَكَ كَثِيرًا · وَنَذْكُرَكَ كَثِيرًا · إِنَّكَ كُنتَ بِنَا بَصِيرًا

*Wajʿal lī wazīran min ahlī · Hārūna akhī · Ushdud bihi azrī · Wa ashrikhu fī amrī · Kay nusabbiḥaka kathīrā · Wa nadhkuraka kathīrā · Innaka kunta binā baṣīrā*

"'**Appoint for me a helper from my family: · Aaron, my brother. · Strengthen my back with him · and make him a partner in my task · that we may glorify You much · and remember You much. · Truly You are ever seeing us.**'"

### وَزِير and رِدْء — two sūras, two words

**The same request occurred with a different word at `028-kasas.md` on 28/34 and was treated there:** ***رِدْء*** — root **ر-د-أ**: **support, a helper who holds one up from behind**; the lexicographers explain the word by **the prop set behind a thing to keep it from toppling.** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the comparison of the two words.**

**وَزِير** — two explanations of its root are transmitted, and both come out at the same picture:

| Derivation | Root | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **و-ز-ر** (*wizr* — a heavy load) | **One who shares the load** |
| 2 | **و-ز-ر** (*wazar* — a mountain of refuge, a shelter) | **One who is leaned upon** |

**The root و-ز-ر was treated in the series at `094-insirah.md` on 94/2 (*wa waḍaʿnā ʿanka wizrak*), at `035-fatir.md` on 35/18 and at `039-zumer.md` on 39/7** — where the root's core of **weight** was recorded. **I rely on those treatments.**

**Now I set the two requests side by side, and the table can be verified from the text:**

| | **Ṭāhā 20/29-32** | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/34 |
|---|---|---|
| Word | ***Wazīr*** — one who shares the load / a support | ***Ridʾ*** — a prop that supports from behind |
| What is asked | ***Ushdud bihi azrī*** + ***ashrikhu fī amrī*** | ***Yuṣaddiqunī*** — that he may confirm me |
| Ground | ***Kay nusabbiḥaka … wa nadhkuraka*** | *Huwa afṣaḥu minnī lisānā* — fluency of speech |
| Emphasis | **Partnership** | **Confirmation** |

**The difference is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** what is asked for in al-Qaṣaṣ is **to be confirmed** — that is, a witness. **What is asked for in Ṭāhā is to be made a partner in the work** — *ashrikhu fī amrī.* **The request in al-Qaṣaṣ arises from a problem of communication, the request in Ṭāhā from the weight of the commission.** **The two sūras record two different grounds for the same request**, and each states its own ground in the text.

**And the answer recorded at `028-kasas.md` connects here too:** *sa-nashuddu **ʿaḍudaka** bi-akhīk* — "We shall strengthen your arm with your brother". **al-Qaṣaṣ gives the answer with the word *ʿaḍud* (upper arm); in Ṭāhā the request is built with the word *azr* (back).** **In both places the support is told through a bodily image** — one the arm, the other the back.

### ٱشْدُدْ بِهِۦٓ أَزْرِى

The root **أ-ز-ر** — **occurs here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **the back, the strength of the back; and the garment wrapped around the waist.** From the same root ***izār*** — the wrap tied at the waist, and ***muʾāzara*** — to stand behind someone.

**The picture is worth recording:** *azr* is where the strength gathers when a load is lifted. **So "strengthen my back" means "make firm the place in me that will lift the load".** And the first derivation of the word *wazīr* (*wizr* — load) lies in the same field. **The three words used across the request — *wazīr*, *azr*, *amr* — build a single scene: a heavy load lifted by two.**

### كَىْ نُسَبِّحَكَ كَثِيرًا — the ground itself

**A point of word order must be recorded: the ground of the entire request is given with *kay* (in order that), and two verbs are named — *nusabbiḥaka* and *nadhkuraka*.**

**The subject of both verbs is plural: "we".** **And both are acts of service, not of commission.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the content of the clause of purpose:** in asking for a helper, Moses does not point to **the success of the work** as his ground. **He does not say "so that we may persuade Pharaoh" or "so that the Children of Israel may be delivered".** The ground he points to is **remembrance** — and this is from the same root as the command in the fourteenth verse of the sūra: *wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta **li-dhikrī***.

| Verse | Expression | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | *Wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta **li-dhikrī*** | **God** — a command |
| **34** | *Wa **nadhkuraka** kathīrā* | **Moses** — the ground of the request |

**The word that occurred in the command comes back twenty verses later as the servant's own ground.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**إِنَّكَ كُنتَ بِنَا بَصِيرًا** (35) — "**Truly You are ever seeing us.**"

**The close of the prayer is worth recording: the request is sealed with a sentence of knowledge.** **And *binā* (us) is plural** — Moses has already brought into the sentence the partner not yet granted.

**And the root ب-ص-ر will return at the end of the sūra:** *wa qad kuntu **baṣīrā*** (125) — this time of the servant himself, and in a sentence of complaint. **This link will be treated in the section on 124-127.**

## 20/36-37 — قَالَ قَدْ أُوتِيتَ سُؤْلَكَ يَٰمُوسَىٰ · وَلَقَدْ مَنَنَّا عَلَيْكَ مَرَّةً أُخْرَىٰٓ

*Qāla qad ūtīta suʾlaka yā Mūsā · Wa laqad manannā ʿalayka marratan ukhrā*

"'**You have been granted your request, O Moses. · And indeed We showed you favour another time before.**'"

### أُوتِيتَ سُؤْلَكَ — the form of the answer

**A point of word order must be recorded, and it has three separate data.**

**One — the verb is passive: *ūtīta* — "you have been granted".** *Ātaytuka* (I have granted you) could have been said; it was not.

**Two — the particle *qad* at the head of a perfect verb conveys the certainty of the thing's having happened.** That is, not "it will be granted" but **"it has been granted".** The request has barely ended and the answer comes as already complete.

**Three — the answer does not enumerate the requests one by one.** There were four separate petitions (the breast, the ease, the tongue, the helper); the answer gathers them all into **a single word**: *suʾlaka* — "your request".

**سُؤْل** — root **س-ء-ل**: to ask, to request. ***Suʾl* is "the thing asked for"** — not a verbal noun but a noun with the sense of the object.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the three points above:** the answer does not take an accounting of the request. **And the answer given to Zechariah's prayer at `019-meryem.md` on 19/7-9 had the same form:** the objection was not debated, the fulfilment was announced directly. I rely on that treatment.

### مَرَّةً أُخْرَىٰ — the second favour

**The sentence ties the present gift to an earlier one, and from the thirty-eighth verse to the forty-first it will tell that earlier one.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** at the moment the commission is given, the one addressed is reminded of **the beginning of his own life.** **The sequence is worth recording: request — acceptance — the telling of the past.**

## 20/38-39 — إِذْ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَىٰٓ أُمِّكَ مَا يُوحَىٰٓ · أَنِ ٱقْذِفِيهِ فِى ٱلتَّابُوتِ فَٱقْذِفِيهِ فِى ٱلْيَمِّ فَلْيُلْقِهِ ٱلْيَمُّ بِٱلسَّاحِلِ يَأْخُذْهُ عَدُوٌّ لِّى وَعَدُوٌّ لَّهُۥ وَأَلْقَيْتُ عَلَيْكَ مَحَبَّةً مِّنِّى وَلِتُصْنَعَ عَلَىٰ عَيْنِىٓ

*Idh awḥaynā ilā ummika mā yūḥā · Ani'qdhifīhi fi't-tābūti fa'qdhifīhi fi'l-yammi fa'l-yulqihi'l-yammu bi's-sāḥili yaʾkhudhhu ʿaduwwun lī wa ʿaduwwun lah, wa alqaytu ʿalayka maḥabbatan minnī wa li-tuṣnaʿa ʿalā ʿaynī*

"'**When We revealed to your mother what was revealed: · "Cast him into the chest, then cast it into the water; let the water throw it onto the shore, and let one who is My enemy and his enemy take him." And I cast upon you a love from Me, that you might be reared before My eye.**'"

### Comparison with al-Qaṣaṣ — two tellings

**This section was treated in detail at `028-kasas.md` on 28/7-13.** There the five elements of the announcement given to the mother (suckle him — when you fear — cast him into the water — do not fear and do not grieve — We shall return him) were tabulated; *fārigh*, *rabaṭnā ʿalā qalbihā* and *ḥarramnā ʿalayhi'l-marāḍiʿ* were analysed; **and what was recorded there was this: "the promise comes about through an ordinary chain of causes."** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the difference between the two tellings, and the whole of it can be verified from the text:**

| | **Ṭāhā 20/38-40** | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/7-13 |
|---|---|---|
| **To whom it is said** | ***To Moses*** — "We revealed to your mother" | **To the reader** — third-person narration |
| The command to suckle | **None** | *Arḍiʿīh* — present |
| The chest | ***Al-tābūt*** — named | **Not named** |
| The verb | ***Iqdhifī*** — cast, hurl (twice) | *Alqī* — set down |
| The role of the water | ***Fa'l-yulqihi'l-yammu bi's-sāḥil*** — let the water throw it onto the shore | **None** |
| The one who takes him | ***ʿAduwwun lī wa ʿaduwwun lah*** | *Fa'ltaqaṭahu ālu Firʿawn* — the family of Pharaoh |
| Consolation to the mother | **None** | *Wa lā takhāfī wa lā taḥzanī* — present |
| Promise to the mother | **None** (announced to Moses: *fa-rajaʿnāka*) | *Innā rāddūhu ilayk* — present |
| **Love** | ***Wa alqaytu ʿalayka maḥabbatan minnī*** | **None** |
| **Being reared** | ***Wa li-tuṣnaʿa ʿalā ʿaynī*** | **None** |

**Three differences are worth recording.**

**One — the addressee changes.** al-Qaṣaṣ tells the story; **Ṭāhā tells the hero of the story his own history.** All the verbs are *ʿalayka*, *ummika*, *tuṣnaʿa* — **second person.**

**Two — Ṭāhā names the water's own act separately.** *Fa'l-yulqihi'l-yammu bi's-sāḥil* — **an imperative, and its subject is the water.** In Arabic this is a third-person command built with the *lām al-amr*. **That is, the sentence commands the water.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the subject of the imperative:** the command given to the mother and the command given to the water are ranged **in the same sentence, in the same mood.** **A human act and the act of the water are given as links in a single chain.** **This is the grammatical counterpart of the observation recorded at al-Qaṣaṣ, that "the promise comes about through an ordinary chain of causes".**

**Three — there are two sentences in Ṭāhā that are not in al-Qaṣaṣ: the love, and the being reared.** These are treated separately below.

### ٱقْذِفِيهِ — the harshness of the verb

The root **ق-ذ-ف** — **occurs here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to hurl a thing far, to throw it.** The word conveys **a forceful throw**; from the same root comes *qadhf* (to hurl an accusation — al-Nūr 24/4).

**The choice of word is worth recording, and it is easily missed:** the verse uses the same verb **twice** — the child is "hurled" into the chest, the chest is "hurled" into the water. **No softened verb has been used.**

**I record this as an observation:** the text records how hard the command was **by not softening the word.** **The same honesty was recorded at `019-meryem.md` on 19/23-25** (Mary's words *yā laytanī mittu qabla hādhā* given as they stand); I rely on that treatment.

**ٱلتَّابُوت** — **a chest, a box.** The word occurs once more in the Qurʾān, at al-Baqara 2/248 (the chest returning to the Children of Israel). **No connection is drawn between the two verses; I record only the distribution of the word.**

**ٱلْيَمّ** — **the sea, or a great river.** The word occurs four times in this sūra (twice in 39, then 78 and 97) **and the fourth is worth recording: in the ninety-seventh verse the calf will be flung into the *yamm*.** **That is, the sūra brings back the water that set the story going as the water that swallows the idol at the story's end.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

### وَأَلْقَيْتُ عَلَيْكَ مَحَبَّةً مِّنِّى

**Every word of the sentence is worth recording.**

**أَلْقَيْتُ** — "I cast, I laid down". **The verb gives the love as something placed.** And its subject is the first person singular: **"I".**

**مَحَبَّة** is indefinite — "a love". **And *minnī* follows: "from Me".** **That is, its source is stated separately.**

**عَلَيْكَ** — "upon you". **The preposition is worth recording:** not *laka* (for you) or *fī qalbika* (in your heart) — but ***ʿalā***, that is, **something laid on top, seen from outside.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the joint construction of the three words:** the verse tells the love not as **a quality of Moses's own** but as **a covering laid over him.** **And the verb is in the past tense: the thing is done.**

**And at `028-kasas.md` on 28/9 the result of this was told:** *qurratu ʿaynin lī wa lak lā taqtulūh* — the words of Pharaoh's wife. **That is, Ṭāhā gives the cause and al-Qaṣaṣ the result.** **Read together the two sūras yield a chain, and I record this as my own reading:** the reason for being protected in the enemy's house is not a power but **a feeling.**

### وَلِتُصْنَعَ عَلَىٰ عَيْنِىٓ

The root **ص-ن-ع**: **to make something with skill, with labour spent on it.** *Ṣināʿa* — a craft; *ṣunʿ* — work made with skill. **What distinguishes the root, as the lexicographers record, is that the work is done *with care*** — *fiʿl* (to do) is general, *ṣunʿ* particular.

**And the verb is passive: *tuṣnaʿa* — "that you might be made, be reared".**

**عَلَىٰ عَيْنِى** — "upon My eye". **In Arabic the idiom conveys being under watch and protection.** **I enter a note per the method (`USLUP.md`):** this expression cannot be understood in a way that ascribes an organ to God. **The note entered at `002-bakara.md` on 2/115 for *wajh* and at `040-gafir.md` on 40/7 for the throne holds here as well:** in the classical tradition two positions are transmitted — accepting the wording as it stands and committing its modality to God (**tafwīḍ**), or interpreting it in the sense of watchful care (**taʾwīl**). **I transmit both and do not impose a preference.**

**And the distribution of the root in the sūra is a datum of the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| **39** | *Wa li-**tuṣnaʿa** ʿalā ʿaynī* | **Passive** — Moses is reared |
| **41** | *Wa'**ṣṭanaʿtuka** li-nafsī* | **God** — "I have made you for Myself" |
| **69** | *Talqaf mā **ṣanaʿū**, innamā **ṣanaʿū** kaydu sāḥir* | **The sorcerers** — what they made |

**The same root three times: twice the work made lasts, once it is swallowed.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this distribution:** the sūra tells Moses as a thing "made", and names the thing the other side "made" with the same root and does away with it. **Two *ṣunʿ*s are set against one another.**

## 20/40-41 — إِذْ تَمْشِىٓ أُخْتُكَ فَتَقُولُ هَلْ أَدُلُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ مَن يَكْفُلُهُۥ فَرَجَعْنَٰكَ إِلَىٰٓ أُمِّكَ كَىْ تَقَرَّ عَيْنُهَا وَلَا تَحْزَنَ وَقَتَلْتَ نَفْسًا فَنَجَّيْنَٰكَ مِنَ ٱلْغَمِّ وَفَتَنَّٰكَ فُتُونًا فَلَبِثْتَ سِنِينَ فِىٓ أَهْلِ مَدْيَنَ ثُمَّ جِئْتَ عَلَىٰ قَدَرٍ يَٰمُوسَىٰ · وَٱصْطَنَعْتُكَ لِنَفْسِى

*Idh tamshī ukhtuka fa-taqūlu hal adullukum ʿalā man yakfuluh, fa-rajaʿnāka ilā ummika kay taqarra ʿaynuhā wa lā taḥzan, wa qatalta nafsan fa-najjaynāka mina'l-ghammi wa fatannāka futūnā, fa-labithta sinīna fī ahli Madyana thumma jiʾta ʿalā qadarin yā Mūsā · Wa'ṣṭanaʿtuka li-nafsī*

"'**When your sister was walking and saying, "Shall I show you someone who will take charge of him?" So We returned you to your mother, that her eye might be cooled and she might not grieve. And you killed a man; We delivered you from that distress and tried you with many a trial. You stayed years among the people of Midian; then you came at an appointed measure, O Moses. · And I made you for Myself.**'"

### A whole life in one verse

**The verse gives in a single verse what `028-kasas.md` tells in eighteen. The comparison can be verified from the text:**

| Event | al-Qaṣaṣ | **Ṭāhā 20/40** |
|---|---|---|
| The sister's watching | 28/11-12 — two verses | ***Idh tamshī ukhtuka fa-taqūlu…*** |
| The return to the mother | 28/13 — one verse | ***Fa-rajaʿnāka ilā ummika*** |
| The killing and the flight | 28/15-21 — seven verses | ***Wa qatalta nafsan fa-najjaynāka mina'l-ghamm*** |
| The years in Midian | 28/22-28 — seven verses | ***Fa-labithta sinīna fī ahli Madyan*** |
| The return | 28/29 | ***Thumma jiʾta ʿalā qadarin yā Mūsā*** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above:** al-Qaṣaṣ **tells** the story, Ṭāhā **reminds** of it. **And a reminder is shorter than a telling** — because the one addressed has already lived it. **The difference in length between the two sūras arises from the difference in addressee.**

### كَىْ تَقَرَّ عَيْنُهَا وَلَا تَحْزَنَ

**The idiom *qarra ʿaynan* was treated at `025-furkan.md` on 25/74** — the picture in the root: **the eye's resting on a place and not straying, its settling where it is.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the pair is worth recording: *taqarra ʿaynuhā* and *lā taḥzan*.** **At `028-kasas.md` on 28/7 the two prohibitions given to the mother were *wa lā takhāfī wa lā taḥzanī***, and it was recorded there: **khawf looks to the future, ḥuzn to the past.** **Here only *ḥuzn* is named** — because what was to be feared is past, and what remains is only the pain of the past. **This is a consistency between the two sūras that can be verified.**

### وَقَتَلْتَ نَفْسًا — a telling that does not cover over

**The sentence must be recorded: the event is recalled to the one addressed not in his own words but in the second person, and it is not covered over.**

**The same event was treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/14-21, and it was recorded there: the text does not cover over a grave event in a prophet's life, and it has him make the assessment himself** (*hādhā min ʿamali'sh-shayṭān*, *innī ẓalamtu nafsī*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**What is peculiar to Ṭāhā is that the event is named in the middle of a list of favours.** The sentence runs thus: we returned you to your mother — **you killed a man** — we delivered you from distress — we tried you — you stayed in Midian — you came.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the ordering of the sentence:** the fault is **not lifted out from among** the favours. **And the verb that follows is a verb of rescue:** *fa-najjaynāka mina'l-ghamm*.

**ٱلْغَمّ** — root **غ-م-م**: **to cover, to overspread.** *Ghamām* — cloud (what covers the sky). ***Ghamm* is the distress that covers a person over** — that is, what **narrows** the breast. **The picture in the word is the exact opposite of the request for *sharḥ* (opening) in the twenty-fifth verse.** I record this as an observation.

### وَفَتَنَّٰكَ فُتُونًا

The concrete sense of the root **ف-ت-ن** is **to put metal into the fire and separate the pure from the dross.** **The root was treated in many places in the series** (`029-ankebut.md` on 29/2-3, `085-buruc.md`, `037-saffat.md`) **and is not repeated here.**

**What belongs here is the pattern of the verbal noun: *futūn*.** This is explained either as the plural of *fitna* or as an intensive verbal noun; **on either explanation the sense comes out as "testing one by one, again and again".** **I render it as "We tried you with many a trial".**

**And the word *fitna* will occur four times in this sūra** (40, 85, 90, 131) **and each of the four names a distinct testing:** the life of Moses, the trial brought by the Sāmirī, Aaron's diagnosis (*innamā futintum bih*), and the trial of worldly goods (*li-naftinahum fīh*). **This distribution can be verified from the text and is another axis of the sūra.**

### ثُمَّ جِئْتَ عَلَىٰ قَدَرٍ

**"You came at an appointed measure."** The root **ق-د-ر**: **to measure, to set a measure to something.** *Qadar* — a measure set, an amount apportioned.

**Different explanations of the sense of the expression are transmitted:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **At the time decreed** — you came at the appointed hour |
| 2 | **In conformity with a measure** — in a state prepared for the mission |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **And both agree with where the verse stands:** the sentence comes after the parts of a life have been enumerated.

### وَٱصْطَنَعْتُكَ لِنَفْسِى

**The root ص-ن-ع, Form VIII: *iṣṭanaʿa* — "to make with care for oneself, to pick out and prepare".**

**A point of word order must be recorded: *li-nafsī* — "for Myself".** **In the thirty-ninth verse the verb was passive (*tuṣnaʿa*); here the agent is explicitly the first person singular.**

| Verse | Pattern | Agent |
|---|---|---|
| **39** | *Li-**tuṣnaʿa** ʿalā ʿaynī* | **Not stated** — passive |
| **41** | *Wa'**ṣṭanaʿtuka** li-nafsī* | **Explicit** — "I" |

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the agent kept unnamed at the beginning of the story is named openly at its end. **And this agrees with the order of the verses: 39 tells of the childhood, 41 of the commissioning.**

## 20/42-44 — ٱذْهَبْ أَنتَ وَأَخُوكَ بِـَٔايَٰتِى وَلَا تَنِيَا فِى ذِكْرِى · ٱذْهَبَآ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · فَقُولَا لَهُۥ قَوْلًا لَّيِّنًا لَّعَلَّهُۥ يَتَذَكَّرُ أَوْ يَخْشَىٰ

*Idhhab anta wa akhūka bi-āyātī wa lā taniyā fī dhikrī · Idhhabā ilā Firʿawna innahu ṭaghā · Fa-qūlā lahu qawlan layyinan laʿallahu yatadhakkaru aw yakhshā*

"'**Go, you and your brother, with My signs, and do not slacken in remembering Me. · Go, the two of you, to Pharaoh; he has transgressed. · Speak to him a gentle word; perhaps he will take heed or will fear.**'"

### وَلَا تَنِيَا — not to slacken

The root **و-ن-ي** — **occurs here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to weaken, to slacken, to slow and come to a halt in a work.** *Wanā fi'l-amr* — he went at the matter slowly. **The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**And the object of the prohibition must be recorded: *fī dhikrī* — "in remembering Me".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the object of the prohibition:** the measure given to the two messengers is not **not to slacken in the commission** — it is **not to slacken in remembrance.** **That is, what is required to be continuous is not the work but that to which the work is attached.**

**And this overlaps with the ground of the request in the twenty-ninth verse** (*wa nadhkuraka kathīrā*). **What Moses gave as his ground is exactly what is commanded of him.** This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra.

## 20/44 — فَقُولَا لَهُۥ قَوْلًا لَّيِّنًا لَّعَلَّهُۥ يَتَذَكَّرُ أَوْ يَخْشَىٰ

**This verse is among the most discussed sentences of the sūra and deserves separate treatment.**

### Who is the addressee of the command

**The datum of the text must be set down first: the addressee of this command is the one described in the forty-third verse as *innahu ṭaghā* (he has transgressed).**

**That is, two consecutive verses say this:**

| Verse | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 43 | *Innahu **ṭaghā*** | **Diagnosis** — the state of the addressee |
| 44 | *Fa-qūlā lahu qawlan **layyinan*** | **Method** — how he is to be spoken to |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses:** **the diagnosis does not change the method.** The gentle word is commanded **after** it has been said that the other side is a transgressor. **That is, gentleness is not a reward contingent on the addressee's deserving it — it is a measure belonging to the side of the speaker.**

### لَيِّن — the concrete sense of the root

The root **ل-ي-ن**: **softness, pliancy; the opposite of hardness.** **The root was treated at `034-sebe.md` on 34/10 (*wa alannā lahu'l-ḥadīd* — We softened iron for him), at `039-zumer.md` on 39/23 (*thumma talīnu julūduhum* — then their skins soften) and at `059-hasr.md`.** **I rely on those treatments.**

**And the use here is worth recording: the same root is applied to iron in Sabaʾ, to skin in al-Zumar, and here to speech.** **The common picture: what is hard becoming pliant.**

**A point of word order:** *qawlan layyinan* — **an object of cognate origin.** That is, not "speak gently" but **"speak a gentle word".** **The measure is set not on the manner but on the speech itself.**

### The other links of the same line

**This command is the hardest instance of a line treated in the series. The same line was treated at `041-fussilet.md` on 41/34 and at `029-ankebut.md` on 29/46, and a third link was recorded at `028-kasas.md` on 28/54.** **I rely on those treatments and complete the table with a fourth link:**

| Place | Expression | Domain | Addressee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/34 | *Idfaʿ billatī hiya aḥsan* — د-ف-ع | **Enmity** | Those who show enmity |
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/46 | *Lā tujādilū illā billatī hiya aḥsan* | **Argument** | The People of the Book |
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/54 | *Yadraʾūna bi'l-ḥasanati's-sayyiʾa* — د-ر-أ | **Evil** | General |
| **Ṭāhā 20/44** | *Qawlan **layyinan*** | **Conveying the message** | **The one described as *ṭaghā*** |

**Four sūras, four different words, one method.** **And the one in Ṭāhā is set apart from the others in one respect, and this difference can be verified from the text:** in the others the addressee is a general or unnamed party; **here the addressee is a man whose end, in this same sūra, is drowning.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse does not give the gentle word as **a guarantee of the outcome.** **Indeed the gentle word was spoken and Pharaoh did not believe.** **That is, the command is bound not to the result the other side will reach but to the road the speaker will keep.**

### لَّعَلَّهُۥ يَتَذَكَّرُ أَوْ يَخْشَىٰ

**The clause of purpose is built with *laʿalla* — that is, the pattern of hope.** **It was recorded at `032-secde.md` on 32/3: in a clause of purpose ending with *laʿalla* the outcome is not guaranteed.** I rely on that treatment.

**And two verbs are named: *yatadhakkaru* (he takes heed) and *yakhshā* (he fears).**

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the third verse of the sūra:** there the work of the Book was described as *tadhkiratan **li-man yakhshā*** — **the same two roots, in the same order.**

| Verse | Expression | For whom |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *Illā **tadhkiratan** li-man **yakhshā*** | **The definition of the Book** |
| **44** | *Laʿallahu **yatadhakkaru** aw **yakhshā*** | **What is hoped for from Pharaoh** |

**That is, the third verse of the sūra said to whom the Book would be of use; in the forty-fourth that door is held open for the most transgressing character in the sūra as well.** **This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as one of the most striking links in it.**

### What this says to the present

**What this verse says to the present is not a piece of advice about manner; it is an ordering.**

**The order the text builds is this: first the diagnosis (he has transgressed), then the method (speak gently), then the hope (perhaps).** **The diagnosis does not determine the method.** The judgement passed on what a person is does not change how he is to be spoken to.

**And there is a second thing:** the addressee of the command is **two people**, and both are bound to the same measure. **That is, the measure has not been left to the difference in temperament** — it is set down as a method that Moses and Aaron alike are to keep.

## 20/45-48 — قَالَا رَبَّنَآ إِنَّنَا نَخَافُ أَن يَفْرُطَ عَلَيْنَآ أَوْ أَن يَطْغَىٰ · قَالَ لَا تَخَافَآ إِنَّنِى مَعَكُمَآ أَسْمَعُ وَأَرَىٰ

*Qālā rabbanā innanā nakhāfu an yafruṭa ʿalaynā aw an yaṭghā · Qāla lā takhāfā innanī maʿakumā asmaʿu wa arā · Fa'tiyāhu fa-qūlā innā rasūlā rabbika fa-arsil maʿanā banī isrāʾīla wa lā tuʿadhdhibhum, qad jiʾnāka bi-āyatin min rabbik, wa's-salāmu ʿalā mani'ttabaʿa'l-hudā · Innā qad ūḥiya ilaynā anna'l-ʿadhāba ʿalā man kadhdhaba wa tawallā*

"They said: '**Our Lord! We fear that he will rush upon us or that he will grow more transgressing still.**' · '**Do not fear; I am with you both, I hear and I see**,' He said. · '**Go to him and say: We are two messengers of your Lord. Send the Children of Israel with us and do not torment them. We have come to you with a sign from your Lord. Peace be upon whoever follows the guidance. · It has been revealed to us that the punishment is upon whoever denies and turns away.**'"

### يَفْرُطَ — the two directions of the fear

The root **ف-ر-ط** — **is analysed here for the first time in the series.** The concrete sense the lexicographers give: **to get out in front of a thing, to dart ahead.** *Fāriṭ* — the man who goes ahead of the caravan to find water. From this, two branches:

| Branch | Sense |
|---|---|
| ***Ifrāṭ*** | **Going past the measure and out in front** — excess |
| ***Tafrīṭ*** | **Leaving behind, neglecting** |

**In the verse *yafruṭa* — "to go to excess, to come upon us in a rush".**

**And two fears are named, and they are distinct:**

| Fear | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *An **yafruṭa** ʿalaynā* | **That he will strike at once** — before the speech is finished |
| 2 | *Aw an **yaṭghā*** | **That he will increase his transgression** — after the speech |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the time of the two verbs:** the first fears the **cutting short** of the message, the second its **backfiring.** **That is, the fear is expressed not as fear for personal safety but as concern for the outcome of the work.**

**And the second verb is worth recording: *yaṭghā* — the same root as *ṭaghā* in the twenty-fourth and forty-third verses.** **That is, the two messengers fear that the addressee will transgress still further.**

### إِنَّنِى مَعَكُمَآ أَسْمَعُ وَأَرَىٰ

**The structure of the answer must be recorded: a prohibition, an announcement, two verbs.**

**A point of word order: *maʿakumā* — dual.** But the verbs *asmaʿu* and *arā* are **first person singular.** **That is, the one who says "I am with you" and the one who says "I hear, I see" are the same subject.**

**And at `026-suara.md` on 26/15 the same reassurance was given in another pattern:** *innā maʿakum **mustamiʿūn***. **The two verses can be set side by side:**

| Place | Expression | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/15 | *Innā maʿakum **mustamiʿūn*** | **Plural of majesty** + active participle |
| **Ṭāhā 20/46** | *Innanī maʿakumā **asmaʿu wa arā*** | **Singular** + two imperfect verbs |

**The difference is worth recording:** in al-Shuʿarāʾ only hearing, **in Ṭāhā hearing and seeing.** **And in Ṭāhā the verbs are imperfect** — that is, a work in progress.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of the two verbs:** the fear of the two messengers was about **speech** and about **action** (will the word be cut short, will he attack). **The answer too gives two senses: the one that hears the word and the one that sees the action.** **That is, the reassurance answers the two items of the fear one by one.**

### وَٱلسَّلَٰمُ عَلَىٰ مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ

**The close of the speech they are commanded to say must be recorded, and it is striking: the peace is given not to the addressee but to an attribute.**

**The sentence does not say *al-salāmu ʿalayka* (peace be upon you); it says *ʿalā mani'ttabaʿa'l-hudā* — "upon whoever follows the guidance".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the object of the preposition:** the peace is neither given nor withheld — **it is made conditional.** **And the condition stands open before the addressee.**

**The line treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/55, `025-furkan.md` on 25/63 and `043-zuhruf.md` on 43/89 connects here** — there too a parting was sealed with a greeting of peace. **The difference in Ṭāhā: the peace seals not a parting but the end of an invitation.**

## 20/49-52 — قَالَ فَمَن رَّبُّكُمَا يَٰمُوسَىٰ · قَالَ رَبُّنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَعْطَىٰ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلْقَهُۥ ثُمَّ هَدَىٰ · قَالَ فَمَا بَالُ ٱلْقُرُونِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · قَالَ عِلْمُهَا عِندَ رَبِّى فِى كِتَٰبٍ لَّا يَضِلُّ رَبِّى وَلَا يَنسَى

*Qāla fa-man rabbukumā yā Mūsā · Qāla rabbuna'lladhī aʿṭā kulla shayʾin khalqahu thumma hadā · Qāla fa-mā bālu'l-qurūni'l-ūlā · Qāla ʿilmuhā ʿinda rabbī fī kitāb, lā yaḍillu rabbī wa lā yansā*

"He said: '**Who then is your Lord, O Moses?**' · He said: '**Our Lord is the One who gave to each thing its creation and then guided.**' · '**What then of the former generations?**' he said. · '**Knowledge of them is with my Lord in a book. My Lord neither errs nor forgets**,' he said."

### فَمَن — the difference in particle from al-Shuʿarāʾ

**The same scene was treated at `026-suara.md` on 26/23-28, and the interrogative particle was discussed there in detail.** In al-Shuʿarāʾ the question was: ***wa mā rabbu'l-ʿālamīn*** — and it was recorded there: **in Arabic *mā* asks after the quiddity (the kind), *man* after the identity; rational beings are asked after with *man*.** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat the discussion of the particle.**

**What belongs here is the difference between the two sūras, and this is a datum of the text:**

| | **Ṭāhā 20/49** | al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/23 |
|---|---|---|
| Particle | ***مَن*** — a question of identity | ***مَا*** — a question of quiddity |
| Construction | *Rabbu**kumā*** — "your Lord" | *Rabbu'l-**ʿālamīn*** — "the Lord of all the worlds" |
| Address | ***Yā Mūsā*** — the name is spoken | The name is not spoken |
| Answer | **One answer**, in three parts | **Three separate answers** |

**I record this as an observation, not as a contradiction between the two tellings:** al-Shuʿarāʾ has him ask after **the concept**, Ṭāhā after **the belonging** — *"who is your Lord"*. **And in Ṭāhā the name appears in the address:** *yā Mūsā.* **The question is put to two men (*rabbukumā*) and directed at one (*yā Mūsā*).**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the mismatch of the two pronouns:** the question is dual, the address singular. **Pharaoh takes one of the two messengers as his interlocutor.**

### أَعْطَىٰ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلْقَهُۥ ثُمَّ هَدَىٰ

**This is one of the densest short sentences in the Qurʾān, and it differs in construction from the three answers in al-Shuʿarāʾ.**

**The three elements of the sentence must be recorded:**

| Element | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Aʿṭā* | **Giving** — a verb of bestowal |
| 2 | *Kulla shayʾin **khalqahu*** | **To each thing its own creation** |
| 3 | *Thumma **hadā*** | **Then guiding** |

**The parsing of the construction has been disputed, and the disagreement must not be concealed:**

| Reading | What *aʿṭā kulla shayʾin khalqahu* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **"He gave to each thing its own creation"** — *kulla shayʾin* the first object, *khalqahu* the second |
| 2 | **"He gave each thing its creation"** — that is, He gave each created thing the form proper to it |
| 3 | **"He gave to everything He created [what it needed]"** — *khalqahu* a fronted construct |

**The explanations are close to one another and bring the sense out largely at the same place.** **I do not impose a preference.**

**ثُمَّ هَدَىٰ — and the weight of the sentence lies here.**

**The particle *thumma* in Arabic conveys sequence and an interval.** That is, the guiding comes not **within** the creation but **after** it.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle itself:** the sentence counts two separate gifts — **bringing into being** and **directing.** **To give a thing existence is not enough to show it what to do;** the verse names the second separately.

**And the object of the verb *hadā* is not stated.** Not "He guided them" or "He guided man" — but **absolute: *hadā*.** The scope is left open.

**I enter a note about the connection to science, and it is per the method (`USLUP.md`):** links have been drawn from this verse to the instinctive behaviour of living things, to migration routes, or to genetic information. **No such link is drawn in this commentary.** The verse is not a proposition of biology but a **declaration of an act**; and what it says ("no being knows of itself what it is to do; it is shown") is already sufficient on its own. **I add this as an observation, not as a claim of a miracle:** the distinction the sentence draws — **existence and instruction** — is a distinction that can also be thought about today for any ordered system, living or not; but the verse does not claim this, and neither do I.

**And a link within the sūra must be recorded:** the verb *hadā* will return in the seventy-ninth verse **negated, of Pharaoh**: *wa aḍalla Firʿawnu qawmahu **wa mā hadā*** — "he led astray and did not guide".

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **50** | *Thumma **hadā*** | **God** — the last word of the answer |
| **79** | *Wa mā **hadā*** | **Pharaoh** — the last word of the block |

**The same verb, the same form, one affirmed and one negated; one the answer to a question, the other the verdict on the story.** **This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as one of its tightest links.**

### فَمَا بَالُ ٱلْقُرُونِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ — the work of the question

***Bāl*: state, condition, outcome.** The question: **"What then will become of the past generations?"**

**With what aim the question was asked has been disputed:**

| Reading | What the question does |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Changing the subject** — not answering the answer but pulling it elsewhere |
| 2 | **An objection** — "they lived without knowing this Lord; what will become of them?" |
| 3 | **Mockery** — asking about something unknowable to corner the messenger |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But I record one indication, not as a preference:** **at `026-suara.md` on 26/25 it was treated how, in al-Shuʿarāʾ's telling of the same scene, Pharaoh does not answer the answer but turns to the listeners** (*alā tastamiʿūn*), **and it was recorded there: "Pharaoh does not refute the answer — he reacts to it."** **The question in Ṭāhā does the same work:** the answer given is not disputed, **another subject is opened.** **Two sūras record the same behaviour in two different forms.**

### لَّا يَضِلُّ رَبِّى وَلَا يَنسَى

**The answer negates two verbs, and they are distinct:**

| Verb | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Yaḍillu* | ض-ل-ل | **To err, to go to the wrong place** — the inaccuracy of knowledge |
| *Yansā* | ن-س-ي | **To forget** — the loss of knowledge |

**I record this as an observation:** one is **error**, the other **loss**. **Both concern knowledge, but they are different failures.**

**And the root ن-س-ي must be recorded: this is one of the hardest-working roots in the sūra, and it occurs in four places:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **52** | *Lā yaḍillu rabbī wa lā **yansā*** | **God** — negated |
| **88** | *Fa-**nasiya*** | **In the matter of the calf** |
| **115** | *Fa-**nasiya** wa lam najid lahu ʿazmā* | **Adam** |
| **126** | *Fa-**nasītahā** wa kadhālika'l-yawma **tunsā*** | **The one who turns away** — and his recompense |

**That is, the sūra first names forgetting by negating it of God; then three times by establishing it of the human being.** This is a distribution that can be verified from the text, and I record this as my own reading: **the sūra's description of the human being is built precisely on the opposition of these two verbs.**

**And the root ض-ل-ل works in the same way:** *lā yaḍillu rabbī* (52) — *wa aḍalla Firʿawnu qawmah* (79) — *fa-lā yaḍillu wa lā yashqā* (123). **Both roots are part of the web the sūra weaves from beginning to end.**

## 20/53-55 — ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلْأَرْضَ مَهْدًا وَسَلَكَ لَكُمْ فِيهَا سُبُلًا وَأَنزَلَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءً فَأَخْرَجْنَا بِهِۦٓ أَزْوَٰجًا مِّن نَّبَاتٍ شَتَّىٰ · كُلُوا۟ وَٱرْعَوْا۟ أَنْعَٰمَكُمْ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتٍ لِّأُو۟لِى ٱلنُّهَىٰ · مِنْهَا خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمْ وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ

*Alladhī jaʿala lakumu'l-arḍa mahdan wa salaka lakum fīhā subulan wa anzala mina's-samāʾi māʾan fa-akhrajnā bihi azwājan min nabātin shattā · Kulū war-ʿaw anʿāmakum, inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-uli'n-nuhā · Minhā khalaqnākum wa fīhā nuʿīdukum wa minhā nukhrijukum tāratan ukhrā*

"**He who made the earth a cradle for you, opened roads for you in it and sent down water from the sky. With it We brought forth pairs of diverse plants. · Eat and pasture your cattle. In that there are signs for those who possess restraint. · From it We created you, to it We shall return you, and from it We shall bring you out one more time.**"

### The answer continues

**A point of word order must be recorded: the fifty-third verse is the continuation of the answer given in the fiftieth.** The relative pronoun *alladhī* attaches to *rabbuna'lladhī aʿṭā*. **That is, Pharaoh's second question (51) has come in between, and the answer nonetheless goes on.**

**And a shift of address must be recorded:** *jaʿala*, *salaka*, *anzala* — **third person**; then suddenly *fa-**akhrajnā*** — **first person plural.** **The speaking party changes in the middle of the sentence.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure, and this pattern has been treated in many places in the series** (`035-fatir.md` on 35/9, `036-yasin.md`): **the telling passes over to the speaker at the most concrete verb.**

**مَهْدًا** — root **م-ه-د**: **to level and spread out, to prepare a bed.** *Mahd* — **a cradle.** **The choice of the word is worth recording:** a cradle is not merely a flat surface — it is a place **prepared for the one inside it, rocking, protective.**

**نُهَى** — root **ن-ه-ي**: **to prevent, to hold back.** ***Ulu'n-nuhā* — "those who possess intelligence", but the word itself means "the restrainers".**

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the sense of the root:** in Arabic, intelligence is named as **what holds a person back from what is harmful.** The same logic is present in the root *ʿaql* (the hobbling of a camel). **That is, intelligence is named not as something that accumulates knowledge — but as something that sets a boundary.**

**The word occurs twice in the Qurʾān, and both are in this sūra: 54 and 128.** **These two verses of the sūra end with the same expression:** *inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-uli'n-nuhā.* **One after the proofs of creation, the other after the remains of generations destroyed.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

### مِنْهَا خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمْ وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ

**Three stages, three prepositions, and the point of word order lies here:**

| Stage | Preposition | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| Creating | ***min*** (from) | *Minhā khalaqnākum* |
| Returning | ***fī*** (into) | *Wa **fīhā** nuʿīdukum* |
| Bringing out | ***min*** (from) | *Wa minhā nukhrijukum* |

**The middle preposition changes: the entry with *fī*, the exits with *min*.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the sentence names the earth three times and uses it **twice as a source and once as a vessel.** **And the expression *tāratan ukhrā* (one more time) counts the resurrection as of the same kind as the first creation.**

**The same proof was treated at `035-fatir.md` on 35/9 and at `050-kaf.md` on 50/11** (*kadhālika'n-nushūr* / *kadhālika'l-khurūj*): **the passage from a seen event to an unseen one.** **I rely on those treatments.**

## 20/56-59 — وَلَقَدْ أَرَيْنَٰهُ ءَايَٰتِنَا كُلَّهَا فَكَذَّبَ وَأَبَىٰ · قَالَ أَجِئْتَنَا لِتُخْرِجَنَا مِنْ أَرْضِنَا بِسِحْرِكَ يَٰمُوسَىٰ … قَالَ مَوْعِدُكُمْ يَوْمُ ٱلزِّينَةِ وَأَن يُحْشَرَ ٱلنَّاسُ ضُحًى

*Wa laqad araynāhu āyātinā kullahā fa-kadhdhaba wa abā · Qāla a-jiʾtanā li-tukhrijanā min arḍinā bi-siḥrika yā Mūsā · Fa-la-naʾtiyannaka bi-siḥrin mithlihi faj-ʿal baynanā wa baynaka mawʿidan lā nukhlifuhu naḥnu wa lā anta makānan suwā · Qāla mawʿidukum yawmu'z-zīnati wa an yuḥshara'n-nāsu ḍuḥā*

"**Indeed We showed him all Our signs; he denied and refused. · He said: 'Have you come to drive us out of our land with your sorcery, O Moses? · Then we shall surely bring you sorcery like it. Set a meeting between us and you which neither we nor you shall break — in an even place.' · 'Your meeting is the day of the festival, at the hour when the people gather in the forenoon,' he said.**"

### فَكَذَّبَ وَأَبَىٰ — and the act of Iblīs

**Two verbs are named, and the second must be recorded: *abā* — he refused, would not come near, resisted.**

**This is the verb used of Iblīs at `002-bakara.md` on 2/34** and it was treated there: *abā wa'stakbara* — **"*abā* is the act itself, *istakbara* the ground of the act."** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the same verb will come back of Iblīs in the hundred and sixteenth verse of this sūra:** *fa-sajadū illā Iblīsa **abā***.

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **56** | *Fa-kadhdhaba **wa abā*** | **Pharaoh** |
| **116** | *Illā Iblīsa **abā*** | **Iblīs** |

**This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** the sūra tells two stories — one the refusal of a king, the other the refusal of a creature — **and names both with a single verb.** **And in neither is the ground of the refusal ignorance:** Pharaoh has seen *all* the signs (*āyātinā kullahā*), and Iblīs received the command directly.

**The sentence recorded at al-Baqara holds here too, and rather than repeat it I name it:** *"unbelief is not a lack of knowledge; it is the covering over of what is known."*

### يَوْمُ ٱلزِّينَةِ — the day of the meeting

**Moses himself sets the time proposed, and the day he chooses is worth recording: *yawmu'z-zīna*, and the hour *ḍuḥā* (the forenoon).**

**I record this as an observation:** the meeting is set not **in a hidden place with few witnesses** but **on a festival day when the people gather, and at the brightest hour of the day.** **And the party who made the proposal had asked for "an even place" (*makānan suwā*)** — that is, a place giving neither of the two an advantage.

**سُوًى** — root **س-و-ي**: equivalence, evenness. ***Suwā* — what is equal for both sides.** **The root was treated at `082-infitar.md` on 82/7 (*fa-sawwāk*) and at `091-sems.md`;** I rely on those treatments.

**The root ز-ي-ن occurs twice in the sūra, and both are worth recording:** *yawmu'z-**zīna*** (59) and *awzāran min **zīnati**'l-qawm* (87 — the ornaments from which the calf was made). **The same root, one the name of a festival, the other the material of an idol.** This is a distribution that can be verified from the text.

## 20/60-64 — فَتَوَلَّىٰ فِرْعَوْنُ فَجَمَعَ كَيْدَهُۥ ثُمَّ أَتَىٰ · قَالَ لَهُم مُّوسَىٰ وَيْلَكُمْ لَا تَفْتَرُوا۟ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ كَذِبًا فَيُسْحِتَكُم بِعَذَابٍ وَقَدْ خَابَ مَنِ ٱفْتَرَىٰ · فَتَنَٰزَعُوٓا۟ أَمْرَهُم بَيْنَهُمْ وَأَسَرُّوا۟ ٱلنَّجْوَىٰ

*Fa-tawallā Firʿawnu fa-jamaʿa kaydahu thumma atā · Qāla lahum Mūsā waylakum lā taftarū ʿala'llāhi kadhiban fa-yusḥitakum bi-ʿadhāb, wa qad khāba mani'ftarā · Fa-tanāzaʿū amrahum baynahum wa asarru'n-najwā · Qālū in hādhāni la-sāḥirāni yurīdāni an yukhrijākum min arḍikum bi-siḥrihimā wa yadhhabā bi-ṭarīqatikumu'l-muthlā · Fa-ajmiʿū kaydakum thumma'tū ṣaffā, wa qad aflaḥa'l-yawma mani'stalā*

"**Pharaoh turned away, gathered his scheme, and then came. · Moses said to them: 'Woe to you! Do not invent a lie against God, or He will wipe you out with a punishment. Whoever has invented has surely lost.' · Then they disputed their affair among themselves and spoke in secret. · They said: 'These two are certainly sorcerers; they want to drive you out of your land with their sorcery and do away with your exemplary way. · So gather your scheme and then come in ranks. Whoever prevails today has surely prospered.**'"

### The word spoken to the sorcerers

**A point of word order must be recorded: in the sixty-first verse the one Moses addresses is not Pharaoh but *lahum* — that is, the sorcerers who have been brought forward.**

**And what is said is not a challenge but a warning:** *lā taftarū ʿala'llāhi kadhiban.*

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the addressee:** Moses does not tell the men set against him **that they ought to lose.** **He tells them what the work they are doing is.** **And this agrees with the measure of the forty-fourth verse:** the other side is given a sentence in which it can see its own condition.

**And the outcome has its counterpart in the text as a datum:** in the sixty-first verse it says *wa qad **khāba** mani'ftarā*; **in the seventieth those same sorcerers will fall down in prostration.** **That is, the warning has found its addressee.**

**يُسْحِت** — root **س-ح-ت**: **to scrape away at the root, to destroy utterly.** From the same root *suḥt* — earnings whose blessing has been scraped away, unlawful gain (al-Māʾida 5/42). **The picture in the word is an erasing from the bottom, not from the surface.**

**فَتَنَٰزَعُوٓا۟ أَمْرَهُم بَيْنَهُمْ وَأَسَرُّوا۟ ٱلنَّجْوَىٰ** — "**they disputed their affair among themselves and kept their whispering secret.**"

**The root of *najwā* was treated in detail at `058-mucadele.md`;** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is an observation about the structure:** *asarrū* (they kept secret) and *najwā* (whispering) stand in the same sentence. **Whispering is secret already; the verse records a further layer of secrecy.** **And the answer to this had been given in advance in the seventh verse of the sūra:** *fa-innahu yaʿlamu's-sirra wa akhfā* — **"He knows the secret and what is more hidden than it".**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *Yaʿlamu's-sirra **wa akhfā*** | **The principle** — a double secrecy is known |
| **62** | *Wa **asarrū**'n-**najwā*** | **The event** — a double secrecy is enacted |

**This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** the abstract sentence at the sūra's opening finds its counterpart as a concrete scene inside the story.

**طَرِيقَتِكُمُ ٱلْمُثْلَىٰ** — "**your exemplary way**". ***Muthlā* is the feminine of *amthal*: "most exemplary, most excellent".** **Worth recording:** the objection is framed not as a religious claim but as **the preservation of an order and a way of life.**

**The same logic was treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/57** (*in nattabiʿi'l-hudā maʿaka nutakhaṭṭaf min arḍinā*), and it was recorded there: **the objection is built not on belief but on a concern for security.** **I rely on that treatment.** **The difference in Ṭāhā: the object of the concern is not the land but the *ṭarīqa* — the order of living.**

## 20/65-70 — قَالُوا۟ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ إِمَّآ أَن تُلْقِىَ وَإِمَّآ أَن نَّكُونَ أَوَّلَ مَنْ أَلْقَىٰ · قَالَ بَلْ أَلْقُوا۟ فَإِذَا حِبَالُهُمْ وَعِصِيُّهُمْ يُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ مِن سِحْرِهِمْ أَنَّهَا تَسْعَىٰ · فَأَوْجَسَ فِى نَفْسِهِۦ خِيفَةً مُّوسَىٰ · قُلْنَا لَا تَخَفْ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · وَأَلْقِ مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ تَلْقَفْ مَا صَنَعُوٓا۟ إِنَّمَا صَنَعُوا۟ كَيْدُ سَٰحِرٍ وَلَا يُفْلِحُ ٱلسَّاحِرُ حَيْثُ أَتَىٰ · فَأُلْقِىَ ٱلسَّحَرَةُ سُجَّدًا قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا بِرَبِّ هَٰرُونَ وَمُوسَىٰ

*Qālū yā Mūsā immā an tulqiya wa immā an nakūna awwala man alqā · Qāla bal alqū, fa-idhā ḥibāluhum wa ʿiṣiyyuhum yukhayyalu ilayhi min siḥrihim annahā tasʿā · Fa-awjasa fī nafsihi khīfatan Mūsā · Qulnā lā takhaf innaka anta'l-aʿlā · Wa alqi mā fī yamīnika talqaf mā ṣanaʿū, innamā ṣanaʿū kaydu sāḥir, wa lā yufliḥu's-sāḥiru ḥaythu atā · Fa-ulqiya's-saḥaratu sujjadan qālū āmannā bi-rabbi Hārūna wa Mūsā*

"They said: '**O Moses! Either you cast, or let us be the first to cast.**' · '**No, you cast**,' he said. And there were their ropes and their staffs, seeming to him from their sorcery to be moving swiftly. · **Moses sensed a fear within himself.** · '**Do not fear; you are the higher**,' We said. · '**Cast what is in your right hand; it will swallow what they have made. What they have made is only a sorcerer's trick. The sorcerer does not prosper, wherever he may come.**' · **At that the sorcerers were cast down in prostration. 'We believe in the Lord of Aaron and Moses,' they said.**"

### Comparison with al-Shuʿarāʾ — the same scene, two cuts

**This scene was treated in detail at `026-suara.md` on 26/43-49. What was recorded there:** that the root ل-ق-ي occurs six times in seven verses and that **at the sixth the voice turns passive** (*fa-ulqiya's-saḥaratu sājidīn* — "they were cast down"); the word ***talqafu*** (ل-ق-ف — to snatch up and swallow); the relation of the oath *bi-ʿizzati Firʿawn* to the name *al-ʿAzīz* in that sūra; and the expression *mā yaʾfikūn*. **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the difference between the two tellings, and the whole of it can be verified from the text:**

| | **Ṭāhā 20/65-70** | al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/43-48 |
|---|---|---|
| The proposal | ***From the sorcerers*** — *immā an tulqiya…* | **From Moses** — *alqū mā antum mulqūn* |
| Moses's answer | ***Bal alqū*** — "no, you cast" | — |
| The sorcerers' oath | **None** | *Bi-ʿizzati Firʿawn* — present |
| What appears | ***Yukhayyalu ilayhi … annahā tasʿā*** — so it seems to the eye | *Ḥibālahum wa ʿiṣiyyahum* — as objects |
| **Moses's fear** | ***Fa-awjasa fī nafsihi khīfatan*** — **present** | **None** |
| The reassurance | ***Lā takhaf innaka anta'l-aʿlā*** | **None** |
| The command to cast | ***Alqi mā fī yamīnika*** | *Fa-alqā Mūsā ʿaṣāh* — narration |
| A verdict on sorcery | ***Wa lā yufliḥu's-sāḥiru ḥaythu atā*** | **None** |
| The prostration | *Fa-**ulqiya**'s-saḥaratu **sujjadan*** | *Fa-**ulqiya**'s-saḥaratu **sājidīn*** |
| The declaration of faith | ***Rabbi Hārūna wa Mūsā*** | *Rabbi'l-ʿālamīn · Rabbi Mūsā wa Hārūn* |

**Four differences are worth recording.**

**One — only in Ṭāhā is Moses's fear named.** *Fa-**awjasa** fī nafsihi **khīfatan** Mūsā.*

The root **و-ج-س** — **occurs here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to sense a very faint sound or movement; for something to dawn within one.** *Wajs* — a sound barely made out. **The word conveys fear at the moment it becomes noticeable.**

**And a point of word order must be recorded:** *fī nafsihi* — **"within himself"**; *khīfatan* indefinite — **"a fear"**; and the subject (*Mūsā*) is left to the very **end** of the sentence.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these three things:** the verse records the fear **at its smallest, inward and passing.** **It is not a fear that shows outwardly.** **And the text does not conceal it** — the same honesty recorded at `019-meryem.md` on 19/23 and at `028-kasas.md` on 28/15-16. I rely on those treatments.

**Two — Ṭāhā names separately what appeared to the eye: *yukhayyalu ilayhi*.**

The root **خ-ي-ل**: **imagination, a thing's appearing to the eye otherwise than it is.** From the same root come *khayāl* (an image with no reality) and *mukhtāl* (one who **sees** himself as great). **The verb is passive: "so it seemed to him".**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not say that the ropes and staffs **really** moved. **What it says is how the appearance came to the eye.** **That is, sorcery is named in the verse not as a transformation of objects but as an operation upon perception.** This can be verified from the text's own word.

**Three — Ṭāhā gives a general verdict about sorcery:** *wa lā yufliḥu's-sāḥiru **ḥaythu atā*** — "the sorcerer does not prosper, wherever he may come". **This sentence is not in al-Shuʿarāʾ.**

**Four — the order of the names in the declaration of faith differs: in Ṭāhā *rabbi Hārūna wa Mūsā*, in al-Shuʿarāʾ *rabbi Mūsā wa Hārūn*.**

**I record this as a datum of the text and issue no ruling.** In the classical commentaries the difference in order is explained by the harmony of the verse-endings (Ṭāhā's endings close on an *-ā* sound), and other explanations of Aaron's being placed first are transmitted. **The explanation from the verse-endings is an observation that can be verified from the text:** all the verse-endings of the sūra close with *alif maqṣūra*, and *Mūsā* fits that pattern while *Hārūn* does not. **I give this as an observation about the language, not as a claim of a definite cause.**

### إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ

**The word of the reassurance must be recorded: *al-aʿlā* — "the higher".** **Root ع-ل-و.**

**And this word, as recorded at `028-kasas.md`, was the spine of Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ** (*inna Firʿawna ʿalā fi'l-arḍ* / *lā yurīdūna ʿuluwwan fi'l-arḍ*). **And the Qurʾān also records from Pharaoh's own mouth the words *anā rabbukumu'l-aʿlā*** (al-Nāziʿāt 79/24 — treated at `079-naziat.md`).

| Place | Of whom *aʿlā* is said | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| al-Nāziʿāt 79/24 | **Of Pharaoh** | **Himself** — a claim |
| **Ṭāhā 20/68** | **Of Moses** | **God** — a reassurance |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses use the same elative:** the same word, in one place claimed for oneself, in the other bestowed. **And there is a note in Ṭāhā's sentence: *innaka anta* — that is, the superiority is given not as an attribute belonging to him but as a position declared for that moment.**

### فَأُلْقِىَ ٱلسَّحَرَةُ سُجَّدًا

**This change of voice was treated at `026-suara.md` on 26/46, and the note recorded there holds here exactly as it stands:** *"fa-sajada's-saḥaratu ('they prostrated') could have been said; it was not. The passive voice conveys that the prostration was not a planned decision but an irresistible response."* **I rely on that treatment.**

**The only difference peculiar to Ṭāhā is the word of state: *sujjadan* (Ṭāhā) / *sājidīn* (al-Shuʿarāʾ).** ***Sujjadan* is the broken plural of *sājid*, and the lexicographers record that this pattern conveys intensity.** **I transmit this as an observation about the language.**

## 20/71 — قَالَ ءَامَنتُمْ لَهُۥ قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ

*Qāla āmantum lahu qabla an ādhana lakum, innahu la-kabīrukumu'lladhī ʿallamakumu's-siḥr, fa-la-uqaṭṭiʿanna aydiyakum wa arjulakum min khilāfin wa la-uṣallibannakum fī judhūʿi'n-nakhli wa la-taʿlamunna ayyunā ashaddu ʿadhāban wa abqā*

"He said: '**You have believed him before I gave you leave! He is surely your chief, the one who taught you sorcery. I shall cut off your hands and your feet on alternate sides, and I shall crucify you on the trunks of palm trees, and you shall certainly know which of us is more severe in punishment and more lasting!**'"

### What was treated at al-Shuʿarāʾ

**This sentence was treated in detail at `026-suara.md` on 26/49, and what was recorded there is not repeated here:**

- ***Qabla an ādhana lakum*** — **"the claim covers not being worshipped but the authority to decide who may believe in what."**
- ***Āmantum lahu*** — not *bihi* but *lahu*: **not "you believed in God" but "you put your trust in him"** — pulling the matter onto a person.
- ***Kabīrukumu'lladhī ʿallamakumu's-siḥr*** — explaining the event as a conspiracy; **and the inconsistency of the explanation within the text** (the sorcerers had only just been assembled, and Moses had not been in Egypt for years).
- ***Min khilāf*** — **on alternate sides**: the right hand with the left foot.
- **And a note of principle:** the verse is not laying down a penal ruling; **it is transmitting the words of a tyrant.** No legal ruling is derived.

**I rely on that treatment.**

### Two additions peculiar to Ṭāhā

**One — the place of the crucifixion is specified: *fī judhūʿi'n-nakhl* — "on the trunks of palm trees".** In al-Shuʿarāʾ there was only *la-uṣallibannakum*.

**Two — and this is the real difference: the threat ends with a sentence of contest.**

***Wa la-taʿlamunna ayyunā ashaddu ʿadhāban wa abqā*** — "**you shall know which of us is more severe in punishment and more lasting.**"

**A point of word order must be recorded: *ayyunā* — "which of us".** **That is, Pharaoh puts himself and the other side into the same sentence, as the two terms of the same measure.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the pronoun is plural:** the sentence sets up a **comparison** between two punishments. **That is, Pharaoh tacitly grants in his own sentence the existence of God's punishment and disputes only its degree.**

**And the word *abqā* occurs here for the first time.** **As was tabulated in the section on structure, this word occurs four times in the sūra, and the first is in this verse.** **In the next verse the sorcerers will answer with the same word.**

## 20/72-73 — قَالُوا۟ لَن نُّؤْثِرَكَ عَلَىٰ مَا جَآءَنَا مِنَ ٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ وَٱلَّذِى فَطَرَنَا فَٱقْضِ مَآ أَنتَ قَاضٍ إِنَّمَا تَقْضِى هَٰذِهِ ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا

*Qālū lan nuʾthiraka ʿalā mā jāʾanā mina'l-bayyināti wa'lladhī faṭaranā faqḍi mā anta qāḍ, innamā taqḍī hādhihi'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā · Innā āmannā bi-rabbinā li-yaghfira lanā khaṭāyānā wa mā akrahtanā ʿalayhi mina's-siḥr, wa'llāhu khayrun wa abqā*

"They said: '**We shall never prefer you to the clear proofs that have come to us, nor to the One who brought us into being. Decree what you will decree! You can only decree in this life of the world. · We have believed in our Lord, that He may forgive us our faults and the sorcery you compelled us to. God is better and more lasting.**'"

### The construction of the answer

**This is one of the densest answers in the sūra, and it is built in four steps:**

| Step | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Lan **nuʾthiraka** ʿalā mā jāʾanā* | **The language of preference** — the matter is put as a choice |
| 2 | *Faqḍi mā anta qāḍ* | **Yielding** — no objection to the decree |
| 3 | *Innamā **taqḍī** hādhihi'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā* | **The limit** — the extent of the decree |
| 4 | *Wa'llāhu khayrun **wa abqā*** | **The measure** — the comparison completed |

**I now record each step separately.**

### لَن نُّؤْثِرَكَ — the word of preference

The root **أ-ث-ر**: **a trace, the mark a thing leaves behind.** Form IV, *īthār*: **to put someone or something forward, to prefer** — and in Arabic *īthār* is used especially of **giving up one's own share to another** (treated at `059-hasr.md` on 59/9, *wa yuʾthirūna ʿalā anfusihim*).

**I record this as an observation:** the sorcerers name the matter not as **a dispute about belief** but as **a preference.** **And the particle *lan* conveys a definite negation for the future.**

**And two things are named as what will not be traded away:** *mā jāʾanā mina'l-bayyināt* (the proofs that have come) and *wa'lladhī faṭaranā* (the One who brought us into being). **That is, one is what they have seen, the other its source.**

The root **ف-ط-ر**: **to split a thing open, to bring it forth for the first time.** **The root was analysed in detail at `035-fatir.md` in connection with the name of that sūra;** I rely on that treatment. **The choice of the word is worth recording:** not *khalaqa* but *faṭara* — **that is, "the one who first split open, who first brought into being".**

### فَٱقْضِ مَآ أَنتَ قَاضٍ — the construction of the sentence

**This is the most striking sentence of the answer, and it carries three separate data of language.**

**One — the root ق-ض-ي occurs three times: *faqḍi* (imperative), *qāḍ* (active participle), *taqḍī* (imperfect).** **The root: to bring a matter to a definite end, to complete a decree.**

**Two — the imperative is used: *faqḍi* — "decree".** In Arabic this pattern here conveys not a command but **defiance (*taḥaddī*) or yielding**: telling the other side "do what you can".

**Three — the expression *mā anta qāḍ* must be recorded: "whatever it is you will decree".** **The sentence does not enumerate the content of the threat.** Pharaoh had spoken of cutting and of crucifying; **the answer does not take these up one by one, but puts them all inside a single indefinite expression.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these three points:** the answer does not **dispute** the threat — **it measures its extent.** And the measuring is done in the next sentence.

### إِنَّمَا تَقْضِى هَٰذِهِ ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا

***Innamā* — the particle of *ḥaṣr* (restriction): "only, nothing but".**

**The sentence does not deny Pharaoh's authority — it bounds it.** **And the boundary is neither geographical nor political: *hādhihi'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā* — "this life of the world".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle *innamā*:** the answer does not say "you cannot decree". **It says "the field in which you can decree is this much".** **That is, the power of the other side is not denied; it is measured.**

**And at `026-suara.md` on 26/50 al-Shuʿarāʾ's telling of the same scene was treated:** there the answer opened with *lā ḍayra* (no harm), and it was recorded: **"the answer does not say 'we shall endure the pain'; it says 'this is not a harm' — that is, the measure on which the threat rests is rejected."**

**The two answers can be set side by side, and the difference can be verified from the text:**

| | **Ṭāhā 20/72** | al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/50 |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | *Lan nuʾthiraka…* — **preference** | *Lā ḍayra* — **no harm** |
| View of the threat | ***Its extent is bounded*** | ***Its character is rejected*** |
| Ground | *Innamā taqḍī hādhihi'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā* | *Innā ilā rabbinā munqalibūn* |
| Hope | *Li-yaghfira lanā khaṭāyānā* | *Innā naṭmaʿu an yaghfira lanā* |

**Both tellings carry the same three steps: refusal, ground, hope.** **But the method used in the refusal differs: one measures the harm, the other the authority.** This is an overlap and a difference between the two sūras that can be verified.

### وَمَآ أَكْرَهْتَنَا عَلَيْهِ مِنَ ٱلسِّحْرِ

**The sentence must be recorded: among the things for which the sorcerers ask forgiveness they place *"the sorcery you compelled us to"* as well.**

**I record this as an observation and issue no ruling:** the verse records having been compelled not as an **excuse** but as something for which forgiveness is nonetheless **asked**. **That is, the compulsion is named, but responsibility is not presented as wholly lifted.** I do not go into the legal consequences of the sentence.

### وَٱللَّهُ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ

**And the answer is sealed with Pharaoh's own word.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **71** | *Ayyunā **ashaddu ʿadhāban wa abqā*** | **Pharaoh** — the question |
| **73** | *Wa'llāhu **khayrun wa abqā*** | **The sorcerers** — the answer |

**Pharaoh had asked "more severe in punishment and more lasting"; the answer says "better and more lasting".** **That is, the second attribute is taken over as it stands and the first is changed:** *khayr* (better) in place of *ashadd* (more severe).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verbal overlap of the two sentences:** the answer does not reject the other side's measure altogether. **It accepts the measure of lastingness** — because it is a sound measure — **and changes only the measure of severity.** **The dispute proceeds not over the measure but over the measure's being correctly applied.**

**And as was tabulated in the section on structure, the same word will occur twice more in the sūra** (127 and 131) — **once of punishment and once of provision.** **That is, the sūra carries the comparison set up in these two verses through to the end.**

### What this says to the present

**The sorcerers' answer is not a display of courage; it is a reckoning.**

**The reasoning the text builds can be counted out:** the authority in the other side's hand is **real** (it is not denied); **but its term is fixed** (*hādhihi'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā*); **and the term of what stands on the other side is not fixed** (*abqā*). **The conclusion follows from these three premises.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the force of the answer comes not from its feeling but from **the openness with which the comparison is made.** **And the observation recorded at `026-suara.md` holds here too:** those who spoke these words were the people in that assembly who knew best what the thing they had seen was not. **Knowledge becomes the cause not of resistance but of surrender.**

## 20/74-76 — إِنَّهُۥ مَن يَأْتِ رَبَّهُۥ مُجْرِمًا فَإِنَّ لَهُۥ جَهَنَّمَ لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ · وَمَن يَأْتِهِۦ مُؤْمِنًا قَدْ عَمِلَ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمُ ٱلدَّرَجَٰتُ ٱلْعُلَىٰ · جَنَّٰتُ عَدْنٍ تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا وَذَٰلِكَ جَزَآءُ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ

*Innahu man yaʾti rabbahu mujriman fa-inna lahu jahannama lā yamūtu fīhā wa lā yaḥyā · Wa man yaʾtihi muʾminan qad ʿamila'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti fa-ulāʾika lahumu'd-darajātu'l-ʿulā · Jannātu ʿadnin tajrī min taḥtiha'l-anhāru khālidīna fīhā, wa dhālika jazāʾu man tazakkā*

"**Whoever comes to his Lord as a criminal, for him there is Hell; he neither dies in it nor lives. · And whoever comes to Him as a believer who has done righteous deeds, for those there are the highest ranks: · gardens of Eden with rivers running beneath them, where they abide. That is the recompense of the one who purifies himself.**"

### To whom the sentence belongs

**A point of word order must be recorded, and it has been disputed in the classical commentaries: are these three verses the continuation of the sorcerers' speech, or a statement interposed by the text?**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The continuation of the sorcerers' speech** — they are explaining the *abqā* of the seventy-third verse |
| 2 | **The text's own statement** — a sentence of verdict following the speech |

**I do not impose a preference.** **I record one indication, not as a preference:** the seventy-fourth verse opens with *innahu*, and this is a particle of explanation attached to the preceding sentence; **but the content of the verses (the gardens of Eden, the ranks) may also be seen as beyond the knowledge of the sorcerers.** Neither reading runs against the text.

### لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ

**Both verbs are negated, and this is a sentence of definition.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence does not give a description of the punishment — **it says that both exits are closed.** **Death is a way out and life is a possibility; neither is there.**

**The same expression was treated at `087-ala.md` on 87/13** (*thumma lā yamūtu fīhā wa lā yaḥyā*). **I rely on that treatment.** **And it lies in the same field as the state of waiting recorded at `019-meryem.md` on 19/68-72** (*jithiyyā* — on the knees).

### ٱلدَّرَجَٰتُ ٱلْعُلَىٰ — and the sixty-eighth verse

***Al-ʿulā* is the feminine plural of *aʿlā*, root ع-ل-و.**

**And a link within the sūra must be recorded:**

| Verse | Expression | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | *Wa's-samāwāti'**l-ʿulā*** | **The heavens** |
| **68** | *Innaka anta'**l-aʿlā*** | **Moses** — for that moment |
| **75** | *Lahumu'd-darajātu'**l-ʿulā*** | **Those who believe and do good** |

**The same root in three separate places in the sūra: the heavens, a servant, and a recompense.** This is a distribution that can be verified from the text.

**And I record a caution about the language, because the two are easily confused:** this sūra contains **two distinct roots** that closely resemble one another and must not be mixed up:

| Word | Root | Sense | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***al-ʿulā*** | **ع-ل-و** | **Highest** | 4 (*al-samāwāti'l-ʿulā*), 75 (*al-darajātu'l-ʿulā*) |
| ***al-ūlā*** | **أ-و-ل** | **First, former** | 21 (*sīratahā'l-ūlā*), 51 (*al-qurūni'l-ūlā*), 133 (*al-ṣuḥufi'l-ūlā*) |

**The two words are close in their written form; their roots are distinct.** I give this as a note about the language.

**تَزَكَّىٰ** — root **ز-ك-و**: **to grow, to be purified.** **The root was treated at `091-sems.md` on 91/9 (*qad aflaḥa man zakkāhā*) and at `087-ala.md` on 87/14 (*qad aflaḥa man tazakkā*).** **I rely on those treatments.**

**And I record a datum of the text: there is an overlap between al-Aʿlā 87/14-15 and this verse** — in al-Aʿlā it said *qad aflaḥa man **tazakkā** · wa dhakara'sma rabbihi fa-ṣallā*: **purification, remembrance and prayer in the same order.** **The same three are distributed across Ṭāhā as well** (14: prayer and remembrance; 76: purification). **I record this overlap as an observation, not as a claim about intent.**

## 20/77-79 — وَلَقَدْ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَىٰ مُوسَىٰٓ أَنْ أَسْرِ بِعِبَادِى فَٱضْرِبْ لَهُمْ طَرِيقًا فِى ٱلْبَحْرِ يَبَسًا لَّا تَخَٰفُ دَرَكًا وَلَا تَخْشَىٰ · فَأَتْبَعَهُمْ فِرْعَوْنُ بِجُنُودِهِۦ فَغَشِيَهُم مِّنَ ٱلْيَمِّ مَا غَشِيَهُمْ · وَأَضَلَّ فِرْعَوْنُ قَوْمَهُۥ وَمَا هَدَىٰ

*Wa laqad awḥaynā ilā Mūsā an asri bi-ʿibādī faḍrib lahum ṭarīqan fi'l-baḥri yabasan lā takhāfu darakan wa lā takhshā · Fa-atbaʿahum Firʿawnu bi-junūdihi fa-ghashiyahum mina'l-yammi mā ghashiyahum · Wa aḍalla Firʿawnu qawmahu wa mā hadā*

"**Indeed We revealed to Moses: 'Travel by night with My servants and strike out for them a dry road through the sea. Do not fear being overtaken, and do not be afraid.' · Pharaoh followed them with his armies, and there covered them from the sea what covered them. · Pharaoh led his people astray and did not guide.**"

### فَغَشِيَهُم مِّنَ ٱلْيَمِّ مَا غَشِيَهُمْ

**This is one of the most striking decisions of narration in the Qurʾān, and a point of word order must be recorded.**

**The sentence does not name the thing that covered them:** *ghashiyahum … **mā ghashiyahum*** — "there covered them what covered them."

**In Arabic this pattern is called *ibhām li't-tafkhīm*: a thing's being deliberately left indefinite so as to magnify it.** The same pattern occurs at al-Najm 53/16 (*idh yaghsha's-sidrata mā yaghshā*) **and was treated at `053-necm.md`.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pattern itself:** the scene of the drowning is **not told.** Neither the height of the water, nor the cries, nor the duration. **The verb is repeated and its object left indefinite.** **And at `026-suara.md` on 26/66 the same event was given in a single phrase:** *thumma aghraqna'l-ākharīn.* **Neither sūra widens the scene.**

**This confirms from the text itself a principle recorded in `USLUP.md`:** one does not enter into a detail the Qurʾān does not give — **because the text itself does not enter into it.**

### وَأَضَلَّ فِرْعَوْنُ قَوْمَهُۥ وَمَا هَدَىٰ

**The last sentence of the block, and one of the tightest links in the sūra, is here.**

**In the fiftieth verse Moses's answer was: *aʿṭā kulla shayʾin khalqahu thumma hadā*.** **The seventy-ninth verse negates the same verb of Pharaoh: *wa mā hadā*.**

| Verse | Expression | Subject | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **50** | *Thumma **hadā*** | **God** | **The last word of the answer** |
| **79** | *Wa mā **hadā*** | **Pharaoh** | **The last word of the story** |

**The distance between the two is twenty-nine verses, and the whole encounter has been told in between.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra delivers the verdict on the dispute not with a sentence of conclusion but **by bringing back the word with which the dispute was opened.** **The question was "who is your Lord"; the core of the answer was "the one who guides"; the core of the verdict is "he did not guide".**

**And the verb *aḍalla* is worth recording:** it is not said of Pharaoh himself that "he went astray" — **it is said that "he led his people astray".** **That is, the verdict is built not on his personal fate but on the responsibility he carried.** **The same line was treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/41** (*wa jaʿalnāhum aʾimmatan yadʿūna ila'n-nār*), and it was recorded there: **"to be in front is a position; what is decisive is where one calls people to."** I rely on that treatment.

## 20/80-82 — يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ قَدْ أَنجَيْنَٰكُم مِّنْ عَدُوِّكُمْ وَوَٰعَدْنَٰكُمْ جَانِبَ ٱلطُّورِ ٱلْأَيْمَنَ وَنَزَّلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْمَنَّ وَٱلسَّلْوَىٰ · كُلُوا۟ مِن طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ فَيَحِلَّ عَلَيْكُمْ غَضَبِى وَمَن يَحْلِلْ عَلَيْهِ غَضَبِى فَقَدْ هَوَىٰ · وَإِنِّى لَغَفَّارٌ لِّمَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًا ثُمَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ

*Yā banī isrāʾīla qad anjaynākum min ʿaduwwikum wa wāʿadnākum jāniba'ṭ-ṭūri'l-aymana wa nazzalnā ʿalaykumu'l-manna wa's-salwā · Kulū min ṭayyibāti mā razaqnākum wa lā taṭghaw fīhi fa-yaḥilla ʿalaykum ghaḍabī, wa man yaḥlil ʿalayhi ghaḍabī fa-qad hawā · Wa innī la-ghaffārun li-man tāba wa āmana wa ʿamila ṣāliḥan thumma'htadā*

"**O Children of Israel! We delivered you from your enemy, We made a promise to you on the right side of the Mount, and We sent down on you manna and quails. · Eat of the good things We have provided you, and do not transgress the measure in it, or My anger will settle upon you. And whoever My anger settles upon has fallen. · And I am indeed most forgiving to whoever repents, believes, does righteous work, and then keeps to the guidance.**"

### جَانِبَ ٱلطُّورِ ٱلْأَيْمَنَ

**The same expression occurred at `019-meryem.md` on 19/52:** *wa nādaynāhu min **jānibi'ṭ-Ṭūri'l-aymani** wa qarrabnāhu najiyyā.* **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the difference is worth recording:** in Maryam the expression appears as **the place of the call**, in Ṭāhā as **the place of the promise given.** **The same place, two different events.**

***Al-aymana* can mean both "the right side" and "the blessed";** the lexicographers record both possibilities. **I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

### وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ — the limit inside the favour

**Where the sentence stands must be recorded: the command and the prohibition come one after the other.** *Kulū* (eat) — *wa lā taṭghaw fīh* (do not transgress the measure in it).

**And the pronoun *fīh* is worth recording: "in it".** That is, the prohibition of transgressing the measure is placed **inside the provision itself.** **The prohibition is not of eating but of going beyond the bound in eating.**

**And the root ط-غ-ي must be recorded — it was tabulated in the treatment of the twenty-fourth verse:** the same root characterised **Pharaoh** in the twenty-fourth and forty-third verses.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the distribution of the root across these three verses:** the delivered party is warned **with the root that characterises the one they were delivered from** — and the subject of the warning is now not oppression but **favour.** **That is, the sūra gives *ṭughyān* not as a character but as a threshold: power and favour alike have a bound that can be crossed.**

**فَيَحِلَّ عَلَيْكُمْ غَضَبِى** — the root **ح-ل-ل**: **to alight, to come down and settle in a place** (as a traveller stops for the night). **That is, the anger does not "come" but "settles" — and what settles stays.**

**فَقَدْ هَوَىٰ** — root **ه-و-ي**: **to fall from above downward.** **From the same root comes *hawā* (the inclination of the self)**, and the relation between these two branches of the word is recorded by the lexicographers: **the inclination of the self is also a fall.** **The root was treated at `053-necm.md` on 53/1 (*wa'n-najmi idhā hawā*);** I rely on that treatment.

**And it lies in the same field of imagery as *fa-tardā* (you will perish — to fall) in the sixteenth verse.** **The sūra tells straying twice, as a fall, with two different roots.**

### ثُمَّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ — the fourth condition

**The eighty-second verse names four conditions, and the fourth is worth recording:**

| Order | Condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Tāba* — **he repented** |
| 2 | *Wa āmana* — **he believed** |
| 3 | *Wa ʿamila ṣāliḥan* — **he did righteous work** |
| 4 | ***Thumma'htadā*** — **and then kept to the guidance** |

**The first three are joined with *wāw*, the fourth with *thumma*.** **In Arabic *thumma* conveys an interval and a sequence.**

**What the fourth condition means has been disputed:**

| Reading | What *thumma'htadā* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Continuance** — he persisted in that state |
| 2 | **Not falling into doubt** — he stood firm in his faith |
| 3 | **Finding the right way** — carrying on his work upon the right way |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference in conjunction:** the first three items are things that can happen in a **moment**; the fourth, separated off by *thumma*, is given as something **spread over time.** **That is, the list distinguishes a decision from the keeping of that decision.**

**And the root ه-د-ي stands here as one of its eight occurrences in the sūra** — in the tenth verse it was the thing Moses sought, in the fiftieth the core of the answer, in the seventy-ninth the thing negated, **and here a condition.**

## 20/83-85 — وَمَآ أَعْجَلَكَ عَن قَوْمِكَ يَٰمُوسَىٰ · قَالَ هُمْ أُو۟لَآءِ عَلَىٰٓ أَثَرِى وَعَجِلْتُ إِلَيْكَ رَبِّ لِتَرْضَىٰ · قَالَ فَإِنَّا قَدْ فَتَنَّا قَوْمَكَ مِنۢ بَعْدِكَ وَأَضَلَّهُمُ ٱلسَّامِرِىُّ

*Wa mā aʿjalaka ʿan qawmika yā Mūsā · Qāla hum ulāʾi ʿalā atharī wa ʿajiltu ilayka rabbi li-tarḍā · Qāla fa-innā qad fatannā qawmaka min baʿdika wa aḍallahumu's-sāmiriyy*

"'**What has hurried you away from your people, O Moses?**' · He said: '**They are there, upon my track. I hastened to You, my Lord, that You might be pleased.**' · '**We have tested your people after you, and the Sāmirī has led them astray**,' He said."

### The question itself

**This is among the most striking questions in the sūra, and a point of word order must be recorded.**

**The question is framed not as a rebuke but as a question about cause:** *wa mā aʿjalaka* — "what has hurried you?" **And it is on the same pattern as the question in the seventeenth verse:** *wa mā tilka bi-yamīnika yā Mūsā.*

| Verse | Question | At the end |
|---|---|---|
| **17** | *Wa mā tilka bi-yamīnika **yā Mūsā*** | **The name is spoken** |
| **83** | *Wa mā aʿjalaka ʿan qawmika **yā Mūsā*** | **The name is spoken** |

**Both questions begin with *wa mā* and both end with the name.** **This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** neither of them asks for information. **The first has him say in his own words what is in his hand, the second what he has done.**

### هُمْ أُو۟لَآءِ عَلَىٰٓ أَثَرِى — the two parts of the answer

**The answer is two sentences, and each does a different work:**

| Part | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Hum ulāʾi ʿalā atharī* | **Information** — "they are behind me" |
| 2 | *Wa ʿajiltu ilayka rabbi **li-tarḍā*** | **Ground** — "that You might be pleased" |

**أَثَر** — root **أ-ث-ر**: **a trace, a footprint, a mark left behind.** *ʿAlā atharī* — "upon my track", that is, **they are coming after me.**

**And the word will return in the ninety-sixth verse: *min athari'r-rasūl*.** **The same word, once of the people's coming after Moses, once in the Sāmirī's speech.** This is a distribution that can be verified from the text.

**The second part of the answer must be recorded: what is offered as the ground is *li-tarḍā* — "that You might be pleased".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the content of the reason given:** the reason offered for the haste is not a fault but **a good intention.** **And the sentence that answers it is not a scolding but a piece of news:** *fa-innā qad fatannā qawmaka min baʿdik.*

**That is, the answer to the question is given by announcing what has happened.** **The text does not say outright why the haste was asked about;** but the announcement, immediately after the question, that the people left behind have been tested **shows that the question was not asked idly.** I record this as an observation about the structure.

### The sūra's link of haste

**The root ع-ج-ل occurs three times in this sūra, and the three form a link. This is one of the plainest internal ties in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **83** | *Wa mā **aʿjalaka** ʿan qawmik* | **God** — a question | **Interrogation** |
| **84** | *Wa **ʿajiltu** ilayka rabbi li-tarḍā* | **Moses** — an answer | **Admission and ground** |
| **114** | *Wa **lā taʿjal** bi'l-Qurʾāni min qabli an yuqḍā ilayka waḥyuh* | **God** — a command | **Prohibition** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the three verses carry the same root:** in the eighty-third verse the sūra asks about one prophet's haste; **in the hundred and fourteenth it forbids haste to another prophet.** **And in neither place is the motive of the haste a bad one** — one wants to please, the other is trying not to lose the revelation.

**That is, what the sūra records is not an ill-intentioned haste: it is that a well-intentioned haste can also break an order.** **This link will be completed in the treatment of verse 114.**

### ٱلسَّامِرِىّ — a note

**What the Qurʾān gives about this person amounts to this: a name (or a relative designation), that he led the people astray (85), that he brought out the calf (87-88), that he speaks one sentence in his own words in the ninety-sixth verse, and that his punishment is announced in the ninety-seventh.**

**Beyond this — who he was, what people he came from, from what his name derives — the Qurʾān says nothing.** **Various explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries, and a significant part of them rests on narratives drawn from the surrounding cultures.**

**I enter a note per the method (`USLUP.md`), and it holds for the whole of this discussion: this commentary does not enter into the Isrāʾīliyyāt and does not manufacture detail the Qurʾān has not given.** **There is no certain knowledge.**

## 20/86-89 — فَرَجَعَ مُوسَىٰٓ إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ غَضْبَٰنَ أَسِفًا

*Fa-rajaʿa Mūsā ilā qawmihi ghaḍbāna asifā, qāla yā qawmi alam yaʿidkum rabbukum waʿdan ḥasanā, a-fa-ṭāla ʿalaykumu'l-ʿahdu am aradtum an yaḥilla ʿalaykum ghaḍabun min rabbikum fa-akhlaftum mawʿidī · Qālū mā akhlafnā mawʿidaka bi-malkinā wa lākinnā ḥummilnā awzāran min zīnati'l-qawmi fa-qadhafnāhā fa-kadhālika alqa's-sāmiriyy · Fa-akhraja lahum ʿijlan jasadan lahu khuwārun fa-qālū hādhā ilāhukum wa ilāhu Mūsā fa-nasiya · A-fa-lā yarawna allā yarjiʿu ilayhim qawlan wa lā yamliku lahum ḍarran wa lā nafʿā*

"**Moses returned to his people angry and grieved. 'My people! Did your Lord not make you a good promise? Did the term seem long to you, or did you want an anger from your Lord to settle upon you, that you broke your promise to me?' he said. · They said: 'We did not break our promise to you of our own will; but we were made to carry loads of the people's ornaments, and we threw them in. The Sāmirī likewise threw.' · Then he brought out for them a calf, a body with a lowing sound. 'This is your god and the god of Moses,' they said; but he forgot. · Did they not see that it returned them no word and had no power over them to harm or to benefit?**"

### غَضْبَٰنَ أَسِفًا — two states

**Two words come side by side, and each conveys a different thing.**

**غَضْبَان** — root **غ-ض-ب**: anger. **The pattern *faʿlān* — in Arabic this measure conveys a state that fills a person and overflows him** (*jawʿān* — writhing with hunger, *ghaḍbān* — filled with anger).

**أَسِف** — root **أ-س-ف**: **a state in which grief and anger are present together.** The lexicographers explain the word as **intense sorrow** or as **anger born of helplessness**; that it is a combination of the two is also recorded. **The word occurs at Yūsuf 12/84 as well (*yā asafā ʿalā Yūsuf*).**

**I record this as an observation:** the text records the state of the returning man **in two words** — and the second does not leave the anger standing alone. **Grief is named beside the anger.**

**And this is the ground of the answer Aaron will give in the ninety-fourth verse:** that answer is a sentence spoken to an angry man.

### بِمَلْكِنَا — of our own will

**The people's answer has three parts, and the first must be recorded: *mā akhlafnā mawʿidaka bi-malkinā*.**

The root **م-ل-ك**: to own, to hold in one's hand. ***Malk* — "as something in our own hands", that is, of our own will.**

**I record this as an observation:** the answer is trying to present the event as **outside their own will.** **And the two sentences that follow complete this:** *ḥummilnā* (we were made to carry — passive) and *fa-kadhālika alqa's-sāmiriyy* (the Sāmirī likewise threw).

**That is, all three elements of the answer carry the responsibility somewhere else: an absence of will, a passive verb, and someone else's example.** This is a structure that can be verified from the text.

**أَوْزَار** — the plural of *wizr*, root **و-ز-ر**: **a heavy load.** **The same root was treated in the discussion of the word *wazīr* in the twenty-ninth verse.** **And the choice of the word is worth recording:** ornaments are named **as "loads".**

### عِجْلًا جَسَدًا لَّهُۥ خُوَارٌ

**Three words, three separate pieces of information:**

| Word | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| ***ʿIjl*** | **A calf** — the shape of the thing |
| ***Jasad*** | **A body, a hulk** — an object without spirit |
| ***Lahu khuwār*** | **Having a lowing** — the sound it made |

**The word *jasad* must be recorded:** in Arabic *jasad* is used of **a body without a spirit.** **That is, in describing the object the verse says by its very word that it is not alive.**

**خُوَار** — root **خ-و-ر**: **the lowing of cattle.** The word occurs in the Qurʾān only here and at al-Aʿrāf 7/148.

**How the sound came about is not stated in the Qurʾān.** **Various explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries (a contrivance through which wind passed, a trick, or other accounts).** **This commentary does not go into them; the text is silent.**

**What can be verified from the text is this, and I record it:** the verse names the sound not as a **miracle** but as a **property** of the object — and immediately afterwards it asks how much use that sound was: *allā yarjiʿu ilayhim **qawlan***.

### فَنَسِىَ — who forgot

**This is the most disputed point in the sentence, and the disagreement must not be concealed. *Fa-nasiya* — "he forgot". Its subject is not stated.**

| Reading | Who *nasiya* is | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Sāmirī** | He forgot his own condition, or the truth, or what is right |
| 2 | **The Sāmirī** — *nisyān* here in the sense of "he abandoned" | He left the right way |
| 3 | **Those who worshipped the calf** | They forgot what Moses had told them |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But I record a datum of the text:** the root ن-س-ي is one of the spines of this sūra (52, 88, 115, 126 — tabulated in the treatment of 20/49-52). **And the occurrence here is the second link of that chain.** **That is, on whichever reading, the sūra has named the affair of the calf with the word *nisyān*** — and in the hundred and fifteenth verse it will name the story of Adam with the same word.

### إِلَٰهُكُمْ وَإِلَٰهُ مُوسَىٰ

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: the claim does not reject what Moses told them.**

**It could have said "this is your god" and stopped; "and the god of Moses" is added.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the second construct:** the deviation here is framed not as a **denial** but as a **displacement.** **The same god is claimed — only he is attached to a visible object.** **And the question of the eighty-ninth verse comes down on precisely this point: that object can neither answer, nor benefit, nor harm.**

**The same measure was treated at `021-enbiya.md`, at `026-suara.md` on 26/72-73 and at `037-saffat.md`** (*hal yasmaʿūnakum idh tadʿūn · aw yanfaʿūnakum aw yaḍurrūn*). **I rely on those treatments: the measure is hearing, and benefit and harm.** **Ṭāhā adds one further item to this measure: *allā yarjiʿu ilayhim qawlan* — the inability to return a word.**

## 20/90-93 — وَلَقَدْ قَالَ لَهُمْ هَٰرُونُ مِن قَبْلُ يَٰقَوْمِ إِنَّمَا فُتِنتُم بِهِۦ وَإِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ فَٱتَّبِعُونِى وَأَطِيعُوٓا۟ أَمْرِى · قَالُوا۟ لَن نَّبْرَحَ عَلَيْهِ عَٰكِفِينَ حَتَّىٰ يَرْجِعَ إِلَيْنَا مُوسَىٰ · قَالَ يَٰهَٰرُونُ مَا مَنَعَكَ إِذْ رَأَيْتَهُمْ ضَلُّوٓا۟ · أَلَّا تَتَّبِعَنِ أَفَعَصَيْتَ أَمْرِى

*Wa laqad qāla lahum Hārūnu min qablu yā qawmi innamā futintum bih, wa inna rabbakumu'r-Raḥmānu fa'ttabiʿūnī wa aṭīʿū amrī · Qālū lan nabraḥa ʿalayhi ʿākifīna ḥattā yarjiʿa ilaynā Mūsā · Qāla yā Hārūnu mā manaʿaka idh raʾaytahum ḍallū · Allā tattabiʿani, a-fa-ʿaṣayta amrī*

"**Aaron had said to them before this: 'My people! You are only being tested by it. Your Lord is the Merciful. Follow me and obey my command.' · 'We shall not cease to devote ourselves to it until Moses returns to us,' they said. · Moses said: 'Aaron! When you saw them go astray, what kept you · from following me? Did you disobey my command?'**"

### Where Aaron's warning stands

**A point of word order must be recorded: the ninetieth verse says *min qablu* (before this).**

**That is, after telling the events the verse goes back and records that Aaron's warning was given before the calf was worshipped.** **This is information given to the reader before Moses's question in the ninety-second verse.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the note *min qablu*:** the text makes Aaron's defence **before his own defence.** **That is, when the reader hears Moses's question he already knows the answer.**

### إِنَّمَا فُتِنتُم بِهِۦ — the word of the diagnosis

**The root of *fitna* was treated at the fortieth verse (*wa fatannāka futūnā*), where its four occurrences in the sūra were tabulated.** **Here is the third occurrence: Aaron names the event by its right name — this is not a straying but a testing.**

**And the name that follows the diagnosis must be recorded: *wa inna rabbakumu'**r-Raḥmān***.**

**I record this as an observation:** the name recalled in the face of the calf is the one that declares **mercy, not punishment.** **And as recorded at `019-meryem.md`, this is the name called upon in moments of fear and of seeking refuge.**

### فَٱتَّبِعُونِى وَأَطِيعُوٓا۟ أَمْرِى

**Two commands: *ittibāʿ* (to follow) and *ṭāʿa* (to obey a command).** **And these two words will return exactly as they are in Moses's question in the ninety-second and ninety-third verses:** *allā **tattabiʿani*** and *a-fa-**ʿaṣayta amrī***.

| Who | Expression | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| **Aaron** (90) | *Fa'**ttabiʿūnī** wa **aṭīʿū amrī*** | **To the people** |
| **Moses** (92-93) | *Allā **tattabiʿani** … a-fa-**ʿaṣayta amrī*** | **To Aaron** |

**The same two words, in two different mouths.** **This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** what Aaron asked of his people is expressed in the same words as what Moses asks of Aaron. **And the ninetieth verse has already told the reader that Aaron had asked for those two things.**

### عَٰكِفِين — the word's return in the sūra

The root **ع-ك-ف**: **to stand constantly over a thing, not to leave it.** From the same root comes *iʿtikāf*. **The root was treated at `026-suara.md` on 26/71 (*fa-naẓallu lahā ʿākifīn*);** I rely on that treatment.

**And the word will return in the ninety-seventh verse:** *ilāhika'lladhī ẓalta ʿalayhi **ʿākifā***. **The same word, once of the people's insistence, once of the Sāmirī's condition.**

## 20/94 — قَالَ يَبْنَؤُمَّ لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَا بِرَأْسِىٓ إِنِّى خَشِيتُ أَن تَقُولَ فَرَّقْتَ بَيْنَ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ وَلَمْ تَرْقُبْ قَوْلِى

*Qāla ya'bna umma lā taʾkhudh bi-liḥyatī wa lā bi-raʾsī, innī khashītu an taqūla farraqta bayna banī isrāʾīla wa lam tarqub qawlī*

"He said: '**Son of my mother! Do not take hold of my beard, nor of my head. I feared that you would say, "You have divided the Children of Israel and have not kept my word."**'"

### يَبْنَؤُمَّ — the choice of address

**The first word of the answer must be recorded: *ya'bna umma* — "son of my mother".**

**A point of word order:** Aaron is Moses's **full brother**; he could have said *yā akhī* (my brother). **He chooses an address that names the mother's side.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the address itself:** the word touches the **softest** place in the brotherhood. **And this is the first word of an answer given to an angry man.**

**And a link within the sūra must be recorded:** in the thirty-eighth and fortieth verses Moses was reminded of **his own mother** (*awḥaynā ilā ummika*, *fa-rajaʿnāka ilā ummik*). **The address Aaron chooses is a reference back to that mother.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text; **it is not a claim about intent but a countable connection.**

### لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَا بِرَأْسِى

**The concreteness of the scene must be recorded: the verse gives the event not as a description but from inside a sentence.**

**What happened is not told directly;** **it is understood from Aaron's words.** **The same event is told from another vantage at al-Aʿrāf 7/150** (*wa akhadha bi-raʾsi akhīhi yajurruhu ilayh*).

The root **ل-ح-ي**: **the jaw, the jawbone;** *liḥya* — **the beard.**

**I record this as an observation:** the text does not **cover over** a prophet's actual conduct in a moment of anger. **The same honesty was recorded at `028-kasas.md` on 28/14-21 and at `019-meryem.md` on 19/23;** I rely on those treatments.

**And two parts of the body are named: the beard and the head.** **Both are places where honour resides in Arab culture** — and both are places the other side's **hand can reach.**

### إِنِّى خَشِيتُ — the ground of the defence

**Aaron's answer is not an excuse but an account of a choice, and its structure must be recorded.**

| Element | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Innī **khashītu*** | **Fear** — but not for himself |
| 2 | *An taqūla **farraqta bayna banī isrāʾīl*** | **The feared outcome** — division |
| 3 | *Wa lam **tarqub** qawlī* | **The second fear** — going against the instruction given |

**That is, Aaron says he chose between two evils:** either he would intervene harshly with the people and bring on **division**, or he would wait for Moses's return.

The root **ر-ق-ب**: **to keep watch, to crane the neck and look.** *Raqaba* — the neck. ***Arqaba* is to watch and to watch over** — whence *murāqaba*. **And *lam tarqub qawlī* means "you did not keep watch over my word".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two items of the answer:** Aaron does not plead his own safety or his lack of authority. **He says that he was caught between two responsibilities.** **And the ninetieth verse has already told the reader that he did not merely wait — that he spoke and warned.**

**And at `028-kasas.md` on 28/34 the quality asked for in Aaron was *yuṣaddiqunī* (that he may confirm me); at Ṭāhā 20/32 what was asked was *ashrikhu fī amrī* (make him a partner in my task).** **The question in the ninety-third verse uses precisely that word (*a-fa-ʿaṣayta amrī*).** This is a connection that can be verified within the sūra.

### What this says to the present

**What the scene records is not a family quarrel but a conflict of responsibilities.**

**Two responsibilities are set side by side: turning a community back from a wrong, and holding that community together.** **And the text records that the two do not always run the same way.**

**The verse imposes no preference** — it gives Moses's question and Aaron's answer both as they stand. **And Moses's next move is to turn to the Sāmirī (95); no answer given to Aaron is recorded.** I record this as an observation.

## 20/95-98 — قَالَ فَمَا خَطْبُكَ يَٰسَٰمِرِىُّ · قَالَ بَصُرْتُ بِمَا لَمْ يَبْصُرُوا۟ بِهِۦ فَقَبَضْتُ قَبْضَةً مِّنْ أَثَرِ ٱلرَّسُولِ فَنَبَذْتُهَا وَكَذَٰلِكَ سَوَّلَتْ لِى نَفْسِى

*Qāla fa-mā khaṭbuka yā sāmiriyy · Qāla baṣurtu bimā lam yabṣurū bihi fa-qabaḍtu qabḍatan min athari'r-rasūli fa-nabadhtuhā wa kadhālika sawwalat lī nafsī · Qāla fa'dhhab fa-inna laka fi'l-ḥayāti an taqūla lā misās, wa inna laka mawʿidan lan tukhlafah, wa'nẓur ilā ilāhika'lladhī ẓalta ʿalayhi ʿākifā, la-nuḥarriqannahu thumma la-nansifannahu fi'l-yammi nasfā · Innamā ilāhukumu'llāhu'lladhī lā ilāha illā huw, wasiʿa kulla shayʾin ʿilmā*

"He said: '**And what was your business, O Sāmirī?**' · He said: '**I saw what they did not see; I took a handful from the track of the messenger and threw it in. Thus did my own self make it seem good to me.**' · '**Go!**' he said. '**In this life it is yours to say "Do not touch me". And there is an appointment for you that will not be broken. Look at your god, over which you have stayed devoted! We shall surely burn it and then scatter it into the sea. · Your god is only God, beside whom there is no god. He encompasses all things in knowledge.**'"

### On 20/96 — a note first

**This verse is one of the sentences in the Qurʾān over which the most speculation has been produced.**

**I draw the boundary at the outset, per the method (`USLUP.md`), and it holds for the whole of this discussion:**

- **No detail the Qurʾān has not given is entered into.**
- **No Isrāʾīliyyāt are entered into.**
- **No fabricated report is transmitted.**
- **There is no certain knowledge about what this verse tells; the disagreement will be given in a table and no preference will be imposed.**

### What the text gives

**First I enumerate what is definitely present in the verse:**

| Element | Expression | What is certain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Baṣurtu bimā lam yabṣurū bih* | **A claim to have seen** — something the others did not see |
| 2 | *Fa-qabaḍtu **qabḍatan*** | **A taking of a handful** — the amount: a handful |
| 3 | *Min **athari'r-rasūl*** | **The source: "from the track of the messenger"** |
| 4 | *Fa-nabadhtuhā* | **An act of throwing** |
| 5 | *Wa kadhālika **sawwalat lī nafsī*** | **His own assessment** |

**None of these is disputed.** **What is disputed is the two words in the third item: *athar* and *al-rasūl*.**

### The table of disagreement

| Reading | What *athari'r-rasūl* is | Its ground | Its difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Earth taken from the messenger's footprint** | The first sense of *athar* is **a footprint** | The text does not say what it was; the details come from reports |
| 2 | **A part of what the messenger brought (the teaching)** | *Athar* also means "what remains of someone"; *al-rasūl* may be Moses | The verb (*qabaḍtu* — I took a handful) suggests something concrete |
| 3 | **That *al-rasūl* is not Moses but someone else** | The word is definite (*al-*) and who is meant is not said | No other messenger is named in the text |
| 4 | **What the object of the verb *nabadhtuhā* is** — whether he threw it onto the calf or elsewhere | The pronoun is indefinite | The indefiniteness is in the text |

**Various combinations of these readings are transmitted in the classical commentaries, and a significant part of the detail added to them rests on narratives outside the Qurʾān.**

**None of them is preferred in this commentary. The record is this: there is no certain knowledge.**

**And I record this as an observation:** the verse itself does not aim to tell **how the event happened.** **The centre of gravity of the sentence is in its last part:** *wa kadhālika **sawwalat lī nafsī*** — "thus did my own self make it seem good to me."

The root **س-و-ل**: **to dress a thing up and make it attractive, to present it to a person as easy and right.** **The word occurs in several places in the Qurʾān** (Yūsuf 12/18 and 12/83, *sawwalat lakum anfusukum*; Muḥammad 47/25, *wa amlā lahum*).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that this is the sentence's last clause:** in telling of his own deed the Sāmirī **begins by claiming a superiority** (*baṣurtu bimā lam yabṣurū bih* — I saw what they did not see) **and ends by tracing its source to his own self.** **That is, the sentence begins with a claim to privileged sight and ends with the self's dressing-up.**

**And the root ب-ص-ر must be recorded: the Sāmirī's first word is a claim to *see*.** **And in the hundred and twenty-fifth verse of the sūra another claim to *see* will come:** *wa qad kuntu **baṣīrā***. **The relation between the two claims will be treated in the discussion of 124-127.**

### لَا مِسَاسَ — the form of the punishment

***Misās* — root م-س-س: to touch.** *Lā misās* — **"no touching".**

**Different explanations of what the punishment is are transmitted:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Isolation from society** — being unable to have contact with anyone, everyone keeping away from him |
| 2 | **The words he himself must say** — having to say to everyone he meets, "do not touch me" |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **What the text gives is that the sentence itself has been set down as the punishment:** *fa-inna laka fi'l-ḥayāti **an taqūla lā misās***.

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the construction of the sentence:** the punishment is described not as an act but as **a thing to be said.** **That is, it becomes a sentence to be repeated for a lifetime.**

### لَّنُحَرِّقَنَّهُۥ ثُمَّ لَنَنسِفَنَّهُۥ فِى ٱلْيَمِّ نَسْفًا

**Two verbs, and both are connected to other places in the sūra.**

**The root ن-س-ف was analysed in detail at `077-murselat.md` on 77/10**, and it was recorded there: **the root has two uses — to tear a thing up by the root and scatter it, and to winnow threshed grain against the wind so as to separate the chaff from the seed (the tool is called a *minsaf*); the common point is lifting what is light into the air and blowing it away.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the same root will be used of the mountains in the hundred and fifth verse of the sūra:** *yansifuhā rabbī **nasfā***.

| Verse | What is scattered | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **97** | **The calf** | *La-nansifannahu fi'l-yammi **nasfā*** |
| **105** | **The mountains** | *Yansifuhā rabbī **nasfā*** |

**The same root, the same object of cognate origin (*nasfā*), two different objects: an idol and all the mountains.** **This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** the sūra tells the end of the object worshipped **with the same word** as the end of the largest objects there are.

**And *fi'l-yamm* — the water that set the story going.** **In the thirty-ninth verse Moses was set into that water; in the ninety-seventh the idol is scattered into it.** This connection was recorded in the discussion of 20/38-39.

### وَسِعَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ عِلْمًا

**The closing sentence of the block must be recorded, and the attribute chosen is striking: *ʿilm*.**

**At the end of the story of an idol the expected close might have been a declaration of power.** **The sentence declares knowledge.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the vocabulary of the section:** across the block a claim to **sight** appeared twice (*baṣurtu bimā lam yabṣurū bih*) and a claim to **knowing** once (the reason for the punishment *lā misās*). **The close says where knowledge is complete.**

**And it overlaps with the fifty-second verse:** *ʿilmuhā ʿinda rabbī fī kitāb, lā yaḍillu rabbī **wa lā yansā***. **Both verses take knowledge as their subject, and both stand at turning points of the story.**

## 20/99-101 — كَذَٰلِكَ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ مِنْ أَنۢبَآءِ مَا قَدْ سَبَقَ وَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَٰكَ مِن لَّدُنَّا ذِكْرًا · مَّنْ أَعْرَضَ عَنْهُ فَإِنَّهُۥ يَحْمِلُ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ وِزْرًا · خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهِ وَسَآءَ لَهُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ حِمْلًا

*Kadhālika naquṣṣu ʿalayka min anbāʾi mā qad sabaq, wa qad ātaynāka min ladunnā dhikrā · Man aʿraḍa ʿanhu fa-innahu yaḥmilu yawma'l-qiyāmati wizrā · Khālidīna fīh, wa sāʾa lahum yawma'l-qiyāmati ḥimlā*

"**Thus do We relate to you some of the reports of what has gone before. We have given you a remembrance from Ourselves. · Whoever turns away from it will carry a heavy load on the Day of Resurrection. · They will abide under it. Evil for them is that burden on the Day of Resurrection!**"

### Why the story was told

**The ninety-ninth verse comes after a story ninety verses long, and it does two things.**

**One — it names the telling itself:** *naquṣṣu ʿalayka* — "We relate to you". **And *min anbāʾi* — "some of the reports".** **That is, the text says that what it gives is not the whole.** **The same note was treated at `028-kasas.md` in connection with the name of that sūra;** I rely on that treatment.

**Two — it names what has been given:** *wa qad ātaynāka min ladunnā **dhikrā***.

**And this overlaps with the third verse of the sūra:** there the work of the Book was described as *tadhkira*. **Here the Book itself is called *dhikr*.**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *Illā **tadhkiratan** li-man yakhshā* | **Function** |
| **99** | *Wa qad ātaynāka min ladunnā **dhikrā*** | **Name** |
| **124** | *Wa man aʿraḍa ʿan **dhikrī*** | **What is abandoned** |

**Three verses, one root.** **And the hundredth verse and the hundred and twenty-fourth open with the same verb: *man aʿraḍa*.**

| Verse | Expression | Its recompense |
|---|---|---|
| **100** | *Man aʿraḍa **ʿanhu*** | *Yaḥmilu … **wizrā*** — a heavy load |
| **124** | *Wa man aʿraḍa **ʿan dhikrī*** | *Maʿīshatan **ḍankan*** — a straitened livelihood |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses are built on the same pattern:** the sūra gives the recompense of turning away **twice** — one a **load** in the hereafter, the other a **narrowness** in this world. **Both punishments are spatial: one weight, the other constriction.**

**وِزْر** — root **و-ز-ر**: **a heavy load.** **The root was treated at `094-insirah.md` on 94/2 (*wa waḍaʿnā ʿanka wizrak*) and at `035-fatir.md` on 35/18;** I rely on those treatments. **And the word *wazīr* in the twenty-ninth verse was from this root as well** — tabulated in the discussion of 20/29-35.

**I record this as an observation:** the sūra uses the same root in two different places — **one a load that is shared** (*wazīr* — a helper), **the other a load that cannot be carried** (*wizr*). **And as recorded at `019-meryem.md` on 19/95, the second is carried alone:** *wa kulluhum ātīhi yawma'l-qiyāmati fardā.*

## 20/102-104 — يَوْمَ يُنفَخُ فِى ٱلصُّورِ وَنَحْشُرُ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ يَوْمَئِذٍ زُرْقًا · يَتَخَٰفَتُونَ بَيْنَهُمْ إِن لَّبِثْتُمْ إِلَّا عَشْرًا · نَّحْنُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا يَقُولُونَ إِذْ يَقُولُ أَمْثَلُهُمْ طَرِيقَةً إِن لَّبِثْتُمْ إِلَّا يَوْمًا

*Yawma yunfakhu fi'ṣ-ṣūri wa naḥshuru'l-mujrimīna yawmaʾidhin zurqā · Yatakhāfatūna baynahum in labithtum illā ʿashrā · Naḥnu aʿlamu bimā yaqūlūna idh yaqūlu amthaluhum ṭarīqatan in labithtum illā yawmā*

"**The day the horn is blown — on that day We shall gather the criminals blue-eyed. · They will speak low among themselves: 'You stayed no more than ten.' · We know best what they say, when the most exemplary of them in his way will say: 'You stayed no more than a day.'**"

### زُرْق — the indefiniteness of the word

The root **ز-ر-ق**: **a colour inclining to blue.** ***Zurq* is the plural of *azraq*.**

**What the word qualifies here has been disputed:**

| Reading | What *zurqā* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The colour of the eyes** — the eyes turning from fear or from punishment |
| 2 | **The colour of the bodies** — a bluish tinge from thirst and terror |
| 3 | **A defect of sight** — the eye's becoming unable to make out light |

**I do not impose a preference.** **And the word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**I record this as an observation:** on whichever reading, the verse names that day by a **colour.** **And this lies in the same field as the gathering *aʿmā* (blind) that will come at the end of the sūra** (124). **Both verses give the gathering with a quality having to do with the eye.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

### يَتَخَٰفَتُونَ — the lowering of the voice

The root **خ-ف-ت**: **to drop the voice, to lower it.** **Form VI, *takhāfut*: to speak with one another in lowered voices.**

**And this is one link of the sūra's axis of sound:**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *Yaʿlamu's-sirra wa **akhfā*** | **The secret of the secret is known** |
| **62** | *Wa **asarrū**'n-**najwā*** | **Whispering is concealed** |
| **103** | *Ya**takhāfatūna** baynahum* | **Speaking in lowered voices** |
| **108** | *Fa-lā tasmaʿu illā **hamsā*** | **The faintest sound** |

**Four verses, four different words, one axis.** **This is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.**

### Two estimates: عَشْرًا and يَوْمًا

**A point of word order must be recorded: two estimates are given, and the second is the shorter.**

**And the one who gives the second estimate is described: *amthaluhum ṭarīqatan* — "the most exemplary of them in his way".**

***Amthal* — an elative from the root *mithl*: "most exemplary, most correct".** **And the word *ṭarīqa* occurred in the sixty-third verse:** *wa yadhhabā bi-**ṭarīqatikumu'l-muthlā***. **The same two words, in two different places.**

**I record this as an observation:** what is defended in this world as "the most exemplary way" and the man named in the hereafter as "the soundest in judgement" are designated **by the same words.** **This is a verbal overlap; I draw no connection of meaning.**

**And the work of the estimates must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** both estimates are **wrong** — and the verse does not say which is right. **The only thing it says is that the one thought soundest names the shortest span.** **That is, the measure is not the length of the time but how short it looks when one turns and looks back.**

**The same measure was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` on 46/35** (*ka-annahum yawma yarawna mā yūʿadūna lam yalbathū illā sāʿatan min nahār*), **and it was recorded there: the measure is taken from the daytime — that is, from the time a person passes awake and aware.** **I rely on that treatment.**

## 20/105-108 — وَيَسْـَٔلُونَكَ عَنِ ٱلْجِبَالِ فَقُلْ يَنسِفُهَا رَبِّى نَسْفًا · فَيَذَرُهَا قَاعًا صَفْصَفًا · لَّا تَرَىٰ فِيهَا عِوَجًا وَلَآ أَمْتًا · يَوْمَئِذٍ يَتَّبِعُونَ ٱلدَّاعِىَ لَا عِوَجَ لَهُۥ وَخَشَعَتِ ٱلْأَصْوَاتُ لِلرَّحْمَٰنِ فَلَا تَسْمَعُ إِلَّا هَمْسًا

*Wa yasʾalūnaka ʿani'l-jibāli fa-qul yansifuhā rabbī nasfā · Fa-yadharuhā qāʿan ṣafṣafā · Lā tarā fīhā ʿiwajan wa lā amtā · Yawmaʾidhin yattabiʿūna'd-dāʿiya lā ʿiwaja lah, wa khashaʿati'l-aṣwātu li'r-Raḥmāni fa-lā tasmaʿu illā hamsā*

"**They ask you about the mountains. Say: 'My Lord will scatter them utterly. · He will leave their places a level, empty plain. · You will see in it neither a hollow nor a rise.' · On that day they will follow the caller, in whom there is no crookedness. Voices will be hushed before the Merciful; you will hear nothing but the faintest sound.**"

### نَسْفًا — and the ninety-seventh verse

**The root ن-س-ف was treated in the discussion of the ninety-seventh verse, relying on `077-murselat.md` on 77/10.** **And the overlap between the two verses was tabulated there:** the same verb, the same object of cognate origin (*nasfā*), once of the calf and once of the mountains.

**What belongs here is the question itself.** *Wa **yasʾalūnaka** ʿani'l-jibāl* — **a question is asked.**

**I record this as an observation:** the answer does not stop at telling what was asked about — **it also describes what will be left in the mountain's place.** **And the description is given in three words.**

### قَاعًا صَفْصَفًا · لَّا تَرَىٰ فِيهَا عِوَجًا وَلَآ أَمْتًا

**Four words, and all four are worth recording. Three of them occur only here in the Qurʾān.**

| Word | Root | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| ***Qāʿ*** | ق-و-ع | **Flat, empty ground** — a plain with no vegetation on it |
| ***Ṣafṣaf*** | ص-ف-ف | **Perfectly level** — like a single rank, no part of it distinct from another |
| ***ʿIwaj*** | ع-و-ج | **A crookedness that is not visible** |
| ***Amt*** | أ-م-ت | **A rise, a slight swelling** |

**The word *amt* occurs only here in the Qurʾān**, and the lexicographers explain it as **the smallest elevation in the ground.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the fineness of the words:** the verse describes the levelness **twice positively and twice negatively.** **And the two things chosen for the negative description are things the eye can barely distinguish: an invisible crookedness and a small rise.** **That is, the levelness is described from the smallest measure** — just as, at `019-meryem.md` on 19/98, absence is measured **from the faintest trace of sound.**

### عِوَج — twice, in two different places

**The root ع-و-ج was treated at `039-zumer.md` on 39/28** (*qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan **ghayra dhī ʿiwaj***), **and it was recorded there: *ʿiwaj* is used of a crookedness invisible to the eye, *ʿawaj* of a visible one — the lexicographers record this distinction.** **The reading recorded at al-Zumar was this: when it would have been enough to say of the text that it is "not crooked", the choice of the word that negates invisible crookedness carries the claim a step further.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is that the word occurs twice in this sūra, and the distribution is worth recording:**

| Verse | Expression | Whose crookedness |
|---|---|---|
| **107** | *Lā tarā fīhā **ʿiwajan** wa lā amtā* | **The earth** — it will be perfectly level |
| **108** | *Yattabiʿūna'd-dāʿiya **lā ʿiwaja lah*** | **The caller** — there is no crookedness in his call |

**Two consecutive verses, the same word.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this adjacency:** the verse first removes the crookedness of **the ground**, then negates the crookedness of **the call.** **That is, on that day there is no place to swerve, neither in the ground nor in the summons.**

**And together with the usage in al-Zumar three links emerge:**

| Place | What *ʿiwaj* concerns |
|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/28 | **The Qurʾān** — no crookedness in the text |
| Ṭāhā 20/107 | **The earth** — no crookedness in the ground that day |
| Ṭāhā 20/108 | **The caller** — no crookedness in the call |

**The same word: a text, a ground, a summons.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

### هَمْسًا — and a comparison with رِكْزًا

The root **ه-م-س** — **is analysed here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **a very faint sound, one heard only with difficulty.** The concrete uses of the root are recorded: **the rustle a foot makes as it treads the ground**, and **the sound made by the moving of the lips, in which no word can be made out.** *Hams* — a sound below even a whisper.

**The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**And the word *rikz* was treated at `019-meryem.md` on 19/98** — the description recorded there: **a very faint sound, one heard only with difficulty; a whisper.** **That too occurs only once in the Qurʾān.** **I rely on that treatment and compare the two words:**

| | ***Rikz*** — Maryam 19/98 | ***Hams*** — Ṭāhā 20/108 |
|---|---|---|
| The picture in the root | **ر-ك-ز**: to drive something into the ground, to hide it; *rikāz* — buried treasure | **ه-م-س**: the rustle of a footfall, the stirring of lips |
| Its work in the sentence | ***Aw tasmaʿu lahum rikzā*** — a question: do you hear a sound from them? | ***Fa-lā tasmaʿu illā hamsā*** — an exception: only this is heard |
| What it measures | **Absence** — no trace at all is left | **Presence** — this is the only sound remaining |
| Whose sound | **Generations destroyed** | **People on the day of reckoning** |
| The verb | ***Tasmaʿu*** — the same verb | ***Tasmaʿu*** — the same verb |

**Both words name the lowest threshold of sound, and both are used with the same verb (*tasmaʿu*).** **The difference is in their function in the sentence:** *rikz* is **a measure of absence**, *hams* **a measure of what remains.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the construction of the two sentences:** in Maryam the word draws **the limit of the negative** — "not even this much is there." In Ṭāhā the word draws **the limit of the positive** — "only this much is there." **The same measure, used from two opposite directions.**

### وَخَشَعَتِ ٱلْأَصْوَاتُ

The root **خ-ش-ع**: **to be lowered, to bow down and shrink back.** **The root was treated at `023-muminun.md` on 23/2 (*alladhīna hum fī ṣalātihim khāshiʿūn*) and at `068-kalem.md`;** I rely on those treatments.

**A point of word order must be recorded: the subject of the verb is *al-aṣwāt* (the voices).** **That is, what is lowered is not the person but the sound itself.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure, and the same technique has been seen elsewhere in the series:** at `028-kasas.md` on 28/66 (*fa-ʿamiyat ʿalayhimu'l-anbāʾ*) **the subject of the blindness was made the reports.** **The same method: the verb is taken from the person and given to what belongs to the person.**

## 20/109-112 — يَوْمَئِذٍ لَّا تَنفَعُ ٱلشَّفَٰعَةُ إِلَّا مَنْ أَذِنَ لَهُ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ وَرَضِىَ لَهُۥ قَوْلًا · يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِهِۦ عِلْمًا · وَعَنَتِ ٱلْوُجُوهُ لِلْحَىِّ ٱلْقَيُّومِ وَقَدْ خَابَ مَنْ حَمَلَ ظُلْمًا · وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِنَ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ فَلَا يَخَافُ ظُلْمًا وَلَا هَضْمًا

*Yawmaʾidhin lā tanfaʿu'sh-shafāʿatu illā man adhina lahu'r-Raḥmānu wa raḍiya lahu qawlā · Yaʿlamu mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum wa lā yuḥīṭūna bihi ʿilmā · Wa ʿanati'l-wujūhu li'l-ḥayyi'l-qayyūm, wa qad khāba man ḥamala ẓulmā · Wa man yaʿmal mina'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti wa huwa muʾminun fa-lā yakhāfu ẓulman wa lā haḍmā*

"**On that day intercession will not avail, save from one to whom the Merciful has given leave and with whose word He is pleased. · He knows what is before them and what is behind them, and they do not encompass Him in knowledge. · Faces will be humbled before the Living, the Sustainer. Whoever carries a load of wrong has surely lost. · And whoever does righteous works as a believer fears neither wrong done to him nor any curtailing of his due.**"

### The two conditions of intercession

**The verse lays down two conditions, and they are distinct:**

| Condition | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Adhina lahu'r-Raḥmān* | **Leave** — for intercession to be made |
| 2 | *Wa **raḍiya** lahu qawlā* | **Acceptance** — of what is said |

**I record this as an observation:** having been given leave is not enough; **the acceptance of what is said is named separately as well.**

**The binding of intercession to leave was treated at `002-bakara.md` on 2/255 and at `019-meryem.md` on 19/87;** I rely on those treatments. **In Maryam the condition was *ʿahd*** (*illā mani'ttakhadha ʿinda'r-Raḥmāni ʿahdā*); **in Ṭāhā, leave and acceptance.**

**And the root ر-ض-و must be recorded. This is one of the sūra's quiet links:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **84** | *Wa ʿajiltu ilayka rabbi **li-tarḍā*** | **Moses** — "that You might be pleased" |
| **109** | *Wa **raḍiya** lahu qawlā* | **The condition of intercession** |
| **130** | *Sabbiḥ … **laʿallaka tarḍā*** | **To the Prophet** — "perhaps you will be pleased" |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the three verses carry the same root:** in the eighty-fourth verse Moses was **hastening** for the sake of good pleasure; in the hundred and thirtieth **patience and glorification** are shown to the Prophet as the road to good pleasure. **The same goal, two different roads** — and it is directly connected with the sūra's link of haste (83-84-114).

### مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ

**The expression was treated at `041-fussilet.md` on 41/25 and 41/42 and mentioned again at `019-meryem.md` on 19/64; it was recorded there that the pair is used to cover every aspect of a thing.** **I rely on those treatments.**

**What belongs here is the second half of the sentence: *wa lā yuḥīṭūna bihi ʿilmā*.**

**A point of word order must be recorded: two clauses are built facing one another.**

| Side | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|
| **He** | *Yaʿlamu* — knows | **What is before them and behind them** |
| **They** | *Lā **yuḥīṭūna** bihi ʿilmā* — do not encompass | **Him** |

***Iḥāṭa* — root ح-و-ط: to surround a thing on every side.** **That is, the sentence negates not knowledge but the encompassing reach of knowledge.** *Bihi ʿilmā* — "in respect of knowledge".

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the verse does not treat human knowledge as nothing at all. **It treats the encompassing as nothing.** **And the opposite of this occurred in the ninety-eighth verse:** *wasiʿa kulla shayʾin **ʿilmā*** — "He encompasses all things in knowledge."

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **98** | ***Wasiʿa** kulla shayʾin ʿilmā* | **God** — there is encompassing |
| **110** | *Wa lā **yuḥīṭūna** bihi ʿilmā* | **People** — there is no encompassing |

**This is a contrast that can be verified within the sūra.**

**And the prayer in the hundred and fourteenth verse must be read together with these two:** *wa qul rabbi **zidnī ʿilmā***. **Three verses, the same word: an encompassing, an inability to encompass, and a request for increase.** **I record this as one of the most striking links in the sūra.**

### وَعَنَتِ ٱلْوُجُوهُ لِلْحَىِّ ٱلْقَيُّومِ

The root **ع-ن-و** — **occurs here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to bow, to submit, to fall captive.** *ʿĀnī* — a captive. **The word conveys a bowing not of one's own will but under compulsion.**

**And the subject of the verb must be recorded: *al-wujūh* (the faces).** **That is, what is bowed is the most visible and most guarded part of a person.**

**I record this as an observation:** the hundred and eleventh verse makes **faces** its subject, the hundred and eighth **voices.** **Neither is the person himself, but something belonging to the person.** This can be verified from the text as a choice of narration in the sūra.

**ٱلْحَىِّ ٱلْقَيُّوم** — **two names together.** **This pair was treated at `002-bakara.md` on 2/255 (the Verse of the Throne);** I rely on that treatment.

**And I record a datum of the text: this pair occurs in three places in the Qurʾān** — al-Baqara 2/255, Āl ʿImrān 3/2 and **Ṭāhā 20/111.** **And the occurrence in Ṭāhā, together with the hundred and tenth verse, carries two separate sentences of the Verse of the Throne side by side:**

| The Verse of the Throne (2/255) | **Ṭāhā** |
|---|---|
| *Al-ḥayyu'l-qayyūm* | **111** — *Li'l-ḥayyi'l-qayyūm* |
| *Yaʿlamu mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum* | **110** — the same sentence |
| *Wa lā yuḥīṭūna bi-shayʾin min ʿilmihi illā bimā shāʾ* | **110** — *wa lā yuḥīṭūna bihi ʿilmā* |
| *Man dha'lladhī yashfaʿu ʿindahu illā bi-idhnih* | **109** — *illā man adhina lahu'r-Raḥmān* |

**Four elements, within three verses.** **This is an overlap between two sūras that can be verified, and it is a countable datum.** **I make no claim about intent; I record the overlap.**

### ظُلْمًا وَلَا هَضْمًا

**Two words, two different kinds of injustice:**

| Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| ***Ẓulm*** | ظ-ل-م | **The loading on of something not deserved** |
| ***Haḍm*** | ه-ض-م | **The curtailing of something deserved** |

**The root ه-ض-م — occurs here for the first time in the series.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to break a thing and press it inward, to diminish it.** From the same root come *haḍm* (the grinding down and reduction of food in digestion) and *haḍīm* (slender-waisted). **The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two words are named separately:** injustice has two faces — **to load on more** and **to cut from what is due.** **The verse negates both of them separately.** **And the asymmetry recorded at `028-kasas.md` on 28/84 attaches here:** the one who brings a good deed is given more, the one who brings an evil deed exactly its like. **Read together, the two verses yield an order in which there is neither curtailing nor over-loading, but in which more may be given.**

## 20/113 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ قُرْءَانًا عَرَبِيًّا وَصَرَّفْنَا فِيهِ مِنَ ٱلْوَعِيدِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ أَوْ يُحْدِثُ لَهُمْ ذِكْرًا

*Wa kadhālika anzalnāhu qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan wa ṣarrafnā fīhi mina'l-waʿīdi laʿallahum yattaqūna aw yuḥdithu lahum dhikrā*

"**Thus have We sent it down as an Arabic Qurʾān and turned about in it many a warning, that perhaps they may guard themselves, or it may bring about in them a remembrance.**"

### صَرَّفْنَا — taṣrīf

The root **ص-ر-ف**: **to turn a thing, to change its direction.** **Form II, *taṣrīf*: to show a thing from its different faces by turning it about.**

**The notion was treated at `046-ahkaf.md`, at `043-zuhruf.md` and at `045-casiye.md`, and compared with *tawṣīl* (delivering link by link) at `028-kasas.md` on 28/51.** **I rely on those treatments and do not repeat the root analysis.**

**What belongs here is the object of the *taṣrīf*: *mina'l-waʿīd* — "of the warnings".** **That is, what is set out by turning it about is here specifically threat and warning.**

### أَوْ يُحْدِثُ لَهُمْ ذِكْرًا

**Two purposes are named, and the second is worth recording.**

The root **ح-د-ث**: **to be new, to come about afterwards.** Form IV, *iḥdāth*: **to bring about something new.**

**A point of word order: the agent of the verb is *it* (the Qurʾān), its object *dhikrā*, and the object is indefinite.** **That is, "it brings about a new remembrance."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** the first purpose is *yattaqūn* (that they may guard themselves) — the continuation of an existing conduct. **The second purpose is the coming to be of something new.** **That is, the text names two different degrees of the effect expected of it.**

**And the root *dhikr* stands here as one of its eight occurrences in the sūra** — and it is striking that **the sūra's last block (124) will name turning away with precisely this word.**

## 20/114 — فَتَعَٰلَى ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمَلِكُ ٱلْحَقُّ وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ مِن قَبْلِ أَن يُقْضَىٰٓ إِلَيْكَ وَحْيُهُۥ وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًا

*Fa-taʿāla'llāhu'l-maliku'l-ḥaqq, wa lā taʿjal bi'l-Qurʾāni min qabli an yuqḍā ilayka waḥyuh, wa qul rabbi zidnī ʿilmā*

"**Exalted is God, the True Sovereign. Do not hasten with the Qurʾān before its revelation to you is completed, and say: 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge.'**"

### The sūra's link of haste closes here

**In the eighty-third and eighty-fourth verses the root ع-ج-ل had occurred twice and was tabulated there. This is the third and last verse of the link.**

| Verse | Expression | To whom | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **83** | *Wa mā **aʿjalaka** ʿan qawmik* | **To Moses** | **A question** |
| **84** | *Wa **ʿajiltu** ilayka rabbi li-tarḍā* | **Moses's answer** | **The ground: good pleasure** |
| **114** | *Wa lā **taʿjal** bi'l-Qurʾān* | **To the Prophet** | **A prohibition** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the three verses carry the same root. The link has three notes:**

**One — neither haste is ill-intentioned.** Moses hastens for his Lord's good pleasure; the haste forbidden to the Prophet is the haste of **not losing the revelation.** **The sūra records that a well-intentioned haste can also break an order.**

**Two — in both hastes there is a *leaving behind*.** Moses left his people behind; hasty reciting leaves the completing of the revelation behind. **That is, in both cases the haste leaves something wanting behind it.**

**Three — and the sūra answers the two hastes in two different ways:** Moses is **told what has happened** (*fa-innā qad fatannā qawmaka min baʿdik*), the Prophet **what he is to do** (*wa qul rabbi zidnī ʿilmā*).

**I record this link as one of the tightest internal ties of the sūra.**

### مِن قَبْلِ أَن يُقْضَىٰٓ إِلَيْكَ وَحْيُهُۥ

***Yuqḍā* — root ق-ض-ي: to bring a matter to an end, to complete it.** **The verb is passive and imperfect.**

**And the root had occurred three times in the seventy-second verse** (*faqḍi mā anta qāḍ innamā taqḍī*) — in the sorcerers' answer. **The same root, there for the limit of a decree, here for the completing of the revelation.**

**The object of the prohibition must be recorded: hastening to recite or to take.** The verse commands waiting for the revelation to be finished.

**I record this as an observation:** the prohibition is placed not on the addressee's **desire** but on his **timing.** **What is wanted is not wrong — its turn has not been waited for.**

**The same line occurs at al-Qiyāma 75/16-19** (*lā tuḥarrik bihi lisānaka li-taʿjala bih*) **and was treated at `075-kiyame.md`.** **I rely on that treatment.** **The difference between the two verses is worth recording:** in al-Qiyāma the object of the prohibition is **the movement of the tongue**, in Ṭāhā **the haste itself.**

### وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًا

**This is one of the most cited sentences of the sūra, and where it stands must be recorded.**

**A point of word order: the prayer comes immediately after a prohibition.** *Do not hasten* — **and say: increase me in knowledge.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the adjacency of the two sentences:** the prohibition could have stood as a **subtraction** — "wait, do not take". **The prayer that follows does not remove the desire; it changes its direction.** **That is, not to hasten does not mean not to want.**

**And the choice of verb is worth recording: *zid* — "increase".** Not *ʿallimnī* (teach me); **what is asked is an addition upon what is already there.**

**And this is the last link of the sūra's axis of knowledge:**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **52** | *Lā yaḍillu rabbī wa lā yansā* | **The unerringness of knowledge** |
| **98** | *Wasiʿa kulla shayʾin ʿilmā* | **Encompassing** |
| **110** | *Wa lā yuḥīṭūna bihi ʿilmā* | **Inability to encompass** |
| **114** | *Rabbi **zidnī** ʿilmā* | **A request for increase** |

**Four verses, one word.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra first says where knowledge is complete, then that the human being cannot encompass it; **and after that an increase is asked for.** **That is, the request is built on ground where the claim to encompass has been refused.**

### What this says to the present

**What this verse says to the present lies in the standing together of two sentences.**

**On one side there is a prohibition: not to take hastily what is not yet complete.** **On the other a prayer: to ask for more.** **The two do not conflict** — because the prohibition looks to **speed**, the prayer to **quantity**.

**And I record one last datum of the text:** a *prayer for increase* commanded to the Prophet occurs in this verse **and its subject is knowledge.** **This is a verifiable record, and I build no claim upon it.**

## 20/115-116 — وَلَقَدْ عَهِدْنَآ إِلَىٰٓ ءَادَمَ مِن قَبْلُ فَنَسِىَ وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُۥ عَزْمًا · وَإِذْ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ ٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِءَادَمَ فَسَجَدُوٓا۟ إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ أَبَىٰ

*Wa laqad ʿahidnā ilā Ādama min qablu fa-nasiya wa lam najid lahu ʿazmā · Wa idh qulnā li'l-malāʾikati'sjudū li-Ādama fa-sajadū illā Iblīsa abā*

"**Indeed We made a covenant with Adam before; but he forgot, and We found in him no resolve. · And when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate to Adam', they all prostrated except Iblīs — he refused.**"

### Where this story stands in the series

**The story was treated in detail at `002-bakara.md` on 2/30-38 and taken up a second time at `038-sad.md` on 38/71-85.** **I rely on both and do not repeat them here.**

**A summary of what was treated at al-Baqara:**

- **That the prostration was not a prostration of worship** is broadly agreed; the one who gave the command is God. **"What Iblīs refused was not Adam but the command."**
- ***Istikbār* is not being great but reckoning oneself great** — the *istifʿāl* form.
- **"Unbelief is not a lack of knowledge; it is the covering over of what is known."**
- **The pattern of the prohibition: *lā taqrabā* (do not come near)** — the boundary begins not from the act but from the distance that leads to the act.
- ***Azallahumā* — *zalla*: a slip.** "The story of the human being begins not with a rebellion but with a slip."
- **That the verbs are in the dual:** the text does not lay the responsibility on one side alone. **Indeed in the discussion at al-Baqara this verse (Ṭāhā 20/120-121) was cited as evidence.**

**A summary of what was treated at Ṣād:**

- **Ṣād tells the story through Iblīs's error of reasoning:** *anā khayrun minh* — **an analogy.**
- **al-Baqara tells it through what the human being is (a vicegerent, one who knows the names), Ṣād through what Iblīs did.**

### The difference between the three sūras

**Now I write what belongs here, and the table can be verified from the text:**

| | **al-Baqara 2/30-38** | **Ṣād 38/71-85** | **Ṭāhā 20/115-123** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | *Innī jāʿilun fi'l-arḍi **khalīfa*** | *Innī **khāliqun** basharan min ṭīn* | ***Wa laqad ʿahidnā ilā Ādam*** |
| The angels' question | **Present** | Absent | Absent |
| The names | **Present** | Absent | Absent |
| Iblīs's ground | Absent | ***Anā khayrun minh*** — an analogy | Absent |
| Iblīs's act | *Abā wa'**stakbara*** | ***Istakbara*** | ***Abā*** — a single verb |
| Adam's act | ***Azallahumā*'sh-shayṭān** — a slip | Not told | ***Fa-nasiya*** — forgetting |
| What was lacking | — | — | ***Wa lam najid lahu ʿazmā*** |
| The whispering | *Fa-**azallahumā*** | Absent | ***Fa-waswasa ilayhi'sh-shayṭān*** — its words are given too |
| What was promised | Absent | Absent | ***Shajarati'l-khuldi wa mulkin lā yablā*** |
| The outcome | *Fa-talaqqā Ādamu … fa-tāba ʿalayh* | *Fa'l-ḥaqqu wa'l-ḥaqqa aqūl* | ***Thumma'jtabāhu rabbuhu fa-tāba ʿalayhi wa hadā*** |

**Three sūras, three different namings of the same event. And this is a countable datum:**

| Sūra | The name given to the matter |
|---|---|
| **al-Baqara** | ***Zalla*** — a slip |
| **Ṣād** | ***Analogy*** — an error of reasoning (on Iblīs's side) |
| **Ṭāhā** | ***Nisyān*** — forgetting, and **a lack of *ʿazm*** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the three texts' own words:** the three tellings do not conflict — **they name three different layers of the same event.** The slip tells the **act**, the analogy **the other side's ground**, the forgetting **the state.**

### فَنَسِىَ — and a frame like the name of the sūra

**The root ن-س-ي is one of the spines of this sūra, and its four occurrences were tabulated in the discussion of 20/49-52.** **Here is the third link.**

**And two readings of the word's sense are transmitted:**

| Reading | What *nasiya* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Really to forget** — the covenant slipping from the mind |
| 2 | **To abandon** — since the root is used in the Qurʾān in the sense of "to leave" as well |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The ground of the second reading is that the same root is used in verse 126 in the form *kadhālika'l-yawma tunsā* ("so today are you left")** — where the sense "to be left" fits better than "to be forgotten".

**I record this as my own reading and give it as a proposal of a frame; it is not binding:** the sūra names the human being's first story with **forgetting**; it ties the ground of the prayer to **remembrance** (14); and it describes turning away as **turning away from remembrance** (124). **That is, the sūra's description of the human being is built upon memory.** **And in the fifty-second verse the opposite of this had already been set down:** *lā yaḍillu rabbī **wa lā yansā***.

### وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُۥ عَزْمًا

**The root ع-ز-م was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` on 46/35 (*ulu'l-ʿazmi mina'r-rusul*)**, and it was recorded there: **to resolve definitely upon a matter, resolve; the sense "to cut off" is also recorded in the root.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the two verses can be set side by side; this is a countable overlap:**

| Place | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/35 | *Ulu'l-**ʿazm**i mina'r-rusul* | **Certain messengers** — possessors of resolve |
| **Ṭāhā 20/115** | *Wa lam najid lahu **ʿazmā*** | **Adam** — no resolve was found |

**The same word: in one place the granting of a quality, in the other its absence.**

**A point of word order must be recorded: *wa lam najid lahu ʿazmā* — "We did not find in him".** **The sentence does not say "he had no resolve"; it says "We did not find".** **The verb is a verb of ascertainment.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** the sentence conveys not an accusation but a **measurement.** **And *ʿazm* comes indefinite: "a resolve" — that is, resolve in a particular matter.**

**And the standing together of the two words is worth recording: *nisyān* and the lack of *ʿazm*.**

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two are ranged in the same sentence:** forgetting and irresolution come from the same place — **an inability to keep hold of something.** **And the sūra's remedy is given from the same place:** remembrance (*dhikr*) and the binding of the prayer to fixed times (14, 130, 132).

### إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ أَبَىٰ — and the fifty-sixth verse

**The verb must be recorded: *abā*, standing alone.** In al-Baqara it was *abā wa'stakbara*, in Ṣād *istakbara*; **Ṭāhā says only *abā*.**

**And the same verb was used of Pharaoh in the fifty-sixth verse of this sūra** — tabulated in the discussion of 20/56-59: *fa-kadhdhaba **wa abā***.

**This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and it binds the sūra's two stories together with a single verb.**

## 20/117-119 — فَقُلْنَا يَٰٓـَٔادَمُ إِنَّ هَٰذَا عَدُوٌّ لَّكَ وَلِزَوْجِكَ فَلَا يُخْرِجَنَّكُمَا مِنَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ فَتَشْقَىٰٓ · إِنَّ لَكَ أَلَّا تَجُوعَ فِيهَا وَلَا تَعْرَىٰ · وَأَنَّكَ لَا تَظْمَؤُا۟ فِيهَا وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ

*Fa-qulnā yā Ādamu inna hādhā ʿaduwwun laka wa li-zawjika fa-lā yukhrijannakumā mina'l-jannati fa-tashqā · Inna laka allā tajūʿa fīhā wa lā taʿrā · Wa annaka lā taẓmaʾu fīhā wa lā taḍḥā*

"**So We said: 'Adam! This is an enemy to you and to your wife. Let him not drive the two of you out of the garden, or you will fall into distress. · It is given you not to be hungry there and not to be naked. · And that you will not thirst there nor be scorched by the sun.'**"

### فَتَشْقَىٰ — the strongest link in the sūra

**This word is from the same root and on the same pattern as *li-tashqā* in the sūra's second verse. It was tabulated in the section on structure; here I complete it.**

| Verse | Expression | To whom | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | *Mā anzalnā ʿalayka'l-Qurʾāna **li-tashqā*** | **To the Prophet** | **Negation** — this is not the purpose |
| **117** | *Fa-lā yukhrijannakumā mina'l-jannati **fa-tashqā*** | **To Adam** | **Warning** — if you are driven out it happens |
| **123** | *Fa-mani'ttabaʿa hudāya fa-lā yaḍillu **wa lā yashqā*** | **To everyone** | **Condition** — whoever follows does not fall |

**I record this as the strongest datum of the text in the sūra, and my own reading is this:**

**In its second verse the sūra said that the Book does not load *shaqā* onto the one addressed.** **In the hundred and seventeenth it names the real source of *shaqā*: being driven out of the garden, that is, falling outside the place one is in.** **And in the hundred and twenty-third it joins the two: whoever follows the guidance does not become *shaqī*.**

**That is, the sūra opens with "the Qurʾān is not a burden to you" and closes with "the burden is in turning away from it".** **This reading rests on the verbal overlap of the three verses and is verifiable.**

**And a detail of word order must be recorded, and it is easily missed:** the prohibition is attached to the **dual** (*yukhrijannakumā* — the two of you), while the consequence is attached to the **singular** (*fa-tashqā* — you will fall into distress).

**Different views of the explanation of this are transmitted:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Harmony of the verse-endings** — all the sūra's verse-endings close with *alif maqṣūra*; the dual form does not fit |
| 2 | **Responsibility for provision** — the one addressed is the one who will carry the burden of livelihood enumerated in the following verses |
| 3 | **Naming the whole by a part** — one is named and both are meant |

**I do not impose a preference.** **The first explanation is an observation that can be verified from the text** (the sūra's scheme of verse-endings), **but it cannot be counted a cause on its own.**

### Four favours, four absences

**The hundred and eighteenth and hundred and nineteenth verses describe what it means to be in the garden by four negations:**

| Order | Expression | Absence of what | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Allā **tajūʿa*** | **Hunger** | **Inward** — food |
| 2 | *Wa lā **taʿrā*** | **Nakedness** | **Outward** — clothing |
| 3 | *Lā **taẓmaʾu*** | **Thirst** | **Inward** — drink |
| 4 | *Wa lā **taḍḥā*** | **Scorching by the sun** | **Outward** — shelter |

**A point of word order must be recorded, and it is striking: the four needs are ranged crosswise — inward, outward, inward, outward.**

**And the pairing is worth recording:** hunger with nakedness in one verse, thirst with the sun in the other. **That is, each verse carries one inward and one outward need.**

The root **ض-ح-و**: **the sun's rising high, being left without shade.** *Ḍuḥā* — the forenoon; **and *taḍḥā* — to be left under the sun without shade.** **The root was treated at `093-duha.md` in connection with the name of that sūra;** I rely on that treatment. **And the same word occurred as a time of day in the fifty-ninth verse of this sūra:** *wa an yuḥshara'n-nāsu **ḍuḥā***.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of the four words:** the verses do not enumerate the favours of the garden — **they enumerate the four basic hardships of life on earth.** **Food, clothing, water, shelter.** **That is, the concrete content of the word *shaqā* is given here:** the necessity of chasing after the four needs that constitute a human life.

**And in the context of `020-taha.md` this joins with the second verse:** the lexical sense of *shaqā* was **"to undergo hardship, to toil"** — tabulated in the discussion of 20/2-3. **The hundred and eighteenth and hundred and nineteenth verses enumerate the four items of that toil.** **This is a completion that can be verified within the sūra.**

## 20/120-123 — فَوَسْوَسَ إِلَيْهِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ قَالَ يَٰٓـَٔادَمُ هَلْ أَدُلُّكَ عَلَىٰ شَجَرَةِ ٱلْخُلْدِ وَمُلْكٍ لَّا يَبْلَىٰ · فَأَكَلَا مِنْهَا فَبَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْءَٰتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِن وَرَقِ ٱلْجَنَّةِ وَعَصَىٰٓ ءَادَمُ رَبَّهُۥ فَغَوَىٰ · ثُمَّ ٱجْتَبَٰهُ رَبُّهُۥ فَتَابَ عَلَيْهِ وَهَدَىٰ · قَالَ ٱهْبِطَا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًۢا بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّى هُدًى فَمَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ هُدَاىَ فَلَا يَضِلُّ وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ

*Fa-waswasa ilayhi'sh-shayṭānu qāla yā Ādamu hal adulluka ʿalā shajarati'l-khuldi wa mulkin lā yablā · Fa-akalā minhā fa-badat lahumā sawʾātuhumā wa ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni ʿalayhimā min waraqi'l-jannah, wa ʿaṣā Ādamu rabbahu fa-ghawā · Thumma'jtabāhu rabbuhu fa-tāba ʿalayhi wa hadā · Qāla'hbiṭā minhā jamīʿan baʿḍukum li-baʿḍin ʿaduww, fa-immā yaʾtiyannakum minnī hudan fa-mani'ttabaʿa hudāya fa-lā yaḍillu wa lā yashqā*

"**Then Satan whispered to him: 'Adam! Shall I show you the tree of everlastingness and a kingdom that does not decay?' he said. · The two of them ate from it, and their nakedness was shown to them, and they began to press leaves of the garden over themselves. Adam disobeyed his Lord and went astray. · Then his Lord chose him, turned to him in mercy, and guided. · 'Go down from it, all of you, some of you enemies to others,' He said. 'And when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance neither goes astray nor falls into distress.'**"

### The words of the whispering

**Ṭāhā is the only place among the three tellings that gives the content of the whispering** — tabulated in the discussion of 20/115-116. **And what is said must be recorded.**

*Hal adulluka ʿalā shajarati'l-khuldi wa mulkin lā yablā* — "shall I show you **the tree of everlastingness** and **a kingdom that does not decay**?"

| Promise | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Shajarati'l-**khuld*** | **Everlastingness** — not to die |
| 2 | *Mulkin **lā yablā*** | **A kingdom that does not decay** — not to lose |

The root **ب-ل-ي**: **to wear out, to fray and decay.** *Balā* is used of a garment's wearing out.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the content of the two promises:** the offer does not make breaking the prohibition attractive **in itself.** **It promises two things, and both promise the removal of a lack: death and losing.** **That is, the whispering aims at the human being's limit.**

**And the offer is framed, worth recording, as a sentence of showing the way: *hal adulluka ʿalā…* — "shall I show you…?"**

**And the same verb occurred in the fortieth verse of this sūra — in the mouth of Moses's sister:** *hal **adullukum** ʿalā man yakfuluh* — "shall I show you someone who will take charge of him?"

| Verse | Expression | Who | What is shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **40** | *Hal **adullukum** ʿalā man yakfuluh* | **Moses's sister** | **The mother** — protection |
| **120** | *Hal **adulluka** ʿalā shajarati'l-khuld* | **Satan** | **The tree** — loss |

**The same pattern, in two different places.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra uses the same sentence-pattern in two offers, one rescuing and one ruinous. **The form of the offer is the same; where it leads is different.** **This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.**

### فَبَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْءَٰتُهُمَا — the dual

**All the verbs are dual: *akalā* (the two ate), *badat lahumā* (was shown to the two), *sawʾātuhumā* (the nakedness of the two), *ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni* (the two began to press over).**

**This note was entered at `002-bakara.md` on 2/35-36, and precisely this verse was cited there as evidence:** *"There is no expression anywhere in the Qurʾān that blames Eve separately, that positions her as a seducer, or that lays the responsibility on her… In Sūrat Ṭāhā the one whispered to is Adam directly (20/120), and then 'the two of them ate from it' (20/121). The woman is not presented as an intermediary."* **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**What is to be added here is a datum of word order:** in the hundred and twentieth verse the whispering is done to **the singular** (*ilayhi*, *yā Ādamu*, *adulluka*), **the verb turns to the dual** (*fa-akalā*), **and at the end of the hundred and twenty-first it comes back down to the singular** (*wa ʿaṣā Ādamu rabbah*). **This is a shift of number within the verse itself, and it is verifiable.**

**يَخْصِفَان** — root **خ-ص-ف**: **to patch one thing over another, to lay them over each other and stitch.** The lexicographers explain the word through **the mending of shoes** (*khaṣf*): laying a new piece over a worn place.

**I record this as an observation:** the verb tells of the leaves **being laid one over another** — that is, a covering is not being sewn, **it is being closed up piece by piece.**

**And there is a verbal connection with the twelfth verse:** there it said *fakhlaʿ naʿlayka* (take off your sandals). **Both verses have verbs of putting on and taking off; I record this only as a verbal overlap and draw no connection of meaning.**

### وَعَصَىٰٓ ءَادَمُ رَبَّهُۥ فَغَوَىٰ

**The sentence must be recorded because it is plain and has not been softened.**

The root **ع-ص-ي**: **not to obey a command.** The root **غ-و-ي**: **not to find what is right, to lose one's way;** the lexicographers record a difference between *ghayy* and *ḍalāl* — *ghayy* is also used of **taking the wrong road while knowing better.**

**And a point of word order must be recorded:** the sentence ranges three separate things one after another, and **the second verse answers it:**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **121** | *Wa **ʿaṣā** Ādamu rabbahu **fa-ghawā*** | **The act and its consequence** |
| **122** | *Thumma'**jtabāhu** rabbuhu fa-**tāba** ʿalayhi **wa hadā*** | **Three responses** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the adjacency of the two verses:** the text does not cover the event over — **and because it does not, the three verbs that follow gain their weight.**

**And the principle recorded at `002-bakara.md` on 2/37 holds here too:** *"the possibility of return is a gift given afterwards to the one who wants to return."* **In Ṭāhā this is given with three verbs:**

| Verb | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Ijtabāhu* | ج-ب-ي | **He chose him** — *ijtibāʾ*: to gather a thing up and select it |
| *Fa-tāba ʿalayhi* | ت-و-ب | **He accepted his repentance** |
| *Wa **hadā*** | ه-د-ي | **He guided** |

**And the third verb must be recorded: *hadā*.** **This is the last word of the answer in the fiftieth verse of the sūra** (*thumma hadā*) **and the verb negated of Pharaoh in the seventy-ninth** (*wa mā hadā*).

| Verse | *Hadā* | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **50** | *Thumma hadā* | **God** — after creation |
| **79** | *Wa mā hadā* | **Pharaoh** — negated |
| **122** | *Wa hadā* | **God** — after repentance |

**The same verb, the same form, in three separate places in the sūra.** **I record this as my own reading:** in the fiftieth verse the guiding came after **creation**; in the hundred and twenty-second it comes after **repentance**. **The sūra attaches the same verb to both beginnings.**

### فَلَا يَضِلُّ وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ — the sūra's two roots meet here

**The hundred and twenty-third verse gathers the sūra's two spinal roots into a single sentence:**

| Root | Its occurrences in the sūra |
|---|---|
| **ض-ل-ل** | 52 (*lā yaḍillu rabbī*), 79 (*wa aḍalla Firʿawnu qawmah*), 85 (*wa aḍallahumu's-sāmiriyy*), 92 (*idh raʾaytahum ḍallū*), **123** |
| **ش-ق-و** | 2 (*li-tashqā*), 117 (*fa-tashqā*), **123** |

**And the structure of the sentence must be recorded: a condition and two consequences.**

*Fa-mani'ttabaʿa hudāya* — **the condition**; *fa-lā yaḍillu* — **the first consequence**; *wa lā yashqā* — **the second consequence.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verbs are named separately:** *ḍalāl* is a loss of **direction**, *shaqā* a disorder of **state**. **One is not knowing where one is, the other struggling in the place where one is.** **The verse removes both at once.**

**And the word *hudā* occurs twice in the verse:** *hudan* (indefinite — "a guidance") and *hudāya* (definite — "My guidance"). **The first is what will come, the second what is to be followed.**

**The same descent and promise were treated at `002-bakara.md` on 2/38**, and it was recorded there: *"The human being is not abandoned to the earth; he is sent to the earth, and it is said that a guide will be sent after him."* **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the difference between the two verses is worth recording:**

| | al-Baqara 2/38 | **Ṭāhā 20/123** |
|---|---|---|
| The consequence | *Fa-lā **khawfun** ʿalayhim wa lā hum **yaḥzanūn*** | *Fa-lā **yaḍillu** wa lā **yashqā*** |
| What is removed | **Fear and grief** — feelings | **Straying and distress** — direction and state |

**Two sūras give the same promise with two different pairs of words.** **And the second word Ṭāhā chooses is the word of the sūra's second verse.** **This is the sūra's most closed link, and it can be verified from the text.**

## 20/124-127 — وَمَنْ أَعْرَضَ عَن ذِكْرِى فَإِنَّ لَهُۥ مَعِيشَةً ضَنكًا وَنَحْشُرُهُۥ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ أَعْمَىٰ · قَالَ رَبِّ لِمَ حَشَرْتَنِىٓ أَعْمَىٰ وَقَدْ كُنتُ بَصِيرًا · قَالَ كَذَٰلِكَ أَتَتْكَ ءَايَٰتُنَا فَنَسِيتَهَا وَكَذَٰلِكَ ٱلْيَوْمَ تُنسَىٰ · وَكَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِى مَنْ أَسْرَفَ وَلَمْ يُؤْمِنۢ بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِۦ وَلَعَذَابُ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ أَشَدُّ وَأَبْقَىٰ

*Wa man aʿraḍa ʿan dhikrī fa-inna lahu maʿīshatan ḍankan wa naḥshuruhu yawma'l-qiyāmati aʿmā · Qāla rabbi lima ḥashartanī aʿmā wa qad kuntu baṣīrā · Qāla kadhālika atatka āyātunā fa-nasītahā, wa kadhālika'l-yawma tunsā · Wa kadhālika najzī man asrafa wa lam yuʾmin bi-āyāti rabbih, wa la-ʿadhābu'l-ākhirati ashaddu wa abqā*

"**Whoever turns away from My remembrance, for him there is a straitened livelihood, and We shall gather him blind on the Day of Resurrection. · He will say: 'My Lord! Why have You gathered me blind, when I used to see?' · He will say: 'So it is: Our signs came to you and you forgot them; and so today you are left.' · Thus do We recompense whoever goes to excess and does not believe in the signs of his Lord. And the punishment of the hereafter is more severe and more lasting.**"

### مَعِيشَةً ضَنكًا

**The root ض-ن-ك — is analysed here for the first time in the series, and the word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**The core sense the lexicographers give: narrowness, constriction.** And the concrete uses of the root are recorded:

| Use | What |
|---|---|
| *Manzilun **ḍank*** | **A narrow house** — a dwelling in which one cannot move freely |
| *ʿAyshun **ḍank*** | **A straitened livelihood** |
| *Rumḥun **ḍayyiqun***, which the lexicographers relate to *ḍank* | **Tight, not slack** |

**The lexicographers record that the root is akin to *ḍayyiq* (narrow) and explain the difference thus: *ḍayyiq* is narrowness in general, while *ḍank* is a narrowness that presses, that stifles.** **I transmit this as the lexicographers record it.**

**مَعِيشَة** — root **ع-ي-ش**: to live. ***Maʿīsha* is "the life lived, the livelihood"** — that is, not life in the abstract but **life lived from day to day.**

**Now it is necessary to dwell on what the expression tells, and the disagreement must not be concealed:**

| Reading | Where *maʿīshatan ḍankan* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **In this world** — the livelihood of the one who turns away, in this life |
| 2 | **In the grave** — the life of the *barzakh* |
| 3 | **In the hereafter** — as a punishment |
| 4 | **In all of them** — no particular place is delimited |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But I record two data of the text:**

**One — the verse gives the two recompenses separately and in different tenses:** *fa-inna lahu maʿīshatan ḍankan* (a nominal sentence, no time specified) **and** *wa **naḥshuruhu yawma'l-qiyāmati** aʿmā* (a verb, its time stated openly). **That is, the time of the second recompense is specified and that of the first is not.** **This is an indication supporting the fourth reading — but it is not a preference.**

**Two — the picture in the word is *narrowness*, not *want*.** The verse does not speak of poverty. **And indeed the Qurʾān does not count abundance of wealth a measure in itself** — as the hundred and thirty-first verse of this very sūra will say (*wa lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka ilā mā mattaʿnā bihi*).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the lexical field of the word:** *ḍank* is not **the smallness of the vessel** but **the cramping of what is inside it.** **The same quantity stifles in a narrow vessel.** **That is, what the verse describes is not the scarcity of what one has — but the impossibility of living with what one has.**

**And a link within the sūra must be recorded. In the hundredth verse the recompense of the sentence beginning with the same verb (*man aʿraḍa*) was a *load*:**

| Verse | What is turned away from | Recompense | The picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **100** | *ʿAnhu* — the Qurʾān | *Yaḥmilu … **wizrā*** | **Weight** |
| **124** | *ʿAn **dhikrī*** | *Maʿīshatan **ḍankan*** | **Narrowness** |

**Both punishments name a bodily distress: one a weight that cannot be carried, the other a narrowness in which one cannot stand.** **This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra.**

**And the exact opposite of this was treated at `094-insirah.md` on 94/1-2:** *alam nashraḥ laka **ṣadrak** · wa waḍaʿnā ʿanka **wizrak*** — **the opening of the breast and the lifting of the load.**

| al-Inshirāḥ 94/1-2 | **Ṭāhā 20/100, 124** |
|---|---|
| *Nashraḥ laka ṣadrak* — **opening** | *Maʿīshatan ḍankan* — **narrowing** |
| *Waḍaʿnā ʿanka wizrak* — **the lifting of the load** | *Yaḥmilu … wizrā* — **the loading of the load** |

**Two sūras use the same two pictures in opposite directions.** **I record this as my own reading; it is a verbal overlap between the two texts that can be verified.**

### What this says to the present

**What this verse says to the present rests in the choice of the word.**

**The verse does not say "his wealth will decrease". *Ḍank* — narrow.** **And narrowness can arise not from the quantity of what one has but from the relation one has with it.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is only the lexical field of the word:** the same income, the same house, the same day — **enough for one and not enough for another.** **The verse says where the difference lies: *ʿan dhikrī* — in turning away from remembrance.** **I do not go beyond this into building a social or psychological theory.**

### أَعْمَىٰ and بَصِيرًا

**The contrast must be recorded, and it has been prepared for from within the sūra.**

**In the ninety-sixth verse the Sāmirī's first words were a claim to sight:** *baṣurtu bimā lam yabṣurū bih* — "I saw what they did not see."

**In the hundred and twenty-fifth verse another claim to sight comes:** *wa qad kuntu **baṣīrā*** — "I used to see."

| Verse | Expression | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **96** | *Baṣurtu bimā lam yabṣurū bih* | **The Sāmirī** | **A claim of superiority** |
| **125** | *Wa qad kuntu baṣīrā* | **The one who turns away** | **An objection** |
| **35** | *Innaka kunta binā **baṣīrā*** | **Moses** — of God | **Submission** |

**The same word in three places: one a claim, one an objection, one a submission.** **This is a distribution that can be verified from the text, and I record it as my own reading.**

**And a kindred verse from within the Qurʾān must be recorded:** *fa-innahā lā taʿma'l-abṣāru wa lākin taʿma'l-qulūbu'llatī fi'ṣ-ṣudūr* (al-Ḥajj 22/46) — **"it is not the eyes that go blind, but the hearts within the breasts."** **I name this verse only as a parallel from within the Qurʾān;** I build no inference upon it and do not explain Ṭāhā's sentence by it, **because Ṭāhā's own answer is given not by way of sight but, as will be seen below, by way of *nisyān*.**

**What belongs here is the sūra's own answer. And the answer does not debate what blindness is:**

*Qāla kadhālika **atatka āyātunā fa-nasītahā**, wa kadhālika'l-yawma **tunsā***.

**A point of word order must be recorded: the answer does not say "you are blind because you did not see".** **The verb used is *nisyān*** — not sight but **forgetting.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** the objection is built on **sight**, the answer is given by way of **memory.** **That is, the verse separates what the eye saw from what the mind held:** the man had seen — *atatka āyātunā* — **but he did not hold it.**

### وَكَذَٰلِكَ ٱلْيَوْمَ تُنسَىٰ — the symmetry of the recompense

**Two verbs from the same root, facing one another:**

| Side | Verb | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| **In this world** | *Fa-**nasītahā*** | **Active** — he forgot |
| **On that day** | *Al-yawma **tunsā*** | **Passive** — he is forgotten |

**And a note is required about the sense of the second verb:** forgetting cannot be ascribed to God — indeed the fifty-second verse of this very sūra negated it openly: *lā yaḍillu rabbī **wa lā yansā***.

**Accordingly *tunsā* here has the sense of "you are left, you are abandoned"**, and it is explained thus in the classical commentaries as well. **This is a note that can be verified from within the sūra itself** — because the sentence of negation is in the same sūra.

**And the four occurrences of the root ن-س-ي in the sūra are completed here** — tabulated in the discussion of 20/49-52: 52 (negation), 88 (the calf), 115 (Adam), **126 (twice: the act and its recompense).**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra first negates forgetting of God, then establishes it in three separate states of the human being, **and at the last occurrence turns it into a recompense.**

**وَلَعَذَابُ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ أَشَدُّ وَأَبْقَىٰ** (127) — **and Pharaoh's word occurs here for the third time.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **71** | *Ayyunā ashaddu ʿadhāban **wa abqā*** | **Pharaoh** — a claim |
| **73** | *Wa'llāhu khayrun **wa abqā*** | **The sorcerers** — an answer |
| **127** | *Wa la-ʿadhābu'l-ākhirati **ashaddu wa abqā*** | **The text** — a verdict |

**The question Pharaoh asked is answered fifty-six verses later by the text's own sentence — and both words used are his own words: *ashadd* and *abqā*.** **I record this as one of the tightest links in the sūra, and it can be verified from the text.**

## 20/128-130 — أَفَلَمْ يَهْدِ لَهُمْ كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا قَبْلَهُم مِّنَ ٱلْقُرُونِ يَمْشُونَ فِى مَسَٰكِنِهِمْ · وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةٌ سَبَقَتْ مِن رَّبِّكَ لَكَانَ لِزَامًا وَأَجَلٌ مُّسَمًّى · فَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ ٱلشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا وَمِنْ ءَانَآئِ ٱلَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ وَأَطْرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ لَعَلَّكَ تَرْضَىٰ

*A-fa-lam yahdi lahum kam ahlaknā qablahum mina'l-qurūni yamshūna fī masākinihim, inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-uli'n-nuhā · Wa law lā kalimatun sabaqat min rabbika la-kāna lizāman wa ajalun musammā · Faṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūna wa sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika qabla ṭulūʿi'sh-shamsi wa qabla ghurūbihā, wa min ānāʾi'l-layli fa-sabbiḥ wa aṭrāfa'n-nahāri laʿallaka tarḍā*

"**Has it not guided them, how many generations We destroyed before them, in whose dwellings they walk about? In that there are signs for those who possess restraint. · Were it not for a word that has gone forth from your Lord and a term appointed, it would have been inevitable. · Bear patiently what they say, and glorify your Lord with praise before the rising of the sun and before its setting. Glorify Him in the watches of the night and at the ends of the day; perhaps you will be well pleased.**"

### أَفَلَمْ يَهْدِ لَهُمْ

**And the root ه-د-ي comes once more towards the end of the sūra — this time with an event as its subject:** *kam ahlaknā* — the number of generations destroyed.

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the subject of the verb is not a person but **a datum of history.** **And a verb of walking is named: *yamshūna fī masākinihim* — "they walk about in their dwellings."** That is, the remains are **seen.**

**The same line was treated at `019-meryem.md` on 19/98** (*wa kam ahlaknā qablahum min qarnin hal tuḥissu minhum min aḥad*) **and the question of the source of knowledge was treated in detail at `028-kasas.md` on 28/43-51.** **I rely on those treatments.**

**And the expression *ulu'n-nuhā* occurs here for the second time** — tabulated in the discussion of the fifty-fourth verse. **Two verses of the sūra end with the same sentence**, and both come after a call to look: one at creation, the other at ruins.

### وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةٌ سَبَقَتْ

***Kalima* — here a judgement given, a decision set down.** **And *lizām* — root ل-ز-م: to stick to a thing and not part from it; whence inevitable, necessary.**

**And two things are named: *kalimatun sabaqat* and *ajalun musammā*.**

**The same deferral was treated at `035-fatir.md` on 35/45 (*yuʾakhkhiruhum ilā ajalin musammā*);** I rely on that treatment.

### فَٱصْبِرْ — and the sūra's second verse

**The command of the block must be recorded: *faṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra's second verse:** the sūra had opened with "the Qurʾān did not come down on you for *shaqā*". **The hundred and thirtieth verse nevertheless commands the one addressed something: patience.** **The two do not conflict** — because what is negated is a **burden** and what is commanded is **endurance**. **And the object of the command is specified: *ʿalā mā yaqūlūn* — what is said.**

### A description like the five prayer times

**The times enumerated for glorification must be recorded, and the ordering is interesting:**

| Order | Expression | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Qabla **ṭulūʿi'sh-shams*** | **Before the sun rises** |
| 2 | *Wa qabla **ghurūbihā*** | **Before it sets** |
| 3 | *Wa min **ānāʾi'l-layl*** | **In some of the hours of the night** |
| 4 | *Wa **aṭrāfa'n-nahār*** | **At the ends of the day** |

**آنَاء** — the plural of *inan*: **the divisions of the night, its hours.**

**In the classical commentaries the relation of this verse to the times of prayer has been disputed, and different correspondences are transmitted.** **No legal ruling is issued in this commentary; nor do I transmit the correspondences as certain knowledge.**

**What can be verified from the text is this, and I record it:** the four spans of time cover **the whole of the day** — the two extremes (rising and setting), the night, and the ends of the day. **And this is on the same line as the command in the fourteenth verse:** *wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta **li-dhikrī***.

**لَعَلَّكَ تَرْضَىٰ** — **and the third link of the root ر-ض-و is here** (tabulated in the discussion of 20/109-112).

**A point of word order must be recorded: *tarḍā* — "you will be well pleased".** **The subject of the verb is the one addressed.**

**And in the eighty-fourth verse Moses had said *li-**tarḍā*** — there its subject was God ("that You might be pleased").**

| Verse | Expression | Subject of the verb |
|---|---|---|
| **84** | *Wa ʿajiltu ilayka rabbi **li-tarḍā*** | **God** — "that You might be pleased" |
| **130** | *Fa-sabbiḥ … **laʿallaka tarḍā*** | **The one addressed** — "that you may be pleased" |

**The same verb, the same pattern, two different subjects.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verbal overlap of the two verses:** Moses had hastened for his Lord's good pleasure; **here the one addressed is shown the road to his own good pleasure, and that road is patience and glorification.** **That is, the sūra has recorded both directions of the same word.**

## 20/131-132 — وَلَا تَمُدَّنَّ عَيْنَيْكَ إِلَىٰ مَا مَتَّعْنَا بِهِۦٓ أَزْوَٰجًا مِّنْهُمْ زَهْرَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ وَرِزْقُ رَبِّكَ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ · وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ وَٱصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا لَا نَسْـَٔلُكَ رِزْقًا نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكَ وَٱلْعَٰقِبَةُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ

*Wa lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka ilā mā mattaʿnā bihi azwājan minhum zahrata'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā li-naftinahum fīh, wa rizqu rabbika khayrun wa abqā · Wa'mur ahlaka bi'ṣ-ṣalāti waṣṭabir ʿalayhā, lā nasʾaluka rizqā, naḥnu narzuquk, wa'l-ʿāqibatu li't-taqwā*

"**Do not stretch your eyes towards what We have given certain groups of them to enjoy — the flower of the life of this world, that We may test them by it. Your Lord's provision is better and more lasting. · Command your household to pray, and be steadfast in it yourself. We do not ask provision of you; it is We who provide for you. And the outcome belongs to godfearing.**"

### لَا تَمُدَّنَّ عَيْنَيْكَ — the stretching of the gaze

The root **م-د-د**: **to stretch, to draw out, to pull long.** *Madd* — extension. From the same root come *madad* (help extended) and *midād* (ink — what flows and runs on).

**A point of word order must be recorded, and it is easily missed: the prohibition is placed not on the verb *to look* but on the verb *to stretch the eye*.**

***Lā tanẓur* (do not look) is not said; *lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka* — "do not stretch your eyes" — is said.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** seeing is not forbidden. **What is forbidden is the gaze's hanging there — the eye being drawn out towards that thing and caught on it.** **That is, the verse takes as its subject not the first glance but the sustaining of the glance.**

**And the verb is strengthened with the emphatic *nūn* (*tamuddanna*) — in Arabic this increases the definiteness of the prohibition.**

### زَهْرَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا

**The root ز-ه-ر — is analysed here for the first time in the series, and the word *zahra* occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**The senses the lexicographers give:**

| Sense | What |
|---|---|
| ***Zahr*** | **A flower** — especially one in bloom that has not yet borne fruit |
| ***Zuhra*** | **Brightness, showiness** |
| ***Azhar*** | **Gleaming white, shining** |

**The two branches of the root are bound together: the flower is the plant's brightest and shortest-lived state.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the lexical field of the word:** the verse likens the life of this world not to **fruit** but to **a flower.** **The flower is the part most looked at and the quickest to wither.** **And the construct is *zahratu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* — "the flower of the life of this world": that is, not the whole of life but its most eye-catching face.**

**And the word's standing together with *lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka* is worth recording:** the prohibition is placed on **the eye**, the object on **what catches the eye.** **The two words share the same sense.**

| al-Furqān 25/23 | *Wa qadimnā ilā mā ʿamilū min ʿamalin fa-jaʿalnāhu **habāʾan manthūrā*** | **The scattering of deeds** — nothing left in hand |
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/79-82 | *Fa-kharaja ʿalā qawmihi **fī zīnatih*** — Qārūn | **Ornament displayed, and its outcome** |
| **Ṭāhā 20/131** | ***Zahrata**'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā … wa rizqu rabbika **khayrun wa abqā*** | **A flower** — and the same two words |

**And a verbal overlap must be recorded, and it is countable: al-Qaṣaṣ 28/60 and Ṭāhā 20/131 end with the same two words — *khayrun wa abqā*.**

**The reading recorded at `025-furkan.md` on 25/23 can be attached here:** of the word *habāʾ* it was recorded there — **"it describes a thing that has not lost its visibility but has become impossible to hold; that is, not annihilation but not remaining in the hand."** **Ṭāhā's word *zahra* lies in the same field:** the flower is not absent, **it is not lasting.** **I record this as my own reading.**

### لِنَفْتِنَهُمْ فِيهِ

**And the ground for what is given is stated in the verse: *li-naftinahum fīh* — "that We may test them by it".**

**The root of *fitna* was treated at the fortieth verse and its four occurrences in the sūra were tabulated.** **This is the fourth.**

| Verse | Who is tested | By what |
|---|---|---|
| **40** | **Moses** | The events of his life |
| **85** | **The people** | The Sāmirī |
| **90** | **The people** | The calf — Aaron's diagnosis |
| **131** | **Those given worldly goods** | The thing given itself |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this distribution:** the sūra names testing in four different forms, **and in the last the instrument of the test is not a deprivation but a gift.** **That is, the very thing looked at is given as the test of the one looking.**

### وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ وَٱصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا

**A point of word order must be recorded: the command has two layers.** *Wa'mur ahlaka* — **command another**; *waṣṭabir ʿalayhā* — **be steadfast in it yourself.**

***Iṣṭabara* — Form VIII, more intensive than *ṣabara*: to be patient by forcing oneself.**

**And at `019-meryem.md` on 19/55 the same quality was recorded of Ishmael:** *wa kāna **yaʾmuru ahlahu** bi'ṣ-ṣalāti wa'z-zakāt.* **The observation recorded there can be carried over:** the quality is given as a responsibility directed not at a wide community but **at one's own household.**

**And the circle closes with the fourteenth verse:** *wa aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta li-dhikrī* — there the prayer was commanded **to the person himself**; **here he is asked to command it to his household, and patience is commanded to him.**

**لَا نَسْـَٔلُكَ رِزْقًا نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكَ** — "**We do not ask provision of you; it is We who provide for you.**"

**Where the sentence stands is worth recording:** it comes immediately after the command to pray, **and what was called "do not stretch your eyes to" in the preceding verse was provision.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** three sentences build a sequence — **do not look at another's goods** (131), **keep up the prayer** (132), **provision is not your business** (132). **That is, on both sides of the middle command has been set the anxiety that makes that command hard, and it is lifted from both sides.**

**وَٱلْعَٰقِبَةُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ** — "**the outcome belongs to godfearing.**"

**A point of word order: *li't-taqwā* — not to the godfearing but to godfearing itself.** **That is, the outcome is attached not to a group but to an attribute.**

**And a counterpart of the same sentence was treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/83** (*wa'l-ʿāqibatu li'l-muttaqīn* — "the outcome belongs to those who guard themselves"). **The two verses can be set side by side:**

| Place | Expression | To what it is attached |
|---|---|---|
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/83 | *Wa'l-ʿāqibatu **li'l-muttaqīn*** | **To persons** — to those who guard themselves |
| **Ṭāhā 20/132** | *Wa'l-ʿāqibatu **li't-taqwā*** | **To an attribute** — to the guarding itself |

**The same sentence, one attached to persons and one to an attribute.** **This is a difference between the two sūras that can be verified, and it agrees with the principle recorded in `USLUP.md`:** a ruling is built on attributes, not on groups.

## 20/133-135 — وَقَالُوا۟ لَوْلَا يَأْتِينَا بِـَٔايَةٍ مِّن رَّبِّهِۦٓ أَوَلَمْ تَأْتِهِم بَيِّنَةُ مَا فِى ٱلصُّحُفِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · وَلَوْ أَنَّآ أَهْلَكْنَٰهُم بِعَذَابٍ مِّن قَبْلِهِۦ لَقَالُوا۟ رَبَّنَا لَوْلَآ أَرْسَلْتَ إِلَيْنَا رَسُولًا فَنَتَّبِعَ ءَايَٰتِكَ مِن قَبْلِ أَن نَّذِلَّ وَنَخْزَىٰ · قُلْ كُلٌّ مُّتَرَبِّصٌ فَتَرَبَّصُوا۟ فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ مَنْ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلصِّرَٰطِ ٱلسَّوِىِّ وَمَنِ ٱهْتَدَىٰ

*Wa qālū law lā yaʾtīnā bi-āyatin min rabbih, awalam taʾtihim bayyinatu mā fi'ṣ-ṣuḥufi'l-ūlā · Wa law annā ahlaknāhum bi-ʿadhābin min qablihi la-qālū rabbanā law lā arsalta ilaynā rasūlan fa-nattabiʿa āyātika min qabli an nadhilla wa nakhzā · Qul kullun mutarabbiṣun fa-tarabbaṣū, fa-sa-taʿlamūna man aṣḥābu'ṣ-ṣirāṭi's-sawiyyi wa mani'htadā*

"**They said: 'If only he would bring us a sign from his Lord!' Has the clear proof of what is in the former scriptures not come to them? · Had We destroyed them with a punishment before this, they would have said: 'Our Lord! If only You had sent us a messenger, that we might have followed Your signs before we were brought low and disgraced.' · Say: 'Everyone is waiting; so wait. You will soon know who are the companions of the even path and who has found the guidance.'**"

### The answer to the objection

**The objection is a demand for a sign; the answer is given with a sign already given.**

***Bayyinatu mā fi'ṣ-ṣuḥufi'l-ūlā* — "the clear proof of what is in the former scriptures".**

**A point of word order must be recorded: the answer does not promise a new sign.** **And the expression *al-ṣuḥufi'l-ūlā* is worth recording** — as tabulated in the discussion of 20/74-76, the *al-ūlā* here is from the root **أ-و-ل**: **"former".**

**I record this as an observation:** for ninety verses the sūra has told the story of an earlier prophet. **The answer of the hundred and thirty-third verse may be attached to that telling itself** — and indeed the ninety-ninth verse said why the story was told: *kadhālika naquṣṣu ʿalayka min anbāʾi mā qad sabaq.* **I record this connection as my own reading; the verse does not state it openly.**

### وَلَوْ أَنَّآ أَهْلَكْنَٰهُم — the excuse closed off

**The hundred and thirty-fourth verse sets up a hypothetical case and closes off an excuse in advance.**

**And the same principle was treated at `028-kasas.md` on 28/59** (*wa mā kāna rabbuka muhlika'l-qurā ḥattā yabʿatha fī ummihā rasūlā*) **and was recorded at `035-fatir.md` on 35/24 as well** (*wa in min ummatin illā khalā fīhā nadhīr*). **I rely on those treatments.**

**What Ṭāhā adds is that the excuse is framed in their own mouths:** *la-qālū rabbanā law lā arsalta ilaynā rasūlā* — "they would have said: if only You had sent us a messenger, that we might have followed."

**I record this as an observation:** the verse transmits a sentence that was never spoken **as though it had been spoken.** **And this can be compared with the actual words in the hundred and thirty-third verse of the same sūra:**

| Verse | Words | Their status |
|---|---|---|
| **133** | *Law lā **yaʾtīnā bi-āyatin** min rabbih* | **Spoken** — a sign is demanded |
| **134** | *Law lā **arsalta ilaynā rasūlan*** | **Unspoken** — hypothetical |

**Both sentences begin with *law lā*.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two sentences are on the same pattern:** the verse sets side by side, **in the same sentence-pattern**, the one who demands a sign of the messenger who came and the one who would have produced an excuse had no messenger come.

### كُلٌّ مُّتَرَبِّصٌ — the close

The root **ر-ب-ص**: **to wait, to watch for the outcome of a thing.** **Form V, *tarabbuṣ*: to keep on waiting.**

**A point of word order must be recorded: *kullun mutarabbiṣ* — "everyone is waiting".** **That is, waiting is given as a state common to both sides.** **And the command follows accordingly: *fa-tarabbaṣū* — "so wait."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word *kull*:** the close is not a threat but **the declaring of a shared position.** **Neither side has yet seen the outcome; the difference lies in what they are waiting for.**

### ٱلصِّرَٰطِ ٱلسَّوِىِّ وَمَنِ ٱهْتَدَىٰ

***Al-ṣirāṭu's-sawiyy* — root س-و-ي: evenness, levelness.** **And the word occurs here for the second time in the sūra:** in the fifty-eighth verse Pharaoh's side had asked for *makānan **suwā*** (a place even for both parties).

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **58** | *Makānan **suwā*** | **The meeting place** — even for both sides |
| **135** | *Al-ṣirāṭi'**s-sawiyy*** | **The path** — even, with no crookedness in it |

**The same root, one for the levelness of a ground, the other for that of a path.** **This is a distribution that can be verified within the sūra.**

**And the sūra's last word must be recorded: *wa mani'htadā*.**

**The root ه-د-ي — one of the hardest-working roots in the sūra, occurring in eight verses** (10, 50, 79, 82, 122, 123, 128, 135).

**And the sūra closes with the root with which it opened in the tenth verse, in the thing Moses sought in the desert:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | *Aw ajidu ʿala'n-nāri **hudā*** | **Moses** — seeking |
| **135** | *Wa mani'**htadā*** | **The close** — who has found |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses carry the same root:** the sūra opens with a man in the night looking for someone to ask the road of **and closes with the sentence "it will be known who has found the road."** **What was sought and what is measured are the same word.**

### The sūra's two stories — how they are joined

**The sūra tells two stories: Moses (9-98) and Adam (115-123). The link between them is not accidental and can be verified from the text:**

| Element | **The story of Moses** | **The story of Adam** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb of refusal | *Fa-kadhdhaba **wa abā*** (56 — Pharaoh) | *Illā Iblīsa **abā*** (116) |
| Forgetting | *Fa-**nasiya*** (88 — in the matter of the calf) | *Fa-**nasiya*** (115) |
| The pattern of the offer | *Hal **adullukum** ʿalā man yakfuluh* (40) | *Hal **adulluka** ʿalā shajarati'l-khuld* (120) |
| The outcome | *Wa aḍalla Firʿawnu qawmahu wa mā **hadā*** (79) | *Fa-tāba ʿalayhi **wa hadā*** (122) |
| The closing promise | *Wa's-salāmu ʿalā **mani'ttabaʿa'l-hudā*** (47) | *Fa-**mani'ttabaʿa hudāya** fa-lā yaḍillu wa lā yashqā* (123) |

**There is an overlap in five elements, and all five use the same roots.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra binds the two stories to one another not by **their subjects** but by **their words.** **And the outcomes of the two stories are opposite: one tells the end of a refusal that did not guide, the other the end of a slip that was guided.**

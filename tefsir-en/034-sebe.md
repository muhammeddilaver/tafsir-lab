# 34. Sūrat Sabaʾ

**Fifty-four verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the people of **سَبَإ** named in **verse 15** — a community that lived in southern Arabia, remembered for its two gardens and its dam. The name of the people occurs only once in the sūra; yet the whole weave of the sūra is worked around that single verse.

**It is one of the five sūras in the Qurʾān that open with *al-ḥamdu lillāh*** (al-Fātiḥa, al-Anʿām, al-Kahf, **Sabaʾ**, Fāṭir). And in this respect it stands side by side with Fāṭir, which follows it in the sequence: **two sūras in a row begin with praise.**

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-9 | Praise, the four verbs, the knowledge of the unseen, and the objection to resurrection | *La-āyatan li-kulli ʿabdin munīb* (9) |
| **II** | 10-14 | **David (Dāwūd) and Solomon (Sulaymān)** — what was given, what was worked, and the command to give thanks | *Mā labithū fi'l-ʿadhābi'l-muhīn* (14) |
| **III** | 15-21 | **Sabaʾ** — two gardens, a flood, and the turn into legend | *Wa rabbuka ʿalā kulli shayʾin ḥafīẓ* (21) |
| **IV** | 22-30 | Those who are called upon, intercession, the manners of disputation, the scope of the mission | *Lā tastaʾkhirūna ʿanhu sāʿatan wa lā tastaqdimūn* (30) |
| **V** | 31-45 | The dispute in the Fire, the *mutraf*s, and **the sentence of judgement of the sūra** | *Fa-kayfa kāna nakīr* (45) |
| **VI** | 46-54 | A single admonition, the coming of the truth, and the close | *Innahum kānū fī shakkin murīb* (54) |

### The backbone of the sūra: ش-ك-ر

**The sūra opens with praise and moves by thanks.** Two peoples are set out one after the other — one is commanded to give thanks, the other turns away from thanks — and the sūra ends with a ruling: **wealth and children do not bring you near.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **13** | *Iʿmalū āla Dāwūda **shukrā***, *wa qalīlun min ʿibādiya'**sh-shakūr*** | **The family of David** — the command, and the note of how few |
| **15** | *Kulū min rizqi rabbikum wa'**shkurū** lah* | **Sabaʾ** — the command |
| **17** | *Wa hal nujāzī illa'l-**kafūr*** | **Sabaʾ** — the command went unanswered |
| **19** | *La-āyātin li-kulli ṣabbārin **shakūr*** | **The one who is to take the lesson** |

**Four occurrences, in three distinct functions: the command, its opposite, and the one who takes the lesson.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

**And the chain is tied to a ruling in the thirty-seventh verse:** *wa mā amwālukum wa lā awlādukum bi'llatī tuqarribukum ʿindanā zulfā.* **That is: the sūra moves from the question "what do you have" to the question "what did you do with what you have".**

The basmala was treated in earlier sections and is not taken up again here; the verse numbers are counted without it.

## 34/1-2 — ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَهُ ٱلْحَمْدُ فِى ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَهُوَ ٱلْحَكِيمُ ٱلْخَبِيرُ · يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا وَمَا يَنزِلُ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا وَهُوَ ٱلرَّحِيمُ ٱلْغَفُورُ

*Al-ḥamdu lillāhi'lladhī lahū mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍi wa lahu'l-ḥamdu fi'l-ākhirati wa huwa'l-ḥakīmu'l-khabīr · Yaʿlamu mā yaliju fi'l-arḍi wa mā yakhruju minhā wa mā yanzilu mina's-samāʾi wa mā yaʿruju fīhā wa huwa'r-raḥīmu'l-ghafūr*

"**Praise belongs to God, whose is whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth; and in the hereafter too the praise is His.** He is wise, aware of all things. **He knows what goes into the earth and what comes out of it, what descends from the sky and what ascends into it.** He is the merciful, the forgiving."

### Praise named in two places

**The sentence says *lahu'l-ḥamd* twice, and sets a difference of time between the two:**

| Order | Expression | Where | What the ground is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Al-ḥamdu lillāhi'lladhī **lahū mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ*** | **This world** | **Ownership** — possession |
| 2 | *Wa **lahu'l-ḥamdu fi'l-ākhira*** | **The hereafter** | The ground is **not stated** |

**The ground of the first praise is given plainly in the verse: whatever is in the heavens and in the earth is His. The second praise is left without a ground.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the difference in how the two clauses are built:** in this world a *cause* for praise is shown, because in this world who owns what is a matter of dispute — the twenty-second verse of the sūra will dispute exactly that. **In the hereafter the dispute will be over, so no ground is needed.**

**And that praise continues in the hereafter is worth recording:** the verse sets praise not as a behaviour belonging to this world but as a state **valid in both worlds at once**. At `035-fatir.md` 35/34 it was treated that the first words out of the mouths of the people of the Garden are a sentence of praise (*al-ḥamdu lillāhi'lladhī adhhaba ʿanna'l-ḥazan*) — **read together, the record made in this first verse appears there as something actually carried out.**

### Two pairs of names

**The two verses end with two pairs of names, and the pairs are not symmetrical:**

| Verse | Closing | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Al-ḥakīmu'l-khabīr* | **Judgement and awareness** — the side of knowledge |
| 2 | *Al-raḥīmu'l-ghafūr* | **Mercy and pardon** — the side of dealing with people |

**What is worth recording is this:** the second verse describes **an all-embracing knowledge** (what goes in, what comes out, what descends, what ascends) — and after such a description of knowledge the expected closing would have been "ʿalīm" or "ḥafīẓ". **In their place the verse sets the names of mercy and pardon.**

**I give this as an observation about the word order:** the attribute named after knowing everything is not punishment but forgiveness. **The completeness of the knowledge is attached not as a threat but as an assurance.**

### Four verbs, two pairs of directions

**The middle of the second verse is a table built on four verbs:**

| Verb | Root | Direction | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Yaliju* | **و-ل-ج** | **Inward** ↓ | **Earth** |
| *Yakhruju* | **خ-ر-ج** | **Outward** ↑ | **Earth** |
| *Yanzilu* | **ن-ز-ل** | **Downward** ↓ | **Sky** |
| *Yaʿruju* | **ع-ر-ج** | **Upward** ↑ | **Sky** |

**Four verbs, two domains, and in each domain one going and one coming.** The sentence thus **leaves no direction uncovered**: what enters the earth, what comes out of the earth, what comes down from the sky and what climbs into the sky all fall within the same knowledge.

**و-ل-ج** — the concrete sense of the root is **to go inside through a narrow place**. The lexicographers describe *wallaja* as "one thing's entering into another"; *īlāj* — to insert. **The same root is used in the Qurʾān for the folding of night into day** (*yūliju'l-layla fi'n-nahār*). **The picture in the word is not disappearing — it is passing into a place that cannot be seen.**

**The root ع-ر-ج was analysed in detail at `070-mearic.md` 70/3-4** (to ascend, to climb step by step along a sloping road; *miʿrāj*, *maʿārij*, *aʿraj*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**There is one difference that belongs here and must be recorded:** in al-Maʿārij the root was used of **the angels and the Spirit** (*taʿruju'l-malāʾikatu wa'r-rūḥu ilayh*) — that is, what ascended was specified. **Here no subject is specified:** *mā yaʿruju fīhā* — "whatever ascends into it". **The word has been widened by being left indefinite.**

| Place | Who / what ascends |
|---|---|
| al-Maʿārij 70/4 | **The angels and the Spirit** — definite |
| **Sabaʾ 34/2** | *Mā yaʿruju fīhā* — **indefinite, and therefore all-embracing** |

**And this table of four verbs prepares from the outset the claim the sūra will make later:** the clause *lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharra*, which comes in the third verse, is these four directions pressed into a single sentence.

## 34/3 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَا تَأْتِينَا ٱلسَّاعَةُ قُلْ بَلَىٰ وَرَبِّى لَتَأْتِيَنَّكُمْ عَٰلِمِ ٱلْغَيْبِ لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ مِثْقَالُ ذَرَّةٍ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَلَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَآ أَصْغَرُ مِن ذَٰلِكَ وَلَآ أَكْبَرُ إِلَّا فِى كِتَٰبٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū lā taʾtīna's-sāʿah, qul balā wa rabbī la-taʾtiyannakum ʿālimi'l-ghayb, lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharratin fi's-samāwāti wa lā fi'l-arḍi wa lā aṣgharu min dhālika wa lā akbaru illā fī kitābin mubīn*

"Those who disbelieved said: '**The Hour will not come to us.**' Say: '**No — by my Lord, the Knower of the unseen, it will certainly come to you.** Not so much as **the weight of a speck** in the heavens or in the earth escapes Him; nor anything smaller than that, nor larger, but it is in a clear book.'"

### قُلْ بَلَىٰ وَرَبِّى — one of the three places where the Prophet is made to swear

**There are three places in the Qurʾān where the Prophet is made to swear directly, and this is one of them. The record is needed, because the pattern is rare:**

| Place | Expression | Subject of the oath |
|---|---|---|
| Yūnus 10/53 | *Qul ī wa rabbī innahū la-ḥaqq* | **The reality of the punishment** |
| **Sabaʾ 34/3** | *Qul **balā wa rabbī** la-taʾtiyannakum* | **That the Hour will come** |
| al-Taghābun 64/7 | *Qul **balā wa rabbī** la-tubʿathunna* | **That the resurrection will take place** |

**All three begin with a command (*qul*), and all three belong to the same family of subjects: the hereafter.** And the last two open with **exactly the same wording**: *qul balā wa rabbī*.

**The counterpart of this verse was treated in detail at `064-tegabun.md` 64/7** — there it was analysed how the word *zaʿama* (a claim without support) sets the frame of the dispute. **The difference that belongs here must be recorded:** in al-Taghābun the objection was given with the verb *zaʿama*, here with a plain *qāla*. **That is, Sabaʾ first transmits the objection bare; what shows it to be without support is not the word but the answer that follows it.**

**بَلَىٰ** — in Arabic this is the particle of reply used **to refute a negative sentence**. It was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/59 (*balā qad jāʾatka āyātī*). **So the answer is not "yes" — it is "no, it is not so".** What is rejected is the negation on which the objection rests.

**And the form of the oath is worth recording:** *wa **rabbī*** — "by my Lord". Not by a general name, but named as **the speaker's own Lord**. **The oath is sworn not on an authority the addressee accepts, but on the authority to which the speaker is bound.**

### لَا يَعْزُبُ — root: ع-ز-ب

**The concrete sense of the root is given by the lexicographers thus: to move away, to be separated, to pass out of sight.** *ʿAzaba'r-rajul* — the man went far off, stayed apart from his family. **And from the same root *ʿāzib* is the shepherd who takes his flock to distant pasture; *ʿazab* (unmarried) is also attached to this family: the one apart from a spouse.**

**I transmit this picture as a relation the lexicographers have built; I do not present it as a settled etymological ruling.**

**What the word does here must be recorded:** the verse does not say "He does not know it", it says **"it does not stay far from Him"**. **That is, knowledge is described not as an act of searching but as an absence of distance.** The four-directional table of the second verse (what goes in, comes out, descends, ascends) is reduced here to a single word: **no direction is far.**

### مِثْقَالُ ذَرَّةٍ — what the word meant then

**Per the method (`USLUP.md`) a limit has to be set here, and I set it plainly: the atom of modern physics cannot be drawn out of this word.** What the word meant in the language in which the Qurʾān came down is known, and the senses the lexicographers give are these:

| Sense given | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **A mote of dust** | The smallest particle seen in the air when sunlight comes through a gap |
| **A small ant** | The smallest of ants; the lexicographers record the word *dharr* in this sense too |
| **The tiniest part of a thing** | General usage: what looks too small to be divided |

**The common point is this: a *dharra* is "the smallest thing that can be seen".** So the word is not a unit of measure but **a description of a limit**: the lowest step the eye can make out.

**And the verse itself compels this reading, because the sentence does not stop there:** *wa lā **aṣgharu** min dhālika wa lā **akbar***. **"Nor anything smaller than that, nor larger."** So the verse does not even leave *dharra* standing as the smallest thing — **it keeps what is below it open too.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the word *aṣghar*:** if *dharra* were the absolutely smallest particle, the phrase "smaller than that" would be meaningless. **The word is not an absolute magnitude but a threshold in the eye of the addressee — and the verse speaks by crossing that threshold.**

**The expression *mithqāla dharra* was treated in detail at `099-zilzal.md` 99/7-8**, and there it was tabulated how the root ث-ق-ل holds the two ends of that sūra together. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is this:** in al-Zilzāl the expression came as **the measure of deeds** (*man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin khayran yarah*); **here it comes as the extent of knowledge**.

| Place | What *mithqāla dharra* measures |
|---|---|
| al-Zilzāl 99/7-8 | **Deeds** — not even the smallest goes unanswered |
| **Sabaʾ 34/3** | **Knowledge** — not even the smallest stays far off |
| **Sabaʾ 34/22** | **Ownership** — they do not own even the smallest |

**The third row matters, and it lies inside the sūra:** the same expression will come once more in the twenty-second verse. **The sūra uses the same measure once for God's knowledge and once for the powerlessness of those who are called upon.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.

### Variant reading: عَٰلِمِ ٱلْغَيْبِ

| Reading | Inflection | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| *ʿĀlim**i**'l-ghayb* (genitive) | An adjective of the word *rabbī* | "By my Lord, the Knower of the unseen" |
| *ʿĀlim**u**'l-ghayb* (nominative) | Subject / predicate of a new sentence | "He is the Knower of the unseen" |

**Both readings are transmitted. The difference in sense is not large; I do not impose a preference.** **What is worth recording is this: on either reading, what the oath rests on is the knowing of the unseen.** And this bears directly on the subject of the objection: **the claim that the Hour will not come is a claim about the unseen.**

## 34/4-5 — لِّيَجْزِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ وَرِزْقٌ كَرِيمٌ · وَٱلَّذِينَ سَعَوْ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِنَا مُعَٰجِزِينَ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ مِّن رِّجْزٍ أَلِيمٌ

*Li-yajziya'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāt, ulāʾika lahum maghfiratun wa rizqun karīm · Wa'lladhīna saʿaw fī āyātinā muʿājizīna ulāʾika lahum ʿadhābun min rijzin alīm*

"**That He may reward those who believed and did righteous deeds.** For them there is forgiveness and **a generous provision**. **And for those who run about to make Our signs of no effect**, there is a painful punishment of the worst kind."

### The ground of the Hour

**The fourth verse begins with a *lām al-taʿlīl* (the lām of cause): *li-yajziya* — "in order to reward".** That is, the Hour whose coming was announced in the previous verse is **tied to a purpose.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the objection said "it will not come"; the answer said first that it **will** come (3), then says **why** it will come (4). **That is, the sentence is not content to refute the claim; it also gives the reason for the event.**

**And in that reason the reward is named first, the punishment second.** This ordering holds throughout the sūra: the fourth verse for the believers, the fifth for the other side.

### سَعَوْ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِنَا مُعَٰجِزِينَ

**س-ع-ي** — the concrete sense of the root is **to walk fast, to bustle about, to exert oneself for a task**. **The word is neutral; the Qurʾān uses it both in praise and in blame.** What settles its direction here is the circumstantial word that follows it.

**مُعَٰجِزِين** — root **ع-ج-ز**: **to be unable, to be powerless.** *Muʿājiz* — the active participle of Form III. **This form conveys reciprocity and striving: "one who tries to render powerless, who strives to outrun and get past".**

**The lexicographers give the word two senses, and both are transmitted:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Those who try to render powerless** — who want to make the signs ineffective |
| 2 | **Those who suppose they will escape** — who think they cannot be caught |

**I do not impose a preference.** **What is worth recording is that the word states not an *act* but an *aim*:** the verse does not say that they made the signs ineffective — **it says they ran about so that it should be so.**

**This expression will come once more in the sūra, in almost the same words:**

| Verse | Expression | What it is answered with |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | *Wa'lladhīna **saʿaw fī āyātinā muʿājizīn*** | *ʿAdhābun min rijzin alīm* |
| **38** | *Wa'lladhīna **yasʿawna fī āyātinā muʿājizīn*** | *Fi'l-ʿadhābi muḥḍarūn* |

**The only difference is the tense of the verb: the fifth has the perfect (*saʿaw*), the thirty-eighth the imperfect (*yasʿawna*).** **I record this as my own reading, and its support is where the two verses stand:** the fifth verse comes inside **a list of rulings**, the thirty-eighth inside a description of **an ongoing behaviour**. The perfect states the ruling, the imperfect describes the state.

**رِجْز — root ر-ج-ز: shaking, disordered movement; and from there filth, punishment. The lexicographers connect the word with the trembling in a camel's hind legs (*rajaz*). The construction is striking: *ʿadhābun min rijzin alīm* — "a punishment out of the worst kind of punishment". That is, the punishment is further qualified from within its own class.**

## 34/6 — وَيَرَى ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ مِن رَّبِّكَ هُوَ ٱلْحَقَّ وَيَهْدِىٓ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَمِيدِ

*Wa yara'lladhīna ūtu'l-ʿilma'lladhī unzila ilayka min rabbika huwa'l-ḥaqqa wa yahdī ilā ṣirāṭi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥamīd*

"**Those who have been given knowledge see that what has been sent down to you from your Lord is the truth itself, and that it guides to the path of the Mighty, the Praiseworthy.**"

### Two sides, two verbs

**In the third verse there was a saying: *qāla'lladhīna kafarū*. In the sixth verse there is a seeing: *yara'lladhīna ūtu'l-ʿilm*.**

| Verse | Who | Verb | What they do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3** | *Alladhīna **kafarū*** | *Qāla* — **said** | They make a claim |
| **6** | *Alladhīna **ūtu'l-ʿilm*** | *Yarā* — **sees** | They identify |

**The contrast is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading; its support is the kind of the two verbs:** one side **speaks**, the other side **sees.** Speech is set against sight. And *ruʾya* here does not mean seeing with the eye but **recognition with the heart** — this use of the verb is common in Arabic.

**أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ** — the verb is passive: **"those who have been given knowledge".** Knowledge, that is, is named not as something acquired but as **something given**. At `035-fatir.md` 35/28 (*innamā yakhsha'llāha min ʿibādihi'l-ʿulamāʾ*) the link between those who know and awe was treated; **I rely on that treatment, and record the difference here:** in Fāṭir those who know **feared**; here they **see.**

**And I issue no narrowing ruling as to who is meant in the verse.** Per the method (`USLUP.md`): what the verse describes is an **attribute** — having been given knowledge — and whoever bears that attribute is included in it.

**صِرَٰطِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَمِيدِ** — and the owner of the path is named with two names. **The second must be recorded: *al-ḥamīd*.** **The sūra opened with praise in the first verse; in the sixth verse the root of praise returns as a name.** This is a link that can be verified within the sūra.

## 34/7-8 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ هَلْ نَدُلُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ رَجُلٍ يُنَبِّئُكُمْ إِذَا مُزِّقْتُمْ كُلَّ مُمَزَّقٍ إِنَّكُمْ لَفِى خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ · أَفْتَرَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ كَذِبًا أَم بِهِۦ جِنَّةٌۢ بَلِ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ وَٱلضَّلَٰلِ ٱلْبَعِيدِ

*Wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū hal nadullukum ʿalā rajulin yunabbiʾukum idhā muzziqtum kulla mumazzaqin innakum la-fī khalqin jadīd · Aftarā ʿala'llāhi kadhiban am bihī jinnatun, bali'lladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi'l-ākhirati fi'l-ʿadhābi wa'ḍ-ḍalāli'l-baʿīd*

"Those who disbelieved said: '**Shall we point you to a man who tells you that when you have been torn utterly to pieces you will be in a new creation?** **Has he invented a lie against God, or is there madness in him?**' **No! Those who do not believe in the hereafter are in the punishment and in far-gone error.**"

### The form of the objection: mockery built as an invitation

**The sentence opens with a question: *hal nadullukum ʿalā rajul* — "shall we point you to a man?"**

**This pattern is worth recording, because the pattern itself is a pattern of *invitation*.** In Arabic *dalāla* (to point out, to indicate) is a verb of doing good; one says "shall I point you to so-and-so" and something valuable follows. **Here the same pattern is used inverted.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the pattern itself:** the mockery is built not in the language of opposition but **in the language of invitation**. The speakers present what they reject as though introducing it — and expect the absurdity to emerge of itself from the content of the introduction.

**And the word *rajul* must be recorded: "a man".** No name is given, no attribute. **At `053-necm.md` 53/2 and `081-tekvir.md` 81/22 the word *ṣāḥibukum* (your companion) was treated** — what was recorded there was that the word rests on **acquaintance**. **Here exactly the opposite is done: a familiar person is turned into an unknown "man".**

| Word | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| *Ṣāḥibukum* | **34/46** (and al-Najm 53/2, al-Takwīr 81/22) | **Brings near** — "you know him" |
| *Rajul* | **34/7** | **Pushes away** — "a man" |

**And this contrast lies inside the sūra:** those who say *rajul* in the seventh verse will be answered in the forty-sixth verse with *ṣāḥibikum*. **This is an exchange that can be verified from the text.**

### مُزِّقْتُمْ كُلَّ مُمَزَّقٍ — root: م-ز-ق

**In the root index this is the first place the root is analysed.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to tear, to rend, to shred.** *Mazaqa'th-thawb* — he tore the cloth. **The original picture in the word is a piece of cloth or leather being torn** — not cut, but **pulled apart.**

**The pattern is doubled, and this must be recorded:** *muzziqtum* (Form II, passive — "you were torn to pieces") + *kulla **mumazzaq*** (the absolute object, *mafʿūl muṭlaq* — "with every kind of tearing"). **That is, the verb is not said once and left; it is reinforced with its own verbal noun.**

**In Arabic this structure (verb + absolute object from the same root) conveys *intensity* and *extent*.** In English it can only be carried by piling a reinforcing word on top: "when you have been torn utterly to pieces".

**And now we come to one of the strongest textual data in the sūra — this needs a table of its own.**

### The same word, twelve verses later

| Verse | Expression | About whom | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7** | *Idhā **muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq*** | **The addressees** — after death | **Those who disbelieved** — in mockery, as a supposition |
| **19** | *Wa **mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq*** | **The people of Sabaʾ** — in history | **God** — as an event that happened |

**The expression is word for word the same in both places: *kulla mumazzaq*.** And the difference lies in the subject of the verb: in the seventh verse it is **passive** (who did the tearing is not said), in the nineteenth **first person** (*mazzaqnā* — We tore them).

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the verbal overlap of the two verses:** those who disbelieve choose a word to describe an event they hold to be remote and ridiculous. **The sūra takes that same word and fastens it to an event that actually happened in history.** The tearing apart that was made an object of mockery is named twelve verses later as something that came upon a people.

**That is, the objection is answered with its own word.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text, and it is one of the most striking points of word order in the sūra.

### أَفْتَرَىٰ … أَمْ بِهِۦ جِنَّةٌ — an accusation in two options

**The sentence offers two possibilities:**

| Option | Expression | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Aftarā ʿala'llāhi kadhibā* | **He is inventing it knowingly** — there is intent |
| 2 | *Am bihī jinnah* | **He is not in his right mind** — there is no intent |

**And together the two options carry out a closing of doors:** the first is a moral charge, the second a medical one. **Both leave outside the possibility that what he says might be true.**

**At `040-gafir.md` 40/28 the two-option argument built by the believing man was treated in detail.** I rely on that treatment and **compare the two structures**, because the same form has been used for two opposite purposes:

| | **Ghāfir 40/28** — the believing man | **Sabaʾ 34/8** — those who disbelieved |
|---|---|---|
| Option 1 | *In yaku kādhiban* — if he is a liar | *Aftarā ʿala'llāhi kadhibā* — if he is inventing |
| Option 2 | *Wa in yaku ṣādiqan* — **if he is telling the truth** | *Am bihī jinnah* — if he has gone mad |
| What the two options share | **One of them contains the possibility of truth** | **Both are denials** |
| The conclusion reached | **Do not kill him** — caution | **Do not listen to him** — rejection |

**The difference lies in a single place, and it is decisive: the second option in Ghāfir leaves the door open, the second option in Sabaʾ closes it.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the second row of the table above:** both sides reason in the form "either this or that". **But one leaves a share of truth to one of the options, while the other builds both options as accusations.** The issue, then, is not the form of the reasoning but **whether the options have been counted honestly.** A real dilemma takes both possibilities into account; a manufactured dilemma offers only two separate routes to rejection.

**بَلِ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ وَٱلضَّلَٰلِ ٱلْبَعِيدِ** — and the answer rejects both options and builds a third sentence: **"no!"**

**بَلْ** — in Arabic the particle that cancels the preceding judgement and puts a new one in its place. **That is, the verse does not choose one of the options; it treats the question as void.**

**ٱلضَّلَٰلِ ٱلْبَعِيدِ** — "far-gone error". **The root ب-ع-د is a backbone word in this sūra, and this is its first occurrence.** It will return in the nineteenth verse inside a prayer (*bāʿid bayna asfārinā*), and in the fifty-second and fifty-third verses as a distance (*min makānin baʿīd*). **I shall tabulate it at the end.**

## 34/9 — أَفَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ إِلَىٰ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ إِن نَّشَأْ نَخْسِفْ بِهِمُ ٱلْأَرْضَ أَوْ نُسْقِطْ عَلَيْهِمْ كِسَفًا مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَةً لِّكُلِّ عَبْدٍ مُّنِيبٍ

*A-fa-lam yaraw ilā mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum mina's-samāʾi wa'l-arḍ, in nashaʾ nakhsif bihimu'l-arḍa aw nusqiṭ ʿalayhim kisafan mina's-samāʾ, inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-kulli ʿabdin munīb*

"**Do they not look at what is before them and behind them of the sky and the earth?** If We willed, We would sink them into the earth, or drop upon them fragments from the sky. **In that there is a sign for every servant who turns back.**"

### The place they are asked to look at

**The verse is built not as a command to look but as a finding that looking is absent:** *a-fa-lam yaraw* — "have they still not looked?"

**And how the place to be looked at is described is worth recording: *mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum* — "what is before them and behind them".** This is not a distant place. **No distance to be travelled for the proof is pointed out; what is pointed out is the place the addressee is already standing in.**

**I record this as an observation:** to those who in the seventh verse counted resurrection remote, the ninth verse points to **what is nearest.** **The claim of remoteness is answered with nearness.**

### The direction of the two threats

| Verb | Root | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| *Nakhsif bihimu'l-arḍ* | **خ-س-ف** — to make cave in, to sink | **Downward** — from under the foot |
| *Nusqiṭ ʿalayhim kisafan mina's-samāʾ* | **س-ق-ط** — to drop | **Upward** — from over the head |

**Two directions, two verbs.** And this is **the narrowed form of the four-verb table in the second verse**: there the extent of knowledge was given as up and down, here the extent of power is given in the same two directions.

**I record this as my own reading; its support is the directional structure of the two verses:** on the same axis the sūra places first **knowledge**, then **power**.

**خ-س-ف** — the concrete sense of the root is **a thing's collapsing into itself, hollowing out**. *Khusūfu'l-qamar* — the eclipse of the moon; the lexicographers explain this by the picture of the moon's "being hidden / caving in".

**كِسَفًا** — the plural of *kisfa*: **a piece, a cut-off portion.** Root **ك-س-ف**: to cut, to break into parts. **The word describes the falling from the sky not of something whole but of *pieces*.**

**عَبْدٍ مُّنِيبٍ** — root **ن-و-ب**: **to come back again and again, to turn towards repeatedly.** The word *nawba* — a turn, a spell of duty, borrowed into Turkish as *nöbet* — is from this root: what comes round in rotation. *Munīb* — the active participle of Form IV: **one who turns back, who turns towards.**

**And the last clause of the verse sets a condition:** the sign is not **for everyone**, but *li-kulli ʿabdin munīb* — **for the servant who turns.** At `035-fatir.md` 35/18 the same limit was treated (*innamā tundhiru'lladhīna yakhshawna rabbahum bi'l-ghayb*). **I rely on that treatment.** **The proof stands in plain view; what turns the proof into a sign is the state of the one who looks.**

## 34/10 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَا دَاوُۥدَ مِنَّا فَضْلًا يَٰجِبَالُ أَوِّبِى مَعَهُۥ وَٱلطَّيْرَ وَأَلَنَّا لَهُ ٱلْحَدِيدَ

*Wa laqad ātaynā Dāwūda minnā faḍlā, yā jibālu awwibī maʿahū wa'ṭ-ṭayr, wa alannā lahu'l-ḥadīd*

"**We gave David a bounty from Us:** 'O mountains! **Echo back** with him; and you birds too!' **And We softened iron for him.**"

### The opening of the block

**Here the sūra moves into a narrative, and the move is not abrupt.** The ninth verse ended with the word *munīb* (one who turns); the tenth verse opens with the name of a servant who turned.

**David was treated in detail at `038-sad.md` 38/17-20** — there *ذَا ٱلْأَيْدِ* (possessor of strength), *أَوَّاب* (much-returning) and the glorification of the mountains were analysed. **I rely on that treatment and record the differences here.**

| | **Ṣād 38/18-19** | **Sabaʾ 34/10** |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence | *Innā **sakhkharna**'l-jibāla maʿahū yusabbiḥn* | *Yā jibālu **awwibī** maʿahū* |
| Pattern | **Statement** — "We made them subservient" | **Vocative** — "O mountains!" |
| Verb | *Yusabbiḥna* — they glorify | *Awwibī* — turn / echo back |
| Birds | *Wa'ṭ-ṭayra **maḥshūra*** — gathered | *Wa'ṭ-ṭayr* — a conjunct, with no attribute |

**The most striking difference is in the first row, and it must be recorded:** in Ṣād the event is **narrated**, in Sabaʾ **the command itself is transmitted.** The text passes from third-person narration straight into direct address.

**I give this as an observation about the word order:** instead of saying "We commanded the mountains", the verse gives the wording of the command. **The reader does not hear about the event; he hears the command.**

### أَوِّبِى — root: أ-و-ب

**The concrete sense of the root: to turn, to come back.** *Āba* — he returned. *Maʾāb* — the place of return. **And the word *awwāb* (much-returning) was treated at `038-sad.md`, where it was recorded that it occurs three times, for David, Solomon and Job (Ayyūb).**

**The verb here is *awwibī* — an imperative of Form II, addressed to a feminine.** And the lexicographers give the word two senses:

| Reading | Sense | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Glorify, turn** | The *awwāb* branch of the root; it overlaps with *yusabbiḥna* in Ṣād 38/18 |
| 2 | **Echo, repeat** | The relation of the verb "to return" with sound: the coming back of a voice |

**Both readings are transmitted and they do not exclude one another; I do not impose a preference.**

**What is worth recording is this, and I give it as my own reading:** both readings meet in the same picture — **a voice coming back.** A word spoken on a mountain returns from the mountain. **The sense "to return" in the root and the physics of an echo meet in the same place; and the attribute *awwāb* (the servant who turns back) is from the same root.** That is, David's attribute and the command given to the mountains come from the same word.

**وَٱلطَّيْرَ** — the lexicographers dwell on the inflection of the word: *ṭayr* is in the accusative. **It is said either to be a conjunct to an elided verb (*wa sakhkharna'ṭ-ṭayra*), or a conjunct to the position of the vocative.** Both explanations are transmitted; **since they do not change the sense, I do not impose a preference.**

### وَأَلَنَّا لَهُ ٱلْحَدِيدَ — the softening of iron

**ل-ي-ن** — the sense of the root is **softness**. *Layyin* — soft. *Alāna* — Form IV: he softened.

**The root ح-د-د and the word *ḥadīd* were analysed in detail at `057-hadid.md`.** What was recorded there holds here unchanged, and its summary is this: **the root means "limit, edge, sharp cutting edge"; *ḥadd* a limit, *ḥudūd* limits, *ḥaddād* a blacksmith — and iron takes its name from its sharpness.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is that the two sūras take hold of iron at two different points, and this comparison must be recorded:**

| | **al-Ḥadīd 57/25** | **Sabaʾ 34/10** |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | *Wa **anzalna**'l-ḥadīd* — We sent down | *Wa **alannā** lahu'l-ḥadīd* — We softened |
| For whom | **People** — general | **David** — a single person |
| Quality | *Fīhi baʾsun shadīdun wa manāfiʿu li'n-nās* | **The lifting** of its hardness |
| Its work | **To keep the measure** — the third leg of justice | **To weave armour** — protection |

**At `057-hadid.md` it was treated how iron is set together with the Book and the balance as one of the three legs of justice.** I rely on that treatment.

**And I record the difference here as my own reading; its support is the third row of the table above:** in al-Ḥadīd iron is named **for its hardness** — *baʾsun shadīd*. **In Sabaʾ the same substance is named for the removal of its hardness.** That is, the two verses take up two opposite qualities of the same substance: **one its staying hard, the other its growing soft.** And both produce a benefit.

**I draw no conclusion about the history of metallurgy from this expression of the verse.** The working of iron with fire, its being heated and beaten, is a known craft, and the verse does not give a description of a craft. **No matching with natural science is constructed;** what the verse says is that something was made possible for David.

## 34/11 — أَنِ ٱعْمَلْ سَٰبِغَٰتٍ وَقَدِّرْ فِى ٱلسَّرْدِ وَٱعْمَلُوا۟ صَٰلِحًا إِنِّى بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ

*Ani'mal sābighātin wa qaddir fi's-sard, waʿmalū ṣāliḥā, innī bimā taʿmalūna baṣīr*

"'**Make wide coats of mail, and keep the weave to measure. Do righteous deeds;** I see what you do.'"

### Three commands, one verse

| Command | To whom | About what |
|---|---|---|
| *Iʿmal sābighāt* | **Singular** — David | **Craft** |
| *Qaddir fi's-sard* | **Singular** — David | **Measure** |
| *Waʿmalū ṣāliḥā* | **Plural** — all of them | **Deed** |

**The most striking place in the verse is the change of number in the third command, and I record this as my own reading:** the first two commands are singular and concern a **craft**; the third command shifts suddenly to the plural and becomes a command about **conduct**.

**And the two commands use the same verb: *iʿmal* (make) and *iʿmalū* (do).** **That is, making armour and doing righteous deeds are named with a single word: ع-م-ل.**

**This choice of word must be recorded.** The verse does not set handwork and righteous deeds up as two separate domains — **it makes them two objects of the same verb.** And at `035-fatir.md` 35/10 (*wa'l-ʿamalu'ṣ-ṣāliḥu yarfaʿuh*) the carrying power of deeds was treated; **I rely on that treatment.**

**And this joins with the command to give thanks later in the sūra:** in the thirteenth verse Solomon will be told *iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā* — **the same verb again, this time with thanks as its object.**

| Verse | Object of the verb *ع-م-ل* |
|---|---|
| **11** | *Sābighāt* — **armour** |
| **11** | *Ṣāliḥā* — **a righteous deed** |
| **13** | *Shukrā* — **thanks** |

**The same verb in three verses, with three different objects: a craft, a conduct, a disposition.** I record this as my own reading, and its support is the repetition of the verb: **the sūra names thanks not as a feeling but as something *done*.**

### سَٰبِغَٰت — root: ص/س-ب-غ

**The root of the word is س-ب-غ. (It is not to be confused with ص-ب-غ; the second is *ṣibgha* — the root of dyeing, of giving colour.) The sense of the root س-ب-غ: to be ample, to be full and wide, to be long enough to cover a thing over.**

*Sābigh* — ample, full, complete. **And the same root is used in the Qurʾān for favour:** *wa **asbagha** ʿalaykum niʿamah* (Luqmān 31/20) — "He spread His favours over you abundantly."

**The form of the word in the verse is striking: *sābighāt* — plural, feminine, and its *mawṣūf* (the noun it qualifies) is not stated.** The word "armour" does not occur in the verse. **What is said is only the attribute: "the ample ones".**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** what is said is not the name of the object but **the property required of it**. That is, the command is not "make armour" but **"make things that cover fully".** That armour is meant is understood from the next command (the measure of the weave).

### وَقَدِّرْ فِى ٱلسَّرْدِ — root: س-ر-د

**The concrete sense of the root: to set things out one after another, in regular sequence.** *Sard* — to link rings into one another in a row; the weave of a coat of mail. **And the same root is used in Arabic for "to recount a discourse without a break, one thing after another"** — close to the English "to reel off".

**That the word is used for the weave of mail is worth recording:** chain mail consists of small rings hooked into one another. **So *sard* is the ordering of the rings.**

**قَدِّرْ** — root **ق-د-ر**: **to set a measure, to determine an amount.** An imperative of Form II: "get its measure right".

**And the command itself is a rule of craft. The lexicographers explain it thus:** the rings of a coat of mail must be neither too thick (it becomes heavy, it cannot be carried) nor too thin (it is pierced). **The measure lies between two excesses.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is that the two commands of the verse stand side by side:** the first command asks for **amplitude** (*sābighāt* — let it cover fully), the second asks for **measure** (*qaddir* — let its rings be set right). **Amplitude and measure are set as two conditions of the same work.** And the command that follows immediately is righteous deeds. **That is, in giving the rule of a craft the text has also given the rule of a conduct: wide enough, but not without measure.**

**The support of this reading is the verse's own sequence; I do not rely on a transmitted report.**

**And the root *ق-د-ر* will occur twice more in the sūra:** in the eighteenth verse (*wa qaddarnā fīha's-sayr* — We set the travel to measure) and in the thirty-sixth and thirty-ninth verses (*yabsuṭu'r-rizqa … wa yaqdir*). **I shall tabulate this at the end.**

**إِنِّى بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ** — and after the plural command the verse ends with a plural watching: *bimā **taʿmalūna*** — "what you do". **The one who asks for measure in the craft and the one who sees the righteousness of the deed are the same subject in the same sentence.**

## 34/12 — وَلِسُلَيْمَٰنَ ٱلرِّيحَ غُدُوُّهَا شَهْرٌ وَرَوَاحُهَا شَهْرٌ وَأَسَلْنَا لَهُۥ عَيْنَ ٱلْقِطْرِ وَمِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ مَن يَعْمَلُ بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِۦ وَمَن يَزِغْ مِنْهُمْ عَنْ أَمْرِنَا نُذِقْهُ مِنْ عَذَابِ ٱلسَّعِيرِ

*Wa li-Sulaymāna'r-rīḥa ghuduwwuhā shahrun wa rawāḥuhā shahr, wa asalnā lahū ʿayna'l-qiṭr, wa mina'l-jinni man yaʿmalu bayna yadayhi bi-idhni rabbih, wa man yazigh minhum ʿan amrinā nudhiqhu min ʿadhābi's-saʿīr*

"**And to Solomon the wind: its going out in the morning a month, its coming back in the evening a month.** **We made the spring of molten copper flow for him.** Some of the jinn worked before him by his Lord's leave. **Whoever of them swerves from Our command, We make him taste the punishment of the blaze.**"

### غُدُوُّهَا شَهْرٌ وَرَوَاحُهَا شَهْرٌ

**The clause is made of two equal halves, and two words are set against one another:**

| Word | Root | Time |
|---|---|---|
| *Ghuduww* | **غ-د-و** | **Morning** — the going out at the start of the day |
| *Rawāḥ* | **ر-و-ح** | **Late afternoon** — the coming back at the end of the day |

**These two words are a pair in Arabic, and together they mean "the whole of the day".** They correspond to the English expression "morning and evening". **At `040-gafir.md` 40/46 the pair *ghuduwwan wa ʿashiyyā* (morning and evening) was treated, and it was recorded there that the same two times are used in two different scenes.** I rely on that treatment.

**The root of *rawāḥ*, ر-و-ح, is at the same time the root of *rīḥ* (wind) and of *rūḥ*.** **That is, there is an overlap in the sentence close to wordplay:** *ar-**rīḥ**a … wa **rawāḥ**uhā*. I record this as an observation about the language; I build no ruling about the meaning on it.

**And the measure itself is worth recording: *shahrun … shahrun* — a month, a month.** **The measure is given not in distance but in time.** That is, the verse does not say "so many leagues"; it says **how long a journey that would normally take a month took.** The measure is built on the addressee's own experience.

**I do not enter into the reports about the details of the story.** How the wind was used, where it went, is not in the verse; **I do not manufacture a detail the text does not give.**

### وَأَسَلْنَا لَهُۥ عَيْنَ ٱلْقِطْرِ

**أَسَلْنَا** — root **س-ي-ل**: **to flow.** Form IV: We made flow. **And the same root will occur four verses later in the name of a disaster: *sayla'l-ʿarim* (16) — "the flood of the ʿArim".**

**I record this repetition and give it as my own reading:** the same root is used in two opposite directions for the two peoples of the sūra.

| Verse | Expression | What flows | To whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12** | *Wa **asalnā** lahū ʿayna'l-qiṭr* | **Molten metal** — a favour | **Solomon** |
| **16** | *Fa-arsalnā ʿalayhim **sayla**'l-ʿarim* | **A flood** — a disaster | **Sabaʾ** |

**The same verbal root: one is "We made it flow for him", the other "We sent it upon them". When the particle changes, the direction changes: *lahū* (in his favour) / *ʿalayhim* (against them).** This is a contrast that can be verified from the text.

**ٱلْقِطْر** — the sense the lexicographers give: **molten copper** (some say "molten metal" generally). **The word is connected with the root *qaṭr* (to drip): the thing that drips, that flows.**

**And the word *ʿayn* must be recorded: *ʿayna'l-qiṭr* — "the spring of copper". *ʿAyn* in Arabic means both eye and spring. That is, the molten metal is named as though it were a spring** — the word used for a source of water has been carried over to metal.

**Its link with the preceding verse is worth recording:** for David iron was softened, for Solomon copper was made to flow. **Two metals for two prophets, and both in the state where their hardness has been undone.**

### وَمَن يَزِغْ مِنْهُمْ عَنْ أَمْرِنَا

**ز-ي-غ** — the concrete sense of the root: **to swerve from the true direction, to slip, to go crooked.** The lexicographers record that the word is used also of **the eye's wandering off true**.

**And the placing of the clause must be recorded:** while enumerating what was given to Solomon, the verse inserts **a note about punishment**. **That is, what was given is not presented as an unlimited disposal;** the command to which the workers are bound is named in the same sentence.

**A note about the jinn, per the method (`USLUP.md`):** at `072-cin.md` the limit was set that one does not go beyond what the text states about unseen beings. **I rely on that.** What the verse says is this: they were working, they were working **by leave**, and whoever swerved from the command had his recompense.

## 34/13 — يَعْمَلُونَ لَهُۥ مَا يَشَآءُ مِن مَّحَٰرِيبَ وَتَمَٰثِيلَ وَجِفَانٍ كَٱلْجَوَابِ وَقُدُورٍ رَّاسِيَٰتٍ ٱعْمَلُوٓا۟ ءَالَ دَاوُۥدَ شُكْرًا وَقَلِيلٌ مِّنْ عِبَادِىَ ٱلشَّكُورُ

*Yaʿmalūna lahū mā yashāʾu min maḥārība wa tamāthīla wa jifānin ka'l-jawābi wa qudūrin rāsiyāt, iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā, wa qalīlun min ʿibādiya'sh-shakūr*

"They made for him whatever he wished: **lofty structures, images, basins like reservoirs, and cauldrons fixed in place.** '**O family of David! Work in thankfulness!**' **But few of My servants are thankful.**"

### The axis of thanks in the sūra is named here

**This verse is where the backbone of the sūra is given its name.** For twelve verses what was given had been enumerated; here what is asked in return is stated.

**And the form of the command must be recorded: *iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā*.**

**The lexicographers dwell on the inflection of the word, and three explanations are transmitted:**

| Reading | What *shukrā* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Direct object** (*mafʿūl bihi*) — the object of the verb | "Work thanks, do thanks" |
| 2 | **Object of purpose** (*mafʿūl li-ajlihi*) — stating a cause | "Work in order to give thanks" |
| 3 | **Circumstantial accusative** (*ḥāl*) — stating a state | "Work while giving thanks" |

**All three explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**But what is worth recording is what all three readings share, and I give it as my own reading:** whichever is taken, **thanks is named together with a *verb*.** The verse does not say "give thanks" (*ushkurū*) — it says **"work, thanks"**. In the eleventh verse David was told *iʿmal* (make); here the same verb comes back to the family in the plural.

**That is, from beginning to end the sūra builds thanks as something *done*.** And in the fifteenth verse the command given to the people of Sabaʾ will come with a different word: *wa'shkurū lah* — there the verb is the verb of thanks itself. **The two commands can be set side by side:**

| To whom | Command | Form |
|---|---|---|
| **The family of David (13)** | *Iʿmalū … shukrā* | Together with **work** |
| **Sabaʾ (15)** | *Kulū … wa'shkurū lah* | Together with **eating** |

**Two peoples, two commands, one root.** And the difference is this: one is commanded to give thanks **while producing**, the other **while consuming**. I record this as my own reading; its support is the verbs standing beside the two commands.

### وَقَلِيلٌ مِّنْ عِبَادِىَ ٱلشَّكُورُ

**"Few of My servants are thankful."**

**The word order of the sentence must be recorded: the predicate (*qalīl*) has been brought forward, the subject (*ash-shakūr*) left to the end.** In Arabic this fronting conveys **emphasis**: **fewness is the first word of the sentence.**

**شَكُور** — the intensive form (*faʿūl*): **one who gives thanks much.** So the sentence does not exactly say "the thankful are few"; it says **"those who give much thanks are few"**.

**This detail matters, and I record it as my own reading; its support is the pattern itself:** the ruling of fewness is set not on thanks itself but on **its continuity**. Giving thanks once may be common; what the word describes is thanks that has become a habit.

**And this pattern will meet us twice more in the sūra:**

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **13** | *Ash-**shakūr*** | The servant who is **few** |
| **17** | *Al-**kafūr*** | The one who **will receive his recompense** |
| **19** | *Ṣabbārin **shakūr*** | The one who **will take the lesson** |

**All three are intensive forms:** *shakūr* and *kafūr* on *faʿūl*, *ṣabbār* on *faʿʿāl*. **That is, in three separate places the sūra names qualities that have hardened into habits.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

### The structures enumerated

**مَحَٰرِيب** — the plural of *miḥrāb*. **The word cannot be reduced to the present-day sense of "mihrab", the prayer niche.** The primary sense the lexicographers give: **the highest, best-protected, foremost part of a house or a palace.** It is connected with the root **ح-ر-ب**, and the lexicographers explain the connection thus: the place that is defended, that is fought over. **At `038-sad.md` 38/21 the same word occurred as David's chamber** (*idh tasawwaru'l-miḥrāb*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**تَمَٰثِيل** — the plural of *timthāl*; root **م-ث-ل**: **to resemble, to make the like of a thing.** The word is thus wider than "statue": **to bring out the image or likeness of something.**

**There is a legal debate about this word, and per the method (`USLUP.md`) I issue no legal ruling.** What is to be recorded is this: **the verse states that these things were made for Solomon; it does not frame a sentence either approving or forbidding them.** Differing views are transmitted on the question in the classical commentaries; **I content myself with transmitting them.**

**جِفَانٍ كَٱلْجَوَابِ** — *jifān*, the plural of *jafna*: **a large bowl, a trough.** *Jawāb* is the plural of *jābiya*: **a basin in which water is stored, a cistern.** **Hence "bowls like reservoirs".**

**قُدُورٍ رَّاسِيَٰتٍ** — *qudūr*, the plural of *qidr*: **a cauldron.** *Rāsiyāt* — root **ر-س-و**: **to stand fast, to be anchored.** The same root is used of mountains as well (*rawāsī*). **Hence "cauldrons that cannot be lifted from their place"** — too large to be carried.

**The order of the list is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** lofty structures → images → bowls → cauldrons. **The series descends from architecture to the kitchen.** And the command that comes at the end of the list is thanks. **That is, the command to give thanks comes immediately after the most concrete objects, not after the splendour.**

## 34/14 — فَلَمَّا قَضَيْنَا عَلَيْهِ ٱلْمَوْتَ مَا دَلَّهُمْ عَلَىٰ مَوْتِهِۦٓ إِلَّا دَآبَّةُ ٱلْأَرْضِ تَأْكُلُ مِنسَأَتَهُۥ فَلَمَّا خَرَّ تَبَيَّنَتِ ٱلْجِنُّ أَن لَّوْ كَانُوا۟ يَعْلَمُونَ ٱلْغَيْبَ مَا لَبِثُوا۟ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْمُهِينِ

*Fa-lammā qaḍaynā ʿalayhi'l-mawta mā dallahum ʿalā mawtihī illā dābbatu'l-arḍi taʾkulu minsaʾatah, fa-lammā kharra tabayyanati'l-jinnu an law kānū yaʿlamūna'l-ghayba mā labithū fi'l-ʿadhābi'l-muhīn*

"**When We decreed his death, nothing showed them his death but a creature of the earth eating his staff.** **When he fell, it became clear that the jinn did not know the unseen:** had they known it, they would not have remained in that humiliating punishment."

### The conclusion the verse itself draws

**A great many details are transmitted around this story: how long he stayed thus, when the worm gnawed the staff, what the jinn did.** **Per the method (`USLUP.md`) I do not go into these details; I do not add to the verse what is not in it.**

**The verse itself states a conclusion, and all the weight lies there:** *an law kānū yaʿlamūna'l-ghayba mā labithū fi'l-ʿadhābi'l-muhīn* — **"had they known the unseen, they would not have remained in that humiliating punishment."**

**That is, the event is told not as a story but as a *proof*.** And the structure of the proof is this:

| Step | Statement |
|---|---|
| Premise | The jinn **went on** working |
| Observation | Solomon **had died** |
| Conclusion | **They did not know** — that is, they do not know the unseen |

**This is a piece of reasoning close to the pattern called *modus tollens* in logic:** had they known, this would not have happened; this happened; therefore they did not know. **The verse draws the inference itself, and builds it with the particle *law* (a condition that was not fulfilled).**

**And the place of this conclusion in the sūra must be recorded. The line of the unseen in the sūra runs thus:**

| Verse | Expression | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *ʿĀlimi'l-**ghayb*** | **He** is the one who knows the unseen |
| **14** | *Law kānū yaʿlamūna'l-**ghayb*** | The jinn do **not** know the unseen |
| **48** | *ʿAllāmu'l-**ghuyūb*** | **The great knower** of things unseen |
| **53** | *Wa yaqdhifūna bi'l-**ghaybi** min makānin baʿīd* | **Throwing stones** at the unseen from far off |

**Four occurrences, in four separate places in the sūra.** And the fourteenth verse holds a special place in this series: **the one concrete piece of evidence is there.** The third verse announced that the unseen is known; the fourteenth verse shows by an event **by whom the unseen is not known.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the verse's own last clause:** the story is told not to describe Solomon's greatness but **to show where knowledge of the unseen is not.** And precisely for that reason it stands in the middle of the sūra's line of the unseen.

### مِنسَأَتَهُ — root: ن-س-أ

**The sense of the root: to postpone, to delay; and to drive, to push.** *Nasaʾa* — he postponed. **And *minsaʾa* is on the *mifʿala* pattern: the instrument with which a work is done.**

**The lexicographers explain the word thus: the staff with which a shepherd *drives* his animals before him.** That is, the staff is a tool used not for leaning on but **for driving**; and its name comes from that work.

**I transmit this explanation as a relation the lexicographers have built; I do not present it as a settled etymological ruling.**

**And the choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say *ʿaṣā* — that is the word that occurs in the stories of Moses (Mūsā). **Here *minsaʾa* is used.** That the word is different is a detail the lexicographers record; **beyond that I frame no ruling about the meaning.**

### دَآبَّةُ ٱلْأَرْضِ

**د-ب-ب** — the concrete sense of the root is **to move slowly and lightly, to stir upon the ground**. **The word was treated at `045-casiye.md` 45/4 and at `035-fatir.md` 35/45** (a moving living thing). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The construction *dābbatu'l-arḍ* — "the creature of the earth".** The sense the lexicographers commonly give is **a wood-worm, a termite**; the word has been understood so in this context.

**This must not be confused with *dābbatan mina'l-arḍ*, which occurs elsewhere in the Qurʾān** (al-Naml 27/82). **The two constructions are different, and their contexts are different too; I do not read one through the other.**

**And the difference of scale the verse sets up is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** for two verses the wind, molten metal, giant cauldrons and lofty structures were enumerated. **Then that whole tableau comes to an end with a staff gnawed by a worm.** The word order of the sentence stresses this: *mā dallahum … **illā** dābbatu'l-arḍ* — **a negation plus an exception.** That is, what gave the news was none of all the powers enumerated; **it was only that.**

**فَلَمَّا خَرَّ** — *kharra*: **to collapse, to fall.** The word is used in the Qurʾān for prostration as well (*kharra sājidan*). **Here the fall itself is described**, and the fall becomes the sign that will give the news.

**ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْمُهِينِ** — root **ه-و-ن**: **lightness, worthlessness, being brought low.** *Muhīn* — the active participle of Form IV: **that which humiliates.** **That is, the quality of the punishment named here is not its severity but its *power to degrade*.**

## 34/15 — لَقَدْ كَانَ لِسَبَإٍ فِى مَسْكَنِهِمْ ءَايَةٌ جَنَّتَانِ عَن يَمِينٍ وَشِمَالٍ كُلُوا۟ مِن رِّزْقِ رَبِّكُمْ وَٱشْكُرُوا۟ لَهُۥ بَلْدَةٌ طَيِّبَةٌ وَرَبٌّ غَفُورٌ

*Laqad kāna li-Sabaʾin fī maskanihim āyah, jannatāni ʿan yamīnin wa shimāl, kulū min rizqi rabbikum wa'shkurū lah, baldatun ṭayyibatun wa rabbun ghafūr*

"**For Sabaʾ there was a sign in their dwelling place: two gardens, one on the right and one on the left.** '**Eat of the provision of your Lord and give thanks to Him. A good land, and a forgiving Lord!**'"

### The only verse in which the name of the sūra occurs

**The name of the sūra occurs in this verse, in a single word: *li-Sabaʾ*.** And in the whole of the sūra the people are never named again.

**سَبَإ** — the name of a community that lived in southern Arabia, in the region of present-day Yemen. **It occurs in two places in the Qurʾān: here and in Sūrat al-Naml (27/22).** It is recorded that the word is transmitted both as the name of a person and as the name of a tribe; **I issue no settled ruling.**

**There is a variant reading in the inflection of the word, and it must be recorded:**

| Reading | Form | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| *Li-Saba**ʾin*** (with tanwīn) | **Fully declinable** — taken as the name of a tribe or a town | The widespread reading |
| *Li-Saba**ʾa*** (without tanwīn) | **Not fully declinable** — a proper noun plus feminine gender or foreign origin | The second reading transmitted |

**Both readings are transmitted and the sense does not change; I do not impose a preference.**

### جَنَّتَانِ عَن يَمِينٍ وَشِمَالٍ

**"Two gardens, one on the right and one on the left."**

**How the description is built is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the verse does not describe the size of the gardens, their produce, or their system of irrigation. **The only thing it describes is position: on the right and on the left.**

**This is a description that puts a human being at its centre.** Right and left are fixed with respect to where an observer stands. **The gardens, that is, are named as they appear to the person standing among them** — and cultivated land stretching along the two slopes of a valley looks just so.

**And the word *āya* comes in the singular: *āyatun* — a sign.** There are two gardens, but the sign is **one**. **That is, what is counted is not the gardens but the scene the gardens make.**

### كُلُوا۟ … وَٱشْكُرُوا۟

**Two commands: *kulū* (eat) and *wa'shkurū* (give thanks).**

**To whom the command is spoken is not stated in the verse — the wording is transmitted directly.** And what is worth recording is that **the first command is not a prohibition but a permission**: *kulū min rizqi rabbikum.* **That is, thanks is asked for not as abandoning the favour but as using it while keeping it attached to its source.**

**This should be read together with the command in the thirteenth verse:** there it was *iʿmalū … shukrā* (work, thanks). **Both commands tie thanks to an act: one to producing, the other to eating.**

### بَلْدَةٌ طَيِّبَةٌ وَرَبٌّ غَفُورٌ

**The sentence consists of two noun phrases and has no verb.** In Arabic such sentences convey **a fixed state**.

**And the setting of the two elements side by side must be recorded:**

| Element | Domain |
|---|---|
| *Baldatun ṭayyiba* | **Place** — geography, soil, climate |
| *Rabbun ghafūr* | **Relationship** — a forgiving Lord |

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the joining of the two phrases with a *wāw*:** the verse sets the two conditions of prosperity side by side — **a good place and a forgiving Lord.** The soil is not named on its own; and the attribute that belongs to the relationship is **forgiveness.**

**ط-ي-ب** — the sense of the root: **to be pleasant, clean, good.** *Ṭayyib* is used of what is good both in taste and in quality. **The root was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/10 (*al-kalimu'ṭ-ṭayyib* — the good word);** I rely on that treatment. **Here the same attribute is given to a land.**

**And how the next verse will undo this picture must be recorded: in the sixteenth verse the gardens will change, but the name of the land will never be mentioned again.**

## 34/16-17 — فَأَعْرَضُوا۟ فَأَرْسَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سَيْلَ ٱلْعَرِمِ وَبَدَّلْنَٰهُم بِجَنَّتَيْهِمْ جَنَّتَيْنِ ذَوَاتَىْ أُكُلٍ خَمْطٍ وَأَثْلٍ وَشَىْءٍ مِّن سِدْرٍ قَلِيلٍ · ذَٰلِكَ جَزَيْنَٰهُم بِمَا كَفَرُوا۟ وَهَلْ نُجَٰزِىٓ إِلَّا ٱلْكَفُورَ

*Fa-aʿraḍū fa-arsalnā ʿalayhim sayla'l-ʿarim, wa baddalnāhum bi-jannatayhim jannatayni dhawātay ukulin khamṭin wa athlin wa shayʾin min sidrin qalīl · Dhālika jazaynāhum bimā kafarū, wa hal nujāzī illa'l-kafūr*

"**But they turned away. So We sent upon them the flood of the ʿArim, and We exchanged their two gardens for two gardens bearing bitter fruit, tamarisk, and a little of the lote tree.** **Thus We recompensed them for their ingratitude. Do We recompense any but the ungrateful?**"

### فَأَعْرَضُوا۟ — a turning point in a single word

**The verse overturns the whole picture of the fifteenth verse with a single verb: *fa-aʿraḍū*.**

**ع-ر-ض** — the concrete sense of the root: **to turn one's breadth towards something, to turn sideways.** *ʿArḍ* — breadth, width. **So *iʿrāḍ* is not exactly to turn the face away — it is *to turn side on*: to show the other not your face but your flank.**

**This picture is worth recording, and I give it as an observation about the language:** the verb describes not a refusal but an **indifference**. Not opposing, but **turning and not looking.**

**And two *fāʾ* particles come one after the other: *fa*-aʿraḍū *fa*-arsalnā.** In Arabic *fāʾ* conveys **following immediately**. **That is, no interval is left between the two clauses: they turned away, We sent.**

### سَيْلَ ٱلْعَرِم

**The lexicographers differ over the sense of this construction, and the disagreement must be set out in a table:**

| View | What *ʿarim* is | Sense of the construction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A dam, a barrage** | "The flood of the dam" — the flood that came when the dam gave way |
| 2 | **Violent, raging** (an adjective) | "The raging flood" |
| 3 | **The valley / the water behind the dam** | "The flood of the valley" |
| 4 | **A rat or mole** (the animal that pierced the dam) | A pointer to the cause of the flood |

**The fourth view is transmitted in some sources; I issue no ruling on its soundness. The first three are more widespread. I do not impose a preference.**

**I make no firm claim about the historical background, and I record this plainly.** That there was a great dam in southern Arabia and that it collapsed at one period **is transmitted**; on this matter both the classical sources and later research name different dates and different phases of collapse. **The verse gives no date and gives no name of a dam.** What the text gives is the event itself and its outcome; **beyond that I do not step outside the language of "it is transmitted".**

**And the verb *arsalnā* must be recorded: "We sent".** The same verb is used in the sūra for sending a prophet as well (28, 34, 44). **That is, the verb "to send" occurs in the sūra both for a warner and for a flood.** I record this as an observation about the language.

### Two gardens, two gardens

**The build of the sentence is extremely regular, and this regularity must be recorded:**

| | Before (15) | After (16) |
|---|---|---|
| Number | *Jannatāni* — **two gardens** | *Jannatayni* — **two gardens** |
| Position | *ʿAn yamīnin wa shimāl* | — |
| Produce | — (not stated) | *Ukulin khamṭin wa athlin wa sidrin qalīl* |
| Name | ***Janna*** | ***Janna*** |

**The most striking point is that the name does not change.** The verse does not say "We turned their gardens into desert". **It says "two gardens" again** — but puts three things inside them: bitter fruit, tamarisk, and **a little** lote.

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the repetition of the word *jannatayn*:** the punishment is described not as the taking away of what was owned but as **the emptying out of the thing that keeps its name.** The garden is still a garden; there is nothing left in it to eat.

**أُكُلٍ خَمْطٍ** — *ukul*: fruit, produce. *Khamṭ* — the sense the lexicographers give: **bitter, sour fruit that has lost its taste**; some mean by it a particular thorny tree. **Both explanations are transmitted.**

**أَثْل** — **the tamarisk.** It grows in the desert, it gives shade, its wood is used; **but its fruit is not eaten.**

**سِدْر** — **the lote or jujube tree** (*sidr*). Its fruit is eaten, but there is little of it. **And the word is qualified by the adjective *qalīl* (little).**

**The order of the three is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** fruit that has become inedible → a tree never eaten from → a tree eaten from **a little**. **The list moves from the useless to the barely useful, and stops there.** So the picture is not total ruin — **it is insufficiency.**

**And the word *qalīl* must be recorded:** in the thirteenth verse it was said *wa **qalīlun** min ʿibādiya'sh-shakūr*. **The same word comes three verses later for the lote trees that remain.**

| Verse | What *qalīl* qualifies |
|---|---|
| **13** | **The thankful servant** — few |
| **16** | **The fruit that remains** — little |

**I record this overlap as my own reading; its support is that the same word occurs twice, three verses apart:** the fewness of thanks and the smallness of what provision remains are named with the same adjective.

### وَهَلْ نُجَٰزِىٓ إِلَّا ٱلْكَفُورَ

**"Do We recompense any but the ungrateful?"**

**The sentence is an *istifhām inkārī*** (a question that expects no answer and states a negation): "We do not."

**And the change of verb must be recorded:**

| Sentence | Verb | Form |
|---|---|---|
| *Dhālika **jazaynā**hum* | *Jazā* | **Form I** |
| *Wa hal **nujāzī*** | *Jazā* | **Form III (*mujāzāt*)** |

**The same root, two different forms — and the lexicographers record the difference.** Form III (*mufāʿala*) conveys **reciprocity**: *mujāzāt* is to give a return equal to the deed done.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the first clause narrates the event (We recompensed them), the second lays down the rule (**an equal return** is given only to the ungrateful). **The change of word marks the passage from event to rule.**

**ٱلْكَفُور** — an intensive form: **utterly ungrateful.** The exact opposite of *ash-shakūr* in the thirteenth verse, on the same pattern. **The two words stand facing one another four verses apart.**

**And a note on how the word is rendered: the root ك-ف-ر here means "ingratitude" as much as "denial".** The concrete sense of the root is **to cover**; ingratitude is covering over a favour. **And the context supports this: what is enumerated before the clause is favours.**

## 34/18-19 — وَجَعَلْنَا بَيْنَهُمْ وَبَيْنَ ٱلْقُرَى ٱلَّتِى بَٰرَكْنَا فِيهَا قُرًى ظَٰهِرَةً وَقَدَّرْنَا فِيهَا ٱلسَّيْرَ سِيرُوا۟ فِيهَا لَيَالِىَ وَأَيَّامًا ءَامِنِينَ · فَقَالُوا۟ رَبَّنَا بَٰعِدْ بَيْنَ أَسْفَارِنَا وَظَلَمُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ أَحَادِيثَ وَمَزَّقْنَٰهُمْ كُلَّ مُمَزَّقٍ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتٍ لِّكُلِّ صَبَّارٍ شَكُورٍ

*Wa jaʿalnā baynahum wa bayna'l-qura'llatī bāraknā fīhā quran ẓāhiratan wa qaddarnā fīha's-sayr, sīrū fīhā layāliya wa ayyāman āminīn · Fa-qālū rabbanā bāʿid bayna asfārinā wa ẓalamū anfusahum fa-jaʿalnāhum aḥādītha wa mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq, inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr*

"**Between them and the towns We had blessed We set towns within sight of one another, and We measured out the journeying between them:** '**Travel through them by night and by day in safety.**' **But they said: 'Our Lord! Set our journeys far apart!'** And so they wronged themselves. **We turned them into tales and tore them utterly to pieces.** **In that there are signs for everyone who endures much and gives thanks much.**"

### قُرًى ظَٰهِرَةً — towns within sight of one another

**The construction describes an order of roads.** *Ẓāhira* — root **ظ-ه-ر**: **to appear, to be in the open, the back.**

**Two senses given by the lexicographers are transmitted:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | Towns **visible from one another** — before one ends, the next comes into view |
| 2 | Towns **on the road, in plain sight** — one travels without straying |

**The two readings do not exclude one another; I do not impose a preference.**

**And what is described is a concrete provision:** a route with settlements to halt at along the way. **That is, the road is described stripped of the risks of a desert crossing.**

**وَقَدَّرْنَا فِيهَا ٱلسَّيْرَ** — "We measured out the journeying". **The root ق-د-ر occurred in the eleventh verse in the command given to David (*qaddir fi's-sard*).** **The same root, the same form (Form II), for two different works:**

| Verse | Expression | Measure of what |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | *Wa **qaddir** fi's-sard* | The measure of **the rings of a coat of mail** — a command |
| **18** | *Wa **qaddarnā** fīha's-sayr* | The measure of **the distance between halts** — a statement |
| **36, 39** | *Yabsuṭu'r-rizqa … wa **yaqdir*** | The measure of **provision** |

**The same root in three places.** **I record this as my own reading, and its support is the three uses of the root in the sūra:** the sūra repeats the idea of "measure" in a craft, in a geography and in a distribution of provision. **And in all three the measure is named as a part of the thing given.**

**سِيرُوا۟ فِيهَا لَيَالِىَ وَأَيَّامًا ءَامِنِينَ** — "travel through them by night and by day in safety."

**The order is worth recording: *layāliya wa ayyāmā* — nights first, then days.** **I record this as an observation:** the dangerous time on a journey is the night. **The verse speaks of safety beginning there.**

**ءَامِنِين** — root **أ-م-ن**: **fearlessness, security.** And the word comes as a *ḥāl*, a circumstantial accusative: travel — **while in safety.** That is, safety is described not as a promise but as a condition already in hand.

### رَبَّنَا بَٰعِدْ بَيْنَ أَسْفَارِنَا — the distance that was asked for

**The clause comes in the form of a *prayer*, and this must be recorded: *rabbanā* — "Our Lord!"**

**That is, what the verse describes is not a sentence of rebellion but a sentence of *request*.** And what is requested is **distance.**

**ب-ع-د** — the sense of the root is **remoteness**. *Bāʿid* — an imperative of Form III: "open up the space between them, set them far apart."

**And the ground of the request is not stated in the verse.** It is widely transmitted that in the classical commentaries this request is explained as **a weariness brought on by prosperity** or as **comfort become ordinary**. **Since the verse gives no ground, I do not settle on one either.**

**But something can be said about the structure of the sentence, and I record this as my own reading; its support is the preceding verse:** what the eighteenth verse describes is that the journey had been **made easy** — the towns close together, the distances measured, the nights safe. **What is asked for in the nineteenth verse is precisely the lifting of all that.** The request, that is, is not for a lack to be made good but for **an ease already given to be taken back.**

**And this is the exact opposite of the sūra's axis of thanks:** in the fifteenth verse it was said "eat and give thanks". **The sentence that comes in the nineteenth verse expresses discomfort at the gift itself.**

### The course of the root ب-ع-د through the sūra

**And now the journey of this word through the sūra must be recorded, because the same root occurs in four separate places:**

| Verse | Expression | Who / what |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *Aḍ-ḍalāli'l-**baʿīd*** | The **error** of those who disbelieve — far |
| **19** | *Rabbanā **bāʿid** bayna asfārinā* | The **request** of Sabaʾ — distance |
| **52** | *Min makānin **baʿīd*** | **Reaching out** from far away |
| **53** | *Min makānin **baʿīd*** | **Throwing stones** from far away |

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the four occurrences of the root:** the sūra has a people who ask for distance (19), and at the end a company trying to reach across a distance (52-53). **The distance that was asked for comes back as the distance that cannot be closed.**

### فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ أَحَادِيثَ — "We turned them into tales"

**This is one of the most striking sentences in the sūra.**

**أَحَادِيث** — the plural of *ḥadīth*; **root ح-د-ث.** **The root was analysed in detail at `093-duha.md` 93/11 (*wa ammā bi-niʿmati rabbika fa-ḥaddith*).** What was recorded there holds here, and its summary is this: **the primary sense of the root is "to be new, to come into being afterwards"; *ḥādith* what comes to be later, *ḥadath* an event, *ḥadīth* both what is new and a saying or a piece of news.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the sense carried by the plural of the word.** *Aḥādīth* in Arabic means **things that are told over and over** — and in this context it means **"a narrative, a story, a cautionary tale, a report that circulates on people's tongues".**

**And the build of the clause is this: *jaʿalnāhum aḥādītha* — "We made them *ḥadīth*s".** That is, a community is **turned into narrative.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the root's principal sense as analysed in al-Ḍuḥā:** at the core of the root lies **being new**. **A people's turning into "ḥadīth" is its losing its real existence and becoming something reconstructed at every telling.** What was concrete is gone; what remains lives in words.

**And the character of the punishment changes here:** in the sixteenth verse their gardens were exchanged — that is a material loss. **Here the people themselves disappear and what is left is "a thing that is told".**

**What this word says to the present is worth recording:** the measure of a community is, in the end, what is told about it. **The verse names this as a punishment** — that is, being spoken about is not the same thing as standing.

### وَمَزَّقْنَٰهُمْ كُلَّ مُمَزَّقٍ

**And here the overlap set up with the seventh verse is completed.**

**The construction is word for word the same and was tabulated above in the treatment of 34/7-8; I recall it briefly:**

| Verse | Expression | Subject of the verb |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *Idhā **muzziqtum** kulla mumazzaq* | **Passive** — not stated |
| **19** | *Wa **mazzaqnāhum** kulla mumazzaq* | ***Naḥnu*** — We |

**The same construction twelve verses apart.** Those who disbelieved chose this word to make the resurrection look ridiculous; **the sūra uses the same word for an event that happened in history.**

**That is, the tearing apart is set down not as a remote supposition but as a result that came about.** This is one of the strongest textual data in the sūra, and it is easy to verify: in both verses, *kulla mumazzaq*.

**And I make no firm claim about the historical counterpart of the dispersal.** That this community of southern Arabia scattered to different regions at one period **is transmitted**; the verse does not enumerate the directions or the branches of the dispersal.

### لِّكُلِّ صَبَّارٍ شَكُورٍ

**The verse ends with the backbone word of the sūra.**

| Word | Root | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| *Ṣabbār* | **ص-ب-ر** | *Faʿʿāl* — **one who endures much** |
| *Shakūr* | **ش-ك-ر** | *Faʿūl* — **one who gives thanks much** |

**Two intensive forms side by side.** And that the two attributes are named together is worth recording.

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the story itself:** in the story of Sabaʾ there are two states — **abundance** (15) and **want** (16). **The answer to abundance is thanks, the answer to want is endurance.** The verse describes the one who is to take the lesson as the one who **stands rightly in both states.**

**That is, the pair of attributes corresponds exactly to the two phases of the story.**

**And the number of the word *āya* changes:** in the fifteenth verse it was *āyatun* (singular), here *āyātin* (plural). **The gardens were one sign; what befell them is many signs.** I record this as an observation.

## 34/20-21 — وَلَقَدْ صَدَّقَ عَلَيْهِمْ إِبْلِيسُ ظَنَّهُۥ فَٱتَّبَعُوهُ إِلَّا فَرِيقًا مِّنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · وَمَا كَانَ لَهُۥ عَلَيْهِم مِّن سُلْطَٰنٍ إِلَّا لِنَعْلَمَ مَن يُؤْمِنُ بِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ مِمَّنْ هُوَ مِنْهَا فِى شَكٍّ وَرَبُّكَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ حَفِيظٌ

*Wa laqad ṣaddaqa ʿalayhim Iblīsu ẓannahū fa'ttabaʿūhu illā farīqan mina'l-muʾminīn · Wa mā kāna lahū ʿalayhim min sulṭānin illā li-naʿlama man yuʾminu bi'l-ākhirati mimman huwa minhā fī shakk, wa rabbuka ʿalā kulli shayʾin ḥafīẓ*

"**Iblīs proved his surmise about them true; all of them followed him but a group of the believers.** **Yet he had no compelling power over them.** We did so only that We might **tell apart** the one who believes in the hereafter from the one who is in doubt of it. Your Lord watches over everything."

### صَدَّقَ … ظَنَّهُ — a surmise proved true

**The build of the sentence is unusual and deserves separate attention.**

**The expected wording would have been: "Iblīs led them astray."** **The sentence the verse builds is this: *ṣaddaqa … ẓannahū* — "he proved his surmise true".**

**ص-د-ق** — root: **to be true, for speech to correspond to fact.** Form II (*ṣaddaqa*): **to confirm, to prove true.**

**ظ-ن-ن** — root: **an opinion that is not certain, a supposition.** *Ẓann* in Arabic lies somewhere between knowledge (*ʿilm*) and doubt (*shakk*): **the outweighing probability.**

**The bringing together of the two words must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** what Iblīs had in hand was not knowledge but **a supposition**. He had made a prediction. **And the verse says that the prediction came true** — but in naming who made it come true it does not say that the agent was Iblīs; it says that **they followed**: *fa'ttabaʿūh*.

**That is, the sentence leaves the responsibility not with the one who made the guess but with those who confirmed it.**

**One more note:** the particle *ʿalayhim* — "**against** them". **The coming true of the surmise is named as an outcome that tells against them.**

### The second verse draws the limit of the first

**And the sentence that follows immediately leaves no door open to misunderstanding: *wa mā kāna lahū ʿalayhim min sulṭān*.**

**سُلْطَٰن** — root **س-ل-ط**: **to prevail, to rule.** The word is used both for "proof" and for "compelling power". **Here the context indicates the second.**

**The balance that emerges when the two verses are set side by side is this:**

| Verse | What it says |
|---|---|
| **20** | **The guess held** — they followed |
| **21** | **There was no compulsion** — they followed of themselves |

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is that the second verse follows immediately on the first:** had the first verse been read alone, it could have been understood as an outside power being decisive. **The second verse closes that possibility off.** And this is a structure recorded in several places in the series: the same sentence was framed at `037-saffat.md` 37/30 as well — *wa mā kāna lanā ʿalaykum min sulṭān* — where the speakers were the very party who had been followed.

| Place | Who says it | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| al-Ṣāffāt 37/30 | **Those who were followed** — in the Fire, defending themselves | *Wa mā kāna lanā ʿalaykum min sulṭān* |
| **Sabaʾ 34/21** | **The text itself** — as a ruling | *Wa mā kāna lahū ʿalayhim min sulṭān* |

**The same sentence from two mouths.** What is said in al-Ṣāffāt as a defence is given in Sabaʾ as a statement of fact. This is an overlap between sūras that can be verified.

### إِلَّا لِنَعْلَمَ — to tell apart

**The lexicographers dwell on this part of the sentence, because the expression *li-naʿlama* ("that We may know") suggests at first sight a lack of knowledge.**

**The answers given in the classical period fall under two headings, and I set them out in a table:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 1 | *ʿIlm* here means **coming to light in fact**: knowledge's passing into what has actually happened |
| 2 | The verb has been used in the sense of **telling apart / bringing to light** (*tamyīz*) |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not enter the theological debate and I do not impose a preference.**

**What the wording of the verse itself says is that the distinction is between *two groups*:**

| Group | Expression |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Man **yuʾminu** bi'l-ākhira* — the one who **believes** in the hereafter |
| 2 | *Man huwa minhā **fī shakk*** — the one who is **in doubt** of it |

**How the contrast is built must be recorded: the other side is not called "the one who disbelieves" — it is called "the one in doubt".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the choice of word:** the distinction is drawn not between faith and denial but between **faith and hesitation**. The measure, that is, is not holding a contrary belief; it is **the absence of certainty.**

**And the word *shakk* will return in the last verse of the sūra:** *innahum kānū fī **shakkin** murīb* (54). **The state the sūra names in the twenty-first verse it makes its closing ruling in the fifty-fourth.** This is a link that can be verified within the sūra.

**حَفِيظ** — root **ح-ف-ظ**: **to guard, to watch over, to let nothing slip.** **The root was treated at `070-mearic.md` 70/29 and 70/34;** I rely on that treatment. **The block closes with a name meaning "the watcher over all things"** — and it arrives at the same place as the clause *lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu* (nothing stays far from Him) in the third verse.

## 34/22 — قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ زَعَمْتُم مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ لَا يَمْلِكُونَ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَلَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا لَهُمْ فِيهِمَا مِن شِرْكٍ وَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْهُم مِّن ظَهِيرٍ

*Quli'dʿu'lladhīna zaʿamtum min dūni'llāh, lā yamlikūna mithqāla dharratin fi's-samāwāti wa lā fi'l-arḍi wa mā lahum fīhimā min shirkin wa mā lahū minhum min ẓahīr*

"Say: '**Call upon those you have alleged besides God!** **They do not own so much as the weight of a speck in the heavens or in the earth; they have no share in the two; and He has no supporter among them.**'"

### زَعَمْتُم — a claim without support

**The root ز-ع-م was analysed in detail at `064-tegabun.md` 64/7 and at `062-cuma.md` 62/6** (a claim without proof, for which the claimant takes on no responsibility; and the *zaʿīm* branch — the guarantor — was treated at `068-kalem.md` 68/40). **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the place of the word in the sentence.** The verse does not name the beings called upon — **it names them by way of the addressee's *claim* about them:** *alladhīna **zaʿamtum***. **That is, what is mentioned is not the name of the object but the words the addressee says about that object.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is that the name is never given at all:** the dispute is conducted not over what the beings are but over **what the claim made about them rests on**. This agrees exactly with the *zaʿm* pattern treated at `062-cuma.md`.

### A closing in three stages

**The verse closes off in turn every possibility of a claim to partnership:**

| Stage | Expression | Possibility closed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Lā yamlikūna mithqāla dharra* | **Full ownership** — they do not own so much as a speck |
| 2 | *Wa mā lahum fīhimā min **shirk*** | **Partial share** — they hold no portion either |
| 3 | *Wa mā lahū minhum min **ẓahīr*** | **Assistance** — they are not even supporters |

**Three stages, three different kinds of relation: to own, to share, to help.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the sequence itself:** the verse begins with the strongest claim and descends to the weakest. **That is, every intermediate step at which an objection might be lodged is closed off by the text itself.** At `035-fatir.md` 35/13-14 the same three-stage closing structure was treated (they do not hear / if they heard they could not answer / in the end they will reject); **I rely on that treatment.**

**ظَهِير** — root **ظ-ه-ر**: **the back.** *Ẓahīr* — **one who backs you, the one you lean your back on.** **The picture in the word is bodily: help is described as support from behind.**

**And the same root occurred in the eighteenth verse: *quran ẓāhira* — towns in view.** **The same root in two different branches: to be visible, and to give backing.** I record this as an observation about the language.

### مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ — the second occurrence in the sūra

**The construction occurred in the third verse. Here it comes a second time, and this repetition is one of the most regular weaves in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | About whom | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3** | *Lā **yaʿzubu** ʿanhu mithqālu dharra* | **God** | Not a speck's weight stays far from His **knowledge** |
| **22** | *Lā **yamlikūna** mithqāla dharra* | **Those called upon** | They do not **own** so much as a speck's weight |

**The same measure, two different claims: one of knowledge, the other of ownership.** And both clauses are built with **a negation**: *lā yaʿzubu* / *lā yamlikūna*.

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the unit of measure the two clauses share:** the sūra weighs the two sides in the same balance. **On one side a knowledge from which not even a speck falls outside; on the other a powerlessness that does not own even a speck.** The balance is the same; the pans are opposite.

## 34/23 — وَلَا تَنفَعُ ٱلشَّفَٰعَةُ عِندَهُۥٓ إِلَّا لِمَنْ أَذِنَ لَهُۥ حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا فُزِّعَ عَن قُلُوبِهِمْ قَالُوا۟ مَاذَا قَالَ رَبُّكُمْ قَالُوا۟ ٱلْحَقَّ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْكَبِيرُ

*Wa lā tanfaʿu'sh-shafāʿatu ʿindahū illā li-man adhina lah, ḥattā idhā fuzziʿa ʿan qulūbihim qālū mādhā qāla rabbukum, qālu'l-ḥaqq, wa huwa'l-ʿaliyyu'l-kabīr*

"**With Him no intercession avails except from the one He has permitted.** At last, **when the terror is lifted from their hearts**, they say: '**What did your Lord say?**' '**The truth**', they say. **He is the high, the great.**"

### The note on intercession

**The question of intercession has been treated in two places in the series, and I rely on those treatments:**

- **`002-bakara.md` 2/255**: *man dha'lladhī yashfaʿu ʿindahū illā bi-idhnih* — **the door is not shut, it is tied to permission.** And the picture that emerges when it is read together with 2/47-48 was recorded there: **there is no intercession that can be counted on of itself, as a right.**
- **`039-zumer.md` 39/43-44**: *qul lillāhi'sh-shafāʿatu jamīʿā* — **intercession is not denied; its owner is named.**

**I do not repeat this. I record the difference that belongs here.**

| Place | Form of the sentence | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/255 | **A question** — *man dha'lladhī yashfaʿu* | It narrows the possibility |
| al-Zumar 39/44 | **A statement** — *lillāhi'sh-shafāʿatu jamīʿā* | It names the owner |
| **Sabaʾ 34/23** | **Negation plus exception** — *lā tanfaʿu … illā li-man adhina lah* | It ties **the benefit** to permission |

**What Sabaʾ adds lies in the verb, and I record this as my own reading; its support is the choice of word:** the verse does not say "there *is* no intercession", it says **"it does not avail"** (*lā **tanfaʿu***). **That is, the speaking of the word and the word's taking effect are separated.** The permission stands as a condition set not on the existence of the intercession but on **its outcome**.

### حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا فُزِّعَ عَن قُلُوبِهِمْ

**ف-ز-ع** — the sense of the root: **sudden fear, being seized with terror, starting in alarm.** *Fazaʿ* — a start of fright.

**And the pattern is striking: *fuzziʿa* — the passive of Form II.** **It is recorded that Form II here conveys *izāla* (removal):** in Arabic some verbs, when they enter Form II, **reverse** their sense — like *jallada* (he stripped off the hide), *marraḍa* (he took the illness away). **That is, *fuzziʿa ʿan qulūbihim* means not "terror was cast into their hearts" but "terror was removed from their hearts".**

**And the particle confirms this: *fuzziʿa ʿan qulūbihim* — "from their hearts". The particle *ʿan* conveys moving away**.

**There is a variant reading, and it must be recorded:**

| Reading | Form | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| *Fuzziʿa* (passive, Form II) | The widespread reading | "Terror **was removed** from their hearts" |
| *Fazzaʿa* (active, Form II) | A transmitted reading | "**He removed it**" — the agent is God |
| *Fuziʿa* (passive, Form I) | A transmitted reading | "They were struck with terror" |

**Different readings are transmitted; between the first two there is no difference in sense, the third changes the direction. I do not impose a preference.**

### مَاذَا قَالَ رَبُّكُمْ — question and answer

**The verse transmits a conversation, and who is speaking is not said.**

**It is widely transmitted that in the classical commentaries this scene takes place among the angels. Since the verse names no one, I too identify no party;** what is to be recorded is the structure of the scene.

| Step | Expression |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A waiting** — terror in the hearts |
| 2 | **The lifting of the terror** — *fuzziʿa ʿan qulūbihim* |
| 3 | **The question** — *mādhā qāla rabbukum* |
| 4 | **The answer** — *al-ḥaqq* |

**And that the answer is a single word is worth recording: *al-ḥaqq*.**

**I give this as my own reading; its support is the length of the answer:** the question asks for a content ("what did He say?"), the answer gives no content — **it gives a quality: "the truth".** That is, what is announced is not what was said but **what kind of thing it was.**

**And this ties back to the preceding clause:** after it has been said that intercession depends on permission, it is recorded that the word coming from the authority that permits is **truth**. **The sentence says, that is, that the permission is not arbitrary.**

**ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْكَبِير** — two names: **high** and **great.** *ʿAliyy* (ع-ل-و) conveys height, *kabīr* (ك-ب-ر) greatness. **And both are names of *scale*, not of *distance*:** what the scene describes is not a remoteness but a standing before greatness.

## 34/24 — قُلْ مَن يَرْزُقُكُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ قُلِ ٱللَّهُ وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى أَوْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Qul man yarzuqukum mina's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ, quli'llāh, wa innā aw iyyākum la-ʿalā hudan aw fī ḍalālin mubīn*

"Say: '**Who provides for you from the heavens and the earth?**' Say: '**God.** **And surely either we or you are upon guidance or in plain error.**'"

### The question asked, and who gives the answer

**There are two commands in the verse, and both are *qul*: "say".**

| Command | What is said |
|---|---|
| 1. *Qul* | **The question** — who provides for you? |
| 2. *Qul* | **The answer** — God |

**That is, the answer to the question is given by the one who asks it.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence does not wait for an answer from the other side. **And this shows that the answer was not in dispute** — the addressees already accepted God as creator; this was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/3 (*the dispute is not over existence but over intermediaries*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the same question was asked at `035-fatir.md` 35/3:** *hal min khāliqin ghayru'llāhi **yarzuqukum** mina's-samāʾi wa'l-arḍ.* **The two verses use the same verb with the same two domains.**

| Place | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/3 | *Hal min khāliqin ghayru'llāh **yarzuqukum***? | **Not given** — the question is left open |
| **Sabaʾ 34/24** | *Man **yarzuqukum***? | *Quli'llāh* — **it is given** |

**The difference is worth recording:** Fāṭir leaves the question to the addressee himself; Sabaʾ has the answer spoken too. **And in Sabaʾ the sentence that comes immediately after the answer sets out the real matter.**

### وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى أَوْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ

**This sentence is one of the most striking points of word order in the sūra and deserves detailed treatment.**

**What the sentence says is: "either we or you — either upon guidance or in plain error."**

**The expected sentence would have been: "we are upon guidance, you are in error."** It is the addressee's position that is in dispute, and it is plain from the whole text that the speaker is certain of his own position. **But the sentence is not built that way.**

**The wording does three things, and I record all three separately:**

**One — the subjects are not separated.** *Innā **aw** iyyākum* — "we **or** you". The two parties have been made the subject of a single sentence. **Which is which, the sentence does not say.**

**Two — the attributes are not distributed to the parties.** *La-ʿalā hudan **aw** fī ḍalālin mubīn* — "upon guidance **or** in plain error". **The two qualities stand in the open; which belongs to whom is not stated.**

**Three — even the particles differ.** A striking detail: *ʿalā* (upon) is used for guidance, *fī* (in) for error.

| State | Particle | Its picture |
|---|---|---|
| *Hudā* | *ʿAlā* — **upon** | To stand on the road, to be high up, to see |
| *Ḍalāl* | *Fī* — **in** | To be sunk into it, to be enclosed by it |

**This difference of particle is consistent in the Qurʾān, and the lexicographers record it.** **I give this as my own reading:** guidance is described like a ground — one stands upon it, one sees around. **Error is described like a volume — one goes into it, and nothing around is visible.**

### Reading this as a discipline of disputation

**Now I return to the sentence as a whole, and I record this reading as my own reading. I give its support plainly too:** no word in the sentence carries the name of a party; the particle *aw* (or) occurs twice, and both times it leaves the division open.

**What the sentence does is to set the addressee before a *possibility* rather than before a *verdict*.**

**And it is worth dwelling on why this is effective:**

| Wording | What the addressee's response becomes |
|---|---|
| "You are in error" | **Defence** — the position is protected, thinking closes |
| "One of the two of us is in error" | **Reasoning** — the position is suspended, thinking opens |

**The first sentence announces a verdict and drives the addressee to defend himself.** The second announces a possibility and **invites the addressee to investigate which possibility is the true one.**

**And note: the sentence does not build a *relativism* (both sides are right).** **The verse itself prevents this**, because the two states are not presented as **equal**: one is *hudā*, the other *ḍalālin **mubīn*** — plain error. **That one of the two is wrong stands inside the sentence. The only thing left unstated is which is which.**

**I record this separately, because the distinction matters:** the sentence does not doubt that there is a truth. **It only leaves the verdict to the addressee's own reasoning.**

**And this attitude continues in the sūra:** in the forty-sixth verse the command *thumma tatafakkarū* (then reflect) will come. **The two verses set up the same method: opening a space for thought instead of imposing a verdict.** I record this as a line that can be verified within the sūra.

**What this says to the present is concrete:** the difference between saying to the other party in a dispute "you are wrong" and saying "one of the two of us is wrong" is that the second sentence **puts the speaker at risk as well.** And that is very often what makes a claim worth taking seriously: the claimant's placing himself inside the dispute too.

## 34/25-26 — قُل لَّا تُسْـَٔلُونَ عَمَّآ أَجْرَمْنَا وَلَا نُسْـَٔلُ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ · قُلْ يَجْمَعُ بَيْنَنَا رَبُّنَا ثُمَّ يَفْتَحُ بَيْنَنَا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَهُوَ ٱلْفَتَّاحُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

*Qul lā tusʾalūna ʿammā ajramnā wa lā nusʾalu ʿammā taʿmalūn · Qul yajmaʿu baynanā rabbunā thumma yaftaḥu baynanā bi'l-ḥaqq, wa huwa'l-fattāḥu'l-ʿalīm*

"Say: '**You will not be questioned about the crime we have committed, nor shall we be questioned about what you do.**' Say: '**Our Lord will gather us together, then He will open judgement between us with the truth. He is the best of judges, the all-knowing.**'"

### The verbal balance of the two clauses

**The twenty-fifth verse continues the attitude of the twenty-fourth and is built with the same care:**

| Half | Verb | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Lā tusʾalūna ʿammā **ajramnā*** | **Us** — *ajramnā* (we committed a crime) |
| 2 | *Wa lā nusʾalu ʿammā **taʿmalūn*** | **You** — *taʿmalūn* (you do) |

**And here lies the most striking detail of the sentence:** the two halves are symmetrical, **but the words are not.**

**For his own side the heavy word is chosen: *ajramnā* — root ج-ر-م: to commit a crime.** **For the other side the light word is chosen: *taʿmalūn* — root ع-م-ل: to do, to work (neutral).**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the semantic weight of the two roots:** while saying that responsibility is separate, the sentence **assigns the heavy word to itself and the neutral word to the other side.** **That is, even while the separation is being drawn, the other side is not accused.**

**And this continues the preceding verse:** in the twenty-fourth verse the verdict was suspended; in the twenty-fifth the language of accusation is abandoned. **The two verses are two steps of the same discipline.**

**And as a principle what it says overlaps with the principle of burden treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/18** (*lā taziru wāziratun wizra ukhrā*) — there it was recorded that the principle is tested in its hardest form. **I rely on that treatment.** **The difference here is this:** in Fāṭir the principle was laid down as a ruling; **in Sabaʾ it is used as a sentence within a dispute.** The principle, that is, has been turned into a rule of conduct governing the relation with the other side.

### يَفْتَحُ بَيْنَنَا — "to open" in the sense of judging

**The root ف-ت-ح was analysed in detail at `048-fetih.md` on the occasion of that sūra's name** (to open what is closed; *fattāḥ*, *miftāḥ*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the root's sense of *giving judgement*, and it must be recorded.** In Arabic *fataḥa bayna'l-khaṣmayn* means "he gave judgement between two adversaries". **It is transmitted by the lexicographers that in the Yemeni dialect a judge is called *fātiḥ*.**

**And the relation of the senses is worth recording:** to give judgement is **to open a matter that was closed.** A dispute is a knot; judgement is the untying of the knot.

**I give this as an observation about the language, and the word order of the verse supports it:** *yajmaʿu baynanā* (He gathers us) → *yaftaḥu baynanā* (He opens between us). **First a bringing together, then a parting.** Both verbs come with *baynanā* (between us).

**ٱلْفَتَّاح** — an intensive form: **the one who opens much, who gives judgement.** In the Qurʾān this name occurs only here. **And *al-ʿalīm* has been set beside it:** the one who judges, **who knows**. **That is, the ground of the judgement is given in the same sentence.**

**At `039-zumer.md` 39/46 (*anta taḥkumu bayna ʿibādika fī mā kānū fīhi yakhtalifūn*) the same structure was treated:** the disagreement is named as a matter and its judgement is reckoned deferred. **I rely on that treatment.** **Sabaʾ has the same thing said from the mouth of one of the parties to the disagreement.**

## 34/27 — قُلْ أَرُونِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَلْحَقْتُم بِهِۦ شُرَكَآءَ كَلَّا بَلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ

*Qul arūniya'lladhīna alḥaqtum bihī shurakāʾ, kallā, bal huwa'llāhu'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm*

"Say: '**Show me those you have attached to Him as partners!**' **No! Rather He is God, the mighty, the wise.**"

### أَلْحَقْتُم — "you attached"

**ل-ح-ق** — the concrete sense of the root: **to catch up from behind, to join, to be appended.** *Laḥiqa* — he caught up, he came alongside. Form IV (*alḥaqa*): **to append, to attach, to add one thing on to another.**

**The Turkish words *ilhak* (annexation) and *lahika* (an appendix) are borrowed from this root.**

**The choice of the verb must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading; its support is the picture in the root:** the verse does not say "those you associated" (*ashraktum*) — it says **"those you attached", "those you tacked on"**. **That is, the partnership is named not as a relation that actually exists but as an *appending* done after the fact.**

**And the agent of the verb is the one addressed: *alḥaqtum* — you appended.** The act is recorded as the party's own doing.

**أَرُونِى** — "show me". **The demand is not for a proof but for a *pointing out*.** It belongs to the same family as the pattern *hātū burhānakum* (bring your proof) treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/111; **I rely on that treatment.** **The difference is this:** there what was asked for was **a demonstration**, here what is asked for is **something that can be pointed at**. The demand has been brought down to its most concrete form.

**كَلَّا** — in Arabic the particle that rejects a claim outright. **The word was treated at `070-mearic.md` 70/15 and 70/39** — there it was recorded as the cutting off of two expectations. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the close of the sentence must be recorded: *bal huwa'llāhu'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm*.** The demand goes unanswered — because there is nothing to point at — **and the empty place is filled with two names.**

## 34/28 — وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَٰكَ إِلَّا كَآفَّةً لِّلنَّاسِ بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*Wa mā arsalnāka illā kāffatan li'n-nāsi bashīran wa nadhīrā, wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn*

"**We have sent you only to all mankind, as a bearer of good news and a warner.** But most people do not know."

### كَآفَّة — the root and the pattern of the word

**The root ك-ف-ف and the word *kāffatan* were treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/208** (*udkhulū fi's-silmi kāffa*). What was recorded there holds here, and its summary is this: **the word is connected with *kaff* (the palm of the hand, gathering in and holding the edge of a thing) — "leaving no piece out at the edge".** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And in al-Baqara two readings were transmitted: *the whole of those entering* (let everyone enter) or *the whole of what is entered* (let it be entered wholly). Here too there is a similar disagreement about the inflection:**

| Reading | Whose *ḥāl* *kāffatan* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The *ḥāl* of ***nās*** | "We sent you to **all** people" |
| 2 | The *ḥāl* of the one addressed (the pronoun *ka*) | "We sent you as one who **encompasses, who gathers in**" |

**The first reading is more widespread. Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**There is a difficulty in the inflection of the word, and the lexicographers record it:** the word *kāffatan* comes **before** *li'n-nās*. Had it been an adjective of *nās*, in Arabic the adjective would be expected to follow the noun it qualifies. **For this reason some lexicographers have preferred the second reading. I transmit this as a dispute about inflection; I frame no ruling.**

**But the place both readings arrive at is the same, and I record this as my own reading:** whether it be "to all people" or "as one who encompasses", **the work of the sentence is to lift a limit.** *Wa mā arsalnāka **illā*** — the pattern of negation plus exception does not narrow the scope; **it removes the possibility of narrowing.**

### بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا — two attributes, two directions

| Attribute | Root | Its work |
|---|---|---|
| *Bashīr* | **ب-ش-ر** — skin, the outer surface of the face | **Good news** — the report that lights up a face |
| *Nadhīr* | **ن-ذ-ر** — to take something upon oneself, to give notice | **Warning** — the report given in advance |

**The relation of the root ب-ش-ر with the skin is recorded by the lexicographers:** *bashara* is the outer surface of the skin; **good news is named from this root because its effect is seen in the face.**

**And the order of the two attributes is worth recording: good news first, then warning.** This order is kept in the sūra: **in the fifteenth verse Sabaʾ was first given a favour, and only afterwards did the warning come to pass.**

**And the word *nadhīr* occurs four times in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **28** | *Bashīran wa **nadhīrā*** — an attribute of the Prophet |
| **34** | *Wa mā arsalnā fī qaryatin min **nadhīrin*** — to every town |
| **44** | *Wa mā arsalnā ilayhim qablaka min **nadhīr*** — none had come to them before |
| **46** | *In huwa illā **nadhīrun** lakum* — again an attribute of the Prophet |

**Four occurrences, in four separate places in the sūra.** And the series builds a frame: **scope (28) → history (34) → the situation of this community (44) → the attribute repeated (46).**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the sentence in the forty-fourth verse:** *wa mā arsalnā ilayhim qablaka min nadhīr* — **"We sent them no warner before you."** In the twenty-eighth verse the scope is declared universal; in the forty-fourth it is said that this community had not been warned before. **Read together, the two verses show why the attribute is given on the scale of "all mankind".**

**وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ** — and the verse closes with a lack of knowledge. **The same sentence will be repeated word for word in the thirty-sixth verse.** **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I shall tabulate it at the end.**

## 34/29-30 — وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ · قُل لَّكُم مِّيعَادُ يَوْمٍ لَّا تَسْتَـْٔخِرُونَ عَنْهُ سَاعَةً وَلَا تَسْتَقْدِمُونَ

*Wa yaqūlūna matā hādha'l-waʿdu in kuntum ṣādiqīn · Qul lakum mīʿādu yawmin lā tastaʾkhirūna ʿanhu sāʿatan wa lā tastaqdimūn*

"They say: '**When is this promise, if you are telling the truth?**' Say: '**You have an appointed day; you can neither put it back by an hour nor bring it forward.**'"

### The form of the question

**The question looks like a request for information, but it comes with a condition: *in kuntum ṣādiqīn* — "if you are telling the truth".**

**This condition changes the character of the question, and I record it as an observation:** the sentence asks "when?" without waiting for an answer; **it asks on the assumption that no answer can be given.** The question, that is, is framed not to learn a date but **to show that no date can be given.**

**And note: the question is put in the plural — *in kuntum* (you), *ṣādiqīn* (plural).** Not to the Prophet alone, but to those who believe along with him.

### What the answer gives

**And the answer does not give the date — but it does not brush the question aside either.** What it gives is **a certainty**.

| Element of the answer | Expression | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Lakum mīʿādu yawmin* | **There is one** — an appointed day |
| 2 | *Lā **tastaʾkhirūna** ʿanhu sāʿah* | **It cannot be delayed** |
| 3 | *Wa lā **tastaqdimūn*** | **It cannot be brought forward** |

**Both verbs are of Form X (*istifʿāl*), and this form conveys *seeking*.** **That is, the sentence does not say "you will not fall behind it / you will not get ahead of it", but exactly: "you cannot *ask for* or *manage* to fall behind or get ahead".**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the choice of form sets the impossibility down not as an incapacity but as **a request that goes unanswered**.

**مِيعَاد** — root **و-ع-د**: to promise. *Mīʿād* — on the *mifʿāl* pattern: **the time or place appointed.** **The word is from the same root as "promise" (*waʿd*) but points to the time.**

**And the word order of the sentence is worth recording:** the question said *hādha'l-waʿd* (this promise); the answer says *mīʿādu yawmin* (the appointed time of a day). **That is, the question was asked with the word "promise", and the answer given with the derivative of the same root that states a time.**

**سَاعَة** — "an hour". **And the word's first occurrence in the sūra must be recorded: in the third verse it was *as-sāʿa* (the Hour).** Here the same word comes in the sense of **a small span of time**.

| Verse | *Sāʿa* | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *Lā taʾtīna's-**sāʿah*** | **The Hour** — the great event |
| **30** | *Lā tastaʾkhirūna ʿanhu **sāʿah*** | **An hour** — the smallest delay |

**The same word on two scales.** **I record this as my own reading, and its support is that the two occurrences turn on the same subject:** the *sāʿa* whose coming was denied in the third verse is announced in the thirtieth as something that will not shift by so much as a *sāʿa*. **The word has turned from the subject of the objection into the measure of the answer.**

**The block closes here.** And its closing clause is an announcement of a time — this lies on the same line as the deferral treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/45 (*yuʾakhkhiruhum ilā ajalin musammā*); **I rely on that treatment.**

## 34/31 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَن نُّؤْمِنَ بِهَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ وَلَا بِٱلَّذِى بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ وَلَوْ تَرَىٰٓ إِذِ ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ مَوْقُوفُونَ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ يَرْجِعُ بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍ ٱلْقَوْلَ يَقُولُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتُضْعِفُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ لَوْلَآ أَنتُمْ لَكُنَّا مُؤْمِنِينَ

*Wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū lan nuʾmina bi-hādha'l-qurʾāni wa lā bi'lladhī bayna yadayh, wa law tarā idhi'ẓ-ẓālimūna mawqūfūna ʿinda rabbihim yarjiʿu baʿḍuhum ilā baʿḍini'l-qawl, yaqūlu'lladhīna'stuḍʿifū li'lladhīna'stakbarū law lā antum la-kunnā muʾminīn*

"Those who disbelieved said: '**We will not believe in this Qurʾān, nor in what came before it.**' **If you could but see the wrongdoers halted before their Lord, sending speech back and forth between them!** **Those who were made weak will say to those who were arrogant: 'But for you, we would surely have been believers.'**"

### The scope of the objection

**The denial is directed at two things at once: *bi-hādha'l-qurʾān* and *bi'lladhī bayna yadayh*.**

**At `035-fatir.md` 35/31 the expression *muṣaddiqan limā bayna yadayh* (confirming what is before it) was treated**, and there `046-ahkaf.md` 46/12 and 46/30 were relied on: **the attachment to a chain.** I rely on that treatment.

**The difference that belongs here must be recorded:** in Fāṭir the expression was in a context of **acceptance** (the Book confirms what came before it); **here the same expression comes in a context of *rejection*.** That is, those who reject build their rejection too along the same chain: both at once.

**I record this as an observation:** the objection is not presented as an objection raised against a new text. **What is rejected is a whole chain.**

**لَن** — the particle that negates the future outright: "we will never believe." **The same particle was treated at `064-tegabun.md` 64/7** (there *an lan yubʿathū*). **In both verses *lan* carries a claim of certainty issuing from the other side's own mouth.**

### The opening of the scene

**And in mid-sentence the direction changes: *wa law tarā* — "if you could but see!"**

**لَوْ** here is the use that conveys **a wish or astonishment**, and its apodosis is not stated. In Arabic this structure (*law* plus the suppression of the apodosis) conveys **indescribability**: "if you saw — [what you would see cannot be told]."

**مَوْقُوفُون** — root **و-ق-ف**: **to stand, to be halted.** The passive participle: **halted.** **The word carries the sense that they are not waiting of their own will.**

**يَرْجِعُ بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍ ٱلْقَوْلَ** — "they send speech back and forth to one another."

**The construction is striking: *yarjiʿu … al-qawl* — to *send back* speech.** Not conversation, that is, but **mutual return.** The word goes from one side to the other and back.

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the verb is not *takallum* (to speak) or *taḥāwur* (to converse) — it is *irjāʿ* (to send back). **The scene is drawn not as a conversation but as a ball being sent to and fro.**

### ٱسْتُضْعِفُوا۟ / ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ — the names of the two sides

| Group | Word | Form | Voice | Sense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ustuḍʿifū* | **Form X** | **Passive** | **Those who were made weak** |
| 2 | *Istakbarū* | **Form X** | **Active** | **Those who made themselves great** |

**Both words are of the same form, but their voices differ — and this difference must be recorded.**

**I give this as my own reading; its support is the voice of the two verbs:** weakness is named as something that **came upon them** (passive: they were made weak), while arrogance is named as **their own doing** (active: they made themselves great). **That is, the text sets a distinction already at the stage of naming.**

**But note: the verse had opened by naming both groups together as *aẓ-ẓālimūn* (the wrongdoers).** **The difference of voice, that is, grants no exemption; it only records how the positions came about.**

**لَوْلَآ أَنتُمْ لَكُنَّا مُؤْمِنِينَ** — "but for you, we would have been believers."

**The sentence is on the pattern *law lā* (*imtināʿ*, prevention): one thing's failing to come about because of the existence of another.** **That is, the fault is tied not to their own choice but to the existence of the other party.**

**At `039-zumer.md` 39/57 the same line was treated:** *law anna'llāha hadānī la-kuntu mina'l-muttaqīn* — there responsibility was passed on to **God**, here to **a human being.** **The two excuses have the same structure: an unfulfilled condition and a responsibility handed over.**

| Place | Where the excuse is addressed |
|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/57 | *Law anna'llāha hadānī* — **God** |
| **Sabaʾ 34/31** | *Law lā antum* — **those who were arrogant** |

**This is an overlap between sūras that can be verified.**

## 34/32-33 — قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ٱسْتُضْعِفُوٓا۟ أَنَحْنُ صَدَدْنَٰكُمْ عَنِ ٱلْهُدَىٰ بَعْدَ إِذْ جَآءَكُم بَلْ كُنتُم مُّجْرِمِينَ · وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتُضْعِفُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ بَلْ مَكْرُ ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ إِذْ تَأْمُرُونَنَآ أَن نَّكْفُرَ بِٱللَّهِ وَنَجْعَلَ لَهُۥٓ أَندَادًا وَأَسَرُّوا۟ ٱلنَّدَامَةَ لَمَّا رَأَوُا۟ ٱلْعَذَابَ وَجَعَلْنَا ٱلْأَغْلَٰلَ فِىٓ أَعْنَاقِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ هَلْ يُجْزَوْنَ إِلَّا مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ

*Qāla'lladhīna'stakbarū li'lladhīna'stuḍʿifū a-naḥnu ṣadadnākum ʿani'l-hudā baʿda idh jāʾakum bal kuntum mujrimīn · Wa qāla'lladhīna'stuḍʿifū li'lladhīna'stakbarū bal makru'l-layli wa'n-nahāri idh taʾmurūnanā an nakfura bi'llāhi wa najʿala lahū andādā, wa asarru'n-nadāmata lammā raʾawu'l-ʿadhāb, wa jaʿalna'l-aghlāla fī aʿnāqi'lladhīna kafarū, hal yujzawna illā mā kānū yaʿmalūn*

"Those who were arrogant will say to those who were made weak: '**Was it we who turned you from the guidance after it came to you? No, you were guilty yourselves.**' And those who were made weak will say: '**No! It was the scheming of night and day, when you kept ordering us to disbelieve in God and to set up rivals to Him.**' **When they see the punishment they will keep their regret inside.** We will put iron collars on the necks of those who disbelieved. **Are they recompensed with anything but what they used to do?**"

### The three steps of the dispute

| Step | Who | What they say |
|---|---|---|
| **31** | The weak → the arrogant | *Law lā antum la-kunnā muʾminīn* — **but for you** |
| **32** | The arrogant → the weak | *A-naḥnu ṣadadnākum* — **was it we who turned you?** |
| **33** | The weak → the arrogant | *Bal makru'l-layli wa'n-nahār* — **scheming night and day** |

**And two *bal*s (no!) come one after the other: in 32 from the arrogant, in 33 from the weak.** **That is, each side rejects the other's sentence.**

### A comparison of the scenes of dispute in the Fire

**Two counterparts of this scene were treated in detail in the series, and the comparison must be set out in a table:**

- **`040-gafir.md` 40/47-50** — *yataḥājjūna fi'n-nār*: the three-stage chain (the weak → the arrogant → the keepers) was tabulated there.
- **`037-saffat.md` 37/27-33** — *yatasāʾalūn*: the reckoning between the two parties, and the comparison with Ghāfir was made there.

**I rely on those treatments; I do not repeat those tables. Here I set down what is different in Sabaʾ:**

| | **Ghāfir 40/47-50** | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/27-33** | **Sabaʾ 34/31-33** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verb | *Yataḥājjūn* (ح-ج-ج) — **to bring proofs** | *Yatasāʾalūn* (س-أ-ل) — **to question one another** | *Yarjiʿu … al-qawl* (ر-ج-ع) — **to send speech back** |
| What the weak say | **A request** — carry part of the load | **An accusation** — you used to come at us from the right | **An accusation** — but for you |
| The answer of the arrogant | *Innā kullun fīhā* — a statement of the situation | *Bal lam takūnū muʾminīn* — **rejection** | *Bal kuntum mujrimīn* — **rejection** |
| A third party | **Present** — the keepers | **None** | **None** |
| Outcome | Unresolved | Shared punishment: *fa-innahum yawmaʾidhin fi'l-ʿadhābi mushtarikūn* | **Regret kept hidden** |

**The clearest difference is in the last row, and it is peculiar to Sabaʾ:** in Ghāfir the dispute goes on with an appeal, in al-Ṣāffāt with a reckoning. **In Sabaʾ the dispute ends in a *silence*:** *wa asarru'n-nadāmata lammā raʾawu'l-ʿadhāb.*

**And the choice of verb must be recorded. I give this as my own reading; its support is the verse's own word:** *asarrū* — **they kept it within, they hid it.** That is, the regret **is felt but not spoken.** The scene opens with mutual accusation and closes in silence.

**أ-س-ر** — the sense of the root: **to hide, to keep inside.** *Sirr* — what is hidden. **And the lexicographers also transmit that the word is used in the opposite sense (*to make known*);** some exegetes have preferred this second sense here.

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **They hid it** — they kept their regret inside |
| 2 | **They made it plain** — the root used in its contrary sense |

**The first reading is more widespread. Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

### بَلْ مَكْرُ ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ

**The construction is striking in Arabic: "the scheming of the night and of the day".**

**On the face of it the construction ascribes the scheming to the night and to the day. The lexicographers explain it thus: the construction states *time* — "the scheming [you carried on] night and day".** That is, night and day are the name not of the agent but **of the duration of the act.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the continuation of the sentence:** *idh taʾmurūnanā* — "when you kept ordering us". **The agent is stated plainly immediately afterwards.** So the schemer is not the night but the other party; night and day announce **the unceasingness** of the work.

**And the picture in the construction is worth recording:** the persuading is named not as a single event but as **a process that fills all the hours.** The other party speaks not of a conversation but of a siege.

**The root م-ك-ر was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/43 (*wa lā yaḥīqu'l-makru's-sayyiʾu illā bi-ahlih*) and at `040-gafir.md` 40/45.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**أَندَاد** — the plural of *nidd*; root **ن-د-د**: **equal, match, like.** The lexicographers record that the word conveys in particular **an opposing or rival equality**: not merely a like, but at the same time one who sets himself against.

### وَجَعَلْنَا ٱلْأَغْلَٰلَ فِىٓ أَعْنَاقِ

**غ-ل-ل** — root: **an iron ring put on the neck or on the captured hand; and to be thrust in between things.** **The root was treated at `036-yasin.md` 36/8 (*innā jaʿalnā fī aʿnāqihim aghlālan*) and at `069-hakka.md` 69/30 and in `076-insan.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the place of the clause in the scene, and I give this as an observation about the word order:** the verse says that collars are put on the necks **after** it has described the mutual accusation and the hidden regret. **The sequence, that is, is: speech → silence → binding.** After the dispute ends, movement ends too.

**هَلْ يُجْزَوْنَ إِلَّا مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ** — "are they recompensed with anything but what they used to do?"

**The sentence is identical in pattern to the one in the seventeenth verse:** *wa hal nujāzī illa'l-kafūr.* **Both sentences are on the pattern *hal … illā* (a question conveying negation, plus exception).**

| Verse | Sentence | What it limits |
|---|---|---|
| **17** | *Wa hal nujāzī **illa'l-kafūr*** | **Who** is recompensed |
| **33** | *Hal yujzawna **illā mā kānū yaʿmalūn*** | **With what** they are recompensed |

**The same pattern, two different limits: the person and the return.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

## 34/34-35 — وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَا فِى قَرْيَةٍ مِّن نَّذِيرٍ إِلَّا قَالَ مُتْرَفُوهَآ إِنَّا بِمَآ أُرْسِلْتُم بِهِۦ كَٰفِرُونَ · وَقَالُوا۟ نَحْنُ أَكْثَرُ أَمْوَٰلًا وَأَوْلَٰدًا وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمُعَذَّبِينَ

*Wa mā arsalnā fī qaryatin min nadhīrin illā qāla mutrafūhā innā bimā ursiltum bihī kāfirūn · Wa qālū naḥnu aktharu amwālan wa awlādan wa mā naḥnu bi-muʿadhdhabīn*

"**To whatever town We sent a warner, those of it who lived in ease said: 'We disbelieve in what you have been sent with.'** **And they said: 'We have more wealth and more children; we are not going to be punished.'**"

### مُتْرَفُوهَا — who is speaking

**The word *mutraf* and the root ت-ر-ف were analysed in detail at `056-vakia.md` 56/45.** The summary of what was recorded there is this: **the root conveys an abundance of favour and the softening and slackening that abundance brings; *mutraf* is the passive participle of Form IV — not "one who runs riot" but "one who has been indulged".** And it was also recorded there: **what is charged is not wealth but the state wealth brings a person to.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The same word in the same pattern was treated at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/23 too** (*illā qāla mutrafūhā innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā umma*), and there it was analysed how the pattern of the sentence (negation plus exception) points to **a saying repeated throughout history**. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the two verses must be set side by side, because the pattern is the same and the saying is different:**

| | **al-Zukhruf 43/23** | **Sabaʾ 34/34** |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | *Mā arsalnā … **illā** qāla **mutrafūhā*** | *Mā arsalnā … **illā** qāla **mutrafūhā*** |
| The saying | *Innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā umma* — **the forefathers** | *Innā bimā ursiltum bihī kāfirūn* — **outright rejection** |
| Ground | **Tradition** | **No ground given** |

**The difference is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** in al-Zukhruf the *mutraf*s state a ground; **in Sabaʾ there is no ground — only rejection.** And the ground comes in the next verse: an abundance of wealth and children. **That is, Sabaʾ divides the rejection and its ground between two verses.**

**And at `056-vakia.md` this note was set down:** almost every time the word *mutraf* occurs in the Qurʾān it is in a context of destruction or of opposition — and **this very verse (Sabaʾ 34/34) was already cited there as an example.** **The circle closes here.**

### نَحْنُ أَكْثَرُ أَمْوَٰلًا وَأَوْلَٰدًا

**The logic of the sentence must be recorded, because it is plainly an inference:**

| Premise | *Naḥnu aktharu amwālan wa awlādā* — **we have more wealth and more children** |
|---|---|
| **Conclusion** | *Wa mā naḥnu bi-muʿadhdhabīn* — **we will not be punished** |

**The argument, that is, runs: abundance is proof of favour.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence does not build denial as a metaphysical claim. **What it builds is an *inference from observation*** — and the observation is correct (they really do have more), it is the inference that is wrong.

**And this is the very point the sūra will answer. The answer will come in three steps:**

| Verse | Answer |
|---|---|
| **36** | **The distribution is not a criterion** — the one who spreads provision out and the one who straitens it are the same |
| **37** | **Wealth and children do not bring near** — the criterion is something else |
| **39** | **What is spent comes back** — the work of wealth is not to accumulate |

**That is, the objection is framed in the thirty-fifth verse, and the answer given in three stages between thirty-six and thirty-nine.** This is a structure that can be verified within the sūra.

**أَمْوَٰلًا وَأَوْلَٰدًا** — and this pair occurs twice in the sūra: here (35) and in the thirty-seventh verse. **In the second place it will be flatly rejected.** I take it up below.

## 34/36 — قُلْ إِنَّ رَبِّى يَبْسُطُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ وَيَقْدِرُ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*Qul inna rabbī yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu wa yaqdir, wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn*

"Say: '**My Lord spreads out provision for whom He wills, and straitens it too.** But most people do not know.'"

### The first answer given to the objection

**The sentence is word for word the same as `042-sura.md` 42/12:** *yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu wa yaqdir.* **There the roots ب-س-ط and ق-د-ر were analysed in detail** (*basṭ* — to spread out, to lay open; *qadr* — to measure, to straiten) **and the distinction between 42/12 and 42/27 was tabulated: the one said that the distribution is in a single hand, the other that the total is measured.** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

**What belongs here is *where* the sentence is said. And that is decisive:**

| Place | What precedes the sentence | Its function |
|---|---|---|
| al-Shūrā 42/12 | *Lahū maqālīdu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* — **the keys of dominion** | **A statement** — who owns the distribution |
| **Sabaʾ 34/36** | *Naḥnu aktharu amwālan wa awlādā* — **the claim from abundance** | **An answer** — the distribution is not a proof |

**The same sentence at two different tasks.** In al-Shūrā a declaration, in Sabaʾ a refutation.

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the context of the two verses:** what the sentence does in Sabaʾ is not to deny the abundance — **it is to say where the abundance comes from and so to strip it of its standing as a criterion.** If both the spreading out and the straitening are the work of the same hand, the amount in one's hand cannot measure the favour of that hand.

**وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ** — and this clause is **word for word identical** with the last clause of the twenty-eighth verse.

| Verse | What is not known |
|---|---|
| **28** | **The scope of the mission** — that it is to all mankind |
| **36** | **The measure of provision** — that the distribution is not a proof |

**The same clause, two different ignorances.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

## 34/37 — وَمَآ أَمْوَٰلُكُمْ وَلَآ أَوْلَٰدُكُم بِٱلَّتِى تُقَرِّبُكُمْ عِندَنَا زُلْفَىٰٓ إِلَّا مَنْ ءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًا فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمْ جَزَآءُ ٱلضِّعْفِ بِمَا عَمِلُوا۟ وَهُمْ فِى ٱلْغُرُفَٰتِ ءَامِنُونَ

*Wa mā amwālukum wa lā awlādukum bi'llatī tuqarribukum ʿindanā zulfā, illā man āmana wa ʿamila ṣāliḥan fa-ulāʾika lahum jazāʾu'ḍ-ḍiʿfi bimā ʿamilū wa hum fi'l-ghurufāti āminūn*

"**It is not your wealth nor your children that will bring you near to Us.** **Only the one who believes and does righteous deeds:** for such there is **a recompense many times over** for what they did, and they are **secure in the high chambers.**"

### The sentence of judgement in the sūra

**This verse is where the sūra arrives. Everything told up to this point is tied here to a ruling.**

**And the scope of the ruling takes in both peoples of the sūra:**

| People | What they had | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **The house of David and Solomon** | Iron, copper, wind, buildings | **They were commanded to give thanks** (13) |
| **Sabaʾ** | Two gardens, safe roads | **They turned away** (16) |
| **The *mutraf*s** | *Amwāl* and *awlād* (35) | **They took this for a proof** |

**And the thirty-seventh verse gives the measure of all three in a single sentence: what is in your hand does not bring you near.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the structure of the sūra:** the sūra had told of two peoples one after the other, and the same thing had been given to both — **abundance.** The difference lay not in what was given but in the stance taken towards it. **The thirty-seventh verse names that difference.**

### The word order of the sentence

**The sentence negates three times:** *wa **mā** amwālukum **wa lā** awlādukum **bi'llatī** tuqarribukum.*

**And the word *bi'llatī* must be recorded: a singular feminine relative pronoun.** Although two things are named (wealth and children), a singular relative has been used. **The lexicographers explain this by taking the two as *a single thing* — that is, "what is owned".** Another explanation is that the relative refers back to the plural *al-amwāl* (a broken plural, which is treated as feminine). **Both explanations are transmitted; since they do not change the sense, I do not impose a preference.**

### زُلْفَىٰ — root: ز-ل-ف

**The root was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/3** (*mā naʿbuduhum illā li-yuqarribūnā ila'llāhi **zulfā***), where `050-kaf.md` 50/31 and `056-vakia.md` were relied on: **to draw near, nearness.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the two verses must be compared, because the word is the same and the sentence is its opposite:**

| | **al-Zumar 39/3** | **Sabaʾ 34/37** |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence | *Mā naʿbuduhum illā li-**yuqarribūnā** ila'llāhi **zulfā*** | *Mā amwālukum … bi'llatī **tuqarribukum** ʿindanā **zulfā*** |
| Who says it | **Those who associate partners** — a claim | **The text itself** — a ruling |
| What is taken to bring near | **Intermediary beings** | **Wealth and children** |
| Polarity of the sentence | **Affirmative** — they bring near | **Negative** — it does not bring near |

**In both verses the same two words stand together: the verb ق-ر-ب and *zulfā*.** And the sentence framed in al-Zumar as **a claim** comes in Sabaʾ in the form of a sentence that is **rejected**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the verbal overlap of the two verses:** it was recorded at `039-zumer.md` that the dispute is **not over existence but over intermediaries.** **Sabaʾ carries the same dispute one step further: the intermediary is not only idols — wealth and children can be used as an intermediary too.** To say "I have this, therefore my standing is good" is another form of leaning on an intermediary.

### إِلَّا مَنْ ءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًا — the exception

**The lexicographers dwell on what the exception is made from, and this is a dispute about inflection:**

| View | What the exception is from | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Disconnected** (*munqaṭiʿ*) | "But the one who believes and does righteous deeds is another matter" — a fresh ruling |
| 2 | From an elided element | "What will bring you near is only faith and deeds" |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **And both arrive at the same result: the criterion is not wealth but faith and deeds.**

**And what is striking is this, and I give it as an observation:** the verse does not **forbid** wealth and children, nor count them evil. **The only thing it says is that they do not *bring near*.** The sentence, that is, is not a counsel of asceticism but **a correction of the criterion**.

**جَزَآءُ ٱلضِّعْفِ** — *ḍiʿf*: root **ض-ع-ف**, **a fold, a multiple.** The construction means "a recompense many times over". **And the root of the word is the same as that of *ustuḍʿifū* (those made weak) in the thirty-first verse.**

| Verse | Root ض-ع-ف | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **31, 32, 33** | *Ustuḍʿifū* | **Those made weak** |
| **37** | *Jazāʾu'ḍ-**ḍiʿf*** | **A recompense many times over** |

**The two ends of one root: weakness and multiplication.** **The lexicographers record this relation:** *ḍiʿf* is a thing's folding over on itself; what folds becomes double. And *ḍaʿf* (weakness) is strength's failure to fold over on itself. **I give this as an observation about the language; I frame no ruling about the meaning.**

**ٱلْغُرُفَٰت** — the plural of *ghurfa*; root **غ-ر-ف**: **to scoop up in the hand, to take from above; and a room set high.** The word is used for **the upper-storey room** of a house.

**And the last word must be recorded: *āminūn* — in safety.** **The same word was used in the eighteenth verse for the roads of Sabaʾ:** *sīrū fīhā layāliya wa ayyāman **āminīn***.

| Verse | Where the *amn* is |
|---|---|
| **18** | **On the roads** — in this world, and it was lost |
| **37** | **In the high chambers** — as a recompense |

**The same root at the two ends of the sūra: one a safety that slipped away, the other a safety that is given.** This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading.

## 34/38-39 — وَٱلَّذِينَ يَسْعَوْنَ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِنَا مُعَٰجِزِينَ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ مُحْضَرُونَ · قُلْ إِنَّ رَبِّى يَبْسُطُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ مِنْ عِبَادِهِۦ وَيَقْدِرُ لَهُۥ وَمَآ أَنفَقْتُم مِّن شَىْءٍ فَهُوَ يُخْلِفُهُۥ وَهُوَ خَيْرُ ٱلرَّٰزِقِينَ

*Wa'lladhīna yasʿawna fī āyātinā muʿājizīna ulāʾika fi'l-ʿadhābi muḥḍarūn · Qul inna rabbī yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu min ʿibādihī wa yaqdiru lah, wa mā anfaqtum min shayʾin fa-huwa yukhlifuh, wa huwa khayru'r-rāziqīn*

"**Those who run about to make Our signs of no effect will be brought into the punishment.** Say: '**My Lord spreads out provision for whom He wills of His servants, and straitens it for him too.** **Whatever you spend, He replaces it.** He is the best of providers.'"

### The repetition of the fifth verse

**The thirty-eighth verse is almost a word-for-word repetition of the fifth. I tabulated it above in the treatment of 34/4-5; here I set their consequences side by side:**

| Verse | Verb | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | *Saʿaw* — **perfect** | *ʿAdhābun min rijzin alīm* — **the kind of the punishment** |
| **38** | *Yasʿawna* — **imperfect** | *Fi'l-ʿadhābi **muḥḍarūn*** — **being brought into the punishment** |

**The difference is in the second column:** the fifth verse states the **quality** of the punishment, the thirty-eighth states **being brought** into it.

**مُحْضَرُون** — root **ح-ض-ر**: **to be present, to be at hand.** The passive participle of Form IV: **those who are brought, those who are made to attend.** **The word conveys not walking there on one's own feet but being brought.**

**And this belongs to the same family as *mawqūfūn* (those halted) in the thirty-first verse: both words are passive.** **Throughout that scene the sūra places people by the act of another.** I record this as an observation about the word order.

### The two words added to the thirty-sixth verse

**The thirty-ninth verse is a repetition of the thirty-sixth — but two additions have been made:**

| | **36** | **39** |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence | *Yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu wa yaqdir* | *Yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu **min ʿibādih** wa yaqdiru **lah*** |
| Addition 1 | — | ***Min ʿibādihī*** — "of His servants" |
| Addition 2 | — | ***Lahū*** — "for him" |
| What follows | *Wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn* | *Wa mā anfaqtum min shayʾin fa-huwa yukhlifuh* |

**Both additions narrow, and I record this as my own reading; its support is the function of the two words:** *min ʿibādih* names the recipient of the provision as a **servant**; *lahū* states that the straitening too is **for the same person**. **That is, the thirty-ninth verse sets the spreading out and the straitening not as two different groups but as two possibilities belonging to one and the same person.**

**And what follows is wholly different:** the thirty-sixth verse ended with a note of ignorance; the thirty-ninth passes to a command about an **act**.

### وَمَآ أَنفَقْتُم مِّن شَىْءٍ فَهُوَ يُخْلِفُهُ

**"Whatever you spend, He replaces it."**

**خ-ل-ف** — the sense of the root: **to come after, to take the place of.** **The root was treated at `048-fetih.md` and at `035-fatir.md` 35/39 (*khalāʾif fi'l-arḍ*).** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The verb here is of Form IV: *akhlafa* — "to put another in its place, to give what comes after it".**

**And the choice of verb must be recorded. I give this as my own reading; its support is the sense of the root:** the verse does not say "He gives its recompense", it says **"He gives what comes in its place"**. **That is, another thing comes *after* what is spent.** The verb describes not a reward but **a replacing.**

**And the place of this clause in the sūra is decisive:**

| Verse | What is said about wealth |
|---|---|
| **35** | *Naḥnu aktharu **amwālan*** — **abundance is a proof** (a claim) |
| **36** | The distribution is **in His hand** |
| **37** | Wealth **does not bring near** |
| **39** | **What is spent** is replaced |

**A chain is built in four steps, and I record this as my own reading; its support is the order of the verses:** the objection was built on the **amount** of wealth. **The answer turns the measure from amount to *flow*.** The sūra does not reject wealth; it changes where wealth stands: **not a heap held in the hand, but something that passes through the hand.**

**And at `057-hadid.md` the trusteeship of wealth (*mustakhlafīna fīh* — 57/7) was treated in detail; I rely on that treatment.** **It is striking that the word in al-Ḥadīd is from the same root: خ-ل-ف.** **That is, the two sūras describe the changing of hands of wealth with the same root.**

| Place | Word | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| al-Ḥadīd 57/7 | *Musta**khlaf**īna fīh* | Wealth **has been made over to you** |
| **Sabaʾ 34/39** | *Fa-huwa yu**khlif**uh* | What is spent **is replaced** |

**The same root in two directions: one says how wealth comes, the other what happens after it goes.** This is an overlap between sūras that can be verified.

**خَيْرُ ٱلرَّٰزِقِين** — and the verse ends with a construction in which providers are named in the plural. **The construction does not deny that others give provision — it states who comes first in the order.** This belongs to the same family as the pattern treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/44 (*lillāhi'sh-shafāʿatu jamīʿā* — not a denial but a naming of ownership).

## 34/40-42 — وَيَوْمَ يَحْشُرُهُمْ جَمِيعًا ثُمَّ يَقُولُ لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ أَهَٰٓؤُلَآءِ إِيَّاكُمْ كَانُوا۟ يَعْبُدُونَ · قَالُوا۟ سُبْحَٰنَكَ أَنتَ وَلِيُّنَا مِن دُونِهِم بَلْ كَانُوا۟ يَعْبُدُونَ ٱلْجِنَّ أَكْثَرُهُم بِهِم مُّؤْمِنُونَ · فَٱلْيَوْمَ لَا يَمْلِكُ بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ نَّفْعًا وَلَا ضَرًّا وَنَقُولُ لِلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ ذُوقُوا۟ عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ ٱلَّتِى كُنتُم بِهَا تُكَذِّبُونَ

*Wa yawma yaḥshuruhum jamīʿan thumma yaqūlu li'l-malāʾikati a-hāʾulāʾi iyyākum kānū yaʿbudūn · Qālū subḥānaka anta waliyyunā min dūnihim, bal kānū yaʿbudūna'l-jinna aktharuhum bihim muʾminūn · Fa'l-yawma lā yamliku baʿḍukum li-baʿḍin nafʿan wa lā ḍarrā, wa naqūlu li'lladhīna ẓalamū dhūqū ʿadhāba'n-nāri'llatī kuntum bihā tukadhdhibūn*

"**On the day He gathers them all together, then says to the angels: 'Was it you these were serving?'** They will say: '**Glory be to You! Our protector is You, not they.** No, they were serving **the jinn**; most of them had faith in them.' **Today you can do one another neither good nor harm.** And We shall say to those who wronged: '**Taste the punishment of the Fire you used to deny.**'"

### To whom the question is put

**The question is not put to those who were served — it is put to *the angels*.** And this ties back to the twenty-second verse of the sūra: there it was said *alladhīna zaʿamtum* (those you have alleged), and their identity was left open. **Here an identity is named.**

**The word order of the question must be recorded: *a-hāʾulāʾi iyyākum kānū yaʿbudūn*.**

***Iyyākum* is the object, and it has been placed before the verb.** In Arabic this fronting conveys **restriction** (*ḥaṣr*): **"was it you who were served?"** The stress falls not on the verb but on the object.

**I give this as an observation about the word order:** the question is not "were these people serving?" — the serving had already taken place. **What is asked is who was served.**

### The three parts of the answer

| Part | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Subḥānaka* | **Declaring Him free of all imperfection** — before answering the question directly |
| 2 | *Anta waliyyunā min dūnihim* | **A statement of position** — "our protector is You" |
| 3 | *Bal kānū yaʿbudūna'l-jinn* | **A correction** — the real attachment was to another |

**The second part is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading; its support is the phrase *min dūnihim*:** the angels do not say "we did not want to be served". **What they say is their own position: "our *walī* is You, not they."** That is, the answer is given not by denying the charge but **by declaring where their own allegiance lies.**

**At `035-fatir.md` 35/14 the same line was treated:** *wa yawma'l-qiyāmati **yakfurūna bi-shirkikum*** — on the day of resurrection they will disown your associating. **And at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/5-6 the note was set down that the relation was never established at all.** I rely on those treatments.

**بَلْ كَانُوا۟ يَعْبُدُونَ ٱلْجِنَّ** — and the correction comes: the one really followed was another.

**The limit set by the method (`USLUP.md`) concerning the jinn holds here too: I rely on the measure laid down at `072-cin.md`** — no description is produced beyond what the text states. **What the verse says is this: the direction of the attachment was not where the one who served supposed it to be.**

**And the clause *aktharuhum bihim muʾminūn* must be recorded: "most of them had *faith* in them".** **The verb is the verb of *īmān*** — and here it is used not in a positive sense but in the sense of **attachment**. That is, the word states a trust whose direction is not specified.

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the verse makes an issue not of believing itself but of **what is believed in.** And that is what the sūra had been disputing all the way to the thirty-eighth verse.

### فَٱلْيَوْمَ لَا يَمْلِكُ بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ نَّفْعًا وَلَا ضَرًّا

**And the verb *ملك* (to own) comes for the second time in the sūra.**

| Verse | Expression | Who does not own what |
|---|---|---|
| **22** | *Lā **yamlikūna** mithqāla dharra* | **Those called upon** — ownership |
| **42** | *Lā **yamliku** baʿḍukum li-baʿḍin nafʿan wa lā ḍarrā* | **Everyone, towards everyone else** — benefit and harm |

**The ruling stated in this world in the twenty-second verse is seen in fact on that day in the forty-second.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading: **the sūra first gives something as a statement and then shows it as a scene.**

**And the phrase *baʿḍukum li-baʿḍ* must be recorded: reciprocity.** The same phrase was there in the thirty-first verse: *yarjiʿu **baʿḍuhum ilā baʿḍin** al-qawl.* **The same two parties, there exchanging speech, here reckoning benefit and harm.**

**ذُوقُوا۟** — "taste". **The root ذ-و-ق is widespread in the Qurʾān for the *tasting* of punishment, and it occurred in the twelfth verse too: *nudhiqhu min ʿadhābi's-saʿīr*.** **Both occurrences are in the same sūra.**

## 34/43 — وَإِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتُنَا بَيِّنَٰتٍ قَالُوا۟ مَا هَٰذَآ إِلَّا رَجُلٌ يُرِيدُ أَن يَصُدَّكُمْ عَمَّا كَانَ يَعْبُدُ ءَابَآؤُكُمْ وَقَالُوا۟ مَا هَٰذَآ إِلَّآ إِفْكٌ مُّفْتَرًى وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لِلْحَقِّ لَمَّا جَآءَهُمْ إِنْ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا سِحْرٌ مُّبِينٌ

*Wa idhā tutlā ʿalayhim āyātunā bayyinātin qālū mā hādhā illā rajulun yurīdu an yaṣuddakum ʿammā kāna yaʿbudu ābāʾukum, wa qālū mā hādhā illā ifkun muftarā, wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū li'l-ḥaqqi lammā jāʾahum in hādhā illā siḥrun mubīn*

"**When Our signs are recited to them plainly they say: 'This is nothing but a man who wants to turn you away from what your fathers served.'** And they say: '**This is nothing but an invented falsehood.**' **And those who disbelieved said of the truth when it came to them: 'This is nothing but plain sorcery.'**"

### One pattern repeated three times

**The verse transmits three sentences, and all three are on the same pattern:**

| Order | Sentence | Its target | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Mā hādhā **illā** rajulun yurīdu an yaṣuddakum* | **The person** | *mā … illā* |
| 2 | *Mā hādhā **illā** ifkun muftarā* | **The text** | *mā … illā* |
| 3 | *In hādhā **illā** siḥrun mubīn* | **The effect** | *in … illā* |

**All three sentences are on the pattern of negation plus exception (*ḥaṣr*): "this is nothing but that".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the pattern the three sentences share:** each of them performs **a reduction to a single explanation**. The pattern itself is one that closes a dispute — **it leaves no other possibility.**

**And the targets change in order: first the one who speaks, then what is said, then the effect of what is said.** Three objections, aimed at three different points of one event.

**And it is striking that the same *ḥaṣr* pattern is used in the sūra's own answering sentences too** — in the twenty-eighth verse (*mā arsalnāka **illā** kāffa*), in the forty-sixth verse (*innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥida*; *in huwa **illā** nadhīr*). **That is, the sūra itself uses the pattern the other side uses.** I record this as an observation about the word order.

### The content of the objection

**The first sentence must be recorded: *rajulun yurīdu an yaṣuddakum ʿammā kāna yaʿbudu ābāʾukum*.**

**The objection is not an objection about truth — it is an objection about *intent*:** "he **wants** to turn you away". **That is, what is put forward is not that what is said is false, but that saying it has a purpose.**

**I record this as an observation:** an objection of this kind does not discuss the content of a claim; **it discusses the claimant's motive.** And when it is framed that way, the claim itself is never examined at all.

**And the ground is again the forefathers: *ʿammā kāna yaʿbudu ābāʾukum*.** **This ground was treated in detail at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/22-23** (*innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā umma*), and there the difference between two words — *muhtadūn* / *muqtadūn* — was tabulated. **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference here must be recorded:** in al-Zukhruf following the forefathers was put forward as **a positive ground** ("we follow them"). **In Sabaʾ it comes in the form of a defence: "he wants to turn you away from it."** The same ground, that is: there a claim, here a thing to be protected.

**إِفْكٌ مُّفْتَرًى** — the root **أ-ف-ك**: **to turn a thing from its direction, to turn it upside down.** *Ifk* — speech that has turned the truth about. **The root was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/11 and 46/22, at `045-casiye.md` and at `043-zuhruf.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And *muftarā* has been added: from the root ف-ر-ي, "invented".** **The standing of the two words side by side is reinforcement: both twisted and invented.**

**And its link with the eighth verse must be recorded:** there it was said *aftarā ʿala'llāhi kadhibā* — **the same root (ف-ر-ي).** **What is asked as a question in the eighth verse is stated as a verdict in the forty-third.**

| Verse | Expression | Form |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *A-**ftarā** ʿala'llāhi kadhibā* | **A question** — as a possibility |
| **43** | *Ifkun **muftarā*** | **A verdict** — as something settled |

**This is a development that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** at the beginning of the sūra the objection had the form of a question; towards the end it turns into the form of an assertion. **In the intervening verses no proof has been offered; the only thing that has changed is the certainty of the expression.**

## 34/44-45 — وَمَآ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُم مِّن كُتُبٍ يَدْرُسُونَهَا وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَآ إِلَيْهِمْ قَبْلَكَ مِن نَّذِيرٍ · وَكَذَّبَ ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ وَمَا بَلَغُوا۟ مِعْشَارَ مَآ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُمْ فَكَذَّبُوا۟ رُسُلِى فَكَيْفَ كَانَ نَكِيرِ

*Wa mā ātaynāhum min kutubin yadrusūnahā wa mā arsalnā ilayhim qablaka min nadhīr · Wa kadhdhaba'lladhīna min qablihim wa mā balaghū miʿshāra mā ātaynāhum fa-kadhdhabū rusulī, fa-kayfa kāna nakīr*

"**Yet We had given them no books to study, nor had We sent them any warner before you.** **Those before them denied too; and these have not attained a tenth of what We gave them.** **They denied My messengers. How then was My rejection of them!**"

### The record that there is no ground

**The forty-fourth verse questions the ground of the objections in the forty-third, and negates two things at once:**

| What is negated | Expression | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **A written source** | *Mā ātaynāhum min kutubin **yadrusūnahā*** | There is no book they read |
| **An oral warning** | *Mā arsalnā ilayhim qablaka min **nadhīr*** | There is no warner who came before |

**Both kinds of proof are closed off: transmission and testimony.** **This pair stands in the same frame as the demand for proof treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/4** (*iʾtūnī bi-kitābin min qabli hādhā aw athāratin min ʿilm* — "bring me a book from before this, or some remnant of knowledge"). **I rely on that treatment.**

**And at `040-gafir.md` 40/56 (*bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum* — without any warrant that has come to them) the same measure was treated.** **Sabaʾ lays down the same measure by way of negation: there is no text they can point to as a ground.**

**يَدْرُسُونَهَا** — root **د-ر-س**: **to read a thing over and over, to work on it; and to efface its trace.** The lexicographers connect the two senses thus: **the trace of a much-trodden place is effaced; a much-read text is learnt by heart.** **I transmit this relation as a link the lexicographers have built.**

**And that the verb comes in the imperfect is worth recording: *yadrusūnahā* — "which they keep reading".** **The point, that is, is not merely that no book was given — it is that there is no text being worked on.**

### مِعْشَار — a tenth

**The word comes from the root *ʿushr* (a tenth) and is on the *mifʿāl* pattern. The lexicographers explain it as "a tenth"; some record that the pattern conveys *multiplicity or intensity*.**

**And the lexicographers differ over what the measure applies to:**

| View | What *miʿshār* concerns |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Power and wealth** — those before them were far stronger in these |
| 2 | **Time and favour** — the lifespan and means given to those before were greater |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference, and the verse does not specify in any case: *mā ātaynāhum* — "what We gave them".**

**The same line was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/44 and at `040-gafir.md` 40/21, 40/82** (*wa kānū ashadda minhum quwwatan* — they were stronger than these). **I rely on those treatments.**

**What belongs here is that the measure is given *numerically*, and I record this as my own reading:** Fāṭir and Ghāfir say "they were stronger"; **Sabaʾ gives a ratio: a tenth.** The comparison, that is, is built not with a qualitative but with a quantitative expression.

**And the structure of the argument is this:** they possessed far more, and even so they were not saved. **This is a direct answer to the claim in the thirty-fifth verse** — there it was said "our wealth is greater, we will not be punished". **The forty-fifth verse refutes that inference from history: those who possessed more wealth denied as well, and received their recompense.**

| Verse | Claim / answer |
|---|---|
| **35** | *Naḥnu **aktharu** amwālan* — **abundance affords protection** |
| **45** | *Wa mā balaghū **miʿshāra** mā ātaynāhum* — **they did not attain even a tenth of it; and they were not protected** |

**This is an exchange that can be verified within the sūra.**

**نَكِير** — root **ن-ك-ر**: **to not recognise, to reject, to find repugnant.** **The word was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/26**, where it was recorded that it is explained as "the consequence of My rejection" and that the lexicographers describe this as the severity of the punishment. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the sentence is word for word the same in two sūras: *fa-kayfa kāna nakīr* (Fāṭir 35/26 and Sabaʾ 34/45).** **In both it closes a block.** This is an overlap between sūras that can be verified.

## 34/46 — قُلْ إِنَّمَآ أَعِظُكُم بِوَٰحِدَةٍ أَن تَقُومُوا۟ لِلَّهِ مَثْنَىٰ وَفُرَٰدَىٰ ثُمَّ تَتَفَكَّرُوا۟ مَا بِصَاحِبِكُم مِّن جِنَّةٍ إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا نَذِيرٌ لَّكُم بَيْنَ يَدَىْ عَذَابٍ شَدِيدٍ

*Qul innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥida, an taqūmū lillāhi mathnā wa furādā thumma tatafakkarū, mā bi-ṣāḥibikum min jinna, in huwa illā nadhīrun lakum bayna yaday ʿadhābin shadīd*

"Say: '**I admonish you with one thing only: that you stand up for God, two by two and one by one, and then reflect.** **There is no madness in your companion.** He is nothing but a warner to you, ahead of a severe punishment.'"

### The densest verse of the sūra

**This verse is where the discipline of disputation the sūra has been building all along turns into a method. It deserves detailed treatment.**

**إِنَّمَآ أَعِظُكُم بِوَٰحِدَةٍ** — "I admonish you with **one thing** only."

**And that one thing is not an article of belief but a *procedure*:** to stand up, then to reflect.

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the verse's own wording:** what comes after *bi-wāḥida* is not "believe that God is one" — it is **"stand up and reflect".** The admonition, that is, is given not as a conclusion but as **a method that leads to a conclusion.**

**And *wāḥida* comes in the feminine: it is taken as the adjective of an elided word (*khaṣla* — a trait, an item).** The lexicographers explain it so.

**وَعْظ** — root **و-ع-ظ**: **to remind in a way that softens the heart, to counsel.** The word differs from giving information: **to remind, with effect, of something already known.**

### أَن تَقُومُوا۟ لِلَّهِ — "that you stand up for God"

**ق-و-م** — the concrete sense of the root: **to rise to one's feet, to stand up, to take charge of a task.**

**And the qualification *lillāh* must be recorded: "for God".** **The direction of the standing up, that is, has been specified.** Not to win an argument, not to please a company.

**Whether the standing here is a bodily movement or a "setting to work" has been debated:**

| View | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **To rise to one's feet in fact** — to get up from where one is sitting and go apart |
| 2 | **To set about a task** — the Arabic idiom *qāma bi'l-amr*: to take a matter in hand |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **And both arrive at the same result: to come out of the state one is in.**

### مَثْنَىٰ وَفُرَٰدَىٰ — two by two and one by one

**These are the two most striking words in the verse.**

**مَثْنَىٰ** — root **ث-ن-ي**: **two, to fold, to double.** **The pattern conveys *distribution*: "two by two".** **This pattern was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/1** (*mathnā wa thulātha wa rubāʿ* — the wings of the angels), and it was recorded there: **these patterns state what falls to each one separately.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**فُرَٰدَىٰ** — the plural of *fard*; root **ف-ر-د**: **to be single, to be alone.** **The word means "one by one".**

**And now attention must be paid to what is *not* in the list.**

| Named | Not named |
|---|---|
| *Mathnā* — **two by two** | **Three by three, four by four** |
| *Furādā* — **one by one** | **All together, in a crowd** |

**In Fāṭir, where the same pattern is used, the number rose as far as three and four (*mathnā wa thulātha wa rubāʿ*). Here it stops at two — and then goes down to *one*.** The series, that is, **decreases**: two, then one.

### The crowd as an obstacle to thinking — my own reading

**I record this as my own reading, and I give its support plainly in four points:**

**One — the numbers go down, not up.** In Fāṭir the pattern widened (2, 3, 4); here it narrows (2, 1). **The verse wants the number to fall.**

**Two — the third verb is *tafakkur*, and it comes with *thumma*.** *Thumma* in Arabic conveys **a sequence with an interval**: not immediately after, but **after a while.** Reflection, that is, is placed not right on the heels of standing up but **after** it.

**Three — what is described in the preceding verse is words spoken in a body.** In the forty-third verse three sentences were transmitted, and all three from a plural mouth: *qālū … wa qālū … wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū*. **What the verse stands against, that is, is sentences repeated in chorus.**

**Four — it sets up the same method as the twenty-fourth verse of the sūra.** There the verdict was suspended and room made for the addressee's reasoning. **The same thing is done here, this time by a physical arrangement.**

**The reading I draw from these four supports is this:** the crowd is set down as the obstacle in the way of thinking. What a person says while in a group is very often not the result of his own reasoning but the common voice of the group. **The verse asks that the group be broken up so that reasoning can work: two people at most, or alone.**

**And being in twos has its place too, and this is worth recording separately:** the verse does not set solitude down as the only remedy. **Two people are the smallest unit in which thinking can be tested against another** — one can speak, one can object, but the pressure of a crowd cannot be brought to bear. **Solitude is the place where the outside voice is cut off altogether.**

**I record that this reading is not binding; similar explanations are widely transmitted in the classical commentaries as well, but I rely on the four supports above.**

**What this says to the present is direct:** in forming an opinion about a matter, the question of where the opinion was formed comes before the opinion itself. **An opinion formed inside a crowd is very often the crowd's opinion.**

### ثُمَّ تَتَفَكَّرُوا۟

**ف-ك-ر** — the sense of the root: **to turn a thing over and over in the mind.** **And the verb is of Form V (*tafaʿʿul*): this form conveys *effort* and *continuity* — "to dwell on it and think it through", not at a single glance.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the form itself says that thinking **takes time**. And the particle *thumma* says the same thing. **Two signs pointing the same way: there is to be no haste.**

**And the object of the verb is not given: *tatafakkarū* — "reflect".** What they are to reflect on is not said. **The subject, that is, is settled not by the verse but by the one who thinks.**

### مَا بِصَاحِبِكُم مِّن جِنَّةٍ

**And the concluding sentence comes: "there is no madness in your companion."**

**The word *ṣāḥibukum* was treated in detail at `053-necm.md` 53/2 and at `081-tekvir.md` 81/22.** The summary of what was recorded there is this: **the root is ص-ح-ب, to be together; the word rests on the acquaintance built by long years of living side by side — "you know him".** And it was tabulated that al-Najm and al-Takwīr use the same proof: **an appeal to the addressee's own memory.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**Sabaʾ adds a third link to this series, and the table can be completed:**

| Place | Sentence | The charge rejected |
|---|---|---|
| al-Takwīr 81/22 | *Mā ṣāḥibukum bi-majnūn* | **Madness** |
| al-Najm 53/2 | *Mā ḍalla ṣāḥibukum wa mā ghawā* | **Straying and going wrong** |
| **Sabaʾ 34/46** | *Mā bi-ṣāḥibikum min jinna* | **Madness** |

**And what Sabaʾ adds is *where* the sentence is placed. I record this as my own reading; its support is the order of the verse:** in al-Takwīr and al-Najm the sentence comes as a direct announcement. **In Sabaʾ it comes after a *procedure*: stand up, reflect — and then you will see that there is no madness.**

**That is, the verdict is set down not as information given to the reader but as a conclusion his own reasoning will arrive at.**

**And the exchange within the sūra is completed:**

| Verse | Word | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *Rajul* — "a man" | **Those who disbelieve** — pushing away |
| **8** | *Am bihī **jinna*** — "is there madness?" | **Those who disbelieve** — as a question |
| **43** | *Rajulun yurīdu an yaṣuddakum* | **Those who disbelieve** — a charge of intent |
| **46** | *Mā bi-**ṣāḥibikum** min **jinna*** | **The answer** — both words come back at once |

**The forty-sixth verse answers both *rajul* and *jinna*: the one with *ṣāḥibikum*, the other with a plain negation.** This is an exchange that can be verified within the sūra, and it is one of its most regular weaves.

**بَيْنَ يَدَىْ عَذَابٍ شَدِيدٍ** — "ahead of a severe punishment".

**The phrase *bayna yaday* — "between his two hands", that is, *before it*.** **The same phrase occurred in the thirty-first verse: *bi'lladhī bayna yadayh* (in what came before it).** **And this temporal use of the phrase is common in Arabic: the front of a thing is what comes before it.**

**And the warner's position is given by this phrase:** the one who stands before the punishment. **A warning, that is, is something that comes before the event, not after it — and that is precisely the condition of its being a warning.**

## 34/47 — قُلْ مَا سَأَلْتُكُم مِّنْ أَجْرٍ فَهُوَ لَكُمْ إِنْ أَجْرِىَ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ شَهِيدٌ

*Qul mā saʾaltukum min ajrin fa-huwa lakum, in ajriya illā ʿala'llāh, wa huwa ʿalā kulli shayʾin shahīd*

"Say: '**Whatever fee I have asked of you, let it be yours.** **My fee rests with God alone.** He is witness over everything.'"

### The unusual build of the sentence

**Sentences stating that the prophets ask no fee are common in the Qurʾān, and they are generally on this pattern: *mā asʾalukum ʿalayhi min ajr* — "I ask you no fee for this."**

**The sentence here is different, and the difference must be recorded:** the verb is in **the perfect** (*saʾaltukum* — I asked) and a gift follows it: *fa-huwa lakum* — **"let it be yours."**

**The lexicographers explain the sentence in two ways:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A conditional sense**: "if I have asked anything, let it be yours" — that is, I never asked |
| 2 | **A hypothetical gift**: "even if there is something I asked for, I have made it over to you" |

**Both explanations are transmitted and both arrive at the same place; I do not impose a preference.**

**But something can be said about the *form* of the sentence, and I record this as my own reading; its support is that the verb is in the perfect:** the sentence does not reject the charge — **it removes the subject of the charge.** To say "I did not ask" opens a dispute (you did / I did not). **To say "if I asked, let it be yours" closes the dispute**, because it leaves the other side nothing to defend.

**And this continues the attitude of the twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth verses of the sūra:** there too the verdict was suspended and the language of accusation abandoned. **In the forty-seventh verse the same attitude takes a third form: giving up a claim.**

**إِنْ أَجْرِىَ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱللَّهِ** — and the fee is not denied; **its address is changed.** **The same pattern was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/10 (*man kāna yurīdu'l-ʿizzata fa-lillāhi'l-ʿizzatu jamīʿā*) and at `039-zumer.md` 39/44 (*lillāhi'sh-shafāʿatu jamīʿā*):** the desire is not rejected, its owner is named. **I rely on those treatments.**

**شَهِيد** — root **ش-ه-د**: **to be present, to see, to bear witness.** **And that it is placed at the end of the sentence is worth recording:** the question of a fee is a matter of account between two parties; **the sentence closes by naming a third party — the one who sees.**

## 34/48-49 — قُلْ إِنَّ رَبِّى يَقْذِفُ بِٱلْحَقِّ عَلَّٰمُ ٱلْغُيُوبِ · قُلْ جَآءَ ٱلْحَقُّ وَمَا يُبْدِئُ ٱلْبَٰطِلُ وَمَا يُعِيدُ

*Qul inna rabbī yaqdhifu bi'l-ḥaqq, ʿallāmu'l-ghuyūb · Qul jāʾa'l-ḥaqqu wa mā yubdiʾu'l-bāṭilu wa mā yuʿīd*

"Say: '**My Lord hurls the truth.** He is the great knower of all things unseen.' Say: '**The truth has come. Falsehood can neither begin anything nor bring it back.**'"

### يَقْذِفُ بِٱلْحَقِّ — root: ق-ذ-ف

**The concrete sense of the root: to throw a thing far, to hurl it.** *Qadhafa bi'l-ḥajar* — he threw a stone. **And the word conveys that what is thrown goes *fast and with force*.**

**That the verb is used in this context is striking and must be recorded:** for revelation, verbs such as "to send down" (*inzāl*), "to announce" (*inbāʾ*), "to recite" (*tilāwa*) are common. **The verb chosen here is *qadhf* — to hurl.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the picture in the root:** *qadhf* is a thing thrown towards a target, and **it does not wait for the other side's acceptance.** The verb pictures the coming of the truth not as an offer but as **a hit.**

**And this verb will be used once more in the sūra, of the other side — this is one of its most striking verbal weaves:**

| Verse | Expression | Who throws | What they throw | From where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **48** | *Rabbī **yaqdhifu** bi'l-**ḥaqq*** | **God** | **The truth** | **Not stated** — it hits |
| **53** | *Wa **yaqdhifūna** bi'l-**ghaybi** min makānin baʿīd* | **Those who disbelieve** | **The unseen** — what is not known | ***From a far place*** |

**The same verb, two sides, two objects.** And the difference lies in three places: **who throws, what is thrown, and the distance.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the verb the two verses share:** on one side **the truth** is thrown, and where it is thrown from is not said; on the other side **the unseen** is thrown, and it is stated that it is thrown **from far off.** **What is thrown from far off does not find its target.** The sūra describes the way both sides make their claims with the same verb, and sets the difference between them in terms of whether the throw lands.

**عَلَّٰمُ ٱلْغُيُوب** — an intensive form (*faʿʿāl*): **one who knows much.** And *ghuyūb* is a plural: **things unseen.**

**This is the fourth step on the sūra's line of the unseen** (3, 14, 48, 53), tabulated above in the treatment of 34/14. **In the third verse it was *ʿālimi'l-ghayb* (singular); here it is *ʿallāmu'l-ghuyūb* (intensive plus plural).** **As the sūra nears its close it repeats the same attribute, enlarged.**

### جَآءَ ٱلْحَقُّ وَمَا يُبْدِئُ ٱلْبَٰطِلُ وَمَا يُعِيدُ

**"The truth has come; falsehood can neither begin nor bring back."**

**Two verbs are set against one another:**

| Verb | Root | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| *Yubdiʾu* | **ب-د-أ** | **To begin** — to bring forth for the first time |
| *Yuʿīdu* | **ع-و-د** | **To bring back** — to repeat |

**And this pair is generally used in the Qurʾān of *creation*** (*yabdaʾu'l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduh* — He begins creation, then repeats it). **Here the same pair is negated of falsehood.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the common use of the two verbs in the Qurʾān:** the denial of the verbs of creating to falsehood says that **falsehood produces nothing.** Falsehood can neither build anything new nor bring back what it built in the past.

**And the lexicographers give the sentence another sense as well:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Falsehood** as subject: falsehood neither begins nor brings back |
| 2 | **Falsehood** as object (with an elided subject, "the party of falsehood"): falsehood can neither be brought forth again nor repeated |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**The place of the sentence in the sūra must be recorded:** in the forty-third verse the truth was called *siḥr* (*wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū **li'l-ḥaqqi** lammā jāʾahum in hādhā illā siḥrun mubīn*). **The forty-ninth verse answers with the same word: *jāʾa'l-ḥaqq*.**

| Verse | Where *ḥaqq* stands |
|---|---|
| **6** | *Huwa'l-**ḥaqq*** — those given knowledge **see** it |
| **23** | *Qālu'l-**ḥaqq*** — what was said is **the truth** |
| **43** | *Li'l-**ḥaqqi** lammā jāʾahum* — when it came they called it **sorcery** |
| **48** | *Yaqdhifu bi'l-**ḥaqq*** — it **is hurled** |
| **49** | *Jāʾa'l-**ḥaqq*** — it **has come** |

**Five occurrences, in five separate places in the sūra.** **And what was rejected in the forty-third verse along with the verb "came" is announced in the forty-ninth with the verb "came" again.** This is an exchange that can be verified within the sūra.

## 34/50 — قُلْ إِن ضَلَلْتُ فَإِنَّمَآ أَضِلُّ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِى وَإِنِ ٱهْتَدَيْتُ فَبِمَا يُوحِىٓ إِلَىَّ رَبِّىٓ إِنَّهُۥ سَمِيعٌ قَرِيبٌ

*Qul in ḍalaltu fa-innamā aḍillu ʿalā nafsī, wa ini'htadaytu fa-bimā yūḥī ilayya rabbī, innahū samīʿun qarīb*

"Say: '**If I go astray, I go astray only against myself. If I am guided, it is by what my Lord reveals to me.** He is hearing, near.'"

### The asymmetry of the two options

**The sentence is built on two conditions — but the answers to the two options are not symmetrical, and the whole point lies there:**

| Condition | Answer | Address |
|---|---|---|
| *In ḍalaltu* — **if I go astray** | *Fa-innamā aḍillu **ʿalā nafsī*** | **To me** — the harm is my own |
| *Wa ini'htadaytu* — **if I am guided** | *Fa-bimā **yūḥī ilayya rabbī*** | **To Him** — the cause is revelation |

**The expected symmetry would have been: "if I go astray, against myself; if I am guided, to my own credit."** **The sentence does not build that.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the structural difference of the two answers:** the wrong is ascribed to oneself, the right to its source. **That is, the speaker takes on the responsibility for the error but claims no share in the success.**

**And this is the final form of the attitude taken in the twenty-fourth verse. The sūra's line of the discipline of disputation is thus completed:**

| Verse | Step |
|---|---|
| **24** | *Innā aw iyyākum* — **the verdict is suspended** |
| **25** | *Lā tusʾalūna ʿammā **ajramnā*** — **the heavy word taken on oneself** |
| **47** | *Mā saʾaltukum min ajrin fa-huwa lakum* — **a claim given up** |
| **50** | *In ḍalaltu fa-innamā aḍillu ʿalā nafsī* — **the possibility of error accepted** |

**Four steps in four separate places, and all of them face the same way: the speaking party does not put itself in a protected position.** This is a line that can be verified from the text, and I record it as my own reading.

**What this says to the present must be recorded:** the seriousness of a claim is measured by how the claimant meets the possibility of being wrong. **The verse does not reject the possibility of error — it takes on its consequence.** And it does not claim the possibility of being right for itself either.

**سَمِيعٌ قَرِيبٌ** — "hearing, near".

**The root ق-ر-ب occurs here for the second time in the sūra, and the third will come in the very next verse:**

| Verse | Expression | Whose nearness |
|---|---|---|
| **37** | *Tu**qarrib**ukum ʿindanā zulfā* | **A human being's** drawing near — not through wealth |
| **50** | *Innahū samīʿun **qarīb*** | **God's** nearness |
| **51** | *Wa ukhidhū min makānin **qarīb*** | The nearness of **being seized** |

**Three occurrences, three different directions.** **And the sequence is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the sūra says first the way by which a person cannot draw near (37), then that God is near already (50), then that escape is blocked from close at hand as well (51). **The same root carries three different relations.**

## 34/51-53 — وَلَوْ تَرَىٰٓ إِذْ فَزِعُوا۟ فَلَا فَوْتَ وَأُخِذُوا۟ مِن مَّكَانٍ قَرِيبٍ · وَقَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا بِهِۦ وَأَنَّىٰ لَهُمُ ٱلتَّنَاوُشُ مِن مَّكَانٍۭ بَعِيدٍ · وَقَدْ كَفَرُوا۟ بِهِۦ مِن قَبْلُ وَيَقْذِفُونَ بِٱلْغَيْبِ مِن مَّكَانٍۭ بَعِيدٍ

*Wa law tarā idh faziʿū fa-lā fawta wa ukhidhū min makānin qarīb · Wa qālū āmannā bihī wa annā lahumu't-tanāwushu min makānin baʿīd · Wa qad kafarū bihī min qabl, wa yaqdhifūna bi'l-ghaybi min makānin baʿīd*

"**If you could but see the moment they are seized with terror! There is no escape now; they are taken from close at hand.** '**We believe**', they say. **But how are they to reach out from a far place?** **They had denied it before, and kept throwing at the unseen from a far place.**"

### فَزِعُوا۟ — for the second time in the sūra

**The root ف-ز-ع occurred in the twenty-third verse: *ḥattā idhā fuzziʿa ʿan qulūbihim* — when the terror was lifted from their hearts. Here the same root comes in Form I and in the active: *faziʿū* — they were seized with terror.**

| Verse | Verb | Who | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **23** | *Fuzziʿa **ʿan** qulūbihim* | Those in the scene | **The lifting of fear** |
| **51** | ***Faziʿū*** | Those who disbelieved | **The coming of fear** |

**The same root in two opposite directions — and twenty-eight verses lie between them.** This is a contrast that can be verified within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading: **on one side fear is lifted, on the other side fear descends.**

### فَلَا فَوْتَ — root: ف-و-ت

**The root was analysed in detail at `067-mulk.md` 67/3 (*mā tarā fī khalqi'r-raḥmāni min tafāwut*).** The summary of what was recorded there is this: **the primary sense of the root is to escape, to slip out of one's hands, to get away through a gap; *fātahu'l-amr* — the matter escaped him. And *tafāwut* (Form VI) is two things' failing to match, a gap remaining between them.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the use of the root in its *bare* form and negated: *fa-lā fawta*.**

**The pattern is *lā al-nāfiya li'l-jins*** (the *lā* that negates a whole class): **"there is no escape at all."** It corresponds to the English "there is nowhere left to run".

**And the way the two sūras use the root can be compared:**

| Place | Word | What escapes |
|---|---|---|
| al-Mulk 67/3 | *Tafāwut* — **discrepancy** | One part of **the creation** escaping another |
| **Sabaʾ 34/51** | *Lā **fawta*** | **A human being's** slipping away — there is none |

**The same root: in one it is said that there is no gap in the creation, in the other that there is no gap to escape through.** I record this as an observation about the language.

**وَأُخِذُوا۟ مِن مَّكَانٍ قَرِيبٍ** — "they were taken from a place close at hand."

**The verb is passive (*ukhidhū*) and in the perfect.** **It is worth recording: the scene is told of the future, but the verb is given in the past tense.** **In Arabic this use conveys the *certainty* of the event:** as certain as if it had already happened.

**And the phrase *min makānin qarīb* is the first link in the weave of distance built in the last three verses of the sūra.**

### ٱلتَّنَاوُش — root: ن-و-ش

**The concrete sense of the root: to stretch out the hand and take a thing, to reach so as to take.** *Nāsha'sh-shayʾa* — he reached out for it, he took it.

**And the pattern is of Form VI (*tafāʿul*): *tanāwush* — to reach out mutually, or with exertion.**

**The picture in the word must be recorded:** *tanāwush* is **the stretching out of the hand towards something within reach.** Like taking a fruit from its branch. **And precisely for this reason the contradiction in the sentence emerges:** *annā lahumu't-tanāwushu **min makānin baʿīd*** — **"how are they to reach out from a far place?"**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the meaning of the word itself:** *tanāwush* is already a verb that **requires nearness.** **It cannot be done from a distance — the definition of the word does not allow it.** The sentence states the impossibility not as a ruling but **from inside the word.**

**And there is a variant reading of the word:**

| Reading | Root | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| *At-tanāwush* | **ن-و-ش** | **To reach out, to try to take** |
| *At-tanāʾush* (with hamza) | **ن-أ-ش** | **To be far off, to be late** — or: to take little by little |

**Both readings are transmitted. On the second reading the sense of "an attempt to take from a distance" is reinforced; I do not impose a preference.**

**أَنَّىٰ** — in Arabic an interrogative particle meaning **"how / from where"**; here it conveys **denial**: "it is not possible."

### Faith after the punishment has been seen

**And what the verse says is a rule recorded several times in the series.**

**At `039-zumer.md` 39/56-59 three excuses were tabulated**, and the third was *law anna lī karratan* (if only I could return once more); it was recorded there that **the second excuse cuts off the third.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And at `040-gafir.md` 40/77-85 the exact counterpart of this sentence was treated:** *fa-lammā raʾaw baʾsanā qālū āmannā bi'llāhi waḥdah … fa-lam yaku yanfaʿuhum īmānuhum lammā raʾaw baʾsanā* — **"their faith after they had seen Our might did not avail them"** — and there it was named *sunnata'llāhi'llatī qad khalat fī ʿibādih*: **not an exception but an ongoing way of working.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**I set the three places side by side:**

| Place | What is said | Why it is not accepted |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/58 | *Law anna lī karratan* — **a wish to return** | The warning had already come (39/59) |
| Ghāfir 40/84 | *Āmannā bi'llāhi waḥdah* — **a declaration of faith** | *Sunnat Allāh* — an ongoing way of working (40/85) |
| **Sabaʾ 34/52** | *Āmannā bihī* — **a declaration of faith** | *Tanāwush* cannot be done from afar — **the word itself** |

**The three verses give the same rule with three different grounds: one by the warning's having already come, one by the continuity of the way, and one by the meaning of a word.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the third row above:** Sabaʾ does not state the ground as a rule. **It gives a picture: a hand reaching from far away.** And the picture already contains the outcome.

### وَيَقْذِفُونَ بِٱلْغَيْبِ مِن مَّكَانٍۭ بَعِيدٍ

**And the fifty-third verse brings back the verb of the forty-eighth. I tabulated it above in the treatment of 34/48-49.**

**The lexicographers dwell on the sense of the construction, and what the transmitted explanations have in common is this: *to throw stones at the unseen* is to speak about something one has not seen without taking any measure.** It is a picture close to the English "shooting in the dark". **I give this comparison as a convenience of explanation, not as something transmitted.**

**And that the word is *ghayb* must be recorded:** what is thrown is what the thrower **does not know.** **That is, the sentence gives both the subject of the claim and the state of knowledge behind it in a single construction.**

### The three occurrences of the word مَكَان

**The last three verses of the sūra build a weave of distance, and this is the backbone of the close:**

| Verse | Construction | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **51** | *Min makānin **qarīb*** | **Being seized** — from near |
| **52** | *Min makānin **baʿīd*** | **Reaching out** — from far, in vain |
| **53** | *Min makānin **baʿīd*** | **Throwing stones** — from far, without a target |

**Three verses, three *makān*s, two distances.** **And the contrast must be recorded: the one who seizes is near, the one who is seized is far.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the construction the three verses share:** the distance needed for escape is not there (they were seized from near), and the nearness needed for holding on is not there either (they reach from far off). **In both directions the distance works against them.**

**And this ties back to the nineteenth verse:** the people of Sabaʾ had said *rabbanā **bāʿid** bayna asfārinā* — "**set** our journeys **far apart**". **The distance that was asked for comes back at the end of the sūra as the distance that cannot be closed.** This is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.

## 34/54 — وَحِيلَ بَيْنَهُمْ وَبَيْنَ مَا يَشْتَهُونَ كَمَا فُعِلَ بِأَشْيَاعِهِم مِّن قَبْلُ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ فِى شَكٍّ مُّرِيبٍ

*Wa ḥīla baynahum wa bayna mā yashtahūna kamā fuʿila bi-ashyāʿihim min qabl, innahum kānū fī shakkin murīb*

"**A barrier is set between them and what they desire** — as was done before with **those of their kind**. **For they were in a doubt that unsettles.**"

### وَحِيلَ بَيْنَهُمْ — root: ح-و-ل

**The concrete sense of the root: to turn, to change; and to come in between two things.** *Ḥāla bayna'sh-shayʾayn* — it came between two things, it separated them. **And from the same root *ḥāʾil* — a screen, a barrier; *ḥawl* — the surroundings of a thing, and a year (time that comes round).**

**The verb is passive: *ḥīla* — "a barrier was set".** **Who did the separating is not said.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** in its last clause too the sūra does not name the agent. **In the thirty-first verse there was *mawqūfūn* (those halted), in the thirty-eighth *muḥḍarūn* (those brought), in the fifty-first *ukhidhū* (they were taken). In the fifty-fourth verse, *ḥīla* (a barrier was set).** **Four passive verbs, distributed through the last section of the sūra.**

**وَبَيْنَ مَا يَشْتَهُونَ** — "between them and what they desire".

**ش-ه-و** — root: **intense wanting, appetite.** **And the verb is of Form VIII (*ishtahā*): to take the desire upon oneself, to crave.**

**And that the object is left indefinite is worth recording: *mā yashtahūn* — "what they desire".** What it is is not said. **The clause, that is, covers not one particular object but the whole of desire.**

**This indefiniteness joins with everything the sūra has enumerated from the beginning, and I record this as my own reading:** throughout the sūra the things held in hand were counted out — gardens, roads, wealth, children, metals, buildings. **The closing clause names none of them; it gathers them all into a single indefinite phrase.** And what it says is not that they were lost — **but that something came in between.** The object does not cease to exist; **the possibility of reaching it is cut off.**

### كَمَا فُعِلَ بِأَشْيَاعِهِم مِّن قَبْلُ

**أَشْيَاع** — the plural of *shīʿa*; root **ش-ي-ع**: **to go after someone, to follow him, to spread.** *Shīʿa* — the group that gathers around a person or a view. **And *shāʾiʿa* (a report that spreads) is from this root too.**

**How the word is rendered must be recorded: "their like" or "those who took the same road".** **What is meant, that is, is not a lineage or a nation — but *those who share the same stance*.**

**A note per the method (`USLUP.md`):** what the verse describes is an **attribute**. No blanket ruling is issued about a particular community; **whoever bears that attribute is included in it.**

**And the clause builds the idea of a settled way once more: *kamā fuʿila … min qabl* — "as was done before".** **At `040-gafir.md` 40/85 *sunnata'llāhi'llatī qad khalat fī ʿibādih* was treated in detail**, and **at `035-fatir.md` 35/43 (*fa-lan tajida li-sunnati'llāhi tabdīlā*) the same concept was treated.** **I rely on those treatments.**

**The difference that belongs here must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** Ghāfir and Fāṭir **name** the rule — they say *sunnat Allāh*. **Sabaʾ does not name the rule; it only draws a likeness: "as was done before."** The same continuity, that is, is given not as a concept but as **a comparison**.

### فِى شَكٍّ مُّرِيبٍ — the last word of the sūra

**And the sūra closes by stating the reason: *innahum kānū fī shakkin murīb*.**

**In the twenty-first verse the distinction had been drawn between *faith* and *doubt*:** *li-naʿlama man yuʾminu bi'l-ākhirati mimman huwa minhā **fī shakk***. **The fifty-fourth verse closes with the same word.**

| Verse | Expression | Its work |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | *Huwa minhā **fī shakk*** | **The measure of the distinction** — one of the two groups |
| **54** | *Kānū **fī shakkin murīb*** | **The ground of the outcome** — the closing verdict |

**The sūra uses as its closing verdict the measure it set down in its middle.** This is a link that can be verified within the sūra.

**مُرِيب** — root **ر-ي-ب**: **doubt, hesitation, uneasy suspicion.** The active participle of Form IV: **that which casts into doubt, that which makes uneasy.**

**And the construction is worth recording: *shakkin murīb* — "a doubt that puts in doubt". Two words from the same field of meaning set side by side.**

**The lexicographers record a difference between *shakk* and *rayb*:** *shakk* is the equality of two possibilities — a lack of knowledge. ***Rayb* is doubt that carries unease** — a suspicion that troubles a person, that gnaws at him.

**I transmit this distinction as a relation the lexicographers have built.**

**And what the construction does is this, and I record it as my own reading:** in closing, the sūra does not deliver a diagnosis of **denial**. **The diagnosis it delivers is *doubt*** — and the kind of doubt is specified: a doubt that troubles, that leaves no peace.

**That is, the sūra leaves the other side not as a settled denier but as someone in hesitation.** And this is consistent with the attitude of the twenty-fourth verse: there too no final verdict was given, and two possibilities were set side by side. **The sūra ends where it began: the verdict deferred, the reasoning left to the addressee.**

## The sūra as a whole — looking back

### The thread of the sūra: from praise to thanks, from thanks to judgement

**The sūra opens with a sentence of praise (1), tells the trial of thanks of two peoples (10-21), and arrives at a sentence of judgement (37).** And that judgement sums up the story of both peoples in a single sentence: **what is in your hand does not bring you near.**

| Stage | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Praise** | 1-2 | Whose the ownership and the knowledge are — in both worlds |
| **Objection** | 3-9 | "The Hour will not come", "when we have been torn to pieces?" |
| **The first people** | 10-14 | It was given, they were **commanded to give thanks**, and it was shown that the unseen is not known |
| **The second people** | 15-21 | It was given, they **turned away**, and they became legend |
| **The measure** | 22-30 | Those called upon own nothing; the discipline of disputation is built |
| **The wrong measure** | 31-35 | "Our wealth is great, we shall not be punished" |
| **The right measure** | 36-39 | **Wealth and children do not bring near; what is spent is replaced** |
| **The method** | 46 | **Stand up, two by two and one by one, then reflect** |
| **The close** | 47-54 | The truth was hurled; no hand reaches from far off; they were in doubt |

### What the sūra says to the present

**Three things are worth recording, and all three come out of the text:**

**One — the question of the measure.** The sentence in the thirty-fifth verse is an inference: I have more, therefore my condition is better. **In rejecting this inference the sūra rejects not wealth but the use of wealth as a measure.** And it puts another measure in its place: faith and deeds (37), and the replacing of what is spent (39).

**Two — the place of thinking.** The forty-sixth verse says that one of the conditions of arriving at a sound conclusion is **where** the thinking is done. **A crowd is not a place for reasoning.**

**Three — the discipline of disputation.** The sentence in the twenty-fourth verse (*either we or you*), the choice of words in the twenty-fifth (the heavy word kept for oneself), the giving up in the forty-seventh and the admission of error in the fiftieth **make up a single method:** to speak without condemning the other side, and by placing oneself inside the dispute as well.

**And even in closing the sūra does not break this attitude:** the final diagnosis is not denial but **doubt**.

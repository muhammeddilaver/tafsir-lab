# 29. Sūrat al-ʿAnkabūt

**Sixty-nine verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed during the Meccan period. It takes its name from the simile of the **ٱلْعَنكَبُوت** (the spider) in verse 41.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-13 | **Trial** — the testing of the claim to faith | *Wa la-yusʾalunna yawma'l-qiyāmati ʿammā kānū yaftarūn* (13) |
| **II** | 14-40 | Noah (Nūḥ), Abraham (Ibrāhīm), Lot (Lūṭ) and the communities destroyed | *Wa lākin kānū anfusahum yaẓlimūn* (40) |
| **III** | 41-52 | **The spider's web** and the proof of the Book | *Wa'lladhīna āmanū bi'l-bāṭili … humu'l-khāsirūn* (52) |
| **IV** | 53-69 | Haste, provision, sincerity at sea, and the closing | *Wa inna'llāha la-maʿa'l-muḥsinīn* (69) |

**The backbone of the sūra is the concept of *fitna* (trial).** The word comes as early as the second verse and turns round through the whole sūra. **And the sūra's question is a single one: is it enough to say "I have believed"?**

---

## 29/1-3 — الٓمٓ · أَحَسِبَ ٱلنَّاسُ أَن يُتْرَكُوٓا۟ أَن يَقُولُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا وَهُمْ لَا يُفْتَنُونَ · وَلَقَدْ فَتَنَّا ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ فَلَيَعْلَمَنَّ ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِينَ صَدَقُوا۟ وَلَيَعْلَمَنَّ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ

"Alif-lām-mīm. **Did people suppose that they would simply be left alone for saying 'we have believed', and not be tested?** We certainly tested those before them. **God will surely know those who speak the truth, and He will surely know the liars.**"

### The question of the sūra

**The note on the disconnected letters has been set down in many places in this series; I do not repeat it here.**

**The second verse is built as a question, and the structure of the question must be recorded:** *a-ḥasiba'n-nāsu an yutrakū* — **"did they suppose they would be left alone?"**

The root **ت-ر-ك**: to leave, to abandon to its own devices. **What is asked about, then, is not the existence of the test — it is the expectation of being left without one.**

**And the same question was asked in different words at `002-bakara.md` 2/214:** *am ḥasibtum an tadkhulu'l-jannata wa lammā yaʾtikum mathalu'lladhīna khalaw min qablikum* — **"did you suppose you would enter the Garden before there came upon you the like of what came upon those who passed before you?"**

| Place | Verb | What was supposed |
|---|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/214 | *Ḥasibtum* | **Entering the Garden** — without hardship |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/2** | *Ḥasiba'n-nās* | **Being left alone** — without being tested |

**The same verb (*ḥasiba* — to suppose) answers to the same expectation in two sūras.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

### فَلَيَعْلَمَنَّ ٱللَّهُ

**The expression "God will surely know" has been discussed in the classical commentaries** — since God's knowledge is already all-encompassing.

**The main explanation transmitted is this:** the *ʿilm* here is **knowledge's coming out into the open**, that is, the hidden becoming visible. **The lexicographers explain this as *ʿilm al-wuqūʿ*, or "the realisation of what is known".**

**I transmit this as an explanation that is reported, and I add the following as my own reading:** the work of the verse is not to make good a deficiency of knowledge — it is **to make the testing of the claim necessary.** Indeed the object of the sentence is not knowledge but **two groups:** those who speak the truth and the liars.

## 29/4-7 — أَمْ حَسِبَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَعْمَلُونَ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ أَن يَسْبِقُونَا سَآءَ مَا يَحْكُمُونَ · مَن كَانَ يَرْجُوا۟ لِقَآءَ ٱللَّهِ فَإِنَّ أَجَلَ ٱللَّهِ لَـَٔاتٍ · وَمَن جَٰهَدَ فَإِنَّمَا يُجَٰهِدُ لِنَفْسِهِۦ

"**Did those who do evil deeds suppose that they would outstrip Us?** How ill they judge! **Whoever hopes for the meeting with God — the term God has set will surely come.** Whoever strives, strives **only for himself**."

### The second "did they suppose"

**In the second verse the question was *a-ḥasiba'n-nās* (did people suppose); in the fourth verse the same verb comes a second time:** *am ḥasiba'lladhīna yaʿmalūna's-sayyiʾāt.*

| Verse | Who | What is supposed |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | *Al-nās* | **Being left without being tested** |
| 4 | *Alladhīna yaʿmalūna's-sayyiʾāt* | **Getting ahead and escaping** |

**The same verb, two different expectations.** This is the structure set up in the opening of the sūra.

**أَن يَسْبِقُونَا** — root **س-ب-ق**: to get ahead in a race. **The root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/148 and at `056-vakia.md` 56/10, 56/60**, and there the active participle (*sābiqūn*) was compared with the passive participle (*masbūqīn*). **Here it comes in verbal form and as an expectation: to get ahead and escape.**

**سَآءَ مَا يَحْكُمُونَ** — "how ill they judge." **The same expression was treated at `045-casiye.md` 45/21**, and it was recorded there: **what the other side does is named a *judgement*.** I rely on that treatment.

**وَمَن جَٰهَدَ فَإِنَّمَا يُجَٰهِدُ لِنَفْسِهِ** — **the sentence that forms a ring with the sixty-ninth verse of the sūra;** it was tabulated in that section.

## 29/8 — وَوَصَّيْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ حُسْنًا وَإِن جَٰهَدَاكَ لِتُشْرِكَ بِى مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِۦ عِلْمٌ فَلَا تُطِعْهُمَآ

"**We enjoined upon the human being kindness towards his parents.** But if they press you to associate with Me something of which you have no knowledge, **do not obey them.**"

### The third ring

**This counsel occurs for the third time in the series, and the three verses can be set side by side:**

| Place | The counsel | Description of hardship | Note on compulsion |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/15 | *Bi-wālidayhi **iḥsānā*** | *Kurhan … kurhā* | — |
| Luqmān 31/14-15 | *Bi-wālidayh* | *Wahnan ʿalā wahn* | **Present** — *wa ṣāḥibhumā fi'd-dunyā maʿrūfā* |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/8** | *Bi-wālidayhi **ḥusnā*** | **Absent** | **Present** — with no continuation |

**The three verses establish the same ruling, but each opens a different side of it.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

**And the difference in al-ʿAnkabūt must be recorded:** here there is **no description of hardship** and **no note saying "keep good company with them" either.** The verse comes in its barest form: the counsel + the exception.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the subject of the sūra:** al-ʿAnkabūt is a sūra of **trial**. **The pressure exerted by parents occurs as one of the forms of trial enumerated in the first block** — indeed *fitna* had been under discussion in the preceding verses. In Luqmān the same verse was part of **a list of counsels**; there the emphasis fell on keeping the relationship going.

## 29/9 — وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ لَنُدْخِلَنَّهُمْ فِى ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ

"**Those who believe and do righteous deeds — We shall surely admit them among the righteous.**"

**The construction of the sentence is worth recording:** the verse does not say "We shall put them into the Garden" — it says ***fi'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*, that is, We shall admit them *into a company***.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the preposition (*fī*):** the recompense is given not as a place but as **a belonging**. **And this must be read together with the preceding verse of the sūra:** there it was said that parents are not to be obeyed where they exert such pressure — **that is, where one bond breaks, admission into another bond is announced.**

**The same root occurs twice: *ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāt* (the deed) and *al-ṣāliḥīn* (the persons).** **The work done and the company joined are named by the same word.**

---

## 29/10-11 — وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ فَإِذَآ أُوذِىَ فِى ٱللَّهِ جَعَلَ فِتْنَةَ ٱلنَّاسِ كَعَذَابِ ٱللَّهِ

"There are people who say, '**We have believed in God**'; but when such a one is made to suffer for God's sake, **he counts the affliction people inflict as though it were the punishment of God.**"

### The finest diagnosis in the sūra

**An equation is set up in the sentence, and the two sides of the equation must be recorded:**

| Side | What |
|---|---|
| *Fitnata'n-nās* | **The affliction people inflict** |
| *Ka-ʿadhābi'llāh* | **The punishment of God** |

**And the verb is *jaʿala* — "he made, he counted".** That is, the person sets up this equation **himself.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle of comparison (*kāf*):** the verse diagnoses not the presence of fear but **a slippage of scale**. **A pressure that is temporary and limited is weighed as having the same weight as what is lasting and unlimited.** The person is not denying — **he is using the wrong balance.**

**فِتْنَة** — root **ف-ت-ن**: **to melt gold in fire in order to test its purity.** **The root was treated at `085-buruc.md` and at `002-bakara.md` 2/191.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The place of the root in this sūra is worth recording:** in the second verse it had occurred as *lā yuftanūn* (they will not be tested); **here the same root is used for the affliction inflicted by people.** **That is, trial does not remain an abstract concept — it is named as a concrete pressure.**

---

## 29/12-13 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱتَّبِعُوا۟ سَبِيلَنَا وَلْنَحْمِلْ خَطَٰيَٰكُمْ وَمَا هُم بِحَٰمِلِينَ مِنْ خَطَٰيَٰهُم مِّن شَىْءٍ

"Those who disbelieve said to those who believe: '**Follow our way, and let us carry your sins.**' **Yet they will carry nothing at all of their sins.**"

### The structure of the offer

**The offer is built not with a threat but with a guarantee:** *wa'l-naḥmil khaṭāyākum* — "let us carry your sins."

**And the verse refutes this with two sentences:**

| Sentence | What it says |
|---|---|
| *Wa mā hum bi-ḥāmilīna min khaṭāyāhum min shayʾ* | **They cannot carry them** |
| *Wa la-yaḥmilunna athqālahum wa athqālan maʿa athqālihim* (13) | **Their own loads increase** |

**So the offer is not merely empty — when it is accepted, it increases the load of the one who makes it.**

**And this is an application of the principle of the load treated across the series:** `053-necm.md` 53/38, `039-zumer.md` 39/7, `035-fatir.md` 35/18 and `031-lokman.md` 31/33. **The same principle in five places, in five different scenes.**

**The addition in Fāṭir is of use here:** there **even a near kinsman** could not carry the load. **Here there is someone who *offers* to carry it — and the invalidity of the offer is settled by the same principle.**

**And the second verse draws a distinction: *wa athqālan maʿa athqālihim* — "other loads along with their own loads."** **That is, the load of the one who leads astray increases; but the one who goes astray also goes on carrying his own.** The two sentences complete one another.

---

## 29/14 — وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا نُوحًا إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ فَلَبِثَ فِيهِمْ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ إِلَّا خَمْسِينَ عَامًا

"We certainly sent Noah to his people; **he remained among them a thousand years less fifty.**"

### The way the number is given

**The verse does not say "nine hundred and fifty years": *alfa sanatin illā khamsīna ʿāmā* — "a thousand years, fifty less".**

**This is a feature of style to which the lexicographers have drawn attention, and it is worth recording.** I give this as my own reading: **the large number is said first, the subtraction comes after.** The figure that stays in the ear is therefore **a thousand**. **That is, the expression is built so as to emphasise the length of the span.**

**And two different words are used: *sana* and *ʿām*.** The lexicographers record a difference of tendency between the uses of these two words in the Qurʾān; **I do not present it as a fixed rule.**

**فَأَخَذَهُمُ ٱلطُّوفَانُ وَهُمْ ظَٰلِمُونَ** — and the outcome of so long a span is given in a single sentence.

**I record this as an observation:** the sūra recounts a prophet's nine hundred and fifty years of effort **in a single verse** — and immediately afterwards (15) speaks of those who were saved. **Since the subject of the sūra is patience and trial, this span is set down as a measure.**

---

## 29/15-23 — After Noah, Abraham: "you are fabricating a lie"

*Wa Ibrāhīma idh qāla li-qawmihi'ʿbudu'llāha wa'ttaqūh · Innamā taʿbudūna min dūni'llāhi awthānan wa takhluqūna ifkā · Inna'lladhīna taʿbudūna min dūni'llāhi lā yamlikūna lakum rizqan fa'btaghū ʿinda'llāhi'r-rizqa wa'ʿbudūhu wa'shkurū lah*

"Abraham too said to his people: '**Serve God and guard yourselves against Him**… You serve idols apart from God, and **you are fabricating a lie.** Those you serve apart from God **have no power to provide for you.** Seek provision with God, serve Him and give thanks to Him.'"

### وَتَخْلُقُونَ إِفْكًا

**The verb is worth recording: *takhluqūn* — root خ-ل-ق.**

**That the root carries the senses "to create" and "to fabricate" together is recorded by the lexicographers:** *ikhtilāq* — fabrication. **The shared core is the shaping of something and bringing it forth.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word order of the verse itself:** the people serve what they **cannot create**; but the verse says that they do **"create"** something — a fabricated lie. **That is, the verb is not taken out of their hands; its object is changed.**

**إِفْك** — root **أ-ف-ك**: to turn something from its direction. **The root was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/11 and 46/28.**

### فَٱبْتَغُوا۟ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرِّزْقَ

**The proof is built by way of provision** — and this will come round again in the sixtieth verse of the sūra (the creatures that cannot carry their own provision). **The link between the two verses can be verified from the text.**

**And the answer of Abraham's people will come in verse 24: *uqtulūhu aw ḥarriqūh*.** **That is, what is given in return for a proof is not a proof.**

**فَـَٔامَنَ لَهُۥ لُوطٌ** (26) — and at the end of this long narrative it is recorded that **a single person believed.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sūra gives the outcome as a number, and the number is one. **Since the subject of the sūra is patience and trial, this figure serves as a measure** — just as Noah's nine hundred and fifty years did the same work in the fourteenth verse.

---

## 29/24-25 — فَمَا كَانَ جَوَابَ قَوْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّآ أَن قَالُوا۟ ٱقْتُلُوهُ أَوْ حَرِّقُوهُ … وَقَالَ إِنَّمَا ٱتَّخَذْتُم مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ أَوْثَٰنًا مَّوَدَّةَ بَيْنِكُمْ فِى ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا

"The answer of his people was only this: '**Kill him, or burn him!**'… Abraham said: '**You have taken idols apart from God only as a bond of affection among yourselves in the life of this world.**'"

### مَّوَدَّةَ بَيْنِكُمْ — where the diagnosis lies

**Abraham's sentence diagnoses the worship of idols not as a matter of belief but as a matter of bond.**

**مَوَدَّة** — root **و-د-د**: affection, attachment. **The root was treated at `042-sura.md` 42/23.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sentence itself:** the verse names the **social function** of shared worship — a belonging that binds people to one another. **And the continuation of the sentence gives the term of that bond: *fi'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* — here only.**

**Indeed the next sentence announces what will become of the bond:** *thumma yawma'l-qiyāmati yakfuru baʿḍukum bi-baʿḍin wa yalʿanu baʿḍukum baʿḍā* — **rejecting and cursing one another.**

**The same reversal was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/6 and `043-zuhruf.md` 43/67** (*al-akhillāʾu yawmaʾidhin baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ʿaduww*). **Three sūras recount the same reversal.**

**ٱقْتُلُوهُ أَوْ حَرِّقُوهُ** — and the answer of the people must be recorded: **not a proof, but two methods of execution.**

**Pharaoh's taking the same course was treated at `040-gafir.md` 40/26** (*dharūnī aqtul Mūsā*) and it was recorded there: **what is done where no answer can be given in words.** I rely on that treatment.

## 29/26-40 — Lot, Shuʿayb and four forms of destruction

**This block names several communities one after another and closes by gathering them all into a single verse.**

### The narrative of Lot (28-35)

*Wa Lūṭan idh qāla li-qawmihī innakum la-taʾtūna'l-fāḥishata mā sabaqakum bihā min aḥadin mina'l-ʿālamīn*

**The side of the narrative treated here is the answer of the people:** *fa-mā kāna jawāba qawmihī illā an qālu'tinā bi-ʿadhābi'llāh* — **"bring us the punishment of God!"**

**I record this as an observation:** the same pattern (*fa-mā kāna jawāba qawmihī illā*) had been used for Abraham as well in the twenty-fourth verse. **In both places what is called an "answer" is not a proof:** one is a proposal of execution, the other a challenge. **The sūra records twice, with the same pattern, that the opposition is not made by way of proof.**

**وَتَأْتُونَ فِى نَادِيكُمُ ٱلْمُنكَرَ** — *nādī*: **a meeting place, an assembly.** The word announces that what is done is done **in the open.**

**And Abraham's exchange with the angels (31-32) is worth recording:** *inna fīhā Lūṭā · qālū naḥnu aʿlamu bi-man fīhā.* **An objection is raised, and the objection is not rejected — it is met with knowledge.**

### فَكُلًّا أَخَذْنَا بِذَنۢبِهِۦ (40)

**The fortieth verse gathers into a single sentence all the communities named in the block, and enumerates four forms of destruction:**

| Form | Expression | From where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Fa-minhum man arsalnā ʿalayhi ḥāṣibā* | **From the sky** — a wind hurling stones |
| 2 | *Wa minhum man akhadhathu'ṣ-ṣayḥa* | **By sound** |
| 3 | *Wa minhum man khasafnā bihi'l-arḍ* | **From the earth** — being swallowed into the ground |
| 4 | *Wa minhum man aghraqnā* | **By water** — drowning |

**Four different directions: from above, by sound, from below, from the water.** This is a classification that can be verified from the text.

**حَاصِب** — root **ح-ص-ب**: to hurl pebbles. **The root was treated at `054-kamer.md` 54/34 and at `067-mulk.md`.**

**وَمَا كَانَ ٱللَّهُ لِيَظْلِمَهُمْ وَلَٰكِن كَانُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ** — and the block closes with a sentence that puts responsibility in its place.

**The word order must be recorded:** *kānū **anfusahum** yaẓlimūn* — **the object (*anfusahum*) comes before the verb.** In Arabic this fronting conveys emphasis: **the ones wronged are themselves.**

## 29/41 — مَثَلُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ أَوْلِيَآءَ كَمَثَلِ ٱلْعَنكَبُوتِ ٱتَّخَذَتْ بَيْتًا وَإِنَّ أَوْهَنَ ٱلْبُيُوتِ لَبَيْتُ ٱلْعَنكَبُوتِ

"**The likeness of those who take protectors apart from God is the likeness of the spider that takes for itself a house. And the frailest of houses is surely the house of the spider.**"

### The simile taken apart

**This is the verse that gives the sūra its name, and the simile carries several layers.**

**First — the verb is shared:** *ittakhadhū* (they took) and *ittakhadhat* (it took). **The same verb on both sides.**

**Second — the object is shared: *bayt* (house).** A house is the place where a person **takes shelter, where he is protected.**

**Third — the verdict: *awhanu'l-buyūt* — the frailest of houses.**

The root **و-ه-ن**: **the loosening of strength, the ebbing of capacity.** **The root was treated at `031-lokman.md` 31/14 (*wahnan ʿalā wahn* — the mother's hardship in carrying) and at `047-muhammed.md` 47/35.**

| Place | *Wahn* |
|---|---|
| Luqmān 31/14 | **The weakness of the mother** — while carrying |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/41** | **The weakness of the house** — the thing sheltered in |

**The same root: one the weakness of what carries, the other the weakness of what is sheltered in.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

### Where the simile does its work

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of words:** a spider's web **is not without function** — it serves the spider very well; it holds its prey there. **What the verse says is that the web is frail *as a house*.** That is, against wind, against rain, against a movement of the hand.

**So the simile says not that the protectors taken serve no purpose at all, but that they cannot be a shelter.** The word in the sentence is *bayt*, not *ṣayd* (prey).

**وَتِلْكَ ٱلْأَمْثَٰلُ نَضْرِبُهَا لِلنَّاسِ وَمَا يَعْقِلُهَآ إِلَّا ٱلْعَٰلِمُونَ** (43) — "**these likenesses We set forth for people; but none understand them except those who know.**"

**A similar sentence was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/28** (*innamā yakhsha'llāha min ʿibādihi'l-ʿulamāʾ*) and it was recorded there: **the sentence is placed as the conclusion of a list of observations.** **The same structure is here too: the simile is given, then the condition for understanding it is stated.**

## 29/42-44 — إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِهِۦ مِن شَىْءٍ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ · وَتِلْكَ ٱلْأَمْثَٰلُ نَضْرِبُهَا لِلنَّاسِ وَمَا يَعْقِلُهَآ إِلَّا ٱلْعَٰلِمُونَ · خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ بِٱلْحَقِّ

"God knows whatever they call upon apart from Him; He is the Mighty, the Wise. **These likenesses We set forth for people; but none understand them except those who know.** God created the heavens and the earth **with truth**."

### The likeness and the condition for understanding

**The forty-third verse comes immediately after the spider simile and lays down a condition:** *wa mā yaʿqiluhā illa'l-ʿālimūn.*

**The same structure was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/28** (*innamā yakhsha'llāha min ʿibādihi'l-ʿulamāʾ*) and it was recorded there: **the sentence is placed as the conclusion of a list of observations.** I rely on that treatment.

**The two verses can be compared:**

| Place | Condition | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/28 | *Al-ʿulamāʾ* | *Yakhshā* — **stands in awe** |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/43** | *Al-ʿālimūn* | *Yaʿqilu* — **understands** |

**The same attribute is tied to two different verbs: one fear, the other understanding.** I record this as an observation.

**And the word *amthāl* (likenesses) is worth recording:** the verse says that the **understanding** of a simile does not come of itself. **That is, giving an example does not guarantee that it will be understood** — indeed a spider's web is something everyone sees.

**بِٱلْحَقِّ** — **this note was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/3, `045-casiye.md` 45/22 and `038-sad.md`.** **The announcement that creation is not in vain joins with the sūra's axis of trial: trial is not a purposeless obligation.**

## 29/45 — ٱتْلُ مَآ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْكَ مِنَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ إِنَّ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ تَنْهَىٰ عَنِ ٱلْفَحْشَآءِ وَٱلْمُنكَرِ وَلَذِكْرُ ٱللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ

"Recite what has been revealed to you of the Book, and perform the prayer. **The prayer surely restrains from indecency and from wrong. And the remembrance of God is greater still.**"

### إِنَّ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ تَنْهَىٰ

**The subject of the sentence is *al-ṣalāt*: the prayer itself performs an act — *tanhā* (it restrains).**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the verse does not say "the one who prays keeps clear of wrong." **It gives the act to the prayer.**

**And at `031-lokman.md` 31/17 the same two words had occurred as a command:** *aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta wa'mur bi'l-maʿrūfi wa'nha ʿani'l-munkar* — **there the *nahy* was the human being's work.**

| Place | The subject of the verb *nahy* |
|---|---|
| Luqmān 31/17 | **The human being** — warn against wrong |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/45** | **The prayer** — restrains from wrong |

**The same root, two different subjects.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text, and I record it as my own reading in this way: **one is a restraining directed outwards, the other a restraining that works upon the person himself.**

**وَلَذِكْرُ ٱللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ** — "the remembrance of God is greater."

**The lexicographers differ over the meaning of the construction, and the disagreement is not concealed:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The remembrance of God** is **greater** than the restraining function of the prayer |
| 2 | **God's remembrance of the servant** is greater than the servant's remembrance of Him |
| 3 | The remembrance within the prayer is greater than the other aspects of the prayer |

**All three readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

## 29/46 — وَلَا تُجَٰدِلُوٓا۟ أَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ إِلَّا بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ مِنْهُمْ وَقُولُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا بِٱلَّذِىٓ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْنَا وَأُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ وَإِلَٰهُنَا وَإِلَٰهُكُمْ وَٰحِدٌ وَنَحْنُ لَهُۥ مُسْلِمُونَ

"**Do not dispute with the People of the Book except in the way that is best** — save those of them who do wrong. And say: '**We believe in what has been sent down to us and in what has been sent down to you; our God and your God is one, and to Him we have surrendered.**'"

### The manner of the dispute

**The command is built not as a prohibition but as a condition of manner:** *illā bi'llatī hiya aḥsan* — **"except in the way that is best."** **That is, disputing is not forbidden; its method is fixed.**

**And this construction had been treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/34:** *idfaʿ bi'llatī hiya aḥsan* — "repel with what is best." **What was recorded there can be carried over here: the act of repelling stands, the instrument changes.**

| Place | Command | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/34 | *Idfaʿ bi'llatī hiya aḥsan* | **Responding to evil** |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/46** | *Lā tujādilū … illā bi'llatī hiya aḥsan* | **Dispute** |

**The same construction in two different domains.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**And that the whole backbone of `040-gafir.md` is *jidāl* was tabulated there** — what was condemned there was **groundless** disputing (*bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum*). **Here too it is not the dispute but the manner of the dispute that is at issue. The two sūras meet at the same point.**

### The sentence one is commanded to say

**The verse itself supplies the words to be said in the dispute, and the construction of the sentence is worth recording:**

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| *Āmannā bi'lladhī unzila ilaynā **wa unzila ilaykum*** | **Common ground** — the two revelations are named together |
| *Wa ilāhunā wa ilāhukum **wāḥid*** | **A common addressee** |
| *Wa naḥnu lahu muslimūn* | **The declaring of one's own position** |

**All three of the sentences begin with what is shared and end with one's own position.** I record this as my own reading: **the utterance is built not by rejecting the other side but by enumerating what is common** — and the divergence is stated only at the end, and not as a claim but as **a definition**.

**A note per the method (`USLUP.md`):** the exception *illa'lladhīna ẓalamū minhum* issues no blanket ruling about a community — **what is excepted are those characterised as *doing wrong*.** The particle *minhum* (of them) shows this as well: **not the whole of the group, but a part of it.** **The same care was preserved at `049-hucurat.md` and `043-zuhruf.md`; it is preserved here too.**

## 29/47 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَنزَلْنَآ إِلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ فَٱلَّذِينَ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُمُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهِۦ وَمِنْ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ مَن يُؤْمِنُ بِهِۦ وَمَا يَجْحَدُ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَآ إِلَّا ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ

"Thus have We sent down the Book to you as well. **Among those to whom We gave the Book there are those who believe in it; and among these too there are those who believe in it.**"

**The word order of the verse must be recorded: both groups are named with the same verb** — *yuʾminūna bih* and *man yuʾminu bih*.

**And the particle *min* occurs explicitly with the second group: *wa min hāʾulāʾi man yuʾminu bih* — "and among these too there are those who believe."**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse issues no blanket ruling about either of the two communities. **The note on *illa'lladhīna ẓalamū minhum* at `029-ankebut.md` 29/46 came from the same place.** **The sūra draws its distinction not between groups but between attitudes** — and this is the line the method (`USLUP.md`) preserves.

**يَجْحَدُ** — root **ج-ح-د**: to deny while knowing. **The root was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/26**, and there the connection the lexicographers record with "ground being barren" (*arḍun jaḥād*) was given. **I do not repeat it here.**

## 29/48 — وَمَا كُنتَ تَتْلُوا۟ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ مِن كِتَٰبٍ وَلَا تَخُطُّهُۥ بِيَمِينِكَ إِذًا لَّٱرْتَابَ ٱلْمُبْطِلُونَ

"**You were not reciting any book before this, nor were you writing one with your right hand.** Had it been so, **those bent on falsehood** would surely have fallen into doubt."

**The verse builds a proof, and the ground of the proof is an absence:** neither reading nor writing.

**تَخُطُّهُ بِيَمِينِك** — *khaṭṭ*: **to draw a line, to write.** And the note *bi-yamīnik* (with your right hand) gives the concrete picture of the act of writing.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not leave the claim to an abstract debate about knowledge — **it makes a proof of a life that passed before the eyes of the one addressed.** Indeed the validity of the proof rests on the existence of those who knew that life.

**إِذًا لَّٱرْتَابَ ٱلْمُبْطِلُون** — *irtiyāb* (to fall into doubt) and *mubṭilūn* (those bent on falsehood).

**The conditional structure of the sentence must be recorded:** *idhan* — "had it been so". **That is, the verse says that no ground exists that could have come into the other side's hands.**

## 29/49-55 — بَلْ هُوَ ءَايَٰتٌۢ بَيِّنَٰتٌ فِى صُدُورِ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ … أَوَلَمْ يَكْفِهِمْ أَنَّآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ

"**No; rather it is clear signs in the breasts of those who have been given knowledge**… **Is it not enough for them that We have sent down to you the Book that is recited to them?**"

### Where the Book is kept

**The verse says where the Book stands: *fī ṣudūri'lladhīna ūtu'l-ʿilm* — in breasts.**

**I record this as an observation:** in the preceding verse (48) it was said that the acts of writing and reading were absent. **This verse specifies the place in which the text is carried** — and the place specified is not a writing surface but **memory.**

**أَوَلَمْ يَكْفِهِمْ** — "is it not enough for them?" **The question comes after a demand for a miracle** (50: *law lā unzila ʿalayhi āyātun min rabbih*).

**And the construction of the answer is worth recording:** the demand is not refused; **what answers it is pointed out:** a book that is recited is already there.

**This comes from the same place as the line of proof treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/4 and at `031-lokman.md` 31/10:** **the proof demanded is compared with the one already in hand.**

**وَيَسْتَعْجِلُونَكَ بِٱلْعَذَابِ وَلَوْلَآ أَجَلٌ مُّسَمًّى لَّجَآءَهُمُ ٱلْعَذَابُ** (53) — **demanding haste.**

**The construction *ajalun musammā* was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/3, `039-zumer.md` 39/42, `035-fatir.md` 35/45 and `031-lokman.md` 31/29.** **The same thing is said in all five places, and here a further function of it appears: the reason for the delay.**

**وَإِنَّ جَهَنَّمَ لَمُحِيطَةٌۢ بِٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ** (54) — *muḥīṭa* (encompassing). **The root ح-و-ط was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/255 and at `041-fussilet.md` 41/54.**

## 29/56-57 — يَٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِنَّ أَرْضِى وَٰسِعَةٌ فَإِيَّٰىَ فَٱعْبُدُونِ · كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَآئِقَةُ ٱلْمَوْتِ

"**O My servants who believe! My earth is wide; so serve Me, and Me alone.** Every soul shall taste death."

### The sentence shared with al-Zumar

**Almost the same expression had occurred at `039-zumer.md` 39/10:** *wa arḍu'llāhi wāsiʿa* — "God's earth is wide." **There it was announced that those who are patient would receive their recompense without reckoning.**

| Place | Expression | Context |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/10 | *Wa **arḍu'llāhi** wāsiʿa* | **Patience** — recompense without reckoning |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/56** | *Inna **arḍī** wāsiʿa* | **Trial** — the command to serve |

**The difference must be recorded: in al-Zumar "God's earth", here "My earth" — the first person.** **And the address is first person too: *yā ʿibādī* — "O My servants."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pronouns:** the first block of the sūra described the person left under pressure (10-11: counting the affliction of people as though it were the punishment of God). **This verse announces a way out from under that pressure — and it does so with the closest form of address.**

**فَإِيَّٰىَ فَٱعْبُدُون** — **the object is fronted** (*iyyāya*), and in Arabic this conveys **restriction**: **not to another.** **The same fronting was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/66** (*bali'llāha fa'ʿbud*).

**كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَآئِقَةُ ٱلْمَوْت** — and immediately afterwards the inevitability of death is recalled.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** read together, the two verses set down, immediately after the call to migrate, the note that **one will die in any case**. **That is, changing one's place is not escaping death — it is choosing in what state one dies.**

## 29/58-59 — وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ لَنُبَوِّئَنَّهُم مِّنَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ غُرَفًا تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا نِعْمَ أَجْرُ ٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ · ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ

"Those who believe and do righteous deeds — We shall settle them in **chambers of the Garden beneath which rivers flow**… **How excellent is the wage of those who work! They are those who are patient and who rely upon their Lord.**"

**لَنُبَوِّئَنَّهُم** — root **ب-و-أ**: **to settle in a place, to give lodging.** The word conveys **the preparing of a place to dwell in.**

**غُرَف** — chambers. **The same word was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/20** (*ghurafun min fawqihā ghurafun mabniyya*) and it was recorded there: **the recompense is described by a structure of storey upon storey.** I rely on that treatment.

**And the place of the verse is worth recording:** in the fifty-sixth verse the call to migrate had been made (*inna arḍī wāsiʿa*). **This verse promises a dwelling to the one who leaves his own.** I give this as my own reading; its ground is the order of the verses.

**ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ** — two attributes: **patience** and **reliance.**

**And the sequence must be recorded:** *ṣabarū* (perfect — done in the past) and *yatawakkalūn* (imperfect — ongoing). **That is, one is a completed endurance, the other a continuing trust.**

## 29/60 — وَكَأَيِّن مِّن دَآبَّةٍ لَّا تَحْمِلُ رِزْقَهَا ٱللَّهُ يَرْزُقُهَا وَإِيَّاكُمْ

"**How many a creature there is that cannot carry its own provision; God provides for it and for you.**"

**لَّا تَحْمِلُ رِزْقَهَا** — "does not carry its provision". **The lexicographers explain this in two ways: it cannot store up its food and carry it with it, or it has not the strength to secure its provision.** **I transmit both explanations.**

**The verse's link with the preceding verses is worth recording:** in verse 56 migration had been commanded. **The most concrete obstacle to migration is anxiety about livelihood** — and this verse is placed exactly there.

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the order of the verses:** the proof is brought not from the human being but **from the animal**. That is, by saying that a creature which cannot carry its own load is fed as well, it is shown **that storing up provisions is not a condition.**

**And at `002-bakara.md` 2/197** (*wa tazawwadū fa-inna khayra'z-zādi't-taqwā*) **it was treated how setting out without provisions was done away with.** **The two verses do not contradict: one commands the precaution, the other says that the precaution does not underwrite the outcome.** I set this down as a note.

## 29/61-63 — وَلَئِن سَأَلْتَهُم مَّنْ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَسَخَّرَ ٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ لَيَقُولُنَّ ٱللَّهُ فَأَنَّىٰ يُؤْفَكُونَ

"**If you were to ask them, 'who created the heavens and the earth, and made the sun and the moon subservient?', they would surely say, 'God'. How then are they turned away?**"

**The same structure was treated at `031-lokman.md` 31/25 and at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/9:** **the other side being brought into contradiction by its own answer.** I rely on that treatment.

**The difference here must be recorded: the question is asked twice in this sūra** — in verse 61 about creation, in verse 63 about rain. **And in both the answer is the same: *la-yaqūlunna'llāh*.**

**فَأَنَّىٰ يُؤْفَكُون** — root **أ-ف-ك**: to turn from its direction. **The root had occurred in the seventeenth verse of this sūra as well** (*wa takhluqūna ifkā*).

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | *Takhluqūna **ifkā*** | **They** — they fabricate a lie |
| 61 | *Fa-annā **yuʾfakūn*** | **They** — they are turned away |

**The same root, one active and one passive.** I record this as an observation: **what they fabricate becomes, in the end, the thing that turns them.**

**قُلِ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْقِلُونَ** — and the section joins with the condition in the forty-third verse: *wa mā yaʿqiluhā illa'l-ʿālimūn.* **The same root (ع-ق-ل), one as a condition and one as a diagnosis.**

## 29/64 — وَمَا هَٰذِهِ ٱلْحَيَوٰةُ ٱلدُّنْيَآ إِلَّا لَهْوٌ وَلَعِبٌ وَإِنَّ ٱلدَّارَ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةَ لَهِىَ ٱلْحَيَوَانُ لَوْ كَانُوا۟ يَعْلَمُونَ

"**This life of the world is only a diversion and a game. The abode of the hereafter — that is the real life** — if only they knew."

### ٱلْحَيَوَان

**The word is worth recording: *al-ḥayawān*, root ح-ي-ي.**

**The lexicographers record that this pattern conveys intensification and completeness: "life itself, the fullest state of being alive".** The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.

**And the word order must be recorded:** *wa inna'd-dāra'l-ākhirata **la-hiya** al-ḥayawān* — **emphasis by means of the pronoun of separation (*hiya*).** That is, not "there is life in the abode of the hereafter" but: **"that is the real life."**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence is not making a comparison — **it is laying the definition down anew.** The life of this world is named with the word **life** (*al-ḥayātu'd-dunyā*), but the real life is given to another word.

**لَهْوٌ وَلَعِبٌ** — **the root ل-ه-و was treated at `031-lokman.md` 31/6** (*lahwa'l-ḥadīth*) **and at `057-hadid.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

---

## 29/65 — فَإِذَا رَكِبُوا۟ فِى ٱلْفُلْكِ دَعَوُا۟ ٱللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ ٱلدِّينَ فَلَمَّا نَجَّىٰهُمْ إِلَى ٱلْبَرِّ إِذَا هُمْ يُشْرِكُونَ

"**When they board the ship they call upon God, making their religion sincerely His. But when He brings them safe to land, at once they associate others with Him.**"

### The comparison with Luqmān

**The same scene occurred at `031-lokman.md` 31/32 in almost the same words** and was treated there. **The two verses can be set side by side, and the difference lies in a single word:**

| | Luqmān 31/32 | **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/65** |
|---|---|---|
| The danger | *Ghashiyahum mawjun ka'ẓ-ẓulal* | *Rakibū fi'l-fulk* |
| The prayer | *Daʿawu'llāha **mukhliṣīna lahu'd-dīn*** | *Daʿawu'llāha **mukhliṣīna lahu'd-dīn*** |
| The rescue | *Fa-lammā najjāhum ila'l-barr* | *Fa-lammā najjāhum ila'l-barr* |
| **The outcome** | *Fa-**minhum muqtaṣid*** | *Idhā hum **yushrikūn*** |

**Three sentences the same, the fourth different.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text, and **it shows that the two sūras end the same scene with two different outcomes.**

**I record this as my own reading:** in Luqmān the outcome was given in **measured** language (*some of them keep to the middle way*) and what the rest did was not said. **In al-ʿAnkabūt that gap is filled.** Read together, the two verses complete the picture.

**And the particle *idhā* must be recorded: *idhā hum yushrikūn* — in Arabic this particle announces what happens all at once.** **That is, the turning is not a process; it is something that comes with setting foot on land.**

## 29/66-68 — لِيَكْفُرُوا۟ بِمَآ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُمْ وَلِيَتَمَتَّعُوا۟ فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ · أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ أَنَّا جَعَلْنَا حَرَمًا ءَامِنًا وَيُتَخَطَّفُ ٱلنَّاسُ مِنْ حَوْلِهِمْ

"**Let them be ungrateful for what We have given them, and let them enjoy themselves; they will soon know.** **Have they not seen that We have made a secure sanctuary, while people are snatched away all around them?**"

**يُتَخَطَّفُ** — root **خ-ط-ف**: **to snatch and carry off swiftly.** The verb announces a seizing that happens all at once, and it comes in **the passive**: the one who snatches is not named.

**The construction of the proof is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the verse shows the one addressed **his own safety** as the proof. **And the comparison is made with the surroundings: what is happening in the same region, in the same age, right beside them.** That is, the proof is taken neither from a distant history nor from an abstract chain of reasoning.

**This shares the same method with the sixtieth verse of the sūra:** there the creatures that cannot carry their own provision were made the proof. **Both verses look at the nearest fact.**

**And the context of the verse joins with the first block of the sūra:** in the tenth verse the person who counts **the affliction people inflict** as though it were the punishment of God was described. **This verse reminds that same addressee that he is being protected.** I record this as an observation.

**أَفَبِٱلْبَٰطِلِ يُؤْمِنُونَ وَبِنِعْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ يَكْفُرُونَ** — and two verbs are set against each other: **belief in falsehood, ingratitude for the favour.**

**The word order must be recorded: in both sentences the object is fronted** (*bi'l-bāṭili*, *bi-niʿmati'llāh*). **In Arabic this fronting conveys emphasis and sharpens the contrast.**

## 29/69 — وَٱلَّذِينَ جَٰهَدُوا۟ فِينَا لَنَهْدِيَنَّهُمْ سُبُلَنَا وَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَمَعَ ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ

"**Those who strive for Our sake — We shall surely guide them to Our ways.** God is surely with those who do good."

### The closing of the sūra and the return to the sixth verse

**The root ج-ه-د had occurred in the sixth verse of this sūra:** *wa man jāhada fa-innamā yujāhidu li-nafsih* — **"whoever strives, strives only for himself."**

| Verse | Expression | What it announces |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | *Man jāhada fa-innamā yujāhidu li-nafsih* | **To whom the striving belongs** |
| **69** | *Alladhīna jāhadū fīnā la-nahdiyannahum subulanā* | **The recompense of the striving** |

**The sūra closes with the same root the matter it opened with.** This is a ring that can be verified from the text.

The root **ج-ه-د**: **to expend strength, to go to the end of one's capacity.** *Jahd* — strength, capacity. **The root occurred at `049-hucurat.md`, `047-muhammed.md` and `060-mumtehine.md`.**

**سُبُلَنَا** — **plural: "Our ways".** The verse does not say the singular *sabīl*.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the plural form of the word:** guidance is named not as a single route but as **more than one way**. **And the condition is not finding the way — it is the striving itself.** The sequence is worth recording: **first the striving, then the guiding.**

**وَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَمَعَ ٱلْمُحْسِنِين** — and the sūra closes with an answer to the question of trial in the first block: **the one who is tested is not left alone.**

**In the second verse the question had been asked: *a-ḥasiba'n-nāsu an yutrakū* — "did they suppose they would be left alone?"** **The sixty-ninth verse gives the opposite of that same verb: *la-maʿa'l-muḥsinīn* — He is with them.** **The sūra opens with "you will not be left" and closes with "you are not alone."**

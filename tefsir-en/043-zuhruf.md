# 43. Sūrat al-Zukhruf

**89 verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from a single word that occurs in verse 35: **زُخْرُف** — *zukhruf*, that is, **gold ornament, gilding.**

The sūra is one of the seven that open with *ḥā-mīm*. What this group has in common is that the opening is followed immediately by mention of **the Book**; al-Zukhruf opens the same way: letters, then an oath by the Book, then the quality of the Book.

## The single question of the sūra: the measure

What al-Zukhruf works on from beginning to end is the question of **measure** — and it appears in three distinct questions:

- **What is counted as valuable?** (the golden ceiling, the silver stairway, *zukhruf*)
- **Who is seen as great?** (*rajulin mina'l-qaryatayni ʿaẓīm*, *a-laysa lī mulku Miṣra*)
- **Which proof is counted as valid?** (*innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā umma*, *fa-lawlā ulqiya ʿalayhi aswiratun min dhahab*)

**All three questions share the same structure:** the other side has already set up a scale; the dispute is not over whether the scale is true but over the weight of what is placed on it. The sūra is disputing the scale itself.

And the sūra works this question not through an abstract principle but through **scenes that complete one another**:

| Scene | What is put forward as the measure | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The announcement of a daughter | Sex — what a child will be good for | 17-18 |
| The proof from the forefathers | Inheritance — what earlier generations did | 22-24 |
| "The great man of the two towns" | Wealth and rank | 31-32 |
| The demand for gold bracelets | Visible splendour | 53 |
| Pharaoh's rivers | Dominion and vista | 51 |
| Golden platters (in the Garden) | The same material, in another place | 71 |

**The last row is worth recording and is the most striking choice the sūra makes:** what is rejected is not gold itself. In the thirty-third verse a silver ceiling is named as a **humiliation**; in the seventy-first, a golden platter as a **reward**. The sūra is not disputing the material but where the material is placed.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-8 | The Book, language, *ṣafḥ*, the end of earlier peoples | *wa maḍā mathalu'l-awwalīn* (8) |
| **II** | 9-14 | Admission and contradiction; the earth, water, the mount; the prayer of the rider | *wa innā ilā rabbinā la-munqalibūn* (14) |
| **III** | 15-25 | Assigning a part to God, the reaction to a daughter, the proof from the forefathers | *ʿāqibatu'l-mukadhdhibīn* (25) |
| **IV** | 26-30 | Abraham's disavowal and the abiding word | *hādhā siḥrun wa innā bihi kāfirūn* (30) |
| **V** | 31-35 | **The measure of value** — the great man of the two towns, the division of provision, *zukhruf* | *wa'l-ākhiratu ʿinda rabbika li'l-muttaqīn* (35) |
| **VI** | 36-45 | Turning away from the reminder, the *qarīn*, the command to hold fast | *ālihatan yuʿbadūn* (45) |
| **VII** | 46-56 | Moses and Pharaoh — the gold bracelets and making light of a man | *salafan wa mathalan li'l-ākhirīn* (56) |
| **VIII** | 57-65 | The likeness of the son of Mary, being a servant, disagreement | *ʿadhābi yawmin alīm* (65) |
| **IX** | 66-73 | The Hour, friendships turning, entry into the Garden | *minhā taʾkulūn* (73) |
| **X** | 74-80 | Hell, the call to Mālik, secret speech | *wa rusulunā ladayhim yaktubūn* (80) |
| **XI** | 81-89 | The conditional sentence, glorification, the close — *faṣfaḥ ʿanhum* | *fa-sawfa yaʿlamūn* (89) |

**The boundaries of the blocks are drawn by the sūra's own repetitions.** The most visible is this: **the verb refused in the fifth verse is commanded in the eighty-ninth.** The same root, the same word, the opposite ruling — and eighty-four verses in between. I shall set this ring out in a table again at the end of the sūra.

### Rings within the sūra

What follows are repetitions that can be verified from the text; what is interpretation is the claim that they build an order, and I offer that as an observation.

| Root / word | First occurrence | Its return |
|---|---|---|
| **ص-ف-ح** | *naḍribu ʿankumu'dh-dhikra **ṣafḥan*** (5) | *fa'**ṣfaḥ** ʿanhum* (89) |
| **ض-ر-ب** | *naḍribu ʿankumu'dh-dhikr* (5) | *bimā **ḍaraba** li'r-Raḥmāni mathalan* (17), *lammā **ḍuriba**'bnu Maryama mathalan* (57) |
| **م-ث-ل** | *wa maḍā **mathalu**'l-awwalīn* (8) | *wa **mathalan** li'l-ākhirīn* (56) |
| **أ-م-م** | ***ummi**'l-kitāb* (4) | *ʿalā **ummatin*** (22, 23), ***ummatan** wāḥida* (33) |
| **ه-د-ي** | *laʿallakum **tahtadūn*** (10) | *ʿalā āthārihim **muhtadūn*** (22), *innanā **la-muhtadūn*** (49) |
| **ذ-ه-ب** | *aswiratun min **dhahab*** (53) | *bi-ṣiḥāfin min **dhahab*** (71) |
| **ق-ر-ن** | *wa mā kunnā lahu **muqrinīn*** (13) | *al-malāʾikatu **muqtarinīn*** (53); and also *fa-huwa lahu **qarīn*** (36) |
| **ن-ق-م** | *fa'**ntaqamnā** minhum* (25) | *innā minhum **muntaqimūn*** (41), *i**ntaqamnā** minhum* (55) |
| **ص-د-د** | *la-**yaṣuddūnahum** ʿani's-sabīl* (37) | *minhu **yaṣiddūn*** (57), *wa-lā **yaṣuddannakumu**'sh-shayṭān* (62) |
| **س-ء-ل** | *wa **yusʾalūn*** (19) | *wa sawfa **tusʾalūn*** (44), *wa'**sʾal** man arsalnā* (45) |
| **ص-ر-ط مستقيم** | *innaka ʿalā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm* (43) | *hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* (61, 64) |

**The most striking of these is the root ض-ر-ب**, because within the sūra it is used for three distinct pieces of work:

| Verse | Expression | What is being done |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | *a-fa-naḍribu ʿankumu'dh-dhikra ṣafḥan* | Is the reminder **to be pushed aside** |
| 17 | *bimā ḍaraba li'r-Raḥmāni mathalan* | A likeness is **ascribed** to al-Raḥmān |
| 57 | *wa lammā ḍuriba'bnu Maryama mathalan* | The son of Mary is **put forward as an example** |
| 58 | *mā ḍarabūhu laka illā jadalan* | The example is **thrown out for the sake of argument** |

The same root: pushing the reminder aside, ascribing a likeness to God, throwing an example into a dispute. **I record this as an observation about density; I do not claim that the sūra is constructing a deliberate play on words.**

### On the occasion of revelation

On the sūra's being Meccan there is something close to agreement. For certain verses (particularly 57-58 and 45) specific accounts of the occasion of revelation are transmitted in the classical commentaries; **I shall give these in their places in the language of "it is transmitted", and I shall present none of them as firm historical information.**

The basmala was treated in earlier sections; the verses are numbered without it.

## 43/1-4 — حمٓ · وَٱلْكِتَٰبِ ٱلْمُبِينِ · إِنَّا جَعَلْنَٰهُ قُرْءَٰنًا عَرَبِيًّا لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ · وَإِنَّهُۥ فِىٓ أُمِّ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ لَدَيْنَا لَعَلِىٌّ حَكِيمٌ

*Ḥā-mīm · Wa'l-kitābi'l-mubīn · Innā jaʿalnāhu qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan laʿallakum taʿqilūn · Wa innahu fī ummi'l-kitābi ladaynā la-ʿaliyyun ḥakīm*

"Ḥā-mīm. By the clear Book. We have made it an Arabic Qurʾān, **so that you may reason**. And it is with Us in **the mother of the Book**; truly exalted, truly wise."

### حمٓ

**The general note on the disconnected letters has been entered elsewhere in the corpus** (`002-bakara.md`, `068-kalem.md`, `050-kaf.md`, `046-ahkaf.md`); I do not repeat it here. **Its summary is this: there is no certain knowledge about the meaning of these letters; many explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries and none of them claims certainty.** Per the method (`USLUP.md`), claims resting on letter-and-number calculation are not entered into.

**What can be recorded is a verifiable fact of arrangement:** in almost every sūra that opens with *ḥā-mīm*, **the Book** is named immediately after the opening. Here it comes twice over: the second verse swears by the Book, and the third says **how the Book was made**.

### وَٱلْكِتَٰبِ ٱلْمُبِينِ — an oath and an attribute

The root **ب-ي-ن** and the word *mubīn* have been treated in many places in the corpus (in detail in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/7-8, by way of the contrast between *bayyināt* and *mubīn*). The core of the root: **the opening of a gap between two things, an interval** — and from there, **to become distinguishable, to be clear.**

***Mubīn* is an active participle of Form IV, and in Arabic this pattern can be read either transitively or intransitively:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Intransitive | The Book that **is itself clear** |
| Transitive | The Book that **makes clear**, that brings to light |

**The lexicographers regard both as possible, and both sit well here.** I do not impose a preference; but I record this: **the third verse builds a chain of reasoning that supports the second reading** — the Book was made Arabic so that it might be reasoned out. Clarity is thus presented not as an ornament of the Book but as its **work**.

### إِنَّا جَعَلْنَٰهُ قُرْءَٰنًا عَرَبِيًّا — the choice of verb

**The sentence does not say "We sent it down". The verb is *jaʿala*: "he made, he set, he brought it about that it should be so".**

*Jaʿala* in Arabic conveys placing something from one state into another — this is what distinguishes it from *khalaqa* (to bring into being out of nothing). **The distinction is worth recording:** the verse speaks not of the Qurʾān's coming into being but of **the form in which it was given**. In the tenth verse the same verb will be used of the earth (*alladhī jaʿala lakumu'l-arḍa mahdan*), and in the twelfth of mounts (*wa jaʿala lakum mina'l-fulki wa'l-anʿāmi mā tarkabūn*).

**In all three the structure is the same: something already existing is put into a form a human being can use.** The ground is levelled, the animal becomes a mount, speech enters a language.

I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the repetition of the verb within the sūra: **the verse places the choice of a language inside an enumeration of favours.**

### عَرَبِيًّا — root: ع-ر-ب

**The root was analysed in `056-vakia.md` in the discussion of 56/37 (*ʿuruban*); I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.** What was recorded there as the core was this:

> *"The core meaning the lexicographers give for the root ع-ر-ب: **to express clearly and plainly, to set one's meaning out without concealing it.** *Aʿraba ʿani'sh-shayʾ* — he made a thing known openly. *Iʿrāb* — bringing out into the open (the grammatical term *iʿrāb* comes from this too: the word's **bringing out into the open** what it does in the sentence, by means of the vowel on its end)."*

**In al-Wāqiʿa this core was applied to an attribute of relationship (*ʿarūb* — one who does not conceal her state). Here the same core is applied to a text.**

**And what must be added here is this:** the word *ʿarabī* does not stand here merely as the name of a people. **The second verse described the Book as *mubīn* (clear); the third says the language is *ʿarabī*.** The two words come from the same field: one is **bayān**, the other **iʿrāb** — both are "bringing to light".

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the root meanings of the two words:** the verse says the same thing twice in two successive descriptions. The Book is clear; and it was given in the language that makes clear.

**The Qurʾān makes this description elsewhere too, and always ties it to the same ground:** Yūsuf 12/2 (*innā anzalnāhu qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan laʿallakum taʿqilūn* — the identical sentence), Ṭāhā 20/113, al-Zumar 39/28, Fuṣṣilat 41/3, al-Shūrā 42/7, al-Aḥqāf 46/12, al-Raʿd 13/37. **In al-Naḥl 16/103 the word occurs as an answer to an objection:** *lisānu'lladhī yulḥidūna ilayhi aʿjamiyyun wa hādhā lisānun ʿarabiyyun mubīn* — here *aʿjamī* is set as the opposite of *ʿarabī*.

The root **ع-ج-م** is the exact opposite of the root ع-ر-ب, and the concrete sense of the root shows it: **closedness, unintelligibility.** *ʿUjma* — a block in the tongue, a stammer. *ʿAjmāʾ* — an animal that has a voice but does not speak. **The contrast between the two roots is therefore not "Arab / non-Arab" but, at the level of the roots the lexicographers record, "clear / closed".**

**This distinction matters here, and per the method (`USLUP.md`) I must state it plainly:** the verse is not proclaiming the superiority of a language or of a people. **The ground the verse itself gives stands at the end of the verse** — and that ground looks to the one addressed, not to the language.

### لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ — the ground itself

**At the end of the sentence there is a *laʿallakum*, and this is the ground for the choice of language.**

The root **ع-ق-ل** has been treated in the corpus (`067-mulk.md`, `049-hucurat.md`, `059-hasr.md`, `089-fecr.md`). **The concrete sense of the root: to tie, to hold, not to let loose.** *ʿIqāl* — the cord bound around a camel's knee. In the picture the root gives, the intellect is what **holds a person, what keeps him from scattering.**

**Now the word order must be set out:**

| Element | What it is |
|---|---|
| *jaʿalnāhu* | The verb — putting into a state |
| *qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan* | What that state is — in a language that makes clear |
| *laʿallakum taʿqilūn* | **Why** |

**The verse thus ties the choice of language to a purpose, and the purpose is that the one addressed should understand.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the causal clause the verse builds for itself:** if the language was chosen so that the one addressed should understand, then **the value of the language lies in its intelligibility.** This stands in the same direction as the whole later argument of the sūra: throughout the sūra the other side will tie value to **appearance** (gold, rank, lineage); the sūra ties value to **function**. **For the Book too the measure is this — that it opens.**

**And one more note:** the same ground will be turned over in the eighteenth verse. There, the expression used by the society speaking about a daughter is this: *wa huwa fi'l-khiṣāmi ghayru mubīn* — "one who cannot bring her meaning to light in a dispute". **The sūra thus brings back before us, fifteen verses later and by way of a human being, the measure it gave to language in the third verse (clarity).** I shall treat this repetition in detail there.

### أُمِّ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ — root: أ-م-م

**The root has come up twice in the corpus** (`062-cuma.md` 62/2 — *ummī*; `075-kiyame.md` 75/12 — *amāmahu*). **In the discussion of al-Qiyāma the family of the root was given as follows; I rely on that:**

> *"Root: أ-م-م. And the family of this root is striking: *umm* (mother), *umma* (community), *imām* (the one who stands in front, who goes ahead), *amām* (the front). The shared idea: **priority, being in front, being at the source.**"*

**I add here one further aspect of the root, because it is that aspect which is at work in this verse.** The lexicographers describe the word *umm* not only as "mother" but as **"the origin of a thing, the source to which it is referred back, the trunk to which it is joined"**, and they record these usages:

- **أُمُّ الرَّأْس** — the membrane of the brain; the place counted as the origin of the head.
- **أُمُّ القُرَى** — the mother of towns (of Mecca; al-Anʿām 6/92, al-Shūrā 42/7).
- **أُمُّ الطَّرِيق** — the main road; the principal road from which the branches separate.

**In the picture the root gives, then, *umm* is not primarily "the one who gives birth" but *the one who is returned to*.** The branches part from it and are attached to it.

**The expression *ummu'l-kitāb* occurs in three places in the Qurʾān**, and in the three it may not carry the same sense:

| Place | Expression | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Zukhruf 43/4** | *fī ummi'l-kitābi ladaynā* | The source of the Qurʾān |
| **al-Raʿd 13/39** | *wa ʿindahu ummu'l-kitāb* | Erasing and confirming |
| **Āl ʿImrān 3/7** | *hunna ummu'l-kitāb* | The clear verses — what is foundational **within** the Book itself |

**The third is distinct from the others and must not be confused with them:** there the expression is used of a group of verses inside the Qurʾān. **I record this as a caution.**

**Whether this expression is the same thing as *al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ* has been debated in the classical commentaries.** A note on this question was entered in `085-buruc.md`, and I keep the same note here:

> *"There are expressions within the Qurʾān that can be related to one another — *kitābin maknūn* (al-Wāqiʿa 56/78), *ummu'l-kitāb* (al-Zukhruf 43/4; al-Raʿd 13/39), *kitābin ḥafīẓ* (Qāf 50/4) — but whether these expressions are identical with one another is itself disputed, and no ruling is issued here."*

**I keep the same limit here: I produce no description of the modality of this expression.**

**But the word order of the verse does say something, and that can be said:**

| Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| *fī ummi'l-kitāb* | **Where** — in the origin, at the source |
| *ladaynā* | **In whose keeping** — with Us |
| *la-ʿaliyyun* | **High** |
| *ḥakīm* | **In its right place, firm** |

**The order moves in this direction: position, then possession, then two attributes.**

The root **ع-ل-و** has been treated in many places in the corpus (`087-ala.md`, `079-naziat.md`, `088-gasiye.md`); its core is **height**. The root **ح-ك-م** has been treated as well (`002-bakara.md` at 2/269 — *ḥakama*, by way of its relation to the bridle; `046-ahkaf.md` 46/2). **Its core: to restrain and hold in place.**

**That the two attributes stand side by side is worth recording, and it can be compared with the opening of al-Aḥqāf:** there *ʿazīz* (mighty) and *ḥakīm* (rightly placed) stood side by side, and what was recorded there was this — "one conveys power, the other rightness of placement; power is not named on its own". **The same structure is here: *ʿaliyy* conveys height, *ḥakīm* conveys rightness of placement.**

**And these two attributes, read together with the third verse, produce a tension — I record it as my own reading:** the third verse says the Book came **down**, into the language of the one addressed; the fourth says the Book is **above**, in the origin. **The same thing is both sent down and high.** The whole argument of the sūra about measure will be built on this tension: **where a thing is located does not determine its value.**

## 43/5 — أَفَنَضْرِبُ عَنكُمُ ٱلذِّكْرَ صَفْحًا أَن كُنتُمْ قَوْمًا مُّسْرِفِينَ

*A-fa-naḍribu ʿankumu'dh-dhikra ṣafḥan an kuntum qawman musrifīn*

"Are We then **to turn the reminder away from you and set it aside**, because you are a people who overstep?"

### The sentence is a question, and the direction of the question must be recorded

**The question is one of denial:** its answer is "no" — it is asked in order to refuse. In Arabic this pattern, opening with *hamza + fāʾ*, attaches to the preceding sentence and says "after all this, are we to do such a thing?"

**What it attaches to is the third and fourth verses:** the Book was made clear, was put into a language, is high and wise in its origin — **is it then to be withdrawn?**

### صَفْح — root: ص-ف-ح

**This root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

**The concrete sense of the root is the name of a surface: *ṣafḥa* — the side of a thing, its flat face.**

- **صَفْحَة** — the side of the face, the cheek; the flat of a sword; the flat side of a thing.
- **صَفَحَ عَنْهُ** — he turned his face away from it; and from there: **he pardoned, he let it pass.**
- **صَفَّحَ** — he flattened it, made it into a sheet. *Ṣafīḥa* — a sheet, a plate.
- **مُصَافَحَة (muṣāfaḥa)** — shaking hands; **the touching of two palms, that is, of two flat surfaces, one to the other.** This is the source of the word *musafaha* in Turkish.
- **تَصَفَّحَ الكِتَاب** — he went through a book, turning its leaves one by one. The word **صَفْحَة** is also used for **one face** of a leaf. (Whether the Turkish word *sayfa*, "page", comes from this root or from the very close root **ص-ح-ف** — *ṣaḥīfa* — is recorded in different ways in the dictionaries; **I adopt no preference.**)

**The picture beneath the root is this: turning the flat face of a thing.**

**And from here two opposite meanings emerge, both from the same movement:**

| Derivative | The movement | The result |
|---|---|---|
| *ṣafḥan* (here) | Turning one's face away and **leaving** | Abandoning, pushing aside |
| *fa'ṣfaḥ ʿanhum* (89) | Turning one's face away and **passing on** | Pardoning, not dwelling on it |

**This is the most striking ring in the sūra, and I point to it already now:** the same root is **refused** in the fifth verse and **commanded** in the eighty-ninth. I shall give the difference in a table at verse 89.

### أَفَنَضْرِبُ … صَفْحًا — the structure of the idiom

**The verb is *ḍaraba*, and here it stands inside an idiom.** The lexicographers explain the construction thus: *ḍaraba ʿanhu* — to **put a thing away from someone, to turn it aside, to withdraw it.**

**Two readings are transmitted for the role of the word *ṣafḥan* in the sentence:**

| Reading | The role of *ṣafḥan* | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Absolute object** (cognate with the verb) | "To turn away with a turning" — to withdraw it altogether |
| 2 | **Circumstantial accusative** (*ḥāl*) | "As one turning away" — by giving up on it |

**Both come out at the same door, and I do not impose a preference.** What is to be recorded is that the verb chosen is *ḍaraba*: **the word carries a movement of the hand — pushing something aside with one's own hand.** This is the sūra's first *ḍaraba*; the root will then return in verses 17 and 57.

### ٱلذِّكْر — the reminder itself

The root **ذ-ك-ر** has been treated at length in the corpus (`050-kaf.md`, `087-ala.md`, `074-muddessir.md`). **Its core: to call to mind, to keep in memory; and to name with the tongue.**

**Here the word comes definite (*al-dhikr*)** and in context indicates the Qurʾān. **In the thirty-sixth verse the same word will return inside a construct:** *dhikri'r-Raḥmān* — the reminder of al-Raḥmān.

### أَن كُنتُمْ قَوْمًا مُّسْرِفِينَ — a variant reading

**Two readings are transmitted for this expression, and the difference touches the meaning:**

| Reading | Written form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *an kuntum* | أَن كُنتُمْ | **"because you are…"** — *an* conveying a ground |
| *in kuntum* | إِن كُنتُمْ | **"if you are…"** — *in* conveying a condition |

**Both are transmitted among the imams of the readings.** The difference in meaning is this: in the first the situation is settled and the question says "are we then to abandon it on that account?"; in the second the situation is made conditional and the question says "even if you were so?" **I do not impose a preference; on either reading the answer to the sentence is the same: no.**

### س-ر-ف — overstepping the limit

**The sense the lexicographers give the root: to pass the boundary in something, to lose the measure.** *Isrāf* — the usual English renderings stay tied to wealth, but in Arabic the root conveys **overstepping the measure in any field whatever**.

**The lexicographers also record the word *sarif*: to go past without noticing, to let a thing escape one's eye.** *Marra fa-sarifahu* — he passed by and did not see it. **Alongside overstepping the measure, then, the root also holds a core of *inattention*.**

**This fits the word's place in the verse, and I record it as an observation:** the verse names the other side not by the name of a crime but by the name of **a fault of measure**. And the whole subject of the sūra is measure.

### What this verse says to the present

**The logic the sentence builds is this:** the condition of the one addressed is not made the ground for cutting off the reminder.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the question is one of denial:** the verse sets out the thought "these people do not understand, let us leave off" as an option, and rejects it. **The reason the thing is given is not the state of the one who receives it; it is the quality of what is given** — the quality enumerated in the third and fourth verses.

## 43/6-8 — وَكَمْ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن نَّبِىٍّ فِى ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ · وَمَا يَأْتِيهِم مِّن نَّبِىٍّ إِلَّا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ · فَأَهْلَكْنَآ أَشَدَّ مِنْهُم بَطْشًا وَمَضَىٰ مَثَلُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ

*Wa kam arsalnā min nabiyyin fi'l-awwalīn · Wa mā yaʾtīhim min nabiyyin illā kānū bihi yastahziʾūn · Fa-ahlaknā ashadda minhum baṭshan wa maḍā mathalu'l-awwalīn*

"How many a prophet We sent among those of old. And no prophet came to them **without their mocking him.** So We destroyed those stronger than these in violence. **The example of those of old has passed.**"

### Three verses, three tenses

**The arrangement uses three distinct tenses, and the sequence is worth recording:**

| Verse | Verb | Tense | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | *arsalnā* | Perfect | **Done** — the sending |
| 7 | *yaʾtīhim … yastahziʾūn* | Imperfect | **Ongoing** — the mockery |
| 8 | *ahlaknā … maḍā* | Perfect | **Finished** — the outcome |

**That the middle verse comes in the imperfect carries information.** In Arabic the imperfect, used in a past context, **brings the scene before the eye and conveys repetition.** The verse is therefore not saying "they mocked"; it is saying **"they would mock, every time"**.

**This builds the same structure as verse 23 later in the sūra:** there too it will say *mā arsalnā … illā qāla mutrafūhā* — "We sent to no town without those of it who lived in ease saying this". **The same pattern: negation plus exception.**

### يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ — root: ه-ز-أ

**This root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

**The sense the lexicographers give: to make light of someone, to belittle, to make a thing an object of amusement.** *Hazaʾa bihi* / *istahzaʾa bihi* — he made sport of him.

**The pattern is striking: Form X (*istifʿāl*).** In Arabic this form most often conveys **"to reckon a thing so, to find it so"** — *istaḥsana* (he found it beautiful), *istathqala* (he found it heavy). **The description that follows is this: *istihzāʾ* is to reckon someone a thing to be laughed at.**

**And this shows the family the root belongs to.** Three more words of the same family will occur in the sūra, and all are inside the question of measure:

| Verse | Word | Root | What is being done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | *yastahziʾūn* | ه-ز-أ | **Reckoning as amusement** |
| 32 | *sukhriyyā* | س-خ-ر | **Subjecting / mocking** |
| 47 | *yaḍḥakūn* | ض-ح-ك | **Laughing** |
| 54 | *fa'stakhaffa* | خ-ف-ف | **Reckoning as light** |

**All four do the same work: lowering the weight of the one in front of them.** I record this as a pattern, and I give its link to the sūra's main question as an observation: **mockery is an operation of weighing.** It puts the other on the scale and declares him light.

**Mockery was treated in detail in `049-hucurat.md` at 49/11; I rely on that treatment.** The finding recorded there was this: the one who mocks turns the other into an **instrument** of his own amusement — he removes him from being a subject.

### أَشَدَّ مِنْهُم بَطْشًا

**بَطْش — root ب-ط-ش.** The root was treated in `050-kaf.md` and in `085-buruc.md`. **Its core: to seize a thing with force and speed, to grasp and strike.**

**This verse was already cited in `085-buruc.md`:** *"We destroyed those stronger than them in violence (ashadda minhum baṭshan)"* (al-Zukhruf 43/8).

**The form of the comparison is worth recording:** the verse does not say "We destroyed them". **It says "those stronger than them".** The one addressed is thus not threatened directly; **what is recounted is what befell someone stronger than he is.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparative pattern (*afʿalu min*):** the verse does not frighten the one addressed, it **corrects his scale.** Without asking "how strong are you?", it supplies the frame within which the answer lies.

### وَمَضَىٰ مَثَلُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ

The root **م-ض-ي**: to pass on, to go forward, to cut through. *Māḍī* (the past tense) comes from this; *sayfun mādin* — a sword that cuts.

***Mathal* is from the root م-ث-ل: a likeness, an example, a cautionary narrative.**

**Two readings of the expression are transmitted:**

| Reading | Its meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | The **condition of those of old has passed away** — it has become history |
| 2 | The **example of those of old has been recounted** — it has occurred in the Qurʾān, it is on record |

**Both are possible in point of language, and I do not impose a preference.** What is to be recorded is that the word will return within the sūra: **in the fifty-sixth verse it will be said of Pharaoh and his people *wa mathalan li'l-ākhirīn* (and an example for those who come after).**

**What is seen when the two ends are set side by side is this:** in the eighth verse the example is taken **from the past**; in the fifty-sixth the example is left **to the future**. **The same word, two directions.**

## 43/9 — وَلَئِن سَأَلْتَهُم مَّنْ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ لَيَقُولُنَّ خَلَقَهُنَّ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

*Wa la-in saʾaltahum man khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa la-yaqūlunna khalaqahunna'l-ʿazīzu'l-ʿalīm*

"And if you ask them, **'Who created the heavens and the earth?'**, they will surely say, **'The Mighty, the Knowing created them.'**"

### The second block begins here, and its method changes

**The first block (1-8) set out the Book and the stance of the other side. The second block opens with a question — and the question is put to the other side.**

**The method is this, and it is one of the most effective moves in the sūra: showing the inconsistency of the other side's position out of their own answer.**

### The construction of the sentence: two emphases side by side

**The verse carries two *lāms*, and both are for emphasis:**

| Element | Its role |
|---|---|
| *wa **la**-in saʾaltahum* | The *lām* at the head of the condition — the herald of an oath (*lām al-tawṭiʾa*) |
| ***la**-yaqūlunna* | The answer to the oath — *lām* plus the nūn of emphasis |

**Grammatically, then, the sentence is an oath formula: "by God, if you ask, by God they will say this".**

**This piling-up of emphasis carries information, and I record it as my own reading:** the verse is not **guessing** what the answer will be, it is stating it as **certain**. What is being said is this: that they will give this answer is already settled. **The dispute is not about who the creator is.**

### The content of the answer must be recorded

**The answer the other side gives is this: *khalaqahunna'l-ʿazīzu'l-ʿalīm*.**

**Note: the answer does not stop at saying "God", it names two attributes.** The exegetes have dwelt on this, and two readings are transmitted:

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | The whole answer is from their mouths — these attributes are part of the answer they give |
| 2 | *Khalaqahunna* is theirs, the attributes are the text's own addition |

**I do not impose a preference.** But this can be recorded: **the two attributes chosen (*ʿazīz* — mighty, unconquerable; *ʿalīm* — knowing) are exactly the subject of the sūra's later argument.** The sūra will dispute who is great and who knows.

### Showing the contradiction by means of the admission itself

**This is one of the methods the Qurʾān most often uses against its Meccan addressees, and it was recorded in `078-nebe.md` as follows:**

> *"The Meccans did not deny that God was the creator (the Qurʾān records this in many places: al-ʿAnkabūt 29/61, al-Zukhruf 43/9). What they denied was that decayed bones could be gathered together again."*

**The same method will recur at the end of the sūra — in verse 87:** *wa la-in saʾaltahum man khalaqahum la-yaqūlunna'llāh* — "if you ask them who created them, they will surely say 'God'."

**The difference between the two verses is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:**

| | 43/9 | 43/87 |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Who created the heavens and the earth | Who created **them** |
| Scale | The universe | **Themselves** |
| Answer | *al-ʿazīzu'l-ʿalīm* | *Allāh* |
| What follows | A list of favours (10-13) | *fa-annā yuʾfakūn* (how are they turned away) |

**The sūra thus asks the same question twice: once about the universe, once about the one addressed himself.** The second comes at the end of the sūra, with the scale reduced. **This stands in the same place as the observation about "the distance of the proof" recorded in the discussion of al-Aḥqāf:** the proof is brought to where the one addressed can reach it.

## 43/10-11 — ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلْأَرْضَ مَهْدًا وَجَعَلَ لَكُمْ فِيهَا سُبُلًا لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ · وَٱلَّذِى نَزَّلَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۢ بِقَدَرٍ فَأَنشَرْنَا بِهِۦ بَلْدَةً مَّيْتًا ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ تُخْرَجُونَ

*Alladhī jaʿala lakumu'l-arḍa mahdan wa jaʿala lakum fīhā subulan laʿallakum tahtadūn · Wa'lladhī nazzala mina's-samāʾi māʾan bi-qadarin fa-ansharnā bihi baldatan maytan · Kadhālika tukhrajūn*

"He who made the earth **a cradle** for you and opened **roads** for you in it — so that you may find your way. And who sent down water from the sky **by a measure**, and with it We gave life to a dead land. **Thus shall you too be brought forth.**"

### Word order: the continuation of the answer, or the text's own speech?

**The verse begins with a relative clause attached to the answer in the ninth verse: *alladhī* — "he who".** The sentence, that is, has not yet ended; the creator is being described.

**And in the middle of the eleventh verse there is an *iltifāt* (a shift in the direction of address), and it must be recorded:**

> *wa'lladhī **nazzala*** (third person) → *fa-**ansharnā*** (first person plural)

**The sentence begins "He sent down" and continues "We gave life".** In Arabic this transition is a known device, and the lexicographers explain it as **the drawing near of the narration**: the agent described from a distance suddenly becomes the party who is speaking.

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** while the answer given from the mouths of the other side (*khalaqahunna'l-ʿazīzu'l-ʿalīm*) is still running on, the owner of the sentence steps in. **The admission is theirs, then; the conclusion is the text's.**

### مَهْدًا — root: م-ه-د

**The root was analysed in detail in `078-nebe.md`** (*mihādan*, al-Nabaʾ 78/6) and occurred also in `074-muddessir.md` and `051-zariyat.md`. **I do not repeat the finding there; its summary is this:**

> *"The concrete sense of the root is to smooth, to spread, to prepare — in particular to spread out a bed, to level the ground beneath and make it ready. **مَهْد** (mahd) — a cradle. **مِهَاد** (mihād) — a spread-out bed, a mattress."*

**In the discussion of al-Nabaʾ, *firāsh* and *mihād* were compared, and this was recorded:** *"Firāsh looks to the ground: this place has been spread. Mihād looks to the one on it: this has been prepared for someone."*

**What is to be added here is the difference of pattern, and the Qurʾān uses these two patterns in different places:**

| Word | Pattern | Where | Its emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| *mihād* | مِفْعَال — wide, spread out | al-Nabaʾ 78/6 | **Breadth** |
| *mahd* | مَفْعَل — noun of place | al-Zukhruf 43/10; Ṭāhā 20/53 | **Cradle** |

***Mahd* in the lexicographers means a cradle outright** — the rocked, carried vessel in which a child is laid. **The Qurʾān uses this word of Jesus (ʿĪsā) as well:** *yukallimu'n-nāsa fi'l-mahdi wa kahlan* (Āl ʿImrān 3/46), *kayfa nukallimu man kāna fi'l-mahdi ṣabiyyan* (Maryam 19/29).

**And this makes a link within the sūra — I record it as my own reading:** the same word is used in the tenth verse of **the earth**, and in other places in the Qurʾān of **Jesus son of Mary**; and verses 57-59 of this sūra are about the son of Mary. **I give this as an association, not as a link the text itself makes.** The sūra makes no such link.

**The work the word does in the verse is this:** the earth is named not as a floor or a place but as **a vessel of care.** A cradle holds what is in it, carries it, and rocks it.

**And can a link to natural science be made here?** In some non-classical commentary one finds conclusions about the motion of the globe drawn from the word *mahd*. **Per the method (`USLUP.md`) I do not go down that road:** the meaning of the word in the lexicographers is plain, and the verse makes no claim about motion. **What the word carries is a picture of care, not a piece of physics.**

### سُبُلًا — roads

The root **س-ب-ل** has been treated in the corpus (`076-insan.md`, `047-muhammed.md`, `063-munafikun.md`). **Its core: a road that stretches out, that flows.** *Sabīl* — a road; *sābil* — falling rain.

**The order is worth recording:** first the **place** (*mahd* — where one stays), then the **road** (*subul* — where one goes). **Together the two describe a field of life: the place to remain and the place to go.**

**لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ** — and the ground: **so that you may find your way.**

**This is the same pattern as *laʿallakum taʿqilūn* in the third verse, and the two should be compared:**

| Verse | What is given | The ground |
|---|---|---|
| 43/3 | An Arabic Qurʾān | *laʿallakum taʿqilūn* — **that you may reason** |
| 43/10 | Roads on the earth | *laʿallakum tahtadūn* — **that you may find your way** |

**The two grounds are not from the same root but they do the same work:** one is the mind finding its direction, the other the foot. **And the root ه-د-ي is a key word in the sūra:** in verse 22 those who walk in the tracks of their forefathers will count themselves *muhtadūn* (guided); in verse 37 it will be said *wa yaḥsabūna annahum muhtadūn* — "and they suppose themselves to be guided"; in verse 49 the people of Pharaoh will give their word, *innanā la-muhtadūn*.

**The same word in four places: once as the ground the verse itself gives, three times as the other side's verdict upon itself.** I record this as a pattern: **the sūra separates the word "guidance" according to whose mouth it comes out of.**

### بِقَدَرٍ — root: ق-د-ر

**The root has been treated in many places in the corpus** (`087-ala.md`, `056-vakia.md`, `067-mulk.md`, `054-kamer.md`, `089-fecr.md` and others). **Its core: to measure, to determine the quantity of, to apportion.**

**And here the word's place in the sūra is special — I record this as my own reading:** the whole subject of the sūra is measure, and **the word for measure itself is used here for the first time, of the coming down of water.**

**بِقَدَرٍ** — "by a measure". It comes indefinite (*nakira*): the quantity is not stated, what is stated is that it **has been measured.**

**Water is the most visible example of measure, and the Qurʾān records this elsewhere too:** al-Muʾminūn 23/18 (*wa anzalnā mina's-samāʾi māʾan bi-qadarin fa-askannāhu fi'l-arḍ*), al-Ḥijr 15/21 (*wa mā nunazziluhu illā bi-qadarin maʿlūm*). **Too little and there is drought, too much and there is flood.** The measure is the narrow interval between two disasters.

**I record this as an observation:** the sūra opens its argument about measure **with an example in which the measure works correctly.** All the blocks that follow will show the faultiness of the measures human beings set up.

### فَأَنشَرْنَا — root: ن-ش-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to spread, to lay out, to open.** *Nashara'th-thawb* — he spread out the cloth. *Nashara'l-kitāb* — he opened the book.

**And the second branch of the root: to bring to life.** *Ansharaʾllāhu'l-mawtā* — God brought the dead to life.

**The lexicographers make the link between the two branches thus:** death is a **folding up**, resurrection an **unfolding**. The Turkish verb *neşretmek* ("to spread, to publish") is from this same root.

**In this verse the word is in Form IV (*anshara*)**, and the lexicographers record that this pattern has settled into the sense "to bring to life". **In al-Takwīr 81/10, in the expression *wa idha'ṣ-ṣuḥufu nushirat* (when the scrolls are spread open), the first branch of the root was at work; it was treated in `081-tekvir.md`.**

### كَذَٰلِكَ تُخْرَجُونَ — the leap the sentence makes

**"Thus shall you too be brought forth."**

**This sentence does exactly the same work as the place where the same structure was treated in `050-kaf.md`**, and it was recorded there as follows: the coming forth of the plant from the soil is described, and then, with no pause at all, *kadhālika'l-khurūj* is said. **In the discussion of Qāf this was identified as the sūra's form of proof: the proof is not an analogy but an image.**

**The same thing happens in al-Zukhruf, and even the pattern is similar:**

| Sūra | Expression | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Qāf 50/11 | *kadhālika'l-**khurūj*** | **Noun** — "such is the coming forth" |
| al-Zukhruf 43/11 | *kadhālika **tukhrajūn*** | **Passive verb** — "thus are you brought forth" |

**The difference is worth recording:** in Qāf the event is named; in al-Zukhruf it is **attached to the one addressed** — and in the passive: who brings forth is not stated, who is brought forth is.

**This verse was already cited in `071-nuh.md`** (among the examples of *"we gave life to dead soil… such too is the resurrection"*); I complete that link here.

## 43/12 — وَٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلْأَزْوَٰجَ كُلَّهَا وَجَعَلَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْفُلْكِ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ مَا تَرْكَبُونَ

*Wa'lladhī khalaqa'l-azwāja kullahā wa jaʿala lakum mina'l-fulki wa'l-anʿāmi mā tarkabūn*

"And who created **all the pairs**; and made for you, of ships and of livestock, **that on which you ride**."

### ٱلْأَزْوَٰج — root: ز-و-ج

**The meaning of the root: the fellow of a thing, its counterpart; that which is one of two.** *Zawj* in Arabic means **one member of a pair** — not the two together, as the English word "pair" does.

**The exegetes differ over the extent of the word here:**

| Reading | What *azwāj* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Male and female in humans and animals** |
| 2 | **All opposing pairs** — night and day, land and sea, sweet and salt |
| 3 | **All kinds** — that everything has its like and its opposite |

**The emphatic *kullahā* ("all of them") appears to support the second and third readings; but I do not impose a preference.**

**This breadth is supported within the Qurʾān itself:** Yā-Sīn 36/36 (*subḥāna'lladhī khalaqa'l-azwāja kullahā mimmā tunbitu'l-arḍu wa min anfusihim wa mimmā lā yaʿlamūn* — "of what the earth brings forth, of themselves, and of **what they do not know**"), al-Dhāriyāt 51/49 (*wa min kulli shayʾin khalaqnā zawjayn*).

**The note at the end of the Yā-Sīn verse is worth recording: *wa mimmā lā yaʿlamūn* — "and of what they do not know".** In making this generalisation, that is, the Qurʾān itself says that the extent of it exceeds human knowledge.

**I draw no direct indication from this verse to the paired structures of modern physics and biology (particle and antiparticle, chromosome pairs).** Per the method (`USLUP.md`), no hunting for scientific miracles is done. **What can be recorded is this:** the verse names the principle of pairing as a matter of observation and leaves its extent open.

### مِّنَ ٱلْفُلْكِ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ مَا تَرْكَبُونَ — word order

**The sequence is striking: first the ship, then the animal.**

**فُلْك** — root **ف-ل-ك**: the core the lexicographers give is **roundness and turning**. *Falak* — the orbit of a heavenly body; *falka* — the round weight at the head of a spindle. **Its use for a ship is explained by the lexicographers by way of the ship's rounded hull or its going through the water by turning and rolling; I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

**In Arabic the word is used in the same form for singular and plural** — here, since *mā tarkabūn* is plural, the plural is understood.

**أَنْعَام** — root **ن-ع-م**: softness and abundance. *Niʿma* is from the same root. **The word denotes the domestic animals whose flesh and milk are eaten (camel, cattle, sheep, goat)**, and the lexicographers make its link with the root's core of "softness" by way of **the softness of their coats and hides**.

**I record this as an observation:** the name of the riding animal is from the same root as **favour**. And in the next verse, what is commanded to be named while mounting that animal is **the favour**: *thumma tadhkurū niʿmata rabbikum*.

**The verse thus turns into a matter of worship what already stands in the root of the word.** I make this link by way of the root the lexicographers record; I do not present it as a link the text makes explicitly.

### The sequence itself

| Verse | The favour | The field |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | The earth — a cradle | **Where one stays** |
| 10 | Roads | **Where one goes** |
| 11 | Measured water | **What is drunk, what makes things grow** |
| 12 | Ship and animal | **What carries** |

**Four steps, enumerating the fields of a single life.** And all four are the continuation of the answer in the ninth verse: the other side said "He is the creator"; the text enumerates what "the creator" has done.

## 43/13-14 — لِتَسْتَوُۥا۟ عَلَىٰ ظُهُورِهِۦ ثُمَّ تَذْكُرُوا۟ نِعْمَةَ رَبِّكُمْ إِذَا ٱسْتَوَيْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ وَتَقُولُوا۟ سُبْحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِى سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَٰذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُۥ مُقْرِنِينَ · وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ

*Li-tastawū ʿalā ẓuhūrihi thumma tadhkurū niʿmata rabbikum idha'stawaytum ʿalayhi wa taqūlū subḥāna'lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā wa mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn · Wa innā ilā rabbinā la-munqalibūn*

"That you may **settle upon their backs**, and then, when you are settled, **name the favour of your Lord** and say: **'Glory be to Him who made this subservient to us; we could never have matched it. And to our Lord we are surely returning.'**"

**I shall dwell on these two verses in detail, because places where the Qurʾān gives the very wording of what is to be said in return for a favour are few.**

### Word order: three verbs, one chain

**The sentence opens with a *lām al-taʿlīl* (the lām of purpose), and three verbs are linked to one another:**

| Order | Verb | Connector | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *li-tastawū* | *li-* | **That you settle** — work of the body |
| 2 | *tadhkurū* | *thumma* | **That you name** — work of the mind |
| 3 | *taqūlū* | *wa* | **That you say** — work of the tongue |

**The connectors must be recorded.** The passage from the first to the second is with ***thumma***: in Arabic *thumma* conveys **an interval in between** (this distinction was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/13). **Between mounting and naming, then, there is a gap: first one sits, one settles, and then one names.**

The passage from the second to the third is with ***wāw***: **no interval.** Naming and saying are at the same moment.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two connectors:** the verse asks for the thanks not before mounting the animal but **after one has mounted and settled.** The favour is named, that is, **while it is being used** — not before it and not after.

### ٱسْتَوَىٰ — root: س-و-ي

**The core of the root: flatness, equality, evenness.** *Sawāʾ* — equal. *Istiwāʾ* — to come to be even and balanced, to settle.

**The verb is not Form X (*istifʿāl*) but Form VIII (*iftiʿāl*): the initial *sīn* is a letter of the root itself.** The meaning the lexicographers give here is **to settle evenly upon something, to come to rest in balance.**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "you mounted", it says **"you came into balance"**. **To ride is to hold one's balance on top of a moving creature or of a hull going through water** — and this is the word's own picture.

**The verb comes twice:** *li-tastawū* and *idha'stawaytum*. **The repetition marks the time: once as the purpose, once inside the condition.**

### ظُهُور — backs

The root **ظ-ه-ر**: the outer face of a thing, its back; and from there **to appear, to come out into the open, to prevail.** *Ẓāhir* — the outer, the visible.

**The pronoun comes singular: *ẓuhūrihi* — "its backs".** Yet two things had been enumerated before it: ships and animals.

**The lexicographers explain it thus:** the pronoun refers back to the expression *mā tarkabūn* — "that which you ride" is singular in wording. **Ship and animal are thus gathered under a single head: the thing ridden.**

**And this can be recorded as an observation:** the verse names the ship and the camel in the same sentence, with the same pronoun. **The difference of technology between the two is not the verse's concern; what they have in common is that a human being covers, on top of something, a road he could not cover by himself.**

**And this common feature can be thought together with the word *maʿārij* (stairways) that will occur in the thirty-third verse:** as recorded in `070-mearic.md`, *maʿārij* occurs in its concrete sense in this sūra alone in the Qurʾān. **This is a sūra that draws attention to the means by which human beings climb and travel.**

### سَخَّرَ — root: س-خ-ر

**The root was treated in `069-hakka.md` and in `049-hucurat.md`; I rely on those treatments.** The finding recorded in the discussion of al-Ḥāqqa was this:

> *"The root س-خ-ر carries two branches of meaning: **سَخَّرَ** (Form II) — to make something submit, to press it into service; **سَخِرَ مِن** — to mock. The lexicographers give the shared core of the two branches thus: **to put someone into a position outside his own will.** In subjection it is the body that is at issue; in mockery, standing."*

**And this makes a very important link within the sūra — because the same root will return in the thirty-second verse:** *li-yattakhidha baʿḍuhum baʿḍan **sukhriyyā***.

**The two occurrences must be set side by side:**

| Verse | Word | Who to whom |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | *sakhkhara lanā hādhā* | **God**, the mount to the human being |
| 32 | *li-yattakhidha baʿḍuhum baʿḍan sukhriyyā* | **The human being**, to the human being |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the root is shared:** the sūra uses the same root in two different places — one for a possibility God has given to the human being, the other for the relation human beings set up with one another. **And the first is answered with glorification, the second is the subject of an argument.**

### وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُۥ مُقْرِنِينَ — root: ق-ر-ن

**The root was treated in two places in `050-kaf.md`** (*qarīn*, 50/23 and 50/27; and *qarn*, 50/36). **The finding there was this:**

> *"Concrete sense: to bind two things to one another, to pair them. **قَرَنَ بَيْنَ شَيْئَيْن** — he brought two things together, bound them. **قِرَان** — joining. **قَرْن** — horn; and generation."*

**I add here the particular pattern of the word in this verse, because that is where the matter lies.**

***Muqrin* is the active participle of Form IV (*iqrān*).** The meaning the lexicographers give for this pattern is: **to be a match for something, to be capable of it, to be in a state of being able to cope with it.**

**How is the link made?** The lexicographers explain: for two camels to be harnessed to the same rope they must be **matched**. *Aqrana li'sh-shayʾ* — he was a match for a thing, he was able to bear it, he was capable of it. **"To be capable", then, is in the root's picture "to be matched enough to be harnessed alongside".**

**From here the meaning of the sentence follows:** *wa mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn* — **"we were not its match", "we could not have harnessed this ourselves", "we would not have been capable of it".**

**The renderings given in the classical commentaries circle among these three aspects:**

| Rendering | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| "We could not have been capable of it" | **Power** |
| "We could not have matched it" | **Measure** |
| "We could not have held it down" | **Mastery** |

**All three come out of the root, and I do not impose a preference.** But the root's core of "matching" is directly related to the sūra's question of measure, and I record this as my own reading: **as a person mounts his beast, he is not counting himself greater than his beast; on the contrary, he is saying that he is not its match.**

**And the word's return within the sūra must be recorded:** the same root will come back in the fifty-third verse — *aw jāʾa maʿahu'l-malāʾikatu **muqtarinīn*** (Form VIII: "accompanying one another, in a train"). **Pharaoh asks, as proof of prophethood, that the angels should come in a train.**

**The difference between the two uses can be given as an observation:** in the thirteenth verse a human being says that he is **not a match**; in the fifty-third Pharaoh demands **a visible procession**. The same root, one in self-lowering, the other in a demand for splendour.

### سُبْحَٰنَ — the pattern of the glorification

The root **س-ب-ح**: to swim, to glide through water or air; and from there **to put far away, to declare free of.** *Subḥāna* is a verbal noun in the accusative, its verb unexpressed (*usabbiḥu*).

**The work of the word: to hold God far from what does not befit Him.**

**And this is part of a pattern within the sūra:** the same word will come again in the eighty-second verse — *subḥāna rabbi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi rabbi'l-ʿarshi ʿammā yaṣifūn*.

| Verse | What the glorification stands against |
|---|---|
| 13 | Against **a favour** — while mounting a beast |
| 82 | Against **an ascription** — against what is imputed |

**The same word, for two distinct reasons.** I record this as an observation: the sūra uses glorification as the language both of thanks and of refusal.

### The prayer itself: mounting counted as a favour

**I come now to the most striking aspect of the verse, and I must treat it in detail.**

**It is a rare thing in the Qurʾān for the very wording of what is to be said in the face of a favour to be given.** The words given here are two sentences, and the passage between them must be recorded:

| Sentence | Subject |
|---|---|
| *Subḥāna'lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā wa mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn* | **This journey** |
| *Wa innā ilā rabbinā la-munqalibūn* | **The real journey** |

**What is said about mounting, then, does not end with mounting.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two sentences are parts of the same utterance:** as a person sets out on a journey, he says that the journey he has set out on lies inside a journey greater than himself. **The mount is moving, the person is moving; and the words bind the whole movement to a single direction.**

### مُنقَلِبُون — root: ق-ل-ب

**The concrete sense of the root: to turn a thing inside out, to overturn it.** *Qalb* (the heart) is from this root too — the lexicographers explain it by **its constant turning, its passing from state to state.**

***Munqalib* is the active participle of Form VII (*infiʿāl*), and this form conveys reflexivity: that which is itself turned, which turns.**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say *rājiʿūn* (those who return), it says *munqalibūn*. **The difference is this: *rujūʿ* is going back, whereas *inqilāb* is turning over, being overturned.**

**I give this as an observation about the word:** the word placed in the mouth of a person moving forward on a mount is a word that conveys **a reversal of direction.**

### The report and the practice

**It is transmitted in the ḥadīth sources that the Prophet recited the wording of this verse when setting out on a journey; the report occurs in Muslim's *Ṣaḥīḥ* from Ibn ʿUmar as a prayer for travel, and the text of this verse occurs within it.** **On the details of the report (the whole of the wording, the additional portions) I issue no ruling; what I record is that the verse has been transmitted as having been used as the text of a prayer.**

**And this use accords with the structure of the verse itself:** the verse already commands the words by saying *wa taqūlū* (and say). **The verse is not giving a piece of information; it is teaching a sentence.**

### What this says to the present

**What the mount is has changed; the relation the verse builds has not.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sentence *mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn*:** the work of the sentence is to say not that the vehicle does not belong to the person but that **the person is not the vehicle's match.** The vehicle is stronger than the human being; being able to use it does not mean being equal to it.

**Today this is easily seen:** a human body can neither travel a hundred kilometres in an hour, nor stand on water, nor stay in the air. **What does these things is the result not of some equality granted to the human being but of a possibility harnessed from outside.**

**And that the verse places the thanks at exactly this point — at *the moment of use* — must be recorded:** the thanks is placed not at the moment the vehicle is bought nor at the moment the journey ends, but **at the moment one has sat down on it and come into balance.** **The moment a favour is most forgotten is the moment it is most used.**

## 43/15-16 — وَجَعَلُوا۟ لَهُۥ مِنْ عِبَادِهِۦ جُزْءًا ۚ إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَكَفُورٌ مُّبِينٌ · أَمِ ٱتَّخَذَ مِمَّا يَخْلُقُ بَنَاتٍ وَأَصْفَىٰكُم بِٱلْبَنِينَ

*Wa jaʿalū lahu min ʿibādihi juzʾā · Inna'l-insāna la-kafūrun mubīn · Ami'ttakhadha mimmā yakhluqu banātin wa aṣfākum bi'l-banīn*

"They assigned to Him **a part** of His own servants. Truly the human being is **plainly ungrateful.** Or has He taken **daughters** for Himself out of what He creates, and set aside **sons** for you?"

### The third block begins here

**The fourteenth verse ended with a glorification from a human mouth: *subḥāna'lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā*. The fifteenth opens with an ascription made by that same human being.**

**The harshness of the transition must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** the distance between the two verses is one word. **Glorification and imputation are set side by side** — and the connector between them is only a *wāw*.

### وَجَعَلُوا۟ — the third *jaʿala*

**The verb comes for the third time in the sūra, and this time the agent has changed:**

| Verse | Agent | Object |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | *jaʿalnā* — **We** | We made the Qurʾān Arabic |
| 10, 12 | *jaʿala* — **He** | He made the earth a cradle, the animal a mount |
| 15 | *jaʿalū* — **They** | They assigned a part to Him |

**The same verb, in the same sūra, three times.** In the first two there is a real transformation: something is put from one state into another. **In the third, a verdict is passed: "they reckoned it so".**

**In Arabic *jaʿala* carries this second sense as well: to accept something as such, to deem it so.** And in the nineteenth verse the same verb will come again in this sense: *wa jaʿalu'l-malāʾikata … ināthā* — "they reckoned the angels female".

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the sūra uses the same verb for **making** and for **deeming**, and shows the difference between them by changing the agent.

### جُزْءًا — root: ج-ز-أ

**This root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

**The sense of the root: to divide, to break into parts; and the part of a whole.** *Juzʾ* — a part, a portion. The Turkish words *cüz* and *cüzi* come from here. **From the same root *ajzaʾa* — he apportioned; *jazāʾ* (in the sense of sufficiency) — one part's standing in place of another.**

**What is the word's work here?** The exegetes transmit two readings:

| Reading | What *juzʾ* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Offspring** — a child is counted as a part of the father |
| 2 | **A share, a portion** — reckoning some of the servants as belonging to Him |

**The first reading takes support from the context**, since immediately after it comes "has He taken daughters and set aside sons for you". **I do not impose a preference; but that this particular word was chosen is worth recording.**

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the meaning of the root:** *juzʾ* means **divisibility**. For a thing to have a part, that thing must be divisible. **Before rejecting the claim, then, the verse names what is inside the claim: division.**

**And this is the same line as the one treated in `112-ihlas.md`.** It was recorded there:

> *"The first addressees were not claiming that God was born… Their claim was that God **had a child**: the angels are His daughters. The Qurʾān raises this belief again and again and rejects it: al-Najm 53/21-22; also al-Naḥl 16/57, al-Ṣāffāt 37/149-153, al-Zukhruf 43/16-19."*

**The analysis of *al-Ṣamad* in the discussion of al-Ikhlāṣ attaches directly here: not divided, not hollow, not broken into parts.** I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.

### لَكَفُورٌ مُّبِينٌ — the pattern

The root **ك-ف-ر**: to cover, to conceal (a farmer is called *kāfir* because he covers the seed with soil). **The root has been treated in many places in the corpus.**

***Kafūr* is an intensive form (فَعُول): one who covers much, a deep ingrate.** And *mubīn* comes beside it — **"plainly".**

**The choice of word must be recorded: the verse does not say *kāfir*, it says *kafūr*.** The lexicographers use the pattern *kafūr* mostly in the sense of **covering over a favour** — not so much the refusal of belief as **failing to make a return.**

**And this accords exactly with the context — I record it as my own reading:** the four preceding verses were enumerating favours (earth, road, water, mount). **The fifteenth verse names the ascription made about the owner of those favours "ingratitude".** The matter is set here, that is, not as an error of creed but as **covering over what has been given.**

**This is the third occurrence of the word *mubīn* in the sūra** (2: *al-kitābi'l-mubīn*; 15: *kafūrun mubīn*; and in 18 *ghayru mubīn* will come). **The same root: the Book is clear, the ingratitude is clear, and a human being is reckoned "not clear".**

### أَمِ ٱتَّخَذَ … بَنَاتٍ وَأَصْفَىٰكُم بِٱلْبَنِينَ

**The verse is a question, and the pattern of the question carries a refusal.**

***Am* here is *munqaṭiʿa*: "or rather…" — and the lexicographers render it with *bal* + hamza (rather + the interrogative of denial).**

**أَصْفَىٰ — root ص-ف-و.** **The concrete sense of the root: a thing's being clear, unmixed, without sediment.** *Ṣafā* — it became clear. *Ṣafwa* — the cleanest, choicest part of a thing. *Iṣṭafā* — he chose out and set apart.

**In Form IV, *aṣfā*: to set apart and choose something for someone, to give it unmixed.**

**The choice of word does the verse's work, and I record it as an observation:** the verse does not say "did He give the sons to you?" **It says "did He *set apart* the sons for you, choose out the pure portion and give it to you?"** **The logic of division inside the claim is thus brought to light:** He gave the good portion not to Himself but to the other side.

**The same logic occurs in a sharper sentence in al-Najm 53/21-22:** *a-lakumu'dh-dhakaru wa lahu'l-unthā · tilka idhan qismatun ḍīzā* — "is the male yours and the female His? That would be an unjust division." **It was treated in `053-necm.md`.**

The root **ض-ي-ز** occurred there: crooked, unjust division. **And in this sūra the same question will return in the thirty-second verse** — *a-hum yaqsimūna raḥmata rabbik* (is it they who divide out your Lord's mercy?). **The sūra thus takes up the idea of "dividing out" twice: once in the divine field (16), once in the human field (32).**

## 43/17-18 — وَإِذَا بُشِّرَ أَحَدُهُم بِمَا ضَرَبَ لِلرَّحْمَٰنِ مَثَلًا ظَلَّ وَجْهُهُۥ مُسْوَدًّا وَهُوَ كَظِيمٌ · أَوَمَن يُنَشَّؤُا۟ فِى ٱلْحِلْيَةِ وَهُوَ فِى ٱلْخِصَامِ غَيْرُ مُبِينٍ

*Wa idhā bushshira aḥaduhum bimā ḍaraba li'r-Raḥmāni mathalan ẓalla wajhuhu muswaddan wa huwa kaẓīm · A-wa man yunashshaʾu fi'l-ḥilyati wa huwa fi'l-khiṣāmi ghayru mubīn*

"And when one of them is given good news of **what he has ascribed to al-Raḥmān**, his face turns black and **he is inwardly choked.** Is one who is raised amid ornament and **cannot bring his meaning to light in a dispute** (to be ascribed to Him)?"

**In treating these two verses I must keep one thing clear from the outset, per the method (`USLUP.md`).**

**The verse issues no ruling about women. The verse reports a society's attitude towards a daughter and shows the contradiction inside that attitude.** I shall show step by step below how this is done; for the force of the verse lies exactly in this manner of reporting.

### The construction of the sentence: the name of what is ascribed is not spoken

**The verse does not say "when one of them is given good news of a daughter".**

**It says: *bushshira aḥaduhum bimā ḍaraba li'r-Raḥmāni mathalan* — "when one of them is given good news of what he has ascribed as a likeness to al-Raḥmān".**

**This is one of the most striking choices of arrangement in the sūra, and it must be treated in detail.**

**The word — "daughter" — does not occur in the sentence.** In its place, the person's own ascription is used as the description. **What the sentence does, that is, is this: "you ascribe such a thing to God; now let us look at what happens when the same thing is given to you."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the expression itself:** the verse joins two situations in a single sentence and forces the reader to set them side by side. **A person ascribes something upward; when the same thing comes to him, his face turns black.**

**The expression occurs in open wording in al-Naḥl 16/57-59**, and the expression in this sūra is like an abbreviation of it:

> *"And when one of them is given good news of a female, his face turns black and he is filled with rage. He hides from the people because of the bad news he has been given: is he to keep her in humiliation, or **to bury her in the earth**? Look how evil is the judgement they make!"* (al-Naḥl 16/58-59)

**This verse was treated in `081-tekvir.md`, and there al-Naḥl's verb (*dassa* — to push into the earth in secret) was compared with al-Takwīr's word (*mawʾūda* — one on whom a weight has been laid).** I rely on that treatment and extend the comparison to three columns:

| | **al-Naḥl 16/58-59** | **al-Takwīr 81/8-9** | **al-Zukhruf 43/17** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who is described | The father | **The child** | The father |
| Point of view | The father's motive — shame, hiding | The victim's position — the weight laid on her | The father's **contradiction** |
| The word | *dassa* — to bury in secret | *mawʾūda* — one weighed down | *bimā ḍaraba … mathalan* — what he ascribed |
| The verse's work | **Diagnosis** | **Reckoning** | **Showing the contradiction** |

**Three verses take hold of the same fact from three different places.** In al-Takwīr the victim herself was made to speak — and, as recorded in `081-tekvir.md`, the question was put not to the doer but to the victim. **In al-Zukhruf, neither doer nor victim is the one addressed: the one addressed is the mind that *both belittles a thing and ascribes it upward*.**

### بُشِّرَ — to be given good news

The root **ب-ش-ر** has been treated in the corpus (`002-bakara.md`, `080-abese.md`, `054-kamer.md` and others). **Its core: the outer surface of the skin (*bashara*); and from there, a piece of news striking the face.**

**The use of the word here produces a tension, and it must be recorded:** *bushshira* — "he was given good news". **The verb is the verb of good tidings.** But the reaction that follows is the reaction to bad news.

**In naming the event, that is, the verse declares its own side:** what happens is **good news**. The wrongness of the reaction is marked at the very head of the sentence by the choice of verb.

**In al-Naḥl 16/58 the same root turns up again elsewhere: *min sūʾi mā bushshira bih* — "because of the evil of what he was given good news of".** There the word comes once more, but this time together with the person's own assessment. **In `080-abese.md` the root's core of "news that strikes the face" was treated; here what strikes the face is a darkening.**

### ظَلَّ وَجْهُهُۥ مُسْوَدًّا

**ظَلَّ** — not a full verb but an *incomplete* verb (*nāqiṣ*): **to be in some state throughout the day, to remain in that state.** The lexicographers record that *ẓalla* is bound up with the day, *bāta* with the night.

**The sentence does not say "his face turned black"; it says "his face remained blackened". It conveys duration.**

**مُسْوَدًّا** — root **س-و-د**, an active participle of Form IX. **In Arabic Form IX (*ifʿilāl*) is used almost only for colours and defects, and the lexicographers record that this form conveys *a colour's settling upon a person*.**

**This is a detail of language, but it carries the meaning:** the pattern conveys not a passing flush but **a colour that settles on the face.**

### كَظِيم — root: ك-ظ-م

**The root was treated in detail in `068-kalem.md` at 68/48 (*makẓūm*); I rely on that treatment.** What was recorded there was this:

> *"The concrete sense of the root: *kaẓama'l-qirba* — he filled the mouth of the waterskin and tied it. That is, a vessel filled inside and stopped at the mouth. **كَظِيم (kaẓīm)** — one who has buried his grief within. The Qurʾān uses it of Jacob (Yaʿqūb): *'His eyes went white with grief; he was filled within (kaẓīm)'* (Yūsuf 12/84)."*

**I add the difference of pattern here, because the two verses use two different patterns:**

| Place | Word | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Qalam 68/48 | *makẓūm* | Passive participle | **The one stopped up** — stopped from outside |
| al-Zukhruf 43/17 | *kaẓīm* | *faʿīl* — intensive | **The one who stops himself up** — who closes himself |
| Yūsuf 12/84 | *kaẓīm* | *faʿīl* | The same pattern — burying his grief within |

**The difference is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the mouth of the one who is *makẓūm* is tied by another; the one who is *kaẓīm* ties his own mouth.

**And the root's picture works here exactly:** the mouth of the skin is tied, but the skin is full. **What is visible from outside is a darkened face; what stands inside is pressure.**

**And a comparison can be made:** the same root occurs in Āl ʿImrān 3/134 as a **virtue** — *wa'l-kāẓimīna'l-ghayẓ* (those who swallow their rage). **The same verb, then, is praised in one place and in another becomes the description of a reaction.** What makes the difference is what is stopped up inside: in the one, rage is held back; in the other, the birth of a child.

### أَوَمَن يُنَشَّؤُا۟ فِى ٱلْحِلْيَةِ

**The eighteenth verse is a question attached to the seventeenth, and the *a-wa* at the head of the sentence conveys denial.**

**Who is the subject of the sentence? Here the exegetes differ, and the disagreement must be set out in a table:**

| View | Who *man yunashshaʾu fi'l-ḥilya* is | The work of the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The angels** — that is, those reckoned "female" | Is what you ascribe to Him the very one you look at by this measure? |
| 2 | **A daughter** — in general | Is one whom you see in this way ascribed to Him? |
| 3 | **The idols** — statues adorned and decked out | Is an adorned object that cannot speak reckoned a god? |

**The majority stand around the first two readings. I do not impose a preference.**

**But whichever reading is taken, the work of the sentence is the same, and I record this as my own reading: the verse is not making a description, it is reporting a *point of view*.**

**The proof of this within the language is as follows:** the sentence is **a question of denial** opening with *a-wa*. In a question of denial, the content put into the question is not the speaker's own verdict — **it is taken from the addressee's own acceptance and handed back to him.** The verse is not saying "a daughter is such and such"; **it is saying "is one whom you see in such a way really so?"**

**Writing this distinction out plainly is required by the method (`USLUP.md`), and it also follows from the text.** The one addressed by the verse is the mind that carries two verdicts at once: the mind that on the one hand counts a daughter low enough for its face to darken, and on the other sees no objection to ascribing the same thing to God. **What the verse shows is that these two cannot stand together.**

### فِى ٱلْحِلْيَة — root: ح-ل-ي

**The root was treated in `076-insan.md` at 76/21 (*ḥullū asāwira min fiḍḍa*).** **Its meaning: ornament, jewellery, adornment.** *Ḥuliyy* — jewellery; *taḥliya* — adorning.

***Yunashshaʾu* — from the root ن-ش-أ, Form II, passive: "is raised".** **The root was treated in detail in `056-vakia.md` at 56/35;** the core recorded there was this: *"it describes not bringing into being out of nothing, but being built upward from a ground that already exists."*

**The pattern matters, and I record it as an observation: the verb is passive.** *Yunashshaʾu* — "is raised". **The subject of the verb, that is, is not the person herself; the one who raises her is another.**

**Here lies the irony within the sentence:** to be raised amid ornament is not the choice of the one who is raised. **A community is putting forward its own manner of upbringing as a defect.** I record this as my own reading; its ground is the passive pattern of the verb.

### فِى ٱلْخِصَامِ غَيْرُ مُبِينٍ

The root **خ-ص-م**: the tip or edge of a thing (*khuṣm* — the corner of a sack); and from there **the opposing party, an adversary; contention.** *Khiṣām* — dispute, the exchange of speech in a suit.

**The word will return in the fifty-eighth verse: *bal hum qawmun khaṣimūn* — "they are a contentious people".**

**What emerges when the two occurrences are set side by side is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:**

| Verse | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | The one ascribed | *fi'l-khiṣāmi **ghayru mubīn*** — unable to bring her meaning out in contention |
| 58 | Those who ascribe | *qawmun **khaṣimūn*** — a contentious people |

**The same root, forty verses apart, for two sides.** On the one side, one counted small because she cannot contend; on the other, those who do nothing but contend.

**ghayru mubīn** — and here we return to the opening verses of the sūra.

**The same word (*mubīn*) has occurred three times in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Of whom or what |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | *al-kitābi'l-**mubīn*** | **The Book** — clear |
| 15 | *kafūrun **mubīn*** | **Ingratitude** — clear |
| 18 | *ghayru **mubīn*** | The one ascribed — **not clear** |

**And the same word will come in the fifty-second verse, of a human being:** Pharaoh will say of Moses *wa lā yakādu **yubīn*** — "he can scarcely make his meaning plain".

**This is one of the clearest patterns in the sūra, and it can be verified:** the same root, throughout the sūra, always by the same measure. **And twice it is used to make a human being small** — in verse 18 of the one raised amid ornament, in verse 52 of Moses.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this repetition:** the sūra shows twice how the measure of "being able to state one's meaning clearly" is used to strip a human being of value. **And in the opening verses it had set up the same measure positively, for the Book.** The measure itself, that is, is not wrong; what is criticised is **the use of the measure to establish a ranking of superiority among human beings.**

### What this says to the present

**The reaction the verse describes is not a record of history.**

**The note entered on this subject in `081-tekvir.md` holds here too, and I repeat it:** sex-selective practices have produced measurable population imbalances in some countries where the means of prenatal determination have become widespread, and have become the subject of legal regulation. **I write this not as a "scientific miracle of the verse" but as a record that the motive the verse describes has not been buried in history.**

**And the verse's own method is one that can still be used today:** the verse does not defend a verdict, **it shows a contradiction.** It says that it is not possible both to count a thing low and to ascribe it upward. This is a test that can be applied to any ranking of value.

## 43/19-21 — وَجَعَلُوا۟ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةَ ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عِبَٰدُ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ إِنَٰثًا ۚ أَشَهِدُوا۟ خَلْقَهُمْ ۚ سَتُكْتَبُ شَهَٰدَتُهُمْ وَيُسْـَٔلُونَ · وَقَالُوا۟ لَوْ شَآءَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ مَا عَبَدْنَٰهُم ۗ مَّا لَهُم بِذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ ۖ إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَخْرُصُونَ · أَمْ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُمْ كِتَٰبًا مِّن قَبْلِهِۦ فَهُم بِهِۦ مُسْتَمْسِكُونَ

*Wa jaʿalu'l-malāʾikata'lladhīna hum ʿibādu'r-Raḥmāni ināthā · A-shahidū khalqahum · Sa-tuktabu shahādatuhum wa yusʾalūn · Wa qālū law shāʾa'r-Raḥmānu mā ʿabadnāhum · Mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilmin in hum illā yakhruṣūn · Am ātaynāhum kitāban min qablihi fa-hum bihi mustamsikūn*

"They reckoned the angels, **who are the servants of al-Raḥmān**, to be female. **Were they witnesses to their creation?** Their testimony will be written down, and they will be questioned. And they said: 'Had al-Raḥmān willed, we would not have served them.' They have no knowledge of this; they are only **guessing.** Or did We give them a Book before this, so that they are **holding fast** to it?"

### ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عِبَٰدُ ٱلرَّحْمَٰن — the interposed clause

**In the middle of the verse there is a relative clause, and the sense would have held even without it.** But it is there, and its work must be recorded.

***Malāʾika* is not simply said and passed over; what is said is "the angels who are the servants of al-Raḥmān".**

**I give this as an observation about the structure:** the claim is refuted not by an argument against it but **by putting the right name to the being that is claimed about.** **What is a servant cannot be an offspring.**

The root **ع-ب-د** has been treated in the corpus (`109-kafirun.md`, `053-necm.md`, `051-zariyat.md` and others). **At its core stand the picture of *submission* and of *a road levelled by the tread of feet* (*ṭarīqun muʿabbad*).**

**And the word stands at a key point in the sūra:** in the fifty-ninth verse the same word will be used of the son of Mary — *in huwa illā **ʿabdun** anʿamnā ʿalayh*. **The sūra, that is, puts the same name to both the angels and Jesus: servant.** Two separate ascriptions are met with a single answer.

### أَشَهِدُوا۟ خَلْقَهُمْ — the question of testimony

**The question is a question about evidence, and its form must be recorded: *a-shahidū* — "were they present?"**

The root **ش-ه-د**: to be present at an event; and from there, **eyewitness testimony.** At the core of the root lies **being there** — testimony, therefore, comes not from hearsay but from **being present in person.**

**The verse asks this: were you there when you passed this verdict?**

**And it passes on without giving the answer. In its place it states a consequence: *sa-tuktabu shahādatuhum wa yusʾalūn* — "their testimony will be written down, and they will be questioned".**

**What is to be recorded is this:** the verse does not name the claim "a lie"; **it names it "testimony" and enters it on the record.** The word, that is, is bound to the one who said it.

**This stands on the same line as verse 80 at the end of the sūra:** *wa rusulunā ladayhim yaktubūn* — "and Our messengers are with them, writing". **The same root (ك-ت-ب), the same work: what is said being put on record.** The question of the record was treated in `082-infitar.md` and `083-mutaffifin.md`; I rely on those treatments.

### لَوْ شَآءَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ مَا عَبَدْنَٰهُم — the objection from decree

**This is an objection the Qurʾān records in many places** (al-Anʿām 6/148, al-Naḥl 16/35). **Its structure is this: had God not willed it, we would not have done this; since we have done it, He willed it.**

**The answer the verse gives looks not at the content of the objection but at its ground:**

| The answer given | What it says |
|---|---|
| *Mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm* | **They have no knowledge** |
| *In hum illā yakhruṣūn* | **They are guessing** |
| *Am ātaynāhum kitāban min qablih* | **Do they have a written ground?** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that all three answers look in the same direction:** the verse does not enter into the argument about decree. **It does not argue whether the objection is right or wrong; it says that the one who advances the objection has no source of knowledge on the matter.**

**This is exactly the method of Sūrat al-Aḥqāf.** It was recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/4: *"the verse asks for two kinds of evidence: observation and transmission. It leaves no third way."* **In al-Zukhruf the same two ways are asked for: were you witnesses (observation — 19), was a Book given (transmission — 21).**

**That even the order is the same is worth recording.**

### يَخْرُصُون — root: خ-ر-ص

**The concrete sense of the root comes, in the lexicographers, from agriculture: *kharaṣa'n-nakhl* — he estimated by eye the crop on the date palm before it had been gathered.**

**The root thus conveys an estimate made without measuring.** *Khāriṣ* — an estimator of crops. And from there: **to lie, to make things up** — because a statement made without measuring, if it hits the mark, does so by chance.

**This word was treated in `051-zariyat.md`, and this verse was cited there too:** *"They are only guessing."* (al-Anʿām 6/116; Yūnus 10/66; al-Zukhruf 43/20). **I rely on that treatment.**

**I add here the word's place within the sūra, and I record this as my own reading:** *kharṣ* is measuring by eye. **The subject of the sūra is measure.** The verse describes the other side's verdict not as "wrong" but as **"unweighed"**. **And in the eleventh verse it was said that the water was sent down *bi-qadarin* (by a measure).** Water sent down by measure and a verdict passed by eye stand at the two ends of the same sūra.

### مُسْتَمْسِكُون — root: م-س-ك

**The sense of the root: to hold, to grasp, to keep in hand.** Form X (*istimsāk*): **to hold on tightly, not to let go.**

**And the word will return in the sūra: in the forty-third verse, *fa'stamsik bi'lladhī ūḥiya ilayk* — "hold fast to what has been revealed to you".**

**The two occurrences must be compared, and this is a datum of the text:**

| Verse | Who | What is held to |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | The other side | **A Book that does not exist** (in the form of a question) |
| 43 | The Prophet | **What is revealed** (in the form of a command) |

**The same pattern (Form X), the same root, twenty-two verses apart.** I record this as a pattern: **the sūra uses the verb "to hold fast" twice, and in both places asks what the thing held to is.**

## 43/22 — بَلْ قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّا وَجَدْنَآ ءَابَآءَنَا عَلَىٰٓ أُمَّةٍ وَإِنَّا عَلَىٰٓ ءَاثَٰرِهِم مُّهْتَدُونَ

*Bal qālū innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā ummatin wa innā ʿalā āthārihim muhtadūn*

"No! They said: **We found our forefathers on a way, and in their tracks we are guided.**"

**This is the argument at the centre of the sūra, and it needs to be treated in detail.**

### بَلْ — the elimination of the third option

**The twenty-first verse had asked "or did We give them a Book?" The twenty-second begins with *bal*: "no, it is not that — what they actually say is this."**

***Bal* (the particle of *iḍrāb*) was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/2; I rely on that treatment.** Two functions were recorded there: cancelling what precedes, or leaving what precedes and leaping to the real matter. **Here it is the second.**

**The arrangement has proceeded thus:**

| Verse | The ground sought | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Testimony — observation | None |
| 21 | A Book — transmission | None |
| 22 | **The real ground** | **The forefathers** |

**The sūra, that is, asked the question of evidence twice and received no answer; then it stated the real ground itself.** I record this as an observation about the structure.

### عَلَىٰٓ أُمَّةٍ — the sense of the word *umma* here

**In the sense in which it has settled into English usage, the word means "community, nation". But that sense does not sit in the sentence here** — because it comes with the preposition *ʿalā*: "**upon** an *umma*".

**Here the lexicographers and the exegetes record another sense of the word, and this sense follows from the root.**

**The root أ-م-م** — as recorded in `075-kiyame.md`, its family is: *umm* (mother, origin), *umma* (community), *imām* (the one who stands in front), *amām* (the front). **The shared idea: priority, being in front, being at the source.**

**And starting from the word *imām*, the word *umma* takes on the sense of "the road that is followed, the line that is held to, the method".** The lexicographers record it thus: *umma* — **the state a person is upon, the road he keeps to, the order he follows.**

**The renderings given for this verse in the classical commentaries are these:**

| Rendering | Its emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Religion** | An order of belief |
| **A community's way / a road** | The line followed |
| **Ṭarīqa, sunna** | The method held to |

**All three come out at the same place, and I do not impose a preference.** What is to be recorded is this: **the word here conveys not a *body of people* but a *line*** — and the preposition (*ʿalā*) confirms it: one stands upon a road, not upon a community.

**And the word's three occurrences within the sūra must be compared:**

| Verse | Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | ***ummi**'l-kitāb* | **The origin, the source** |
| 22, 23 | *ʿalā **ummatin*** | **The line followed** |
| 33 | ***ummatan** wāḥida* | **A single community** |

**The same root, in three distinct senses, in one sūra.** I record this as a datum of the text.

**And I add this observation as my own reading:** in the fourth verse the **origin** of the Book was named — the source to which it is referred back. In the twenty-second verse the other side also points to an **origin**: their forefathers. **Both sides rest on a source; the dispute is over what the source is.**

### ءَاثَٰرِهِم — root: أ-ث-ر

**The root was analysed in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/4 (*athārata min ʿilm* — a remnant of knowledge); I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.** What was recorded there was this:

> *"The root أ-ث-ر: **a trace, a sign left behind.** *Athar* — a footprint, a remnant, a report that has been transmitted."*

**The root also occurred in `079-naziat.md`, `087-ala.md` and `059-hasr.md`; in `079-naziat.md` this verse was already cited.**

**What is to be added here is that the word stays *concrete* in this verse — and this matters.**

**In al-Aḥqāf, *athāra* was a remnant of knowledge: something transmitted, and therefore something with content.** **In al-Zukhruf, *āthār* is plural and stands in the concrete sense that looks to footprints:** *innā ʿalā āthārihim muhtadūn* — "in their tracks we find our way."

**I set the difference out in a table:**

| | **al-Aḥqāf 46/4** | **al-Zukhruf 43/22** |
|---|---|---|
| Word | *athāra min ʿilm* | *āthārihim* |
| What | **A remnant of knowledge** | **Footprints** |
| Who asks for it / uses it | The verse asks for it (as evidence) | The other side uses it (as evidence) |
| Its content | A piece of knowledge | **Only a direction** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of pattern and context between the two words:** what was asked for in al-Aḥqāf was a scrap of **knowledge**; what is put forward in al-Zukhruf is a **trace** — a sign with no content, only a direction.

**A footprint tells you who passed; it does not tell you where he was going.**

### مُّهْتَدُون — and the weight of the word

**The word is an active participle of Form VIII from the root ه-د-ي: "those who have found the way".**

**And this word is the very word the text uses of itself in the tenth verse of the sūra:** *wa jaʿala lakum fīhā subulan laʿallakum **tahtadūn***.

**The two occurrences side by side:**

| Verse | Who says it | What |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **The text** | Roads were opened so that **you may find your way** |
| 22 | **The other side** | We are in the tracks, **we have found the way** |

**I record this as a pattern:** the same word, once as a **possibility**, once as a **claim**. And in the thirty-seventh verse the text will pass its verdict on that claim: *wa yaḥsabūna annahum muhtadūn* — **"and they suppose themselves to be guided".**

**The sūra thus works the word in three stages: the possibility given (10), the claim advanced (22), and the name of the claim (37).**

## 43/23 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ مَآ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ فِى قَرْيَةٍ مِّن نَّذِيرٍ إِلَّا قَالَ مُتْرَفُوهَآ إِنَّا وَجَدْنَآ ءَابَآءَنَا عَلَىٰٓ أُمَّةٍ وَإِنَّا عَلَىٰٓ ءَاثَٰرِهِم مُّقْتَدُونَ

*Wa kadhālika mā arsalnā min qablika fī qaryatin min nadhīrin illā qāla mutrafūhā innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā ummatin wa innā ʿalā āthārihim muqtadūn*

"So it is: to whatever town We sent a warner before you, **those of it who lived in ease** said exactly this: **'We found our forefathers on a way, and we are following in their tracks.'**"

### What the verse does: the same sentence repeated in every age

**A sentence was given in the twenty-second verse. The twenty-third gives the same sentence once more, but this time for *all the communities of the past*.**

**The two verses are almost word for word the same, and a single word changes:**

| | 43/22 | 43/23 |
|---|---|---|
| Who says it | **Those addressed** | **Those of earlier peoples who lived in ease** |
| The sentence | *innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā umma* | *innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā umma* |
| Final word | ***muhtadūn*** — having found the way | ***muqtadūn*** — following, taking as a pattern |

**This is the only word that changes, and the difference must be recorded.**

**The root ق-د-و** — *iqtidāʾ*: to walk behind someone, to take him as a model. *Qudwa* — the one taken as a model. **The word conveys behaviour, not a verdict: "we are following".**

***Muhtadūn*, on the other hand, conveys a verdict: "we are in a state of having found the way".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of sense between the two words:** in the twenty-second verse those addressed claim the result (we have found the truth); in the twenty-third the earlier peoples confess the act (we are following). **The same ground, at two different degrees of certainty.**

**And the verse's real work is to show that this sentence *has been repeated throughout history*.**

**The pattern of the sentence is: negation plus exception** — *mā arsalnā … illā qāla*. **"We sent no warner without their saying this."** This pattern was used in the seventh verse of the sūra as well: *wa mā yaʾtīhim min nabiyyin illā kānū bihi yastahziʾūn.*

| Verse | Pattern | What is repeated |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | *mā yaʾtīhim … illā … yastahziʾūn* | **Mockery** |
| 23 | *mā arsalnā … illā qāla* | **The sentence about the forefathers** |

**The two verses build the same structure, and I record this as a datum of the text.**

**I add as my own reading:** what the verse does is not to isolate the one addressed but the very opposite — **to show that the sentence he is uttering is not original.** The other side puts its ground forward as though it were a choice; the verse says that the same sentence has come out of the same place in every age.

**And this is a question of measure:** if a ground is repeated in every age, in communities with no knowledge of one another, in the same words, then that ground is the result not of **thinking** but of a **position**. **And the verse names the position too: *mutrafūhā*.**

### مُتْرَفُوهَا — root: ت-ر-ف

**The root was analysed in `056-vakia.md` at 56/45; I rely on that treatment.** What was recorded there was this:

> *"The root meaning the lexicographers give: abundance of favour, and the softening and slackening that abundance produces. *Tarifa* — he lived in abundance. *Atrafahu* — he drowned him in comfort, spoilt him. **The pattern matters: *mutraf* is a passive participle of Form IV.** The word means **not 'one who runs to excess' but 'one who has been made to run to excess'**. The action worked on them from outside."*

**And this too was recorded there:** *"Wealth itself is not set down in the verse as the offence; what is set down as the offence is the state into which wealth brings a person."* And also: *"almost every time the word mutraf occurs in the Qurʾān it is in a context of destruction or of opposition. Al-Isrāʾ 17/16… In Sabaʾ 34/34 it is stated that the first to oppose every warner were the mutrafūn."*

**I add here what is particular to al-Zukhruf, and it is directly connected with the main question of the sūra.**

**The verse attaches the proof from the forefathers not to everyone but to a particular section: *mutrafūhā* — "those of that place who had been given ease".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the subject of the verse itself:** the defender of the proof from the forefathers is shown to be not the ordinary people but **the section with the greatest means.**

**The logic of this is consistent with the rest of the sūra, and it can be seen in two steps:**

1. **An inheritance concerns most the one who has most to inherit.** Those with the greatest interest in the continuation of the existing order are those who stand highest within it.
2. **And eight verses later the sūra will say this outright:** *law lā nuzzila hādha'l-qurʾānu ʿalā rajulin mina'l-qaryatayni ʿaẓīm* (31) — the objection will again come from a measure of rank.

**The sūra thus attaches the proof from the forefathers and the measure of wealth to the same section, and this is a datum of the text:** in verse 23 the subject is *mutrafūn*, in verse 31 the measure of the objection is *ʿaẓīm* (a great man).

**And there is one further difference between the *mutraf* of al-Wāqiʿa and the one here, worth recording:**

| | **al-Wāqiʿa 56/45** | **al-Zukhruf 43/23** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | The worldly state of the people of the Fire | The first to oppose the warner **in every town** |
| Time | A look back | **A repeating pattern** |
| Function | The ground of the punishment | **The sociology of the objection** |

**Al-Wāqiʿa uses the word as a state; al-Zukhruf as a position.**

## 43/24-25 — قَٰلَ أَوَلَوْ جِئْتُكُم بِأَهْدَىٰ مِمَّا وَجَدتُّمْ عَلَيْهِ ءَابَآءَكُمْ ۖ قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّا بِمَآ أُرْسِلْتُم بِهِۦ كَٰفِرُونَ · فَٱنتَقَمْنَا مِنْهُمْ ۖ فَٱنظُرْ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْمُكَذِّبِينَ

*Qāla a-wa law jiʾtukum bi-ahdā mimmā wajadtum ʿalayhi ābāʾakum · Qālū innā bimā ursiltum bihi kāfirūn · Fa'ntaqamnā minhum · Fa'nẓur kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-mukadhdhibīn*

"He said: **'Even if I have brought you something that guides better than what you found your forefathers upon?'** They said: 'We **reject** what you have been sent with.' So We took retribution from them. **Look, then, how the end of the deniers was.**"

### The construction of the question: the claim is accepted and something is set on top of it

**The warner's answer is not a refusal but a question.** And the structure of the question is striking.

***A-wa law* — "even if…?"** In Arabic this pattern supposes an acceptance and adds a condition on top of it.

**The answer, that is, does not say: "your forefathers were in error."**

**It says: "granted that they were on a way; what if I have brought something that guides better?"**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pattern used:** the answer does not **reject** the other side's measure, it **puts it to work.** The other side's measure is "being on the right way" (*muhtadūn*, 22). The answer takes that same measure and puts it into the comparative: ***ahdā*** — "one that guides better".

**The question, then, is this: if what you are looking for is the right way, why do you not take the one that is more right?**

**This stands on the same line as the answer recorded in `002-bakara.md` at 2/170**, which was cited in `071-nuh.md` as well: *"Even if their forefathers understood nothing and were not on the right way?"* (al-Baqara 2/170).

**The difference between the two answers is worth recording:**

| Verse | The route of the answer |
|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/170 | **Questioning the condition of the forefathers** — what if they did not understand |
| al-Zukhruf 43/24 | **An offer of comparison** — what if there is something more right |

**The second is less confrontational, and I give this as an observation:** the verse does not speak ill of the forefathers, **it only proposes a comparison.**

### Where the answer lands

**And the answer given does not touch the offer at any point:** *innā bimā ursiltum bihi kāfirūn* — "we reject what you have been sent with."

**Note: the sentence is plural — *ursiltum* ("you [plural] were sent"), whereas *jiʾtukum* was singular.**

**The exegetes explain the shift thus:** the answer is given not to a single warner but **to the whole chain of warners**. **I transmit this as it has been transmitted.**

**And I record this as an observation:** the verse leaves no debate at all between the offer and the answer. **The question looked to content (is it more right?); the answer does not touch the content at all.**

**This is the same as the finding recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/11:** *"the objection is not an argument but an assumption about ranking."* **So it is here too: the answer is not an assessment but the announcement of a position.**

### فَٱنتَقَمْنَا — root: ن-ق-م

**The sense of the root: to find a thing ugly and reject it; and from there to punish, to return the like of it.** *Naqama minhu* — he took retribution from him. *Mā tanqimu minnā* — what do you hold against us (it occurs in al-Aʿrāf 7/126).

**The usual English rendering, "vengeance", is narrower than the root:** in the root there stands first **finding a thing ugly, withholding approval**; the punishment is the consequence of that.

**The word occurs three times in the sūra, and this is a pattern:**

| Verse | Expression | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | *fa'ntaqamnā minhum* | **To earlier peoples** |
| 41 | *fa-innā minhum muntaqimūn* | **To those addressed** (future) |
| 55 | *intaqamnā minhum* | **To the people of Pharaoh** |

**All three from the same root, all three in the same structure.** I record this as a datum of the text: **the sūra shows the same outcome at three separate times** — it has happened in the past, it will happen in the future, and in one instance it is recounted in detail.

### فَٱنظُرْ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْمُكَذِّبِينَ

The root **ع-ق-ب** has been treated in the corpus (`090-beled.md`, `091-sems.md`, `059-hasr.md`). **Its core: the heel, what comes behind.** *ʿĀqiba* — what follows upon a matter, its end.

**And the word will return in the twenty-eighth verse: *fī ʿaqibihi* — "in his line / among those who come after him".**

**The two occurrences can be set side by side, and I give this as an observation:**

| Verse | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | *ʿāqibatu'l-mukadhdhibīn* | **The end of the deniers** |
| 28 | *kalimatan bāqiyatan fī ʿaqibih* | **The word left behind Abraham** |

**The same root: one conveys an ending, the other a continuation.** Three verses stand between them, and this prepares the passage to the next block of the sūra.

**م-ك-ذ-ب** — *mukadhdhibīn*, an active participle of Form II: **the deniers.** The root **ك-ذ-ب** has been treated in the corpus. **What is to be recorded is that the verdict is attached to an act:** no name of a people and no lineage is named; **a behaviour** is named. **Per the method (`USLUP.md`) this distinction is kept everywhere: what the verse describes is an attribute.**

## 43/26-27 — وَإِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَٰهِيمُ لِأَبِيهِ وَقَوْمِهِۦٓ إِنَّنِى بَرَآءٌ مِّمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ · إِلَّا ٱلَّذِى فَطَرَنِى فَإِنَّهُۥ سَيَهْدِينِ

*Wa idh qāla Ibrāhīmu li-abīhi wa qawmihi innanī barāʾun mimmā taʿbudūn · Illa'lladhī faṭaranī fa-innahu sa-yahdīn*

"And when Abraham (Ibrāhīm) said to his father and to his people: **'I am quit of what you serve — save Him who brought me into being. He will guide me.'**"

### The placing of the story here

**The twenty-fifth verse ended by saying "look how the end of the deniers was". The twenty-sixth opens with a particle of recollection: *wa idh* — "and when".**

**And the example chosen is striking: the exact counter-example to the proof from the forefathers.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the placement of the block:** against those who say "we found our forefathers on a way", the sūra recounts **someone who parted from his father.** And the person chosen is the name those addressed set at the very top of their own chain of descent.

**The counter-argument, that is, is not fetched from outside.** Those addressed said they were descended from Abraham; the sūra names their forefather as **someone who refused to follow his forefathers.**

**This stands in the same place as the finding about "the distance of the proof" recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`: the proof is taken from what is nearest to the one addressed.**

### بَرَآء — root: ب-ر-أ

**The root has come up in several places in the corpus** (`060-mumtehine.md`, `057-hadid.md`, `059-hasr.md`, `082-infitar.md`, `054-kamer.md`). **Here I dwell on the word's particular pattern.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to part from something, to come clear of it, to be cleared.**

- **بَرِئَ مِنَ المَرَض** — he recovered from an illness, came clear of it.
- **بَرِئَ مِنَ الدَّيْن** — he was freed of a debt, his liability was cleared.
- **بَرَاءَة** — acquittal; a document declaring no connection, a distancing. The other name of Sūrat al-Tawba.
- **بَارِئ** — the creator; the lexicographers explain this as "the one who brings a thing into being by separating it out from something else, by singling it out".

**The pattern of the word matters, and the lexicographers record it separately: *barāʾ* is a *verbal noun*, not an adjective.**

**The sentence therefore does not say "I am far from it" but, exactly, "I am the distance itself".**

**In Arabic the use of a verbal noun of a person conveys intensification** — as in *rajulun ʿadlun* ("a man who is justice itself"). **And because it is a verbal noun the word makes no distinction of singular and plural:** the same word serves for one person and for a group. **Thus in al-Mumtaḥana 60/4 it comes with a plural subject:** *innā **buraʾāʾu** minkum* — there the plural pattern (*buraʾāʾ*) is used. **It was treated in `060-mumtehine.md`.**

**I record this as a point of grammar:** Abraham is not stating an attitude here, he is stating **a position**. The construction of the sentence takes the separation out of the class of a temporary stance and makes it the definition of the person.

### إِلَّا ٱلَّذِى فَطَرَنِى — the place of the exception

**The sentence continues with an exception, and the exegetes dwell on what the exception attaches to:**

| Reading | What the exception attaches to | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | To *mimmā taʿbudūn* (connected, *muttaṣil*) | I am quit of what you serve — **except Him who brought me into being** (that is, they served Him too) |
| 2 | A disconnected exception (*munqaṭiʿ*) | I am quit of what you serve; **but He who brought me into being is another matter** |

**The first reading accords with the fact that the Meccan addressees accepted God as well** — the ninth and eighty-seventh verses of the sūra already record this. **The second is possible in point of language as well. I do not impose a preference.**

### فَطَرَنِى — root: ف-ط-ر

**The root has come up in many places in the corpus** (`067-mulk.md`, `073-muzzemmil.md`, `084-insikak.md`, `082-infitar.md`, `054-kamer.md`, `077-murselat.md`). **Here I open the core of the root once more, in the aspect this verse requires.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to split, to crack, to open a thing from end to end.**

- **فَطَرَ النَّابُ** — the camel's tooth split the flesh and came through.
- **اِنْفَطَرَتِ السَّمَاء** — the sky was split (al-Infiṭār 82/1 — treated in `082-infitar.md`).
- **فِطْر** — to break a fast; the opening of the mouth's closure. *Ifṭār* and the feast of *fiṭr* come from here.
- **فِطْرَة** — the state of the first creation.

**The picture of the root is this: something closed being split open for the first time.**

**And the sense of creating comes from here — the lexicographers record it plainly: *faṭara* is to bring a thing forth *for the first time, with no prior model*.** There is indeed a well-known saying ascribed to Ibn ʿAbbās: it is transmitted that he understood the meaning of the word *fāṭir* from two bedouin in a dispute over a well who said "*anā faṭartuhā*" ("I opened it, I was the first to dig it"). **I transmit this account as it has been transmitted; I issue no ruling on its chain.**

**Now the reason for the choice of this word in this verse can be seen, and I record it as my own reading:**

**In rejecting the proof from the forefathers, Abraham uses the verb *faṭara*. That is, the verb "the one who opened for the first time".**

| The other side's ground | Abraham's ground |
|---|---|
| *Wajadnā ābāʾanā* — **we found** | *Faṭaranī* — **He split me open, He brought me forth for the first time** |
| Handed down | **First** |
| On a trace | **Attached to the source** |

**The words come from exactly opposite places: one is "what was found behind", the other "the one who first opened".**

**And this connects with the fourth verse as well:** there it was said that the Book is in the *ummu'l-kitāb* — that is, in **the origin**. **The sūra asks the same thing of both the Book and the human being: is your ground in the origin, or in the traces?**

### فَإِنَّهُۥ سَيَهْدِينِ — the sentence that looks to the future

**The verb is in the imperfect and has a *sīn* at its head: *sa-yahdīn* — "He will guide me".**

***Sīn* in Arabic conveys the near future.**

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** Abraham does not say "I am on the right way." **He says "He will guide me."**

**Guidance, that is, is set down not as a property held in hand but as a work that is awaited.**

**And this stands in exact opposition to the sentence in the twenty-second verse:**

| Verse | Sentence | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | *innā … la-**muhtadūn*** | **Nominal sentence** — a fixed state, done and settled |
| 27 | *fa-innahu **sa-yahdīn*** | **Verbal sentence + future** — still to happen |

**The difference is at the level of grammar, and for that reason it is a verifiable datum.** In Arabic a nominal sentence conveys continuity and fixity, a verbal sentence occurrence and renewal.

**I record this as my own reading:** those who defend the proof from the forefathers speak of guidance as **something they possess**; Abraham as **something that will be done for him.**

## 43/28-30 — وَجَعَلَهَا كَلِمَةًۢ بَاقِيَةً فِى عَقِبِهِۦ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ · بَلْ مَتَّعْتُ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ وَءَابَآءَهُمْ حَتَّىٰ جَآءَهُمُ ٱلْحَقُّ وَرَسُولٌ مُّبِينٌ · وَلَمَّا جَآءَهُمُ ٱلْحَقُّ قَالُوا۟ هَٰذَا سِحْرٌ وَإِنَّا بِهِۦ كَٰفِرُونَ

*Wa jaʿalahā kalimatan bāqiyatan fī ʿaqibihi laʿallahum yarjiʿūn · Bal mattaʿtu hāʾulāʾi wa ābāʾahum ḥattā jāʾahumu'l-ḥaqqu wa rasūlun mubīn · Wa lammā jāʾahumu'l-ḥaqqu qālū hādhā siḥrun wa innā bihi kāfirūn*

"He made it **an abiding word among those who came after him** — that they might return. But I let these and their forefathers **enjoy their portion**, until the truth and a plain messenger came to them. And when the truth came to them they said: **'This is sorcery, and we reject it.'**"

### وَجَعَلَهَا — the fourth *jaʿala*

**The verb comes for the fourth time in the sūra, and the agent has changed again: this time it is God, and the object is a *word*.**

**What does the pronoun *hā* refer to?** Here the exegetes transmit two readings:

| Reading | What the pronoun refers to |
|---|---|
| 1 | **To the sentence of disavowal** — to the words "I am quit of what you serve" |
| 2 | **To the word of tawḥīd** — to the belief in one God, which is the substance of the sentence |

**Both come out at the same place, and I do not impose a preference.**

### كَلِمَةًۢ بَاقِيَةً — an abiding word

The root **ب-ق-ي**: to remain, to be left over, to continue. *Bāqī* — that which remains.

**The expression is worth recording, and the arrangement should be dwelt on:**

| Element | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| *kalimatan* | **A word** — not a building, an institution or a property |
| *bāqiyatan* | **Abiding** |
| *fī ʿaqibih* | **Among those who come after him** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the three elements stand together:** what remains of Abraham is not named as a building, a piece of land or a fortune. **It is named as a sentence.**

**And this is directly related to the sūra's question of measure:** the sūra will shortly be speaking of silver ceilings, golden stairways and gold bracelets. **Here, what the abiding thing is, is stated — and it is not material.**

**There is also a reversal, and I record it:** in the twenty-second verse the other side said they had inherited something from their forefathers — *āthārihim* (traces). **In the twenty-eighth verse too there is a handing down, but what is handed down is not the shape of a footprint but the content of a word.**

| | What the other side inherited | What Abraham left |
|---|---|---|
| What | *Āthār* — **traces** | *Kalima* — **a word** |
| Its content | None, only a direction | **There is one** |
| Its work | To follow | *Laʿallahum yarjiʿūn* — **to return** |

**The last row matters:** the ground the verse gives is *yarjiʿūn* (they may return) — that is, not **to follow** but **to return.** The word does not set them on a road, it leaves open a possibility of turning back.

The root **ر-ج-ع**: to turn back. **And the root will come again at the end of the sūra:** *wa ilayhi turjaʿūn* (85) — "and to Him you will be returned". **The same root, in one place for a person's own returning, in another for the return that is not chosen.**

### فِى عَقِبِهِۦ — those who come after

The root **ع-ق-ب** occurred in the twenty-fifth verse as *ʿāqiba*; here it comes as *ʿaqib*. **The concrete sense of the root: the heel.** *ʿAqib* — the heel, and from there **descent, the generation that comes after.**

**The concrete picture of the word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the word used for descent is **the part of the foot that stays behind.** A generation, that is, is named not as something that goes in front but as **a trace left behind.**

**And in the twenty-second verse the other side too said *āthār* (traces).** Both words come from the same picture: the foot and its print. **The difference lies in what the print carries.**

### بَلْ مَتَّعْتُ — and the change of person

**Something happens in the twenty-ninth verse and must be recorded: the verb shifts to the first person singular.**

***Mattaʿtu*** — "I let them enjoy". **Throughout the sūra the first person plural had been used (*jaʿalnā*, *arsalnā*, *ansharnā*, *ahlaknā*, *intaqamnā*).**

**This shift is a known Qurʾānic device, and the lexicographers explain it thus:** the plural pattern conveys majesty and breadth; the shift to the singular attaches the act **directly.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure; I do not present it as a fixed rule.**

The root **م-ت-ع**: to benefit from something, to draw profit from it; and *matāʿ* — a passing benefit, provision for the road, goods. **The word will return in the thirty-fifth verse: *matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā*.**

**And the work of the sentence must be recorded:** *bal mattaʿtu hāʾulāʾi wa ābāʾahum* — "but I let these and their forefathers enjoy their portion."

***Bal* here makes a correction:** the word Abraham left was a possibility of return; but what happened was not return, it was **the continuation of enjoyment.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the sentence is linked with *ḥattā*:** *ḥattā jāʾahumu'l-ḥaqq* — "until the truth came to them". **The verse, that is, sets a long period of ease and the arrival of a warning one after the other.** And in the twenty-third verse the *mutrafūn* had already been named. **The two verses say the same thing: the longer the period of enjoyment, the harsher the way the warning that comes is met.**

### هَٰذَا سِحْرٌ وَإِنَّا بِهِۦ كَٰفِرُونَ

**The answer is two sentences, and the relation between them must be recorded:**

| Sentence | What it does |
|---|---|
| *Hādhā siḥrun* | **Naming** — putting a name to what has come |
| *Wa innā bihi kāfirūn* | **The announcement of a position** — the declaration of refusal |

**This double structure was present in the twenty-fourth verse of the sūra too:** there also the answer did not touch the content, and a position was announced outright (*innā bimā ursiltum bihi kāfirūn*). **The same pattern twice.**

**The charge of "sorcery" is the objection the Qurʾān records most often**, and it was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/7. The finding recorded there was this: *"the other side does not say 'we do not understand'; it accepts the effect and rejects the source."*

**And the same stance was recorded in `054-kamer.md`** (*siḥrun mustamirr*).

**I add here what is particular to al-Zukhruf:** what is here called "sorcery" is, by the verse's own naming, **al-ḥaqq**. The sentence is built thus: *wa lammā jāʾahumu'l-**ḥaqqu** qālū hādhā **siḥrun***.

**After putting the name to what has come, that is, the verse reports the name the other side puts to it.** Two names side by side: **the truth** and **sorcery.** This is an observation about the arrangement, and it can be verified.

## 43/31 — وَقَالُوا۟ لَوْلَا نُزِّلَ هَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانُ عَلَىٰ رَجُلٍ مِّنَ ٱلْقَرْيَتَيْنِ عَظِيمٍ

*Wa qālū law lā nuzzila hādha'l-qurʾānu ʿalā rajulin mina'l-qaryatayni ʿaẓīm*

"And they said: **'Should this Qurʾān not have been sent down upon some great man of the two towns?'**"

**The fifth block of the sūra opens here, and this block (31-35) is its densest part. The name of the sūra will occur here too.**

### The construction of the objection

***Law lā* here is the particle of *taḥḍīḍ*: the pattern that asks why something was not done, conveying reproach.**

**The subject of the sentence is the Qurʾān, its predicate *nuzzila* (should it not have been sent down), and the condition is looked for in a person: *rajulin … ʿaẓīm*.**

**The point to notice, and I record it:** the objection is directed **not at the content of the Qurʾān but at its recipient.** What the Qurʾān says is not disputed; **to whom it was given** is.

### مِّنَ ٱلْقَرْيَتَيْنِ — the two towns

**A dual pattern: *al-qaryatayn* — "the two towns".**

**Which towns these are is not stated in the verse.** In the classical commentaries it is widely transmitted that they are **Mecca and Ṭāʾif**. **I transmit this as it has been transmitted; since the verse gives no name, I do not present it as certain knowledge.**

**Likewise, various names are transmitted in the classical commentaries for who is meant by "a great man". I do not report them, because the verse gives no name and I cannot issue a ruling on the soundness of the names.**

**That the verse gives no name is itself a piece of information, and I record this as my own reading:** had a name been given, the matter would have been confined to that person. **Because no name is given, it is the measure itself that becomes the subject.**

### عَظِيم — the root of the word

The root **ع-ظ-م**: **bone.** *ʿAẓm* — bone. And from there **greatness**: the largeness of a thing's skeleton, of its frame.

**The concrete sense of the root is meaningful here, and I give it as an observation:** *ʿaẓīm*, in the picture of the root, means **large of frame.** The word conveys, that is, a greatness that is measurable and visible from outside.

**And this is exactly the measure the objection uses: visible greatness.**

**The objection is recorded in many places in the Qurʾān and has been cited earlier in the corpus:**

- This verse was cited directly in `083-mutaffifin.md` and `104-humeze.md`.
- In `054-kamer.md` it was given under the heading "the objection about selection", together with Ṣād 38/8: *a-unzila ʿalayhi'dh-dhikru min bayninā* — "has the reminder been sent down on him, out of all of us?"

**And in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/11 the same logic was recorded in another sentence** — bringing it over here for comparison is called for, and the comparison is fruitful:

| | **al-Aḥqāf 46/11** | **al-Zukhruf 43/31** |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence | *Law kāna khayran mā sabaqūnā ilayh* | *Law lā nuzzila … ʿalā rajulin … ʿaẓīm* |
| Of whom it is said | About **those who believe** | About **the Prophet** |
| The logic | What is good comes **first to the superior** | What is valuable is given **to the great man** |
| Direction of inference | From the recipient → **to the value of the thing** | From the thing → **to the person who ought to receive it** |
| Shared assumption | **Value is read off the position of the one who carries it** | The same |

**The finding recorded in al-Aḥqāf was this:** *"the objection is not an argument but an assumption about ranking."* **In al-Zukhruf the same assumption is built from the opposite direction.**

**I record the difference as my own reading:** the objection in al-Aḥqāf draws a **conclusion** (if these people have received it, it is not good); the objection in al-Zukhruf frames a **demand** (then it should have been given to so-and-so). **The first looks backward, the second forward. But the scale both rest on is the same.**

**And this too must be recorded:** neither objection **contains any information about a prophet or a message.** Both are assumptions about social ranking.

## 43/32 — أَهُمْ يَقْسِمُونَ رَحْمَتَ رَبِّكَ ۚ نَحْنُ قَسَمْنَا بَيْنَهُم مَّعِيشَتَهُمْ فِى ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا ۚ وَرَفَعْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ دَرَجَٰتٍ لِّيَتَّخِذَ بَعْضُهُم بَعْضًا سُخْرِيًّا ۗ وَرَحْمَتُ رَبِّكَ خَيْرٌ مِّمَّا يَجْمَعُونَ

*A-hum yaqsimūna raḥmata rabbik · Naḥnu qasamnā baynahum maʿīshatahum fi'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā · Wa rafaʿnā baʿḍahum fawqa baʿḍin darajātin li-yattakhidha baʿḍuhum baʿḍan sukhriyyā · Wa raḥmatu rabbika khayrun mimmā yajmaʿūn*

"**Is it they who divide out your Lord's mercy?** It is **We** who divided out among them their livelihood in the life of this world, and **raised some of them above others in degrees** — that some of them might put others to work. **And your Lord's mercy is better than what they gather.**"

**This is the densest verse in the sūra, and it consists of four separate sentences. I treat them one by one.**

### The first sentence: أَهُمْ يَقْسِمُونَ رَحْمَتَ رَبِّكَ

**The question is one of denial, and its arrangement must be recorded: the subject (*hum*) comes before the verb.**

**In an ordinary Arabic verbal sentence the order is verb then agent. Fronting the agent conveys restriction (*qaṣr*).**

**The question, then, is not "do they divide out?" but, exactly: "is it *they* who divide out?"**

**I record this as a point of word order, and the next sentence confirms it:** *naḥnu qasamnā* — there too the subject is fronted. **The two sentences stand facing each other:**

| Sentence | What is fronted | What |
|---|---|---|
| *A-**hum** yaqsimūn* | **They** | The question — refused |
| ***Naḥnu** qasamnā* | **We** | The answer — settled |

**The same pattern, one after the other, with opposite subjects.** This is a verifiable datum of arrangement.

**This verse was cited in `052-tur.md`:** *"Is it they who divide out your Lord's mercy?"* (al-Zukhruf 43/32). **I complete that link here.**

### The second sentence: نَحْنُ قَسَمْنَا بَيْنَهُم مَّعِيشَتَهُمْ

**And here something happens that needs to be treated in detail.**

**The question was "is it they who distribute prophethood?" The answer speaks not of prophethood but of *livelihood*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two sentences have different subjects:** the verse makes its evidence out of something the other side already sees in its own life. **Nobody arranges the distribution of provision for himself.** Who is born where, and with what means, is not a choice.

**The answer, then, is this: "your hand does not even touch the distribution nearest to you; how is it that you are deciding about the greater one?"**

**This is the same as the principle of "the distance of the proof" recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`.**

**مَعِيشَة — root ع-ي-ش:** to live, to make a living. *Maʿīsha* — the means of living, what makes living possible. **The word differs from *ḥayāt*: *ḥayāt* is being alive, *maʿīsha* is the means of getting a living.**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "your provision" or "your property"; it says **"your livelihood"** — that is, the means by which life is kept going.

### The third sentence: وَرَفَعْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ دَرَجَٰتٍ

**And this sentence must be read carefully, because it is easily misunderstood.**

**The verse does not deny the fact of difference among human beings. On the contrary, it records it: there are degrees, and it is He who raises.**

**But the continuation of the sentence says what this difference is for — and what it says is diametrically opposed to the measure the other side uses.**

The root **د-ر-ج**: to advance step by step. *Daraja* — a step. *Tadrīj* — by stages. **The word comes in the plural and indefinite, *darajāt*: neither number nor kind is stated.**

**And note: the sentence comes immediately after the qualification *fi'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā*.** What is ranked, that is, is livelihood **in the life of this world**. **In al-Aḥqāf 46/19 another *darajāt* occurred, and that was a different thing:** *wa li-kullin darajātun mimmā ʿamilū* — **degrees according to what was done.** It was treated in `046-ahkaf.md`.

**I set the two *darajāt* out in a table, because confusing them destroys the main question of the sūra:**

| Place | What the *darajāt* go by | Field |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Zukhruf 43/32** | **Livelihood** — given, not chosen | The life of this world |
| **al-Aḥqāf 46/19** | **Deeds** — done | Recompense |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two verses' own statements:** there are two distinct rankings in the Qurʾān, and neither stands in for the other. **The sūra's whole objection is to the first being used in place of the second.**

### لِّيَتَّخِذَ بَعْضُهُم بَعْضًا سُخْرِيًّا — the disagreement

**The exegetes differ over this expression, and the disagreement must be set out in a table.**

**The word is from the root س-خ-ر, and the two branches of the root were treated in `069-hakka.md` and `049-hucurat.md`:** making subservient / pressing into service, and mocking. **The shared core was given there as:** *"to put someone into a position outside his own will."*

| View | What *sukhriyyā* is | The sense of the sentence | Its ground |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Putting to work** | **Employing** one another, a division of labour | The degrees exist so that people may put one another to work (and so that life may go on) | The *sakhkhara* branch; the sense of *taskhīr* is common in the Qurʾān (Ibrāhīm 14/33, al-Jāthiya 45/12) |
| **2 — Mockery** | **Belittling** one another | Those who are raised make objects of mockery of those below | The *sakhira min* branch |

**A variant reading is also bound up with this disagreement and must be transmitted:**

| Reading | Written form | To whom it is ascribed |
|---|---|---|
| *sukhriyyā* (with *ḍamma*) | سُخْرِيًّا | The majority |
| *sikhriyyā* (with *kasra*) | سِخْرِيًّا | A group of the imams of the readings |

**Some of the lexicographers connect the reading with *ḍamma* to the sense "pressing into service" and the reading with *kasra* to the sense "mockery"; others record that either reading can carry either sense.** **I give this distinction as it has been transmitted; I do not present it as a fixed rule.**

**I do not impose a preference. But I record this, and I mark it as my own reading:**

**On the first reading the verse states a *function*** — difference produces mutual need; no one can do everything himself. **On this reading the degrees are not an order of superiority but the ground of a division of labour.**

**On the second reading the verse states a *result*** — the one who is raised makes light of the one below.

**And the two readings do not exclude one another:** the same fact (difference) can give rise both to a division of labour and to mockery. **In the seventh verse of the sūra *yastahziʾūn* occurs, and in the fifty-fourth *fa'stakhaffa* — the sūra, that is, already works the second possibility with other words.**

**And finally, I record once more the link with the thirteenth verse:** there, *sakhkhara lanā hādhā* — God had made the mount subservient to the human being, and the human being glorified Him in the face of it. **Here the human being becomes the subject of the same root towards another human being.** **The same root on two levels: vertical (God–human–animal) and horizontal (human–human).**

### The fourth sentence: وَرَحْمَتُ رَبِّكَ خَيْرٌ مِّمَّا يَجْمَعُونَ

**And the verse binds the whole picture it has built with a single sentence.**

**A comparative pattern (*khayrun min*) is used, and two sides are set out:**

| Side | Word | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| On one side | *raḥmatu rabbik* | — (what is given) |
| On the other | *mimmā **yajmaʿūn*** | **What they gather** |

The root **ج-م-ع**: to collect, to bring together. **And that the verb comes in the imperfect conveys continuity: "what they keep gathering".**

**The choice of word is worth recording and connects with `104-humeze.md`.** There *jamaʿa mālan wa ʿaddadah* (he gathered wealth and counted it over) was treated, and the finding recorded was this: **that value is read off the position of the one who carries it.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence compares mercy with wealth — that is, it **puts them on the same scale.** And this is speaking in the objection's own language: the objection had set up the measure of "a great man"; the answer likewise sets up a comparison, but changes what is placed on the scale.

## 43/33-35 — وَلَوْلَآ أَن يَكُونَ ٱلنَّاسُ أُمَّةً وَٰحِدَةً لَّجَعَلْنَا لِمَن يَكْفُرُ بِٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ لِبُيُوتِهِمْ سُقُفًا مِّن فِضَّةٍ وَمَعَارِجَ عَلَيْهَا يَظْهَرُونَ · وَلِبُيُوتِهِمْ أَبْوَٰبًا وَسُرُرًا عَلَيْهَا يَتَّكِـُٔونَ · وَزُخْرُفًا ۚ وَإِن كُلُّ ذَٰلِكَ لَمَّا مَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا ۚ وَٱلْءَاخِرَةُ عِندَ رَبِّكَ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ

*Wa law lā an yakūna'n-nāsu ummatan wāḥidatan la-jaʿalnā li-man yakfuru bi'r-Raḥmāni li-buyūtihim suqufan min fiḍḍatin wa maʿārija ʿalayhā yaẓharūn · Wa li-buyūtihim abwāban wa sururan ʿalayhā yattakiʾūn · Wa zukhrufā · Wa in kullu dhālika lammā matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā · Wa'l-ākhiratu ʿinda rabbika li'l-muttaqīn*

"**Were it not that people would become a single community**, We would have made, for the houses of those who reject al-Raḥmān, **ceilings of silver** and **stairways** by which they might climb; and doors to their houses, and **couches** on which to recline — and **gold ornament.** All of this is nothing but **the passing enjoyment of the life of this world.** The hereafter, with your Lord, is **for those who guard themselves.**"

**The name of the sūra occurs in this verse, and the whole block is the summit of the question of measure.**

### The construction of the condition: لَوْلَآ أَن يَكُونَ ٱلنَّاسُ أُمَّةً وَٰحِدَةً

**The sentence opens with a *law lā* condition — this time not in the sense "why was it not so" but in the sense "were it not so" (the *law lā* of prevention, *imtināʿ*): the presence of one thing has caused another not to happen.**

**What does the expression *ummatan wāḥidatan* describe?** Here the exegetes differ:

| Reading | What *ummatan wāḥidatan* is | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A single community united in rejection** | It was not given so that people, seeing the wealth, should not slide wholesale into rejection |
| 2 | **A single undifferentiated mass** | So that the difference should not disappear |

**The first reading is the reading of the majority; I give it as it has been transmitted.**

**And this must be recorded: the word *umma* occurs for the third time in the sūra**, and this time in its primary sense: a community. **In the twenty-second and twenty-third verses it was in the sense "the line followed".**

### What the sentence does — and how harsh it is

**The verse says this: were there no objection to it, houses with silver ceilings would be given to those who reject.**

**Wealth, that is, is named not as a sign of nearness but in exactly the opposite context.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the conditional structure of the sentence:** the verse does not merely say that the distribution of wealth **is not an indicator of value**; it says that it is withheld because **giving it would carry an objection.**

**And this is the final answer to the objection in the thirty-first verse:** the objection said that the message ought to have been given to "a great man". **This verse shows how cheap the thing counted as the measure of greatness is: something that could have been handed out wholesale, were there no objection to it.**

### سُقُفًا مِّن فِضَّةٍ — silver ceilings

The root **س-ق-ف**: to cover, to close over. *Saqf* — a ceiling, a roof. **The Qurʾān names the sky *saqf* as well:** *wa jaʿalna's-samāʾa **saqfan** maḥfūẓā* (al-Anbiyāʾ 21/32).

**This link is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the same word indicates in one place the sky and in another the ceiling of a house. **What the verse describes is a house that puts a silver ceiling between itself and the sky.**

**فِضَّة** — root **ف-ض-ض**: to scatter, to separate, to break up. *Infaḍḍa* — it dispersed. **The use of this word for silver is explained by the lexicographers by the metal's being workable and divisible into parts.** *Fiḍḍa*, the metal that can be poured out and scattered.

### وَمَعَارِجَ عَلَيْهَا يَظْهَرُونَ — stairways

The root **ع-ر-ج**: to rise, to climb; and *aʿraj* — lame (one who rises to one side as he walks).

**This verse was already cited in `070-mearic.md`, and this was recorded there:**

> *"مَعَارِج — it occurs once more in the Qurʾān, and there in its concrete sense: '…[We would have made] ceilings of silver for their houses, and stairways by which they might climb' (al-Zukhruf 43/33). Al-Zukhruf 43/33 gives the concrete form of the word: the stairway by which a house is climbed."*

**I rely on that treatment and add here the link within the sūra.**

***Yaẓharūn* — from the root ظ-ه-ر: to come out on top, to become visible.** **The same root occurred in the thirteenth verse as *ẓuhūrihi* (their backs).**

**I record this as a datum of the text:** in the thirteenth verse a human being climbed onto the **back** of a mount and glorified God. **In the thirty-third he climbs by a stairway onto the top of his house.** The same root, two ascents — one named with thanks, the other with display.

### وَسُرُرًا عَلَيْهَا يَتَّكِـُٔونَ — couches

**سُرُر** — the plural of *sarīr*. The root **س-ر-ر**: the lexicographers reckon this word to be from the same root as *surūr* (joy) and make the link thus: **a *sarīr* is a place of joy and ease.**

**The word occurred in `056-vakia.md` in a description of the Garden** (*ʿalā sururin mawḍūna*). **And this leads to the most striking aspect of the block.**

***Yattakiʾūn* — Form VIII from the root و-ك-أ: to recline, to lean.** **The same verb occurs again and again in the Qurʾān's descriptions of the Garden** (al-Kahf 18/31, Yā-Sīn 36/56, al-Raḥmān 55/54, al-Wāqiʿa 56/16).

**What is enumerated in the thirty-fourth verse, then — couches, reclining — are the words of the descriptions of the Garden.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the words are shared:** in describing what could be given in this world, the verse speaks **with the words of what will be given in the hereafter.** **And immediately afterwards it sets down the difference: *wa in kullu dhālika lammā matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā*.**

**The difference is not in the object; it is in how long the object lasts.**

### وَزُخْرُفًا — the name of the sūra

The root **ز-خ-ر-ف** is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.

**The root is quadriliteral, and the senses the lexicographers give are these:**

- **زُخْرُف** — gold; and from there **ornament, decoration, gilding.**
- **زَخْرَفَ البَيْت** — he adorned the house, decked it out.
- **زُخْرُف القَوْل** — the ornamented kind of speech, polished speech. **The Qurʾān uses this construct in al-Anʿām 6/112:** *yūḥī baʿḍuhum ilā baʿḍin **zukhrufa'l-qawli** ghurūrā* — "they whisper to one another gilded speech, to deceive."
- **أَخَذَتِ الأَرْضُ زُخْرُفَهَا** — the earth took on its ornament; **the Qurʾān uses this in Yūnus 10/24:** *ḥattā idhā akhadhati'l-arḍu **zukhrufahā** wa'zzayyanat* — and there too what immediately follows is the account of everything being mown down and gone.

**The word's relation to the verb *zakhara* (ز-خ-ر) is recorded by the lexicographers, and the relation is worth dwelling on.**

***Zakhara'l-baḥr*** — the sea swelled, its water rose, it brimmed over. ***Zakhara'n-nabt*** — the plant grew rank, shot up.

**Some of the lexicographers attach the word *zukhruf* to this root and explain it thus: ornament is a thing's *swelling*, its appearing fuller than it is.** **I transmit this derivational relation as the lexicographers record it; I do not present it as a settled etymological ruling.**

**But once the relation is made, the picture of the word becomes this, and I give it as my own reading:**

**Swollen water is not as deep as it looks.** When the sea swells its volume grows, but the swelling does not come from the water itself — it comes from the motion beneath it. **Ornament likewise enlarges a thing's volume, not the thing.**

**And this explains why the sūra's name is this word — I record it as an observation:** from beginning to end the sūra works the difference between **the volume a thing appears to have** and **its own weight.**

| Verse | What swells | Its real weight |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | Being raised amid ornament | The value of a human being is not measured from here |
| 22 | The tracks of the forefathers | A direction with no content |
| 31 | "The great man of the two towns" | Visible greatness |
| 33-35 | Silver ceiling, gold ornament | *Matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* |
| 53 | Gold bracelets | Not a proof of prophethood |

**The arrangement must be recorded too: *wa zukhrufā* comes as a separate verse, as a one-word addition.**

**After the enumeration is finished — ceiling, stairway, door, couch — "and gold ornament" is said, as a verse standing on its own.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement:** what is added when the list is done is not a thing that does any work. A ceiling covers, a stairway raises, a door admits, a couch supports. ***Zukhruf* does no work at all.** **And that is the name of the sūra.**

### وَإِن كُلُّ ذَٰلِكَ لَمَّا مَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا

**The construction of the sentence carries a question of variant readings, and it must be transmitted:**

| Reading | Written form | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| *lammā* (with *shadda*) | لَمَّا | *In* as negation + *lammā* as exception = "…is nothing but this" |
| *la-mā* (without *shadda*) | لَمَا | *In* as emphasis (the lightened form of *inna*) + *lām* + *mā* = "…is truly this" |

**Both readings have been transmitted, and the sense comes out at the same place.** The difference is at the level of structure: in the first the sentence is negation plus exception, in the second affirmation plus emphasis.

**I give this as a note on the readings; since it produces no change in meaning I do not set up a question of preference.**

The root **م-ت-ع** occurred in the twenty-ninth verse as *mattaʿtu*. **The core of the word: to draw a passing benefit from something.** *Matāʿ* — provision for the road, portable goods, a temporary advantage.

**And the lexicographers record one more branch of the root: *amtaʿa'l-ḥabl* — the rope stretched, drew out.** **In the root, that is, there is an idea of *lengthening and then ending*.**

### وَٱلْءَاخِرَةُ عِندَ رَبِّكَ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ — the measure set in its place

**And the block closes with a single sentence.**

**The arrangement must be recorded:**

| Element | What |
|---|---|
| *wa'l-ākhiratu* | **The subject** — fronted |
| *ʿinda rabbika* | **Where** — with your Lord |
| *li'l-muttaqīn* | **Whose** — those who guard themselves |

**The interposed *ʿinda rabbika* is worth recording.** The sentence could have been built as "the hereafter is for those who guard themselves". "With your Lord" comes in between.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the seemingly unnecessary position of the expression:** the things enumerated throughout the block were **in houses** — ceiling, stairway, door, couch. **The closing sentence gives the name of another place: "with your Lord".** Two locations are set side by side.

The root **و-ق-ي**: to protect, to set a shield in front of something. **Taqwā, in the picture of the root, is *to protect oneself with something*.** The root has been treated in many places in the corpus.

**And the choice of word closes the question of measure:** throughout the block the measures that separate people were enumerated — wealth, rank, lineage, appearance. **The measure enumerated at the close is an *act*: guarding oneself.** Not something given, but something done.

**And this completes the distinction between the two *darajāt* in the thirty-second verse:** the ranking in this world is given (*qasamnā*, *rafaʿnā*); the recompense in the hereafter is attached to an attribute (*li'l-muttaqīn*).

### The block as a whole — a note

**The fifth block (31-35) makes a complete turn in five verses:**

| Verse | Step |
|---|---|
| 31 | **The objection** — value is read off rank |
| 32 | **Answer I** — the distribution is not in your hands |
| 33-34 | **Answer II** — this distribution is not a sign of value; were there no objection it would have been given wholesale |
| 35a | **Naming** — all of this is *matāʿ* |
| 35b | **Setting in place** — what matters is elsewhere, by another measure |

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the block opens with an objection and closes with a measure, and in between the **thing itself** on which the objection rests is taken up — not by rejecting it, but by **stating its price.**

## 43/36-37 — وَمَن يَعْشُ عَن ذِكْرِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ نُقَيِّضْ لَهُۥ شَيْطَٰنًا فَهُوَ لَهُۥ قَرِينٌ · وَإِنَّهُمْ لَيَصُدُّونَهُمْ عَنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ وَيَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ

*Wa man yaʿshu ʿan dhikri'r-Raḥmāni nuqayyiḍ lahu shayṭānan fa-huwa lahu qarīn · Wa innahum la-yaṣuddūnahum ʿani's-sabīli wa yaḥsabūna annahum muhtadūn*

"Whoever **narrows his eyes** against the reminder of al-Raḥmān, We **fit** a satan to him, and he becomes his **inseparable companion.** They turn these away from the road, while these **suppose themselves to be on the right way.**"

### يَعْشُ — root: ع-ش-و

**The root occurred in `079-naziat.md` as *ʿashiyya* (evening); I rely on that treatment.** **Here I open the branch of the root that has to do with the eye, because that is what is at work in this verse.**

**The concrete sense of the root: the weakening of the eye, not being able to see well in twilight.**

- **عَشَا / عَشِيَ** — his eye grew weak; especially **not being able to see at night or in twilight.**
- **الأَعْشَى** — night-blind; one who sees by day and cannot see at dusk. (The by-name of the famous Arab poet **al-Aʿshā** comes from this; it is an example the lexicographers cite often.)
- **عِشَاء** — the night prayer; the hour when the light has withdrawn altogether.
- **عَشَاء** — the evening meal.

**And the root has a second branch, and it matters in this verse:** *ʿashā ila'n-nār* — **he went towards the fire, narrowing his eyes.** That is, the turning of one who sees poorly towards a light.

**Now the construction of the verb in this verse:**

***Yaʿshu ʿan dhikri'r-Raḥmān* — it comes with the preposition *ʿan*.**

**This gives rise to two readings, and the exegetes differ:**

| Reading | What *ʿan* conveys | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Turning away** | **To turn one's face from** the reminder, to turn one's back on it |
| 2 | **Weakness of sight** | **To look at** the reminder **with narrowed eyes**, to look and not see |

**The first reading takes it as equivalent to *aʿraḍa ʿan* (he turned away). The second preserves the eye-sense of the root.**

**I do not impose a preference. But the concrete core of the root supports the second reading, and I record that as my own reading:**

**The verse does not say "the one who is blind".** The word *aʿmā* (blind) will come separately in the fortieth verse: *aw tahdi'l-ʿumy*. **The word chosen here is the word used for an eye that sees but cannot make out.**

**The difference is this, and it matters:** the blind man sees nothing. **The *aʿshā* sees — but sees blurred, and misidentifies what he sees.**

**And the continuation of the verse says exactly this: *wa yaḥsabūna annahum muhtadūn* — "they suppose themselves to be on the right way."**

**Between the defect of the eye in the thirty-sixth verse and the supposition in the thirty-seventh, that is, there is a direct link:** the one who sees blurred does not know that he is not seeing; **he supposes that he does see.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses come one after the other.**

### نُقَيِّضْ — root: ق-ي-ض

**The concrete sense of the root comes in the lexicographers from the egg: *qayḍ* — the shell of an egg.** *Taqayyaḍa'l-bayḍu* — the egg broke, its shell came away.

**And the second branch of the root: to put one thing in the place of another, to exchange, to match.** *Qāyaḍahu* — he made an exchange with him. *Qayyaḍa lahu* — he **matched something to him, set it upon him, fastened it on him.**

**The lexicographers make the link between the two branches thus: the eggshell fits exactly what is inside it; and *muqāyaḍa* is likewise one thing's coming exactly level with another.** **I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

**The sense of the word in this verse: to prepare one thing to fit another exactly, to match it to it.**

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the root's core of "matching":** the verse is not speaking of a random affliction. **The word conveys a measured pairing.**

**And this is related to the sūra's question of measure — and comes from the same field as *muqrinīn* (to be a match) in the thirteenth verse.** The two words are from different roots (ق-ي-ض / ق-ر-ن) and I make no derivational link between them; **what I record is that both carry the sense of "coming level with".**

### فَهُوَ لَهُۥ قَرِينٌ — root: ق-ر-ن

**The root was treated in detail in `050-kaf.md` at 50/23 and 50/27; I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.** What was recorded there: *"Concrete sense: to bind two things to one another, to pair them. Qarn — horn; and generation."*

**In Qāf *qarīn* occurred twice, and there too there was a scene of dispute** (*qāla qarīnuhu rabbanā mā aṭghaytuhu*, 50/27).

**What is particular to al-Zukhruf is this, and it must be recorded: here it is told how the companion comes.**

| | **Qāf 50/23-27** | **al-Zukhruf 43/36-38** |
|---|---|---|
| Where the *qarīn* appears | In the scene of reckoning | **In this world, in the course of things** |
| How he came | Not stated | *Nuqayyiḍ lahu* — **he is matched to him** |
| Its cause | Not stated | ***Yaʿshu ʿan dhikri'r-Raḥmān*** |
| Its end | A dispute | *Yā layta baynī wa baynaka…* |

**And the structure of the conditional sentence must be recorded:**

> **The condition:** *man yaʿshu ʿan dhikri'r-Raḥmān* — **the human act**
> **The apodosis:** *nuqayyiḍ lahu shayṭānan* — **the consequence**

**The order, then, is settled: first the narrowing of the eye, then the coming of the companion.** I record this as an observation about the arrangement; this is the order the verse establishes.

### وَيَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ

The root **ح-س-ب**: to count, to reckon; and from there **to suppose** (to make a reckoning in the mind).

**The link between the two branches of the root is worth recording:** *reckoning* and *supposing* are from the same root. **The lexicographers explain it thus: to suppose is to make a reckoning with the data in hand — but with incomplete data.**

**And the word *muhtadūn* comes for the third time in the sūra.** The three-stage pattern set out above (in the discussion of 43/22) is completed here:

| Verse | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | The text | *Laʿallakum tahtadūn* — **the possibility** |
| 22 | The other side | *Innā … muhtadūn* — **the claim** |
| 37 | The text | *Yaḥsabūna annahum muhtadūn* — **the name of the claim** |
| 49 | The people of Pharaoh | *Innanā la-muhtadūn* — **the promise given** |

**Four occurrences, one root.** This is a verifiable datum of the text.

## 43/38-39 — حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا جَآءَنَا قَالَ يَٰلَيْتَ بَيْنِى وَبَيْنَكَ بُعْدَ ٱلْمَشْرِقَيْنِ فَبِئْسَ ٱلْقَرِينُ · وَلَن يَنفَعَكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ إِذ ظَّلَمْتُمْ أَنَّكُمْ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ مُشْتَرِكُونَ

*Ḥattā idhā jāʾanā qāla yā layta baynī wa baynaka buʿda'l-mashriqayni fa-biʾsa'l-qarīn · Wa lan yanfaʿakumu'l-yawma idh ẓalamtum annakum fi'l-ʿadhābi mushtarikūn*

"Until, when he comes to Us, he says: **'Would that between me and you were the distance of the two easts!'** What an evil companion! **Since you did wrong, it is of no use to you today that you share in the punishment.**"

### بُعْدَ ٱلْمَشْرِقَيْنِ — "as far as the two easts"

**The expression is an idiom, and the lexicographers explain it separately.**

**The word is dual: *al-mashriqayn* — "the two easts".** And here there is a well-known usage of Arabic: **when two opposites are put in the dual, the name of one of them often stands for both.**

**The familiar examples the lexicographers give:**

| Construction | What it stands for | Its sense |
|---|---|---|
| *al-ʿUmarān* | Abū Bakr and ʿUmar | Both, under the name of one |
| *al-qamarān* | The sun and the moon | Both together |
| *al-abawān* | Mother and father | Both together |

**The lexicographers call this usage *taghlīb* (bringing one forward and including the other in it).**

**Accordingly two readings are transmitted for *al-mashriqayn*:**

| Reading | What *al-mashriqayn* is | The measure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **East and west** — by *taghlīb* | The **two furthest known points** on the earth |
| 2 | **Two points of sunrise** — the extremes at which the sun rises in summer and in winter | An observable **yearly swing** |

**The first reading is the reading of the majority and is supported within the Qurʾān itself:** in al-Raḥmān 55/17 there occurs *rabbu'l-**mashriqayni** wa rabbu'l-**maghribayn*** (treated in `055-rahman.md`) — there the four extremes are enumerated separately.

**The second reading is recorded by the lexicographers as well and has an observational ground:** the point at which the sun rises swings along the horizon between two extremes over the course of the year; **this is a fact that can be followed with the naked eye and is familiar to a people that finds its way in the desert.**

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But the work of the idiom is the same on either reading, and I record it as my own reading:** the distance chosen as the measure is **the greatest distance the person can know.** The sentence does not say "were it very far"; **it says "as far as the furthest known".**

**And this connects with the sūra's question of measure:** distances and magnitudes have been argued about throughout the sūra — a great man, high degrees, silver ceilings. **Here a person uses, for the first time, the limit of his own scale.**

### يَٰلَيْتَ — the pattern of regret

***Layta* in Arabic is used for a wish whose fulfilment is not possible.** (*Laʿalla*, by contrast, conveys what is possible — as in the third and tenth verses.)

**This distinction is worth recording and can be verified within the sūra:**

| Particle | Verse | What |
|---|---|---|
| *laʿalla* | 3, 10, 28, 48 | **What is hoped for** — still possible |
| *layta* | 38 | **What is wished for** — now impossible |

**Two particles in the same sūra, two times.** *Laʿalla* in this world, *layta* afterwards.

### فَبِئْسَ ٱلْقَرِينُ — whose sentence is it?

**The exegetes differ over whose words this sentence is:**

| View | Who says it |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The person himself** — the continuation of his words |
| 2 | **The text** — a verdict added to the person's words |

**Both are possible, and I do not impose a preference.** *Biʾsa* in Arabic is a **verb of dispraise** (a frozen verb conveying blame) and is generally used where the text delivers its own verdict.

### أَنَّكُمْ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ مُشْتَرِكُونَ

**The thirty-ninth verse makes an addition to the thirty-eighth, and the addition is striking.**

**What is said is this: the sharing is of no use.**

The root **ش-ر-ك**: to be a partner, to have a share in something. **The root has been treated in the corpus** (`072-cin.md`, `073-muzzemmil.md`, `068-kalem.md`).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the sentence is built with *lan yanfaʿakum*:** in this world, sharing a burden lightens it. **The verse says that this will not work there.**

**And the interposed *idh ẓalamtum* gives the ground: "since you did wrong".** The sharing does not divide the punishment, because the act was not divided either — **each did his own act.**

## 43/40-45 — أَفَأَنتَ تُسْمِعُ ٱلصُّمَّ أَوْ تَهْدِى ٱلْعُمْىَ وَمَن كَانَ فِى ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ · فَإِمَّا نَذْهَبَنَّ بِكَ فَإِنَّا مِنْهُم مُّنتَقِمُونَ · أَوْ نُرِيَنَّكَ ٱلَّذِى وَعَدْنَٰهُمْ فَإِنَّا عَلَيْهِم مُّقْتَدِرُونَ · فَٱسْتَمْسِكْ بِٱلَّذِىٓ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْكَ ۖ إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ · وَإِنَّهُۥ لَذِكْرٌ لَّكَ وَلِقَوْمِكَ ۖ وَسَوْفَ تُسْـَٔلُونَ · وَسْـَٔلْ مَنْ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ مِن رُّسُلِنَآ أَجَعَلْنَا مِن دُونِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ءَالِهَةً يُعْبَدُونَ

*A-fa-anta tusmiʿu'ṣ-ṣumma aw tahdi'l-ʿumya wa man kāna fī ḍalālin mubīn · Fa-immā nadhhabanna bika fa-innā minhum muntaqimūn · Aw nuriyannaka'lladhī waʿadnāhum fa-innā ʿalayhim muqtadirūn · Fa'stamsik bi'lladhī ūḥiya ilayk · Innaka ʿalā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm · Wa innahu la-dhikrun laka wa li-qawmik · Wa sawfa tusʾalūn · Wa'sʾal man arsalnā min qablika min rusulinā a-jaʿalnā min dūni'r-Raḥmāni ālihatan yuʿbadūn*

"**Is it you who will make the deaf hear, or guide the blind and one who is in plain error?** Even if We take you away, We shall take retribution from them. Or We shall show you what We promised them; **We have power over them.** So **hold fast to what has been revealed to you.** You are on a straight path. It is a **reminder** for you and for your people; **you will be questioned before long.** Ask those of Our messengers whom We sent before you: **did We ever appoint gods to be served besides al-Raḥmān?**"

### أَفَأَنتَ تُسْمِعُ ٱلصُّمَّ — the arrangement of the question

**Once again the subject stands before the verb: *a-fa-anta tusmiʿu*.** The same structure was in the thirty-second verse (*a-hum yaqsimūn*).

**The fronted subject sets the direction of the question: the issue is not whether making them hear is possible but *whether you can do it*.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement, and I link it with the finding recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/35:** there the sūra confined its own task to a single word — *balāgh* (conveying). **Here the same limit is drawn by a question.**

**And the two defects chosen must be recorded: deafness and blindness.**

| Defect | Which channel | Its counterpart in the Qurʾān |
|---|---|---|
| *al-ṣumm* | **Hearing** | The channel through which speech enters |
| *al-ʿumy* | **Sight** | The channel through which evidence enters |

**Both attach to what the sūra has already set up:** in the thirty-sixth verse there was *yaʿshu* (blurred sight) — **a partial defect of vision.** Here there is *ʿumy* (blindness) — **the complete one.** **Two degrees in the same field.**

**And the finding recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/26 attaches here:** there it was recounted that the people of ʿĀd were given hearing, sight and heart, but that these were of no use, and this was recorded: *"the verse is not speaking of an impossibility; it is speaking of a possibility left unused."*

**I keep the same distinction here:** the Qurʾān uses these words not for physical impairment but for **an unused sense.** **Per the method (`USLUP.md`) I write this out plainly: the verse issues no ruling about people with impaired hearing or sight; it uses a figure, and the subject of the figure is the person whose senses work but who does not use them.**

### فَإِمَّا نَذْهَبَنَّ بِكَ … أَوْ نُرِيَنَّكَ — two possibilities

**Two verses enumerate two possibilities, and both are tied to the same outcome:**

| Possibility | Sentence | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Nadhhabanna bika* — **if We take you away** | *Fa-innā minhum muntaqimūn* |
| 2 | *Nuriyannaka'lladhī waʿadnāhum* — **if We show you** | *Fa-innā ʿalayhim muqtadirūn* |

**The structure is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** both possibilities concern **whether the Prophet sees it or not.** **The outcome does not change in either.**

**The verse, that is, says this: the coming to pass of the outcome does not depend on your witnessing it.**

***Muqtadirūn* — an active participle of Form VIII from the root ق-د-ر.** **And the root occurred in the eleventh verse as *bi-qadarin* (by a measure).** The same root: in one place the measure of water, in another having power.

### فَٱسْتَمْسِكْ بِٱلَّذِىٓ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْكَ

**And the word of the twenty-first verse returns: *istamsaka* (Form X — to hold on tightly).**

**I set out this ring above (in the discussion of 43/21); here I complete it:**

| Verse | Pattern | Who | To what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21 | *mustamsikūn* — active participle | The other side | **To a Book that does not exist** |
| 43 | *fa'stamsik* — **imperative** | The Prophet | **To what is revealed** |

**The difference is not only in content but in mood:** one is the statement of a state (inside a question), the other a command. **I record this as a datum of the text.**

**إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ** — and the expression will occur three times in the sūra: 43, 61, 64. **Two of them are in the block about Jesus.**

The root **ص-ر-ط**: the core the lexicographers give is **a wide, open road**; the word's relation to *sariṭa* (to swallow) is recorded — the road's taking into itself the one who travels on it. **The root was treated in `001-fatiha.md`.**

The root **ق-و-م** and *istiqāma* were treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/13: **to continue without swerving.**

### وَإِنَّهُۥ لَذِكْرٌ لَّكَ وَلِقَوْمِكَ ۖ وَسَوْفَ تُسْـَٔلُونَ

**This verse needs to be dwelt on, because it is open to two different readings.**

**In what sense is the word *dhikr* used here?**

| Reading | What *dhikr* is | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reminder, admonition** | This Qurʾān is an admonition for you and for your people |
| 2 | **Being spoken of, honour** | This Qurʾān is a **renown** for you and for your people (in the sense of *dhikr* = being named) |

**Both are transmitted in the classical commentaries, and I do not impose a preference.**

**But if the second reading is taken, the verse's place in the sūra is striking, and I record this as my own reading:** in the thirty-first verse a measure of **honour** was put forward — "the great man of the two towns". **The forty-fourth verse can be read as saying where honour lies: in what is carried.**

**And the note that follows the sentence balances this: *wa sawfa tusʾalūn* — "you will be questioned before long."**

**What is given, that is, is not left as a privilege; it is tied to a responsibility.** I record this as an observation about the arrangement: between the two sentences there is only a *wāw*, and the second qualifies the first.

The root **س-ء-ل** occurs three times in the sūra, and this is a pattern:

| Verse | Expression | Who is questioned |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | *wa **yusʾalūn*** | **The other side** — about their testimony |
| 44 | *wa sawfa **tusʾalūn*** | **Those addressed and the Prophet** |
| 45 | *wa'**sʾal** man arsalnā* | **A command** — the Prophet asks the question |

**The same root in three positions: the one questioned, the one who will be questioned, and the one who asks.**

### وَسْـَٔلْ مَنْ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ

**How this verse is to be understood has been debated in the classical commentaries, because its wording commands the asking of a question and those addressed are not alive.**

**The chief readings transmitted are these:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Ask their communities and their learned men** — that is, the People of the earlier Book |
| 2 | **Look into their books** — put the question to the content of the earlier revelations |
| 3 | **A hypothetical question** — in the sense "were you to ask, this is the answer you would get"; not a real command to ask |
| 4 | It is transmitted that it relates to an event that took place on a particular night | 

**I do not report in detail the account named in the fourth row, because it is recorded as disputed even in its sources and I cannot give it as certain knowledge.**

**The reading of the majority is the third: the question is framed as a question whose answer is already settled.** **I transmit this as it has been transmitted.**

**And the content of the question must be recorded:** *a-jaʿalnā min dūni'r-Raḥmāni ālihatan yuʿbadūn* — **"did We ever appoint gods to be served besides al-Raḥmān?"**

**The verb is again *jaʿala*, and it comes for the fifth time in the sūra.** **This time in the interrogative, and expecting a negative answer.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the repetition of the verb within the sūra:** the sūra uses the verb *jaʿala* like a register. **Who made what? The text: the Qurʾān Arabic, the earth a cradle, the animal a mount. The other side: the angels female, a part assigned to God. And now the question: did We ever make a god?**

**ٱلرَّحْمَٰن** — and this name occurs densely in the sūra: 17, 19, 20, 33, 36, 45, 81. **Seven occurrences.**

**I record this as a datum of the text and add this as an observation:** most of the places where the name occurs are places where **something is ascribed to Him** (17, 19, 20, 81) or where **He is turned away from** (36). **The sūra, that is, uses the name that most conveys mercy in the sentences that most recount refusal.**

The root **ر-ح-م** has been treated in the corpus (`060-mumtehine.md`, `090-beled.md`, `049-hucurat.md` and others); its core is bound up with *raḥim* (the womb): what encloses and grows something within itself.

## 43/46-50 — Moses and Pharaoh: the setting of the scene

**وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا مُوسَىٰ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَآ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ وَمَلَإِي۟هِۦ فَقَالَ إِنِّى رَسُولُ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · فَلَمَّا جَآءَهُم بِـَٔايَٰتِنَآ إِذَا هُم مِّنْهَا يَضْحَكُونَ · وَمَا نُرِيهِم مِّنْ ءَايَةٍ إِلَّا هِىَ أَكْبَرُ مِنْ أُخْتِهَا ۖ وَأَخَذْنَٰهُم بِٱلْعَذَابِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ · وَقَالُوا۟ يَٰٓأَيُّهَ ٱلسَّاحِرُ ٱدْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ بِمَا عَهِدَ عِندَكَ إِنَّنَا لَمُهْتَدُونَ · فَلَمَّا كَشَفْنَا عَنْهُمُ ٱلْعَذَابَ إِذَا هُمْ يَنكُثُونَ**

*Wa laqad arsalnā Mūsā bi-āyātinā ilā Firʿawna wa malaʾihi fa-qāla innī rasūlu rabbi'l-ʿālamīn · Fa-lammā jāʾahum bi-āyātinā idhā hum minhā yaḍḥakūn · Wa mā nurīhim min āyatin illā hiya akbaru min ukhtihā · Wa akhadhnāhum bi'l-ʿadhābi laʿallahum yarjiʿūn · Wa qālū yā ayyuha's-sāḥiru'dʿu lanā rabbaka bimā ʿahida ʿindaka innanā la-muhtadūn · Fa-lammā kashafnā ʿanhumu'l-ʿadhāba idhā hum yankuthūn*

"We sent Moses (Mūsā) with Our signs to Pharaoh and his chiefs. He said: 'I am a messenger of the Lord of all the worlds.' And when he came to them with Our signs, there they were **laughing at them.** Every sign We showed them was **greater than the one before it.** And We seized them with punishment, that they might return. They said: **'Sorcerer! Call upon your Lord for us by the covenant He has made with you; we shall certainly be guided.'** But when We lifted the punishment from them, there they were **breaking their word.**"

### The place of the story in the sūra

**The seventh block begins here, and a selected section is recounted.**

**The Qurʾān tells the story of Moses in many places, and in each sūra a different section is brought forward. The section al-Zukhruf chooses is this, and the choice does not look accidental:**

| Verse | Scene | Its link to the sūra's question |
|---|---|---|
| 47 | **Laughter** | Measure: counting the other light |
| 51 | **"Is the kingdom of Egypt not mine?"** | Measure: superiority through dominion |
| 52 | **"He cannot make his meaning plain"** | Measure: the capacity to express |
| 53 | **"If only gold bracelets had been put on him"** | Measure: visible splendour |
| 54 | ***Fa'stakhaffa qawmahu*** | Measure: finding one's own people light |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is what these five scenes have in common:** the sūra has cut out of the story of Moses **exactly its own subject.** What is recounted is not a series of miracles; **it is a question of the scale.**

### إِذَا هُم مِّنْهَا يَضْحَكُونَ — the *idhā* of surprise

***Idhā* here is not the particle of condition but the particle of suddenness (*fujāʾiyya*): "and there they were".**

**This particle occurs twice in this block of the sūra:**

| Verse | Sentence | What would be expected | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 47 | *idhā hum minhā **yaḍḥakūn*** | To be shaken before a sign | **Laughter** |
| 50 | *idhā hum **yankuthūn*** | To keep their word | **Breaking their word** |

**The same particle, the same structure, twice.** This is a verifiable datum of arrangement, and I record it as a pattern: **the block is built upon two scenes of suddenness.**

The root **ض-ح-ك**: to laugh. **The lexicographers record one further branch of the root: *ḍaḥika'r-rāʾiʿ* — the crop blossomed, the bud split open.** And *ḍaḥk* — opening, coming out. **I transmit this relation as it has been transmitted.**

**The word's work here must be recorded, and it is bound up with the seventh verse of the sūra:** there was *yastahziʾūn* (they were mocking). **Here it is *yaḍḥakūn* (they are laughing).** **Both belong to the same family: lowering the weight of the one in front of them.**

### أَكْبَرُ مِنْ أُخْتِهَا — "greater than its sister"

**The expression is striking: the signs are named as one another's *sisters*.**

**In Arabic this is a known usage and the lexicographers record it: like things are counted one another's *akh* / *ukht*.**

**That the word comes in the feminine (*ukht*, sister) is because the word *āya* is feminine** — it is a requirement of grammar, not an emphasis on gender. **I record this plainly.**

**And what the expression describes is a series:** each sign greater than the one before. **The evidence, that is, comes not diminishing but increasing.**

**I record this as an observation and link it with the fifty-third verse:** despite this whole increasing series, the proof Pharaoh will ask for is **a gold bracelet.** **The verse says first that the signs were increasing, in order to show that a shortage of evidence was not the problem.**

### يَٰٓأَيُّهَ ٱلسَّاحِرُ — the contradiction in the address

**The sentence is a request for help, but the address is "sorcerer".**

**The exegetes have noticed this oddity, and two readings are transmitted:**

| Reading | What *sāḥir* is here | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A belittling** — they still count him a sorcerer | The known sense of the word; consistent with the laughter in the forty-seventh verse |
| 2 | **A title in that society meaning a learned or wise man** | Some exegetes transmit that *siḥr* was used in that age for advanced knowledge as well |

**I transmit the second reading as it has been transmitted; I do not present it as certain knowledge.**

**If the first reading is taken, what the verse shows is plain, and I record it as my own reading:** the one asking for help calls the one he asks by a name that still belittles him. **Need, that is, does not change the verdict.**

### A point of orthography: أَيُّهَ

**In the muṣḥaf this word is written without the final alif: أَيُّهَ.** This was recorded in `055-rahman.md`:

> *"In the muṣḥaf this word is written in the form أَيُّهَ, without the final alif (normally *ayyuhā*). The same spelling is found in two other places in the Qurʾān: al-Nūr 24/31 and al-Zukhruf 43/49."*

**I complete that link here. This is a fact of orthography (the *rasm* of the muṣḥaf); it produces no difference in meaning and I draw no conclusion from it.**

### إِنَّنَا لَمُهْتَدُونَ — the pattern of the promise

**And the word *muhtadūn* comes for the fourth time.** It completes the table above.

**Here the mood of the word must be recorded: a nominal sentence plus two emphases (*inna* + *lām*).** The promise, that is, is given in the strongest possible pattern.

**And one verse later it is broken.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses come one after the other:** the sūra shows, **within the space of two verses**, that there is no relation between the force of a promise and the keeping of it.

### يَنكُثُون — root: ن-ك-ث

**The concrete sense of the root: to untwist a twisted rope or unpick spun wool, to undo it.** *Nakatha'l-ḥabl* — he untwisted the rope, unpicked its plaiting.

**And from there: to go back on one's word, to break an agreement.**

**The Qurʾān uses this picture explicitly in al-Naḥl 16/92:** *wa lā takūnū ka'llatī naqaḍat ghazlahā min baʿdi quwwatin ankāthā* — "do not be like the woman who **unravelled** her yarn after spinning it strong." **There the root is ن-ق-ض, here ن-ك-ث; both carry the same picture.**

**I record this as an observation about the word:** giving one's word is, in the picture of the root, **spinning**; going back on it is **unpicking**. The work done is taken back.

## 43/51-52 — وَنَادَىٰ فِرْعَوْنُ فِى قَوْمِهِۦ قَالَ يَٰقَوْمِ أَلَيْسَ لِى مُلْكُ مِصْرَ وَهَٰذِهِ ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِىٓ ۖ أَفَلَا تُبْصِرُونَ · أَمْ أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ هَٰذَا ٱلَّذِى هُوَ مَهِينٌ وَلَا يَكَادُ يُبِينُ

*Wa nādā Firʿawnu fī qawmihi qāla yā qawmi a-laysa lī mulku Miṣra wa hādhihi'l-anhāru tajrī min taḥtī · A-fa-lā tubṣirūn · Am anā khayrun min hādha'lladhī huwa mahīnun wa lā yakādu yubīn*

"And Pharaoh proclaimed among his people: **'My people! Is the kingdom of Egypt not mine, and these rivers flowing beneath me? Do you not see? Or am I not better than this contemptible man who can scarcely make his meaning plain?'**"

### The construction of the speech

**Pharaoh's speech consists of three steps, and all three are operations of weighing:**

| Step | Sentence | What is being done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *A-laysa lī mulku Miṣr* | **He puts his own weight on the scale** — dominion |
| 2 | *Hādhihi'l-anhāru tajrī min taḥtī* | **Visible proof** — the vista |
| 3 | *Am anā khayrun min hādhā…* | **Comparison** — he works the scale |

**And the other side's weight is also enumerated under two heads: *mahīn* and *lā yakādu yubīn*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the speech itself:** Pharaoh is not answering a claim. **He does not touch on anything Moses said.** The only thing he does is to put two men on the scale.

**And its link with the thirty-first verse of the sūra is direct:**

| Verse | Sentence | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | *Law lā nuzzila … ʿalā rajulin … **ʿaẓīm*** | **A great man** — rank |
| 51-52 | *A-laysa lī **mulku Miṣr** … am anā **khayrun*** | **The owner of a dominion** — rank |

**The same measure, in two different ages, in two different mouths.** I record this as a datum of the text.

### وَهَٰذِهِ ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِى

**The expression should be dwelt on, because it sets up an interesting relation with the Qurʾān's own language.**

***Tajrī min taḥtī* — "flowing beneath me".**

**And the expression that occurs most often in the Qurʾān's descriptions of the Garden is this: *tajrī min taḥtiha'l-anhār* — "beneath which rivers flow".**

**The difference between the two expressions is one letter: *taḥtihā* / *taḥtī*.**

**I record this as an observation and leave it there, without forcing a link:** Pharaoh's sentence is the expression used of the Garden turned into **the first person singular.** **I do not claim that the text makes such a link; what I record is the closeness of wording between the two expressions.**

**The exegetes transmit two readings for the expression *min taḥtī*:**

| Reading | Its meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Beneath his palace** — the branches of the Nile flowing below his position |
| 2 | **Under my command** — under my rule |

**The first is closer to the wording; the second is a figure. I do not impose a preference.**

### مَهِين — root: م-ه-ن

**The sense of the root: held in contempt, counted worthless; and menial service.** *Mahna* — service, heavy work. *Māhin* — a servant.

**The word occurs elsewhere in the Qurʾān in a very different position, and the comparison is fruitful:**

> *"Did We not create you from a contemptible fluid (*min māʾin **mahīn***)?"* (al-Mursalāt 77/20 — treated in `077-murselat.md`)
> *"Then He made his progeny from the essence of a fluid of no account."* (The same word occurs in al-Sajda 32/8 as well.)

**The same word, then, is used in one place for the beginning of every human being, and in another to make a prophet small.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two uses of the word in the Qurʾān:** the word Pharaoh uses as an insult is the word the Qurʾān uses **of everyone.** **The difference lies not in whom the word is stuck on, but in who counts himself outside it.**

### وَلَا يَكَادُ يُبِينُ — and the fourth *mubīn* of the sūra

**The verb *kāda* conveys approaching: "almost".** Its negative (*lā yakādu*) gives, in Arabic, **a strong negation**: "he almost cannot do it" — that is, **he does it only with great difficulty**, or not at all.

**And the root ب-ي-ن comes for the sixth time in the sūra.** I complete here the table I set out above (in the discussion of 43/18):

| Verse | Expression | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | *al-kitābi'l-**mubīn*** | **The Book** |
| 15 | *kafūrun **mubīn*** | **Ingratitude** |
| 18 | *ghayru **mubīn*** | The one raised amid ornament |
| 29 | *rasūlun **mubīn*** | **A messenger** |
| 40 | *ḍalālin **mubīn*** | Error |
| 52 | *lā yakādu **yubīn*** | **Moses** |
| 62 | *ʿaduwwun **mubīn*** | Satan |

**The root occurs seven times in the sūra. This is a datum of the text.**

**And what the two occurrences (18 and 52) have in common must be recorded, and it is one of the most striking findings of the sūra:**

**In both places the same measure is used to make a human being small.** In the eighteenth verse, "one who cannot bring her meaning to light in a dispute"; in the fifty-second, "one who can scarcely make his meaning plain".

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two expressions are from the same root and in the same function:** the sūra shows **in two separate scenes** how the capacity to express is used as a measure of value — one in a society's verdict about a daughter, the other in a king's verdict about a prophet.

**And the sūra uses this measure itself — but elsewhere: of the Book (2), of the messenger (29).** **The issue, that is, is not the existence of the measure but to whom it is applied.**

**One further note:** the difficulty in Moses's speech is mentioned elsewhere in the Qurʾān — it occurs in his own prayer in Ṭāhā 20/27: *wa'ḥlul ʿuqdatan min lisānī · yafqahū qawlī* ("and loosen the knot in my tongue, that they may understand my speech"). **What Pharaoh says, then, is not an entirely baseless slander; the detail of it is recorded elsewhere in the Qurʾān.**

**This needs recording, because it makes the verse's work still clearer:** Pharaoh is not lying. **He is using a correct observation on a wrong measure.**

## 43/53 — فَلَوْلَآ أُلْقِىَ عَلَيْهِ أَسْوِرَةٌ مِّن ذَهَبٍ أَوْ جَآءَ مَعَهُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ مُقْتَرِنِينَ

*Fa-law lā ulqiya ʿalayhi aswiratun min dhahabin aw jāʾa maʿahu'l-malāʾikatu muqtarinīn*

"**Should bracelets of gold not have been cast upon him? Or angels have come with him in a train?**"

**This verse is the clearest expression of the sūra's main question, and it needs to be treated in detail.**

### What is demanded: gold expected as the proof of prophethood

**Pharaoh asks for a proof. The proof he asks for is this: a gold bracelet.**

**And this must be read together with the forty-eighth verse of the sūra:** there it was said that the signs came **each greater than the last.** **The proofs, that is, had already come, and had been increasing.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses stand in the same block:** in order to show that a shortage of proof was not the issue, the verse says first that the proofs had come, and then reports the "proof" that is demanded.

**And what is demanded is not a proof but an *insignia*.**

### أَسْوِرَة — root: س-و-ر

**The sense of the root: to encircle, to surround, to form a ring.**

- **سُور** — a city wall; the wall that encircles.
- **سِوَار** — a bracelet; the ring that **encircles** the wrist. Its plural is *aswira* / *asāwir*.
- **سُورَة** — a section, a tier; and the name of the sections of the Qurʾān. **The lexicographers attach this word too to the same root and explain it as "an enclosed section with settled bounds" or "a rising tier"; I transmit this derivation as it has been transmitted.**

**The concrete picture of the word: a ring, an encircling.**

The root **ذ-ه-ب**: to go; and *dhahab* — gold. **The lexicographers make the link thus: gold is the thing that keeps going out of one's hand, that keeps changing hands.** **I give this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

### Historical background — what can be said

**In the classical commentaries this explanation is transmitted for this verse: that in ancient Egypt and the surrounding cultures, when someone was given authority, gold bracelets and a collar were put on him; that this was an insignia of rank.**

**I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

**And I can add this, because it is a verifiable field:** that jewellery and collars were used as indicators of rank in ancient Egyptian and Near Eastern cultures is a matter known from archaeological finds and from the visual sources of the period. **I record this as general historical knowledge, not in order to force a scientific confirmation onto the verse.**

**And a comparison can be made within the Qurʾān itself:** Yūsuf 12/72 speaks of a king's drinking-cup in Egypt (*ṣuwāʿa'l-malik*); Yūsuf 12/31 recounts the women cutting their hands. **The Qurʾān knows and uses the objects of this culture.**

### The verse's place in the sūra — and its link with *zukhruf*

**Now the verse must be read together with the name of the sūra.**

**In the thirty-fifth verse *zukhruf* (gold ornament) was named and tied to this verdict: *wa in kullu dhālika lammā matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā*.**

**In the fifty-third verse, the proof demanded for the truth of a prophet is gold.**

**I set this out in a table, because the backbone of the sūra becomes visible here:**

| Verse | Where the gold/silver is | The verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 33 | Silver ceiling — for the one who rejects | Not given; **there is an objection** |
| 35 | *Zukhruf* — gold ornament | ***Matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā*** |
| 53 | Gold bracelet — **demanded as the proof of prophethood** | Not even refuted; *fa'stakhaffa* comes next |
| 71 | Golden platters — in the Garden | **Given as a reward** |

**Four occurrences, one material, four different positions.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these four occurrences:** the sūra neither forbids gold nor speaks ill of it. **The only thing it does is to expose the use of gold as *proof*.** In the thirty-fifth verse gold is a *matāʿ*; in the fifty-third it is put in the place of evidence. **This is what the sūra objects to.**

**And I add this:** Pharaoh's logic is this — **one who comes from God must be marked with the most valuable thing here.** The proof of divine approval, that is, is taken to be **a local badge of status.**

**This logic is the very same as the objection in the thirty-first verse:** there too the revelation was expected to be given to the man of highest rank in that society. **The sūra, that is, shows the same error in two cultures, in two ages.**

### مُقْتَرِنِين — root: ق-ر-ن

**The root occurred in the thirteenth verse (*muqrinīn*) and in the thirty-sixth (*qarīn*). Here it comes for the third time, this time in Form VIII: *muqtarinīn*.**

**Form VIII (*iftiʿāl*) here conveys reciprocity or accompaniment: accompanying one another, in a train, rank upon rank.**

**I set the three occurrences out in a table, because a single root coming in three different patterns within one sūra is worth recording:**

| Verse | Word | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | *muqrinīn* | IV | **To be a match, to be capable** |
| 36 | *qarīn* | (adjective) | **Inseparable companion** |
| 53 | *muqtarinīn* | VIII | **In a train, accompanying one another** |

**And the opposition between the thirteenth and the fifty-third verse is worth recording as my own reading:**

| | 43/13 | 43/53 |
|---|---|---|
| Who | A human being mounting his beast | **Pharaoh** |
| Sentence | *Mā kunnā lahu **muqrinīn*** | *Jāʾa maʿahu'l-malāʾikatu **muqtarinīn*** |
| What it says | **"We were not its match"** | **"Angels should have come in a train"** |
| Direction | **Lowering oneself** | **Demanding splendour of the other** |

**The same root, two opposite stances.** The one accepts his own measure before a possibility that has been given; the other makes a proof conditional on his own measure of splendour.

**I record this as a pattern, and I do not claim that the sūra is constructing a deliberate play on words.** What I record is that the root has been used in this sūra in three different patterns and in positions opposed to one another.

## 43/54-56 — فَٱسْتَخَفَّ قَوْمَهُۥ فَأَطَاعُوهُ ۚ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ قَوْمًا فَٰسِقِينَ · فَلَمَّآ ءَاسَفُونَا ٱنتَقَمْنَا مِنْهُمْ فَأَغْرَقْنَٰهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ · فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ سَلَفًا وَمَثَلًا لِّلْءَاخِرِينَ

*Fa'stakhaffa qawmahu fa-aṭāʿūh · Innahum kānū qawman fāsiqīn · Fa-lammā āsafūna'ntaqamnā minhum fa-aghraqnāhum ajmaʿīn · Fa-jaʿalnāhum salafan wa mathalan li'l-ākhirīn*

"**He made light of his people, and they obeyed him.** They were a people who had broken bounds. And when they angered Us, We took retribution from them and drowned them all. We made them **a past and an example for those who come after.**"

### فَٱسْتَخَفَّ — root: خ-ف-ف

**The root was treated in `101-karia.md` at 101/8, and this verse was already cited there; it was cited in `079-naziat.md` as well.** What was recorded in the discussion of al-Qāriʿa was this:

> *"Root: خ-ف-ف. To be light. **اِسْتَخَفَّ** — to reckon light, to belittle, to make light of. 'He made light of his people, and they obeyed him' (al-Zukhruf 43/54). This last derivative is worth dwelling on. In Arabic the same root carries both **lightness on the scale** and **not taking seriously.** English has the same relation: we say 'to make light of'."*

**I rely on that treatment and add here what is particular to al-Zukhruf.**

**The pattern is Form X (*istifʿāl*), and the finding I recorded above for *yastahziʾūn* (7) holds here too:** this form conveys **"to reckon a thing so, to find it so"**.

**But the verb *istakhaffa* has a second sense in the lexicographers as well, and the exegetes are divided between the two in this verse:**

| Reading | What *istakhaffa qawmahu* means | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **He found his people light, he belittled them** | He saw their minds as light and therefore spoke as he did |
| 2 | **He made his people light, he unsettled them** — *istakhaffahu* = he stirred him up and set him moving | With his words he swept them along |

**Both are recorded by the lexicographers. I do not impose a preference.**

**But it must be recorded that both readings are tied to the same outcome: *fa-aṭāʿūh* — "and they obeyed him".**

### The real weight of the sentence: the connector *fāʾ*

**Between the two verbs there is a *fāʾ*: *fa'stakhaffa … fa-aṭāʿūh*.**

***Fāʾ* in Arabic conveys both sequence and cause: immediately after, and on account of it.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the connector itself:** the verse does not set two events side by side; **it makes the one the cause of the other.** Making light becomes the cause of obedience.

**And at first sight this seems inverted — ordinarily one who is made light of does not obey.**

**What the verse shows is this, and it is directly connected with the sūra's main question:** Pharaoh offered his people no proof. **He showed them three things: dominion (51), a vista (51), and the smallness of the other side (52-53).** None of these was a ground. **The people obeyed all the same.**

**The verse, that is, describes how a want of measure meets with a response:** a people made light of follows the one who makes light of it. **And that this is possible means that the making light of them has been borne out.**

**I mark this as my own reading; the wording of the verse does not yield this conclusion, the linking of the two verbs with *fāʾ* does.**

**And the end of the verse sets the verdict: *innahum kānū qawman fāsiqīn*.**

The root **ف-س-ق**: the concrete sense the lexicographers give is **the date's coming out of its skin** (*fasaqa'r-ruṭabu min qishrihi*). **That is, splitting one's husk and coming out.** From there: to go outside the bound.

**In `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/35 the same word occurred at the close of that sūra, and this was recorded there:** *"the close ties the measure of destruction to an act: fāsiqūn — those who go outside the bound. No lineage, no people, no community is named; an attribute is named."*

**I keep the same line here: the verdict is tied not to who the obedient were, but to what they did.**

### ءَاسَفُونَا — root: أ-س-ف

**The sense of the root: severe grief; and anger.** The lexicographers record both branches of the root and explain them thus: *asaf* is **the state one is in before a loss that has no recompense** — a state which, looked at from below, is grief, and from above, anger.

**The Qurʾān uses the word of Jacob:** *yā asafā ʿalā Yūsuf* (Yūsuf 12/84) — there it is in the sense of grief. **And of Moses:** *ghaḍbāna asifā* (al-Aʿrāf 7/150; Ṭāhā 20/86) — there it comes together with anger.

**In Form IV (*āsafa*): to make someone angry, to grieve him.**

**And here a note must be entered.**

**The agent of the verb is human beings, and its object is the first person plural pronoun (*-nā*): "they angered Us".**

**Expressions of this kind (expressions ascribing feeling to God) have been debated in the classical literature of theology, and two approaches have developed:**

| Approach | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Consignment / affirmation (*tafwīḍ*)** | The wording of the revealed text (*naṣṣ*) is kept; the modality is not entered into, and no likeness to a human state is drawn |
| **Interpretation (*taʾwīl*)** | The word is understood through its outcome: "anger" = the coming of the punishment |

**Both have been defended in the Islamic tradition. I do not impose a preference and I issue no ruling on the modality.** **What is to be recorded is what both approaches accept in common: the expression is not to be understood as the counterpart of human anger.**

### فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ سَلَفًا وَمَثَلًا لِّلْءَاخِرِينَ

**And the verb *jaʿala* comes for the sixth time in the sūra.**

The root **س-ل-ف**: to go in front, to pass ahead. *Salaf* — those who went before. *Sulfa* — what is paid in advance. **The Turkish word *selef* ("one's predecessor in an office") comes from here.**

**Two readings are transmitted for the word:**

| Reading | What *salafan* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A vanguard** — a group going ahead of those who come after |
| 2 | **A past** — an example left behind |

**Both are possible; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the word *mathal* connects with the eighth verse of the sūra. I complete here the ring I set out above:**

| Verse | Expression | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | *wa maḍā **mathalu'l-awwalīn*** | Looks **to the past** |
| 56 | *wa **mathalan li'l-ākhirīn*** | Left **to the future** |

**The same word, at the two ends of the sūra, in two opposite directions.** This is a verifiable datum of the text.

**And I add as an observation:** the eighth verse said "the example of those of old has passed." **The fifty-sixth shows what it is that makes such an example: a people turns into an example for those who come after to read.**

## 43/57-58 — وَلَمَّا ضُرِبَ ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ مَثَلًا إِذَا قَوْمُكَ مِنْهُ يَصِدُّونَ · وَقَالُوٓا۟ ءَأَٰلِهَتُنَا خَيْرٌ أَمْ هُوَ ۚ مَا ضَرَبُوهُ لَكَ إِلَّا جَدَلًۢا ۚ بَلْ هُمْ قَوْمٌ خَصِمُونَ

*Wa lammā ḍuriba'bnu Maryama mathalan idhā qawmuka minhu yaṣiddūn · Wa qālū a-ālihatunā khayrun am huwa · Mā ḍarabūhu laka illā jadalā · Bal hum qawmun khaṣimūn*

"And when the son of Mary was put forward as an example, there was your people **clamouring about him.** And they said: **'Are our gods better, or he?'** They put him to you only **for the sake of argument.** Indeed they are **a contentious people.**"

**In treating this block I must set a limit from the outset, per the method (`USLUP.md`).**

**These verses issue no blanket ruling about a religious group, and neither shall I.** What the verses work on is **a stance taken at a particular moment of argument**: an example being put forward, without its content being discussed, in order to win the argument. **What the verdict is attached to is not the name of a community but an attribute: *qawmun khaṣimūn* — a contentious people.**

**And in the sixty-third verse Jesus's own words will be reported; in the sixty-fifth, those who fell into disagreement will be named *al-aḥzāb* — again an attribute, again not a name.**

### ضُرِبَ … مَثَلًا — and the ض-ر-ب pattern of the sūra

**The root comes for the fourth time in the sūra.** I complete here the table I set out at the head of the sūra (see "The structure of the sūra"):

| Verse | Expression | Who | What is being done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | *a-fa-naḍribu ʿankumu'dh-dhikra ṣafḥan* | **The text** | Pushing the reminder aside |
| 17 | *bimā ḍaraba li'r-Raḥmāni mathalan* | **The other side** | Ascribing a likeness to al-Raḥmān |
| 57 | *ḍuriba'bnu Maryama mathalan* | **Passive** | An example is put forward |
| 58 | *mā ḍarabūhu laka illā jadalā* | **The other side** | Putting the example forward for argument |

**The root four times, in three distinct pieces of work.** This is a verifiable datum of the text.

**And that the verb comes in the *passive* in the fifty-seventh verse must be recorded: *ḍuriba* — "was put forward".** **By whom it was put forward is not stated.**

**I give this as an observation about the arrangement:** the verse does not name the one who put the example forward; **it names the reaction.**

### يَصِدُّون — a variant reading and the meaning

**Two readings of the word are transmitted, and the difference touches the meaning:**

| Reading | Root / sense | Its meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *yaṣiddūn* (with *kasra* on the *ṣād*) | **ص-د-د** — to make an uproar, to clamour, to shout | **They clamour, they make an uproar** |
| *yaṣuddūn* (with *ḍamma* on the *ṣād*) | **ص-د-د** — to turn away, to obstruct | **They turn away** |

**Both have been transmitted by the imams of the readings.**

**In the discussion of `047-muhammed.md` at 47/1 the two branches of the root ص-د-د were treated, and this verse was cited there:**

> *"**Intransitive** — *ṣadda ʿan*: he himself turned away. 'When they see you they **turn away** from you' (in al-Zukhruf 43/57, *yaṣiddūn* from the same root)."*

**I rely on that treatment and add here the root's other branch.**

**The lexicographers record for *ṣadīd* / *ṣadda* the sense "noise, clamour" as well, and illustrate it with the sound of a camel.** **The lexicographers do not settle the link between the two branches; there are also those who count them two separate roots. I transmit this disagreement as it has been transmitted.**

**I do not impose a preference.** But this can be recorded: **on either reading what is described is not an argument but a *reaction*** — either an uproar or a turned back. **Neither goes into the content.**

**And the root occurs three times in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 37 | *la-**yaṣuddūnahum** ʿani's-sabīl* | The companions **turn** people from the road |
| 57 | ***yaṣiddūn*** | The people react |
| 62 | *wa-lā **yaṣuddannakumu**'sh-shayṭān* | Let Satan not **turn** you away |

### ءَأَٰلِهَتُنَا خَيْرٌ أَمْ هُوَ — the structure of the question

**The question is a comparison, and it is added to the sūra's patterns of comparison.**

**The comparisons in the sūra built with the word *khayrun* (better) are these:**

| Verse | Comparison | Who frames it |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | *wa raḥmatu rabbika **khayrun** mimmā yajmaʿūn* | **The text** |
| 52 | *am anā **khayrun** min hādha'lladhī…* | **Pharaoh** |
| 58 | *a-ālihatunā **khayrun** am huwa* | **The people** |

**Three comparisons in one sūra.** Two of them a claim of superiority, one a correction of measure.

**I record this as a datum of the text and add as an observation:** the sūra does not refuse to make comparisons. **It makes one itself (32).** The objection is to **what is being compared with what.**

### The disagreement: which discussion do these verses answer?

**In the classical commentaries different accounts are transmitted about the conversation after which these verses came, and they do not agree with one another. I report them as far as they have been transmitted, without issuing a ruling:**

| Transmitted reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | It is transmitted that a dispute arose over the verse in al-Anbiyāʾ 21/98, *innakum wa mā taʿbudūna min dūni'llāhi ḥaṣabu jahannam*, and that it was asked: "then the son of Mary too?" |
| 2 | It is transmitted that they wanted the respect shown to the son of Mary to hold for what they themselves served as well |
| 3 | An account of a dispute is transmitted, named with a particular person's name |

**I give none of these accounts as certain knowledge.** Even in their sources it is stated that they are recorded in differing forms.

**And the verse itself already delivers a verdict, and this verdict needs no accounts: *mā ḍarabūhu laka illā jadalā*.**

### جَدَل — root: ج-د-ل

**The concrete sense of the root: to twist or plait a rope or a strap tightly.** *Jadala'l-ḥabl* — he twisted the rope firm. *Jadīl* — a plaited strap, a camel's halter.

**And from here comes the sense of dispute. The lexicographers make the link thus: two disputing parties each try to twist the other, to pull him over to their side** — like *mujādala* in wrestling. **Indeed *jadala* is also used in the sense of throwing someone to the ground.**

**The picture of the word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** *jadal*, at its root, is not **arriving at a truth** but **twisting the other side.** The aim is not a conclusion but getting the upper hand.

**And the Qurʾān's stance on *jadal* is not a blanket prohibition:** in al-Naḥl 16/125 it commands *wa jādilhum bi'llatī hiya aḥsan* (dispute with them in the best manner). **The issue, that is, is not disputation itself but the purpose of the disputation.**

The root **خ-ص-م** — it occurred in the eighteenth verse as *khiṣām*. I complete here the comparison I set out above (in the discussion of 43/18): **in one place, one counted small because she cannot contend; in the other, those who do nothing but contend.**

***Khaṣimūn* is an intensive form (the plural of *faʿil*): one who contends much.**

## 43/59-60 — إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا عَبْدٌ أَنْعَمْنَا عَلَيْهِ وَجَعَلْنَٰهُ مَثَلًا لِّبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · وَلَوْ نَشَآءُ لَجَعَلْنَا مِنكُم مَّلَٰٓئِكَةً فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ يَخْلُفُونَ

*In huwa illā ʿabdun anʿamnā ʿalayhi wa jaʿalnāhu mathalan li-banī Isrāʾīl · Wa law nashāʾu la-jaʿalnā minkum malāʾikatan fi'l-arḍi yakhlufūn*

"**He is nothing but a servant on whom We bestowed favour;** and We made him an example for the Children of Israel. Had We willed, We could have made of you **angels to succeed you** on the earth."

### إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا عَبْدٌ — the pattern of restriction

**The sentence is built with one of the strongest patterns of limitation in Arabic: *in* (negation) + *illā* (exception). This is called *ḥaṣr* (restriction).**

**The sentence, that is, does not say "he is a servant"; it says "he is nothing other than a servant".**

**And the root ع-ب-د occurred in the sūra at the nineteenth verse: *alladhīna hum ʿibādu'r-Raḥmān* (of the angels).**

**The two verses must be set side by side, and this is one of the most consistent places in the sūra:**

| Verse | About whom | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | **The angels** | *alladhīna hum **ʿibādu'r-Raḥmān*** |
| 59 | **The son of Mary** | *in huwa illā **ʿabdun** anʿamnā ʿalayh* |

**Two separate ascriptions, one answer: *ʿabd*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses use the same word:** the sūra does not answer the two different claims (that the angels are God's daughters / the claim about the son of Mary) **separately.** It puts the same name to both.

**And the word is not a belittling — this needs writing out plainly.** The continuation of the sentence says *anʿamnā ʿalayhi* (on whom We bestowed favour). **To be a "servant", that is, is not outside the favour but inside it.**

The root **ن-ع-م** occurred in the twelfth verse as *anʿām* (livestock) and in the thirteenth as *niʿmata rabbikum*. **The same root comes here for the third time.**

### وَجَعَلْنَٰهُ مَثَلًا لِّبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ

**And the verb *jaʿala* comes for the seventh time in the sūra. The word *mathal* for the third.**

**In what sense is *mathal* here?** Two readings are transmitted:

| Reading | Its meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **An example, a lesson** — one from whom instruction is taken |
| 2 | **An extraordinary proof** — a sign of God's power (birth without a father) |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**What is to be recorded is the paths the word *mathal* takes within the sūra:**

| Verse | Whose *mathal* | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Those of old | **The text** |
| 17 | What is ascribed to al-Raḥmān | **The other side** |
| 56 | The people of Pharaoh | **The text** |
| 57-58 | The son of Mary | **The other side** (for the sake of argument) |
| 59 | The son of Mary | **The text** (for the Children of Israel) |

**The same word five times and from two different hands.** The fifty-seventh and the fifty-ninth verses are about the same person, but they are two different *mathal*s.

**I record this as a datum of the text and add as an observation: the sūra keeps track of whose hand the word "example" is in.** The other side makes the example into material for argument; the text places the same person as a sign.

### وَلَوْ نَشَآءُ لَجَعَلْنَا مِنكُم مَّلَٰٓئِكَةً فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ يَخْلُفُونَ

**The exegetes differ over this verse, and the disagreement must be set out in a table.**

| Reading | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|
| 1 | **"We would have put angels in your place"** — human beings depart, angels succeed them on the earth |
| 2 | **"We would have brought forth angels from among you"** — creating angels out of human beings; that is, the kind of creation is something that can be chosen |
| 3 | **"Angels would have come after you"** — in the sense of the succession of generations |

**The majority stand around the first and third readings. I do not impose a preference.**

***Yakhlufūn* — from the root خ-ل-ف: to come after someone, to take his place.** *Khalīfa* is from the same root.

**The verse's work within the block can be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** the block was arguing about the position of the angels and of the son of Mary. **This verse removes the ground of the argument by recalling that the angels too are created and that the order of creation is a matter of choice.**

## 43/61-62 — وَإِنَّهُۥ لَعِلْمٌ لِّلسَّاعَةِ فَلَا تَمْتَرُنَّ بِهَا وَٱتَّبِعُونِ ۚ هَٰذَا صِرَٰطٌ مُّسْتَقِيمٌ · وَلَا يَصُدَّنَّكُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ ۖ إِنَّهُۥ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ

*Wa innahu la-ʿilmun li's-sāʿati fa-lā tamtarunna bihā wa'ttabiʿūn · Hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm · Wa lā yaṣuddannakumu'sh-shayṭān · Innahu lakum ʿaduwwun mubīn*

"**He is a knowledge of the Hour**; so **do not be in doubt** about it, and follow me. This is a straight path. And let Satan not **turn** you away; he is a plain enemy to you."

### The question of the pronoun — and two variant readings

**This verse is among the most debated places in the sūra, and two separate disagreements are entangled in it.**

**The first disagreement: what does the pronoun *innahu* refer to?**

| View | What the pronoun refers to | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To Jesus, the son of Mary** | His case is a sign that the Hour will come |
| 2 | **To the Qurʾān** | This Book is what gives knowledge of the Hour |

**Both have been defended in the classical commentaries.**

**The second disagreement is a variant reading and is connected with the first:**

| Reading | Written form | Its meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *la-ʿilmun* | لَعِلْمٌ | **A knowledge** — a source of knowledge, a sign |
| *la-ʿalamun* | لَعَلَمٌ | **A mark, a token** — a road-sign |

**The second reading has also been transmitted.** *ʿAlam* in Arabic means **a marker stone set up on a road, a mountain, a banner** — that is, **a sign by which, once seen, one knows where one is.**

**I do not impose a preference and do not go into the detail, because the matter turns on transmission and the transmissions differ from one another in the detail.**

**This much can be recorded and follows from the text: the sentence does not state the *time* of the resurrection, it states that it *will come*.** **And the sūra will note this explicitly in the eighty-fifth verse: *wa ʿindahu ʿilmu's-sāʿa* — the knowledge of the Hour is with Him.**

**This limit was treated in `079-naziat.md`, and this sūra was cited there:** *"The knowledge of the Hour is with Him."* (Luqmān 31/34; al-Zukhruf 43/85; Fuṣṣilat 41/47). **I rely on that treatment.**

**There is thus a balance within the sūra itself, and I record it as a datum of the text:** the sixty-first verse gives a **sign**, the eighty-fifth says **where the knowledge is.** **The two do not conflict: a sign shows that a thing will come, not when.**

### تَمْتَرُنَّ — root: م-ر-ي

**The concrete sense of the root comes from herding: *marā'n-nāqata* — to rub and stroke the she-camel's udder in order to milk her.**

**And from there: *mirya* — doubt, hesitation. The lexicographers make the link thus: like a udder being milked, to turn a matter over and over, to keep working at it.** **I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

**The picture of the word is worth recording:** doubt, in the picture of the root, is **holding a thing in the hand and rubbing it** — not letting go, but not arriving at a conclusion either.

***Mirya*, in Form VIII (*imtirāʾ*), takes the sense of hesitation and contention.**

### هَٰذَا صِرَٰطٌ مُّسْتَقِيمٌ — and the repetition

**The expression occurs three times in the sūra: 43, 61, 64.**

| Verse | Who says it | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 43 | **The text** (to the Prophet) | *innaka **ʿalā** ṣirāṭin mustaqīm* |
| 61 | **Disputed as to who** | *hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* |
| 64 | **Jesus** | *hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* |

**The pattern in the forty-third verse differs from the others: there the person is *upon* the path, here something *is* the path.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement.**

**Whose mouth the expression *wa'ttabiʿūn* (follow me) in the sixty-first verse comes out of has also been debated** — is it a command addressed to the Prophet, or the beginning of Jesus's words? **In the sixty-third verse Jesus's words begin explicitly, and there too *wa aṭīʿūn* (obey me) occurs. This resemblance is counted a proof by those who support the second reading. I do not impose a preference.**

### وَلَا يَصُدَّنَّكُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ

The root **ص-د-د** for the third time in the sūra. I complete here the table I set out above (in the discussion of 43/57).

**The verb comes with the *heavy nūn of emphasis* (*nūn al-tawkīd al-thaqīla*): *yaṣuddannakum*.** **This pattern is the strongest form of prohibition and warning in Arabic.**

***ʿAduwwun mubīn* — the root ب-ي-ن for the seventh time in the sūra.** It completes the table above.

**And the word for enmity will return in the sixty-seventh verse: *baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ʿaduww*.** **Two occurrences: one of Satan, the other of friends.**

## 43/63-65 — وَلَمَّا جَآءَ عِيسَىٰ بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ قَالَ قَدْ جِئْتُكُم بِٱلْحِكْمَةِ وَلِأُبَيِّنَ لَكُم بَعْضَ ٱلَّذِى تَخْتَلِفُونَ فِيهِ ۖ فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ · إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ رَبِّى وَرَبُّكُمْ فَٱعْبُدُوهُ ۚ هَٰذَا صِرَٰطٌ مُّسْتَقِيمٌ · فَٱخْتَلَفَ ٱلْأَحْزَابُ مِنۢ بَيْنِهِمْ ۖ فَوَيْلٌ لِّلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ مِنْ عَذَابِ يَوْمٍ أَلِيمٍ

*Wa lammā jāʾa ʿĪsā bi'l-bayyināti qāla qad jiʾtukum bi'l-ḥikmati wa li-ubayyina lakum baʿḍa'lladhī takhtalifūna fīh · Fa'ttaqu'llāha wa aṭīʿūn · Inna'llāha huwa rabbī wa rabbukum fa'ʿbudūh · Hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm · Fa'khtalafa'l-aḥzābu min baynihim · Fa-waylun li'lladhīna ẓalamū min ʿadhābi yawmin alīm*

"And when Jesus came with the clear proofs he said: **'I have come to you with wisdom, and to make clear to you some of what you differ about.** So be mindful of God and obey me. **God is my Lord and your Lord; serve Him.** This is a straight path.' Then **the parties among them fell into disagreement.** Woe to those who did wrong, from the punishment of a painful day!"

### The reporting of Jesus's own words

**What the verse does must be recorded: the words of the person who was made the subject of the argument are reported.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the block:** in verses fifty-seven and fifty-eight an argument was recounted; in the fifty-ninth the text delivered a verdict (*in huwa illā ʿabd*); in the sixty-third **the person himself speaks.**

**And what he says stands in the same place as the verdict of the fifty-ninth verse: *inna'llāha huwa rabbī wa rabbukum*.**

**The arrangement is worth recording: *rabbī* first, *rabbukum* after.** **The sentence, that is, states his own position first.**

### بِٱلْحِكْمَة and وَلِأُبَيِّنَ — two tasks

**Jesus's words describe a task under two heads:**

| Item | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *jiʾtukum **bi'l-ḥikma*** | **What is brought** |
| 2 | *wa **li-ubayyina** lakum baʿḍa'lladhī takhtalifūna fīh* | **The work to be done** |

The root **ح-ك-م** occurred in the fourth verse (*ḥakīm*) and has been treated in the corpus: **to restrain and hold in place; the bridle.**

The root **ب-ي-ن** — the densest root in the sūra — comes here as a verb: *li-ubayyina* (that I may make clear). **And this is the same root as *al-kitābi'l-mubīn* in the second verse.**

**I record this as a datum of the text:** the sūra uses the root ب-ي-ن of the Book (2), of the messenger (29), and here of a prophet's task (63). **And the other side used the same root twice to make a human being small (18, 52).**

### بَعْضَ ٱلَّذِى تَخْتَلِفُونَ فِيهِ — the limit itself

**And there is a word here that must be dwelt on: *baʿḍa* — "some of".**

**The sentence does not say "that I may make clear the things you differ about". It says "some of".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word itself:** the prophet's task is not described as **resolving all disagreements.** **A limited task is described.**

**And it stands on the same line as the finding recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/35:** there the sūra limited its own task to *balāgh* (conveying). **Here too there is a limit, and the one who sets the limit is the one doing the work.**

### فَٱخْتَلَفَ ٱلْأَحْزَابُ مِنۢ بَيْنِهِمْ

**And the verse states the outcome: disagreement.**

The root **خ-ل-ف** occurred in the sixtieth verse as *yakhlufūn*. **In Form VIII (*ikhtilāf*) the sense changes: to fall behind one another — that is, not to walk on the same road.**

**The lexicographers explain the word *ikhtilāf* thus: two people, one going in one direction, the other in another.** **Its link with the root's core of "coming behind" lies here: not following in one another's tracks.**

**And this speaks quietly with the twenty-second verse of the sūra:** there the other side said *ʿalā āthārihim muhtadūn* — "in their tracks". **What is recounted here is the tracks not being followed.**

**I record this as an observation and do not present it as a link the text makes.**

The root **ح-ز-ب**: a group, a company; and *ḥizb* — a party, a faction. **The lexicographers also record the root's sense of "the hardship or weight of an affair" (*ḥazabahu'l-amr*).**

***Al-aḥzāb* occurs elsewhere in the Qurʾān as well** (Hūd 11/17, Maryam 19/37, al-Muʾmin 40/5, 40/30) and **is mostly used of groups that have fallen into disagreement and split into parties.**

**Per the method (`USLUP.md`) I enter a note here:** the verse issues no ruling about a religious tradition as a whole. **What is recounted is a fact: the parting of parties after a prophet's words.** And the verdict is attached not to a group but **to an act**: *li'lladhīna **ẓalamū*** — to those who did wrong.

**This is the line kept throughout the sūra, and it was recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/35 as well: the verdict is attached not to a name but to an attribute.**

The root **ظ-ل-م**: the core the lexicographers give is **darkness**; and **putting a thing somewhere other than its place.** **The root has been treated in the corpus.**

**And the word's occurrences in the sūra are worth recording:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| 39 | *idh **ẓalamtum*** — since you did wrong |
| 65 | *li'lladhīna **ẓalamū*** — to those who did wrong |
| 76 | *wa mā **ẓalamnāhum** wa lākin kānū humu'**ẓ-ẓālimīn*** |

**Three occurrences, and the last (76) will settle the verdict.**

## 43/66-67 — هَلْ يَنظُرُونَ إِلَّا ٱلسَّاعَةَ أَن تَأْتِيَهُم بَغْتَةً وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ · ٱلْأَخِلَّآءُ يَوْمَئِذٍۭ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ إِلَّا ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ

*Hal yanẓurūna illa's-sāʿata an taʾtiyahum baghtatan wa hum lā yashʿurūn · Al-akhillāʾu yawmaʾidhin baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ʿaduwwun illa'l-muttaqīn*

"Are they waiting for anything but **the Hour, that it should come upon them suddenly while they do not notice?** **On that day friends will be enemies to one another — except those who guard themselves.**"

### بَغْتَةً — root: ب-غ-ت

**The sense of the root: to come suddenly, to catch unprepared.** *Baghatahu* — it came upon him without warning.

**And the arrangement must be recorded: *baghtatan wa hum lā yashʿurūn* — two qualifications, one upon the other.**

| Qualification | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| *Baghtatan* | The manner of **the event** — suddenly |
| *Wa hum lā yashʿurūn* | The state of **the people** — not noticing |

**The two do not say the same thing:** a thing can come suddenly and yet a person be on the alert. **The verse closes off both.**

The root **ش-ع-ر**: to become aware of something fine; *shaʿr* — hair. **The lexicographers make the link thus: *shuʿūr* is to sense a thing as fine as a hair.** *Shāʿir* (poet) is from the same root — one who notices what is fine.

### ٱلْأَخِلَّآء — root: خ-ل-ل

**The concrete sense of the root: a gap, a space, a hole.** *Khalal* — an interval, a gap, a flaw (the Turkish idiom *halel gelmek*, "to suffer impairment", uses this word). *Khilāl* — the space between two things.

**And the word *khalīl* (friend) comes from here. The lexicographers record two explanations:**

| Explanation | The link |
|---|---|
| 1 | Friendship enters into **a person's inner gaps**; love works into the interstices of the heart |
| 2 | *Khalla* — need, want; a friend is the one to whom you open your need |

**I transmit both explanations as they have been transmitted.**

**And the picture of the word is worth recording:** *khalīl*, in the picture of the root, is the one who **gets into the gaps.** **Friendship, that is, has been named as the closing of distance.**

**And the verse chooses this word and says the opposite of it: *baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ʿaduww*.**

### Friendships turning about — a comparison with al-Aḥqāf

**Something similar was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/6:** *wa idhā ḥushira'n-nāsu kānū lahum aʿdāʾan wa kānū bi-ʿibādatihim kāfirīn* — "and when people are gathered, they will be enemies to them and will disown their having been served."

**The finding recorded there was this:** *"and in the end the relation is not merely broken, it is turned about… what is to be recorded here is that the sūra begins working the idea of 'the thing you took for a support' on its very first page."*

**I compare the two verses:**

| | **al-Aḥqāf 46/6** | **al-Zukhruf 43/67** |
|---|---|---|
| The parties | **A person and what he served** | **A person and his friend** |
| The kind of relation | Worship — vertical | **Friendship — horizontal** |
| What happens | Enmity + disowning | **Enmity** |
| Is there an exception | **No** | **Yes: *illa'l-muttaqīn*** |

**Two differences are worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:**

**First, the kind of relation.** What was broken in al-Aḥqāf was a relation of worship — and that it was unreciprocated was said in the verse itself (*wa hum ʿan duʿāʾihim ghāfilūn*). **What is broken in al-Zukhruf is a mutual relation.** Friendship has two sides.

**Second, the exception.** There was no exception in the sentence in al-Aḥqāf. **In al-Zukhruf there is: *illa'l-muttaqīn*.**

**And the setting of the exception changes what the sentence says:** the verse does not say "friendship is empty". **It says "one kind of friendship will not stand on that day"** — and it says which kind will.

### Its link with the question of measure

**And this verse attaches to the sūra's main question — I record it as my own reading.**

**Friendship too is a measure.** Who is with whom marks a position in this world. The objection in the thirty-first verse was also an objection about position, and Pharaoh in the fifty-second was counting a position too.

**The sixty-seventh verse says what these positions will be when that day comes: the relations reverse direction.**

**And the only measure that stands is the same as the measure named in the thirty-fifth verse: *al-muttaqīn*.**

| Verse | Expression | What remains |
|---|---|---|
| 35 | *wa'l-ākhiratu ʿinda rabbika **li'l-muttaqīn*** | **The hereafter** |
| 67 | *baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ʿaduwwun **illa'l-muttaqīn*** | **Friendship** |

**The same word, in two separate exceptions.** This is a verifiable datum of the text.

## 43/68-70 — يَٰعِبَادِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ تَحْزَنُونَ · ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا وَكَانُوا۟ مُسْلِمِينَ · ٱدْخُلُوا۟ ٱلْجَنَّةَ أَنتُمْ وَأَزْوَٰجُكُمْ تُحْبَرُونَ

*Yā ʿibādi lā khawfun ʿalaykumu'l-yawma wa lā antum taḥzanūn · Alladhīna āmanū bi-āyātinā wa kānū muslimīn · Udkhulu'l-jannata antum wa azwājukum tuḥbarūn*

"**My servants! Today there is no fear upon you, nor shall you grieve.** They who believed in Our signs and **had surrendered.** **Enter the Garden — you and your spouses — and be gladdened.**"

### يَٰعِبَادِ — the address itself

**The address is direct: *yā ʿibādi* — "my servants".**

**And the word completes the sūra's ع-ب-د pattern:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | *min **ʿibādihi** juzʾā* | Assigning a part out of the servants |
| 19 | ***ʿibādu'r-Raḥmān*** | The angels |
| 45 | *ālihatan **yuʿbadūn*** | The supposed gods that are served |
| 59 | *in huwa illā* **ʿabdun** | The son of Mary |
| 64 | *fa'**ʿbudūh*** | A command |
| 68 | ***yā ʿibādi*** | **An address** |

**Six occurrences, one root.** This is a verifiable datum of the text.

**And I add as an observation:** throughout the sūra the word "servant" was argued about as a **position** — are the angels servants, is the son of Mary a servant. **In the sixty-eighth verse the same word comes as an address, and this time it turns to the one addressed.**

### لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْكُمْ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ تَحْزَنُونَ

**This construction occurs in many places in the Qurʾān and was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/13. I repeat the finding there:**

> *"This construction closes off both directions of time: *khawf* belongs to the future, *ḥuzn* to the past. That is, neither what waits ahead nor what is left behind."*

**I add here a note particular to this sūra:** the expression comes here with the qualification *al-yawm* (today). **A time, that is, is specified.** In al-Aḥqāf there was no such qualification.

### وَكَانُوا۟ مُسْلِمِينَ — a difference of form

**In the sixty-ninth verse two verbs stand side by side, and their forms differ:**

| Verb | Form | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| *āmanū* | **Perfect** | Done — belief |
| *kānū muslimīn* | ***Kāna* + participle** | **A continuing state** — being in surrender |

**In Arabic the structure *kāna* + noun conveys continuity.**

**I record this as an observation about grammar and link it with `046-ahkaf.md` 46/13:** there was *qālū rabbuna'llāhu **thumma**'staqāmū*, and this was recorded: *"between the saying and the standing straight there is a span of time: saying happens in a moment, standing straight goes on."*

**The same structure is built here by another route: *āmanū* (once) and *kānū muslimīn* (continuing).**

### تُحْبَرُون — root: ح-ب-ر

**The root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

**The lexicographers record several branches of the root, and all of them meet at one point:**

- **حَبْر / حِبْر** — ink; and **a fine trace, an adornment.** *Ḥibr* — the beautiful mark a thing leaves.
- **حَبْر** — a scholar, a learned man (particularly of the People of the Book: *aḥbār*). **The lexicographers connect this with "one who leaves a fine mark, who leaves a trace".**
- **حَبَرَهُ** — he gladdened him, adorned him, made him fair.
- **حَبْرَة / حُبُور** — **joy; and the trace joy leaves on the face.**

**The shared core: the fine trace that remains upon a thing.**

**And the pattern of the word in this verse is passive: *tuḥbarūn* — "you will be gladdened, you will be honoured".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the root's core of "trace" and the passive pattern:** the word does not merely say "joy". **It conveys joy's becoming visible, its showing on the face.**

**And it sets up a contrast within the sūra — this is a verifiable datum of the text:**

| Verse | Expression | What is on the face |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | *ẓalla **wajhuhu muswaddā*** | **A darkening** |
| 70 | ***tuḥbarūn*** | **The trace of joy** |

**In the seventeenth verse a face darkened at good news. In the seventieth, joy comes down on a face at a call.**

**The same root occurs in al-Rūm 30/15:** *fa-hum fī rawḍatin **yuḥbarūn***.

### أَنتُمْ وَأَزْوَٰجُكُمْ — spouses named together

**This verse was already cited in `056-vakia.md`, and this was recorded there:**

> *"Across the Qurʾān as a whole it is stated plainly that recompense is not divided by sex — al-Naḥl 16/97… There are also places in the descriptions of the Garden where spouses are named together: Yā-Sīn 36/56, al-Zukhruf 43/70 (*udkhulu'l-jannata antum wa azwājukum tuḥbarūn*), Ghāfir 40/8."*

**I rely on that treatment and add here what is particular to al-Zukhruf.**

**In the discussion of al-Wāqiʿa a note was entered on the language of the descriptions of the Garden, and I keep the same note here:** how much of the descriptions is literal and how much figurative has been debated in the classical commentaries, and **no preference was imposed there;** I impose none here either.

**What is particular to al-Zukhruf is this, and it is visible at the level of arrangement:**

**The imperative sentence begins with *udkhulū* (enter), and then the ones addressed are enumerated under two heads: *antum wa azwājukum*.**

**The pronoun *antum* is grammatically unnecessary** — since *udkhulū* is already a plural imperative and contains its subject. **Setting the pronoun down separately is for emphasis in Arabic, and here it has a task: so that *wa azwājukum* can be added.**

**The sentence, that is, names the first party separately in order to be able to add the second.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement.**

**The word *azwāj* occurred in the twelfth verse as *al-azwāja kullahā* (all the pairs).** **The same root at two different scales: in one place the pairedness of creation, in the other a person's spouse.**

**And the exegetes differ over the extent of the word *azwāj* here:**

| Reading | Who *azwājukum* are |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Their spouses in this world** — if they believed together |
| 2 | **Those paired with them** — the spouses of the Garden |
| 3 | **Their like, their equals** — in the sense of *zawj* as "counterpart, match" |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

## 43/71-73 — يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِم بِصِحَافٍ مِّن ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ ۖ وَفِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِيهِ ٱلْأَنفُسُ وَتَلَذُّ ٱلْأَعْيُنُ ۖ وَأَنتُمْ فِيهَا خَٰلِدُونَ · وَتِلْكَ ٱلْجَنَّةُ ٱلَّتِىٓ أُورِثْتُمُوهَا بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ · لَكُمْ فِيهَا فَٰكِهَةٌ كَثِيرَةٌ مِّنْهَا تَأْكُلُونَ

*Yuṭāfu ʿalayhim bi-ṣiḥāfin min dhahabin wa akwāb · Wa fīhā mā tashtahīhi'l-anfusu wa taladhdhu'l-aʿyun · Wa antum fīhā khālidūn · Wa tilka'l-jannatu'llatī ūrithtumūhā bimā kuntum taʿmalūn · Lakum fīhā fākihatun kathīratun minhā taʾkulūn*

"**Platters of gold and cups** are passed round among them. In it is all that **souls desire and eyes delight in.** And you are to abide in it. **This is the Garden given to you in inheritance for what you used to do.** In it you have abundant fruit; of it you eat."

### بِصِحَافٍ مِّن ذَهَبٍ — and the sūra's ring of gold

**And here the sūra's most striking reversal takes place.**

**In the thirty-third verse silver was named as a humiliation; in the fifty-third gold was demanded as a proof. In the seventy-first gold is given as a reward.**

**I complete here the table I set out above (in the discussion of 43/53), and from here I tie up the question that bears the sūra's name.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of the four occurrences:**

**The sūra does not speak ill of gold. The sūra's objection is to gold's being used as an *instrument of measure*.**

- **In the thirty-fifth verse** gold is a *matāʿ* — a passing benefit; and what is said there is not "it is evil" but **"it does not last"**.
- **In the fifty-third verse** gold is put **in the place of a proof**, and the sūra rejects this.
- **In the seventy-first verse** gold is given as a **reward** — that is, as part of an outcome, not as a measure.

**The difference is not in the object; it is in the work the object is put to.**

**صِحَاف — root ص-ح-ف.** *Ṣaḥfa* — a broad, flat dish. *Ṣaḥīfa* — a leaf, a page of writing. **The shared core: a broad, flat surface.**

**And this root is different from the root ص-ف-ح in the fifth verse of the sūra, though very close to it** — both rest on the picture of a "flat surface", with the letters transposed. **The lexicographers record such affinities (*qalb*, the transposition of letters); but I do not reckon the two roots one. I give this as a note of resemblance, not as a claim of derivation.**

**أَكْوَاب** — the plural of *kūb*. **It was treated in `088-gasiye.md` at 88/14, and this verse was cited there:**

> *"The detail the lexicographers record is important: a *kūb* is a cup **with no handle and no spout**. One with a handle is called an *ibrīq*, and other names are given to what resembles a jug. The word occurs in several places in the Qurʾān: al-Zukhruf 43/71, al-Insān 76/15, al-Wāqiʿa 56/18."*

**I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

### مَا تَشْتَهِيهِ ٱلْأَنفُسُ وَتَلَذُّ ٱلْأَعْيُنُ

**The expression removes the limits of the description: what is given is not an enumerated list but a scope.**

**Two channels are named, and they differ:**

| Channel | Verb | What |
|---|---|---|
| *al-anfus* — souls | *tashtahī* — **to feel appetite** | **A desire that comes from within** |
| *al-aʿyun* — eyes | *taladhdhu* — **to take delight** | **A pleasure that comes from outside** |

The root **ش-ه-و**: intense desire, appetite. The root **ل-ذ-ذ**: to please, to taste.

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement:** the first describes the one who wants, the second the one who receives. **Both the demand and its answer, that is, are in the same sentence.**

**And a variant reading is transmitted: *tashtahīhi* (with the pronoun) and *tashtahī* (without).** **Since it produces no difference in meaning I do not dwell on it.**

### أُورِثْتُمُوهَا — root: و-ر-ث

**The root has been treated in the corpus** (`057-hadid.md`, `089-fecr.md`). **Its sense: to come into an inheritance, to receive what is left by another.**

**And the word's use in this verse needs dwelling on, because it carries a tension.**

***Ūrithtumūhā* — passive: "it was given to you in inheritance".** An inheritance is normally what comes **without being worked for.**

**But the sentence continues: *bimā kuntum taʿmalūn* — "for what you used to do".**

**In the same sentence, then, there is both inheritance and deed.**

**The exegetes explain this tension thus, and I transmit it as it has been transmitted:** the word says at one and the same time that the Garden is not something **bought against a desert**, and that it is not given **for nothing** either. The deed is a cause, not a price.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two expressions stand in the same sentence:** the verse ties the recompense to **an act** (consistently with the sūra's question of measure: not to lineage, wealth or rank), but names **the manner of its giving** an inheritance.

**And this completes the distinction of the thirty-second verse:**

| Verse | Distribution | By what |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | *qasamnā … maʿīshatahum* | **Given** — not chosen |
| 72 | *ūrithtumūhā **bimā kuntum taʿmalūn*** | **By what was done** |

**Two distributions, two distinct measures.** The sūra's whole objection was to the first being put in the place of the second. **The seventy-second verse states the second outright.**

### فَٰكِهَةٌ كَثِيرَةٌ

The root **ف-ك-ه**: the lexicographers record two branches of the root — **fruit** and **pleasant talk, jesting** (*fukāha*). **As the shared core they give "what pleases, what is not a basic need but gives enjoyment".**

**This distinction is worth recording and was treated in `056-vakia.md` in connection with the descriptions of the Garden:** *fākiha* is not a staple food. **It is not bread but fruit** — that is, not a need but a surplus.

**And this attaches to the sūra's question of measure — I record it as my own reading:** most of the things enumerated in the description of the Garden are **surplus**: golden platters, ornament, fruit. **The sūra had named such surpluses in this world a *matāʿ* (35).** Here things of the same kind are given, with no term stated: *wa antum fīhā **khālidūn***.

**Again the difference is not in the object but in the duration.**

## 43/74-76 — إِنَّ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ فِى عَذَابِ جَهَنَّمَ خَٰلِدُونَ · لَا يُفَتَّرُ عَنْهُمْ وَهُمْ فِيهِ مُبْلِسُونَ · وَمَا ظَلَمْنَٰهُمْ وَلَٰكِن كَانُوا۟ هُمُ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ

*Inna'l-mujrimīna fī ʿadhābi jahannama khālidūn · Lā yufattaru ʿanhum wa hum fīhi mublisūn · Wa mā ẓalamnāhum wa lākin kānū humu'ẓ-ẓālimīn*

"**The guilty abide in the punishment of Hell.** It is not **slackened** for them, and in it they are **without hope.** **We did not wrong them; it was they themselves who did the wrong.**"

### Arrangement: two scenes linked by the same word

**The seventy-first verse ended with *wa antum fīhā khālidūn*. The seventy-fourth begins with *fī ʿadhābi jahannama khālidūn*.**

**The same word, at the end of one scene and the beginning of the other.** This is a verifiable datum of arrangement.

The root **خ-ل-د**: to remain long in a state, to go on and on. **The lexicographers also record the root's sense of "not ageing, not changing".**

**م-ج-ر-م** — from the root *jurm* (ج-ر-م). **The concrete sense of the root: to pluck fruit from a tree, to cut it off.** *Jarama'n-nakhla* — he harvested the palm. **And from there: to earn, and to earn something evil — a crime.**

**The picture of the word is worth recording:** *mujrim*, in the picture of the root, is one who **plucks something off and takes it for himself.** **Crime, that is, is named as something "earned".**

### لَا يُفَتَّرُ عَنْهُمْ — root: ف-ت-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to slacken, to lose one's force, to let up.** *Fatara'l-ḥarru* — the heat abated. *Futūr* — slackness, a let-up.

**And the lexicographers record the root's sense of "tepidness" as well: *māʾun fātir* — water neither hot nor cold.**

**In Form II, passive: *yufattaru* — "it is not slackened, not lightened".**

**And that the verb comes with the preposition *ʿan* must be recorded: *lā yufattaru ʿanhum* — "it is not lightened from off them".** What is at issue, that is, is not the punishment itself but **the pressure upon them.**

### مُبْلِسُون — root: ب-ل-س

**The sense of the root: to be cut off from hope, to be at a loss and fall silent, not to know what to say.** *Ablasa* — he fell into despair and was silent.

**The lexicographers record these two aspects of the word together, and this is worth recording: *iblās* is not despair alone but the silence that comes of despair.**

**And the lexicographers also record the relation of the name *Iblīs* to this root: "the one who has despaired of mercy".** **I transmit this derivation as it has been transmitted; on the origin of the name (whether it is Arabic, or a foreign word arabicised) there is disagreement among the lexicographers, and I issue no ruling in that disagreement.**

**And the word must be read together with the seventy-seventh verse:** there the same people will **speak** — *wa nādaw yā Mālik*.

**I record this as an observation:** the seventy-fifth verse describes silence, the seventy-seventh a call. **The two verses between them prepare the transition.**

### وَمَا ظَلَمْنَٰهُمْ وَلَٰكِن كَانُوا۟ هُمُ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ

**The arrangement in the second half of the sentence must be recorded, and it is one of the strongest patterns of restriction in Arabic:**

> *kānū **humu'ẓ-ẓālimīn***

**The pronoun *hum* (the pronoun of separation, *ḍamīr al-faṣl*) together with the definiteness of *al-ẓālimīn* (with *al-*) produces a restriction: "it was *they* who did the wrong" — no one else.**

**I record this as a point of word order.**

The root **ظ-ل-م** comes for the third time in the sūra and completes the table I set out above (in the discussion of 43/65).

**And the core of the root is meaningful here:** the lexicographers describe *ẓulm* as **"putting a thing somewhere other than where it belongs"**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this description of the root:** the whole subject of the sūra is things being put in the wrong place — a daughter placed low, angels placed as females, wealth placed as evidence, gold placed as proof. **And the word the sūra uses in naming this condition says exactly that in its root.**

**I give this link as my own reading; the wording of the verse makes no such link.**

## 43/77 — وَنَادَوْا۟ يَٰمَٰلِكُ لِيَقْضِ عَلَيْنَا رَبُّكَ ۖ قَالَ إِنَّكُم مَّٰكِثُونَ

*Wa nādaw yā Māliku li-yaqḍi ʿalaynā rabbuk · Qāla innakum mākithūn*

"**They called out: 'Mālik! Let your Lord make an end of us!'** He said: **'You are staying.'**"

**This verse has been cited in several places in the corpus** (`087-ala.md`, `069-hakka.md`, `074-muddessir.md`); here it is treated in detail for the first time.

### يَٰمَٰلِكُ — a name or a description?

**The word is understood as the name of a keeper, and it is transmitted so in most of the classical commentaries.**

The root **م-ل-ك**: to own a thing, to have the power of disposal over it. **The root was treated in detail in `067-mulk.md`, and verse 85 of this sūra was cited there.**

**The other information the Qurʾān gives about the keepers of Hell was gathered in `074-muddessir.md`, and this verse was cited there:**

> *"The other information the Qurʾān gives about the keepers of Hell: 'over it are angels harsh and severe' (al-Taḥrīm 66/6); and a call: 'Mālik!' (al-Zukhruf 43/77)."*

**I rely on that treatment and keep the same limit: I produce no description beyond what the text states.**

**A variant reading is transmitted: a reading in the form *yā Māli* (with the final *kāf* dropped) has also been recorded.** **In Arabic, dropping a letter from the end of a noun in the vocative (*tarkhīm*) is a known usage. It produces no difference in meaning.**

### لِيَقْضِ عَلَيْنَا رَبُّكَ — the form of the request

**And the construction of the request must be treated in detail, because every word of it says something.**

| Element | What it says |
|---|---|
| *li-yaqḍi* | **The lām of command** — "let him make an end" |
| *ʿalaynā* | **Upon us** — we are the ones to be ended |
| ***rabbuk*** | **"Your Lord"** — not *rabbunā* |

**The last row is worth recording, and the exegetes dwell on it.**

**The request is framed not by saying "our Lord" but by saying "your Lord".**

**The readings transmitted for this expression are these:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Seeking an intermediary** — since they cannot call out directly themselves, they ask help of the keeper |
| 2 | **An admission of distance** — a record, from their own mouths, that the relation is broken |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**But this can be recorded, and I give it as an observation about the arrangement:** in the sixty-eighth verse of the sūra the address was direct — *yā ʿibādi* ("my servants"). **In the seventy-seventh the call is made to a keeper, and the Lord is named as someone else's Lord.**

**The difference between the two scenes is not only place but the direction of the address.**

The root **ق-ض-ي**: to complete a matter, to finish it, to bring a verdict to its conclusion. **And the construction *qaḍā ʿalayhi* means "he finished him off, he killed him".**

**What is being asked for, then, is plain and must be recorded: an ending.** **The request is not for a deliverance but for an end.**

### إِنَّكُم مَّٰكِثُونَ — root: م-ك-ث

**The root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

**The sense of the root: to remain in a place, to wait, to linger; to stay without haste.**

- **مَكَثَ** — he remained, he waited.
- **مُكْث / مَكْث** — stopping and waiting in a place; and **acting slowly, not hurrying.**
- **مَاكِث** — one who remains, one who waits.

**The root occurs in several places in the Qurʾān and in most of them in the sense of waiting:** al-Naml 27/22 (*fa-makatha ghayra baʿīd* — "he did not stay away long"); Ṭāhā 20/10 (*umkuthū* — "stay, wait"); al-Isrāʾ 17/106 (*li-taqraʾahu ʿala'n-nāsi ʿalā **mukth*** — "that you may recite it to people slowly").

**The last example is worth recording, and the comparison is fruitful:**

| Place | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| al-Isrāʾ 17/106 | *ʿalā mukth* | Reciting **slowly, without haste** |
| al-Zukhruf 43/77 | *mākithūn* | **Staying, waiting** |

**The same root: in one place a tempo of recitation, in another a condition.**

**And the word the answer chooses must be recorded — I give this as my own reading:**

**The answer does not say "no". It does not say "you cannot get out". It says "you are staying".**

***Mākith*, in the picture of the root, is the one who is waiting.** And the request was precisely for the waiting to end.

**The answer, then, gives back not the opposite of the request but its subject: the waiting continues.**

**And in `069-hakka.md` this verse was linked with another** (*yā laytahā kānati'l-qāḍiya* — al-Ḥāqqa 69/27, "would that it had been the end"); **there this verse was cited as support and not presented as binding.** **I keep the same note.**

**This verse was cited in `087-ala.md` as well** — there together with *lā yamūtu fīhā wa lā yaḥyā* (al-Aʿlā 87/13). **The two verses say the same thing: it neither ends nor goes on.**

## 43/78-80 — لَقَدْ جِئْنَٰكُم بِٱلْحَقِّ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَكُمْ لِلْحَقِّ كَٰرِهُونَ · أَمْ أَبْرَمُوٓا۟ أَمْرًا فَإِنَّا مُبْرِمُونَ · أَمْ يَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّا لَا نَسْمَعُ سِرَّهُمْ وَنَجْوَىٰهُم ۚ بَلَىٰ وَرُسُلُنَا لَدَيْهِمْ يَكْتُبُونَ

*Laqad jiʾnākum bi'l-ḥaqqi wa lākinna aktharakum li'l-ḥaqqi kārihūn · Am abramū amran fa-innā mubrimūn · Am yaḥsabūna annā lā nasmaʿu sirrahum wa najwāhum · Balā wa rusulunā ladayhim yaktubūn*

"We brought you **the truth**, but most of you **had no liking** for the truth. Or have they **made a matter fast?** Then **We too make fast.** Or do they suppose **that We do not hear their secrets and their whisperings?** Indeed We do! **And Our messengers are with them, writing.**"

### لَقَدْ جِئْنَٰكُم بِٱلْحَقِّ — whose sentence is it?

**The exegetes differ over where this sentence is spoken:**

| Reading | Who, where |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Spoken there** — the continuation of the seventy-seventh verse, to those in Hell |
| 2 | **Spoken here** — a return to this world, addressing those addressed |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**The content of the sentence must be recorded, and its arrangement says something:**

> *wa lākinna aktharakum **li'l-ḥaqqi kārihūn***

***Li'l-ḥaqq* — the prepositional phrase (*jārr wa majrūr*) has come before the active participle (*kārihūn*).** In Arabic this fronting conveys **specification.**

**The sentence, then, does not say "most of you had no liking"; it says "what you had no liking for was the truth itself".**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement and link it with the thirtieth verse of the sūra:** there it was said *wa lammā jāʾahumu'l-ḥaqqu qālū hādhā siḥr*. **The two verses say the same thing: the name of what is refused is put by the text.**

The root **ك-ر-ه** was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/216 and 2/256, and also in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/15: **dislike, difficulty, heaviness.**

### أَمْ أَبْرَمُوٓا۟ أَمْرًا — root: ب-ر-م

**The root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

**The concrete sense of the root comes from rope-making:** *abrama'l-ḥabl* — he **twisted the rope double and made it strong.**

- **مُبْرَم** — a rope twisted double, a strong rope. Its opposite is **مُسْحَل** — single-stranded.
- **أَبْرَمَ الأَمْر** — he made the matter fast, he settled it. **A picture close to the English idiom "to tie up a matter".**
- **بَرِيم** — a band woven of two-coloured thread.

**And the word's work in this verse is exactly this picture: making a matter certain by twisting it, tightening it.**

**And the answer is given with the same word: *fa-innā mubrimūn*.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement:** the verse does not use a different verb. **It gives the same verb straight back.**

**This is a known device in the Qurʾān — in `046-ahkaf.md` the giving back of the word *ifk* to its author was treated** (46/11 and 46/28), **and something similar was recorded in places such as `068-kalem.md` and `086-tarik.md`** (*yakīdūna kaydan wa akīdu kaydā*).

**And what is to be recorded in this verse is this, and it attaches to the sūra's question of measure:** *ibrām* is making a rope strong with one's own hand. **The work the other side does, that is, is sound by its own measure.** The verse does not deny it. **It only says that the same act works at another scale as well.**

**And the *nakth* (unpicking a rope) in the fiftieth verse and the *ibrām* (twisting a rope) in this one come from the same field.** The two words are from different roots (ن-ك-ث / ب-ر-م); **what I record is that the sūra uses, in two separate places, words that rest on the picture of rope-making.** I give this as an observation, not as a link the text makes.

### أَمْ يَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّا لَا نَسْمَعُ سِرَّهُمْ وَنَجْوَىٰهُمْ

**With a third *am* comes a third question.** This particle is used often in the sūra: 16, 21, 52, 58, 79, 80.

The root **ح-س-ب** occurred in the thirty-seventh verse (*yaḥsabūna annahum muhtadūn*). **The same verb, in the same pattern.**

**The two occurrences can be set side by side:**

| Verse | What is supposed |
|---|---|
| 37 | **About themselves** — that they are on the right way |
| 80 | **About the other side** — that they are not heard |

**Both are errors of reckoning; one about oneself, one about what is outside.**

### سِرَّهُمْ وَنَجْوَىٰهُمْ — two distinct kinds of secrecy

**Two words stand side by side, and they are not the same thing. The difference must be recorded:**

| Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| *sirr* | **س-ر-ر** | **What is held within** — told to no one |
| *najwā* | **ن-ج-و** | **Whispering** — spoken among a few |

The concrete sense of the root **ن-ج-و**: **high ground, a rise; and separating a thing off and saving it.** *Najāt* (deliverance) is from the same root. **The lexicographers explain *najwā* thus: two people drawing apart from the rest and speaking in a space of their own** — that is, setting speech apart.

**The root was treated in `058-mucadele.md`** (where the subject of *najwā* was taken up in detail); I rely on that treatment.

**And the root س-ر-ر is the same root as *surur* (couches) in the thirty-fourth verse.** **The lexicographers make the link between *sirr* (secrecy) and *sarīr* (couch) in differing ways; I issue no ruling on this relation.**

**The choice of words conveys a scope, and I record this as my own reading:** the verse closes off both layers — what has never been spoken and what has been spoken to a few. **It leaves no gap between them.**

### بَلَىٰ وَرُسُلُنَا لَدَيْهِمْ يَكْتُبُونَ

***Balā* in Arabic is the affirmative answer given to a negative question: "no, it is not so — on the contrary".**

**And the continuation of the answer must be recorded: the answer does not content itself with saying "We hear", it adds a record too.**

| Layer | Expression |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hearing** — *nasmaʿu* (in the question) |
| 2 | **Writing** — *yaktubūn* |

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement:** hearing is momentary, writing is lasting. **The verse lays the two one on top of the other.**

The root **ك-ت-ب** comes for the second time in the sūra. **In the nineteenth verse there was *sa-tuktabu shahādatuhum* (their testimony will be written down).**

**Two occurrences, at the two ends of the sūra, doing the same work:**

| Verse | What is written |
|---|---|
| 19 | **Testimony** — what is said openly |
| 80 | **Secret and whisper** — what is not said openly |

**The sūra, that is, sets up the idea of a record twice: once for open speech, once for hidden speech.** This is a verifiable datum of the text.

**The question of the record was treated in detail in the sections `082-infitar.md` (*kirāman kātibīn*) and `083-mutaffifin.md` (*kitābun marqūm*); I rely on those treatments and do not repeat them. The same subject was taken up in `050-kaf.md` at 50/17-18 (*raqībun ʿatīd*) as well.**

## 43/81-83 — قُلْ إِن كَانَ لِلرَّحْمَٰنِ وَلَدٌ فَأَنَا۠ أَوَّلُ ٱلْعَٰبِدِينَ · سُبْحَٰنَ رَبِّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَرْشِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ · فَذَرْهُمْ يَخُوضُوا۟ وَيَلْعَبُوا۟ حَتَّىٰ يُلَٰقُوا۟ يَوْمَهُمُ ٱلَّذِى يُوعَدُونَ

*Qul in kāna li'r-Raḥmāni waladun fa-anā awwalu'l-ʿābidīn · Subḥāna rabbi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi rabbi'l-ʿarshi ʿammā yaṣifūn · Fa-dharhum yakhūḍū wa yalʿabū ḥattā yulāqū yawmahumu'lladhī yūʿadūn*

"Say: **If al-Raḥmān had a child, I would be the first to serve him.** Glory be to the Lord of the heavens and the earth, the Lord of the Throne, **far above what they ascribe.** Leave them to plunge and to play **until they meet the day they are promised.**"

### The construction of the conditional sentence — a place that needs careful treatment

**Because of its construction this verse is among the most debated in the classical commentaries. I shall set the disagreement out in a table and show the logic of each reading separately.**

**The structure of the sentence is a conditional:**

> **Condition:** *in kāna li'r-Raḥmāni waladun* — "if al-Raḥmān had a child"
> **Apodosis:** *fa-anā awwalu'l-ʿābidīn* — "I would be the first to serve"

**Now the transmitted readings:**

| Reading | What *in* is | What *ʿābidīn* is | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Conditional** | The particle of condition | **Those who serve** | Suppose it were so; if it were, I would be the first to comply. **But since it is not so, there is no apodosis either** |
| **2 — Negation** | *In* = *mā* (negation) | Those who serve | "Al-Raḥmān has **no** child; and I am the first of those who serve" |
| **3 — *ʿābid* from a different root** | The particle of condition | **Those who refuse, who will not accept** | "If it were so, I would be **the first to refuse** it" |

**The ground of the third reading is a lexical matter and must be transmitted:** the lexicographers record that the verb *ʿabida* (عَبِدَ), as distinct from *ʿabada* (to serve), carries the sense **"to be angry, to be disgusted, to refuse"**. **On this reading *ʿābid* means "one who refuses".** **I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted; it is also recorded that it is not widespread among the lexicographers.**

**I do not impose a preference. But I must open out the logic of the first reading separately, because the force of the verse lies there.**

### The logic of the first reading: an impossible condition

**In Arabic a conditional sentence turns into an instrument of refusal when it is known that the condition has not been fulfilled. In logic this is called *taʿlīq bi'l-muḥāl* — "attaching to the impossible".**

**The Qurʾān uses this method elsewhere too, and the examples are plain:**

- *Law kāna fīhimā ālihatun illa'llāhu la-fasadatā* (al-Anbiyāʾ 21/22) — "were there gods in them other than God, the order of both would break down."
- *Wa mā kāna maʿahu min ilāhin idhan la-dhahaba kullu ilāhin bimā khalaq* (al-Muʾminūn 23/91).

**The structure is this: the claim is treated as if accepted, its consequence is shown, and the impossibility of the consequence refutes the claim.**

**But what is done in this verse is somewhat different, and I record this as my own reading:**

**What is shown here as the consequence is not a contradiction but a submission.**

**The sentence, that is, says this: "if it were so, I would not be the one objecting to it — I would be the first to accept."**

**And what this carries is the following, and it is consistent with the whole question of the sūra:** the reason for the objection is not **obstinacy.** It is not a preference or a taking of sides. **The objection comes from the claim's not being true.**

**Moreover the sentence is the counterpart, in the person of the Prophet, of the question of measure the sūra has worked from the beginning:** throughout the sūra the other side drew its verdicts from **position** — from who was great, from who brought what. **This sentence says that position does not determine the verdict:** "if it were true, I would be the first to accept it."

**I mark this as my own reading; its ground is the condition-apodosis structure of the sentence.**

**And the word *awwal* in the expression *awwalu'l-ʿābidīn* must be recorded:** the verse does not say "I too would serve", it says **"I would be the first"**. **That is, foremost in the line.**

**And throughout the sūra "being in front" had been argued about as a measure** — *al-awwalīn* (6, 8), *salafan* (56), *ābāʾanā* (22, 23). **Here the same word is used for an order of submission.**

### سُبْحَٰنَ رَبِّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ — the second glorification

**And the word *subḥāna* comes for the second time in the sūra.** In the thirteenth verse it was in the mouth of a person mounting his beast; here it stands against an ascription.

**I complete here the table I set out above (in the discussion of 43/13).**

**And the object of the glorification must be recorded: *ʿammā yaṣifūn* — "above what they describe".**

The root **و-ص-ف**: to characterise a thing, to describe it. *Waṣf* — a quality. **And the lexicographers record the root's core of "setting a thing out as it is".**

**I give this as an observation:** the verse names what the other side does **describing**. **The matter, that is, is set not as a matter of knowledge but as a matter of characterisation** — and in the nineteenth verse of the sūra the ground of that characterisation was asked for (*a-shahidū khalqahum*).

***Rabbi'l-ʿarsh* — and the word ʿarsh occurs in many places in the Qurʾān.** **A note was entered on the question of the Throne in `085-buruc.md`, and I keep the same note: no description of its modality is produced.**

### فَذَرْهُمْ يَخُوضُوا۟ وَيَلْعَبُوا۟

**The command is a command to leave off: *dharhum* — "leave them".**

The root **و-ذ-ر**: to leave, to abandon. **The lexicographers record that the perfect and the verbal noun of this verb are not used — it occurs only in the imperative and imperfect patterns.**

The concrete sense of the root **خ-و-ض**: **to go into water, to plunge; particularly to step on foot into muddy or deep water.** *Khāḍa'l-māʾ* — he plunged into the water.

**And from there: to plunge into a subject without knowing its measure; to be sunk in idle talk.**

**The picture of the word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** *khawḍ* is entering a place **on foot** — not by swimming. **That is, stepping into water whose bottom is not known.**

The root **ل-ع-ب**: to play, to occupy oneself with something not serious. **The lexicographers record the root's relation to "spittle" (*luʿāb*): what runs from a child's mouth and serves no purpose.** **I transmit this derivation as it has been transmitted.**

**Two verbs side by side, and both attach to the sūra's question of measure — I record this as my own reading:** *khawḍ* is entering without measure, *laʿb* is occupying oneself without measure. **Once more the sūra names the other side's stance with a word of measure** — in the fifth verse *musrifīn* (those who overstep), in the twentieth *yakhruṣūn* (those who estimate by eye), and here *yakhūḍū wa yalʿabū*.

**The same command occurs in al-Ṭūr 52/45 and in al-Maʿārij 70/42 as well; it was treated in `052-tur.md` and `070-mearic.md`.**

## 43/84-86 — وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ إِلَٰهٌ وَفِى ٱلْأَرْضِ إِلَٰهٌ ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلْحَكِيمُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · وَتَبَارَكَ ٱلَّذِى لَهُۥ مُلْكُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا وَعِندَهُۥ عِلْمُ ٱلسَّاعَةِ وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · وَلَا يَمْلِكُ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِهِ ٱلشَّفَٰعَةَ إِلَّا مَن شَهِدَ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ

*Wa huwa'lladhī fi's-samāʾi ilāhun wa fi'l-arḍi ilāh · Wa huwa'l-ḥakīmu'l-ʿalīm · Wa tabāraka'lladhī lahu mulku's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa mā baynahumā wa ʿindahu ʿilmu's-sāʿati wa ilayhi turjaʿūn · Wa lā yamliku'lladhīna yadʿūna min dūnihi'sh-shafāʿata illā man shahida bi'l-ḥaqqi wa hum yaʿlamūn*

"**He is God in the heaven and God on the earth.** He is wise, knowing. **Blessed is He to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and all that is between them!** The knowledge of the Hour is with Him, and to Him you will be returned. **Those they call upon besides Him have no power of intercession** — except those who bear witness to the truth, and know it."

### فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ إِلَٰهٌ وَفِى ٱلْأَرْضِ إِلَٰهٌ — the work of the repetition

**The word comes twice: *ilāh*, *ilāh*.**

**The sentence could have been built as "He is God in the heaven and on the earth". It is not: the two places are enumerated separately, and the word is repeated in each.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement and propose a ground for it as my own reading:** what was argued throughout the sūra was the question of **position** — who stands where, who is above, who below. **This sentence abolishes position: the place changes, the attribute does not.**

**And this connects with the fourth verse:** there, of the Book, it was said both *fī ummi'l-kitābi ladaynā* (above, with Us) and *qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan* (below, in a language). **The tension at the opening of the sūra is met at its close with a single sentence.**

**I mark this as my own reading.**

***Al-ḥakīmu'l-ʿalīm* — two attributes.** **In the fourth verse there was *ʿaliyyun ḥakīm*; in the ninth, *al-ʿazīzu'l-ʿalīm*.** **Three pairs in three places, then, and *ḥakīm* and *ʿalīm* are joined here.**

### وَتَبَارَكَ — root: ب-ر-ك

**The concrete sense of the root: a camel's kneeling down, settling in a place and staying there.** *Baraka'l-baʿīr* — the camel knelt. *Mubārak* — one on whom blessing has **settled**. *Birka* — a pool; the place where water gathers and stays.

**And *tabāraka* is in Form VI; the lexicographers explain this pattern here as "He is exalted indeed, blessed indeed", and record that the verb is used of God alone.**

**The picture of the root is worth recording:** blessing, in the picture of the root, is not **increase** but **staying**. Kneeling in a place and remaining there.

**`067-mulk.md` opens with this verb (*tabāraka'lladhī bi-yadihi'l-mulk*), and verse 85 of this sūra was cited there:**

> *"'Blessed is He to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth' (al-Zukhruf 43/85)… the verse does not say *of what* the dominion is. It does not say 'the dominion of the heavens and the earth'; it says only 'al-mulk'. In al-Zukhruf 43/85 a construct was made; here none is."*

**I rely on that treatment and complete the link.**

**And the word *mulk* comes for the second time in the sūra: in the fifty-first verse it was in Pharaoh's mouth — *a-laysa lī mulku Miṣr*.**

**I set the two occurrences side by side, and this is one of the clearest oppositions in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Extent |
|---|---|---|
| 51 | *lī **mulku Miṣr*** | **One country** |
| 85 | *lahu **mulku's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa mā baynahumā*** | **The heavens, the earth and what is between them** |

**The same word, two scales.** And the sūra compares them, thirty-four verses apart, with the same word. **This is a verifiable datum of the text.**

**And the comparison itself is the sūra's main question: the real weight of what is placed on a scale.**

### وَعِندَهُۥ عِلْمُ ٱلسَّاعَةِ

**The question of the sixty-first verse closes here.** I complete the link I set out above: **a sign is given, a time is not.**

**This verse was cited in `079-naziat.md`; I rely on that treatment.**

### وَلَا يَمْلِكُ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِهِ ٱلشَّفَٰعَةَ

**And the root *malik* comes for the third time — this time as a verb: *lā yamlikū* — "they have no power over it, it is not in their hands".**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "they cannot intercede". **It says "they are not *mālik* of intercession"** — that is, intercession is not at their disposal.

**This accords with the line treated in places such as `085-buruc.md` and `074-muddessir.md`; I rely on those treatments.**

The concrete sense of the root **ش-ف-ع**: **to make double, to pair what is single.** *Shafʿ* — even (its opposite being *witr* — odd; it occurs in al-Fajr 89/3 and was treated in `089-fecr.md`). **Intercession, then, in the picture of the root: someone joining the one who is alone.**

### إِلَّا مَن شَهِدَ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ

**The exegetes differ over whom the exception refers to:**

| Reading | To whom the exception refers |
|---|---|
| 1 | To ***alladhīna yadʿūn*** — that is, to "those called upon"; excepting those among them who bear witness to the truth (such as Jesus, the angels) |
| 2 | **To those who will be interceded for** — those who bear witness to the truth attain intercession |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But the condition must be recorded, and it has two heads:**

| Condition | Expression |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Shahida bi'l-ḥaqq* — **bearing witness to the truth** |
| 2 | *Wa hum yaʿlamūn* — **knowingly** |

**The second condition is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** testimony is not enough; **knowing testimony** is made the condition.

**And this connects with the twentieth verse of the sūra:** there it was said of the other side *mā lahum bi-dhālika min **ʿilm*** (they have no knowledge of this). **The same word at the two ends of the sūra: in one place its lack, in the other the condition.**

## 43/87-88 — وَلَئِن سَأَلْتَهُم مَّنْ خَلَقَهُمْ لَيَقُولُنَّ ٱللَّهُ ۖ فَأَنَّىٰ يُؤْفَكُونَ · وَقِيلِهِۦ يَٰرَبِّ إِنَّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ قَوْمٌ لَّا يُؤْمِنُونَ

*Wa la-in saʾaltahum man khalaqahum la-yaqūlunna'llāh · Fa-annā yuʾfakūn · Wa qīlihi yā rabbi inna hāʾulāʾi qawmun lā yuʾminūn*

"And if you ask them **who created them**, they will surely say **'God'.** How then are they **turned away?** And his **word**: 'My Lord! These are a people who do not believe.'"

### The return of the ninth verse

**And the sūra repeats the sentence of the ninth verse almost word for word.**

**I complete here the comparison I set out above (in the discussion of 43/9):**

| | 43/9 | 43/87 |
|---|---|---|
| Question | *man khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* | *man **khalaqahum*** |
| Scale | **The universe** | **Themselves** |
| Answer | *khalaqahunna'l-ʿazīzu'l-ʿalīm* | ***Allāh*** |
| What follows | A list of favours (10-13) | *fa-annā **yuʾfakūn*** |

**The sūra asks the same question twice and, the second time, reduces the scale.**

**And that the answer is shorter the second time must be recorded:** in the ninth verse the answer came with two attributes; here it is a single word: *Allāh*.

**I give this as an observation about the arrangement.**

### يُؤْفَكُون — root: أ-ف-ك

**The root was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/11; I rely on that treatment.** What was recorded there was this:

> *"**إِفْك** — root أ-ف-ك: to turn a thing away from its direction, to turn it inside out. That is, the word used for 'fabrication' means 'turned about'."*

**I add here the pattern in this verse: *yuʾfakūn* — passive: "they are turned away".**

**The pattern is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** the verse does not say "how do they turn", it says **"how are they turned"**. **The verb is passive.** And in the thirty-sixth verse of the sūra it was said that a companion is **matched** to a person.

**And the interrogative particle *annā* must be recorded:** in Arabic *annā* means both "how" and "to where / from where". **The question, that is, is a question about direction.**

### وَقِيلِهِۦ — the grammatical question of the sentence

**This expression is the most disputed place in the sūra grammatically, and the disagreement must be transmitted.**

**First the variant reading:**

| Reading | Written form | Case |
|---|---|---|
| *qīlihi* (with *kasra*) | وَقِيلِهِ | Genitive |
| *qīlahu* (with *fatḥa*) | وَقِيلَهُ | Accusative |

**Both have been transmitted.**

**And for each reading different explanations are given of the word's connection within the sentence:**

| Reading | What it attaches to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *qīlihi* (genitive) | In apposition to *ʿilmu's-sāʿa* in the eighty-fifth verse — "and the knowledge of his word is with Him" | The word too is on record |
| *qīlihi* (genitive) | To an unexpressed particle of oath — "by his word" | An oath |
| *qīlahu* (accusative) | To an unexpressed verb (*wa yaʿlamu qīlahu*) — "and He knows his word too" | Being known |
| *qīlahu* (accusative) | To *dharhum* in the eighty-third verse — "and leave his word" | Held to be weak |

**In this disagreement I impose no preference.** What is to be recorded is this: **whichever explanation is taken, the content of the sentence is the same** — a complaint being put on record.

**قِيل — a verbal noun from the root ق-و-ل** (*qawl*, *qīl* and *maqāla* are verbal nouns of the same root). **The lexicographers record that the *qīl* pattern turns speech that has been uttered into a noun.**

### The content itself: a complaint

**And the content of the sentence must be recorded: *yā rabbi inna hāʾulāʾi qawmun lā yuʾminūn*.**

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the one the sentence is addressed to:** the sentence is not said to the other side. **It is said to the Lord.**

**One of the last utterances of the sūra, then, is a sentence the Prophet says not to people but to God. And the sentence contains no request** — it asks for no punishment and no help. **It states a finding, and nothing more.**

**And the verse that comes immediately after is the answer to that finding.**

## 43/89 — فَٱصْفَحْ عَنْهُمْ وَقُلْ سَلَٰمٌ ۚ فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ

*Fa'ṣfaḥ ʿanhum wa qul salām · Fa-sawfa yaʿlamūn*

"**So turn away from them and say 'peace'.** They will know before long."

### The closing of the ring: the *ṣafḥ* of verse 5 returns

**And the sūra ends with the word it refused in the fifth verse.**

**This is the clearest and most verifiable ring in the sūra. The same root (ص-ف-ح), in the same sūra, twice — and under two verdicts.**

**I set the difference out in a table:**

| | **43/5** | **43/89** |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | *a-fa-naḍribu ʿankumu'dh-dhikra **ṣafḥan*** | *fa'**ṣfaḥ** ʿanhum* |
| Mood | **A question** — of denial | **A command** |
| Verdict | **Refused** | **Commanded** |
| What is turned from | ***Al-dhikr*** — the reminder itself | ***ʿAnhum*** — from them |
| Who turns | **The text** (God) | **The Prophet** |
| Outcome | The reminder **continues** | The dispute **is left** |
| What happens | The Book goes on being given | Answering back comes to an end |

**And the difference lies in the fourth row of this table — I record it as my own reading, and its ground is the objects in the two verses:**

**In the fifth verse what was to be turned away from was the reminder: *naḍribu ʿankumu'dh-dhikra*. What was at issue was the cutting off of the message, and that was refused.**

**In the eighty-ninth verse what is to be turned away from is people:** *fa'ṣfaḥ **ʿanhum***. The message is not cut off; **the contention is.**

**Looking at what happens across the sūra makes the distinction still clearer:**

- The message was given (1-4).
- The other side's grounds were taken up one by one (15-25, 31-35, 57-65).
- Answers were given, comparisons were made, examples were shown (26-30, 46-56).
- And in the eighty-eighth verse a finding was stated: *inna hāʾulāʾi qawmun lā yuʾminūn*.

**The command of *ṣafḥ*, that is, comes not at the beginning of the message but at its end. After everything that was to be said has been said.**

**I mark this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra's own order: what was refused in the fifth verse was giving up at the outset. What is commanded in the eighty-ninth is not carrying on the argument once the work is done.**

### وَقُلْ سَلَٰمٌ — the work of the word

**And the command ends not with silence but with a word: *wa qul salām*.**

The root **س-ل-م**: to be sound, whole, free of harm. *Salāma* — being healthy and entire. *Silm* — peace. *Islām* — surrender.

**The core of the root has been treated in the corpus.**

**And the exegetes differ over the sense of the word here:**

| Reading | What *salām* is here |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A word of parting** — "peace to you; I have no business with you" (as in *qālū salāmā* in al-Furqān 25/63) |
| 2 | **A declaration of peace** — not entering into contention |
| 3 | **A greeting** — a word of courtesy |

**The majority stand around the first and second readings. I do not impose a preference.**

**But the choice of the word itself must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:**

**Across eighty-nine verses the sūra has recounted an argument. Mockery (7), laughter (47), insult (52), clamour (57), contention (58), whispering (80) — all of it was put on record.**

**And the word commanded at the close answers none of it.**

***Salām***, at its root, means **not doing harm and not taking harm.** **The verse, that is, ends the argument not by establishing a superiority but by leaving no injury behind.**

**And *fa-sawfa yaʿlamūn* — "they will know before long" — leaves the verdict to time.**

The root **ع-ل-م** stands at a key place in the sūra, and it is the sūra's last word:

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | *al-ʿazīzu'l-**ʿalīm*** | **God** |
| 20 | *mā lahum bi-dhālika min **ʿilm*** | **Lacking** in the other side |
| 84 | *al-ḥakīmu'l-**ʿalīm*** | **God** |
| 85 | *wa ʿindahu **ʿilmu's-sāʿa*** | **With God** |
| 86 | *wa hum **yaʿlamūn*** | The condition of intercession |
| 89 | *fa-sawfa **yaʿlamūn*** | **The other side — later** |

**The sūra's last word is a verb of knowing, and it is thrown forward into the future.** This is a verifiable datum of the text.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra began by diagnosing a lack of knowledge (*mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm*, 20) and ends by saying that the knowledge will come. **The whole argument in between was an argument about measure; and in its last sentence the sūra says when the measure will be seen.**

### What this says to the present

**The command the verse gives is not a giving up but an ordering — I record this as my own reading.**

**The fifth verse refused leaving off at the outset. The eighty-ninth commands not carrying on once the work is done.**

**Together the two set up a measure:**

| What is not to be done | What is to be done |
|---|---|
| **Leaving it unsaid** (5) | **Saying what is to be said** (1-88) |
| **Carrying on the contention after it has been said** (89) | **Saying *salām*** (89) |

**And this is consistent with the sūra's main question:** what was criticised throughout the sūra was turning an argument into a contest of superiority — *jadalā* (58), *qawmun khaṣimūn* (58). **The closing command shows where the argument ends.**

### The method of the sūra — a note

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above:**

**Al-Zukhruf does not reject the other side's measures; it *puts them to work*.**

- In the twenty-fourth verse the measure of "the right way" is accepted and put into a comparison: *bi-ahdā mimmā wajadtum* — one that guides better.
- In the thirty-second verse making a comparison is not itself refused; **what is placed on the scale is changed**: *wa raḥmatu rabbika khayrun mimmā yajmaʿūn*.
- In the thirty-third verse counting wealth valuable is not refused; **its price is stated**: something that could have been handed out wholesale were there no objection to it.
- In the seventy-first verse gold is not refused; it is used **in another place, for another work.**

**The sūra, that is, does not draw up a list of prohibitions. It adjusts a scale.**

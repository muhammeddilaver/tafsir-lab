# 32. Sūrat al-Sajda

**Thirty verses.** It is widely reported that it came down in the Meccan period. It takes its name from **the expression stating prostration in the fifteenth verse**: *they fall down prostrate* — **خَرُّوا سُجَّدًا**. This is also the verse of the sūra at which the prostration of recitation is performed (32/15).

The sūra is one of those that open with *Alif-lām-mīm*; and it joins the series that opens with these letters and then speaks directly of **the Book**.

Its name can be confused with that of another sūra: **Sūrat Fuṣṣilat too is called *Ḥā-mīm al-Sajda*** (recorded in `041-fussilet.md`). This sūra, in turn, is called for short **al-Sajda** or **Alif-lām-mīm al-Sajda**.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What ends the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-3 | The source of the Book, and a people who were not warned | *Laʿallahum yahtadūn* (3) |
| **II** | 4-9 | Creation, the circulation of the command, the making of man | *Qalīlan mā tashkurūn* (9) |
| **III** | 10-14 | The objection, death, and the scene of bowed heads | *Bimā kuntum taʿmalūn* (14) |
| **IV** | 15-22 | Those who fall prostrate, those who rise from their beds, those who are not equal | *Innā mina'l-mujrimīna muntaqimūn* (22) |
| **V** | 23-30 | Moses (Mūsā), leadership won by patience, dry ground, and the close | *Innahum muntaẓirūn* (30) |

**The backbone of the sūra is the distance between what is given and what is used.** In the ninth verse man is given **hearing, eyes and hearts**, and immediately afterwards it is said *qalīlan mā tashkurūn* (how little you give thanks). **In the twelfth verse the same two organs come back from the mouths of the other side:** *abṣarnā wa samiʿnā* — "we have seen and we have heard". **That is, the sūra sets the giving, and the far too late acknowledgement of that giving, face to face in two separate scenes.** This link will be shown separately below.

**A second line runs from beginning to end as well:** turning towards. Its name is **prostration** (15), it is **rising from the bed** (16), and on the other side it is **the head's falling forward of itself** (12). The sūra shows the same movement twice, once willing and once compelled.

## 32/1-3 — الٓمٓ · تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ مِن رَّبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · أَمْ يَقُولُونَ ٱفْتَرَىٰهُ بَلْ هُوَ ٱلْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكَ لِتُنذِرَ قَوْمًا مَّآ أَتَىٰهُم مِّن نَّذِيرٍ مِّن قَبْلِكَ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ

*Alif-lām-mīm · Tanzīlu'l-kitābi lā rayba fīhi min rabbi'l-ʿālamīn · Am yaqūlūna'ftarāh, bal huwa'l-ḥaqqu min rabbika li-tundhira qawman mā atāhum min nadhīrin min qablika laʿallahum yahtadūn*

"Alif-lām-mīm. **The sending down of this Book — there is no doubt in it — is from the Lord of all the worlds.** Or do they say, '**he has fabricated it**'? No: it is the truth from your Lord, **that you may warn a people to whom no warner came before you** — that they may perhaps find the way."

### الٓمٓ

**The note on the disconnected letters has already been entered in this series:** at length in `002-bakara.md` 2/1, in summary in `050-kaf.md`, and mentioned again in `045-casiye.md` and `046-ahkaf.md`. **I do not repeat it here.**

Its summary was this: **the meaning of these letters is not known with certainty**; one group of scholars refers the meaning to God (*tafwīḍ*); among the views that seek an explanation, the one that finds the most support from within the text is the explanation that the letters, by pointing to the **raw material** of the Qurʾān, make the challenge meaningful. **And, as was written there plainly: no numerical reckoning, no *abjad*, no counting of letters is carried out over these letters.**

**The only thing that belongs here is that the regularity recorded in `050-kaf.md` holds here too:** immediately after the group of letters there comes **an expression that speaks of the Book** — *tanzīlu'l-kitāb*. **This is a verifiable fact and not a claim.**

### تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ — the pair of names that changes from sūra to sūra

**In `041-fussilet.md` 41/2 and in `045-casiye.md` 45/2 it was tabulated how this sentence changes from sūra to sūra. The occurrence here is added to that table:**

| Sūra | Sentence | How the One who sends down is named |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/1 | *Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥakīm* | *al-ʿAzīz* · *al-Ḥakīm* — **power and aptness** |
| Ghāfir 40/2 | *Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ʿalīm* | *al-ʿAzīz* · *al-ʿAlīm* — **power and knowledge** |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/2 | *Tanzīlun mina'l-raḥmāni'l-raḥīm* | *al-Raḥmān* · *al-Raḥīm* — **mercy** |
| al-Jāthiya 45/2 · al-Aḥqāf 46/2 | *Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥakīm* | *al-ʿAzīz* · *al-Ḥakīm* |
| **al-Sajda 32/2** | *Tanzīlu'l-kitābi lā rayba fīhi **min rabbi'l-ʿālamīn*** | **No pair of names — a construct of *rubūbiyya*** |

**The difference is worth recording and is verifiable:** in the other five places the One who sends down is named **by two attribute-names**. **Here, in place of the pair of attributes, a construct comes:** *min rabbi'l-ʿālamīn* — **from the Lord of all the worlds.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the continuation of the third verse:** the third verse says that those addressed are a people **who had never received a warner before**. **That is, the address is built for a hearer meeting the Book for the first time.** For such a hearer, to introduce the One who sends down **by His scope** rather than **by His attributes** — the Lord of *all the worlds* — fits the work of the sentence. **This is an observation; I do not impose a preference.**

**Further, the transition between the construct *rabbi'l-ʿālamīn* and the *min rabbika* (your Lord) that comes immediately after must be recorded:** in the second verse the **widest** relation (the worlds), in the third verse the **narrowest** relation (you). **Two verses one after the other, moving forward by narrowing the scope.**

### لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ — the note that enters the middle of the sentence

**The same construct was analysed in `002-bakara.md` 2/2** (*dhālika'l-kitābu lā rayba fīh*), **and the link in the word *rayb* between "doubt" and "the unease of doubt" was treated there. I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is its *place* in the sentence.** In al-Baqara the construct came **in the flow of the sentence**, as the predicate of the subject *dhālika'l-kitāb*. **Here, on the other hand, it enters between the subject and the predicate:**

> *Tanzīlu'l-kitābi* — **[lā rayba fīhi]** — *min rabbi'l-ʿālamīn*

**In Arabic a sentence that comes in like this is called a *jumla muʿtariḍa* (a parenthetical sentence):** without severing the elements of the main sentence from one another, it enters a note in between.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the absence of doubt is not counted at the end as **an attribute** of the Book; it comes in the middle, **before the statement of the source is complete**. The sentence itself meets the objection without waiting to be finished.

### أَمْ يَقُولُونَ ٱفْتَرَىٰهُ

***Am* as a particle here states a transition, not a question — the usage the lexicographers call *am munqaṭiʿa*: "or do they say…?" That is, the sentence is built as though it were changing addressee: a moment ago the source of the Book was stated; now the objection raised against that statement is being reported.**

**ٱفْتَرَىٰ** — root **ف-ر-ي**. **The concrete sense given by the lexicographers: to cut leather, to cut it out.** *Farā'l-jilda* — he slit the hide, he cut it. **And from here two branches divide:** one **to make, to cut out and sew** (useful work), the other **to fabricate, to cut out and set forth what does not exist.**

**Iftirāʾ** is Form VIII and runs along the second branch only: **to cut out and bring into being a thing that does not exist.** **The picture in the word is worth recording:** the charge is not "he told a lie" but **"he cut and shaped"**. That is, the other side is saying that the text was **manufactured**.

**بَلْ هُوَ ٱلْحَقُّ** — the answer comes with *bal*: **the particle that cancels what precedes and puts something in its place.** And what is put in its place is *al-ḥaqq* — **definite (*maʿrifa*).** In Arabic a predicate that comes definite conveys **restriction** (*ḥaṣr*): "it is the truth itself."

### لِتُنذِرَ قَوْمًا مَّآ أَتَىٰهُم مِّن نَّذِيرٍ مِّن قَبْلِكَ

"That you may warn a people to whom no warner came before you."

**More than one reading of this sentence is transmitted in the classical works of exegesis, and the disagreement must not be concealed:**

| Reading | What *mā atāhum min nadhīr* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **No warner ever came** — no prophet had reached that people directly |
| 2 | **None came recently** — a long interval had fallen in between; the warning had been erased from memory |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And in `035-fatir.md` 35/24 the other end of this question was treated:** *wa in min ummatin illā khalā fīhā nadhīr* — **"there is no community but a warner has come and gone among it."** There the verb was in the **perfect**: *khalā* — "came and passed".

| Place | Expression | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/24 | *In min ummatin illā **khalā** fīhā nadhīr* | **One had come to every community** — past tense |
| **al-Sajda 32/3** | *Qawman **mā atāhum** min nadhīrin min qablik* | **None had come to these** |

**When the two verses are read together, the second reading (the interval) is seen to have a ground within the text:** Fāṭir says that the warning reached everywhere **in the past**, while al-Sajda says that none came to this people **before you**. **The distance between the two is time.** I record this as an observation and do not turn it into a preference.

**لَعَلَّهُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ** — and the purpose clause ends with *laʿalla*, that is, with **the pattern of hope**: the outcome is not guaranteed.

## 32/4-5 — ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا فِى سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ مَا لَكُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ مِن وَلِىٍّ وَلَا شَفِيعٍ أَفَلَا تَتَذَكَّرُونَ · يُدَبِّرُ ٱلْأَمْرَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ يَعْرُجُ إِلَيْهِ فِى يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُۥٓ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ

*Allāhu'lladhī khalaqa'l-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā fī sittati ayyāmin thumma'stawā ʿala'l-ʿarsh, mā lakum min dūnihī min waliyyin wa lā shafīʿ, a-fa-lā tatadhakkarūn · Yudabbiru'l-amra mina'l-samāʾi ila'l-arḍi thumma yaʿruju ilayhi fī yawmin kāna miqdāruhū alfa sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn*

"It is God who created the heavens, the earth and what is between them **in six days**, then **established Himself upon the Throne**. **You have, apart from Him, neither a friend nor an intercessor.** Will you still not reflect? **He directs the affair from the sky down to the earth; then the affair ascends to Him in a day whose measure is a thousand years of what you count.**"

### My limit on these two verses — I write it first

**This verse is one of those most often made the subject of a claim about physics in the modern period. Per the method (`USLUP.md`) I draw the line at the outset:**

**From here I do not go to the theory of relativity, to the relativity of time, to the speed of light, or to any modern proposition of physics.** I draw no such connection, and I do not support those that have been drawn.

**The ground was written in detail in `070-mearic.md`, and I rely on that treatment.** It was summarised in three items: **(1)** the verse states not a proposition of physics but a **measure**; **(2)** the principle recorded in the method (`USLUP.md`) is that modern information is not to be forced onto a verse; **(3)** when such a connection is drawn, the truth of the text is made to hang on the fate of the connection drawn.

**What the verse says is enough in itself, and that much I record:** man's measure of time and the measure of time of this working are not the same. **The verse says this itself:** *mimmā **taʿuddūn*** — **"of what you count".** That is, the sentence keeps expressly on record **by whose reckoning** the measure is taken.

### يُدَبِّرُ ٱلْأَمْرَ — root: د-ب-ر

**The root was analysed in detail in `047-muhammed.md` 47/24 on the occasion of *tadabbur*. I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

The core recorded there was this: **the concrete sense of the root is "the back, the rear of a thing"** (*dubur*). And the family of meanings comes out of this picture: **to look at the back** of an affair, that is, **to think of its end.**

**What belongs here is the difference of pattern, and it must be recorded:**

| Pattern | Place | Who does it | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **تَدَبُّر** — Form V | Muḥammad 47/24 | **Man** | To think through the back of the text, to carry it to its end |
| **تَدْبِير** — Form II | **al-Sajda 32/5** | **God** | To **order** the affair with an eye to its end |

**Form V is reflexive: work a person does upon himself.** **Form II, on the other hand, is transitive: work done upon an object.** *Yudabbiru'l-amra* — **"He orders the affair".**

**The same root, in two forms, with two different subjects.** I record this as my own reading: **when the two verses are read together, the act asked of man and the act ascribed to God share the same picture — looking to the back, to the end.** The difference lies in the one carrying a text to its end and the other an order. **This is an observation about the language; I set up no theological equation.**

**ٱلْأَمْر** — the word means both **affair/event** and **command/order**, and in the classical works of exegesis its sense here is dwelt on:

| Reading | What *al-amr* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Command** — the ruling/revelation that comes down from the sky and returns |
| 2 | **Affair, event** — the running order of the universe |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** Both senses of the word are live in Arabic, and the word order of the sentence is open to either.

### ثُمَّ يَعْرُجُ إِلَيْهِ — root: ع-ر-ج

**The root was analysed in detail in `070-mearic.md` on the occasion of that sūra's name:** **to ascend, to climb step by step along a sloping way.** *Miʿrāj* — a ladder; and the second branch the lexicographers record is **lameness** (*aʿraj*), where the link lies in the picture of "a movement that does not go straight, that slopes". **I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference here must be recorded, and the two sūras can be set side by side:**

| Place | Who or what ascends | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| al-Maʿārij 70/4 | **The angels and the Spirit** | *Taʿruju'l-malāʾikatu wa'l-rūḥ* |
| **al-Sajda 32/5** | ***Al-Amr*** — the affair / the command | *Thumma yaʿruju ilayh* |

**In al-Maʿārij what ascends is beings; in al-Sajda what ascends is the affair. I record this as an observation: the sentence of al-Sajda sets up a cycle — *mina'l-samāʾi ila'l-arḍ* (from the sky to the earth) … *thumma yaʿruju ilayh* (then it ascends to Him). Descent and ascent in a single sentence, the same road in two directions.**

**And the note recorded in `070-mearic.md` holds here too:** the expression *ilayhi* (to Him) **states no place**; what is reached is not a position but **the centre of the command and the ruling**. The common principle is al-Shūrā 42/11: *"There is nothing like Him."*

### A thousand years and fifty thousand years

**This comparison was already made and tabulated in `070-mearic.md` 70/4. I carry that table over here, because the question belongs in this verse's own place:**

| Verse | Expression | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Sajda 32/5** | *"…in a day whose measure is **a thousand years** of what you count"* | **The command's going down and coming back** |
| al-Ḥajj 22/47 | *"A day with your Lord is **like a thousand years** of what you count"* | The demand that the punishment be hastened |
| al-Maʿārij 70/4 | *"…in a day whose measure is **fifty thousand years**"* | The ascent of the angels |

**The principal explanations of the classical exegetes about these three — as transmitted in `070-mearic.md`:**

| Explanation | How it is put |
|---|---|
| **Different days** | The "day" in al-Sajda and the "day" in al-Maʿārij are not the same day: one speaks of the down-and-up cycle of the command, the other of the day of resurrection. A different event, a different measure |
| **Different stations of the same day** | The day of resurrection has stages called *mawqif*; the measure of each stage is separate. The whole must not be confused with the part |
| **Different distances of ascent** | The level from which the ascent sets out, and so the distance, differ |
| **The number states weight, not duration** | In Arabic large numbers most often state **magnitude** rather than an exact count |

**I do not impose a preference.** **In comparing the two measures, the one verifiable difference that belongs here is this, and I record it:** in al-Sajda what the measure is tied to is **the affair that goes down and comes up**, in al-Maʿārij **the beings that ascend**, in al-Ḥajj **the punishment that is held back**. **Three verses speak of three separate events; two different numbers are not given for the same event.** This is an observation that supports the first explanation from within the text — but it is not a preference, because the other explanations are not contrary to the wording either.

### ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ

**On this construct I enter a note per the method (`USLUP.md`) and do not open the discussion.**

**Two main positions are transmitted in the classical tradition:**

| Position | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Tafwīḍ** | The wording is accepted as it stands and the manner is referred to God; the question "how" is not asked |
| **Taʾwīl** | The wording is interpreted in the sense of sovereignty being established / the affair being taken in hand |

**Both positions are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The same distinction was treated in `089-fecr.md` (*wa jāʾa rabbuk*) and was mentioned again in `070-mearic.md`. I rely on that treatment.** The common principle is again al-Shūrā 42/11.

**And what is to be recorded in the word order is this:** immediately after the sentence *thumma'stawā ʿala'l-ʿarsh* comes **the fifth verse: *yudabbiru'l-amr*.** That is, the sentence continues not with a **halt** but with a **directing**. **I record this as an observation about the word order:** the verse does not recount creation as a finished work and leave it at that; it goes on to name an ordering that continues. **The same line was recorded in `035-fatir.md` 35/3** — there too creating was asked about together with an ongoing **providing of sustenance**.

### مَا لَكُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ مِن وَلِىٍّ وَلَا شَفِيعٍ

**The place of the sentence is worth recording.** Immediately after the creation of the universe has been recounted, **the address turns to the hearer**, and what is said is the absence of an intermediary.

**Two words are chosen: *walī* (a friend, one who takes on your affair) and *shafīʿ* (one who intercedes).** **I record this as an observation: both are words of relation.** That is, what is rejected is not a being but **a form of connection.**

**And the sūra will come back to this word:** in the thirteenth verse it will be said that the ruling has already been given, and in the twenty-ninth that faith which does not come in time will be of no use. **Being without an intermediary is a line carried through to the end of the sūra.**

## 32/6 — ذَٰلِكَ عَٰلِمُ ٱلْغَيْبِ وَٱلشَّهَٰدَةِ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

*Dhālika ʿālimu'l-ghaybi wa'l-shahādati'l-ʿazīzu'l-raḥīm*

"**That is He: the Knower of what is unseen and what is seen; He is mighty, He is merciful.**"

**ذَٰلِكَ — the far demonstrative.** Not "this" but "that". **As was recorded in `098-beyyine.md`, in Arabic the far demonstrative conveys *taʿẓīm* (exaltation) here:** what is far is pointed to in order to raise the rank of what is being said.

**ٱلْغَيْبِ وَٱلشَّهَٰدَة — the unknown and the seen. The same construct occurred inside a prayer in `039-zumer.md` 39/46 (*ʿālima'l-ghaybi wa'l-shahāda*). In both places the pair is built in order to give the compass of the knowledge: two ends are named and what lies between them is taken in.**

**ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ** — and the two names are worth recording.

**In `041-fussilet.md` 41/2 it was tabulated that the names of the One who sends down change from sūra to sūra: there, in place of *al-ʿAzīz · al-Ḥakīm*, came *al-Raḥmān · al-Raḥīm*.** **Here there is a third combination: *al-ʿAzīz* and *al-Raḥīm* side by side.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is what is missing in the second verse:** in the second verse the One who sends down was named not by a pair of names but by *rabbi'l-ʿālamīn*. **The pair of attributes has been deferred by four verses and comes in the sixth — and comes as a pair that joins power with mercy.** **The same pair appears in Sūrat al-Rūm as well** (*wa huwa'l-ʿazīzu'l-raḥīm* — al-Rūm 30/5); it will be mentioned in `030-rum.md`.

## 32/7-9 — ٱلَّذِىٓ أَحْسَنَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقَهُۥ وَبَدَأَ خَلْقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنِ مِن طِينٍ · ثُمَّ جَعَلَ نَسْلَهُۥ مِن سُلَٰلَةٍ مِّن مَّآءٍ مَّهِينٍ · ثُمَّ سَوَّىٰهُ وَنَفَخَ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِهِۦ وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلسَّمْعَ وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرَ وَٱلْأَفْـِٔدَةَ قَلِيلًا مَّا تَشْكُرُونَ

*Alladhī aḥsana kulla shayʾin khalaqahū wa badaʾa khalqa'l-insāni min ṭīn · Thumma jaʿala naslahū min sulālatin min māʾin mahīn · Thumma sawwāhu wa nafakha fīhi min rūḥihī wa jaʿala lakumu'l-samʿa wa'l-abṣāra wa'l-afʾidah, qalīlan mā tashkurūn*

"**He is the one who made beautiful everything He created.** He began the creation of man **from clay**. Then He made his progeny **from the essence of a despised water**. Then He **shaped** him and breathed into him of His own **spirit**. **He gave you hearing, eyes and hearts. How little you give thanks!**"

### أَحْسَنَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقَهُۥ — a variant reading that changes the meaning

**Two readings of this sentence are transmitted, and because the difference changes the meaning it must be recorded:**

| Reading | Word | The meaning of the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **خَلَقَهُۥ** — *khalaqahū* (perfect verb) | "He made beautiful everything **that He created**" — an adjectival clause qualifying *kulla shayʾ* |
| 2 | **خَلْقَهُۥ** — *khalqahū* (verbal noun, *lām* with *sukūn*) | "He made beautiful the **creation** of everything" — *khalqahū* being the second object of *aḥsana*, or a substitute |

**Both readings are transmitted; both are recorded in the sources. I do not impose a preference.**

**The difference between them is this, and it is verifiable:** on the first reading the beauty is ascribed **to the things created**, on the second **to the act of creating**. **The result is close, but the emphasis differs:** one praises the outcome, the other the operation.

**أَحْسَنَ** — root **ح-س-ن**. **The pattern is Form IV and here has the sense "he made beautiful"** — not "more beautiful" (the elative). The word order of the sentence shows this: *aḥsana* takes an object.

**And there is a detail of word order worth recording:** *kulla shayʾ* — **everything without exception.** **Immediately afterwards a single thing is set apart and gone into: man.** That is, the sentence comes down from the general to the particular: **everything → man → man's progeny → his organs.**

### وَبَدَأَ خَلْقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنِ مِن طِينٍ

**طِين** — clay: a mixture of earth and water. **And the verb must be recorded: *badaʾa* — "he began".**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not say "He created man from clay"; it says **"He *began* the creation of man from clay"**. **The verb states a starting point, not a list of materials.** Indeed the next sentence continues with *thumma* — **an interval is placed between the beginning and the coming of the progeny.**

### مِن سُلَٰلَةٍ مِّن مَّآءٍ مَّهِينٍ

**سُلَالَة — root: س-ل-ل.** **The root is analysed here for the first time in the series.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to draw a thing slowly out of its sheath, sliding it free.** *Salla'l-sayfa* — **he drew the sword from its scabbard.** From the same root:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **سَلَّ** | To draw out slowly, to slip free |
| **إِنْسِلَال** | To slip away quietly, to filter out from between |
| **سُلَالَة** | The **essence filtered out** of a thing |
| **سَلِيل** | Lineage, offspring — what is drawn out of a stock |

**Both sides of the word work here at once, and this is a matter the lexicographers record:** *sulāla* carries both the sense of **the filtering of an essence** and that of **the continuation of a line**. **The verse already says *naslahū* (his progeny) — that is, two words say the same idea from two separate roots.**

**نَسْل — root: ن-س-ل.** **This root too occurs here for the first time in the series.** **The core given by the lexicographers: to come away from a thing and fall, to be shed.** *Nasala'l-shaʿr* — the hair was shed. **And from this comes the sense "offspring, line": what comes away from the stock.**

**The picture in the two roots looks the same way: *sulāla* tells of what is filtered out, *nasl* of what comes away.** I record this as an observation about the language.

**مَّآءٍ مَّهِينٍ** — **the root م-ه-ن was treated in `077-murselat.md` 77/20 (*māʾin mahīn*) and in `068-kalem.md` (*mahīn*).** **I do not repeat it here.** The core: **counted worthless, despised, weak.**

**What belongs here is the coming together of the two words, and it is worth recording:**

> *sulālatin min **māʾin mahīn*** — **the essence of a despised water.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is that the two words carry opposite values:** *sulāla* states **what has been selected, what has been filtered** — that is, it raises. *Mahīn* states **what is counted worthless** — that is, it lowers. **Two opposite values in a single construct.**

**And this must be read together with the *aḥsana kulla shayʾ* of the preceding sentence:** the one who made everything beautiful builds man's line **from the essence of what is despised**. **The verse looks for beauty not in the material but in the operation.**

### ثُمَّ سَوَّىٰهُ وَنَفَخَ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِهِۦ

**سَوَّىٰ** — root **س-و-ي**: **to make equal, to level, to bring to completeness.** **The root has been treated in several places in the series** — in `082-infitar.md` 82/7 (*fa-sawwāka fa-ʿadalak*), in `087-ala.md` 87/2 (*alladhī khalaqa fa-sawwā*), and in `075-kiyame.md`. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And this root will come back in the eighteenth verse of the sūra: *lā yastawūn* — "they are not equal".** **The same root, one in the making and one in the ruling.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and it will be tabulated separately at the end.

**مِن رُّوحِهِ — "of His own spirit". A note per the method (`USLUP.md`): no account of the manner is produced about this construct. What the lexicographers and the exegetes mostly record is that the particle *min* here does not state a part; what it states is relation and honour. (Indeed the same structure is seen in constructs such as "the House of God", "the she-camel of God".) I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted and go no further.**

### وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلسَّمْعَ وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرَ وَٱلْأَفْـِٔدَةَ — the fourth link

**The triad of ear, eye and heart has been treated in three places in the series. This is the fourth, and the four must be read together:**

| Place | Sentence | What the triad does |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/26 | *Wa jaʿalnā lahum samʿan wa abṣāran wa afʾidatan **fa-mā aghnā ʿanhum…*** | **Given — it was of no avail** |
| al-Jāthiya 45/23 | *Wa **khatama** ʿalā samʿihī wa qalbihī wa jaʿala ʿalā baṣarihī ghishāwatan* | **Closed** — a seal and a veil |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/20 | *Shahida ʿalayhim samʿuhum wa abṣāruhum wa **julūduhum*** | **It bore witness** — the third being the *skin* |
| **al-Sajda 32/9** | *Wa **jaʿala lakum** … qalīlan mā tashkurūn* | **Given — no thanks were given** |

**In all four places the same three doors are counted; in each place in a different condition.**

**The meaning of the order was recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` 46/26 and holds here too:** the movement is from outside inwards — **what is heard → what is seen → what is weighed within.** The root of the word **أَفْـِٔدَة** (**ف-ء-د**, the burning of an ember; *fuʾād*, the kindled side of the heart) was analysed there; **I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference between al-Aḥqāf and al-Sajda is worth recording, because both are sentences of *giving*:**

| | al-Aḥqāf 46/26 | **al-Sajda 32/9** |
|---|---|---|
| To whom it is given | ***Lahum*** — **to them** (peoples that were destroyed) | ***Lakum*** — **to you** (those addressed) |
| Outcome | *Fa-mā aghnā ʿanhum* — **it was of no avail** | *Qalīlan mā tashkurūn* — **thanks are not given** |
| Time | **Finished** — the story of a past people | **Continuing** — an imperfect verb |

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the pronoun and the tense of the verb:** al-Aḥqāf recounts a finished example in the third person; **al-Sajda turns the same triad into the second person and leaves it in the present.** That is, the example is turned back upon the hearer himself.

**قَلِيلًا مَّا تَشْكُرُونَ** — the word order must be recorded: *qalīlan* has been **brought forward** (*taqdīm*). In Arabic an element brought forward carries emphasis. **And *mā* here is either a strengthener of the negation or the particle of the verbal noun; both parsings are transmitted.** In either case the ruling of the sentence comes out at the same place: **thanks fall short beside what is given.**

**And this sentence will form a link with the twelfth verse of the sūra.** It is shown below.

## 32/10-11 — وَقَالُوٓا۟ أَءِذَا ضَلَلْنَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أَءِنَّا لَفِى خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍۭ بَلْ هُم بِلِقَآءِ رَبِّهِمْ كَٰفِرُونَ · قُلْ يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ ٱلَّذِى وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ ثُمَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ تُرْجَعُونَ

*Wa qālū a-idhā ḍalalnā fi'l-arḍi a-innā la-fī khalqin jadīd, bal hum bi-liqāʾi rabbihim kāfirūn · Qul yatawaffākum malaku'l-mawti'lladhī wukkila bikum thumma ilā rabbikum turjaʿūn*

"They said: '**When we have been lost in the earth, shall we really be in a new creation?**' The truth is that they deny **the meeting with their Lord**. Say: '**The angel of death, who has been given charge of you, will take your souls; then you will be returned to your Lord.**'"

### ضَلَلْنَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ — the choice of the word

**ضَلَّ** — root **ض-ل-ل**: **to lose the way; and to be lost inside something and become impossible to tell apart.** **The "going astray" branch of the root has been treated in many places in the series** — it was analysed in `001-fatiha.md` (*wa la'l-ḍāllīn*) and in `053-necm.md` 53/2 (*mā ḍalla ṣāḥibukum*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The branch here is the other one, and it must be recorded:** *ḍalla'l-shayʾu fi'l-shayʾ* — one thing **was lost, mingled away, became impossible to pick out** inside another. The lexicographers record this use separately: like water lost in milk.

**I record this as an observation:** the objection is built not as "after we die" but as **"after we have mingled with the soil and become impossible to tell apart"**. **That is, what the other side finds hard is not death but *inseparability*.** If the parts have mingled with one another, which is the one that will be brought back?

**And the answer the verse gives is given not to this question but in the question's place:** *bal hum bi-liqāʾi rabbihim kāfirūn* — **"this is not the real matter: they deny the meeting."**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the particle *bal*:** *bal* cancels what precedes and puts something in its place. **The verse does not accept the technical objection as a ground; it names the refusal that lies beneath the objection.** **This is the line treated all along the axis of `045-casiye.md`** — there too the ground of the claim was asked for.

**And the word *liqāʾ* must be recorded: it will occur twice in the sūra** — here (10) and in the twenty-third verse (*fa-lā takun fī miryatin min liqāʾih*). **The same word: one denied, the other closed off to doubt.**

### مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ ٱلَّذِى وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ

**وُكِّلَ** — root **و-ك-ل**: to commit an affair to another, to appoint someone to a task. **The verb is passive and Form II: "he was made deputy, he was charged".**

**The choice of the word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the angel of death is named here not as an independent power but as one **charged with a task**. **That the verb comes passive means the one who charged him is not stated in the sentence** — but the continuation of the sentence states him anyway: *thumma ilā **rabbikum** turjaʿūn.*

**And the word order sets up a sequence:** *yatawaffākum* (he takes your souls) → *turjaʿūn* (you are returned). **Both verbs stand in a position close to the passive: the subject of the first is one under orders, that of the second is not stated.** **That is, at no stage of the process is any disposal of man's own named.**

**A note per the method (`USLUP.md`):** about the name, the number and the manner of the angel of death I write nothing beyond what the text states. **The limit laid down about the angels in `035-fatir.md` 35/1 holds here too; I rely on that treatment.**

## 32/12 — وَلَوْ تَرَىٰٓ إِذِ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ نَاكِسُوا۟ رُءُوسِهِمْ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ رَبَّنَآ أَبْصَرْنَا وَسَمِعْنَا فَٱرْجِعْنَا نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا إِنَّا مُوقِنُونَ

*Wa law tarā idhi'l-mujrimūna nākisū ruʾūsihim ʿinda rabbihim, rabbanā abṣarnā wa samiʿnā fa'rjiʿnā naʿmal ṣāliḥan innā mūqinūn*

"**If only you could see: the guilty, before their Lord, with their heads bowed low**: '**Our Lord! We have seen and we have heard. Send us back that we may do righteous work; now we are certain.**'"

### وَلَوْ تَرَىٰ — a condition whose answer is not stated

**The sentence begins with *law* (an unrealised condition), and the answer to the condition is not stated.** In Arabic this is called *ḥadhf jawāb al-sharṭ*: the answer is left to the hearer's imagining.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** "if only you could see…" is said, and the sentence passes to the scene. **What is not said is not said because it is heavier than the scene itself.**

### نَاكِسُوا۟ رُءُوسِهِمْ — root: ن-ك-س

**The root is analysed here for the first time in the series.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to turn a thing over, to set it head down, to bring its top to the bottom.** *Nakasa'l-shayʾa* — he turned it over.

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **نَكَسَ** | To turn over, to set head down |
| **نَاكِس** | One whose head has fallen forward (active participle) |
| **نُكْس / نُكَاس** | The **relapse** of an illness — turning back while on the way to recovery |
| **مَنْكُوس** | Turned over |

**The English "relapse" carries exactly this picture, though it comes from an unconnected word: to fall back while going forward.**

**And the pattern here is the plural of the active participle: *nākisū ruʾūsihim* — "those who bow their heads".** **That is, the head is recounted not as bent from outside but as the person's own condition.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the pattern:** the sentence does not say "their heads were bowed" (passive), it says "those who have bowed their heads" (active participle). **No compelling hand appears in the scene; the posture itself is recounted.**

**And it must be set face to face with the fifteenth verse of the sūra:**

| Verse | Who | Bodily movement | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12** | *Al-mujrimūn* | **Head fallen forward** — *nākisū ruʾūsihim* | **On that day**, at the meeting |
| **15** | *Alladhīna … dhukkirū bihā* | **Falling prostrate** — *kharrū sujjadan* | **Now**, when reminded |

**The same direction: head downwards.** **The difference lies in the time and in the will.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses stand in the same sūra, three verses apart:** the sūra shows the same bodily posture twice — one in its time and of itself, the other after the time has passed.

### أَبْصَرْنَا وَسَمِعْنَا — a return to the ninth verse

**This is the clearest internal link in the sūra, and it must be shown.**

**In the ninth verse three doors were given to man:** *wa jaʿala lakumu'l-**samʿa** wa'l-**abṣāra** wa'l-afʾidah* — **hearing, eyes, hearts.** And the sentence had ended with *qalīlan mā tashkurūn*.

**In the twelfth verse the other side names two of those doors with its own mouth:** *abṣarnā wa samiʿnā* — **"we have seen and we have heard".**

| Verse | Who says it | Expression | What it states |
|---|---|---|---|
| **9** | **The text** | *Jaʿala lakumu'l-samʿa wa'l-abṣāra wa'l-afʾida* | **The giving** |
| **12** | **The other side** | *Abṣarnā wa samiʿnā* | **Acknowledgement — but in the scene** |

**Three details must be recorded, and all three can be verified from the text:**

**One.** In the ninth verse the order is **ear → eye** (*samʿ* first). **In the twelfth verse the order is reversed: eye → ear** (*abṣarnā* first). **The other side says first what it saw.**

**Two.** **The third member of the triad of the ninth verse is missing here**: *afʾida* (hearts) is not named. **Two organs are counted, the heart is not.**

**Three.** But at the end of the sentence there is a word that names the heart's work: *innā **mūqinūn*** — **"now we are certain".** **Yaqīn is an act of the heart.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that these three details stand together:** all three of the doors given in the ninth verse are stated in the twelfth as having **worked** — two by name, the third by its act. **That is, the scene shows not that the instruments did not work but that they worked late.** **The observation recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` 46/26 goes one step further here:** there was "an opportunity not used"; **here there is an opportunity used but past its time.**

### فَٱرْجِعْنَا نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا — the same request, two sūras

**The same request was treated in `035-fatir.md` 35/37:** *rabbanā **akhrijnā** naʿmal ṣāliḥan **ghayra'lladhī kunnā naʿmal*** — "Our Lord! Bring us out, that we may do a righteous work **other than what we used to do**!"

**The two requests can be set side by side, and their differences must be recorded:**

| | Fāṭir 35/37 | **al-Sajda 32/12** |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | *Akhrijnā* — **bring out** (from the fire) | *Irjiʿnā* — **send back** (to before) |
| Where it is said | **Inside the fire** — *yaṣṭarikhūna fīhā* (crying out) | **In the presence** — *ʿinda rabbihim* |
| Additional note | *Ghayra'lladhī kunnā naʿmal* — **other than what we did** | *Innā mūqinūn* — **now we know for certain** |
| What it rests on | **Admitting that the deed was wrong** | **Having acquired the knowledge** |
| The answer given | *A-wa-lam nuʿammirkum…* — **time had been given** | **No answer** — verse 13 says something else |

**The difference is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the request in Fāṭir puts forward the mending of **the deed**, the request in al-Sajda puts forward **knowledge**. **Both make a ground of the thing they claim was lacking in the world — and both grounds are made void by the verse itself:** in Fāṭir by saying "time had been given", in al-Sajda by stating in the thirteenth verse that the ruling has already been laid down.

**And there is a difference of place between the scene in Fāṭir and the scene here:** there the request comes **from inside** the fire, here **from the presence**. **That is, the scene of al-Sajda is the earlier one — and even so there is no return.**

## 32/13-14 — وَلَوْ شِئْنَا لَءَاتَيْنَا كُلَّ نَفْسٍ هُدَىٰهَا وَلَٰكِنْ حَقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ مِنِّى لَأَمْلَأَنَّ جَهَنَّمَ مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ أَجْمَعِينَ · فَذُوقُوا۟ بِمَا نَسِيتُمْ لِقَآءَ يَوْمِكُمْ هَٰذَآ إِنَّا نَسِينَٰكُمْ وَذُوقُوا۟ عَذَابَ ٱلْخُلْدِ بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

*Wa law shiʾnā la-ātaynā kulla nafsin hudāhā wa lākin ḥaqqa'l-qawlu minnī la-amlaʾanna jahannama mina'l-jinnati wa'l-nāsi ajmaʿīn · Fa-dhūqū bimā nasītum liqāʾa yawmikum hādhā, innā nasīnākum wa dhūqū ʿadhāba'l-khuldi bimā kuntum taʿmalūn*

"**Had We willed, We would have given every soul its own guidance.** But this word from Me came true: '**I shall fill Hell with jinn and men all together.**' **So taste — because you forgot the meeting with this day of yours. We too have forgotten you.** Taste the **lasting punishment** for what you used to do."

### وَلَوْ شِئْنَا — a possibility stated and not used

**The sentence is again built with *law*: an unrealised condition.** **And what is said is a statement of power:** had We willed, it would have been — **but it was not willed.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not say that the guiding of everyone is **impossible**. **It grants the possibility openly and states that the possibility was not used.** That is, the matter is not one of power but one of **how the order was set up**.

**A limit per the method (`USLUP.md`):** from here I do not enter the discussion of destiny and compulsion, and I do not argue the views of the schools of theology. **What the verse says in its wording is two things:** the willing was possible; and a word came true beforehand.

**حَقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ مِنِّى** — *ḥaqqa* — root **ح-ق-ق**: **to be fixed, to come true, to find its place as a right.** **The same verb in the same pattern occurred in `041-fussilet.md` 41/25** (*ḥaqqa ʿalayhimu'l-qawl*), and there it was the sentence that ended the block. **The two sūras use the same expression.**

**The difference is worth recording:** in Fuṣṣilat *ḥaqqa **ʿalayhim*** — "it came true **against them**"; in al-Sajda *ḥaqqa'l-qawlu **minnī*** — "the word came true **from Me**". **One says whom the word touches, the other from whom it issued.**

### بِمَا نَسِيتُمْ … إِنَّا نَسِينَٰكُمْ

**The same verb twice, reciprocally: *nasītum* (you forgot) — *nasīnākum* (We forgot you).**

**The same reciprocal structure was treated in `045-casiye.md` 45/34** (*nansākum kamā nasītum liqāʾa yawmikum hādhā*), and it was recorded there as a "verb built reciprocally". **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two verses are built with almost the same words, and the difference must be recorded:**

| Place | Expression | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| al-Jāthiya 45/34 | *Nansākum **kamā** nasītum* | **A particle of likeness is present** — "as you forgot" |
| **al-Sajda 32/14** | *Bimā nasītum … **innā** nasīnākum* | **A particle of cause is present** — "because you forgot" |

**Al-Jāthiya builds the response as a *likeness*, al-Sajda as a *cause and effect*.** This is a difference between the two sūras that can be verified.

**And the explanation recorded in the classical works of exegesis about the ascription of *nisyān* (forgetting) to God is this, and I transmit it as it has been transmitted:** the word here is not in the sense of **real forgetting** but in the sense of **"leaving as though forgotten, remaining unconcerned"** — indeed *nisyān* carries this use in Arabic. **The same explanation was recorded in `045-casiye.md` as well.**

**فَذُوقُوا۟ … وَذُوقُوا۟** — and the command is repeated twice, on two separate grounds: the first is tied to **the forgetting**, the second to **what was done**. **That is, the ground takes in both a neglect and an act.**

## 32/15-16 — إِنَّمَا يُؤْمِنُ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِّرُوا۟ بِهَا خَرُّوا۟ سُجَّدًا وَسَبَّحُوا۟ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّهِمْ وَهُمْ لَا يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ · تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمْ عَنِ ٱلْمَضَاجِعِ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُمْ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ

*Innamā yuʾminu bi-āyātina'lladhīna idhā dhukkirū bihā kharrū sujjadan wa sabbaḥū bi-ḥamdi rabbihim wa hum lā yastakbirūn · Tatajāfā junūbuhum ʿani'l-maḍājiʿi yadʿūna rabbahum khawfan wa ṭamaʿan wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn*

"**Only these believe in Our verses:** those who, when they are reminded by them, **fall down prostrate**, who glorify their Lord with praise, and **who do not grow proud.** **Their sides withdraw from their beds;** they call upon their Lord in fear and in hope; and they spend of the provision We have given them."

**This verse is the prostration verse of the sūra, and the place that gives the sūra its name.**

### إِنَّمَا — restriction and the direction of the description

**The sentence begins with *innamā* (only, none but): the particle of restriction.** **And the direction of the restriction must be recorded:** the sentence does not say "those who believe do these things"; it says **"those who do these things believe"**.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** faith is described not as a claim but **by way of its signs**. **And the description fits the hearer of the sūra:** in the tenth verse the other side had said something (*a-idhā ḍalalnā…*); in the twelfth verse it had changed what it said (*innā mūqinūn*). **Where a word is no measure, the sūra ties the description to behaviour.**

### خَرُّوا۟ سُجَّدًا

**خَرَّ** — root **خ-ر-ر**. **The concrete sense the lexicographers record: to fall from a height; and the sound made in falling.** *Kharīr* — the sound water makes as it flows. **That is, the root holds both a fall and a sound.**

**The choice of the verb is worth recording:** the verse does not say *sajada* (he prostrated) — it says ***kharrū sujjadan***: **"they fell down prostrating".**

**سُجَّدًا** — the plural of *sājid*, on the *fuʿʿal* pattern; **in the position of a circumstantial qualifier (*ḥāl*).** That is, the prostration is **the form** of the fall.

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the choice of verb:** the sentence recounts not a **decision** but a **fall**. **And this is the exact counterpart of the scene in the twelfth verse:** there the head fell forward of itself (*nākisū ruʾūsihim*), here the body goes down to the ground of itself. **The same direction, two separate times.**

**وَهُمْ لَا يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ** — and the description is completed with a negation. **Form X of the root ك-ب-ر (*istikbār*) has been treated in many places in the series** — in `045-casiye.md` 45/8 and in `041-fussilet.md` 41/15 (*fa-stakbarū fi'l-arḍ*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**But the place of the negation must be recorded:** after three positive attributes have been counted, a negative attribute is added. **That is, "something he does not do" enters the list as well.**

### تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمْ عَنِ ٱلْمَضَاجِعِ — root: ج-ف-و

**The root is analysed here for the first time in the series.**

**The concrete sense of the root: a gap opening between two things, their parting and drawing away from one another.** *Jafā'l-janbu ʿani'l-firāsh* — the side **rose, drew apart** from the bed. The lexicographers illustrate the root thus: **the saddle's not sitting flush on the horse's back and staying a little clear of it.**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **جَفَا** | To draw apart, to move away, not to sit flush |
| **تَجَافَى** (Form V) | **Reciprocal / continuous**: to keep oneself away, to stand clear |
| **جَفَاء** | Roughness, harshness — **distance in a relation** |
| **جُفَاء** | The scum a flood throws up — what is cast aside, what does not hold |

**Turkish has borrowed *cefa* ("ill-treatment") from this root, and it carries the root's picture: the cutting off of closeness.**

**The pattern is Form V (*tafāʿul*), and the lexicographers record in this form gradualness and continuity: not a single rising, but the state of standing clear.**

**جُنُوب** — the plural of *janb*: **the side, the flank.** **مَضَاجِع** — the plural of *maḍjaʿ*, root **ض-ج-ع**: to lie on one's side. *Maḍjaʿ* — **the place where one lies on one's side, a bed.**

### The word order of the sentence — who draws away from whom

**This is the most striking side of the verse, and it must be recorded.**

**The subject of the sentence is not the person:** *tatajāfā **junūbuhum*** — **"their sides draw away".** That is, the act is ascribed not to the man but to **a part of the body**.

**And the object too is not a bed; the bed itself is a plural:** *ʿani'l-**maḍājiʿ*** — **"from the beds".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is two grammatical details — *junūb* as the subject, *maḍājiʿ* as a plural:** the sentence is built not like a declaration of will but like a **description of a state**. **And this runs in the same direction as the verb *kharrū* in the fifteenth verse:** there too the body went down of itself. **The sūra makes its description twice by way of the body — and both times the subject of the verb is not the person.**

**The plural *maḍājiʿ* carries one further overtone, and I give it as an observation:** had "bed" been singular, a particular night could have been understood; **the plural turns this into a repeated condition.**

**خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا** — "in fear and in hope". **Two verbal nouns in the position of circumstantial qualifiers, side by side.** **The same pair occurs at al-Rūm 30/24 as well** (*yurīkumu'l-barqa khawfan wa ṭamaʿan*) and will be treated in `030-rum.md`. **In both places the pair is named together: neither fear alone nor hope alone.**

**وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ** — **the same expression was analysed in `002-bakara.md` 2/3** (*wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn*), and it was recorded there that the particle *min* states the **partitive** — "of what We have given", not all of it. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the order of the description is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** prostration (the body going down to the ground) → glorification (the tongue) → not growing proud (the inner attitude) → rising from the bed (the body, at night) → prayer (the tongue) → spending (property). **Six attributes; and the last is the only one that touches another person.** The list begins with the person himself and moves outward.

## 32/17 — فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَّآ أُخْفِىَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ جَزَآءًۢ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ

*Fa-lā taʿlamu nafsun mā ukhfiya lahum min qurrati aʿyun, jazāʾan bimā kānū yaʿmalūn*

"**No soul knows what delight of the eyes has been hidden away for them, as a recompense for what they used to do.**"

### فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ — knowledge closed off

**And here, immediately after the description, instead of the recompense being recounted, its unrecountability is stated.**

**The same line was treated in `056-vakia.md` 56/61:** *wa nunshiʾakum **fī mā lā taʿlamūn*** — "and We shall build you up in a form **you do not know**". **What was recorded there was this: "Here the verse refuses to give a description."** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two verses can be set side by side, and the difference must be recorded:**

| Place | Expression | What is not known |
|---|---|---|
| al-Wāqiʿa 56/61 | *Wa nunshiʾakum **fī mā lā taʿlamūn*** | **The form of the rebuilding** |
| **al-Sajda 32/17** | *Fa-lā taʿlamu nafsun **mā ukhfiya lahum*** | **The recompense that has been hidden** |

**Both close off knowledge, but by two separate doors:** al-Wāqiʿa makes **the process** unknowable, al-Sajda **the outcome**.

**نَفْسٌ — indefinite (*nakira*) and within a negation.** **In Arabic an indefinite that comes within a negation conveys *ʿumūm* (generality): "no soul at all".** **That is, no exception is left.**

**أُخْفِىَ** — the verb is **passive**: the one who hides is not stated. **Root خ-ف-ي: to hide, to cover.** **The same root was treated in `040-gafir.md` 40/19** (*wa mā tukhfi'l-ṣudūr*) — there what was hidden was **what is held within**; **here what is hidden is the recompense, and the party doing the hiding has changed.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is that the two verses use the same root in opposite directions:** in Ghāfir man hides and God knows; **in al-Sajda God hides and man does not know.** **The same verb, in two directions.**

### قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ — root: ق-ر-ر

**The construct is analysed here for the first time in the series.**

**Root ق-ر-ر: to stay in a place, to settle, to remain fixed.** *Qarār* — the place where one settles; *istiqrār* — settledness. **And the lexicographers record a second branch of the root as well: to be cold** (*qurr* — cold).

**The two branches meet in the expression *qurratu'l-ʿayn*, and the lexicographers give two explanations:**

| Explanation | How it is built |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The eye's coming to rest** — its ceasing to look for anything else to look at; the end of searching |
| 2 | **The eye's growing cool** — in the Arabic language the tear of joy is counted cool and the tear of grief hot |

**Both explanations are transmitted by the lexicographers and do not exclude one another. I do not impose a preference.**

**The usual English rendering is "delight of the eyes"; but the picture in the Arabic is not delight but stillness or coolness.** I record this difference.

**And *aʿyun* is an indefinite plural — "eyes", indefinite.** **The expression itself carries an indefiniteness too: what the delight of the eyes consists in is not said.** **The sentence had already said this: *lā taʿlamu nafsun.***

**جَزَآءًۢ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ** — "as a recompense for what they did". **The verb *kānū yaʿmalūn* — a past that states continuity.** **That is, the recompense is tied not to a single act but to a doing that went on.**

## 32/18-20 — أَفَمَن كَانَ مُؤْمِنًا كَمَن كَانَ فَاسِقًا لَّا يَسْتَوُۥنَ · أَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ فَلَهُمْ جَنَّٰتُ ٱلْمَأْوَىٰ نُزُلًۢا بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · وَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ فَسَقُوا۟ فَمَأْوَىٰهُمُ ٱلنَّارُ كُلَّمَآ أَرَادُوٓا۟ أَن يَخْرُجُوا۟ مِنْهَآ أُعِيدُوا۟ فِيهَا وَقِيلَ لَهُمْ ذُوقُوا۟ عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ ٱلَّذِى كُنتُم بِهِۦ تُكَذِّبُونَ

*A-fa-man kāna muʾminan ka-man kāna fāsiqan, lā yastawūn · Ammā'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'l-ṣāliḥāti fa-lahum jannātu'l-maʾwā nuzulan bimā kānū yaʿmalūn · Wa ammā'lladhīna fasaqū fa-maʾwāhumu'l-nār, kullamā arādū an yakhrujū minhā uʿīdū fīhā wa qīla lahum dhūqū ʿadhāba'l-nāri'lladhī kuntum bihī tukadhdhibūn*

"**Is one who is a believer like one who has broken out of bounds? They are not equal.** As for those who believe and do righteous works: they shall have **the Gardens of Refuge as a lodging** for what they used to do. As for those who broke out of bounds: **the place they come to is the Fire.** Every time they want to come out of it **they are turned back into it**, and they are told: '**Taste the punishment of the Fire that you used to call a lie**'."

### The "they are not equal" series — the fourth link

**This expression has been treated in three places in the series. This is the fourth, and the four must be read together:**

| Place | How it is built | What is compared |
|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/19-22 | **Four pairs, one after another** | Blind/seeing · darkness/light · shade/heat · living/dead |
| Ghāfir 40/58 | *Wa mā yastawi'l-aʿmā wa'l-baṣīru…* | **Blind/seeing**, then **the one who believes / the one who does evil** |
| al-Jāthiya 45/21 | *Am ḥasiba… an najʿalahum ka'lladhīna āmanū, **sawāʾan** maḥyāhum wa mamātuhum* | **Equality in life and in death** |
| **al-Sajda 32/18** | *A-fa-man kāna **muʾminan** ka-man kāna **fāsiqan**, lā yastawūn* | **The believer / the fāsiq** |

**In all four places the same root: س-و-ي.** **And the differences among the four are worth recording:**

**One — likeness or direct statement.** Fāṭir and Ghāfir build the contrast **by a comparison drawn from the senses** (blind/seeing). **Al-Sajda uses no comparison: it names the two sides directly.**

**Two — question or statement.** Al-Jāthiya objects with *am ḥasiba* to a **supposition** ("did they suppose so?"). **Al-Sajda first asks a question** (*a-fa-man…*) **and then gives its own answer:** *lā yastawūn.* **That is, the question is not left open.**

**Three — number.** *Lā yastaw**ūn*** is plural, whereas there are two sides being compared; the dual (*lā yastawiyāni*) might have been expected. **The lexicographers explain this by the relative pronoun *man* being singular in wording and plural in sense:** what is compared is not two **persons** but two **classes**.

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is this detail of number:** the verse compares not two men but two groups. **And the "series moving from the lighter to the heavier" recorded in `035-fatir.md` 35/19-22 is absent here** — al-Sajda sets up a single pair, but **opens it out at once in two verses** (19 and 20). **Fāṭir lists them and leaves it there; al-Sajda goes into the single pair in detail.**

### فَاسِق — root: ف-س-ق

**The concrete sense of the root is given by the lexicographers thus: *fasaqati'l-ruṭabatu ʿan qishrihā* — the ripe date came out of its skin.** **That is, *fisq* is a thing's coming out of its own covering, its own boundary.**

**That *muʾmin* is set opposite the word is worth recording:** the contrast is built not with *kāfir* but with **fāsiq**. **And the verb carries this on:** in the twentieth verse *alladhīna **fasaqū*** — a verb, not a noun.

**I record this as an observation:** what the verse describes is not a declaration of belief but **the act of stepping outside the boundary.** **The same root was treated in `049-hucurat.md` 49/6-7; I rely on that treatment.**

### جَنَّٰتُ ٱلْمَأْوَىٰ and فَمَأْوَىٰهُمُ ٱلنَّارُ — the same word, two sides

**The two verses use the same root (أ-و-ي), and in the word order this is a deliberate symmetry:**

| Verse | Expression | The position of the word |
|---|---|---|
| **19** | *Jannātu'l-**maʾwā*** | **The governing term** — the name of the gardens |
| **20** | *Fa-**maʾwāhum**u'l-nār* | **The predicate** — the place they come to |

**The root أ-و-ي: to take refuge, to shelter, to come back and settle.** *Maʾwā* — **the place one returns to and takes refuge in.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is that the two verses share the same word:** for both sides the recompense is named **"the place of refuge"**. **The word is the same; the content is opposite.** And this is the counterpart in word order of the ruling in the eighteenth verse of the sūra (*lā yastawūn*): **two things that are not equal are put into two places bearing the same name.**

**نُزُلًا** — root **ن-ز-ل**: to come down. *Nuzul* is **the hospitality prepared for a guest when he alights.** **The word was treated in `056-vakia.md` 56/56 and 56/93** — there *nuzul* was said of both sides. **I do not repeat it here.**

### كُلَّمَآ أَرَادُوٓا۟ أَن يَخْرُجُوا۟ مِنْهَآ أُعِيدُوا۟ فِيهَا

**كُلَّمَا** — "every time": **a particle of time that states repetition.**

**And the relation between the two verbs must be recorded:** *arādū* (they wanted) — **active**; *uʿīdū* (they were turned back) — **passive.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the subject of the verb of wanting is stated, the subject of the verb of turning back is not. **That is, in the scene the will is on one side and the outcome on the other.** **And *kullamā* does not reduce this to a single occasion: the wanting is repeated, and so is the outcome.**

**This is the continuation of the request in the twelfth verse (*fa'rjiʿnā*), and it is a line that can be verified within the sūra:** there a return was asked for, here a coming out. **Both requests go unanswered.**

## 32/21-22 — وَلَنُذِيقَنَّهُم مِّنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ دُونَ ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْأَكْبَرِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ · وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن ذُكِّرَ بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِۦ ثُمَّ أَعْرَضَ عَنْهَآ إِنَّا مِنَ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ مُنتَقِمُونَ

*Wa la-nudhīqannahum mina'l-ʿadhābi'l-adnā dūna'l-ʿadhābi'l-akbari laʿallahum yarjiʿūn · Wa man aẓlamu mimman dhukkira bi-āyāti rabbihī thumma aʿraḍa ʿanhā, innā mina'l-mujrimīna muntaqimūn*

"**We shall surely make them taste of the nearer punishment, short of that greatest punishment, that they may perhaps turn back.** **And who does greater wrong than one who is reminded by the verses of his Lord and then turns away from them?** From the guilty We are **takers of retribution.**"

### ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ — which punishment

**أَدْنَى — root: د-ن-و** (some lexicographers connect it with أ-د-ن-و as well): **to be near.** **Both senses of the word are live: "nearest" and "lowest, least".** **The root has been treated in many places in the series** — in `053-necm.md` 53/9 (*aw adnā*) and, on the occasion of *al-dunyā* (the life that is nearest), in many sections. **I do not repeat it here.**

**The contrast here must be recorded, and the word order carries an asymmetry:**

| | Word | The opposite expected | What comes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The first punishment | *al-**Adnā*** — **nearest / lowest** | *al-Abʿad* (farthest) | — |
| The second punishment | — | — | *al-**Akbar*** — **greatest** |

**That is, opposite "near" is set not "far" but "great".** **I record this as an observation about the word order:** the two punishments are named not both **by distance**; the one is named by distance and the other **by measure**. **The choice of words implies that the two are not two degrees of one kind.**

**There is a disagreement in the classical works of exegesis over what "the nearer punishment" is, and it must not be concealed:**

| View | What *al-ʿadhābu'l-adnā* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The hardships that befall in this world** — famine, sickness, loss |
| 2 | **Particular historical events** — the calamities that befell that people |
| 3 | **The state of the grave** — the stage before the great punishment |

**All three views are transmitted in the sources. I do not impose a preference, and I set up no equation with a particular historical event.**

**What the verse says in its wording can be recorded, and that much I record:** there is an **order** between the two punishments (*dūna* — before it, short of it), and the **purpose** of the first is stated: *laʿallahum **yarjiʿūn*** — **that they may perhaps turn back.**

**This purpose is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the verse says that the smaller one is not an **end** but a **call**. **And the same verb (رجع) occurs for the third time in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | *Thumma ilā rabbikum **turjaʿūn*** | **Passive** — you are returned |
| 12 | *Fa'**rjiʿnā*** | **Request** — send us back |
| **21** | *Laʿallahum **yarjiʿūn*** | **Hope** — that they may perhaps turn back |

**The same root in three separate patterns: a statement, a request, a hope.** **This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.** **And the sequence is worth recording:** the return is named first as an unavoidable event (11), then as a request refused (12), and finally as **a door still open** (21). **The door is opened after the scene has been recounted.**

### وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن ذُكِّرَ … ثُمَّ أَعْرَضَ عَنْهَا

**The sentence is an *istifhām inkārī*: a negation in the form of a question, whose answer is "no one".**

**And the verb must be recorded: *dhukkira* — passive, Form II: "he was reminded".** **The same verb was passive in the fifteenth verse too:** *alladhīna idhā **dhukkirū** bihā kharrū sujjadan.*

**The two verses begin with the same verb and go to two separate places:**

| Verse | The same verb | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| **15** | *Idhā **dhukkirū** bihā* | *Kharrū sujjadan* — **they fell down prostrate** |
| **22** | *Man **dhukkira** bi-āyāti rabbih* | *Thumma aʿraḍa ʿanhā* — **he turned away** |

**The same entrance, two separate exits.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the verb stands in the same pattern in both places:** the reminding is done to both sides — what separates them is not the reminding but **what comes after.** **And the ruling of the eighteenth verse (*lā yastawūn*) takes concrete shape between these two verses.**

**The particle *thumma* must be recorded separately:** *ثُمَّ أَعْرَضَ* — "**then** he turned away". **In Arabic *thumma* states an interval.** **That is, the turning away is recounted not as a reaction given at once to the reminding but as a stance taken after time has come in between.** **The same work of this particle (the distance between a word and a stance) was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` 46/13 and in `041-fussilet.md` 41/30** — there in the positive direction, here in the negative.

**مُنتَقِمُونَ** — root **ن-ق-م**: **to find a thing ugly and reject it**; and from this **to repay it.** **This is the core the lexicographers record: retribution is first a *not approving*.** **The same root was treated in `085-buruc.md` 85/8** (*wa mā naqamū minhum illā an yuʾminū* — "they found nothing in them ugly except that they believed"). **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the picture recorded there is reversed here:** in al-Burūj the subject of the verb *naqama* was those who did wrong; **here the same root is ascribed to the side that gives the response.**

## 32/23-25 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَا مُوسَى ٱلْكِتَٰبَ فَلَا تَكُن فِى مِرْيَةٍ مِّن لِّقَآئِهِۦ وَجَعَلْنَٰهُ هُدًى لِّبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · وَجَعَلْنَا مِنْهُمْ أَئِمَّةً يَهْدُونَ بِأَمْرِنَا لَمَّا صَبَرُوا۟ وَكَانُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا يُوقِنُونَ · إِنَّ رَبَّكَ هُوَ يَفْصِلُ بَيْنَهُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ فِيمَا كَانُوا۟ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ

*Wa laqad ātaynā Mūsa'l-kitāba fa-lā takun fī miryatin min liqāʾihī wa jaʿalnāhu hudan li-banī Isrāʾīl · Wa jaʿalnā minhum aʾimmatan yahdūna bi-amrinā lammā ṣabarū wa kānū bi-āyātinā yūqinūn · Inna rabbaka huwa yafṣilu baynahum yawma'l-qiyāmati fīmā kānū fīhi yakhtalifūn*

"Assuredly We gave Moses the Book. **Do not you be in doubt about the meeting with him.** We made it a guidance for the Children of Israel. **When they were patient, We raised up among them leaders who guided by Our command;** and they were certain of Our verses. **On the Day of Resurrection your Lord will give the decision between them concerning that in which they differed.**"

### فَلَا تَكُن فِى مِرْيَةٍ مِّن لِّقَآئِهِ

**مِرْيَة — root: م-ر-ي.** **It was analysed in `041-fussilet.md` 41/54, and the concrete sense recorded by the lexicographers was given there: to squeeze and pull at an animal's udder in milking;** from this comes the sense "to wrangle over a matter, to waver in doubt". **I do not repeat it here.**

**The two occurrences can be compared, and the difference must be recorded:**

| Place | Expression | Who is in doubt |
|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/54 | *Alā innahum fī **miryatin** min liqāʾi rabbihim* | **The other side** — a declarative sentence |
| **al-Sajda 32/23** | *Fa-lā takun fī **miryatin** min liqāʾih* | **The one addressed** — a prohibition |

**The same word, one a ruling and one a command.** **I record this as my own reading; its ground is the difference of mood between the two sentences:** Fuṣṣilat states the condition of the other side, while al-Sajda addresses the Prophet. **And the pattern of prohibition states not that the doubt exists but that it is a place one must not be in.** This use is widespread in Arabic, and the lexicographers generally explain it as **an address to the community by way of the one addressed**; **I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

### لِّقَآئِهِ — to whom the pronoun belongs

**There is a disagreement in this verse, and it must not be concealed:** *liqāʾih* — "the meeting with him" — **the referent of the pronoun is not stated.**

| Reading | To whom the pronoun belongs | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To the Book** (*al-kitāb*) | There is no doubt that Moses received the Book / the meeting with the Book |
| 2 | **To Moses** | The meeting with Moses |
| 3 | **To God** | The meeting with the Lord — the same as *liqāʾi rabbihim* in verse 10 of the sūra |

**All three readings are transmitted in the classical works of exegesis. I do not impose a preference.**

**But I record that the third reading has a ground from within the sūra, and I give this as an observation:** in the tenth verse *bi-**liqāʾi** rabbihim kāfirūn* occurred. **The same word, in the same sūra, thirteen verses apart.** **This is not a preference but an overlap that can be verified from the text.**

### وَجَعَلْنَا مِنْهُمْ أَئِمَّةً يَهْدُونَ بِأَمْرِنَا لَمَّا صَبَرُوا۟

**أَئِمَّة** — the plural of *imām*; **root أ-م-م: to head towards, to aim at.** *Amma'l-makāna* — he headed for that place. **And *imām* means "the one headed towards, the one who stands in front and towards whom the one behind heads".** From the same root come *umma* (a community that looks in one common direction) and *amām* (the front).

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** an *imām* is not one who commands but **one who stands in front**. **And the verse limits this at once:** *yahdūna **bi-amrinā*** — **"they guide by Our command".** That is, the guiding is not out of the leader's own source.

### لَمَّا صَبَرُوا۟ — a variant reading

**Two readings of this word are transmitted, and because it changes the meaning it must be recorded:**

| Reading | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **لَمَّا** — *lammā* | "**When they were patient**" — it states a time |
| 2 | **لِمَا** — *limā* (*lām* with *kasra*, *mīm* without *shadda*) | "**Because of their patience**" — it states a cause |

**Both readings are recorded in the sources. I do not impose a preference.**

**The difference is worth recording:** on the first, leadership falls **at the same time** as the patience; on the second, patience becomes **the cause** of leadership. **The second reading brings the verse closer to a sentence stating a rule.**

**And what is common to both readings is this, and I record it as my own reading:** within the word order of the verse leadership stands in the position of a **result** — after the verb *jaʿalnā* two conditions come: **patience** and **certainty** (*kānū bi-āyātinā yūqinūn*). **That is, the verse recounts leadership not by way of a lineage, an office or an appointment, but by way of two inward attributes.**

**And the word *yūqinūn* occurs for the second time in the sūra:**

| Verse | Who | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **12** | *Al-mujrimūn* | *Innā **mūqinūn*** — **"now we know for certain"**, in the scene |
| **24** | *Aʾimma* | *Kānū bi-āyātinā **yūqinūn*** — **continuity**, in the world |

**The same root (ي-ق-ن), two separate times.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the tenses of the verbs:** in the twelfth verse *yaqīn* is given by **a nominal sentence**, as a state reached at that moment; in the twenty-fourth by *kānū … yūqinūn*, as **a state that had gone on**. **What the sūra separates is not the presence of certainty but when it was acquired.**

### إِنَّ رَبَّكَ هُوَ يَفْصِلُ بَيْنَهُمْ

**The root ف-ص-ل has been analysed in many places in the series** — in `077-murselat.md` and `078-nebe.md` in the context of *yawmu'l-faṣl*, and in `041-fussilet.md` 41/3 with the *tafṣīl* pattern. **I do not repeat it here.** The core: **to separate two things from one another, to put a distance between them.**

**And the line of leaving a disagreement to the judgement has been treated in several places in the series:** `002-bakara.md` 2/213 and 2/253, `045-casiye.md` 45/17, `041-fussilet.md` 41/45, `039-zumer.md` 39/3 and 39/46. **In all of them the same thing is said: the disagreement arises *after* the Book has come; and its judgement is deferred.** **I rely on those treatments.**

**What belongs here is the emphasis in the sentence:** *inna rabbaka **huwa** yafṣilu* — the pronoun *huwa* comes in between. **In Arabic this pronoun coming between subject and predicate is called *ḍamīr al-faṣl* (the pronoun of separation) and conveys restriction: "the one who will do the separating is He alone."**

**The play on words is worth recording, and I give it as an observation about the language:** to fix the subject of the verb *faṣl* (separating), the pronoun of *faṣl* is used. **The same root, one in the ruling and one in the grammar.**

## 32/26 — أَوَلَمْ يَهْدِ لَهُمْ كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا مِن قَبْلِهِم مِّنَ ٱلْقُرُونِ يَمْشُونَ فِى مَسَٰكِنِهِمْ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَٰتٍ أَفَلَا يَسْمَعُونَ

*A-wa-lam yahdi lahum kam ahlaknā min qablihim mina'l-qurūni yamshūna fī masākinihim, inna fī dhālika la-āyātin a-fa-lā yasmaʿūn*

"**Has it not guided them, how many generations We destroyed before them?** And yet they walk about **in the dwellings of those people.** In this there are surely signs. **Will they still not hear?**"

### يَمْشُونَ فِى مَسَٰكِنِهِمْ — where the proof lies

**Of all the proofs the verse chooses the one most easily reached: the ground underfoot.**

**The same proof was treated in `035-fatir.md` 35/44 and in `040-gafir.md` 40/21, 40/82** (*a-wa-lam yasīrū fi'l-arḍi fa-yanẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'lladhīna min qablihim*), **and what was recorded there was this: the proof is brought not from a distant history but from the ruins along the road.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference here must be recorded, and it is verifiable:**

| Place | Verb | What is asked |
|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/44 · Ghāfir 40/21, 82 | *A-wa-lam **yasīrū** fi'l-arḍ* | **Let them go and look** — a journey is asked for |
| **al-Sajda 32/26** | ***Yamshūna** fī masākinihim* | **They are already walking there** — no journey is needed |

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the mood of the verb:** in the other verses there is **a command or an urging** (go, look); **here there is a declarative sentence — they are there already.** **That is, al-Sajda removes even the distance needed to take up the proof: the ground being walked on is itself the proof.**

**مَسَاكِن — root س-ك-ن: to come to rest, to settle. The root was analysed in detail in `048-fetih.md` 48/4 (*sakīna*), and the note was entered there that a *maskan* is "not a house but the place where movement ends". I do not repeat it here.**

**The use of the word here is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the word chosen for those who were destroyed is from the root that states **coming to rest**. **And now there are people walking there** — *yamshūna*. **Stopping in one root, walking in the other; in the same place.**

### أَفَلَا يَسْمَعُونَ — the unexpected sense

**The end of the verse must be recorded, and it sets up a pattern within the sūra.**

**The verse recounts a proof of sight (walking among the ruins), but ends the question with hearing: *a-fa-lā yasmaʿūn***.

**And the next verse does the reverse:** the reviving of soil by rain is recounted, and the question ends with **sight**: *a-fa-lā **yubṣirūn***.

| Verse | Proof | Closing question |
|---|---|---|
| **26** | **Ruins** — what is seen | *A-fa-lā **yasmaʿūn*** — will they not hear |
| **27** | **Dry soil and the crop** — what is seen | *A-fa-lā **yubṣirūn*** — will they not see |

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is that the two verses come one after the other and end with two different senses:** the first two members of the triad given in the ninth verse (*samʿ* and *abṣār*) are here distributed over the closes of two separate verses. **That is, at the end of the block of proofs the sūra tests one by one the instruments it gave.** **And these are the same two words as *abṣarnā wa samiʿnā* in the twelfth verse.**

**One further choice of word must be recorded:** the twenty-sixth verse speaks of past generations — **they are not seen; only the *report* of them is heard.** **That is, the question fits the way the proof arrives.** This is an observation.

**قُرُون** — the plural of *qarn*, root **ق-ر-ن**: to bind two things side by side. **The root was treated in `041-fussilet.md` 41/25 (*quranāʾ*).** **The derivative here, *qarn*, means "a generation that lived together in one period"** — before it states a measure of time, the word states a **being together**.

## 32/27 — أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ أَنَّا نَسُوقُ ٱلْمَآءَ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْجُرُزِ فَنُخْرِجُ بِهِۦ زَرْعًا تَأْكُلُ مِنْهُ أَنْعَٰمُهُمْ وَأَنفُسُهُمْ أَفَلَا يُبْصِرُونَ

*A-wa-lam yaraw annā nasūqu'l-māʾa ila'l-arḍi'l-juruzi fa-nukhriju bihī zarʿan taʾkulu minhu anʿāmuhum wa anfusuhum, a-fa-lā yubṣirūn*

"**Have they not seen that We drive the water to the bare ground and bring out with it a crop of which their cattle and they themselves eat?** Will they still not see?"

### نَسُوقُ ٱلْمَآءَ — the choice of the verb

**سَاقَ** — root **س-و-ق**: **to drive before one, to conduct.** The word is used especially of **driving a herd of animals from behind.** From the same root come *sāʾiq* (the driver), *sūq* (a market — the place goods are driven to) and *sāq* (the leg, what one walks on).

**The choice of the verb is worth recording:** the water does not "come down" or "flow" — **it is driven.** **That is, the sentence gives the water a direction and a driver.**

**And the choice of the object must be recorded too:** the water is driven not to the place where the rain falls but **to the dry ground**. **The verb puts the distance between them into the sentence.**

### ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْجُرُزِ — root: ج-ر-ز

**The root occurs here for the first time in the series.**

**The core given by the lexicographers: to cut and clear, to tear up by the root, to leave nothing on top.** *Jarazahū* — he cut it, he mowed it. *Sayfun jurāz* — a sword that cuts and shears. **And *arḍun juruz*: ground on which no plant is left, ground eaten bare.**

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** for "dry ground" the verse does not choose a word meaning "waterless". **The word it chooses states that what was on the ground has been *taken away*.** That is, the dryness is recounted not as a lack but as **a having-been-emptied**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the root's sense of "to cut and clear":** the ground on which the proof is built is not soil that **never yielded** but soil **whose yield has been taken**. **And this fits where the proof is going — all through the sūra the subject is whether a thing that has happened once can happen again.**

### The same proof, three sūras

**This proof has been treated in two other places in the series, and the three must be compared:**

| Place | The water's road | Outcome | Where the proof is tied |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/9 | *Arsala'l-riyāḥa fa-tuthīru saḥāban **fa-suqnāhu** ilā **baladin mayyit*** | *Fa-aḥyaynā bihi'l-arḍa baʿda mawtihā* | ***Kadhālika'l-nushūr*** — **the resurrection** |
| al-Zumar 39/21 | *Anzala mina'l-samāʾi māʾan **fa-salakahū yanābīʿa** fi'l-arḍ* | *Zarʿan mukhtalifan alwānuh … **ḥuṭāmā*** | ***Dhikrā li-uli'l-albāb*** — **a reminder** |
| **al-Sajda 32/27** | ***Nasūqu'l-māʾa** ila'l-arḍi'l-**juruz*** | *Zarʿan **taʾkulu minhu anʿāmuhum wa anfusuhum*** | ***A-fa-lā yubṣirūn*** — **a question** |

**Three differences are worth recording, and all three can be verified from the text:**

**One — the verb.** In Fāṭir *suqnāhu* (We drove) is perfect; **in al-Sajda *nasūqu* is imperfect: We are driving.** **Fāṭir recounts a finished event, al-Sajda a work that is going on.** **The change of the verb tenses was recorded separately as an observation about the word order in `035-fatir.md` 35/9; I rely on that treatment.**

**Two — the outcome.** Al-Zumar goes as far as the crop **turning yellow and becoming chaff**: the proof is built on **transience**. **Al-Sajda stops at the eating of the crop: the proof is built on *usefulness*.** **The same event, cut off at two different points.**

**Three — who eats.** Al-Sajda names the two sides together: ***anʿāmuhum wa anfusuhum*** — **their cattle and they themselves.** **And the order must be recorded: the animals first.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order:** the sentence does not put man at the head of the list. **There are two parties at the same table, and man is named second.** **This runs in the same direction as the observation recorded in `035-fatir.md` 35/45:** there too the scope was not kept limited to man (*min dābba*).

**فَنُخْرِجُ بِهِۦ زَرْعًا** — *zarʿ* — root **ز-ر-ع**: to sow. **This root was treated in detail in `056-vakia.md` 56/63-64** (*a-antum tazraʿūnahū am naḥnu'l-zāriʿūn* — "is it you who make it grow?"), **and there man's share and the ground on which the outcome rests were separated.** **I rely on that treatment.** **Here the same distinction is made by the voice of the verb: *nukhriju* — it is We who bring it out.**

## 32/28-29 — وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَٰذَا ٱلْفَتْحُ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ · قُلْ يَوْمَ ٱلْفَتْحِ لَا يَنفَعُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ إِيمَٰنُهُمْ وَلَا هُمْ يُنظَرُونَ

*Wa yaqūlūna matā hādha'l-fatḥu in kuntum ṣādiqīn · Qul yawma'l-fatḥi lā yanfaʿu'lladhīna kafarū īmānuhum wa lā hum yunẓarūn*

"'**When is this fatḥ, if you are truthful?**' they say. Say: '**On the day of the fatḥ, the faith of those who disbelieved will not profit them, nor will they be granted any respite.**'"

### ٱلْفَتْح — root: ف-ت-ح

**The root was analysed in detail in `048-fetih.md` on the occasion of that sūra's name** (to open what is shut; *fattāḥ*, *miftāḥ*) **and occurred again in `035-fatir.md` 35/2 with the verb *yaftaḥ*.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**There is a disagreement in the classical works of exegesis over the use here, and it must not be concealed:**

| View | What *al-fatḥ* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Judgement, separation** — from the root's sense of "opening": the case being opened and brought to a decision. Indeed the word *fātiḥ* is used in Arabic for a **judge** as well, and *fatāḥa* is judgement |
| 2 | **The coming of the punishment** — an outcome like what befell those peoples |
| 3 | **The Day of Resurrection** — the final parting |

**All three views are transmitted in the sources. I do not impose a preference, and I set up no equation with a particular historical event.**

**I record that the first view has a ground from within the language, and I give this as an observation:** the preceding block had ended with *inna rabbaka huwa **yafṣilu** baynahum* (25) — **separating.** **And the sense of judgement in the word *fatḥ* says the same work with another root.** This is a closeness that can be verified from the text — but it is not a preference.

### مَتَىٰ هَٰذَا ٱلْفَتْحُ — the form of the question

**The question asks for a date and carries a condition: *in kuntum ṣādiqīn* — "if you are truthful".**

**A question of the same structure was treated in `070-mearic.md` 70/1** (*saʾala sāʾilun bi-ʿadhābin wāqiʿ*), **and the observation recorded there holds here too: the questioner asks for a date, the verse gives no date.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The form of the answer here must be recorded, and it is verifiable:** the answer **does not touch** the *matā* question at all. **The question is "when?" — the answer is "what will happen on that day."**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is how the answer is built:** *qul **yawma'l-fatḥi** lā yanfaʿu…* — the sentence takes the word of the question (*al-fatḥ*), turns it into **an adverb of time**, and builds a ruling upon it. **That is, the question is not answered; the word of the question is set inside a ruling.**

**لَا يَنفَعُ … إِيمَٰنُهُمْ** — and what is said is heavy: **it is said not that faith will not be had but that the faith that is had will not profit on that day.**

**This is directly connected with the twelfth verse and is a line that can be verified within the sūra:** there *innā mūqinūn* had been said — certain knowledge had been acknowledged in the scene. **The twenty-ninth verse gives the ruling on the same thing: faith at that hour does not count.**

**وَلَا هُمْ يُنظَرُونَ** — root **ن-ظ-ر**, passive of Form IV: **"they will not be granted a respite".** **The "looking" and "granting a respite" branches of the root were tabulated in `044-duhan.md` 44/59** (in the comparison of *naẓar* / *irtiqāb*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the choice of the word is worth recording:** the next verse ends with two words from the same root — *wa'ntaẓir innahum muntaẓirūn*. **That is, the last two verses of the sūra use the same root three times.** It is tabulated below.

## 32/30 — فَأَعْرِضْ عَنْهُمْ وَٱنتَظِرْ إِنَّهُم مُّنتَظِرُونَ

*Fa-aʿriḍ ʿanhum wa'ntaẓir innahum muntaẓirūn*

"**So turn away from them and wait; they too are waiting.**"

### The close of the sūra

**Three words, two commands and a statement.**

**فَأَعْرِضْ عَنْهُمْ** — root **ع-ر-ض**: breadth, width; and from this *iʿrāḍ* — **to turn side on, to turn the breadth of the face away.** **The root was treated in `041-fussilet.md` 41/4 (*fa-aʿraḍa aktharuhum*) and 41/51 (*naʾā bi-jānibih*).** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And there is a reversal worth recording:** in Fuṣṣilat *iʿrāḍ* was the act of **the other side**. **Here the same verb is commanded to the one addressed.** **The same root, in two directions: one a turning away from the text, the other a turning away from the one who turns away.**

**A note per the method (`USLUP.md`):** from this I draw no ruling of estrangement or of wholesale abandonment. **The context of the sentence is plain: in the twenty-eighth verse a date was asked for, and in the twenty-ninth an answer was given. *Iʿrāḍ* is the not-continuing of the argument.**

### وَٱنتَظِرْ إِنَّهُم مُّنتَظِرُونَ — a comparison with al-Dukhān

**In `044-duhan.md` 44/59 the close of that sūra was built with the same structure, and this verse was already mentioned there.** **I rely on that treatment and carry the comparison over here, to its own place:**

| Sūra | Close | Root |
|---|---|---|
| al-Dukhān 44/59 | *Fa'**rtaqib** innahum **murtaqibūn*** | **ر-ق-ب** |
| **al-Sajda 32/30** | *Fa-aʿriḍ ʿanhum wa'**ntaẓir** innahum **muntaẓirūn*** | **ن-ظ-ر** |

**The difference between the two roots was tabulated in `044-duhan.md`, and its summary was this:**

| Root | Core | What it states |
|---|---|---|
| **ن-ظ-ر** | **To look** | Looking; and in Form IV, **to grant a respite** |
| **ر-ق-ب** | **To watch out for** | To watch a thing **so as not to miss it**; *raqaba* — the neck |

**Two differences belong here, and both are verifiable:**

**One.** **In the close of al-Sajda there is one command more: *fa-aʿriḍ ʿanhum*.** Al-Dukhān passes straight to the waiting; **al-Sajda commands first a breaking off and then a waiting.** **The two verbs set up a sequence: first withdrawal from the argument, then waiting.**

**Two.** **The choice of root fits the sūra's own words.** In the twenty-ninth verse *wa lā hum **yunẓarūn*** had occurred — **the same root, in the sense "no respite is granted".**

| Verse | Word | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **29** | *Yunẓarūn* | **Form IV, passive** | **No respite is granted** |
| **30** | *Intaẓir* | **Form VIII, imperative** | **Wait** |
| **30** | *Muntaẓirūn* | **Form VIII, active participle** | **Those who wait** |

**The same root, three times in two verses.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this threefold repetition:** the twenty-ninth verse says that there is **no respite**; the thirtieth commands **waiting**. **That is, the sūra commands not a waiting without an end but a waiting whose end has been stated.** The length of the waiting is not known; **what will happen when it ends is known.**

**And the observation recorded in `044-duhan.md` works here too:** the sentence does not say "you wait, and they will be destroyed". **It joins both sides in the same verb** — *muntaẓirūn*, the plural active participle of the same form. **The difference will appear not in the act but at the end of the act.**

### And where the sūra leaves off

**The sūra opened with a statement about a Book** (2) **and closed with a command to wait** (30). **Between them there are two scenes, and both carry the same two words: in the ninth verse hearing and eyes are *given*, and in the twelfth it is said that they *were heard and were seen*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the overlap of words between these two verses:** what the sūra separates is not the instrument but **the time**. **Those described in the fifteenth verse use the same instruments in their time; those in the twelfth after the time has passed.** **And the twenty-first verse is the one sentence that says that the time has not yet passed:** *laʿallahum yarjiʿūn.*

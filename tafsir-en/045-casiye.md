# 45. Sūrat al-Jāthiya

**37 verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the word **جَاثِيَة** in verse 28: **kneeling.**

The sūra is one of the seven that open with *ḥā-mīm*, and it stands immediately before al-Aḥqāf. **What is seen when the two sūras are read together is one and the same question taken up from two different places:** al-Aḥqāf opens with a demand for proof, al-Jāthiya opens by enumerating proofs.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-6 | A three-step list of proofs | *Fa-bi-ayyi ḥadīthin baʿda'llāhi wa āyātihī yuʾminūn* (6) |
| **II** | 7-11 | The type who resists although he has heard | *Wa lahum ʿadhābun min rijzin alīm* (11) |
| **III** | 12-20 | What has been given over to command, and the example of the Children of Israel | *Hudan wa raḥmatan li-qawmin yūqinūn* (20) |
| **IV** | 21-26 | Two measures: those who cannot be made equal | *Wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn* (26) |
| **V** | 27-37 | The kneeling communities, and the close | *Wa huwa'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm* (37) |

**The backbone of the sūra is a question about knowledge.** The words repeated from beginning to end show this: *yūqinūn*, *yaʿqilūn*, *ʿilm*, *ẓann*, *lā yaʿlamūn*. **The sūra treats denial as a claim to knowledge and asks what that claim rests on.**

## 45/1-2 — حمٓ · تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَكِيمِ

*Ḥā-mīm · Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥakīm*

"Ḥā-mīm. The sending down of this Book is from God, the Mighty, the Wise."

**The note on the disconnected letters has already been entered elsewhere in this series** (`002-bakara.md`, `050-kaf.md`, `046-ahkaf.md`): **there is no certain knowledge about the meaning of these letters**, many explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries, and none of them claims certainty. Claims resting on letter-and-number reckoning are not entered into, per the method (`USLUP.md`). **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is a verifiable fact:** the second verse **is word for word the same as the second verse of `046-ahkaf.md`** (al-Aḥqāf 46/2).

| Sūra | Verse | Text |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Jāthiya 45/2** | 2 | *Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥakīm* |
| **al-Aḥqāf 46/2** | 2 | *Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥakīm* |

**The two sūras stand one after the other and open with the same sentence.** In the section on al-Aḥqāf the construction of this sentence — the subject being a verbal noun, that is, the bringing forward not of the verb but of **the act itself** — was treated in detail. **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference is shown by the third verses:** al-Aḥqāf, by saying that creation is *bi'l-ḥaqq* and with *ajalin musammā*, brings **the term** forward; al-Jāthiya, by saying that there are signs in the heavens and the earth, brings **the proof** forward. **The same door is entered, and two different directions are taken.**

## 45/3-6 — إِنَّ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ لَءَايَٰتٍ لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · وَفِى خَلْقِكُمْ وَمَا يَبُثُّ مِن دَآبَّةٍ ءَايَٰتٌ لِّقَوْمٍ يُوقِنُونَ · وَٱخْتِلَٰفِ ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ وَمَآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مِن رِّزْقٍ فَأَحْيَا بِهِ ٱلْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا وَتَصْرِيفِ ٱلرِّيَٰحِ ءَايَٰتٌ لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْقِلُونَ

*Inna fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi la-āyātin li'l-muʾminīn · Wa fī khalqikum wa mā yabuththu min dābbatin āyātun li-qawmin yūqinūn · Wa'khtilāfi'l-layli wa'n-nahāri … āyātun li-qawmin yaʿqilūn*

"In the heavens and the earth there are signs for those who believe. In your own creation, and in the moving creatures He scatters abroad, there are signs for **those who know with certainty**. In the alternation of night and day, in the provision God sends down from the sky with which He revives the earth after its death, and in the turning about of the winds, there are signs for **those who use their reason**."

### Three verses, three addressees — and this is no accident

**The same pattern is repeated three times, and each time the addressee changes:**

| Verse | Field of the proof | Addressee |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **The heavens and the earth** — the outermost | *li'l-**muʾminīn*** |
| 4 | **Your own creation and living creatures** | *li-qawmin **yūqinūn*** |
| 5 | **Night and day, rain, wind** — the order at work | *li-qawmin **yaʿqilūn*** |

**This is a structure that can be verified within the sūra:** three verses, three sets of proof, three distinct attributes.

**I record the following as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence:** as the proofs come from the far to the near, the attribute of the addressee shifts from **faith** to **certainty**, and from there to **the use of reason**. That is, the verse is not setting up a hierarchy; it is saying that **the same set of proofs can be entered by different doors**. The sky says something to the one who believes; his own body, to the one who seeks certain knowledge; the order at work, to the one who puts his reason to use.

**What the three have in common should also be recorded:** all are named with the word *āyāt* — **sign**. Proof is set down not as a conclusion in itself but as something that points to something else.

### وَمَا يَبُثُّ مِن دَآبَّةٍ

**بَثّ** — root **ب-ث-ث**: to spread, to disperse, to scatter. The lexicographers record for the root the picture of "strewing something about".

**دَآبَّة** — root **د-ب-ب**: that which moves, that which crawls upon the earth. **The word is not the name of a species; it means *a moving creature*.** With this word the Qurʾān names living things by way of their movement.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not classify living creatures, it makes **their being spread abroad** the proof. What is named is not diversity but **distribution**.

### تَصْرِيفِ ٱلرِّيَٰحِ

The root **ص-ر-ف**: to turn, to make change direction. **Form II conveys repetition and variation.** The root was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/27 (*wa ṣarrafna'l-āyāt*).

**That the same root is used in two places is worth recording:** there the **signs** were being turned about and set forth again and again; here the **winds** are turned about. **The changing of direction by the wind and the changing of direction by the exposition are named with the same verb.**

### فَبِأَىِّ حَدِيثٍۭ بَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ وَءَايَٰتِهِۦ يُؤْمِنُونَ

"In what discourse, after God and His signs, will they believe?"

**The block closes with a question, and the word of the question is *ḥadīth* — "discourse".** The same word occurred in `056-vakia.md` at 56/81 (*a-fa-bi-hādha'l-ḥadīthi antum mudhinūn*) and was treated there.

**The logic of the question is this:** across three verses the proofs were enumerated. **If these are not enough, what will be?** The question closes off the claim that proof is lacking.

## 45/7-11 — وَيْلٌ لِّكُلِّ أَفَّاكٍ أَثِيمٍ · يَسْمَعُ ءَايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِ ثُمَّ يُصِرُّ مُسْتَكْبِرًا كَأَن لَّمْ يَسْمَعْهَا

*Waylun li-kulli affākin athīm · Yasmaʿu āyāti'llāhi tutlā ʿalayhi thumma yuṣirru mustakbiran ka-an lam yasmaʿhā*

"Woe to every fabricator of lies, every one sunk in sin! He **hears** the signs of God being recited to him, and then persists in arrogance **as though he had not heard them at all**."

### Hearing and being as though one had not heard

**At the centre of the verse there is a contradiction, and it is set out openly:** *yasmaʿu* (he hears) … *ka-an lam yasmaʿhā* (as though he had not heard them).

**The issue, then, is not that the sound fails to arrive.** It arrives; **then something happens.** That something is named with two words:

| Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Yuṣirru* | ص-ر-ر | **To persist** — to knot oneself upon it |
| *Mustakbiran* | ك-ب-ر | **To make oneself great** |

**The root ص-ر-ر was analysed in `056-vakia.md` at 56/46** (to tie, to knot; *ṣurra* — a purse tied at the mouth; a sound that continues without a break). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The comparison with the sequence in al-Wāqiʿa is worth recording:** there the order was *mutraf → persistence → speech* — that is, abundance, resistance, denial. **Here the order is *hearing → persistence → self-magnification*.** The two sūras place the same act (*iṣrār*) at the centre, but one describes the cause and the other the moment.

**أَفَّاك** — root **أ-ف-ك**: to turn something from its direction, to overturn it. **The word is on the *faʿʿāl* pattern: an intensive form — one who does it constantly and much.** The root was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/11 and 46/28 (*ifkun qadīm* — *ifkuhum*).

**وَإِذَا عَلِمَ مِنْ ءَايَٰتِنَا شَيْـًٔا ٱتَّخَذَهَا هُزُوًا** — "and when he comes to know something of Our signs, he takes them in mockery."

**This sentence is worth recording, because the verb is *ʿalima* — "he came to know".** That is, the mockery does not come from ignorance; it comes **after learning**. **The sūra's axis of knowledge appears here openly for the first time.**

## 45/12-15 — ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِى سَخَّرَ لَكُمُ ٱلْبَحْرَ لِتَجْرِىَ ٱلْفُلْكُ فِيهِ بِأَمْرِهِۦ · وَسَخَّرَ لَكُم مَّا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ جَمِيعًا مِّنْهُ

"It is God who has made the sea **subservient** to you, so that ships may run upon it by His command… And He has made subservient to you all that is in the heavens and all that is in the earth, all of it, as a gift from Him."

### سَخَّرَ — root: س-خ-ر

**The root carries two branches, and the lexicographers record them together:** **to make subservient, to set to a task**, and **to mock**. The root was treated in `069-hakka.md` and in `049-hucurat.md` at 49/11 (in its branch of mockery).

**The branch here is the first, and the pattern is Form II:** to arrange something in such a way that it is put into service.

**What the word says and what it does not say must be kept apart — I record this as my own reading:** *taskhīr* states not that man **rules the sea** but that **the sea is so constituted that it can carry a ship**. Buoyancy, the order of the winds, the shape of the coasts — none of these is man's work. **Man's work is to build the ship.**

**This distinction is the very same line drawn in the four proofs at `056-vakia.md` 56/58-72:** man's share is granted, and the ground on which the outcome rests is set apart.

### قُل لِّلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ يَغْفِرُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ لَا يَرْجُونَ أَيَّامَ ٱللَّهِ

"Say to those who believe: **let them forgive those who do not look for the days of God.**"

**The placing of this verse in this block is striking.** Before it, favours are enumerated; after it, it is said that everyone will meet his own deed (*man ʿamila ṣāliḥan fa-li-nafsih*). **In the middle there is a command about conduct: forgiveness.**

**And the one addressed by the command and its object are distinct:** those who believe are told to forgive **those who do not believe**.

**أَيَّامَ ٱللَّهِ** — "the days of God". It is transmitted that in the classical commentaries this construction is explained either as **what befell communities in the past** or as **the day of reckoning**. **I transmit both explanations; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse does not forbid requital; **it defers the ruling.** The continuation of the sentence says as much: *li-yajziya qawman bimā kānū yaksibūn* — the one who will give the requital is another.

## 45/16-20 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَا بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحُكْمَ وَٱلنُّبُوَّةَ … فَمَا ٱخْتَلَفُوٓا۟ إِلَّا مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا جَآءَهُمُ ٱلْعِلْمُ بَغْيًۢا بَيْنَهُمْ

"We gave the Children of Israel the Book, the judgement and prophethood… **They fell into disagreement only after knowledge had come to them, out of envy among themselves.**"

### The same sentence, a third time

**This expression has already been treated in two places in this series:** `002-bakara.md` 2/213 and 2/253. **In all three the same thing is said: disagreement arises not from the *absence* of proof, but *after* it has come.**

| Place | Expression |
|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/213 | *Min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu'l-bayyināt* — **baghy**an baynahum |
| al-Baqara 2/253 | *Min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu'l-bayyināt* |
| **al-Jāthiya 45/17** | *Min baʿdi mā jāʾahumu'l-**ʿilm*** — **baghy**an baynahum |

**The difference lies in a single word: in al-Baqara *bayyināt* (clear proofs), here *ʿilm* (knowledge).** **This is a choice in keeping with the sūra's axis of knowledge**, and it can be verified from the text.

**بَغْي** — root **ب-غ-ي**: to overstep the bound, to begrudge. The root was treated in `049-hucurat.md` at 49/9.

### ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَٰكَ عَلَىٰ شَرِيعَةٍ مِّنَ ٱلْأَمْرِ فَٱتَّبِعْهَا

**شَرِيعَة** — root **ش-ر-ع**. The concrete sense recorded by the lexicographers is worth recording: ***sharīʿa* is the path down which one goes to a river or a water source, the opening at which water is reached.** *Shāriʿ* — an open road.

**That the word carries this picture also describes the concept itself:** before it is a body of regulations, it is **a path opened in order to arrive somewhere.** And the place it arrives at is named with water, that is, with life.

**وَلَا تَتَّبِعْ أَهْوَآءَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ** — and what is set opposite it: **the *hawā* of those who do not know.**

**The opposition should be recorded: *sharīʿa* / *ahwāʾ*.** One is **an opened path**, the other **an inclination.** And the attribute of the second group is *lā yaʿlamūn* — those who do not know. **Once again the sūra ties the measure to knowledge.**

**هَٰذَا بَصَٰٓئِرُ لِلنَّاسِ** — *baṣāʾir* is the plural of *baṣīra*: **sight, insight.** The Book is named not as "rules" but as **"instruments of seeing"**.

## 45/21-22 — أَمْ حَسِبَ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱجْتَرَحُوا۟ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ أَن نَّجْعَلَهُمْ كَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ سَوَآءً مَّحْيَاهُمْ وَمَمَاتُهُمْ

"Do those who **commit** evil deeds suppose that We shall treat them like those who believe and do righteous deeds, **that their life and their death shall be equal**?"

### ٱجْتَرَحُوا۟ — root: ج-ر-ح

**The root is analysed here for the first time in this series.**

**The concrete sense: to wound, to cut open.** *Jurḥ* — a wound. **And from the same root comes *jawāriḥ*: birds of prey and — strikingly — the *limbs* of a person, that is, the hand, the foot, the eye.**

**The lexicographers make the connection thus:** the limbs are called *jawāriḥ* because it is with them that **gain is acquired**; and *ijtirāḥ* likewise means "to earn, to acquire". **The "wounding" branch of the root and the "earning" branch meet by way of the picture of hunting: the bird of prey earns by wounding.**

**I transmit this as a relation established by the lexicographers, not as a settled etymological ruling.**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** for those who do evil the verse does not use the verb *ʿamilū* (they did) — **it uses a verb that carries the sense of earning and of wounding.** For the other side it says *ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāt*.

### سَوَآءً مَّحْيَاهُمْ وَمَمَاتُهُمْ

**What the sentence rejects is that the two groups should be held equal *both in life and in death*.**

**There is disagreement over how this expression is to be understood:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | That their life and their death alike should be one — that is, that no distinction be made at all |
| 2 | That they should be equal in the hereafter as they are in this world |
| 3 | That they will die as they lived; that is, each group's death resembles its own life |

**All three readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**سَآءَ مَا يَحْكُمُونَ** — "how evil is the judgement they make." **And the verb is *yaḥkumūn* — to pass judgement.** That is, what the other side does is named as a **verdict**. The sūra's axis of knowledge passes here onto the plane of judgement: **the claim is not merely a claim to knowledge, it is a claim to a ruling.**

**وَخَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَلِتُجْزَىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍۭ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ** — and the ground is given: creation is *bi'l-ḥaqq*. **The same note was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/3** (*illā bi'l-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā*); **I rely on that treatment.** **The ground of the distinction is given not as a desire to punish but as a stipulation set upon creation itself.**

## 45/23 — أَفَرَءَيْتَ مَنِ ٱتَّخَذَ إِلَٰهَهُۥ هَوَىٰهُ وَأَضَلَّهُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ وَخَتَمَ عَلَىٰ سَمْعِهِۦ وَقَلْبِهِۦ وَجَعَلَ عَلَىٰ بَصَرِهِۦ غِشَٰوَةً

*A-fa-raʾayta mani'ttakhadha ilāhahū hawāhu wa aḍallahu'llāhu ʿalā ʿilmin wa khatama ʿalā samʿihī wa qalbihī wa jaʿala ʿalā baṣarihī ghishāwatan*

"Have you seen **the one who has taken his own craving as his god**? God has led him astray upon a knowledge, and has sealed his hearing and his heart, and laid a covering over his sight."

### إِلَٰهَهُۥ هَوَىٰهُ — the order of the words

**There are two words in the sentence, and which of them is the object is disputed among the lexicographers:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **"He took his craving as his god"** — that is, he put his desire in the place of God |
| 2 | **"He took his craving as the measure of his god"** — that is, he determined what to choose as a god according to his desire |

**Both readings lie within the limits Arabic allows, and both are mentioned in the classical commentaries. I do not impose a preference.**

**The second reading is worth recording, because it says something finer:** the person does not openly deify his desire; **he chooses a god, but he makes the choice according to the direction of his desire.** The first block of the sūra had enumerated the proofs; **on this reading the issue is not the proof but the measure by which the proof is selected.**

**هَوَىٰ** — root **ه-و-ي**: to fall downward; and desire, inclination. **The two branches of the root and the relation between them were treated in detail in `053-necm.md`** (the verbal identity between *idhā hawā* in al-Najm 53/1 and *ʿani'l-hawā* in 53/3). **I do not repeat it here.**

### وَأَضَلَّهُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ — a disputed note

**Whose the expression *ʿalā ʿilmin* is, is disputed, and it must not be concealed:**

| Reading | Whose knowledge *ʿalā ʿilmin* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **God's knowledge** — knowing his condition |
| 2 | **The person's knowledge** — he went astray although he knew |

**Both readings are transmitted.** **The second reading is consistent with the sūra as a whole** — in verse 8 the type who hears and resists, and in verse 9 the one who mocks after learning, had been described. **I record this as a supporting indication, not as a preference.**

**خَتَمَ … وَجَعَلَ عَلَىٰ بَصَرِهِۦ غِشَٰوَةً** — three organs are enumerated: **hearing, heart, sight.**

**The same three had been enumerated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/26** (*samʿan wa abṣāran wa afʾidatan*) and were treated there: **the instruments had been given, and had been of no use.** **Here the same three are named in their closed condition.** The two sūras use the same list of organs in two different scenes.

**And the verbs are different:** hearing and heart are **sealed** (*khatm* — to stamp a seal and close), while over the sight a **covering** is laid (*ghishāwa*). **What is closed at the entrance is distinguished from what is veiled in front.**

## 45/24 — وَقَالُوا۟ مَا هِىَ إِلَّا حَيَاتُنَا ٱلدُّنْيَا نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا وَمَا يُهْلِكُنَآ إِلَّا ٱلدَّهْرُ وَمَا لَهُم بِذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ

"They said: **Life is nothing but this life of ours in the world; we die and we live. Nothing destroys us but *dahr*.** Yet they have no knowledge of this whatever; they only **conjecture.**"

### The claim and the plane on which it is answered

**The verse conveys the other side's view exactly as it is, and it can be divided into three parts:**

1. **Life is only this** — there is no other life
2. **We die and we live** — the generations succeed one another
3. **Nothing destroys us but *dahr*** — the cause is time

**And the answer is given not to the content of the claim but to what it rests on:** *wa mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm* — **"they have no knowledge of this."**

**This is the clearest observation about method in the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** the verse does not say "you are mistaken"; it says **"you are not saying this on the basis of knowledge."** **That is, the other side's claim is treated as a claim to knowledge, and that attribute is taken from it.**

***In hum illā yaẓunnūn* — "they only conjecture."** The opposition between *ẓann* and *ʿilm* is the axis the sūra has been building from the start: in verse 4 *yūqinūn* (those who know with certainty), in 5 *yaʿqilūn*, in 20 *yūqinūn*, and here *yaẓunnūn*.

### ٱلدَّهْر — root: د-ه-ر

**The word means "long time, the age that goes on".** The root was treated in `076-insan.md` at 76/1 (*hal atā ʿala'l-insāni ḥīnun mina'd-dahr*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is this: there the word occurred as a *span*, here it is named as an *agent*** — "what destroys us".

**I record this as an observation:** the claim does not deny death; **it charges death to time.** That is, the observed fact (everyone dies) is correct; **the cause assigned to it is what is being questioned.** This meets the list of proofs in the sūra's first block at the very same point: **the alternation of night and day was named there as a *sign*; here the same working is counted as an *agent*.**

## 45/25-26 — وَإِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتُنَا بَيِّنَٰتٍ مَّا كَانَ حُجَّتَهُمْ إِلَّآ أَن قَالُوا۟ ٱئْتُوا۟ بِـَٔابَآئِنَآ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ

"And when Our signs are recited to them clearly, **their only argument** is to say: '**Bring back our forefathers, if you are truthful!**'"

### مَّا كَانَ حُجَّتَهُمْ إِلَّآ

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: the verse names the words spoken as *ḥujja* (argument)** — and limits it with *illā*: **they have no other argument.**

**حُجَّة** — root **ح-ج-ج**: to head towards a goal, to intend; and a word that binds the other party. **The word's sense of "proof" comes from its dragging the opposing party to a certain place in a dispute.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse does not begin by saying that what is said is not an argument; **it counts it as an argument and reports it as such, and leaves its weakness before the reader.** The demand is this: let the dead forefathers now be brought back.

**This demand had occurred in the same form in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/17** (*fa'tū bi-ābāʾinā in kuntum ṣādiqīn*) and was treated there: **the objection is framed by choosing the hardest example.**

**قُلِ ٱللَّهُ يُحْيِيكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يَجْمَعُكُمْ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ** — and the answer does not meet the demand: **it recalls the sequence.**

**Three verbs, joined by *thumma*:** He gives life → He gives death → He gathers. **In Arabic *thumma* conveys an interval; that is, there is time between the three verbs.** The difference between what is demanded (bring them now) and what is announced (they are gathered at an appointed time) lies exactly in this particle.

**The same answer had been given with the same word in `056-vakia.md` at 56/49-50** (*la-majmūʿūna ilā mīqāti yawmin maʿlūm*) and was treated there: **the gathering is tied not to a place but to a time.**

## 45/27-29 — وَتَرَىٰ كُلَّ أُمَّةٍ جَاثِيَةً كُلُّ أُمَّةٍ تُدْعَىٰٓ إِلَىٰ كِتَٰبِهَا · هَٰذَا كِتَٰبُنَا يَنطِقُ عَلَيْكُم بِٱلْحَقِّ إِنَّا كُنَّا نَسْتَنسِخُ مَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

*Wa tarā kulla ummatin jāthiya · Kullu ummatin tudʿā ilā kitābihā · Hādhā kitābunā yanṭiqu ʿalaykum bi'l-ḥaqq · Innā kunnā nastansikhu mā kuntum taʿmalūn*

"You will see **every community kneeling**. Every community will be called to its own book. 'This book of Ours **speaks** against you with the truth. We were having recorded what you were doing.'"

### جَاثِيَة — the name of the sūra

The root **ج-ث-و** — **analysed here for the first time in this series.**

**The sense the lexicographers give: to fall upon the knees.** *Jathā* — he knelt. From the root *juthwa* (a heap, a pile of stones) is also recorded.

**This posture of sitting is worth recording:** in Arab culture kneeling is the posture of **waiting and of reckoning** — in court, or the bearing of one who awaits a decision. It is not a posture of ease; **it is the state of being ready to rise but not yet risen.**

**And the verse gives this state to *every community*** — without distinction, all of them. **The distinction will be made at the next step, when the books are opened.**

### يَنطِقُ عَلَيْكُم — the speaking book

The root **ن-ط-ق**: to speak, to express in sound.

**The choice of verb is striking: the book does not "write" or "show", it *speaks*.** And the particle is *ʿalaykum* — **"against you".** In Arabic *ʿalā* here conveys being to one's detriment.

**I record this as my own reading:** a document is normally read; the party reading it interprets it. **The verse removes this intermediary: the document itself speaks.** The distance between the document and whatever might be said about it is thereby closed.

### نَسْتَنسِخُ — root: ن-س-خ

**The root occurs here for the first time in this series.**

**The senses of the root: to put one thing in the place of another (*naskh*), and to transfer a writing to another place (*istinsākh* — to make a copy of it).** The word *nuskha* comes from here.

**The pattern is Form X: *nastansikh*.** The lexicographers record that the pattern here has the sense of **"to have recorded, to have a copy made"** — that is, the act is not direct but by way of **having it written down**.

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** the act is described as **transferring what happens into a copy**. **The original is the life lived; the book is its copy.** For this reason the book in verse 29 is set down not as a document brought in from outside but as **a copy of what was lived.**

## 45/30-31 — فَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ فَيُدْخِلُهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ فِى رَحْمَتِهِۦ · وَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ أَفَلَمْ تَكُنْ ءَايَٰتِى تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْكُمْ فَٱسْتَكْبَرْتُمْ

"As for those who believe and do righteous deeds, their Lord admits them **into His mercy**… But to those who disbelieved: '**Were My signs not recited to you? And you made yourselves great.**'"

### Two requitals, two different languages

| Group | Requital | How it is stated |
|---|---|---|
| Those who believe and act | *Fī raḥmatih* — **admits them into His mercy** | **A declarative sentence** |
| Those who disbelieve | *A-fa-lam takun āyātī tutlā ʿalaykum* | **An interrogative sentence** |

**No verdict is announced to the second group — a question is asked.** And the content of the question is not a question about information: **"were they not recited?"**

**This corresponds exactly to the eighth verse of the sūra:** there the type had been described as *yasmaʿu āyāti'llāhi tutlā ʿalayhi thumma yuṣirru **mustakbiran***. **Here the same two elements return: being recited, and making oneself great.**

| Verse | Recitation | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | *Āyāti'llāhi tutlā ʿalayhi* | *Yuṣirru **mustakbiran*** |
| 31 | *Āyātī tutlā ʿalaykum* | *Fa'**stakbartum*** |

**The same two words twenty-three verses apart, one in the third person and one in the second.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and **it shows that the reckoning is framed with exactly the same words as the diagnosis made at the head of the sūra.**

The expression **فِى رَحْمَتِهِۦ** is also worth recording: the requital is named **not with the name of a place** but with an attribute — to be admitted *into* mercy. **Compared with the places in `056-vakia.md` where the descriptions of the Garden are enumerated object by object, the abstractness of the account here is a choice.**

## 45/32-35 — وَإِذَا قِيلَ إِنَّ وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ حَقٌّ وَٱلسَّاعَةُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهَا قُلْتُم مَّا نَدْرِى مَا ٱلسَّاعَةُ إِن نَّظُنُّ إِلَّا ظَنًّا وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمُسْتَيْقِنِينَ

"When it was said to you, 'God's promise is true, and there is no doubt about the Hour', you said: '**We do not know what the Hour is; we hold no more than a conjecture, and we are not among those who know with certainty.**'"

### The admission itself becomes the proof

**What is striking in the words the verse reports is this: the other side describes its own position correctly.** *In naẓunnu illā ẓannan wa mā naḥnu bi-mustayqinīn* — "we conjecture, we do not know with certainty."

**And this is the same as what was said about them in verse 24:** *in hum illā yaẓunnūn.*

| Verse | Who says it | What |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | **The text** — about them | *In hum illā yaẓunnūn* |
| 32 | **They themselves** — about themselves | *In naẓunnu illā ẓannā* |

**The same verdict, from two mouths.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** the discussion of knowledge sustained throughout the sūra closes here. **The other side says itself that it does not possess certain knowledge; and yet it issues a categorical verdict** (*mā hiya illā ḥayātuna'd-dunyā* — 24). **This is the inconsistency the verse displays: an absolute ruling issued on the basis of conjecture.**

**وَقِيلَ ٱلْيَوْمَ نَنسَىٰكُمْ كَمَا نَسِيتُمْ لِقَآءَ يَوْمِكُمْ هَٰذَا** — "today We **forget** you, just as you forgot the meeting of this day of yours."

**The verb is framed reciprocally and equated by the particle *kamā* (just as).** It is recorded in the classical commentaries that the *nisyān* here has the sense of **abandoning, of paying no regard**, and that forgetting is not in question with respect to God. **I enter this note;** the care for divine transcendence preserved elsewhere in this series for similar expressions holds here too.

## 45/36-37 — فَلِلَّهِ ٱلْحَمْدُ رَبِّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَرَبِّ ٱلْأَرْضِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · وَلَهُ ٱلْكِبْرِيَآءُ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ

"Praise belongs to God, Lord of the heavens, Lord of the earth, **Lord of all beings**. To Him belongs **greatness** in the heavens and the earth; He is the Mighty, the Wise."

### How the sūra closes

**In the close the word *rabb* is repeated three times:** Lord of the heavens, Lord of the earth, Lord of all beings. **The third already includes the first two** — that is, the repetition is not there to add information but **to widen the scope step by step.**

**ٱلْكِبْرِيَآء** — root **ك-ب-ر**: greatness.

**And the choice of the word ties back to the whole sūra:** in the eighth verse the type who resists was characterised as *mustakbiran* (making himself great) — **the same root.**

| Verse | Word | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | *Mustakbiran* | **Man** — resisting by making himself great |
| 37 | *al-Kibriyāʾ* | **God** — greatness is His |

**The sūra closes by returning to its place the attribute man had taken upon himself.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it is the cleanest ring in the sūra.

## The sūra as a whole — looking back

### The axis of knowledge

**Throughout the sūra the words to do with knowledge are distributed in an order:**

| Verse | Word | To whom it belongs |
|---|---|---|
| 3-5 | *Muʾminīn · yūqinūn · yaʿqilūn* | **Those able to receive the proof** |
| 9 | *ʿAlima* | The one who learns and then mocks |
| 17 | *Jāʾahumu'l-ʿilm* | Those who divided after knowledge came |
| 18 | *Alladhīna lā yaʿlamūn* | Those whose craving is not to be followed |
| 20 | *Yūqinūn* | The people for whom the Book is guidance |
| 23 | *ʿAlā ʿilmin* | Disputed — whose knowledge |
| 24 | *Mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm · yaẓunnūn* | **Those who issue a verdict on the basis of conjecture** |
| 26 | *Lā yaʿlamūn* | Most people |
| 32 | *Mā nadrī · naẓunnu · mā naḥnu bi-mustayqinīn* | **An admission from their own mouths** |

**This distribution is too orderly to be counted an accident, and it shows that the sūra is built around a single question.**

### Repetitions that can be verified within the sūra

| Root / word | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **ك-ب-ر** | *mustakbiran* (8) — *al-kibriyāʾ* (37) | The returning of the attribute |
| **ظ-ن-ن** | *yaẓunnūn* (24) — *naẓunnu* (32) | The same verdict, from two mouths |
| **ع-ل-م** | 9, 17, 18, 20, 23, 24, 26 | The backbone of the sūra |
| **ن-س-ي** | *nansākum* — *nasītum* (34) | A verb framed reciprocally |
| **س-خ-ر** | *sakhkhara* (12, 13) | The branch of making subservient |

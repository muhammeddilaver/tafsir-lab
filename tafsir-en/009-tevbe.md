# 9. Sūrat al-Tawba

**129 verses.** It is widely reported to belong to a late stage of the Medinan period. It is the **only sūra in the Qurʾān that does not open with the basmala**, and it is the sūra in this series that must be handled with the greatest care.

The sūra opens with a **declaration that treaties are dissolved**, continues with a **call to arms**, carries in its middle a long **analysis of hypocrisy**, and closes at the end with two verses — an envoy's compassion for those he addresses. The distance between these two poles produces every difficulty there is in reading the sūra.

**I write out at the start the limits that will be applied in this commentary, because they hold for the whole of the sūra:**

| # | Limit | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **No wholesale ruling will be issued about any religious or ethnic group.** What the verses describe are **attributes**; whoever carries the attribute is included in it | `STYLE.md`, the section "Prohibitions" |
| **2** | **No legal ruling will be issued**; the views of the schools and of the exegetes will be conveyed in tables, and no preference will be imposed | `STYLE.md`, the section "Disagreement" |
| **3** | **No link whatever will be made to present-day politics, present-day conflicts, or any party to them** | `STYLE.md`, the section "Prohibitions" |
| **4** | **No fabricated report.** Historical details will be given in the language of "it is reported" and without assertion; no personal names will be given | `STYLE.md`, the section "Accuracy" |
| **5** | My own inferences will be set apart with the phrase **"I record this as my own reading"** | `STYLE.md`, the section "Accuracy" |

**And a note on method:** the procedure applied in `047-muhammed.md` in treating 47/4 will be applied here too — **the limits set by the wording of the verse itself will be shown first, then the disagreement will be tabulated, and no ruling will be issued.** The measures laid down in `008-enfal.md` for the verses on war, for captives and for the etiquette of treaties are the ground of this sūra, and reference will be made to them continually.

## The two names of the sūra

The sūra is known by two names, and **both come from within the sūra itself:**

| Name | Where it comes from | Verse |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلتَّوْبَة** (*al-Tawba*) | The word **tawba**, repeated throughout the sūra, and in particular verses 117-118: *laqad tāba'llāhu ʿalā'l-nabiyyi wa'l-muhājirīna wa'l-anṣār* | 3, 5, 11, 15, 27, 74, 102, 104, 106, 112, 117, 118, 126 |
| **ٱلْبَرَآءَة** (*al-Barāʾa*) | The **first word** of the sūra: *Barāʾatun mina'llāhi wa rasūlih* | 1 |

**This doubleness is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** one and the same sūra is known both by the word for "keeping clear of, cutting off relations" (*barāʾa*) and by the word for "returning, being accepted back" (*tawba*). **The two names name the two ends of the sūra** — the first verse and the hundred and seventeenth.

The root **ب-ر-أ**: **to slip free of something, to draw away from it, to sever relations with it.** From the same root come *barīʾ* (clear of, unconnected), *barāʾa* (acquittal, a certificate of non-connection), and *bāriʾ* (the Maker — the one who separates a thing out of nothingness). **The root occurred in `008-enfal.md` at 8/48:** *innī barīʾun minkum* — what Satan says as he withdraws. **The same word: there an abandonment, here a declaration.**

The root **ت-و-ب**: **to turn, to come back.** That the word, when used of a servant, means "to turn one's back on the fault and come back", and when used of God (*tāba ʿalā*) means "to accept the return", is what the lexicographers record in common. **This is the sūra in which the root is used most densely in the Qurʾān.**

## Its beginning without the basmala

The sūra is **the only sūra in the Qurʾān that does not begin with the basmala**. This is a fact that can be seen directly from the text. **The reason for it, however, is not something known with certainty.**

Several explanations are transmitted in the classical sources. **I convey them in a table and prefer none of them, because none of them is certain knowledge:**

| Explanation | Content | What is to be recorded |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | That al-Tawba was counted as **a continuation of al-Anfāl**; that the two were seen as a single sūra, or that they complete one another in subject matter | The shared subject matter of the two sūras (treaties, the conduct of war, spoils) can be seen from the text; **the claim that they are joined, on the other hand, rests on transmitted report** |
| **2** | That it has to do with the **content** of the sūra: that a declaration dissolving a treaty is not opened with an expression of mercy | This is a **judgement**, not a transmitted report; it appears consistent with the first verse of the sūra but stands in tension with its last two verses |
| **3** | That in the period of the Companions there was **hesitation** as to whether the basmala should be placed at the head of the sūra, and that for this reason it was not written | It is so transmitted in the sources; **the reports differ from one another in detail** |
| **4** | That there is a technical reason having to do with the arrangement of the codex | It is the explanation conveyed with the least detail |

**No preference is adopted in this commentary.** The only thing verifiable from the text is this: **there is no basmala.** Everything said about the reason belongs to the explanations of a later period, and none of it claims certainty.

**One further note, as my own reading:** the second explanation above (the argument from content) is repeated very often; but the sūra is a sūra full of the word *mercy* — forgiveness and mercy appear openly in verses 27, 99, 104, 117, 118 and 128, and the sūra closes with *raʾūfun raḥīm*. **The argument from content, that is, does not fit the whole of the sūra.** I record this not as an objection but as the ground for adopting no preference.

## The historical frame: three layers

I set up here the three-layered frame established in `048-fetih.md`, because in this sūra a blurring of that boundary has the gravest consequences: **what the Qurʾān says / what the Qurʾān does not say / what the transmitted reports relate.**

### The first layer — what the text of the Qurʾān says

The facts that can be drawn directly from the sūra are these:

- There are **treaties that were made**, and some of them **have been broken** (1, 7, 8, 12, 13).
- Those who did **not** break their treaty **are excepted**, and what was promised them will be completed to the end of its term (4, 7).
- A **four-month period of free movement** is declared (2).
- One who **seeks refuge** is to be protected, is to be enabled to hear, and is to be **brought to a place of safety** (6).
- There is an announcement on a **day of the greater pilgrimage** (3).
- An engagement took place at a place called **Ḥunayn**; there, numbers secured no advantage (25-26).
- A regulation was made concerning entry to **al-Masjid al-Ḥarām** (28).
- An obligation called **jizya** is mentioned (29).
- It is stated that **the number of months is twelve and that the sacred months are four**, and that a practice called *nasīʾ* is abolished (36-37).
- There is a **call to an expedition**; heat (81), distance (42), and **ʿusra** (hardship) are mentioned (117).
- **A cave** is mentioned: the envoy and the single person with him (40).
- **A mosque built in order to do harm** is spoken of (107).
- **The categories to which the alms are to be applied** are enumerated as eight (60).
- **Three people who were left behind** are mentioned; their names are not given (118).

### The second layer — what the Qurʾān does not say

**Place names are extremely few.** The names of places and locations that occur in the text: **Ḥunayn** (25), **al-Masjid al-Ḥarām** (7, 19, 28), **Medina** (101, 120), and, in the context of the names of peoples, **Madyan** (70). Apart from these, the name of the place to which the expedition went **does not occur in the Qurʾān**; the word "Tabūk" is not in the Qurʾān.

None of the following is in the text: the text of the treaties, the names of the parties, how many treaties there were; by whom the declaration was read out; in what year it took place; the location of the cave in verse 40 and the name of the person with him; the names of the three people in verse 118; who built the mosque in verse 107; the proportions and measures of the categories in verse 60; the amount of the jizya.

**All of these come from the biographical, hadith and historical reports.**

### The third layer — what the transmitted reports relate

The following are **reported** in the classical sources (the sources differ from one another in the details, and in this commentary no detail has been made a condition of the meaning of a verse):

- It is reported that the first section of the sūra was declared in the ninth year after the emigration, during the pilgrimage season.
- It is reported that the engagement mentioned in verse 25 took place after the taking of Mecca, and that the believers were numerous.
- It is reported that a large part of the sūra came down on the occasion of a long and difficult expedition to the north; the verses about those who did not join the expedition are related as having come in that context.
- An account is transmitted concerning the demolition of the mosque mentioned in verse 107.
- The names of the three people in verse 118 and the process by which their case was decided are related in detail in the sources.

### How I use these three layers

**One.** The reports supply the context; the sūra does not narrate the event, it **refers to a situation its addressee already knows.** For this reason I shall use the background.

**Two.** The reports are not of the same degree of certainty as the Qurʾān. For this reason **I give no personal names**, I do not enter into narrative, and I give the details in the language of "it is reported".

**Three.** The logic of the sūra itself does not depend on the reports. The separating of the one who broke the treaty from the one who did not (4), the protection of the one who seeks refuge (6), the setting apart of those with a genuine excuse (91), the suspension of judgement (106) — all of these are **in the wording of the text itself.**

**Four.** Per the method (`STYLE.md`), **no wholesale ruling will be issued about any group.** The sūra uses names such as *mushrikīn*, *ahl al-kitāb*, *aʿrāb*, *munāfiqūn*; **each of these will be read in this commentary as an attribute, not as a label of identity.** The ground for this within the text is the sūra itself: verse 4 excepts a group from among the *mushrikīn*, verse 6 grants protection to one of the *mushrikīn*, verse 99 sets apart a group from among the *aʿrāb*, verse 100 praises a community. **The sūra divides its own categories.**

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-16 | The dissolution of the treaties; the exceptions; the right of refuge; the ethics of treaty | *wa'llāhu khabīrun bimā taʿmalūn* (16) |
| **II** | 17-24 | The tending of the mosques; who is to tend them; the weighing of eight ties | *wa'llāhu lā yahdi'l-qawma'l-fāsiqīn* (24) |
| **III** | 25-27 | Ḥunayn: numbers coming to nothing | *wa'llāhu ghafūrun raḥīm* (27) |
| **IV** | 28-35 | The regulation of al-Masjid al-Ḥarām; the jizya; the claims; the hoarding of wealth | *fa-dhūqū mā kuntum taknizūn* (35) |
| **V** | 36-37 | The number of the months and *nasīʾ* — the calendar set back in its place | *wa'llāhu lā yahdi'l-qawma'l-kāfirīn* (37) |
| **VI** | 38-41 | The call to the expedition; the growing heavy; the cave; *sakīna* | *in kuntum taʿlamūn* (41) |
| **VII** | 42-59 | Those who ask leave; the analysis of the excuse; the non-acceptance of their spending | *innā ila'llāhi rāghibūn* (59) |
| **VIII** | 60 | **The eight categories to which the alms are applied** | *wa'llāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm* (60) |
| **IX** | 61-70 | The block on the hypocrites; *udhun*; *baʿḍuhum min baʿḍ* | *wa ulāʾika humu'l-khāsirūn* (69) |
| **X** | 71-72 | **The opposite description:** *baʿḍuhum awliyāʾu baʿḍ* | *dhālika huwa'l-fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm* (72) |
| **XI** | 73-99 | Those left behind; the separating of the false excuse from the true | *inna'llāha ghafūrun raḥīm* (99) |
| **XII** | 100-110 | Those who went ahead; those who confessed; **those left suspended**; two foundations | *wa'llāhu lā yahdi'l-qawma'l-ẓālimīn* (109) |
| **XIII** | 111-118 | The transaction; nine attributes; the limit of asking forgiveness; **the acceptance of turning back** | *innahū huwa'l-tawwābu'l-raḥīm* (118) |
| **XIV** | 119-127 | Being with the truthful; going out for knowledge; those who are near | *bi-annahum qawmun lā yafqahūn* (127) |
| **XV** | 128-129 | **The portrait of the envoy, and the close** | *wa huwa rabbu'l-ʿarshi'l-ʿaẓīm* (129) |

**There are three things in the structure that must be recorded:**

**One. The sūra opens with a dissolution and closes with a mercy.** The first verse is *barāʾa* (the severing of a relation), the hundred and twenty-eighth *raʾūfun raḥīm*. **This is the greatest structural tension in the sūra, and it will be treated separately at the end.**

**Two. There is a symmetry in the middle of the sūra.** The sixty-seventh verse describes the hypocrites as *baʿḍuhum min baʿḍ* (they are of one another); the seventy-first describes the believers as *baʿḍuhum awliyāʾu baʿḍ* (they are the protectors of one another). **The two sentences are built on the same pattern and say two different things.** It will be given in a table at verse 71.

**Three. The pattern "wa minhum" (and among them are some) recurs in the sūra** (58, 61, 75, 98, 101 and elsewhere) — and each time it separates **a part** from a whole. **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra's own language is built in such a way as to block the issuing of wholesale rulings.

## Three axes repeated in the sūra

**The first axis: oath and word.** The words *ʿahd*, *mīthāq*, *aymān*, *ḥalafa* cluster in the sūra. The first block of the sūra treats the keeping or not keeping of **a word given**; the block on the hypocrites treats **the use of the oath as a shield**. **This second line was treated in detail in `063-munafikun.md` at 63/2 (*ittakhadhū aymānahum junnatan*); I rely on that treatment.**

**The second axis: sitting and rising.** *Qaʿada* (he sat), *nafara* (he went out), *iththāqala* (he grew heavy), *tathabbaṭa* (he was held back), *mukhallafūn* (those left behind), *khawālif* (those who stay behind). **The sūra is built on an axis of movement and immobility.**

**The third axis: wealth.** The word *amwāl* occurs again and again in the sūra — among the ties (24), in the censure of hoarding (34-35), in the non-acceptance of spending (53-54), in the categories to which the alms are applied (60), as an occasion of purification (103), and in the transaction (111). **The same word occurs within the sūra as a test, as an instrument, and as a price.**

## A preliminary note on the first block (1-16)

**This is the most critical section of the sūra and the place in this series that must be handled with the greatest care.** I apply here the procedure applied in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/4:

1. First I shall show **who is addressed**, from the wording of the verse itself.
2. Then I shall give, all together, **the limits the verses themselves lay down**.
3. Then I shall convey the discussion in the classical commentaries as **a neutral table of disagreement**.
4. **I shall issue no legal ruling, impose no preference, and make no link to any present-day event.**

**And I write this at the outset:** the verses of this block will not be attributed to any group, any party or any event of the present day. This is not a sentence of precaution; **it is a limit required by the wording of the verses themselves.** Its ground will be shown below, at verses 1 and 4.

## 9/1-2 — بَرَآءَةٌ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦٓ إِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ عَٰهَدتُّم مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · فَسِيحُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَٱعْلَمُوٓا۟ أَنَّكُمْ غَيْرُ مُعْجِزِى ٱللَّهِ

*Barāʾatun mina'llāhi wa rasūlihī ila'lladhīna ʿāhadtum mina'l-mushrikīn · Fa-sīḥū fi'l-arḍi arbaʿata ashhurin wa'ʿlamū annakum ghayru muʿjizi'llāhi wa anna'llāha mukhzi'l-kāfirīn*

"**A declaration from God and His Messenger, severing relations, to those of the mushrikūn with whom you made a treaty.** · **Travel about the earth for four months, and know that you cannot frustrate God; God will disgrace those who reject.**"

### To whom is the barāʾa declared — the wording of the verse itself

**This is the key to the whole block, and it is read off the first sentence.**

The sentence could have been built like this, and it is not:

- *Barāʾatun … ila'l-mushrikīn* — "to the mushrikūn." **It does not say this.**
- *Barāʾatun … ila'l-nās* — "to the people." **It does not say this.**

**What the sentence says is this: إِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ عَٰهَدتُّم مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ** — *ila'lladhīna **ʿāhadtum** mina'l-mushrikīn*: "to those of the mushrikūn **with whom you made a treaty**."

**The one addressed, that is, is fixed by a *relative pronoun* and a *relative clause*:**

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| **ٱلَّذِينَ** | Relative pronoun — it points to a determinate group |
| **عَٰهَدتُّم** | Relative clause — "with whom you made a treaty" |
| **مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ** | *Min* — **partitive** (a part out of a whole): "who are of the mushrikūn" |

**These three elements together do this: the one addressed is not a general human category; it is *a determinate party with whom a treaty had been made*.**

**And that the particle *min* is partitive must be recorded separately.** This reading is not an interpretation; **it is confirmed within the text by verses 4 and 6:** verse 4 excepts a part of that same group, and verse 6 lays down a separate ruling for *aḥadun mina'l-mushrikīn* (one of the mushrikūn). **The sūra itself, that is, does not use this name of a whole.**

**The note entered in `048-fetih.md` for the word *aʿrāb* and in `002-bakara.md` for the root ك-ف-ر holds here too: the name states not an identity but an attribute and a position.** I rely on that treatment.

### بَرَآءَة — root: ب-ر-أ

**The concrete sense of the root: to slip free of something, to separate from it, to sever relations with it.** The lexicographers record that beneath the root lies the idea of **"a thing's coming out of another thing, drawing away from it"**.

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **بَرِيء** (*barīʾ*) | Having no connection, clear of, acquitted |
| **بَرَآءَة** (*barāʾa*) | Non-connection; being clear of a liability; the declaration of this |
| **بَرَأَ** (*baraʾa*) | He created — He separated it out of nothingness and brought it forth |
| **بَرِئَ مِنَ ٱلْمَرَض** | He was freed of the illness, he recovered |

**The word occurred in `008-enfal.md` at 8/48** (*innī barīʾun minkum*), and there it stated an **abandonment**. **Here it is a *declaration*** — and its being a declaration is the procedural side of the verse.

### Word order: a statement, not a command

**The verse does not begin with a command.** *Barāʾatun* — an indefinite noun, standing at the head as subject (*mubtadaʾ*). In Arabic, sentences of this kind that begin with an indefinite belong to the style of **proclamation** and **announcement**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the sentence is a nominal one:** the verse does not command an act, **it makes the declaration of a state of affairs.** The act will come four verses later, and tied to conditions.

**And this coincides exactly with the procedure treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/58:** *wa immā takhāfanna min qawmin khiyānatan **fa'nbidh ilayhim ʿalā sawāʾ*** — "throw their treaty back to them **openly, on equal terms**." It was recorded there: **even in a case where treachery is suspected, acting in secret is shut off; the dissolving of a treaty is itself bound to a procedure.**

**The first verse of al-Tawba is that procedure carried out.** I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verbal overlap of the two verses: al-Anfāl says "throw it back with notice", and al-Tawba opens with a text of **notice**.

### فَسِيحُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ

The root **س-ي-ح**: **water flowing and spreading over the ground.** *Sayḥ* — flowing water; *sāʾiḥ* — one who moves about, who travels. The lexicographers record that when the verb is used of a person it means **"to move about unhindered"**.

**The choice of word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the command is not "flee" or "hide" — it is ***sīḥū***, that is, **move about freely.** The water-image of the root carries the fact that the movement is unimpeded.

**The same root will occur again in verse 112 of the sūra as *al-sāʾiḥūn***, and there it will be discussed in another sense. **I shall compare the two occurrences at 112.**

**أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ — four months.** The declaration of dissolution **does not take effect immediately**; a fixed period is granted.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the existence of the period:** a stretch of time is placed between the declaration and its consequence. **The function of the period is to give the other party room to move** — and indeed the command is a command to move (*sīḥū*).

## 9/3 — وَأَذَٰنٌ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦٓ إِلَى ٱلنَّاسِ يَوْمَ ٱلْحَجِّ ٱلْأَكْبَرِ

*Wa adhānun mina'llāhi wa rasūlihī ila'l-nāsi yawma'l-ḥajji'l-akbari anna'llāha barīʾun mina'l-mushrikīna wa rasūluh; fa-in tubtum fa-huwa khayrun lakum, wa in tawallaytum fa'ʿlamū annakum ghayru muʿjizi'llāh*

"**And an announcement from God and His Messenger to the people on the day of the greater pilgrimage: God is clear of the mushrikūn, and so is His Messenger. If you turn back, that is better for you; and if you turn away, know that you cannot frustrate God.**"

### أَذَٰن — root: أ-ذ-ن

**The concrete sense of the root: the ear.** *Udhun* — ear. From this derive the senses of **hearing**, of **giving leave** (hearing and accepting), and of **announcing** (bringing to the ear). **The root was treated in `022-hac.md` at 22/27 (*wa adhdhin fi'l-nāsi bi'l-ḥajj*) and in `014-ibrahim.md` at 14/7 (*wa idh taʾadhdhana rabbukum*).**

**أَذَٰن** here: **a public announcement, a proclamation.**

**And the choice of word must be recorded:** the same root will occur again in verse 61 of the sūra as ***udhun*** (ear), in verses 44-45 as ***yastaʾdhinuka*** (he asks your leave), and in verse 90 as ***li-yuʾdhana lahum***. **The sūra puts this root to four separate uses:**

| Verse | Derivative | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *Adhān* | **Public announcement** |
| **44-45, 83, 86, 90, 93** | *Istiʾdhān* | **Asking leave** — among the most repeated verbs of the sūra |
| **61** | *Udhun* | **Ear** — as a nickname |
| **49** | *Iʾdhan lī* | **"Give me leave"** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the number of these repetitions:** the sūra opens with an **announcement** and is then built upon **the asking of leave**. The same root is the word both for open proclamation and for covert avoidance.

### يَوْمَ ٱلْحَجِّ ٱلْأَكْبَرِ

**Which day the expression "the day of the greater pilgrimage" refers to has been debated in the classical commentaries. I convey the disagreement and impose no preference:**

| View | Which day | Cited as ground |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The day of sacrifice (*yawm al-naḥr*) | Transmitted reports |
| **2** | The day of ʿArafa | Transmitted reports |
| **3** | The whole of the pilgrimage season | The sense of the word *yawm* as "time, period" |

**By what the epithet "greater" (*akbar*) is greater has also been debated:** greater than the ʿumra (the ʿumra being counted as *al-ḥajj al-aṣghar*, the lesser pilgrimage), or greater by the scope of the declaration made in that year. **The Qurʾān does not explain this; I do not speak with certainty.**

### فَإِن تُبْتُمْ فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ

**The declaration does not end by closing the door.** The sentence carries a condition: *fa-in tubtum* — "if you turn back."

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the presence of the conditional clause:** into the harshest of notices, **a possibility capable of changing the situation** has been set. And that possibility lies in the other party's own hands.

**The same structure occurred in `008-enfal.md` at 8/33:** one of the two things holding back the punishment was *wa hum yastaghfirūn* — **an obstacle lying in the other party's own hands.** I rely on that treatment.

## 9/4 — إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ عَٰهَدتُّم مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ ثُمَّ لَمْ يَنقُصُوكُمْ شَيْـًٔا وَلَمْ يُظَٰهِرُوا۟ عَلَيْكُمْ أَحَدًا فَأَتِمُّوٓا۟ إِلَيْهِمْ عَهْدَهُمْ إِلَىٰ مُدَّتِهِمْ

*Illa'lladhīna ʿāhadtum mina'l-mushrikīna thumma lam yanquṣūkum shayʾan wa lam yuẓāhirū ʿalaykum aḥadan fa-atimmū ilayhim ʿahdahum ilā muddatihim; inna'llāha yuḥibbu'l-muttaqīn*

"**Except those of the mushrikūn with whom you made a treaty and who then did not fall short with you in anything and did not back anyone against you. Complete their treaty with them to the end of its term. God loves those who guard themselves.**"

### This verse is the axis of the block

**The fourth verse divides in two the party addressed in the first verse** — and the measure by which it divides is **not identity but conduct.**

| Verse | Who | What they have done | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | *Alladhīna ʿāhadtum mina'l-mushrikīn* | (Later in the block: broke it — 7, 8, 12, 13) | **A declaration of barāʾa** |
| **4** | *Alladhīna ʿāhadtum mina'l-mushrikīn* | *Lam yanquṣūkum* + *lam yuẓāhirū* | **The treaty is completed to its term** |

**The two verses use the same expression (*alladhīna ʿāhadtum mina'l-mushrikīn*) and issue two different rulings.** The only difference between them is **what the second group has not done.**

**This is a fact verifiable from the wording of the verse itself, and it is decisive for the reading of the block:** the ruling is tied not to **the name of a group** but to **an act.**

### The two conditions

**إِلَّا** — the particle of exception. **And the scope of the exception is fixed by two negative verbs:**

| Condition | Wording | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **ثُمَّ لَمْ يَنقُصُوكُمْ شَيْـًٔا** | **To have taken nothing away from the treaty** |
| **2** | **وَلَمْ يُظَٰهِرُوا۟ عَلَيْكُمْ أَحَدًا** | **To have backed no one against you** |

The root **ن-ق-ص**: **to diminish, to reduce.** Note: the verse uses not the root *nakatha* (to break) but the root **ن-ق-ص**. **The measure, that is, is not the wholesale repudiation of the treaty; even a piece taken away from it counts.**

The root **ظ-ه-ر**: **the back.** *Ẓāhara* (Form III) — **to lend one's back, to back, to support.** The concrete picture is plain: to stand behind someone. **The root occurred in `048-fetih.md` and in `033-ahzab.md`.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** both conditions are framed **negatively** — not "those who have done such and such" but "**those who have not done such and such**." **To fall within the scope of the exception, that is, one need do nothing; not having broken it is enough.**

### فَأَتِمُّوٓا۟ إِلَيْهِمْ عَهْدَهُمْ إِلَىٰ مُدَّتِهِمْ

**The command is unqualified: *atimmū* — complete it.**

The root **ت-م-م**: **to bring to completeness, to carry through to the end.**

**And two things must be recorded:**

**One. *ʿAhdahum* — "their treaty."** The pronoun belongs to the other party. **The treaty is named as the other party's right.**

**Two. *Ilā muddatihim* — "to the end of their term."** The term too is ascribed to the other party; no authority to shorten it is left to this side.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two pronouns (*hum*):** the verse frames the treaty not as **a favour granted by this side** but as **a right belonging to the other.**

### إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ

**The close of the verse must be recorded, and I give it as an observation:** the keeping of a word that has been given is named **taqwā**. **The same close will recur in verse 7** — that is, the sūra repeats the same ground twice, at the end of two separate notices about treaty.

## 9/5 — فَإِذَا ٱنسَلَخَ ٱلْأَشْهُرُ ٱلْحُرُمُ فَٱقْتُلُوا۟ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ حَيْثُ وَجَدتُّمُوهُمْ

*Fa-idha'nsalakha'l-ashhuru'l-ḥurumu fa'qtulu'l-mushrikīna ḥaythu wajadtumūhum wa khudhūhum wa'ḥṣurūhum wa'qʿudū lahum kulla marṣad; fa-in tābū wa aqāmu'l-ṣalāta wa ātawu'l-zakāta fa-khallū sabīlahum; inna'llāha ghafūrun raḥīm*

"**When the sacred months have passed, kill the mushrikūn wherever you find them, and seize them, and besiege them, and lie in wait for them at every place of watch. And if they turn back, and keep up the prayer, and give the alms, then leave their way open to them. God is forgiving, merciful.**"

---

**This is one of the verses of the Qurʾān most often cut away from its context. Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I shall do five things here, in order, and go beyond none of them:**

1. I shall show **how the sentence is built**.
2. I shall analyse the words.
3. I shall give, all together, **the limits the verse lays down within itself and within the block**.
4. I shall convey the discussion in the classical commentaries — the claim about the "verse of the sword" and those who reject it — as **a neutral table of disagreement**.
5. **I shall issue no legal ruling, impose no preference, and make no link to any present-day event.**

---

### The first question: how does the sentence begin?

**The verse does not begin with a command. It begins with a *condition of time*.**

**فَإِذَا ٱنسَلَخَ ٱلْأَشْهُرُ ٱلْحُرُمُ** — *fa-idha'nsalakha'l-ashhuru'l-ḥurum*: "**when the sacred months have passed**."

**This is the very structure treated in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/4** — there it was *fa-idhā laqītum* (when you meet), and it was recorded there: **the apodosis of a conditional sentence is not in force until the condition is realised.** The same note holds here.

**And the condition here is a condition of *time*:** the verse ties its own ruling to **the ending of a determinate period.** That period is the four months declared in verse 2.

**The sequence, then, is this, and it can be verified from the text:**

```
verse 1  — the declaration (barāʾa)
verse 2  — the period (four months, free movement)
verse 3  — the public announcement + the door of turning back
verse 4  — the exception of those who did not break the treaty
verse 5  — the ruling at the end of the period
verse 6  — the protection of the one who seeks refuge
```

**The fifth verse is the fifth link in this chain. Read on its own, the first four links of the chain disappear.**

### ٱنسَلَخَ — root: س-ل-خ

**The concrete sense of the root: to flay a hide, to separate it from the body.** *Salkh* — the flaying of a hide; *insalakha* (Form VII, reflexive) — **to slip out, to be peeled away and separated.**

**This word occurred in `036-yasin.md` at 36/37:** *wa āyatun lahumu'l-laylu **naslakhu** minhu'l-nahār* — "We **peel** the day **off** the night."

**The choice of verb is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the verse does not say "when the months end"; it says **"when the months have slipped away"**. The picture in the root is of a thing **being separated from a body**. **Time, that is, is described as a skin peeled off.**

### ٱلْأَشْهُرُ ٱلْحُرُمُ

**"The sacred months" — they will be defined in verse 36 of the sūra:** *minhā arbaʿatun ḥurum* — four of the twelve months. **They will be treated separately at that verse.**

**The debate here must be recorded:** in the classical commentaries it has been disputed whether this expression means **the four months declared in verse 2** or **the known sacred months of the year**. **I convey the disagreement and impose no preference:**

| View | What *al-ashhuru'l-ḥurum* is | Cited as ground |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The four-month period of verse 2** | The context; the link between the two verses |
| **2** | **The four sacred months of the year** | The *al-* being that of the known referent (*maʿhūd*) |

### The sequence of the commands

**The verse enumerates four verbs, and the four are not all of one kind:**

| Verb | Root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱقْتُلُوا۟** | ق-ت-ل | Kill |
| **خُذُوهُمْ** | أ-خ-ذ | **Seize** — take captive |
| **ٱحْصُرُوهُمْ** | ح-ص-ر | **Besiege** — narrow their room to move |
| **ٱقْعُدُوا۟ لَهُمْ كُلَّ مَرْصَدٍ** | ق-ع-د / ر-ص-د | **Lie in wait at the places of watch** |

**Three of the four verbs are not killing.** *Akhdh* (seizing), *ḥaṣr* (besieging) and *quʿūd ʿalā marṣad* (keeping watch) — all three are verbs of **bringing under control**.

The root **ر-ص-د**: **to watch, to wait.** *Marṣad* — a place of watch, the head of a pass. **The word was treated in `072-cin.md` at 72/9 and 72/27 (*raṣadā*).**

**I record this as an observation:** the enumeration of these four verbs together shows that the scene is **a field of conflict** — besieging, holding a pass and taking captives are acts of war. **And the measure recorded in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/4 holds here too: the kind of the verbs makes plain the kind of the scene.**

### فَإِن تَابُوا۟ … فَخَلُّوا۟ سَبِيلَهُمْ

**The verse brings its own ruling to an end within itself.**

The root **خ-ل-و**: **to be empty, to clear.** *Khallū sabīlahum* — **"clear their road", that is, set them free, open the way before them.**

**This is the last sentence of the verse, and it is a command to *let go*.** And the verse closes with **ghafūrun raḥīm** (forgiving, merciful).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verse's own sequence:** one and the same verse carries both the harshest command and **the condition on which that command ends.** **To read the verse as the "verse of the sword" and leave off its last sentence is to read half of it.**

### The limits laid down by the verse and by the block — all together

**Every one of the items below is in the wording of the text. Not one is a note brought in from outside:**

| # | Limit | Its ground in the wording |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The one addressed is **a determinate party with whom a treaty had been made** | **1:** *ila'lladhīna **ʿāhadtum** mina'l-mushrikīn* |
| **2** | The particle *min* states a **partitive** — not the whole of a whole | **1:** *mina'l-mushrikīn* |
| **3** | The ruling is **tied to a period**; it does not take effect immediately | **2:** *arbaʿata ashhur* · **5:** *fa-idha'nsalakha* |
| **4** | Throughout the period there is **free movement** | **2:** *fa-sīḥū fi'l-arḍ* |
| **5** | **Those who did not break their treaty are outside the ruling** | **4:** *illa'lladhīna … lam yanquṣūkum* |
| **6** | With those who did not break it, the treaty **is completed to the end of its term** | **4:** *fa-atimmū ilayhim ʿahdahum ilā muddatihim* |
| **7** | **One who seeks refuge is protected and brought to a place of safety** | **6:** *fa-ajirhu … thumma ablighhu maʾmanah* |
| **8** | The ruling **ends with turning back** | **5:** *fa-in tābū … fa-khallū sabīlahum* |
| **9** | Most of the commands are verbs **not of killing but of bringing under control** | **5:** *khudhūhum*, *uḥṣurūhum*, *uqʿudū* |
| **10** | The verse closes **with a name of forgiveness** | **5:** *ghafūrun raḥīm* |
| **11** | In verse 12 the condition of fighting is **the breaking of the oath** | **12:** *wa in nakathū aymānahum* |
| **12** | In verse 13 the ground is **that the other side began it** | **13:** *wa hum badaʾūkum awwala marra* |

**All twelve of these items are in the block's own wording.**

**And the measure recorded in `047-muhammed.md` holds here too and is worth repeating:** to "cut a verse away from its context" means exactly this — **to drop the first, third, fifth and seventh of the items above and take the middle sentence on its own.** In that state the verse says something else; but what it says is no longer what the verse says.

### The debate over the "verse of the sword" — a table of disagreement

**In the classical literature of exegesis and legal theory this verse has been called *āyat al-sayf* ("the verse of the sword"), and some scholars have argued that it abrogates the verses of peace, pardon and forbearance in the Qurʾān. There have also been those who rejected this.**

**I convey the disagreement in a table. I adopt no preference and issue no legal ruling:**

| View | Its claim | Cited as ground | Cited as the other side's objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Those who count it abrogating** | The verse has lifted the earlier verses of forbearance and peace | The seemingly unrestricted wording of the verse; the ruling's coming at a late period | The verse is built **within itself** as conditional and time-bound; wording alone is not enough for abrogation |
| **B. Those who limit the claim of abrogation** | What the verse abrogates is only the earlier rulings about waiting, and only in respect of **those who break a treaty** | The qualification *alladhīna ʿāhadtum* in verses 1 and 4 | On this view the name "verse of the sword" is misleading |
| **C. Those who reject the claim of abrogation** | The verse is not a verse of abrogation; it regulates a determinate situation | The presence of verses 4 and 6 in the same block; the fact that 8/61 (*wa in janaḥū li'l-salmi fa'jnaḥ lahā*) remains in force | — |
| **D. Those who narrow the scope of the concept of abrogation** | In the early period the word "abrogation" was used **more broadly** than it is today; specification and qualification too were called "abrogation" | The history of the term in the literature of legal theory | — |

**No preference is adopted in this commentary. What is to be recorded is this, and the same was recorded in `047-muhammed.md`:** the debate itself shows that the verse was not read as **a general ruling standing alone and unconditional**. **The classical tradition has always handled this verse together with other verses. To take the verse on its own and present it as a general ruling is the exact opposite of what that tradition did.**

**And one further limit:** the question of *abrogation* is a debate in **legal theory**, and both its definition and its scope differ between the schools. **This commentary does not enter that debate and takes no side in it.**

### What this says to the present

**Here I must be careful. The most mistaken thing that could be written under the heading "what this says to the present" for this verse is to tie it to some present-day conflict, some party, or some group. I shall not do that.**

**I record a single observation, ascribed to no party:**

**The harshest sentence of a text is read together with the qualifications that the text itself lays down.** This block carries twelve separate qualifications, and all of them are within the text. To take the harsh sentence and leave the qualifications is not reading but **selection**. This measure holds not only for this verse but for every text.

## 9/6 — وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ ٱسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّىٰ يَسْمَعَ كَلَٰمَ ٱللَّهِ ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُۥ

*Wa in aḥadun mina'l-mushrikīna'stajāraka fa-ajirhu ḥattā yasmaʿa kalāma'llāhi thumma ablighhu maʾmanah; dhālika bi-annahum qawmun lā yaʿlamūn*

"**And if one of the mushrikūn asks you for protection, grant him protection — so that he may hear the word of God. Then bring him to the place where he will be safe. That is because they are a people who do not know.**"

### This verse comes immediately after the fifth

**This is a fact decisive for the reading of the block, and it can be verified from the text:** **immediately behind** the verse that carries the harshest command, a **ruling of protection** has been placed for a single individual from the other side.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses:** at the very place where it issues the ruling, the text also opens the door that leads out of it. **And the side that opens the door is the side that issued the ruling.**

### ٱسْتَجَارَكَ / فَأَجِرْهُ — root: ج-و-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: a side, a neighbourhood.** *Jār* — **neighbour**. From the same root:

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **جَار** (*jār*) | Neighbour; **one who is under another's protection** |
| **أَجَارَ** (*ajāra*) | He protected, he took under his protection |
| **ٱسْتَجَارَ** (*istajāra*) | Form X — **he sought protection, he took refuge** |
| **جِوَار** (*jiwār*) | Neighbourhood; protection |

**The root was treated in `067-mulk.md`, `023-muminun.md`, `016-nahl.md` and `072-cin.md`.** **The occurrence of the word in `008-enfal.md` at 8/48 must be recorded separately:** there Satan's words were *wa innī **jārun** lakum* ("I am beside you / your protector"), and **he did not keep his word.**

**The two occurrences can be set side by side:**

| Place | Who says it | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| al-Anfāl 8/48 | Satan: *innī jārun lakum* | *Nakaṣa ʿalā ʿaqibayh* — he drew back |
| **al-Tawba 9/6** | The command: *fa-ajirhu* | *Thumma ablighhu maʾmanah* — **he is brought to a place of safety** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the presence of the same root in two separate verses:** in one, protection is promised and then abandoned; in the other, protection is commanded and required to be **carried through to the end.**

### The three stages of the command

**The verse sets out three verbs, and each is a separate obligation:**

| Stage | Wording | Obligation |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **فَأَجِرْهُ** | **Grant protection** — do not refuse the request |
| **2** | **حَتَّىٰ يَسْمَعَ كَلَٰمَ ٱللَّهِ** | **Enable him to hear** — the purpose of the protection |
| **3** | **ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُۥ** | **Bring him to his place of safety** — even when the protection is over |

**The third stage must be recorded, and it is the most striking point in this verse.**

**م-أ-م-ن — a noun of place** from the root **أ-م-ن**: *maʾman* — **the place in which one will be safe.**

**And the verb is *abligh*: "bring him through."** That is, not merely "let him go" — an **obligation to deliver** is laid down.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle *thumma* together with the transitivity of the verb:** whether or not the hearing has borne fruit — no condition of acceptance is laid down — the verse places the person's **safety in this side's charge.**

**And this note is important:** *ḥattā yasmaʿa kalāma'llāh* is a condition of **hearing**, not a condition of **acceptance**. The verse does not say "until he believes"; it says "**until he hears**". And what is to be done afterwards is to take him to a place of safety even if he has not accepted.

### ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ لَّا يَعْلَمُونَ

**The ground must be recorded: *lā yaʿlamūn* — they do not know.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse ties the ground of the protection to **not knowing**. **The other side's position, that is, is explained not by enmity but by ignorance** — and the remedy for not knowing is that the chance to hear be given.

**The same measure was recorded in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/16:** *the verse issues no ruling against one who does not know.* I rely on that treatment.

## 9/7 — كَيْفَ يَكُونُ لِلْمُشْرِكِينَ عَهْدٌ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ وَعِندَ رَسُولِهِۦٓ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ عَٰهَدتُّمْ عِندَ ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْحَرَامِ

*Kayfa yakūnu li'l-mushrikīna ʿahdun ʿinda'llāhi wa ʿinda rasūlihī illa'lladhīna ʿāhadtum ʿinda'l-masjidi'l-ḥarām; fa-ma'staqāmū lakum fa'staqīmū lahum; inna'llāha yuḥibbu'l-muttaqīn*

"**How can the mushrikūn have a treaty with God and with His Messenger — except those with whom you made a treaty at al-Masjid al-Ḥarām. So long as they are straight with you, be straight with them. God loves those who guard themselves.**"

### The second exception

**There was an exception in the fourth verse; the seventh lays down a second.** And the measure of this second exception is different: **the place where the treaty was made.**

| Verse | The measure of the exception |
|---|---|
| **4** | **Conduct** — not having broken it |
| **7** | **The place where the treaty was made** — *ʿinda'l-masjidi'l-ḥarām* |

**I record this as an observation:** the block holds **two separate sections** of the party bearing the same name outside the ruling. **The sūra breaches its own category twice.**

### فَمَا ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟ لَكُمْ فَٱسْتَقِيمُوا۟ لَهُمْ

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded, and it is the very structure treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/61.**

Form X of the root **ق-و-م**: **istiqāma** — to stand straight, not to bend.

**And on both sides the same verb is used, in the same form:**

| Side | Verb |
|---|---|
| They | *Ma'staqāmū lakum* |
| You | *Fa'staqīmū lahum* |

**The same symmetry was recorded in `008-enfal.md` at 8/61:** *in janaḥū li'l-salmi **fa'jnaḥ** lahā* — "if they incline to peace, incline to it too." **The measure recorded there holds here as well: the response is given with the same verb and in the same form.** I rely on that treatment.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the overlap of the verbs in the two verses:** the Qurʾān builds the treaty relation on **reciprocity** — and it shows the reciprocity by means of the language itself, laying the same verb on both sides.

**And the particle *mā* must be dwelt on:** *fa-**mā** staqāmū lakum* — here *mā* states time: "**so long as** they are". **The obligation, that is, continues together with the conduct of the other side.**

## 9/8 — كَيْفَ وَإِن يَظْهَرُوا۟ عَلَيْكُمْ لَا يَرْقُبُوا۟ فِيكُمْ إِلًّا وَلَا ذِمَّةً

*Kayfa wa in yaẓharū ʿalaykum lā yarqubū fīkum illan wa lā dhimma; yurḍūnakum bi-afwāhihim wa taʾbā qulūbuhum wa aktharuhum fāsiqūn*

"**How can it be: if they get the upper hand over you, they observe towards you neither *ill* nor *dhimma*. They please you with their mouths, while their hearts refuse; and most of them are transgressors.**"

### إِلّ — root: إ-ل-ل

**This word occurs in the Qurʾān only in this sūra (verses 8 and 10), and in this series it is analysed here for the first time.**

**On the concrete sense of the root the lexicographers record more than one thing, and all of them come out at the same place:**

| Sense recorded | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Sharpness, pointedness** | *All* — the head of a spear; *alla* — to sharpen |
| **To raise a loud voice** | *Alīl* — moaning, crying out |
| **The bond of kinship, relatedness** | The sense most of the lexicographers give for this verse |
| **Oath, covenant** | The sense some lexicographers give |
| **A word given in God's name** | The sense some exegetes give |

**What the word means in this verse is disputed. I convey the disagreement and impose no preference:**

| View | What *ill* is here |
|---|---|
| **1** | **The bond of kinship** — blood tie, relatedness |
| **2** | **Oath, covenant** — a word given |
| **3** | **Reverence towards God** — the word being taken as bearing on the divine name |

**I record this as an observation about the language:** I cannot establish with certainty the link between the root's senses of "sharpness" and "moaning" and its sense of "kinship", and I do not try to establish it. **The note entered in `101-karia.md` holds here too: meaning is not derived from a shared sound.**

### ذِمَّة — root: ذ-م-م

**The concrete sense of the root: to blame, to censure, to find fault.** *Dhamm* — the opposite of praise (*madḥ*). **The root was treated in `068-kalem.md`** (*madhmūm* — blamed).

**And the derivation of the word *dhimma* from this root is worth recording.**

**ذِمَّة** — the sense the lexicographers give: **the bond a person is obliged to keep, and for neglecting which he will be *blamed*.** *Dhimma*, that is, is "a responsibility whose neglect will be a cause of blame". From the same root **ذِمِّىّ** (*dhimmī*) — one under protection.

**I record this as an observation about the language, resting on the derivation the lexicographers give:** the word names the responsibility **by the shame of abandoning it.** **The name of the bond, that is, comes from the consequence of trampling on the bond.**

### The two words coming together

**Both words occur together in verses 8 and 10 alike: *illan wa lā dhimma*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the semantic field of the two words:** *ill* (on the first reading above) is the bond that comes **by birth** — kinship; *dhimma* is the bond that is **established afterwards** — a word given. **The two together cover the two kinds of bond that bind a person: the one that comes by blood and the one that is built by a word.**

**And what the verse says is that neither bond is observed.** The measure is tied not to identity but to **the refusal to recognise a bond.**

### يُرْضُونَكُم بِأَفْوَٰهِهِمْ وَتَأْبَىٰ قُلُوبُهُمْ

**The distance between the mouth and the heart.** **This structure was treated in detail in `048-fetih.md` at 48/11 (*yaqūlūna bi-alsinatihim mā laysa fī qulūbihim*), and it was the backbone of `063-munafikun.md`. I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference here must be recorded:** in al-Fatḥ and al-Munāfiqūn the structure was built with the **tongue** (*lisān*); here it is built with the **mouth** (*fam/afwāh*). **And the word *afwāh* will occur three times in the sūra: 8, 30, 32.** In all three what is related is **that the word spoken has nothing behind it to answer to it.**

| Verse | Expression | What is being said |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *Yurḍūnakum bi-afwāhihim* | A pleasing word |
| **30** | *Dhālika qawluhum bi-afwāhihim* | A claim |
| **32** | *Yurīdūna an yuṭfiʾū nūra'llāhi bi-afwāhihim* | An attempt to put out |

**This set of three is verifiable from the text and must be recorded.** **I give it as my own reading:** the sūra uses the word *mouth* each time in order to show **the gap between word and reality.** In the thirty-second verse this reaches its plainest form: light is not put out with a mouth.

---

## 9/9-11 — ٱشْتَرَوْا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا · لَا يَرْقُبُونَ فِى مُؤْمِنٍ إِلًّا وَلَا ذِمَّةً · فَإِن تَابُوا۟ وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتَوُا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ فَإِخْوَٰنُكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ

*Ishtaraw bi-āyāti'llāhi thamanan qalīlan fa-ṣaddū ʿan sabīlih; innahum sāʾa mā kānū yaʿmalūn · Lā yarqubūna fī muʾminin illan wa lā dhimma; wa ulāʾika humu'l-muʿtadūn · Fa-in tābū wa aqāmu'l-ṣalāta wa ātawu'l-zakāta fa-ikhwānukum fi'l-dīn; wa nufaṣṣilu'l-āyāti li-qawmin yaʿlamūn*

"**They exchanged the signs of God for a small price and turned others from His way. Evil indeed is what they have been doing.** · **Towards a believer they observe neither *ill* nor *dhimma*. Those are the ones who overstep.** · **And if they turn back, and keep up the prayer, and give the alms, then they are your brothers in religion. We set the signs out in detail for a people who know.**"

### ٱشْتَرَوْا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا

**This expression was treated at several places in `002-bakara.md`** (*wa lā tashtarū bi-āyātī thamanan qalīlā*), and **the detail of the image of buying and selling** was given there. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What is to be recorded here is its place within the sūra:** in verse 111 of the same sūra **another transaction** will be related — *inna'llāha'shtarā mina'l-muʾminīna anfusahum wa amwālahum bi-anna lahumu'l-janna.* **The two transactions stand at the two ends of the sūra, and they will be compared at verse 111.**

### The difference between verses 8 and 10

**The two verses build almost the same sentence, and one word changes:**

| Verse | Wording | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *Lā yarqubū **fīkum** illan wa lā dhimma* | **Towards you** — the community addressed |
| **10** | *Lā yarqubūna **fī muʾminin** illan wa lā dhimma* | **Towards a believer** — an attribute |

**This difference must be recorded, and it is verifiable from the text.** **I give it as my own reading:** the first sentence names **a determinate community**, the second names **an attribute**. **The description, that is, widens from "your group" to "everyone who believes"** — and what widens is **the side that is protected**, not the side against which a ruling is issued.

The root **ر-ق-ب**: **to watch, to keep an eye on; the neck.** *Raqaba* — neck; *murāqaba* — watching. **The root was treated in `058-mucadele.md`, `090-beled.md` and `047-muhammed.md`.** **And the same root will occur again in verse 60 of this sūra as *fi'l-riqāb*.**

### فَإِخْوَٰنُكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ

**The eleventh verse is one of the most striking sentences of the block and must be recorded.**

**After the gravest description has been made in verses eight and nine, the eleventh verse says this: *fa-ikhwānukum fi'l-dīn* — "then they are your brothers in religion."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the distance between the two verses:** the separation is **not narrowed**; when the condition is met it closes **completely.** No intermediate status is laid down, no note of "they used to be such and such" is entered. **The word is straightforwardly *brotherhood*.**

**And all three conditions are acts:** *tābū* (they turned back), *aqāmu'l-ṣalāta* (they kept up the prayer), *ātawu'l-zakāta* (they gave the alms). **The same three occurred at the end of verse 5 as well.** **In both verses the same conditions yield two different outcomes:**

| Verse | Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | Turning back + prayer + alms | *Fa-khallū sabīlahum* — **set them free** |
| **11** | Turning back + prayer + alms | *Fa-ikhwānukum fi'l-dīn* — **they are your brothers** |

**I record this as an observation:** one and the same block attaches two returns to the same condition — one legal (inviolability), the other social (brotherhood).

## 9/12 — وَإِن نَّكَثُوٓا۟ أَيْمَٰنَهُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ عَهْدِهِمْ وَطَعَنُوا۟ فِى دِينِكُمْ فَقَٰتِلُوٓا۟ أَئِمَّةَ ٱلْكُفْرِ

*Wa in nakathū aymānahum min baʿdi ʿahdihim wa ṭaʿanū fī dīnikum fa-qātilū aʾimmata'l-kufr; innahum lā aymāna lahum laʿallahum yantahūn*

"**And if, after their treaty, they break their oaths and attack your religion with their tongues, then fight the leaders of unbelief. They have no oaths; it may be that they will desist.**"

### What is the condition?

**The verse opens with two conditions, and both are acts:**

| Condition | Wording | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **نَّكَثُوٓا۟ أَيْمَٰنَهُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ عَهْدِهِمْ** | **They broke their oaths after the treaty** |
| **2** | **وَطَعَنُوا۟ فِى دِينِكُمْ** | **They attacked your religion with their tongues** |

**The first condition is the verb that is the axis of this block, and it must be underlined: *nakth*.**

The root **ن-ك-ث**: **to undo a twisted cord, to unravel it.** **The root was treated in detail in `048-fetih.md` at 48/10**, and it was recorded there: *"nakth is used of wool or hair that has been unravelled: spun, and then undone. To give one's word is to spin a cord; to break one's word is to unravel the cord that was spun."* **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**And the difference of the usage here from that in al-Fatḥ must be recorded:**

| Place | Who breaks it | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| al-Fatḥ 48/10 | *Fa-man **nakatha** fa-innamā yankuthu ʿalā nafsih* | **Against the breaker himself** |
| **al-Tawba 9/12** | *Wa in **nakathū** aymānahum* | **A condition of fighting** |

**I record this as my own reading:** the same verb is used, in one place of a pledge of allegiance on one's own side, in the other of a treaty on the opposing side — **and in both verses the cause of the outcome is the act itself.**

### طَعَنُوا۟ — root: ط-ع-ن

**The concrete sense of the root: to thrust with a spear, to pierce.** *Ṭaʿn* — a bayonet-thrust. From this, figuratively, **to wound with the tongue, to disparage, to slander.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the verse names an assault made with words **by a verb of piercing.** The concrete state of the root carries the weight of the figure.

### أَئِمَّةَ ٱلْكُفْر

**The object must be recorded, and this is the most striking point in the verse.**

The verse does not say "fight them". It says *Fa-qātilū **aʾimmata'l-kufr*** — "fight **the leaders of unbelief**."

***Imām***, from the root **أ-م-م**: **one who goes in front, one who is followed.** Its plural is *aʾimma*.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word itself:** the target is named not as the whole of a community but as **the position that goes out in front and directs.** **The choice of the word is a narrowing.**

**And I give this not as a legal ruling but as a note about the language.** The scope of this expression in the verse has been debated in the classical commentaries; **I do not enter the debate and issue no ruling.**

### لَعَلَّهُمْ يَنتَهُونَ

**The purpose of the verse must be recorded: *laʿallahum yantahūn* — "it may be that they will desist."**

The root **ن-ه-ي**: **end, ending; desisting.** *Intihāʾ* — coming to an end.

**I record this as an observation:** the purpose placed at the end of the verse is not to destroy but **to stop.** And the particle *laʿalla* states **hope** — not certainty.

## 9/13-14 — أَلَا تُقَٰتِلُونَ قَوْمًا نَّكَثُوٓا۟ أَيْمَٰنَهُمْ وَهَمُّوا۟ بِإِخْرَاجِ ٱلرَّسُولِ وَهُم بَدَءُوكُمْ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ · قَٰتِلُوهُمْ يُعَذِّبْهُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِأَيْدِيكُمْ

*A-lā tuqātilūna qawman nakathū aymānahum wa hammū bi-ikhrāji'l-rasūli wa hum badaʾūkum awwala marra; a-takhshawnahum fa'llāhu aḥaqqu an takhshawhu in kuntum muʾminīn · Qātilūhum yuʿadhdhibhumu'llāhu bi-aydīkum wa yukhzihim wa yanṣurkum ʿalayhim wa yashfi ṣudūra qawmin muʾminīn*

"**Will you not fight a people who broke their oaths, who set out to drive the Messenger away, and who began against you the first time? Do you fear them? If you are believers, God is more deserving of your fear.** · **Fight them: God will punish them by your hands, and disgrace them, and give you victory over them, and heal the breasts of a believing people.**"

### Three grounds

**The verse enumerates the grounds of the call itself, and all three are acts of the other side:**

| # | Ground | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Breaking an oath** | *Nakathū aymānahum* |
| **2** | **Setting out to drive the envoy away** | *Hammū bi-ikhrāji'l-rasūl* |
| **3** | **Being the one who began** | *Wa hum badaʾūkum awwala marra* |

**The third ground must be recorded, and it is the plainest note in this verse: *wa hum badaʾūkum awwala marra* — "it is they who began, the first time."**

The root **ب-د-أ**: **to begin, to do first.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the presence of this sentence:** the verse records openly **who started** the conflict. **And this coincides with the three attempts mentioned in `008-enfal.md` at 8/30** (*li-yuthbitūka aw yaqtulūka aw yukhrijūk*) — there too "driving out" was one of the three options.

**And it must be read together with the limit laid down in `002-bakara.md` at 2/190 (*wa lā taʿtadū*).** In this commentary I refer to that verse and draw no ruling from it.

### وَيَشْفِ صُدُورَ قَوْمٍ مُّؤْمِنِينَ

**The last sentence of the fourteenth verse is worth recording.**

The root **ش-ف-ي**: **healing, recovery; and to come to the edge of a thing** (*shafā* — edge; it will occur in verse 109 of the sūra as *shafā jurufin hār*).

**I record this as an observation:** the verse names the outcome as a **healing** — *ṣudūr* (breasts) is described as a wound. **And the fifteenth verse continues: *wa yudhhib ghayẓa qulūbihim* — "and remove the rage of their hearts."**

**Among the things enumerated as purposes, that is, is also the passing of the addressed community's own rage.** I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verb *yudhhib*: rage is set down not as **a permanent state** but as **something to be removed.**

## 9/15-16 — وَيُذْهِبْ غَيْظَ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَيَتُوبُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ مَن يَشَآءُ · أَمْ حَسِبْتُمْ أَن تُتْرَكُوا۟

*Wa yudhhib ghayẓa qulūbihim; wa yatūbu'llāhu ʿalā man yashāʾ; wa'llāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm · Am ḥasibtum an tutrakū wa lammā yaʿlami'llāhu'lladhīna jāhadū minkum wa lam yattakhidhū min dūni'llāhi wa lā rasūlihī wa la'l-muʾminīna walīja; wa'llāhu khabīrun bimā taʿmalūn*

"**And remove the rage of their hearts. God accepts the turning back of whom He wills; God is knowing, wise.** · **Or did you suppose you would be left before God had distinguished those of you who strove, and who took no confidant apart from God, His Messenger and the believers? God is aware of what you do.**"

### وَيَتُوبُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ مَن يَشَآءُ

**The close of the block must be recorded, and it is the word that gives the sūra its name.**

**The harshest block, sixteen verses long, closes with a sentence about the acceptance of a turning back.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the position of the sentence:** at the end of the block it is stated that who, on the other side, will turn back is **beyond this side's knowledge.** **Even as the ruling is being made final, that is, the possibility of stepping outside the ruling is left open.**

**The same close will be repeated in verse 27 as well, after the passage on Ḥunayn:** *thumma yatūbu'llāhu min baʿdi dhālika ʿalā man yashāʾ.* **Both blocks close with the same sentence.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

### وَلِيجَة — root: و-ل-ج

**The concrete sense of the root: to go inside a thing.** *Walaja* — he entered; *wulūj* — entering. **The root occurred in `034-sebe.md` at 34/2 (*yaʿlamu mā yaliju fi'l-arḍ*).**

**وَلِيجَة** — the sense the lexicographers give: **one slipped in between, one taken inside; a confidant, an intimate friend.** The word states someone **who is from outside and has been taken within.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the word states a **position** — "one who has been taken inside." **And the measure of the verse is not an identity but a choice of intimacy.**

**The same field will be opened again at 9/23-24, where eight ties are enumerated.**

### أَمْ حَسِبْتُمْ أَن تُتْرَكُوا۟

**This pattern was treated in detail in `029-ankebut.md` at 29/2 (*a-ḥasiba'l-nāsu an yutrakū an yaqūlū āmannā wa hum lā yuftanūn*)**, and the concept of testing was analysed there. **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**The difference here must be recorded:**

| Place | The subject of the testing |
|---|---|
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/2 | **The claim to faith** — *an yaqūlū āmannā* |
| **al-Tawba 9/16** | **Joining, and the choice of intimacy** — *jāhadū* and *walīja* |

**And the expression *lammā yaʿlam* must be recorded:** *lammā* — "has not yet…". **The verb is a verb of knowing, and this is a usage that has been debated in the Qurʾān.** In the classical commentaries it is widely reported that this means **"its coming out in act, its being distinguished"**. **I convey this explanation and do not enter the theological debate.**

## 9/17-18 — مَا كَانَ لِلْمُشْرِكِينَ أَن يَعْمُرُوا۟ مَسَٰجِدَ ٱللَّهِ شَٰهِدِينَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِم بِٱلْكُفْرِ · إِنَّمَا يَعْمُرُ مَسَٰجِدَ ٱللَّهِ مَنْ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ

*Mā kāna li'l-mushrikīna an yaʿmurū masājida'llāhi shāhidīna ʿalā anfusihim bi'l-kufr; ulāʾika ḥabiṭat aʿmāluhum wa fi'l-nāri hum khālidūn · Innamā yaʿmuru masājida'llāhi man āmana bi'llāhi wa'l-yawmi'l-ākhiri wa aqāma'l-ṣalāta wa āta'l-zakāta wa lam yakhsha illa'llāh; fa-ʿasā ulāʾika an yakūnū mina'l-muhtadīn*

"**It is not for the mushrikūn to tend the mosques of God while they bear witness against themselves to unbelief. Their works have come to nothing, and in the fire they abide.** · **Only he tends the mosques of God who believes in God and the last day, keeps up the prayer, gives the alms, and fears none but God. It may be hoped of such that they will be among those who are guided.**"

### عَمَرَ — root: ع-م-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to stay long in a place, to keep it alive and inhabited.** From the same root:

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **عُمْر** (*ʿumr*) | A lifespan — the time of remaining in a body |
| **عِمَارَة** (*ʿimāra*) | Keeping a place tended and in working order |
| **عُمْرَة** (*ʿumra*) | The visiting of a place |
| **مَعْمُور** (*maʿmūr*) | Made lively, in working order |
| **ٱسْتَعْمَرَ** (*istaʿmara*) | To ask that it be tended |

**The root was treated in `030-rum.md` at 30/9 (*wa ʿamarūhā akthara mimmā ʿamarūhā*) and in `052-tur.md` at 52/4 (*al-bayti'l-maʿmūr*).**

**At the centre of the word there is a single idea, and it is decisive for understanding the verse: *ʿimāra* is not the erecting of a building but the *keeping alive* of a place.** That it shares a root with the word for lifespan shows this: a place becomes *maʿmūr* by the time spent within it.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the semantic field of the root:** the verse speaks not of construction but of **the life of the place.** **And the eighteenth verse enumerates in four verbs what that life is** — faith, prayer, alms, and fearing God alone. **The list of "tending", that is, contains no stone and no mortar.**

### شَٰهِدِينَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِم بِٱلْكُفْرِ

**The qualification the verse lays down must be recorded, and it is important for the method.**

**The verse ties the obstacle not to an identity but to a declaration: *shāhidīna ʿalā anfusihim bi'l-kufr* — "while bearing witness against themselves to unbelief."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the presence of the circumstantial clause:** the sentence does not say "the mushrikūn cannot tend them"; it says "it is not for them to tend them **while they themselves bear witness to their own unbelief**". **The qualification is their own admission.**

### وَلَمْ يَخْشَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ

**The fourth condition of the eighteenth verse must be recorded.**

The root **خ-ش-ي**: **a fear felt before something great** — the lexicographers distinguish it from *khawf*: *khashya* is a reverent fear resting on knowledge.

**I record this as an observation:** three of the conditions are positive verbs (faith, prayer, alms); the fourth is framed **negatively** — "not to fear another." **The last item on the list of tending, that is, is not the doing of something but the giving up of something.**

### فَعَسَىٰٓ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ أَن يَكُونُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْمُهْتَدِينَ

**The close of the verse is striking and must be recorded: *ʿasā* — "it may be hoped that."**

**Even for those who fulfil the four conditions the sentence is not framed as a certainty.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle *ʿasā*:** the text declares **no certain outcome** even for someone on its own side. This will reach a much plainer form in verse 106 of the sūra (*murjawna li-amri'llāh*) and will be treated in detail there.

---

## 9/19-22 — أَجَعَلْتُمْ سِقَايَةَ ٱلْحَآجِّ وَعِمَارَةَ ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْحَرَامِ كَمَنْ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ وَجَٰهَدَ فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ

*A-jaʿaltum siqāyata'l-ḥājji wa ʿimārata'l-masjidi'l-ḥarāmi ka-man āmana bi'llāhi wa'l-yawmi'l-ākhiri wa jāhada fī sabīli'llāh; lā yastawūna ʿinda'llāh; wa'llāhu lā yahdi'l-qawma'l-ẓālimīn · Alladhīna āmanū wa hājarū wa jāhadū fī sabīli'llāhi bi-amwālihim wa anfusihim aʿẓamu darajatan ʿinda'llāh; wa ulāʾika humu'l-fāʾizūn*

"**Have you made the giving of water to the pilgrims and the tending of al-Masjid al-Ḥarām the like of one who believes in God and the last day and strives in the way of God? They are not equal with God.** · **Those who believed, and emigrated, and strove in the way of God with their wealth and their lives are greater in rank with God. It is they who attain.**"

### The point in the way the comparison is built

**There is an oddity of word order in the sentence, and the lexicographers dwell on it.**

**The two sides of the comparison are not of the same kind:**

| Side | What |
|---|---|
| The first | *Siqāya* and *ʿimāra* — **two activities** (verbal nouns) |
| The second | *Man āmana wa jāhada* — **a person** |

**An "activity", that is, is compared with a "person".** The lexicographers resolve this in two ways: either there is an implied element on the first side (*ahla siqāya* — those who do the work of giving water), or on the second (*ka-īmāni man āmana* — like the faith of one who believes).

**I convey the disagreement and impose no preference.** **And I add this as my own reading:** this **difference of kind** between the two sides is consistent with what the sentence says. On one side stand **services**, on the other **the whole of a person**. The verse compares a service with a life.

### سِقَايَة — root: س-ق-ي

**The sense of the root: to give water, to irrigate.** *Siqāya* — the work of giving water, and the place where water is given.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does **not belittle** the works ascribed to the other side — both are, after all, valuable works. What it states is an **inequality**, not an **absence**: *lā yastawūna ʿinda'llāh* — "they are not equal."

### دَرَجَة

The root **د-ر-ج**: **a step, a rank; to advance step by step.** **The word was treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/4** (*lahum darajātun ʿinda rabbihim*). **I rely on that treatment.**

## 9/23 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَا تَتَّخِذُوٓا۟ ءَابَآءَكُمْ وَإِخْوَٰنَكُمْ أَوْلِيَآءَ إِنِ ٱسْتَحَبُّوا۟ ٱلْكُفْرَ عَلَى ٱلْإِيمَٰنِ

*Yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū lā tattakhidhū ābāʾakum wa ikhwānakum awliyāʾa ini'staḥabbu'l-kufra ʿala'l-īmān; wa man yatawallahum minkum fa-ulāʾika humu'l-ẓālimūn*

"**O you who believe! Do not take your fathers and your brothers as protectors if they have preferred unbelief to faith. Whoever among you takes them as protectors — it is they who do wrong.**"

### The place of the condition

**The verse ties the prohibition to a condition: *ini'staḥabbu'l-kufra ʿala'l-īmān*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the conditional clause:** the prohibition is tied not to **kinship** but to **the kinsman's choice**. **And the word *walī* states not a closeness but an *allegiance*** — the bond of walāya was treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/72-75, and it was recorded there: **a treaty bond can take precedence over a call to solidarity.** I rely on that treatment.

### ٱسْتَحَبُّوا۟ — root: ح-ب-ب

**Form X: *istiḥbāb* — to prefer one thing to another, to find it more lovable.** **The root was treated in `049-hucurat.md` and `014-ibrahim.md`.**

**And the choice of form must be recorded:** the verb means not "they loved" but "**they preferred**" — Form X states a **wanting/choosing**. **The same root will recur in the next verse as *aḥabb* (more beloved).**

## 9/24 — قُلْ إِن كَانَ ءَابَآؤُكُمْ وَأَبْنَآؤُكُمْ وَإِخْوَٰنُكُمْ وَأَزْوَٰجُكُمْ وَعَشِيرَتُكُمْ وَأَمْوَٰلٌ ٱقْتَرَفْتُمُوهَا وَتِجَٰرَةٌ تَخْشَوْنَ كَسَادَهَا وَمَسَٰكِنُ تَرْضَوْنَهَآ أَحَبَّ إِلَيْكُم مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦ

*Qul in kāna ābāʾukum wa abnāʾukum wa ikhwānukum wa azwājukum wa ʿashīratukum wa amwālun iqtaraftumūhā wa tijāratun takhshawna kasādahā wa masākinu tarḍawnahā aḥabba ilaykum mina'llāhi wa rasūlihī wa jihādin fī sabīlihī fa-tarabbaṣū ḥattā yaʾtiya'llāhu bi-amrih; wa'llāhu lā yahdi'l-qawma'l-fāsiqīn*

"**Say: if your fathers, your sons, your brothers, your spouses, your kin, the wealth you have gained, the trade whose slackening you fear, and the dwellings you are pleased with are more beloved to you than God and His Messenger and striving in His way, then wait until God brings His command. God does not guide a people who have gone out of the way.**"

### Eight ties

**The verse enumerates eight things, and the order is not accidental. I give it in a table:**

| # | Tie | Word | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **ءَابَآؤُكُمْ** | Fathers | **Blood tie — upward** |
| **2** | **أَبْنَآؤُكُمْ** | Sons | **Blood tie — downward** |
| **3** | **إِخْوَٰنُكُمْ** | Brothers | **Blood tie — sideways** |
| **4** | **أَزْوَٰجُكُمْ** | Spouses | **The chosen tie** |
| **5** | **عَشِيرَتُكُمْ** | Clan, the wider kin | **The tie of community** |
| **6** | **أَمْوَٰلٌ ٱقْتَرَفْتُمُوهَا** | The wealth you have gained | **Property acquired** |
| **7** | **تِجَٰرَةٌ تَخْشَوْنَ كَسَادَهَا** | The trade whose slackening you fear | **Income continuing** |
| **8** | **مَسَٰكِنُ تَرْضَوْنَهَآ** | The dwellings you are pleased with | **Settledness** |

**There is an order in the sequence, and I record this as my own reading:** the list begins with **the closest blood tie**, passes to **the chosen tie**, widens to **the community**, then descends to **the material**, and ends with **settledness**. **The first five are people, that is, and the last three are things.**

**And to each of the last three items a relative clause has been attached, while none is attached to the first five:**

| Item | The note attached | What it states |
|---|---|---|
| **Amwāl** | *Iqtaraftumūhā* — **which you have gained** | That it is the return on labour |
| **Tijāra** | *Takhshawna kasādahā* — **whose slackening you fear** | **Fear** |
| **Masākin** | *Tarḍawnahā* — **which you are pleased with** | **Contentment** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the presence of the three relative clauses:** the verse does not name wealth, trade and house **on their own**; it names each of them together with **the emotional relation a person builds with it.** Beside trade stands *fear*, and beside the house *contentment*.

### ٱقْتَرَفْتُمُوهَا — root: ق-ر-ف

**The concrete sense of the root: to peel bark, to scrape a thing off its surface and gather it.** *Qirf* — the bark of a tree. From this the sense of **to gain, to acquire** derives; and the word is used in the Qurʾān both of good and of bad gain.

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the word pictures gaining as **scraping and gathering** — a verb carrying the labour of the effort within it.

### كَسَاد — root: ك-س-د

**The sense of the root: goods falling out of demand, finding no buyer, going slack.** **The word occurs in the Qurʾān only here.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse names the fear that belongs to trade **exactly where it lies** — not losing, but **coming to a standstill**. This is the real fear of someone engaged in trade.

### A comparison: Luqmān 31/15 and al-ʿAnkabūt 29/8

**This verse must be set beside the verses about parents treated in this series. I make the comparison as the task requires:**

| Place | Subject | Measure | The continuation of the relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luqmān 31/15** (`031-lokman.md`) | Parents **compelling to associate partners with God** | *Fa-lā tuṭiʿhumā* — **do not obey** | **Present:** *wa ṣāḥibhumā fi'l-dunyā maʿrūfā* — "keep good company with them" |
| **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/8** (`029-ankebut.md`) | Parents **compelling to associate partners with God** | *Fa-lā tuṭiʿhumā* — **do not obey** | **Absent** — no continuation is given |
| **al-Tawba 9/23** | Relatives **preferring unbelief** | *Lā tattakhidhūhum awliyāʾ* — **do not take as protectors** | Not stated |
| **al-Tawba 9/24** | The eight ties **being more beloved than God** | *Fa-tarabbaṣū* — **wait** | Not stated |

**The differences between the four verses must be recorded:**

**One. What is forbidden is not the same everywhere.** Luqmān and al-ʿAnkabūt forbid **obedience**; al-Tawba 23 forbids **allegiance**; al-Tawba 24 forbids nothing at all — **it lays down a measure.**

**Two. In Luqmān the continuation of the relationship is commanded openly.** This was recorded in `031-lokman.md`: *"the verse does not spread a disagreement on one matter over the whole of a relationship."* **al-Tawba does not repeat that note, but neither does it cancel it.**

**Three. The sentence of al-Tawba 9/24 is not a sentence of prohibition.** *Fa-tarabbaṣū ḥattā yaʾtiya'llāhu bi-amrih* — "wait." **The verse tells the one addressed not to do something but to await an outcome.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verb itself (*tarabbaṣa* — to wait):** the verse sets up a **scale**; it issues no command to **sever**. What is measured is not the existence of the ties but **their place in the order**: *aḥabba ilaykum* — "more beloved to you."

**And the measure recorded in `008-enfal.md` at 8/28 holds here too:** *innamā amwālukum wa awlādukum fitna* — wealth and children are **not a gain but a trial**. The list in al-Tawba is that sentence in detail.

## 9/25-27 — وَيَوْمَ حُنَيْنٍ إِذْ أَعْجَبَتْكُمْ كَثْرَتُكُمْ فَلَمْ تُغْنِ عَنكُمْ شَيْـًٔا

*Laqad naṣarakumu'llāhu fī mawāṭina kathīratin wa yawma ḥunaynin idh aʿjabatkum kathratukum fa-lam tughni ʿankum shayʾan wa ḍāqat ʿalaykumu'l-arḍu bimā raḥubat thumma wallaytum mudbirīn · Thumma anzala'llāhu sakīnatahū ʿalā rasūlihī wa ʿala'l-muʾminīna wa anzala junūdan lam tarawhā wa ʿadhdhaba'lladhīna kafarū; wa dhālika jazāʾu'l-kāfirīn · Thumma yatūbu'llāhu min baʿdi dhālika ʿalā man yashāʾ; wa'llāhu ghafūrun raḥīm*

"**God has helped you in many places, and on the day of Ḥunayn too: when your numbers pleased you, and were of no use to you at all; and the earth, for all its breadth, closed in on you; and then you turned your backs and fled.** · **Then God sent down His sakīna upon His Messenger and upon the believers, and sent down armies you did not see, and punished those who rejected. That is the recompense of those who reject.** · **Then, after that, God accepts the turning back of whom He wills. God is forgiving, merciful.**"

### إِذْ أَعْجَبَتْكُمْ كَثْرَتُكُمْ

**This sentence is the sūra's plainest criticism of its own side, and it must be recorded.**

The root **ع-ج-ب**: **to astonish, to strike with wonder.** *Aʿjaba* — it pleased him, it won his admiration. **What the form states here is a *self-satisfaction*.**

**And the same verb will occur three more times in the sūra — and in all three as something forbidden:**

| Verse | Expression | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **25** | *Aʿjabatkum kathratukum* | **The community addressed** — numbers |
| **55** | *Fa-lā tuʿjibka amwāluhum wa awlāduhum* | The hypocrites — wealth and children |
| **85** | *Wa lā tuʿjibka amwāluhum wa awlāduhum* | The same expression again |

**This set of three is verifiable from the text.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra uses the same verb first to relate **the error of its own side**, then to regulate **how one looks at the wealth of another**. Being pleased, that is, is bounded both inwardly and outwardly.

### The line that joins with al-Anfāl

**This verse joins directly with two places treated in `008-enfal.md`, and I join them as the task requires:**

| Place | What is said | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Anfāl 8/26** | *Idh antum qalīlun mustaḍʿafūna fi'l-arḍ* — **you were few, and counted weak** | *Fa-āwākum wa ayyadakum bi-naṣrih* — **shelter and support** |
| **al-Anfāl 8/45-47** | *Wa lā tanāzaʿū fa-tafshalū wa tadhhaba rīḥukum* — **quarrelling → slackening → the going of your strength** | The command to endure |
| **al-Tawba 9/25** | *Aʿjabatkum **kathratukum*** — **your numbers pleased you** | *Fa-lam tughni ʿankum shayʾan* — **they were of no use at all** |

**What emerges when the three verses are read together is this, and I record it as my own reading:** al-Anfāl says that being few is no obstacle; al-Tawba says that being many is not enough. **Number, that is, is not counted as the cause of the outcome in either direction.** The measure recorded in `008-enfal.md` at 8/17 (*fa-lam taqtulūhum wa lākinna'llāha qatalahum*) is the head of this line: **the outcome is tied not to a human act but to somewhere else.**

**And its link to the chain in al-Anfāl 8/45-47 must be recorded as well:** there the going of strength was tied to **quarrelling**; here the uselessness of strength is tied to **self-admiration.** Two separate causes, the same outcome.

### وَضَاقَتْ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْأَرْضُ بِمَا رَحُبَتْ

**This expression will be repeated almost word for word in verse 118 of the sūra and will be treated in detail there. Here I record only the repetition:**

| Verse | Expression | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **25** | *Wa ḍāqat ʿalaykumu'l-arḍu bimā raḥubat* | **Those who fled** — in the moment of battle |
| **118** | *Ḥattā idhā ḍāqat ʿalayhimu'l-arḍu bimā raḥubat **wa ḍāqat ʿalayhim anfusuhum*** | **The three who were left behind** — in the moment of waiting |

**This repetition is verifiable from the text and is one of the strongest internal weaves of the sūra.** **In the hundred and eighteenth verse one further clause is added**, and it will be treated there.

### فَأَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُۥ

**The root س-ك-ن and the word *sakīna* were analysed in detail in `048-fetih.md` at 48/4** — the pattern (*faʿīla*), the semantic field (*maskan*, *miskīn*, *sakīna*), and the description "the ceasing of inner agitation" were all given there. **I do not repeat it here and rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is this, and it is a datum of the text:** the word occurs in **six places** in the Qurʾān, and **two of them are in this sūra** (26 and 40). **The other four: al-Baqara 2/248 and al-Fatḥ 48/4, 48/18, 48/26.**

| Place | On whom it descends | At what moment |
|---|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/248 | On a community | When a sign was asked for |
| al-Fatḥ 48/4 | Into the **hearts** of the believers | In the setting of the pledge |
| al-Fatḥ 48/18 | **Upon** those who pledged | After the hearts were known |
| al-Fatḥ 48/26 | On the Messenger and the believers | While there was *ḥamiyya* on the other side |
| **al-Tawba 9/26** | On the Messenger and the believers | **After a rout** |
| **al-Tawba 9/40** | **On a single person alone** | **In the cave, when they were two** |

**The measure recorded in `048-fetih.md` is confirmed here too:** *sakīna* **descends every time at a moment of tension.** **And what al-Tawba adds must be recorded:** in verse 26 the sakīna descends **not after a success but after a collapse.** **This is the most adverse ground in the series.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the verse:** *thumma wallaytum mudbirīn* (you turned your backs) — *thumma anzala'llāhu sakīnatahū*. **Between the two sentences no correction, no turning back, no return is related.** The sakīna is placed immediately behind the flight.

### ثُمَّ يَتُوبُ ٱللَّهُ … عَلَىٰ مَن يَشَآءُ

**The twenty-seventh verse repeats the same sentence as the fifteenth.** **Both blocks carry the same close**, and this is a weave verifiable within the sūra.

3. **No legal ruling will be issued; the note "it is not binding" will be entered.**

## 9/28 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِنَّمَا ٱلْمُشْرِكُونَ نَجَسٌ فَلَا يَقْرَبُوا۟ ٱلْمَسْجِدَ ٱلْحَرَامَ بَعْدَ عَامِهِمْ هَٰذَا

*Yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū innama'l-mushrikūna najasun fa-lā yaqrabu'l-masjida'l-ḥarāma baʿda ʿāmihim hādhā; wa in khiftum ʿaylatan fa-sawfa yughnīkumu'llāhu min faḍlihī in shāʾ; inna'llāha ʿalīmun ḥakīm*

"**O you who believe! The mushrikūn are but a *najas*; so let them not approach al-Masjid al-Ḥarām after this year of theirs. And if you fear poverty, God will enrich you out of His bounty if He wills. God is knowing, wise.**"

### نَجَس — root: ن-ج-س

**The word occurs in the Qurʾān only here.**

**The lexical sense of the root: uncleanness, filth, not being pure.** The lexicographers record that the word is used both of **material dirt** and of **immaterial dirt**; *najāsa* states material filth, while *najas* states a more general state of uncleanness.

**And the pattern of the word must be dwelt on: *najas* is a *verbal noun*.** In Arabic, making a verbal noun the predicate states **intensification**: rather than "they are unclean", a construction like "**they are uncleanness itself**" is built. This is a point the lexicographers record for this verse.

### How it was understood in the classical commentaries — a table of disagreement

**What the expression means has been debated in the classical commentaries. I convey the disagreement in a table. I impose no preference and issue no legal ruling:**

| View | What *najas* means | Cited as ground |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Uncleanness of belief** — a state not of the body but of belief | The immaterial use of the word; the fact that the verse's context is a regulation of entry to a place |
| **B** | **Legal uncleanness** — a ruling laid down in respect of entering a particular place | That the verse continues with *fa-lā yaqrabū* |
| **C** | **Material uncleanness** | The primary lexical sense of the word |
| **D** | **Intensification** — an attribute named in its extreme expression | That the word *najas* is a verbal noun |

**I adopt no preference among these views.** What is to be recorded is this: **the majority of the views have not read the expression as a bodily quality**; but this is a note, not a tally, and it carries no claim of certainty.

### What is to be recorded per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**One. The verse issues no wholesale ruling about a group; it makes a regulation about entry to a particular place.** The ground for this in the wording is the verse itself: the sentence that carries the ruling is *fa-lā yaqrabu'l-**masjida'l-ḥarām*** — the subject, that is, is **a place**, not the general status of persons. And there is a note of time as well: *baʿda ʿāmihim hādhā* — "**after this year of theirs**."

**Two. The "Prohibitions" clause of `STYLE.md` bears directly here:** *"No wholesale ruling is issued about an ethnic or religious group; what the verse describes are attributes."* **In this commentary the verse is read as a regulation speaking about an attribute; no judgement is issued about any community.**

**Three. The legal rulings drawn from this verse have been debated between the schools** — which places it covers, whom it covers, whether it is permanent or temporary. **This commentary does not enter that debate, issues no ruling, and counts no view as binding.**

**Four. No present-day controversy is entered into.**

### وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ عَيْلَةً

**The second half of the verse must be recorded, and it is often passed over.**

The root **ع-ي-ل**: **poverty, straitened livelihood, the burden of providing for a large family.** *ʿAyla* — poverty. From the same root *ʿiyāl* (the household one is bound to provide for).

**I record this as an observation:** the verse foresees the **economic consequence** of the regulation and **names by its name** the fear of the one addressed. As the ruling is laid down, that is, the anxiety it will produce is not passed over in silence.

**The same structure was treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/62:** after the command to incline to peace, the anxiety was met by a separate sentence. **I rely on that treatment and add this as my own reading:** the Qurʾān meets an anxiety arising after a command not **by cancelling the command** but **by a separate sentence**. al-Tawba 9/28 is an instance of this.

## 9/29 — قَٰتِلُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱللَّهِ وَلَا بِٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ … حَتَّىٰ يُعْطُوا۟ ٱلْجِزْيَةَ عَن يَدٍ وَهُمْ صَٰغِرُونَ

*Qātilu'lladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi'llāhi wa lā bi'l-yawmi'l-ākhiri wa lā yuḥarrimūna mā ḥarrama'llāhu wa rasūluhū wa lā yadīnūna dīna'l-ḥaqqi mina'lladhīna ūtu'l-kitāba ḥattā yuʿṭu'l-jizyata ʿan yadin wa hum ṣāghirūn*

"**Fight those, from among those who were given the Book, who do not believe in God nor in the last day, who do not hold forbidden what God and His Messenger have forbidden, and who do not take the religion of truth as their religion — until they give the jizya *ʿan yad* and are *ṣāghirūn*.**"

---

**In respect of the method this is the most sensitive verse of the sūra. I apply here, to the letter, the five-point procedure I wrote out above.**

---

### The first question: who is addressed — the wording of the verse itself

**There is a particle *min* in the sentence, and it is decisive.**

**مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ** — *mina'lladhīna ūtu'l-kitāb*: "from among those who were given the Book."

**The function of this particle has been debated in the classical commentaries, and there are two readings. I convey the disagreement and impose no preference:**

| Reading | What *min* states | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Partitive** | **A part out of a whole** | The ones addressed are the section **from among** the People of the Book who carry the three attributes |
| **2. Explicative** | **Explanation** | The ones addressed are the whole of the People of the Book |

**In `048-fetih.md` at 48/29 the same grammatical question (whether the particle *minhum* is partitive or explicative) was given in a table and no preference was imposed. I keep the same posture here.**

**And I record this as an observation about the structure:** in the sentence the one addressed is described **by three negative relative clauses** — not believing, not holding forbidden, not taking the religion of truth as one's religion. **The description, that is, is built not by the name of a group but by a sequence of attributes.** The name of the group (*alladhīna ūtu'l-kitāb*) comes at the **end** of the sentence, and with a *min*.

### The second question: the lexical senses of the words

#### جِزْيَة — root: ج-ز-ي

**The sense of the root: to give the return for a thing, to pay its price, to suffice.** From the same root:

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **جَزَاء** (*jazāʾ*) | A return — for good and for bad alike |
| **جَزَىٰ** (*jazā*) | He gave the return; it sufficed, it was enough |
| **جِزْيَة** (*jizya*) | The return paid, the price |
| **تَجْزِى** | "It suffices" — *lā tajzī nafsun ʿan nafsin shayʾā* |

**The root was treated in `056-vakia.md`, `076-insan.md`, `092-leyl.md` and `053-necm.md`.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the word carries in its root an idea of **return** — not punishment but **a price**. The English word usually reached for here, "penalty", does not reflect the word's semantic field in Arabic.

**The amount of the jizya, from whom it is to be taken, who is exempt, and what is provided in return are not stated in the Qurʾān.** All of these have been debated in the legal literature, and there are divergences between the schools. **This commentary does not enter that debate and issues no ruling.**

#### عَن يَدٍ

**This expression occurs in the Qurʾān only here, and its meaning has been debated in the classical commentaries. I convey the disagreement in a table:**

| View | What *ʿan yad* means | Cited as ground |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **From hand to hand, in person** — payment without an intermediary | The concrete sense of the word *yad* |
| **B** | **In a state of power and superiority** — the sense of *yad* as "power" | That *yad* states might in Arabic |
| **C** | **In cash, down** | Its use in the language of trade |
| **D** | **In return for a benefit** — in return for the safety granted them | The sense of *yad* as "benefit, favour" |
| **E** | **In a state of obedience, without resistance** | The context |

**All five views are mentioned in the sources. I adopt no preference.**

**And one further note:** that the word *yad* carries so many senses in Arabic is the reason the expression **cannot be fixed in a single determinate meaning**. I give this as an observation about the language.

#### صَٰغِرُونَ — root: ص-غ-ر

**The sense of the root: to be small.** *Ṣaghīr* — small. **The root was treated in `027-neml.md` at 27/37**, and it was recorded there: *"Ṣāghir — one brought low not by his own will but of necessity."* **I rely on that treatment.**

**And two occurrences can be set side by side — this is a datum of the text:**

| Place | Expression | Context |
|---|---|---|
| al-Naml 27/37 | *Wa la-nukhrijannahum minhā adhillatan **wa hum ṣāghirūn*** | The reply sent by a ruler |
| **al-Tawba 9/29** | *Ḥattā yuʿṭu'l-jizyata ʿan yadin **wa hum ṣāghirūn*** | The paying of an obligation |

**The measure recorded in `027-neml.md` should be mentioned here too:** there the threat **was not carried out**, because the door left open had been used. This is recorded not in order to establish a link of ruling between the two verses but in order to show the word's use in the Qurʾān.

**What the expression states in this verse has been debated in the classical commentaries:**

| View | What *wa hum ṣāghirūn* states |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Legal subjection** — having come under the rule of an order |
| **B** | **The end of resistance** — the ending of a state of war |
| **C** | **The bearing at the moment of payment** — a manner of transaction |

**I adopt no preference.** **And I record this separately:** the forms of practice drawn from view C have been debated in the legal literature, and some scholars have rejected them. **That debate too is not entered into.**

### The third question: the frame of the verse — what is to be recorded per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**One. The verse regulates a state of war and lays down a point at which it ends.**

**The structure of the sentence is this: *qātilū … ḥattā yuʿṭu'l-jizyata*.** ***Ḥattā*** is a **particle of purpose**, and it says **where the verb ends.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle *ḥattā*:** the verse writes into its own sentence that the fighting is **not endless.** **The same structure occurred twice in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/4** (*ḥattā idhā athkhantumūhum*, *ḥattā taḍaʿa'l-ḥarbu awzārahā*), and it was recorded there: *"the text says where what it permits ends, at the very place where it permits it."* **I rely on that treatment.**

**Two. The verse issues no wholesale ruling about a religious group.**

The grounds for this in the wording are as follows, and all of them are within the text:
- The one addressed is described **by three attributes**, not by an identity.
- There is the particle **مِنَ**, and on at least one reading it states a partitive.
- The same Qurʾān separately praises a section from among the People of the Book (Āl ʿImrān 3/113-115) — I only refer to those verses; those sūras have not yet been treated in this series.

**The relevant clause of `STYLE.md` is applied here directly:** *"No wholesale ruling is issued about an ethnic or religious group; what the verse describes are attributes, and whoever carries the attribute is included in it."* **In this commentary no ruling about any community is drawn from this verse.**

**Three. No legal ruling is issued, and no view conveyed is binding.**

The rulings drawn from this verse — from whom the jizya is to be taken, its amount, its exemptions, the security provided in return, and whether the regulation is bound to historical conditions — **have been debated at length in the legal and political literature, and there are divergences between the schools.** In this commentary **the existence of the disagreement is recorded, no view is preferred, and no ruling is issued. None of what is conveyed is binding.**

**Four. The verse regulates a determinate historical situation.**

The ground for this is the sūra's own notes of time: the preceding verse says *baʿda ʿāmihim hādhā* (after this year of theirs); the following block is built around a particular expedition and a particular season. **This does not mean that the meaning of the verse is imprisoned in history; but that the verse is a *regulation of a situation* can be read from the text's own notes.**

**Five. No link whatever is made to present-day politics, present-day conflicts or their parties.** This verse is ascribed to no state, no community and no event of the present day.

## 9/30 — وَقَالَتِ ٱلْيَهُودُ عُزَيْرٌ ٱبْنُ ٱللَّهِ وَقَالَتِ ٱلنَّصَٰرَى ٱلْمَسِيحُ ٱبْنُ ٱللَّهِ ۖ ذَٰلِكَ قَوْلُهُم بِأَفْوَٰهِهِمْ

*Wa qālati'l-yahūdu ʿUzayru'bnu'llāh wa qālati'l-naṣāra'l-masīḥu'bnu'llāh; dhālika qawluhum bi-afwāhihim; yuḍāhiʾūna qawla'lladhīna kafarū min qabl; qātalahumu'llāh; annā yuʾfakūn*

"**The Jews said, 'ʿUzayr is the son of God'; and the Christians said, 'The Messiah is the son of God.' That is their word with their mouths; they make it resemble the word of those who rejected before. May God confound them — how they are turned away!**"

### A limit per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**This verse and what follows it are about claims ascribed to two religious communities. The measure applied in this commentary is as follows, and I write it out plainly:**

**What is criticised is a *word*, not a *community*.** The wording of the verse itself says so: ***dhālika qawluhum* — "that is their word."** The subject is whether a sentence is true.

**In this commentary no ruling is issued about any religious community, and no quality is ascribed to any community. The relevant clause of `STYLE.md` operates here too.**

**And one further note:** as to the historical setting in which the word about *ʿUzayr* mentioned in the verse was held, and how widespread it was, there are differing assessments in the classical and modern sources. **This commentary does not enter that question and advances no historical claim.** The wording of the verse records a word; this commentary conveys that record.

### يُضَٰهِـُٔونَ — root: ض-ه-أ

**The sense of the root: to resemble, to make like.** *Muḍāhāt* — making one thing resemble another, fitting it to it.

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the verse names the claim as an **imitation** — not a word issuing from themselves but a word **made to resemble the word of another.**

**And the expression *bi-afwāhihim* must be recorded.** This is the second link of the set of three treated at verse 8: **8, 30, 32.** In all three the word is ascribed to **the mouth** — that is, its source is not shown; what is recorded is that **it is being said**.

## 9/31 — ٱتَّخَذُوٓا۟ أَحْبَارَهُمْ وَرُهْبَٰنَهُمْ أَرْبَابًا مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْمَسِيحَ ٱبْنَ مَرْيَمَ

*Ittakhadhū aḥbārahum wa ruhbānahum arbāban min dūni'llāhi wa'l-masīḥa'bna Maryam; wa mā umirū illā li-yaʿbudū ilāhan wāḥidā; lā ilāha illā hū; subḥānahū ʿammā yushrikūn*

"**They took their rabbis and their monks as lords apart from God, and the Messiah, son of Mary (Maryam). Yet they were commanded only to serve one God. There is no god but He; He is far above what they associate with Him.**"

### أَحْبَار and رُهْبَان

**أَحْبَار** — the singular is *ḥabr* or *ḥibr*. **The root ح-ب-ر: to make beautiful, to adorn; and ink** (*ḥibr*). The lexicographers record that the word is used of **a scholar of religion**, and that it is said to come from its connection with writing and ink. **I do not give this derivation as certain.**

**رُهْبَان** — root **ر-ه-ب**: **fear, wariness.** *Rahba* — fear mixed with reverence. *Rāhib* — one who fears; the word's sense of **one who withdraws from the world and shuts himself up in worship** comes from here.

### The classical explanation of the verse

**What "taking as lords" means has been explained in the classical commentaries, and that explanation must be recorded.**

**The explanation widely transmitted is this:** the "taking as lords" here is not prostrating to them or believing them to be gods. **What is meant is the giving over to them of *the authority to determine the lawful and the forbidden*** — that is, accepting as lawful what they count lawful, and as forbidden what they count forbidden.

**This explanation is widespread in the classical exegetical tradition. It is also reported to rest on a hadith report; since I cannot give the wording and the source of that report with certainty, I do not transmit it.** Per the method (`STYLE.md`): **I do not ascribe to a source a saying of which I am not certain.**

**What can be verified from the wording of the verse itself is this, and I record it as my own reading:** the continuation of the sentence is ***wa mā umirū illā li-yaʿbudū ilāhan wāḥidā*** — "yet they were commanded only **to serve** one God." **The verse, that is, names what was done within the field of *service*.** And the scope of the word *ʿibāda* in the Qurʾān is not confined to prostration.

**One further limit, per the method (`STYLE.md`):** this verse cannot be read as a verse condemning **every scholar of religion or every religious authority**. What the verse describes is an **act**: the displacing of authority. **Whoever performs that act falls under the description; this issues no wholesale ruling about any community.** I record this on the basis of the relevant clause of `STYLE.md`.

## 9/32-33 — يُرِيدُونَ أَن يُطْفِـُٔوا۟ نُورَ ٱللَّهِ بِأَفْوَٰهِهِمْ وَيَأْبَى ٱللَّهُ إِلَّآ أَن يُتِمَّ نُورَهُۥ

*Yurīdūna an yuṭfiʾū nūra'llāhi bi-afwāhihim wa yaʾba'llāhu illā an yutimma nūrahū wa law kariha'l-kāfirūn · Huwa'lladhī arsala rasūlahū bi'l-hudā wa dīni'l-ḥaqqi li-yuẓhirahū ʿala'l-dīni kullihī wa law kariha'l-mushrikūn*

"**They want to put out the light of God with their mouths, while God will have nothing but the completing of His light — however much those who reject may hate it.** · **It is He who sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth, to make it prevail over all religion — however much the mushrikūn may hate it.**"

### Putting out with the mouth

**This is the third link of the set of three (8, 30, 32), and the picture is completed here.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the repetition of *afwāh* in the three verses:** the sūra relates the **ineffectiveness** of the word by ascribing it to the mouth. **In the thirty-second verse this turns into an image: light is not put out with a mouth.** Blowing is enough for a candle; not for a light.

The root **ط-ف-أ**: **to go out, to put out.** The word occurs rarely in the Qurʾān.

### وَيَأْبَى ٱللَّهُ إِلَّآ أَن يُتِمَّ نُورَهُۥ

The root **أ-ب-ي**: **not to accept, to refuse, to resist.** **The same verb occurred in verse 8:** *wa **taʾbā** qulūbuhum* — "their hearts refuse."

**This repetition must be recorded, and it is verifiable from the text:**

| Verse | Who refuses | What |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *Taʾbā **qulūbuhum*** | What they say with their mouths |
| **32** | *Wa yaʾba'**llāhu*** | That His light should not be completed |

**I record this as my own reading:** the same verb is given to two separate subjects within the sūra — and the second use stands like the answer to the first.

**And the construction of the sentence must be recorded:** *yaʾbā … **illā** an yutimma* — a negative verb, followed by an exception. In Arabic this structure states **restriction** (*qaṣr*) and produces the strongest affirmation: "He will accept **nothing but** the completing of it."

### لِيُظْهِرَهُۥ عَلَى ٱلدِّينِ كُلِّهِ

**What this expression means has been debated in the classical commentaries. I convey the disagreement and impose no preference:**

| View | What *iẓhār* means | Cited as ground |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **To make prevail in respect of proof and argument** | The sense of the word *ẓāhir* as "plain, evident" |
| **B** | **An actual spread** | The sense of the word as "to get the upper hand" |
| **C** | A state realised in a determinate region | A historical reading |
| **D** | A statement referring, in respect of time, to the future | That the verb is in the form of purpose |

**No preference is adopted in this commentary. And per the method (`STYLE.md`): no present-day political or social conclusions are drawn from this verse.**

## 9/34-35 — وَٱلَّذِينَ يَكْنِزُونَ ٱلذَّهَبَ وَٱلْفِضَّةَ وَلَا يُنفِقُونَهَا فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ

*Yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū inna kathīran mina'l-aḥbāri wa'l-ruhbāni la-yaʾkulūna amwāla'l-nāsi bi'l-bāṭili wa yaṣuddūna ʿan sabīli'llāh; wa'lladhīna yaknizūna'l-dhahaba wa'l-fiḍḍata wa lā yunfiqūnahā fī sabīli'llāhi fa-bashshirhum bi-ʿadhābin alīm · Yawma yuḥmā ʿalayhā fī nāri jahannama fa-tukwā bihā jibāhuhum wa junūbuhum wa ẓuhūruhum; hādhā mā kanaztum li-anfusikum fa-dhūqū mā kuntum taknizūn*

"**O you who believe! Many of the rabbis and the monks devour people's wealth by false means and turn others from the way of God. And those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in the way of God — give them the news of a painful punishment.** · **On the day when these are heated in the fire of Gehenna and their foreheads, their sides and their backs are branded with them: 'This is what you hoarded for yourselves; taste, then, what you were hoarding!'**"

### How the two sentences are joined

**The word order must be recorded, and it is often missed.**

**The verse begins with "many of the rabbis and the monks"; then it continues with *wa'lladhīna yaknizūn*** — and the subject of this second sentence is **left unspecified**, tied to no group.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the relative pronoun comes without restriction:** the ruling on hoarding is tied not **to a group** but **to an act.** The sentence does not say "those of them who hoard"; it says "**those who hoard**".

**And the qualification *inna kathīran min* must also be recorded separately:** *kathīran min* — "many of." **Even the first sentence, that is, does not cover the whole.** Per the method (`STYLE.md`): **no wholesale ruling is issued about any community, and the wording of the verse does not do so in the first place.**

### كَنَزَ — root: ك-ن-ز

**The word is analysed here for the first time in this series.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to press a thing together and gather it, to heap it up.** The lexicographers record that *kanz* properly means **"property buried in the ground and hidden"**; and that the verb is also used of **filling a vessel and pressing it down.**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **كَنْز** (*kanz*) | Buried treasure; heaped-up property |
| **كَنَزَ** (*kanaza*) | He heaped up, pressed together, buried |
| **مَكْنُوز** | Tightly packed |

**The word occurred in `018-kehf.md` at 18/82** (*wa kāna taḥtahū kanzun lahumā*) — there it was **property kept for two orphans**. **The two uses can be set side by side, and I give this as my own reading:**

| Place | *Kanz* | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| al-Kahf 18/82 | **Kept for another** | A work whose preservation was commanded |
| **al-Tawba 9/34** | *Yaknizūn* + *wa lā yunfiqūnahā* | **A warning** |

**The difference lies not in the property itself but in whom it is held for.** In al-Kahf the property is kept for another; in al-Tawba it says *li-anfusikum* (for yourselves) — and indeed the word appears openly in verse 35.

### The line that joins with al-Humaza and al-Ḥadīd

**The hoarding of wealth was analysed in detail in `104-humeze.md`, and I rely on what was recorded there.** What was analysed there was this: *alladhī jamaʿa mālan wa ʿaddadah* — **to gather and to count**; and *yaḥsabu anna mālahū akhladah* — **the assumption of permanence** underlying accumulation. And this was recorded there: *"The verse says neither 'rich' nor gives any amount. What it describes is a chain of behaviour."*

**The same measure holds for al-Tawba 9/34, and the wording of the verse supports it:**

| Sūra | Verb | The note attached |
|---|---|---|
| al-Humaza 104/2 | *Jamaʿa* (he gathered) + *ʿaddada* (he counted) | None — the verbs themselves suffice |
| **al-Tawba 9/34** | *Yaknizūn* (they hoard) | ***Wa lā yunfiqūnahā fī sabīli'llāh*** — **they do not spend** |

**This difference must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** al-Tawba does not censure accumulation **on its own**; it adds a **negative** beside it. The subject of the warning is not the heaping itself but **the heaping taking the place of the spending.**

**The measure recorded in `057-hadid.md` at 57/7 is the ground of this place, and I rely on it:** *anfiqū mimmā jaʿalakum mustakhlafīna fīh* — "spend out of that **in which He has made you successors**." It was recorded there: *"that ownership is a trust is the ground of spending."* **al-Tawba 9/34 is the negative face of the same principle: a trust, when it is not spent, turns into a heap.**

**And in `057-hadid.md` the difference between spending and the alms was recorded** (the alms a fixed proportion, spending general). **The legal scope of this verse — whether property on which the alms have been paid counts as "kanz" — has been debated in the classical sources.** I convey the disagreement, and **issue no ruling and count no view as binding:**

| View | What *kanz* is |
|---|---|
| **A** | Property whose due (the alms) has not been paid |
| **B** | Property heaped up and not spent — whatever its amount |
| **C** | A warning belonging to a determinate historical situation |

### يَوْمَ يُحْمَىٰ عَلَيْهَا … فَتُكْوَىٰ بِهَا جِبَاهُهُمْ وَجُنُوبُهُمْ وَظُهُورُهُمْ

**Three parts of the body are enumerated, and the order must be recorded: forehead, side, back.**

The root **ك-و-ي**: **to brand** — to burn with heated iron. This is a concrete operation used in the branding of animals and in old medicine.

**I record this as an observation:** the very metal that was hoarded is **heated and turned into an instrument of branding.** **The punishment, that is, is of the same kind as the thing hoarded.**

**And explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries about the choice of these three parts** (the turning away of the face, the sidling off, the turning of the back). **I do not present these explanations as certain knowledge; the wording of the verse enumerates three parts and gives no reason.**

**A note on the word order:** all three parts are **outward-facing** surfaces of the body — the forehead looks forward, the sides to either hand, the back behind. **I give this as my own reading:** the enumeration covers every direction of the body.

### هَٰذَا مَا كَنَزْتُمْ لِأَنفُسِكُمْ

**The clause must be recorded: *li-anfusikum* — "for yourselves."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this word:** the verse sets before them the **intention** behind the hoarding — and that intention is counted as having been fulfilled. "You hoarded for yourselves; here it is before you." **This comes out at the same place as the diagnosis *yaḥsabu anna mālahū akhladah* recorded in `104-humeze.md`:** wealth secures its owner no permanence; it stays with its owner.

## 9/36 — إِنَّ عِدَّةَ ٱلشُّهُورِ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱثْنَا عَشَرَ شَهْرًا فِى كِتَٰبِ ٱللَّهِ يَوْمَ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ مِنْهَآ أَرْبَعَةٌ حُرُمٌ

*Inna ʿiddata'l-shuhūri ʿinda'llāhi'thnā ʿashara shahran fī kitābi'llāhi yawma khalaqa'l-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa minhā arbaʿatun ḥurum; dhālika'l-dīnu'l-qayyim; fa-lā taẓlimū fīhinna anfusakum; wa qātilu'l-mushrikīna kāffatan kamā yuqātilūnakum kāffa; wa'ʿlamū anna'llāha maʿa'l-muttaqīn*

"**The number of the months with God is twelve, in the book of God, from the day He created the heavens and the earth; four of them are sacred. That is the upright religion. So do not wrong yourselves in them. And fight the mushrikūn all together, as they fight you all together. And know that God is with those who guard themselves.**"

### عِدَّة — root: ع-د-د

**The sense of the root: to count.** **The root was treated in detail in `104-humeze.md` at 104/2 (*ʿaddadah*)**, and three senses (to count / to make ready / to diversify) were tabulated there. **I rely on that treatment.**

***ʿIdda* here: number, count.**

### فِى كِتَٰبِ ٱللَّهِ يَوْمَ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ

**The note of time must be recorded, and I give it as an observation:** the verse ties the number of the months not to **a custom** or **an agreement** but to **the creation.** **The source of the number, that is, is not counted as a human arrangement.**

**And there is a concrete counterpart to this:** the lunar calendar rests on the phases of the moon, and twelve cycles come close to a solar year. **This is an observation; it is not a scientific claim drawn from the verse.** Per the method (`STYLE.md`): **no hunting for scientific miracles is done. What the verse states is the source of the number; not an astronomical detail.**

### مِنْهَآ أَرْبَعَةٌ حُرُمٌ

**The verse does not say which the four months are.**

**In the classical sources it is widely reported that they are Dhu'l-Qaʿda, Dhu'l-Ḥijja, Muḥarram and Rajab; it is also related that three are consecutive and one stands apart. This is information from transmitted report and is not in the Qurʾān; I do not present it as certain knowledge.**

The root **ح-ر-م**: **to make inviolable, to draw a boundary.** *Ḥaram* — an inviolable area; *iḥrām* — entering the state of inviolability; *maḥram* — one it is forbidden to approach. **The root was treated in `002-bakara.md` and `022-hac.md`.**

**At the centre of the root there is a single idea: a boundary drawn around something.** And the sacred months are **a boundary drawn around time** — not around space.

### فَلَا تَظْلِمُوا۟ فِيهِنَّ أَنفُسَكُمْ

**To what the pronoun refers has been debated in the classical commentaries:**

| View | What *fīhinna* refers to |
|---|---|
| **A** | **The four sacred months** |
| **B** | **All twelve months** |

**I convey the disagreement and impose no preference.**

**And the object of the verb must be recorded: *anfusakum* — "yourselves."** The prohibition is framed not as wronging another but as **wronging oneself**. **This pattern recurs in the Qurʾān and was treated in `035-fatir.md` at 35/32 (*ẓālimun li-nafsih*).**

### كَمَا يُقَٰتِلُونَكُمْ كَآفَّةً

**In the last sentence of the verse there is a note of reciprocity, and it must be recorded.**

***Kamā*** — "as…". **The command, that is, is framed as equal to the act of the other side.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle *kamā*:** the sentence carries a **measure**. **And this comes from the same place as the principle of equivalence treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/194 (*fa-mani'ʿtadā ʿalaykum fa'ʿtadū ʿalayhi bi-mithli mā'ʿtadā ʿalaykum*).**

The root **ك-ف-ف**: **the palm of the hand; and to gather a thing and enclose it.** *Kāffa* — **all together, all at once.** The word will occur again in verse 122 of the sūra, and there it will carry **the exact opposite** ruling: *wa mā kāna'l-muʾminūna li-yanfirū **kāffa*** — "the believers were not to go out all together."

**This repetition is verifiable from the text and will be compared at verse 122.**

## 9/37 — إِنَّمَا ٱلنَّسِىٓءُ زِيَادَةٌ فِى ٱلْكُفْرِ

*Innama'l-nasīʾu ziyādatun fi'l-kufri yuḍallu bihi'lladhīna kafarū yuḥillūnahū ʿāman wa yuḥarrimūnahū ʿāman li-yuwāṭiʾū ʿiddata mā ḥarrama'llāhu fa-yuḥillū mā ḥarrama'llāh; zuyyina lahum sūʾu aʿmālihim; wa'llāhu lā yahdi'l-qawma'l-kāfirīn*

"***Nasīʾ* is an increase in unbelief; by it those who reject are led astray. They make it lawful one year and forbidden another — so as to match the number of what God has made forbidden, and thus make lawful what God has forbidden. Their evil deeds have been made to seem fair to them. God does not guide a rejecting people.**"

### نَسِىٓء — root: ن-س-أ

**The concrete sense of the root: to put a thing off, to leave it to later, to extend its term.** From the same root:

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **نَسَأَ** (*nasaʾa*) | He postponed, he delayed |
| **نَسِيئَة** (*nasīʾa*) | **A sale on credit** — one whose price is left to later |
| **نَسِىٓء** (*nasīʾ*) | Postponement; the thing postponed |
| **مُنْسَأَة** (*minsaʾa*) | A support, a staff — it occurred in `034-sebe.md` at 34/14 |

**The root was treated in `034-sebe.md`; I rely on that treatment.**

**The pattern of the word must be dwelt on:** the *faʿīl* pattern can carry both the sense of an **active participle** (one who postpones) and of a **passive participle** (one that is postponed). The lexicographers record both readings here.

### What the practice was — in the language of "it is reported"

**The Qurʾān does not relate in detail what this practice was. What can be read directly from the verse is this:**

- They were counting something **lawful one year and forbidden another** (*yuḥillūnahū ʿāman wa yuḥarrimūnahū ʿāman*).
- The aim was **to match the number** (*li-yuwāṭiʾū ʿiddata mā ḥarrama'llāh*) — that is, the number four was preserved.
- The result was **the making lawful of what had been made forbidden**.

**The following is reported in the classical sources about the historical background of this practice — I convey it without assertion and present none of it as certain knowledge:**

It is reported that in the pre-Islamic period, because the sacred months prevented fighting, one month was put in the place of another sacred month; that this was done by delaying a month one year and putting it back the next; and that in this way the number of the sacred months was preserved while **their places were shifted**. It has also been said that the practice was an intercalation intended to correct the drift between the lunar calendar and the seasons. **The sources differ from one another in the details, and in this commentary no detail has been made a condition of the meaning of the verse.**

### The verse's diagnosis

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the expression *li-yuwāṭiʾū ʿiddata*:** the verse **says itself** that the number was preserved. What is criticised, that is, is not the corruption of the number — but **the emptying out of the content while the number is kept.**

**The form stands in place and does no work. And this joins a line treated in this series:** in `008-enfal.md`, when *mukāʾ wa taṣdiya* was treated at 8/35, this was recorded: *"the verse uses the word *ṣalāt* and empties out its content — that is, the form is there, and nothing answers to it."* **And in `002-bakara.md` at 2/42 (dressing falsehood in the clothing of truth) the same working was present.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the expression *ziyādatun fi'l-kufr* must be recorded:** the word is not "unbelief" but "**an increase in unbelief**". **The verse, that is, names the practice not as a new category but as a layer added on top of what was already there.**

### زُيِّنَ لَهُمْ سُوٓءُ أَعْمَٰلِهِمْ

**The verb comes in the passive: *zuyyina*.** **This difference was recorded in `008-enfal.md` at 8/48:** the same verb comes in one place in the active with its agent named (*zayyana lahumu'l-shayṭān*), and in another in the passive. **In `035-fatir.md` at 35/8 and `040-gafir.md` at 40/37 it was passive too.** **I rely on that treatment; here too it is passive.**

## 9/38-39 — مَا لَكُمْ إِذَا قِيلَ لَكُمُ ٱنفِرُوا۟ فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ

*Yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū mā lakum idhā qīla lakumu'nfirū fī sabīli'llāhi'ththāqaltum ila'l-arḍ; a-raḍītum bi'l-ḥayāti'l-dunyā mina'l-ākhira; fa-mā matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'l-dunyā fi'l-ākhirati illā qalīl · Illā tanfirū yuʿadhdhibkum ʿadhāban alīman wa yastabdil qawman ghayrakum wa lā taḍurrūhu shayʾā; wa'llāhu ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr*

"**O you who believe! What is the matter with you, that when you are told, 'Go out in the way of God,' you sink heavily to the ground? Are you content with the life of this world in place of the hereafter? The enjoyment of the life of this world is but little beside the hereafter.** · **If you do not go out, He will punish you with a painful punishment and put another people in your place, and you will do Him no harm at all. God has power over everything.**"

### ٱنفِرُوا۟ — root: ن-ف-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to startle, to spring from one's place, to leave in haste.** The lexicographers explain the root by way of **an animal taking fright and bolting.** From the same root:

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **نَفَرَ** (*nafara*) | He took fright and fled; he went out to a task at a run |
| **نُفُور** (*nufūr*) | Shying away in fright |
| **نَفِير** (*nafīr*) | A group going out to a task together |
| **ٱسْتَنفَرَ** | He startled; he called out to the field |

**The root was treated in `067-mulk.md` at 67/21, in `074-muddessir.md` at 74/50 (*ka-annahum ḥumurun mustanfira* — startled wild asses) and in `017-isra.md` at 17/41 (*mā yazīduhum illā nufūrā*).**

**I record this as an observation about the language, and convey it as the lexicographers give it:** the same root states both **fleeing** and **going out**. **The idea in common is not staying in one's place** — the difference of direction comes afterwards.

**And this coincides with the sūra's axis:** the second axis of the sūra was **sitting and rising**. **The root of the word stands exactly on that axis.**

### ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ — root: ث-ق-ل

**This is one of the most concrete words of the sūra, and the task requires that I open out its picture.**

**The sense of the root: weight.** *Thiql* — a load; *thaqīl* — heavy. **The root was treated in `099-zilzal.md` at 99/2 (*athqālahā*), in `035-fatir.md` at 35/18 (*muthqalatun*), in `073-muzzemmil.md` at 73/5 (*qawlan thaqīlā*) and in `055-rahman.md` at 55/31 (*al-thaqalān*).**

**The verb here is in a particular form: ٱثَّاقَلْتُمْ.** Its original is *tathāqaltum* (Form VI, *tafāʿala*) — the form that states **reciprocity and gradualness**. The *tāʾ* has been assimilated to the *thāʾ*, and a connecting hamza has come at the head.

**What Form VI states here, according to the lexicographers, is this: a state's coming about *of itself and little by little*** — a person's **surrendering** to the weight.

**And the note *ila'l-arḍ* (towards the ground) completes the picture.**

**The concrete picture of the word is this, and I give it as an observation about the language:** a body that has been asked to rise, **left to its own weight and sinking towards the ground.** It is not pushed, not felled — **its own weight draws it down.**

**And the opposition of the two verbs must be recorded:**

| Verb | Root | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| *Infirū* | ن-ف-ر | **To spring from one's place** — up and out |
| *Iththāqaltum* | ث-ق-ل | **To sink to the ground** — down and in |

**Two verbs in the same sentence, one a command and one a reproach.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sentence sets two opposite movements side by side and thereby shows the distance.

### أَرَضِيتُم بِٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا مِنَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ

**The particle must be recorded: *min* here states substitution — "*in place of* the hereafter."**

**I record this as an observation of grammar:** the sentence sets up an **exchange**. And the question is *a-raḍītum* — "are you content?" **It speaks, that is, as though a choice had been made.**

### وَيَسْتَبْدِلْ قَوْمًا غَيْرَكُمْ

The root **ب-د-ل**: **to put another thing in the place of a thing.** **The root was treated as a weave running between `048-fetih.md` 48/15 and 48/23; I rely on that treatment.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse says that the place of the one addressed **can be filled.** **And it continues with *wa lā taḍurrūhu shayʾā*: there is no harm to be done.**

## 9/40 — إِلَّا تَنصُرُوهُ فَقَدْ نَصَرَهُ ٱللَّهُ إِذْ أَخْرَجَهُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ ثَانِىَ ٱثْنَيْنِ إِذْ هُمَا فِى ٱلْغَارِ إِذْ يَقُولُ لِصَٰحِبِهِۦ لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَنَا

*Illā tanṣurūhu fa-qad naṣarahu'llāhu idh akhrajahu'lladhīna kafarū thāniya'thnayni idh humā fi'l-ghāri idh yaqūlu li-ṣāḥibihī lā taḥzan inna'llāha maʿanā; fa-anzala'llāhu sakīnatahū ʿalayhi wa ayyadahū bi-junūdin lam tarawhā wa jaʿala kalimata'lladhīna kafaru'l-suflā; wa kalimatu'llāhi hiya'l-ʿulyā; wa'llāhu ʿazīzun ḥakīm*

"**If you do not help him — God had already helped him: when those who rejected drove him out, the second of two; when the two of them were in the cave; when he was saying to his companion, 'Do not grieve, God is with us.' Then God sent down His sakīna upon him, and supported him with armies you did not see, and made the word of those who rejected the lowest. And the word of God, that is the highest. God is mighty, wise.**"

### The reducing of the number

**ثَانِىَ ٱثْنَيْنِ** — "**the second of two**", that is, **one of two people.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this construction:** the verse reduces the number to **the smallest plurality possible.** **And this stands in direct opposition to a passage fifteen verses earlier:**

| Verse | Number | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **25** | *Aʿjabatkum **kathratukum*** — **numbers** | *Fa-lam tughni ʿankum shayʾā* — **they were of no use** |
| **40** | ***Thāniya'thnayn*** — **two people** | *Fa-anzala'llāhu sakīnatahū ʿalayh* — **the sakīna came down** |

**This opposition is verifiable from the text and is one of the strongest internal weaves of the sūra.** **I give it as my own reading:** the sūra shows the same matter from both ends — numbers were not enough, fewness was no obstacle. **And the line treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/26 (*idh antum qalīlun mustaḍʿafūn*) is completed here.**

### إِذْ … إِذْ … إِذْ

**The adverb of time, repeated three times, must be recorded.**

**The verse narrows the scene in three stages:**

| *Idh* | What is related | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *Idh akhrajahu'lladhīna kafarū* | **Being driven out of a city** — wide |
| **2** | *Idh humā fi'l-ghār* | **A cave** — narrow |
| **3** | *Idh yaqūlu li-ṣāḥibih* | **A sentence** — narrowest |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the three *idh*:** the scene narrows from a city to a cave, from a cave to a single utterance. **And what is said at the narrowest point is the widest sentence of all: *inna'llāha maʿanā*.**

### لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَنَا

**There are two sentences, and both must be recorded.**

The root **ح-ز-ن**: **grief; and as the concrete sense of the root the lexicographers record *ḥazn* as "hard, broken, rough ground".** Grief, that is, is within the word **a ground hard to walk on**.

***Lā taḥzan* — "do not grieve."** **This expression occurs in several places in the Qurʾān**, and each time it is a sentence of **consolation**.

***Inna'llāha maʿanā* — "God is with us."** **And the pronoun must be recorded: *maʿanā* — "with us", not "with me".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pronoun:** the consolation states not the speaker's own position but **the position the two of them hold in common.**

**And in `008-enfal.md` at 8/19 a block of that sūra closed with *wa anna'llāha maʿa'l-muʾminīn*;** in verse 46 of the same sūra there is *inna'llāha maʿa'l-ṣābirīn*. **The same structure occurred in verse 36 of this sūra as well: *anna'llāha maʿa'l-muttaqīn*, and it will come again in verse 123.**

| Place | With whom |
|---|---|
| al-Anfāl 8/19 | *Maʿa'l-muʾminīn* |
| al-Anfāl 8/46 | *Maʿa'l-ṣābirīn* |
| al-Tawba 9/36 | *Maʿa'l-muttaqīn* |
| **al-Tawba 9/40** | ***Maʿanā*** — **inside a sentence, to two people** |
| al-Tawba 9/123 | *Maʿa'l-muttaqīn* |

**I record this as my own reading:** in four places the being-with is tied to an **attribute**; in the fortieth verse it is tied to a **moment**. **The difference is that the sentence is not a description but an utterance.**

### فَأَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُۥ عَلَيْهِ

**The pronoun must be recorded: *ʿalayhi* — singular.**

**In `048-fetih.md` the three descents of the sakīna at 48/4, 48/18 and 48/26 were compared, and there the ones addressed were plural.** **Here the one addressed is singular, and this is the word's only singular use in the Qurʾān.**

**I record this as a datum of the text.** To whom the pronoun refers has been debated in the classical commentaries:

| View | To whom *ʿalayhi* refers |
|---|---|
| **A** | **The Messenger** — the subject of the sentence and the centre of the context |
| **B** | **His companion** — as the one being consoled |

**I convey the disagreement and impose no preference.** **And per the method (`STYLE.md`): the historical and sectarian debates conducted by way of this verse are not entered into.**

**The measure recorded in `048-fetih.md` holds here too:** *sakīna* is **the ceasing of inner agitation** — not the removal of the thing to be feared. **And in this verse the thing to be feared is plainly still in place:** there is pursuit, there is a driving out, two people are in a cave. **What is lifted is the motion that flings a person about.**

## 9/41-42 — ٱنفِرُوا۟ خِفَافًا وَثِقَالًا وَجَٰهِدُوا۟ بِأَمْوَٰلِكُمْ وَأَنفُسِكُمْ فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ

*Infirū khifāfan wa thiqālan wa jāhidū bi-amwālikum wa anfusikum fī sabīli'llāh; dhālikum khayrun lakum in kuntum taʿlamūn · Law kāna ʿaraḍan qarīban wa safaran qāṣidan la'ttabaʿūka wa lākin baʿudat ʿalayhimu'l-shuqqa; wa sa-yaḥlifūna bi'llāhi lawi'staṭaʿnā la-kharajnā maʿakum; yuhlikūna anfusahum; wa'llāhu yaʿlamu innahum la-kādhibūn*

"**Go out light and heavy, and strive with your wealth and your lives in the way of God. That is better for you, if you but knew.** · **Had there been a near gain and an easy journey they would have followed you; but the hard distance seemed far to them. They will swear by God, 'Had we been able, we would have gone out with you.' They are destroying themselves; and God knows that they are lying.**"

### خِفَافًا وَثِقَالًا

**The forty-first verse repeats the verb of the thirty-eighth (*infirū*) — but this time it places a pair of adjectives beside it.**

The root **ث-ق-ل** occurred three verses earlier, in *iththāqaltum*. **Now the same root comes not as an *excuse* but as a *condition*: *thiqālan*.**

**This is one of the finest points of word order in the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:**

| Verse | Root | How it occurs |
|---|---|---|
| **38** | ث-ق-ل | *Iththāqaltum* — **a behaviour, censured** |
| **41** | ث-ق-ل | *Thiqālan* — **a condition, accepted** |

**Three verses apart, that is, the same root is put to two separate uses: growing heavy is censured, being heavy is not.** The distinction lies in whether the heaviness is **a person's situation** or **a person's choice.**

**The scope of the expression has been explained in various ways in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | *Khifāf wa thiqāl* |
|---|---|
| **A** | The young and the old |
| **B** | Those whose burden is light and those with a large household |
| **C** | Mounted and on foot |
| **D** | Willing and unwilling |
| **E** | Those lightly armed and those in heavy equipment |

**These explanations are mentioned in the sources; I adopt no preference.** **And I record this:** the multiplicity of the explanations comes from the expression's being **comprehensive**. Setting two opposite adjectives side by side covers, in Arabic, **every state that falls between them**.

### شُقَّة — root: ش-ق-ق

**The concrete sense of the root: to split, to cleave in two.** *Shaqq* — a cleft. From this derive **mashaqqa** (what splits a person, what strains him) and ***shuqqa*** — **a distance hard to cross.**

**The root was treated in `084-insikak.md` by way of the sūra's name and in `050-kaf.md` at 50/44.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the distance is named as **something that must be split open.** Distance is not something covered but something **cleft**.

### عَرَضًا قَرِيبًا وَسَفَرًا قَاصِدًا

The root **ع-ر-ض**: **breadth, a side; and what comes before one.** *ʿAraḍ* — **a passing gain that comes to hand.** The word states what is not lasting.

The root **ق-ص-د**: **to head straight for; to be measured.** *Safaran qāṣidan* — **a middling, easy journey.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse explains the failure to join by **two measures** — the remoteness of the gain and the hardness of the road. **And this comes from the same place as the preference treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/7:** there too *ghayra dhāti'l-shawka* — **they wanted the easy one.** I rely on that treatment.

## 9/43-45 — عَفَا ٱللَّهُ عَنكَ لِمَ أَذِنتَ لَهُمْ حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَكَ ٱلَّذِينَ صَدَقُوا۟

*ʿAfa'llāhu ʿanka lima adhinta lahum ḥattā yatabayyana laka'lladhīna ṣadaqū wa taʿlama'l-kādhibīn · Lā yastaʾdhinuka'lladhīna yuʾminūna bi'llāhi wa'l-yawmi'l-ākhiri an yujāhidū bi-amwālihim wa anfusihim; wa'llāhu ʿalīmun bi'l-muttaqīn · Innamā yastaʾdhinuka'lladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi'llāhi wa'l-yawmi'l-ākhiri wa'rtābat qulūbuhum fa-hum fī raybihim yataraddadūn*

"**May God pardon you; why did you give them leave before those who spoke truly had become clear to you and you knew the liars?** · **Those who believe in God and the last day do not ask your leave to be excused from striving with their wealth and their lives.** · **Only those ask your leave who do not believe in God and the last day, and whose hearts have fallen into doubt; and in their doubt they waver.**"

### عَفَا ٱللَّهُ عَنكَ

**This form of address has been debated in the classical commentaries. I convey the disagreement and impose no preference:**

| View | What the sentence states |
|---|---|
| **A** | A **declaration of pardon** — its coming at the head of the address softens the sharpness of the question that follows |
| **B** | A **formula of prayer** — an opening of address in Arabic like "may God be pleased with you" |
| **C** | An ***ʿitāb*** (a gentle reproof) — on account of the question that comes after it |

**No preference is adopted in this commentary.** **And the question of the prophets' impeccability is a debate in *theology*; there are differences of scope between the schools. The note entered in `048-fetih.md` at 48/2 is kept here too: that debate is not entered into.**

**What can be verified from the wording of the verse is this, and I give it as an observation about the structure:** the sentence comes **before** the question. The order, that is, is this: first the pardon, then the question. **The reverse was also possible; it is not what we have.**

### ٱرْتَابَتْ … فِى رَيْبِهِمْ يَتَرَدَّدُونَ

The root **ر-ي-ب**: **misgiving, a doubt that leaves one uneasy.** **The root was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/2 (*lā rayba fīh*).**

The root **ر-د-د**: **to turn back.** *Taraddud* (Form V) — **to go to a point and come back, to go and return.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the word pictures indecision as a **movement** — going forward and coming back. **And it lies in the same field as the description "having two doors" recorded in `063-munafikun.md`; I rely on that treatment.**

## 9/46-49 — وَلَوْ أَرَادُوا۟ ٱلْخُرُوجَ لَأَعَدُّوا۟ لَهُۥ عُدَّةً وَلَٰكِن كَرِهَ ٱللَّهُ ٱنۢبِعَاثَهُمْ فَثَبَّطَهُمْ

*Wa law arādu'l-khurūja la-aʿaddū lahū ʿuddatan wa lākin kariha'llāhu'nbiʿāthahum fa-thabbaṭahum wa qīla'qʿudū maʿa'l-qāʿidīn · Law kharajū fīkum mā zādūkum illā khabālan wa la-awḍaʿū khilālakum yabghūnakumu'l-fitnata wa fīkum sammāʿūna lahum; wa'llāhu ʿalīmun bi'l-ẓālimīn · Laqadi'btaghawu'l-fitnata min qablu wa qallabū laka'l-umūra ḥattā jāʾa'l-ḥaqqu wa ẓahara amru'llāhi wa hum kārihūn · Wa minhum man yaqūlu'dhan lī wa lā taftinnī; a-lā fi'l-fitnati saqaṭū; wa inna jahannama la-muḥīṭatun bi'l-kāfirīn*

"**Had they wanted to go out they would have made some preparation for it; but God disliked their setting forth, so He held them back, and it was said, 'Sit with those who sit.'** · **Had they gone out among you they would have added nothing to you but disorder, and would have hurried about in your midst seeking to stir up trouble; and among you there are those who listen to them.** · **They sought to stir up trouble before this too, and turned matters upside down for you — until the truth came and the command of God appeared, much as they disliked it.** · **And among them is one who says, 'Give me leave, and do not put me to the test.' Take note: they have already fallen into the test. Gehenna encompasses those who reject.**"

### فَثَبَّطَهُمْ — root: ث-ب-ط

**The sense of the root: to hold back from a task, to make one drag one's feet, to break one's eagerness.** **The word occurs rarely in the Qurʾān.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse names the sitting as a **being held back** — and ties the cause of it not to the persons' unwillingness but to the **acceptance** of that unwillingness. **And before it stands a conditional clause: *law arādu'l-khurūja la-aʿaddū lahū ʿudda* — "had they wanted, they would have made preparation."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this conditional clause:** the verse reads intention off **preparation.** **The measure, that is, is not a word but a preparation made.**

### خَبَال — root: خ-ب-ل

**The sense of the root: corruption, the mind's becoming confused, a limb's losing its function.** *Khabāl* — disorder, confusion.

### وَلَأَوْضَعُوا۟ خِلَٰلَكُمْ — root: و-ض-ع

**The verb here has a particular sense: *īḍāʿ* — to drive a camel or a mount fast, to make it run.**

**I record this as an observation about the language, and convey it as the lexicographers give it:** the word states **hurrying about in the gaps** — a concrete picture resembling going swiftly from place to place carrying words.

***Khilālakum* — "in your midst", that is, in the gaps between the ranks.**

### فِيكُمْ سَمَّٰعُونَ لَهُمْ

**The meaning of this expression has been debated in the classical commentaries:**

| View | What *sammāʿūn* means |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Those who listen to them** — who lend an ear to what they say |
| **B** | **Spies** — who gather information for them |

**I convey the disagreement and impose no preference.** **And the pattern must be recorded: *faʿʿāl* — the intensive form, "one who listens much."**

### The three occurrences of the word فِتْنَة

**The word occurs four times within four verses (47, 48, 49) and must be recorded:**

| Place | How *fitna* occurs |
|---|---|
| **47** | *Yabghūnakumu'l-fitnata* — **it is sought** |
| **48** | *Ibtaghawu'l-fitnata min qabl* — **it was sought before too** |
| **49** | *Lā taftinnī* — **what one wishes to avoid** |
| **49** | *Fi'l-fitnati saqaṭū* — **already fallen into** |

**The root ف-ت-ن was analysed in detail in `029-ankebut.md` at 29/2-10** (the testing of gold in fire) **and was treated in two separate senses in `008-enfal.md` at 8/25 and 8/28.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The point in the forty-ninth verse must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** a man asks not to join, saying *lā taftinnī* (do not put me to the test); the verse answers *fi'l-fitnati saqaṭū* (they have fallen into the test). **The name of the thing to be avoided, that is, is given to the act of avoidance itself.** That the word is used twice in the same verse, in two different directions, confirms this.

The root **س-ق-ط**: **to fall.** The verb *saqaṭū* is in the perfect — **the falling is recorded not as something that will happen but as something that has happened.**

## 9/50-52 — إِن تُصِبْكَ حَسَنَةٌ تَسُؤْهُمْ وَإِن تُصِبْكَ مُصِيبَةٌ يَقُولُوا۟ قَدْ أَخَذْنَآ أَمْرَنَا مِن قَبْلُ

*In tuṣibka ḥasanatun tasuʾhum; wa in tuṣibka muṣībatun yaqūlū qad akhadhnā amranā min qablu wa yatawallaw wa hum fariḥūn · Qul lan yuṣībanā illā mā kataba'llāhu lanā; huwa mawlānā; wa ʿala'llāhi fa'l-yatawakkali'l-muʾminūn · Qul hal tarabbaṣūna binā illā iḥda'l-ḥusnayayni; wa naḥnu natarabbaṣu bikum an yuṣībakumu'llāhu bi-ʿadhābin min ʿindihī aw bi-aydīnā; fa-tarabbaṣū innā maʿakum mutarabbiṣūn*

"**If good comes to you it grieves them; and if a misfortune befalls you they say, 'We took our precautions beforehand,' and they turn away rejoicing.** · **Say: nothing will befall us but what God has written for us. He is our protector; and in God let the believers put their trust.** · **Say: are you waiting for anything to happen to us but one of the two good things? We too are waiting for God to bring you a punishment from Himself or by our hands. Wait, then; we are waiting with you.**"

### إِحْدَى ٱلْحُسْنَيَيْنِ

**The construction must be recorded: *iḥda'l-ḥusnayayn* — "one of two good things."**

**The verse names both of the two possibilities as good.** In the classical commentaries it is widely reported that these two are **victory** and **martyrdom**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the dual *al-ḥusnayayn*:** the sentence does not accept the **gain/loss** pairing set up by the ones addressed. **The other side is waiting for two outcomes; the verse names both of those outcomes with a single word.**

### The clustering of the root ر-ب-ص

**The root occurs four times in these verses: *tarabbaṣūna*, *natarabbaṣu*, *tarabbaṣū*, *mutarabbiṣūn*.**

**The sense of the root: to wait and watch for a thing to happen.** **And the same root occurred as a command in verse 24 of the sūra: *fa-tarabbaṣū ḥattā yaʾtiya'llāhu bi-amrih*.**

**I record this as an observation:** the same verb is given to both sides — waiting is what both sides do. **The difference lies in what is waited for.**

## 9/53-55 — قُلْ أَنفِقُوا۟ طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًا لَّن يُتَقَبَّلَ مِنكُمْ

*Qul anfiqū ṭawʿan aw karhan lan yutaqabbala minkum; innakum kuntum qawman fāsiqīn · Wa mā manaʿahum an tuqbala minhum nafaqātuhum illā annahum kafarū bi'llāhi wa bi-rasūlihī wa lā yaʾtūna'l-ṣalāta illā wa hum kusālā wa lā yunfiqūna illā wa hum kārihūn · Fa-lā tuʿjibka amwāluhum wa lā awlāduhum; innamā yurīdu'llāhu li-yuʿadhdhibahum bihā fi'l-ḥayāti'l-dunyā wa tazhaqa anfusuhum wa hum kāfirūn*

"**Say: spend willingly or unwillingly; it will not be accepted from you. For you are a people who have gone out of the way.** · **What prevents their spending from being accepted from them is only that they have rejected God and His Messenger, that they come to the prayer only in listlessness, and that they spend only unwillingly.** · **Let their wealth and their children not draw your admiration; God wants only to punish them by these in the life of this world, and that their souls should depart while they reject.**"

### The form of the command

**The sentence is in the form of a command but is not a command: *anfiqū … lan yutaqabbala minkum*.**

**In Arabic this structure — the form of a command plus a negative outcome — is what the lexicographers call the command of threat (*amr al-tahdīd*) or the command of equalisation (*amr al-taswiya*): it states that both options come out at the same result.**

**I record this as an observation of grammar and convey it as the lexicographers give it.**

### كُسَالَىٰ — root: ك-س-ل

**The sense of the root: laziness, dragging one's feet, unwillingness.** *Kusālā* — the plural of *kasīl*.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse accepts that both acts **are performed** — they do come to the prayer, they do spend. **What is refused is not the act but the state in which the act is carried.** Both clauses are built on the pattern *illā wa hum*: "only as…".

**And the measure recorded in `063-munafikun.md` holds here too:** *"The existence of a second door does not mean that the first door is false."* I rely on that treatment.

### فَلَا تُعْجِبْكَ أَمْوَٰلُهُمْ

**This is the second of the three occurrences of the root ع-ج-ب (25, 55, 85). It was tabulated at verse 25.**

**And the construction of the sentence must be recorded:** the prohibition is directed at **the one who looks** — not at the one who owns. **What is regulated, that is, is the way one looks at another's wealth.**

## 9/56-59 — وَيَحْلِفُونَ بِٱللَّهِ إِنَّهُمْ لَمِنكُمْ وَمَا هُم مِّنكُمْ وَلَٰكِنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ يَفْرَقُونَ

*Wa yaḥlifūna bi'llāhi innahum la-minkum wa mā hum minkum wa lākinnahum qawmun yafraqūn · Law yajidūna maljaʾan aw maghārātin aw muddakhalan la-wallaw ilayhi wa hum yajmaḥūn · Wa minhum man yalmizuka fi'l-ṣadaqāt; fa-in uʿṭū minhā raḍū wa in lam yuʿṭaw minhā idhā hum yaskhaṭūn · Wa law annahum raḍū mā ātāhumu'llāhu wa rasūluhū wa qālū ḥasbuna'llāhu sa-yuʾtīna'llāhu min faḍlihī wa rasūluhū innā ila'llāhi rāghibūn*

"**They swear by God that they are of you; but they are not of you — rather they are a people who take fright.** · **Could they find a refuge, or caves, or a hole to creep into, they would turn to it and bolt.** · **And among them is one who finds fault with you over the alms. If they are given some of them they are pleased; if they are not given some of them, at once they are angry.** · **Had they been content with what God and His Messenger gave them, and said, 'God is enough for us; God will give us of His bounty, and His Messenger too; to God we turn in hope' …**"

### يَفْرَقُونَ — root: ف-ر-ق

**The root was treated in `025-furkan.md` at 25/1 and in `008-enfal.md` at 8/29** (to separate, to distinguish). **Here the verb has another sense: *faraq* — fear.**

**The lexicographers tie this sense to the root's idea of "separating": fear's tearing a person from his place, scattering him.** **I convey this derivation as the lexicographers give it, not in a language of certainty.**

### مَلْجَأً أَوْ مَغَٰرَٰتٍ أَوْ مُدَّخَلًا

**Three words enumerate a place of refuge that grows steadily narrower:**

| Word | Root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **مَلْجَأ** | ل-ج-أ | **A refuge** — a place where a person shelters |
| **مَغَٰرَٰت** | غ-و-ر | **Caves** — a natural hollow |
| **مُدَّخَل** | د-خ-ل | **A hole to creep into** — a place merely squeezed into |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the three words:** the list descends **from shelter to hole.** **And the expression *lā maljaʾa mina'llāhi illā ilayh* will come again in verse 118 of the sūra** — there the same word will stand in an entirely different place.

**يَجْمَحُونَ** — root **ج-م-ح**: **a horse taking no heed of the bit and bolting off.** The lexicographers use the word of **an animal that cannot be reined in.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the flight is pictured as **an unbridled gallop** — a movement in which the control does not lie with the person.

### يَلْمِزُكَ — root: ل-م-ز

**The root was analysed in detail in `104-humeze.md` by way of the sūra's name** (*humaza–lumaza*), and it was recorded there: *"The ground common to the two words is the act itself — belittling someone — and the difference lies in the instruments of that act."* **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**The same root will occur again in verse 79 of the sūra** (*alladhīna yalmizūna'l-muṭṭawwiʿīna*) **and will be compared there.**

### يَسْخَطُونَ — root: س-خ-ط

**The sense of the root: displeasure, anger — the opposite of *riḍā*.**

**And the word order must be recorded:** in the verse *raḍū* (they were pleased) and *yaskhaṭūn* (they grow angry) are set against one another. **And the measure is stated openly: *in uʿṭū minhā* / *wa in lam yuʿṭaw minhā* — whether or not they are given.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse diagnoses **what contentment is tied to.** And the fifty-ninth verse says what ought to be, with the same word: *wa law annahum **raḍū** mā ātāhumu'llāh* — **contentment is tied to what is given.**

The root **ر-غ-ب**: **to turn towards a thing, to want it; and as the concrete sense of the root, *raghba* carries the idea of "breadth"** — the lexicographers record *raghīb* in the sense of "wide".

## 9/60 — إِنَّمَا ٱلصَّدَقَٰتُ لِلْفُقَرَآءِ وَٱلْمَسَٰكِينِ وَٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا وَٱلْمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَفِى ٱلرِّقَابِ وَٱلْغَٰرِمِينَ وَفِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱبْنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ

*Innama'l-ṣadaqātu li'l-fuqarāʾi wa'l-masākīni wa'l-ʿāmilīna ʿalayhā wa'l-muʾallafati qulūbuhum wa fi'l-riqābi wa'l-ghārimīna wa fī sabīli'llāhi wa'bni'l-sabīl; farīḍatan mina'llāh; wa'llāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm*

"**The alms are only for these: the poor, the destitute, those who work upon them, those whose hearts are to be won over, and for (the freeing of) necks, and the debt-burdened, and in the way of God, and the traveller cut off from his road. An obligation from God. God is knowing, wise.**"

### The eight categories — a table

| # | Category | Root | Lexical sense of the word | Particle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **ٱلْفُقَرَآء** (*fuqarāʾ*) | ف-ق-ر | ***Faqr*** — the backbone; *faqīr*: **one whose spine is broken.** The lexicographers explain the word by this concrete picture | **لِ** (*li*) |
| **2** | **ٱلْمَسَٰكِين** (*masākīn*) | س-ك-ن | ***Miskīn*** — one left motionless, unable to stir. **The root was treated in `048-fetih.md` at 48/4 by way of *sakīna*** | **لِ** |
| **3** | **ٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا** | ع-م-ل | **Those who work upon them** — who carry out the collecting and the distributing | **لِ** |
| **4** | **ٱلْمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ** | أ-ل-ف | **Those whose hearts are knit together / won over.** **The root was treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/63** (*allafa bayna qulūbihim*) | **لِ** |
| **5** | **وَفِى ٱلرِّقَاب** | ر-ق-ب | ***Raqaba*** — **the neck.** The word is used in the context of gaining one's freedom | **فِى** |
| **6** | **ٱلْغَٰرِمِين** (*ghārimīn*) | غ-ر-م | ***Ghurm*** — **a burden falling on a person through no fault of his own, a debt one is obliged to pay.** **The root was treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/66 (*innā la-mughramūn*) and in `052-tur.md` at 52/40** | **لِ** |
| **7** | **وَفِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ** | س-ب-ل | **In the way of God** | **فِى** |
| **8** | **وَٱبْنِ ٱلسَّبِيل** | س-ب-ل | ***The son of the road*** — one stranded on a journey, cut off from his means while travelling | **لِ** (by conjunction) |

### A point of word order: two particles

**This is the grammatical feature of the verse most dwelt upon, and it must be recorded.**

**Six of the eight categories come with the particle لِ (*li* — "for"), and two with the particle فِى (*fī* — "in"):**

| Particle | Categories |
|---|---|
| **لِ** | The poor, the destitute, those who work, those whose hearts are to be won over, the debt-burdened, the traveller cut off |
| **فِى** | **The necks** (*fi'l-riqāb*) and **the way of God** (*fī sabīli'llāh*) |

**The lexicographers and the exegetes record this difference and explain it thus: *li* states ownership and transfer of title, while *fī* states a heading of expenditure.** In the first six, that is, the share becomes the person's; in the two that come with *fī*, the money is spent on a **purpose** and is not made over to a person.

**I convey this explanation as the lexicographers give it.** **And I add this as my own reading:** what the two items introduced by *fī* have in common is **that neither of them is the name of a person** — one is a limb (*riqāb*), the other a direction (*sabīl*). The difference of particle follows the difference of naming.

### فَرِيضَةً مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ

The root **ف-ر-ض**: **to cut, to open a notch; to fix, to determine.** The lexicographers record the concrete sense of the root as **"the notch cut for a bowstring"** — that is, **a fixed, determinate place.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** *farīḍa* carries in its root the idea of **"a share cut out, a boundary drawn"**. And its coming at the end of the verse closes the list.

### A comparison with al-Anfāl 8/41

**I make the comparison as the task requires. In `008-enfal.md` the division of spoils was treated at 8/41, and it was recorded there:** *"four of the five groups enumerated are the same as the addresses of spending treated in al-Baqara 2/177 and 2/215."*

| | **al-Anfāl 8/41** — spoils | **al-Tawba 9/60** — the alms |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Property taken after war | The alms collected |
| **Those enumerated** | God and His Messenger, near kin, orphans, the destitute, the traveller cut off | Eight categories |
| **Those in common** | **The destitute** (*masākīn*), **the traveller cut off** (*ibn al-sabīl*) | The same two categories |
| **In al-Anfāl but not in al-Tawba** | Near kin (*dhi'l-qurbā*), orphans (*yatāmā*) | — |
| **In al-Tawba but not in al-Anfāl** | — | The poor, those who work, those whose hearts are to be won over, the necks, the debt-burdened, the way of God |
| **Close** | — | *Farīḍatan mina'llāh* |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of the two lists:** al-Anfāl's list is built on **kinship and age** (near kin, orphans); al-Tawba's list on **situation** — being in debt, being stranded, doing a piece of work, seeking to gain one's freedom. **The two lists do not cancel one another; two different kinds of property have two different addresses.**

**And the two categories in common must be recorded:** *masākīn* and *ibn al-sabīl*. **These two names are the most frequently repeated in the Qurʾān's lists of spending** (treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/177 and 2/215).

### The disagreements — in the language of "it is reported"

**The scope of the categories, their measure, and how some of them are to be understood today have been widely debated in the classical and later sources. I convey the disagreement. I issue no legal ruling; nothing conveyed here is binding:**

| Matter | Views transmitted |
|---|---|
| **The difference between *faqīr* and *miskīn*** | Which of the two is the more needy has been debated: some scholars count the *faqīr* the more needy, some the *miskīn*; and there are those who say that the two state the same thing |
| ***Muʾallafat al-qulūb*** | Whether this share was to be applied in later periods has been debated; there are those who say it was withdrawn and those who say the ruling continues |
| ***Fi'l-riqāb*** | Differing views are transmitted about its scope |
| ***Fī sabīli'llāh*** | Whether its scope is narrow (a determinate field) or wide (all works of good) is the most debated item |
| **Distribution among the eight categories** | Whether it is obligatory to divide the money among all eight, and whether giving to some of them suffices, is a point of divergence between the schools |
| **The relation of alms to the obligatory alms** | The scope of the word *ṣadaqāt* in the verse has been debated |

**No view is preferred in this commentary and no ruling is issued. Per the method (`STYLE.md`): no legal ruling is issued; the views of the schools are conveyed.**

**What is to be recorded on the linguistic side of the verse is this, and I give it as my own reading:** the list enumerates **the causes of need** — poverty, immobility, debt, bondage, travel — and adds beside them **those who do the work** and **a direction**. **The list, that is, is not only a list of "the needy"; it is the list of a *working system*: who will receive, who will distribute, on what it will be spent.**

## 9/61 — وَمِنْهُمُ ٱلَّذِينَ يُؤْذُونَ ٱلنَّبِىَّ وَيَقُولُونَ هُوَ أُذُنٌ

*Wa minhumu'lladhīna yuʾdhūna'l-nabiyya wa yaqūlūna huwa udhun; qul udhunu khayrin lakum yuʾminu bi'llāhi wa yuʾminu li'l-muʾminīna wa raḥmatun li'lladhīna āmanū minkum; wa'lladhīna yuʾdhūna rasūla'llāhi lahum ʿadhābun alīm*

"**And among them are those who hurt the Prophet and say, 'He is an ear.' Say: an ear of good for you; he believes in God, and trusts the believers, and is a mercy to those of you who believe. And for those who hurt the Messenger of God there is a painful punishment.**"

### أُذُن — how a nickname works

**What is said is not a simile but a name: *huwa udhun* — "he is an ear."**

**In Arabic, naming a person by an organ is a way of intensification stating that the function of that organ is excessive in him.** The lexicographers record this: to call a man "that eye" is to say he looks a great deal; to call him "that ear" is to say he **listens to everything said**.

**The nickname, then, says this: he believes whatever he is told, he listens to everyone.**

**And how the nickname works must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** the nickname takes a **good quality** and presents it as a **defect**. Listening is a virtue; the nickname turns it into gullibility. **This is the most economical way of disparaging a person: that the thing you disparage should really be there.**

### قُلْ أُذُنُ خَيْرٍ لَّكُمْ

**The construction of the answer is one of the finest points of word order in the sūra.**

**The answer does not reject the nickname — it accepts it and qualifies it: *udhunu khayrin lakum* — "an ear that is good for you."**

**The construction is a genitive one: *udhunu khayr* — "an ear of good."** Being an ear, that is, is not denied; **what he is an ear of** is stated.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the genitive construction (*iḍāfa*):** the answer does not turn the insult around, it **moves its place.** The same word carries two different values in the same verse.

### يُؤْمِنُ بِٱللَّهِ وَيُؤْمِنُ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

**The two verbs are the same, the two particles differ, and this difference must be recorded:**

| Expression | Particle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Yuʾminu **bi**'llāh* | **بِ** | **To believe** — to affirm |
| *Yuʾminu **li**'l-muʾminīn* | **لِ** | **To trust** — to credit what is said |

**The lexicographers record this difference between *āmana bi-* and *āmana li-*.** **And this completes the answer of the verse:** the other side says "he listens to everyone"; the verse says "**he trusts the believers**". **By stating the address of the listening, the nickname is bounded.**

### وَرَحْمَةٌ لِّلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ مِنكُمْ

**The qualification *minkum* in the sentence must be recorded.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse separates out those who believe **from within** the very community in which the ones who give hurt are found. **And the mercy is ascribed to that separated section.** The category is divided once again.

**And this word — *mercy* — will come again in verse 128 of the sūra as an attribute of the envoy:** *bi'l-muʾminīna raʾūfun raḥīm*. **The link between the two verses will be treated at 128.**

## 9/62-63 — يَحْلِفُونَ بِٱللَّهِ لَكُمْ لِيُرْضُوكُمْ وَٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥٓ أَحَقُّ أَن يُرْضُوهُ

*Yaḥlifūna bi'llāhi lakum li-yurḍūkum wa'llāhu wa rasūluhū aḥaqqu an yurḍūhu in kānū muʾminīn · A-lam yaʿlamū annahū man yuḥādidi'llāha wa rasūlahū fa-anna lahū nāra jahannama khālidan fīhā; dhālika'l-khizyu'l-ʿaẓīm*

"**They swear to you by God in order to please you; but if they are believers, it is God and His Messenger whom they ought rather to please.** · **Did they not know that whoever draws a line against God and His Messenger has the fire of Gehenna, abiding in it? That is the great disgrace.**"

### أَحَقُّ أَن يُرْضُوهُ — a point about a pronoun

**There are two subjects in the sentence — *wa'llāhu wa rasūluhū* — but the pronoun is singular: *yurḍū**hu***.**

**This is a place the lexicographers dwell on, and explanations are transmitted:**

| Explanation | Ground |
|---|---|
| **A** | That the pleasing of the two is **a single thing**; that it admits of no separation |
| **B** | The Arabic usage of mentioning one of two things and attaching the other to it (ellipsis, *ḥadhf*) |
| **C** | That the pronoun refers to the last mentioned (the Messenger) |

**I convey the explanations and impose no preference.**

### يُحَادِدِ — root: ح-د-د

**The concrete sense of the root: a boundary, an edge; and iron** (*ḥadīd*). ***Ḥadd*** — the line where one thing ends and another begins.

**Form III *muḥādda*: to draw a boundary on the opposite side, to take one's place in the opposing rank.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the word relates opposition **by a geographical image** — crossing to the other side and marking out the line between. **And in `057-hadid.md` the same root was treated by way of *ḥadīd* (iron); the root's idea of "sharpness/decisiveness" was given there.**

## 9/64-66 — يَحْذَرُ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ أَن تُنَزَّلَ عَلَيْهِمْ سُورَةٌ تُنَبِّئُهُم بِمَا فِى قُلُوبِهِمْ

*Yaḥdharu'l-munāfiqūna an tunazzala ʿalayhim sūratun tunabbiʾuhum bimā fī qulūbihim; quli'stahziʾū inna'llāha mukhrijun mā taḥdharūn · Wa la-in saʾaltahum la-yaqūlunna innamā kunnā nakhūḍu wa nalʿab; qul a-bi'llāhi wa āyātihī wa rasūlihī kuntum tastahziʾūn · Lā taʿtadhirū qad kafartum baʿda īmānikum; in naʿfu ʿan ṭāʾifatin minkum nuʿadhdhib ṭāʾifatan bi-annahum kānū mujrimīn*

"**The hypocrites are wary lest a sūra be sent down upon them telling them what is in their hearts. Say: mock on; God will bring out what you are wary of.** · **And if you ask them, they will surely say, 'We were only chattering and playing.' Say: was it at God, His signs and His Messenger that you were mocking?** · **Offer no excuses; you have rejected after your faith. Even if We pardon a section of you, We shall punish a section, because they were guilty.**"

### What is feared

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the object of the verb:** what is feared is not a punishment but a **text** — *an tunazzala ʿalayhim sūra*. **And what the sūra will do is stated as well: *tunabbiʾuhum bimā fī qulūbihim* — to tell them what is in their hearts.**

The pronoun must be recorded: *tunabbiʾu**hum*** — not others, but **them.**

**I give this as my own reading:** what is feared is not that the hidden thing should become known to others, but **that it should be said to them.** It coincides with the description "having two doors" recorded in `063-munafikun.md`: the one who must least of all know of the second door is its owner.

### إِنَّمَا كُنَّا نَخُوضُ وَنَلْعَبُ

The root **خ-و-ض**: **to step into water and stir up its mud; to walk in shallow water.** From this derives the sense of **talk that has no depth and muddies things.**

**The root was treated in `074-muddessir.md` at 74/45 (*wa kunnā nakhūḍu maʿa'l-khāʾiḍīn*).**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the word pictures idle talk as **muddying water** — the spoiling of clarity.

**And the form of the excuse must be recorded: *innamā* — "only."** **I give this as an observation:** the excuse does not deny the act; **it belittles its importance.** The answer comes by enumerating the **objects** of the act: *a-bi'llāhi wa āyātihī wa rasūlih* — three objects.

### لَا تَعْتَذِرُوا۟

The root **ع-ذ-ر**: **excuse; and as one sense of the root, *ʿudhr*, "the cause that makes not doing a thing justified."**

**This verb occurs several times in the sūra: 66, 90 (*al-muʿadhdhirūn*), 94 (*yaʿtadhirūna*).** **And in the ninety-fourth verse the same command will be repeated: *lā taʿtadhirū*.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra treats the excuse as a **kind**, and in verses 91-93 it sets apart **those with a genuine excuse**. **The sūra, that is, does not reject the excuse; it separates out the false one.** This distinction will be treated in detail at verse 91.

### إِن نَّعْفُ عَن طَآئِفَةٍ مِّنكُمْ

**The last sentence of the verse must be recorded, and it is important in respect of the method.**

**The sentence uses the word *ṭāʾifa* (a section) twice: a section is pardoned, a section is punished.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the repetition of the word:** even inside the gravest sentence of ruling a **division** is made. **The sūra's language blocks the issuing of wholesale rulings from within itself.**

## 9/67-68 — ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ وَٱلْمُنَٰفِقَٰتُ بَعْضُهُم مِّنۢ بَعْضٍ

*Al-munāfiqūna wa'l-munāfiqātu baʿḍuhum min baʿḍ; yaʾmurūna bi'l-munkari wa yanhawna ʿani'l-maʿrūfi wa yaqbiḍūna aydiyahum; nasu'llāha fa-nasiyahum; inna'l-munāfiqīna humu'l-fāsiqūn · Waʿada'llāhu'l-munāfiqīna wa'l-munāfiqāti wa'l-kuffāra nāra jahannama khālidīna fīhā; hiya ḥasbuhum; wa laʿanahumu'llāh; wa lahum ʿadhābun muqīm*

"**The hypocrite men and the hypocrite women are of one another: they enjoin what is wrong and forbid what is right, and they hold their hands shut. They forgot God, so He forgot them. The hypocrites are the ones who have gone out of the way.** · **God has promised the hypocrite men, the hypocrite women and those who reject the fire of Gehenna, abiding in it. It is enough for them. God has cursed them; and for them there is a lasting punishment.**"

### I rely on `063-munafikun.md`

**The detailed analysis of the concept of hypocrisy — the root ن-ف-ق, the description of "a nest with two doors", the behavioural portrait, and why the phenomenon appeared in Medina — was made in `063-munafikun.md`. I do not repeat it here and rely on that treatment.**

**Three things belong here:**

### One: بَعْضُهُم مِّنۢ بَعْضٍ

**The construction must be recorded: *baʿḍuhum min baʿḍ* — "some of them are of some others."**

***Min* here states kind: being of the same sort, being the continuation of one another.**

**I record this as an observation of grammar:** the expression does not state a **being together** — it states a **likeness**. Between them there is not a bond but a **sameness**. **And in verse 71 this expression will change; it will be given there in a table.**

### Two: three verbs

**The verse enumerates three verbs, and all three are a negation:**

| Verb | What |
|---|---|
| *Yaʾmurūna bi'l-munkar* | **Enjoining what is wrong** |
| *Wa yanhawna ʿani'l-maʿrūf* | **Forbidding what is right** |
| *Wa yaqbiḍūna aydiyahum* | **Holding the hands shut** |

**The third verb must be recorded.** The root **ق-ب-ض**: **to close the palm, to take hold of a thing and draw it in.** *Qabḍ* — the closing of an open hand.

**I record this as an observation about the language:** miserliness is named **by the physical state of the hand.** **And its opposite will come in verse 71 as *yuʾtūna'l-zakāt* (they give the alms).**

### Three: نَسُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ فَنَسِيَهُمْ

**The same verb twice, with two different subjects.**

The root **ن-س-ي**: **to forget; and to abandon.** The lexicographers record the root's second sense (to leave a thing and take no interest in it).

**This pattern — the same verb returned upon the other party — has been treated in many places in this series.** **The same structure was present in `027-neml.md` at 27/50 (*wa makarū makran wa makarnā makrā*) and in `008-enfal.md` at 8/30 (*wa yamkurūna wa yamkuru'llāh*).** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And in ascribing the verb to God a note of transcendence is required:** the Qurʾān itself says *wa mā kāna rabbuka nasiyyā* (Maryam 19/64). **For this reason the *fa-nasiyahum* here is explained, by the common note of the lexicographers and the exegetes, in the root's second sense — abandoning, taking no interest.** **The verse in question was treated in `019-meryem.md`; I rely on that treatment.**

## 9/69-70 — كَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ كَانُوٓا۟ أَشَدَّ مِنكُمْ قُوَّةً وَأَكْثَرَ أَمْوَٰلًا وَأَوْلَٰدًا

*Ka'lladhīna min qablikum kānū ashadda minkum quwwatan wa akthara amwālan wa awlādan fa'stamtaʿū bi-khalāqihim fa'stamtaʿtum bi-khalāqikum kama'stamtaʿa'lladhīna min qablikum bi-khalāqihim wa khuḍtum ka'lladhī khāḍū; ulāʾika ḥabiṭat aʿmāluhum fi'l-dunyā wa'l-ākhira; wa ulāʾika humu'l-khāsirūn · A-lam yaʾtihim nabaʾu'lladhīna min qablihim qawmi Nūḥin wa ʿĀdin wa Thamūda wa qawmi Ibrāhīma wa aṣḥābi Madyana wa'l-muʾtafikāt*

"**Like those before you: they were stronger than you in power, and greater in wealth and children; and they took their enjoyment of their portion. You too have taken your enjoyment of your portion — as those before you took their enjoyment of their portion. And you plunged in, as they plunged in. Their works have come to nothing in this world and the next; it is they who are the losers.** · **Has the news of those before them not come to them: the people of Noah (Nūḥ), ʿĀd, Thamūd, the people of Abraham (Ibrāhīm), the people of Madyan, and the overturned towns…**"

### The texture of the sentence

**This verse is one of the most densely repetitive sentences in the Qurʾān. The same pattern occurs three times:**

```
fa'stamtaʿū      bi-khalāqihim
fa'stamtaʿtum    bi-khalāqikum
kama'stamtaʿa    …bi-khalāqihim
wa khuḍtum       ka'lladhī khāḍū
```

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this repetition:** the sentence itself does what it relates — **repetition.** The same verb, the same object, different subjects. **The sentence "you did the same thing", that is, is built by repeating the same words.**

### خَلَاق — root: خ-ل-ق

The sense of the root: to shape to measure, to create; and *khalāq* — **a portion set apart, a share.**

**This sense of the word comes from the root's idea of "measuring":** a share cut and measured out for a person.

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the verse names what is taken from this world as **"your portion"** — and the verb is *istamtaʿa* (he enjoyed, he took benefit). **The verse, that is, does not forbid taking benefit; it censures the counting of it as *the whole*.**

### ٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰت — root: أ-ف-ك

**The sense of the root: to turn a thing over on its face, to overturn it.** *Ifk* — a lie that turns the truth upside down. **The root was treated in `024-nur.md` (*ifk*) and in `051-zariyat.md` at 51/9.**

***Muʾtafikāt*** — **settlements overturned, turned upside down.**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the same root names both **a lie** and **the overturning of a place.** **And the fate of these towns was treated in `030-rum.md` and in the other sūras of narrative; here they are mentioned only by name.**

**And the six communities enumerated must be recorded:** the people of Noah, ʿĀd, Thamūd, the people of Abraham, the people of Madyan, and the *muʾtafikāt*. **This list is repeated in several places in the Qurʾān; their stories were treated in this series in `026-suara.md` and `011-hud.md`.**

## 9/71 — وَٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنَٰتُ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَآءُ بَعْضٍ يَأْمُرُونَ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ

*Wa'l-muʾminūna wa'l-muʾminātu baʿḍuhum awliyāʾu baʿḍ; yaʾmurūna bi'l-maʿrūfi wa yanhawna ʿani'l-munkari wa yuqīmūna'l-ṣalāta wa yuʾtūna'l-zakāta wa yuṭīʿūna'llāha wa rasūlah; ulāʾika sa-yarḥamuhumu'llāh; inna'llāha ʿazīzun ḥakīm*

"**The believing men and the believing women are the protectors of one another: they enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong, they keep up the prayer, they give the alms, and they obey God and His Messenger. Upon these God will have mercy. God is mighty, wise.**"

### The sūra's strongest point of word order: an exact opposition

**This verse is the exact counterpart of verse 67, and the opposition is built word for word. I give it in a table, because this is the strongest weave in the sūra:**

| Element | **9/67 — the hypocrites** | **9/71 — the believers** |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening** | *Al-munāfiqūna wa'l-munāfiqātu* | *Wa'l-muʾminūna wa'l-muʾminātu* |
| **The bond** | *Baʿḍuhum **min** baʿḍ* — **they are of one another** | *Baʿḍuhum **awliyāʾu** baʿḍ* — **they are the protectors of one another** |
| **Enjoining/forbidding** | *Yaʾmurūna bi'l-**munkar**i wa yanhawna ʿani'l-**maʿrūf*** | *Yaʾmurūna bi'l-**maʿrūf**i wa yanhawna ʿani'l-**munkar*** |
| **The hand** | *Wa **yaqbiḍūna** aydiyahum* — **closing the hands** | *Wa **yuʾtūna'l-zakāta*** — **giving** |
| **Prayer** | — (in verse 54: *illā wa hum kusālā*) | *Wa yuqīmūna'l-ṣalāta* |
| **Relation** | *Nasu'llāha fa-nasiyahum* — **a mutual forgetting** | *Wa yuṭīʿūna'llāha wa rasūlah* — **obedience** |
| **Outcome** | *Wa laʿanahumu'llāh* (68) | *Sa-yarḥamuhumu'llāh* |
| **Close** | *Inna'l-munāfiqīna humu'l-fāsiqūn* | *Inna'llāha ʿazīzun ḥakīm* |

**This opposition is verifiable from the text and is no accident: four verses apart, in the same order, the same elements are given inverted.**

### The difference between the two bonds

**The finest distinction is between *min* and *awliyāʾ*, and it must be recorded:**

| Expression | What it states |
|---|---|
| *Baʿḍuhum **min** baʿḍ* | **Unity of kind** — being of the same sort. Not a bond but a likeness |
| *Baʿḍuhum **awliyāʾu** baʿḍ* | **Walāya** — mutual responsibility, standing up for one another |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two constructions:** the first group **resemble one another**; the second group **are responsible for one another.** Resemblance is passive; walāya is an obligation.

The root **و-ل-ي**: **nearness, adjacency; to take up another's affair.** **The root was treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/72-75 by way of the bond of walāya, and in `002-bakara.md`.** **I rely on that treatment.**

### Five verbs

**The verse enumerates five verbs, and the order must be recorded:**

| # | Verb | Field |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *Yaʾmurūna bi'l-maʿrūf* | **Turned towards society** |
| **2** | *Yanhawna ʿani'l-munkar* | **Turned towards society** |
| **3** | *Yuqīmūna'l-ṣalāt* | **Turned upward** |
| **4** | *Yuʾtūna'l-zakāt* | **Turned towards wealth** |
| **5** | *Yuṭīʿūna'llāha wa rasūlah* | **Allegiance** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order:** the list begins **with two verbs turned towards society.** **The same pair occurred in `031-lokman.md` at 31/17 in the middle of a list of counsel** (*wa'mur bi'l-maʿrūfi wa'nha ʿani'l-munkar*); there it was an individual piece of advice. **Here it is the description of a *community*.** The difference is that the subject is plural.

### وَٱلْمُؤْمِنَٰتُ

**In the verse men and women are named separately — both in 67 and in 71.**

**I record this as an observation:** both verses build the same structure, and every one of the verbs enumerated is ascribed **to both sides**. **Enjoining, forbidding, prayer, alms and obedience — all five verbs belong to a shared subject.**

## 9/72-74 — وَعَدَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنَٰتِ جَنَّٰتٍ … وَرِضْوَٰنٌ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ

*Waʿada'llāhu'l-muʾminīna wa'l-muʾmināti jannātin tajrī min taḥtiha'l-anhāru khālidīna fīhā wa masākina ṭayyibatan fī jannāti ʿadn; wa riḍwānun mina'llāhi akbar; dhālika huwa'l-fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm · Yā ayyuha'l-nabiyyu jāhidi'l-kuffāra wa'l-munāfiqīna wa'ghluẓ ʿalayhim; wa maʾwāhum jahannam; wa biʾsa'l-maṣīr · Yaḥlifūna bi'llāhi mā qālū wa laqad qālū kalimata'l-kufri wa kafarū baʿda islāmihim wa hammū bimā lam yanālū; wa mā naqamū illā an aghnāhumu'llāhu wa rasūluhū min faḍlih*

"**God has promised the believing men and the believing women gardens with rivers running beneath them, abiding in them, and goodly dwellings in the gardens of ʿAdn. And a contentment from God is greater. That is the great attainment.** · **O Prophet! Strive against those who reject and against the hypocrites, and be firm with them. Their refuge is Gehenna; and evil is that journey's end.** · **They swear by God that they did not say it, and they did say the word of unbelief, and rejected after their submission; and they set out for what they did not attain. And they resented nothing but that God and His Messenger had enriched them out of His bounty.**"

### وَمَسَٰكِنَ طَيِّبَةً

**The word must be recorded: *masākin*.**

**This word occurred in verse 24 of the sūra as well — there it was the last of the eight ties that were weighed: *wa masākinu tarḍawnahā* (the dwellings you are pleased with).**

**The two occurrences can be set side by side, and I give this as my own reading:**

| Verse | *Masākin* | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **24** | *Tarḍawnahā* — **which you are pleased with** | Placed on the scale |
| **72** | *Ṭayyibatan fī jannāti ʿadn* — **goodly** | Promised |

**The same word occurs in one place in the sūra as something whose relinquishing is weighed, and in another as something promised.** **And the word is from the root س-ك-ن — the same root as *sakīna*.** The semantic field recorded in `048-fetih.md` at 48/4 (*maskan*: shelter; *sakīna*: stillness) is at work here too.

### وَرِضْوَٰنٌ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ

**The sentence sets up a comparison and does not state one of its sides: greater than what?**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** *akbar* is an elative, and the term of comparison has been elided. **What is understood from the context is the gardens and dwellings just enumerated.** After all the enumerated things have been named, that is, one further thing is named and called "greater".

### وَٱغْلُظْ عَلَيْهِمْ

The root **غ-ل-ظ**: **thickness, coarseness; the opposite of fineness and softness.** *Ghilẓa* — firmness.

**The same root will occur again in verse 123 of the sūra: *wa'l-yajidū fīkum ghilẓa*.** **The two occurrences will be compared at 123.**

**A note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** no present-day attitude and no political conclusion is drawn from this verse. **And the one addressed by the word is plain in the verse: singular, the envoy.**

### وَمَا نَقَمُوٓا۟ إِلَّآ أَنْ أَغْنَىٰهُمُ ٱللَّهُ

**The sentence must be recorded, and it is a pattern that occurs in several places in the Qurʾān.**

The root **ن-ق-م**: **to find a thing ugly and be angry at it, to take revenge for it.**

**The structure of the sentence: *wa mā naqamū illā* — a negation plus an exception, that is, restriction (*qaṣr*).** "The only cause of their anger is…"

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this structure of restriction:** the verse sets down as the cause of the anger **something unexpected** — not an injury but a **benefit**. This is the function of the pattern: the sentence puts, in the place of what would count as a ground, something that cannot count as one.

## 9/75-78 — وَمِنْهُم مَّنْ عَٰهَدَ ٱللَّهَ لَئِنْ ءَاتَىٰنَا مِن فَضْلِهِۦ لَنَصَّدَّقَنَّ

*Wa minhum man ʿāhada'llāha la-in ātānā min faḍlihī la-naṣṣaddaqanna wa la-nakūnanna mina'l-ṣāliḥīn · Fa-lammā ātāhum min faḍlihī bakhilū bihī wa tawallaw wa hum muʿriḍūn · Fa-aʿqabahum nifāqan fī qulūbihim ilā yawmi yalqawnahū bimā akhlafu'llāha mā waʿadūhu wa bimā kānū yakdhibūn · A-lam yaʿlamū anna'llāha yaʿlamu sirrahum wa najwāhum wa anna'llāha ʿallāmu'l-ghuyūb*

"**And among them is one who made a covenant with God: 'If He gives us of His bounty, we shall certainly give in alms and shall certainly be among the righteous.'** · **Then when He gave them of His bounty they were miserly with it and turned away, averse.** · **So He fixed hypocrisy in their hearts until the day they meet Him, because they broke with God what they had promised Him, and because they were lying.** · **Did they not know that God knows their secret and their whispering, and that God is the knower of all that is hidden?**"

### The structure of the promise

**The promise is reinforced with two *lāms* and two *nūns*: *la-naṣṣaddaqanna wa la-nakūnanna*.** In Arabic this (the *lām* of emphasis plus the *nūn* of emphasis) is **the strongest reinforcement an oath can have.**

**I record this as an observation of grammar:** the word is given in the most definite form language can give it. **And it is not kept.** **The verse, that is, shows by the sentence itself that reinforcement is no guarantee.**

### فَأَعْقَبَهُمْ نِفَاقًا فِى قُلُوبِهِمْ

The root **ع-ق-ب**: **the heel; to come after a thing.** *Aʿqaba* — it left a consequence behind it.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verb itself:** hypocrisy is set down here not as a **beginning** but as a **consequence.** **The order is this: the word was given → it was not kept → hypocrisy took root.**

**This coincides with the order treated in `063-munafikun.md` at 63/3:** *bi-annahum āmanū thumma kafarū fa-ṭubiʿa ʿalā qulūbihim* — there too the sealing of the heart came **after an act.** **I rely on that treatment.**

### أَخْلَفُوا۟ — root: خ-ل-ف

**The sense of the root: the back, being behind; and *ikhlāf* — not standing behind one's word, not keeping a promise given.**

**The root was treated in detail in `048-fetih.md` by way of *mukhallafūn*, and it will recur with various derivatives in verses 81, 83, 87, 118 and 120 of this sūra.** **The distribution of the root within the sūra will be given all together at verse 118.**

## 9/79-80 — ٱلَّذِينَ يَلْمِزُونَ ٱلْمُطَّوِّعِينَ مِنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ فِى ٱلصَّدَقَٰتِ وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَجِدُونَ إِلَّا جُهْدَهُمْ

*Alladhīna yalmizūna'l-muṭṭawwiʿīna mina'l-muʾminīna fi'l-ṣadaqāti wa'lladhīna lā yajidūna illā juhdahum fa-yaskharūna minhum; sakhira'llāhu minhum wa lahum ʿadhābun alīm · Istaghfir lahum aw lā tastaghfir lahum; in tastaghfir lahum sabʿīna marratan fa-lan yaghfira'llāhu lahum; dhālika bi-annahum kafarū bi'llāhi wa rasūlih; wa'llāhu lā yahdi'l-qawma'l-fāsiqīn*

"**Those who find fault, over the alms, with the believers who give freely, and with those who find nothing to give but their utmost, and who mock them…** · **Ask forgiveness for them or do not ask forgiveness for them; even if you ask forgiveness for them seventy times, God will not forgive them.**"

### Two groups, one mockery

**The verse enumerates separately the two sections that are made the object of the mockery:**

| Who | Wording | Their situation |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *Al-muṭṭawwiʿīna mina'l-muʾminīn* | **Those who give freely, beyond what is due** |
| **2** | *Alladhīna lā yajidūna illā juhdahum* | **Those who can find nothing beyond their utmost** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two groups are enumerated side by side:** the mockery strikes at once the people at **both ends** — the one who gives much and the one who can give little. **The measure of the mockery, that is, is not the amount; it is the position of the one who mocks.**

The root **ط-و-ع**: **to submit readily, to do willingly.** *Taṭawwuʿ* — **what is done freely, over and above what is obligatory.** **The word was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/158 and 2/184.**

The root **ج-ه-د**: **strength, capacity, being strained.** **The root was analysed in detail in `047-muhammed.md`**, and it was recorded there: *"Juhd is a person's coming up against the last limit of his strength."* **I rely on that treatment.**

***Lā yajidūna illā juhdahum*** — "**those who find nothing but their utmost**." **What is given, that is, is the person's last limit.**

### سَبْعِينَ مَرَّةً

**The function of the number has been debated in the classical commentaries:**

| View | The number seventy |
|---|---|
| **A** | **A number stating multiplicity** — in Arabic determinate numbers can state multiplicity rather than exactness |
| **B** | **An actual number** — a limit |

**It is reported that the majority incline to A; I impose no preference.**

**And the construction of the sentence must be recorded: *istaghfir lahum aw lā tastaghfir lahum* — both options are given and the outcome is the same.** **Almost the same sentence was treated in `063-munafikun.md` at 63/6** (*sawāʾun ʿalayhim astaghfarta lahum am lam tastaghfir lahum*). **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**The difference here must be recorded:** in al-Munāfiqūn the sentence was built with *sawāʾun* (it is equal); **in al-Tawba a number is added.** **I give this as my own reading: the number added shows the equality across a distance** — repetition does not change the outcome.

## 9/81-83 — فَرِحَ ٱلْمُخَلَّفُونَ بِمَقْعَدِهِمْ خِلَٰفَ رَسُولِ ٱللَّهِ … وَقَالُوا۟ لَا تَنفِرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْحَرِّ

*Fariḥa'l-mukhallafūna bi-maqʿadihim khilāfa rasūli'llāhi wa karihū an yujāhidū bi-amwālihim wa anfusihim fī sabīli'llāhi wa qālū lā tanfirū fi'l-ḥarr; qul nāru jahannama ashaddu ḥarrā; law kānū yafqahūn · Fa'l-yaḍḥakū qalīlan wa'l-yabkū kathīran jazāʾan bimā kānū yaksibūn · Fa-in rajaʿaka'llāhu ilā ṭāʾifatin minhum fa'staʾdhanūka li'l-khurūji fa-qul lan takhrujū maʿiya abadan wa lan tuqātilū maʿiya ʿaduwwā*

"**Those left behind rejoiced at their sitting still, in opposition to the Messenger of God; they were unwilling to strive with their wealth and their lives in the way of God, and they said, 'Do not go out in the heat.' Say: the fire of Gehenna is hotter — if only they understood.** · **Let them laugh a little and weep much: as a recompense for what they have been earning.** · **And if God brings you back to a section of them and they ask your leave to go out, say: you will never go out with me, and you will never fight an enemy alongside me.**"

### ٱلْمُخَلَّفُون

**The word is a passive participle: those left behind, not "those who stayed behind".**

**This word was treated in detail in `048-fetih.md`**, and the opposition between *mukhallaf* and *mustakhlaf* was recorded there. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the point here must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** the word is passive — "they were left." **But the continuation of the verse gives them the verbs: *fariḥa* (they rejoiced), *karihū* (they were unwilling), *qālū* (they said).** **The verse, that is, names them with a passive noun and loads active verbs upon them.** This tension between the naming and the verbs shows how the excuse is built.

### لَا تَنفِرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْحَرِّ

**The words spoken must be recorded: not an excuse but a summons.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence is in the imperative and is directed **at others**. The matter, that is, is not an individual holding back but **a summons that spreads.**

**And the construction of the answer must be recorded: *qul nāru jahannama ashaddu ḥarrā*.** **The answer is given in the objector's own word: *ḥarr* (heat).** The same word, twice in the same sentence.

**I record this as my own reading:** the answer does not reject the excuse — **it changes its scale.** The heat really is hot; the comparison is made with another heat.

### فَلْيَضْحَكُوا۟ قَلِيلًا وَلْيَبْكُوا۟ كَثِيرًا

**Two verbs in the imperative, two opposite adverbs.**

**And this is not a command like the *amr al-taswiya* of verse 53; it is what the lexicographers call *khabar maʿna'l-amr*: a statement given in the form of a command.** I convey the explanation, not in a language of certainty.

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the sentence crosses two verbs with two quantities — laughing little, weeping much. **And it is grounded with *jazāʾan bimā kānū yaksibūn*: a recompense.**

## 9/84-85 — وَلَا تُصَلِّ عَلَىٰٓ أَحَدٍ مِّنْهُم مَّاتَ أَبَدًا وَلَا تَقُمْ عَلَىٰ قَبْرِهِۦ

*Wa lā tuṣalli ʿalā aḥadin minhum māta abadan wa lā taqum ʿalā qabrih; innahum kafarū bi'llāhi wa rasūlihī wa mātū wa hum fāsiqūn · Wa lā tuʿjibka amwāluhum wa awlāduhum; innamā yurīdu'llāhu an yuʿadhdhibahum bihā fi'l-dunyā wa tazhaqa anfusuhum wa hum kāfirūn*

"**And never pray over any of them who dies, and do not stand at his grave. For they rejected God and His Messenger, and died having gone out of the way.** · **Let their wealth and their children not draw your admiration…**"

### The subject of the ruling

**What is to be recorded per the method (`STYLE.md`) is this:** the ground of the verse **is given openly**, and it is not an identity but a state: *innahum kafarū … wa mātū wa hum fāsiqūn* — **the state at the moment of death.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the clause of grounds:** the ruling is tied not to a group but **to persons who died in a determinate state.** **And no general ruling is drawn from this in this commentary. No legal ruling is issued.**

**And two verbs must be recorded:** *lā tuṣalli* (do not pray) and *lā taqum ʿalā qabrih* (do not stand at his grave). **Both are verbs of *participation*.**

### The repetition of verses 55 and 85

**The two verses are almost identical, and the difference lies in a single word:**

| Verse | Expression | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| **55** | *Fa-lā **tuʿjibka** amwāluhum **wa lā** awlāduhum* | Joined with *fāʾ*; *lā* repeated |
| **85** | *Wa lā **tuʿjibka** amwāluhum **wa** awlāduhum* | Joined with *wāw*; a single *lā* |

**And one further difference:** verse 55 has *fi'l-ḥayāti'l-dunyā*, verse 85 *fi'l-dunyā*.

**I record this repetition as a datum of the text.** **And I add this as my own reading:** the repetition of the same sentence thirty verses apart shows that this part of the sūra carries **a turning point** — the first occurrence after the non-acceptance of spending, the second after the ruling on the funeral.

## 9/86-87 — وَإِذَآ أُنزِلَتْ سُورَةٌ أَنْ ءَامِنُوا۟ بِٱللَّهِ وَجَٰهِدُوا۟ مَعَ رَسُولِهِ ٱسْتَـْٔذَنَكَ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلطَّوْلِ مِنْهُمْ

*Wa idhā unzilat sūratun an āminū bi'llāhi wa jāhidū maʿa rasūlihi'staʾdhanaka ulu'l-ṭawli minhum wa qālū dharnā nakun maʿa'l-qāʿidīn · Raḍū bi-an yakūnū maʿa'l-khawālifi wa ṭubiʿa ʿalā qulūbihim fa-hum lā yafqahūn*

"**And when a sūra is sent down, 'Believe in God and strive alongside His Messenger,' those of means among them ask your leave and say, 'Leave us; let us be with those who sit.'** · **They were content to be with those who stay behind, and their hearts were sealed; so they do not understand.**"

### أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلطَّوْل — root: ط-و-ل

The sense of the root: length; and *ṭawl* — **means, capacity, wealth.**

**The lexicographers tie this sense to the root's idea of "reaching out": having a long arm, that is, being able to reach.** **I convey this derivation as the lexicographers give it.**

**I record this as an observation:** those who ask leave are named not as **those without means** but as **those of means**. **And this is the ground of the distinction in verses 91-93:** the ninety-third verse will say *wa hum aghniyāʾ* (while they were rich).

### ٱلْخَوَالِف — root: خ-ل-ف

The word is the plural of *khālifa*: **those who stay behind.**

**Its meaning has been debated in the classical commentaries:**

| View | Who the *khawālif* are |
|---|---|
| **A** | Those who did not go out on the expedition and stayed at home |
| **B** | Women and children |
| **C** | Those who are of no use — from the word's sense of "what is left over" |

**I convey the disagreement and impose no preference.** **And a note per the method (`STYLE.md`): no social ruling is drawn from view B; the verse uses a word, it does not make an assessment.**

**And the verb must be recorded: *raḍū* — "they were content."** **This is the opposite of the *raḍū* in verse 59:** there contentment was the thing asked for (*wa law annahum raḍū mā ātāhumu'llāh*), here contentment is the thing censured. **The same verb carries two different values with two different objects.**

## 9/88-89 — لَٰكِنِ ٱلرَّسُولُ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ مَعَهُۥ جَٰهَدُوا۟ بِأَمْوَٰلِهِمْ وَأَنفُسِهِمْ

*Lākini'l-rasūlu wa'lladhīna āmanū maʿahū jāhadū bi-amwālihim wa anfusihim; wa ulāʾika lahumu'l-khayrāt; wa ulāʾika humu'l-mufliḥūn · Aʿadda'llāhu lahum jannātin tajrī min taḥtiha'l-anhāru khālidīna fīhā; dhālika'l-fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm*

"**But the Messenger and those who believed with him strove with their wealth and their lives. All good things are theirs; it is they who prosper.** · **God has prepared for them gardens with rivers running beneath them, abiding in them. That is the great attainment.**"

### لَٰكِنِ

**The particle of correction (*istidrāk*): "but."** **The verse is set directly opposite the preceding block.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the sūra closes each block of negative description with a **counter-description**. **The same structure was present at 67-71** (the hypocrites / the believers). **The same thing is repeated here at 86-88.** This is a weave verifiable within the sūra.

**And *lahumu'l-khayrāt* must be recorded:** *khayrāt* — the plural of *khayr*; not one determinate thing but **the whole of good things.**

## 9/90 — وَجَآءَ ٱلْمُعَذِّرُونَ مِنَ ٱلْأَعْرَابِ لِيُؤْذَنَ لَهُمْ

*Wa jāʾa'l-muʿadhdhirūna mina'l-aʿrābi li-yuʾdhana lahum wa qaʿada'lladhīna kadhabu'llāha wa rasūlah; sa-yuṣību'lladhīna kafarū minhum ʿadhābun alīm*

"**Those of the bedouin who put forward excuses came so that leave might be given them; while those who lied to God and His Messenger sat still. A painful punishment will befall those of them who rejected.**"

### ٱلْمُعَذِّرُون — a question of variant reading and of meaning

**There is disagreement over the reading and the meaning of the word, and I record it because it changes the sense:**

| Reading | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلْمُعَذِّرُون** (*al-muʿadhdhirūn*) | Active participle of Form II | **Those who fabricate excuses** — who put forward a pretext that does not hold |
| **ٱلْمُعْذِرُون** (*al-muʿdhirūn*) | From Form IV | **Those who have a genuine excuse** |

**Both readings are mentioned in the sources, and the sense reverses. I impose no preference.**

**And I record this as my own reading:** the continuation of the verse names a **second group** — *wa qaʿada'lladhīna kadhabu'llāha wa rasūlah* — that is, **those who did not come and sat still.** **The sentence separates two behaviours: those who came and stated an excuse, and those who did not come at all.** This distinction itself shows that, whichever reading is taken, the word carries a **gradation**.

### ٱلْأَعْرَاب

**The word was treated in `048-fetih.md` and `049-hucurat.md`, and it was recorded there: *aʿrāb* does not mean "the Arabs" but the bedouin* — communities not settled, living in the desert. I rely on that treatment.**

**And the particle *min* here too states a partitive: *mina'l-aʿrāb* — "of the bedouin."** **And indeed verse 99 of the sūra will name a section from within that same community separately.**

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`): no wholesale ruling about any ethnic or social group is drawn from this word.**

## 9/91-93 — لَّيْسَ عَلَى ٱلضُّعَفَآءِ وَلَا عَلَى ٱلْمَرْضَىٰ وَلَا عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَجِدُونَ مَا يُنفِقُونَ حَرَجٌ إِذَا نَصَحُوا۟ لِلَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦ

*Laysa ʿala'l-ḍuʿafāʾi wa lā ʿala'l-marḍā wa lā ʿala'lladhīna lā yajidūna mā yunfiqūna ḥarajun idhā naṣaḥū li'llāhi wa rasūlih · Mā ʿala'l-muḥsinīna min sabīl*

"**There is no blame upon the weak, nor upon the sick, nor upon those who find nothing to spend — provided they are sincere towards God and His Messenger. There is no way against those who do good.**"

### The separating out of the excuse

**Since the eighty-eighth verse the sūra had been relating those who stayed behind.** **The ninety-first verse separates out a section from within that same group** — and the measure of separation is given by **three concrete conditions**:

| Measure | Who |
|---|---|
| *al-Ḍuʿafāʾ* | **The weak** |
| *al-Marḍā* | **The sick** |
| *Alladhīna lā yajidūna mā yunfiqūn* | **Those without means** |

**And upon all three a condition is laid: *idhā naṣaḥū li'llāhi wa rasūlih*.**

The root **ن-ص-ح**: the core sense the lexicographers give is **to make a thing unmixed** — *nuṣḥ* is used also of honey's being strained and separated from its dross; and the sense of **sewing up a tear** (*niṣāḥa* — sewing thread) is recorded as well.

**I record this as my own reading:** both branches of the root (purifying and sewing) carry **the mending of a relationship.** **And the condition is tied not to an outward act but to an inward posture:** the person without means **does not go**, but his bond is not cut.

**مَا عَلَى ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ مِن سَبِيلٍ** — "**there is no way against those who do good.**"

***Sabīl* here means "a way that can be worked against someone, a way of liability".** **The same expression was treated in `048-fetih.md` at 48/17 (*laysa ʿala'l-aʿmā ḥarajun wa lā ʿala'l-aʿraji ḥaraj*);** I rely on that treatment. **And in `024-nur.md` at 24/61 the same list occurred in the context of the etiquette of the table.** **Three sūras build the same principle in three different fields: the expedition, war, the table.**

### وَلَا عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا مَآ أَتَوْكَ لِتَحْمِلَهُمْ قُلْتَ لَآ أَجِدُ مَآ أَحْمِلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ تَوَلَّوا۟ وَّأَعْيُنُهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ ٱلدَّمْعِ حَزَنًا أَلَّا يَجِدُوا۟ مَا يُنفِقُونَ (92)

"**Nor upon those who, when they came to you for you to mount them and you said, 'I find nothing to mount you on,' turned back with their eyes overflowing with tears out of grief that they found nothing to spend.**"

This verse shows how the excuse is established, and I record it as my own reading: the measure is not the word spoken — it is **having wept on the way back.**

**تَفِيضُ مِنَ ٱلدَّمْعِ** — *fayḍ*: **a vessel's filling and overflowing.** **The root was treated in `048-fetih.md` and in `002-bakara.md` at 2/198 (*fa-idhā afaḍtum min ʿArafāt*)** — there it was used of a crowd pouring out. **Here it is used of the eye: overflowing.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verb relates the tears not as **dripping** but as **overflowing** — that is, a filling beyond what the vessel can hold.

### إِنَّمَا ٱلسَّبِيلُ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَـْٔذِنُونَكَ وَهُمْ أَغْنِيَآءُ (93)

**And the distinction is completed: *wa hum aghniyāʾ* — those who ask leave while they have the means.**

**The word *sabīl* is denied in verse 91 and affirmed in verse 93.** **Two verses apart, the same word in two opposite rulings.** This is a repetition verifiable within the sūra.

## 9/94-96 — يَعْتَذِرُونَ إِلَيْكُمْ إِذَا رَجَعْتُمْ إِلَيْهِمْ قُل لَّا تَعْتَذِرُوا۟ لَن نُّؤْمِنَ لَكُمْ قَدْ نَبَّأَنَا ٱللَّهُ مِنْ أَخْبَارِكُمْ

"**When you return to them they will offer you excuses. Say: 'Offer no excuses; we shall not believe you. God has told us your news.'**"

**And the measure is shifted from what is said to the act:** *wa sa-yara'llāhu ʿamalakum wa rasūluh* — **"God and His Messenger will see your work."**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse does not refuse **to listen** to the excuse — it refuses to let the excuse **count as proof**. **And in its place it lays down a measure directed forward: the act.**

**فَأَعْرِضُوا۟ عَنْهُمْ إِنَّهُمْ رِجْسٌ** (95) and **يَحْلِفُونَ لَكُمْ لِتَرْضَوْا۟ عَنْهُمْ فَإِن تَرْضَوْا۟ عَنْهُمْ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَرْضَىٰ عَنِ ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلْفَٰسِقِينَ** (96)

**The oath and the contentment are worth recording:** *yaḥlifūna lakum li-tarḍaw ʿanhum.* **That is, even the purpose of the oath is stated — not persuasion but obtaining contentment.**

**And the construction of the answer:** *fa-in tarḍaw ʿanhum fa-inna'llāha lā yarḍā.* **The separating of people's contentment from the ruling.**

## 9/97-99 — ٱلْأَعْرَابُ أَشَدُّ كُفْرًا وَنِفَاقًا وَأَجْدَرُ أَلَّا يَعْلَمُوا۟ حُدُودَ مَآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ رَسُولِهِۦ … وَمِنَ ٱلْأَعْرَابِ مَن يُؤْمِنُ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْـَٔاخِرِ

"**The bedouin are harder in rejection and hypocrisy, and more apt not to know the limits of what God has sent down upon His Messenger**… **And among the bedouin is one who believes in God and the last day.**"

### A note required per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**No ruling can be drawn without reading these three verses together**, and I record this openly.

**The ninety-seventh verse makes an observation; the ninety-eighth relates one section; and the ninety-ninth, saying *wa mina'l-aʿrābi man yuʾminu bi'llāhi wa'l-yawmi'l-ākhir*, names an opposite section from within the same community.**

| Verse | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 97 | *al-Aʿrāb* — **a general observation** | *Ashaddu kufran wa nifāqā* |
| 98 | *Wa mina'l-aʿrābi man…* | One who counts what he gives a **loss** |
| 99 | *Wa mina'l-aʿrābi man…* | One who counts what he gives a **nearness** |

**In both verses there is the particle *min* (partitive): "among the bedouin is one who."** **The verse, that is, divides the community in two and names each by its own attribute.**

**And the ground of the ninety-seventh verse is given in the text as well:** *wa ajdaru allā yaʿlamū ḥudūda mā anzala'llāh* — **their living far from the rulings.** **The observation, that is, is not a ruling about descent or identity but the observation of a *situation*; and its cause is geographical distance.**

***That the word aʿrāb* means not "the Arabs" but the bedouin was treated in `048-fetih.md` and in `049-hucurat.md` at 49/14;** I rely on that treatment. **Per the method (`STYLE.md`) no wholesale ruling is issued about any ethnic or social group, and the sūra itself does not issue one in any case.**

**مَغْرَمًا** (98) — root **غ-ر-م**: debt, loss, a price that must be paid. **The root was treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/66 (*innā la-mughramūn*) and in `052-tur.md` and `068-kalem.md`.**

**قُرُبَٰتٍ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ** (99) — **and the opposite section counts the same act (*mā yunfiqu*) a nearness.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses use the same verb:** what is spent is the same; **what changes is what the spender counts it to be.** **And in `009-tevbe.md` at 9/60 the headings of expenditure of the alms were enumerated — here what the alms amount to *in their giver* is related.**

## 9/100 — وَٱلسَّٰبِقُونَ ٱلْأَوَّلُونَ مِنَ ٱلْمُهَٰجِرِينَ وَٱلْأَنصَارِ وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّبَعُوهُم بِإِحْسَٰنٍ رَّضِىَ ٱللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا۟ عَنْهُ

"**The first to go ahead — of the emigrants and of the helpers — and those who followed them in goodness: God is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him.**"

**The root س-ب-ق was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/148 and in `056-vakia.md` at 56/10** — there *al-sābiqūn* was analysed as the name of a class. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the construction is worth recording: *wa'lladhīna'ttabaʿūhum bi-iḥsān* — "those who followed them in goodness."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the qualification *bi-iḥsān*:** the list is not built as **a closed circle**. **The third group is unbounded in respect of time, and it has one condition only: that the following be *in goodness*.**

**And the *fa-inna'llāha lā yarḍā ʿani'l-qawmi'l-fāsiqīn* of the ninety-sixth verse stands face to face with the *raḍiya'llāhu ʿanhum* here.** **The same root (ر-ض-و), four verses apart, in two opposite rulings.**

## 9/101-106 — Three groups, and those left suspended

*Wa mimman ḥawlakum mina'l-aʿrābi munāfiqūn wa min ahli'l-madīnati maradū ʿala'l-nifāq lā taʿlamuhum naḥnu naʿlamuhum*

"**Among the bedouin around you, and among the people of Medina, there are hypocrites; they have grown obstinate in hypocrisy. You do not know them; We know them.**"

**مَرَدُوا۟** — root **م-ر-د**: **to grow used to a thing and harden, to form a crust.** The lexicographers record its relation to the root's sense of "hairless, bare" (*amrad*): **that nothing is left on it to take hold of.**

**I convey this as the lexicographers give it and add this as an observation:** the word describes hypocrisy not as an **act** but as a **settledness**.

**لَّا تَعْلَمُهُمْ نَحْنُ نَعْلَمُهُمْ** — **and the limit of knowledge is drawn openly.** **This is one of the most striking notes in the sūra, and I give it as my own reading:** throughout the sūra the hypocrites have been related at length; **here it is said that who they are, one by one, is not known.** **What is described, that is, is an *attribute*, not an established *list*.**

### وَءَاخَرُونَ ٱعْتَرَفُوا۟ بِذُنُوبِهِمْ خَلَطُوا۟ عَمَلًا صَٰلِحًا وَءَاخَرَ سَيِّئًا (102)

**A third group: those who confess.** **And their description is worth recording: *khalaṭū ʿamalan ṣāliḥan wa ākhara sayyiʾā* — "they mixed a good deed with another that was bad."**

The root **خ-ل-ط**: **to mix.** **I record this as an observation:** the verse does not separate them into good or bad — **it names them as mixed.** **And a state is described that is the exact opposite of the concept of *ikhlāṣ* (the sifting out of what is mixed) treated in `039-zumer.md` at 39/2.**

**خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ إِنَّ صَلَوٰتَكَ سَكَنٌ لَّهُمْ** (103)

***Sakanun lahum* — "a stillness for them."** **The root س-ك-ن was treated in `048-fetih.md` at 48/4 (*sakīna*) and in `006-enam.md` at 6/96 (*jaʿala'l-layla sakanā*).** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And two verbs must be recorded: *tuṭahhiruhum* (it cleanses them) and *tuzakkīhim* (it purifies and increases them).** **The root ز-ك-و states both cleanness and growth.**

### وَءَاخَرُونَ مُرْجَوْنَ لِأَمْرِ ٱللَّهِ إِمَّا يُعَذِّبُهُمْ وَإِمَّا يَتُوبُ عَلَيْهِمْ (106)

"**And others are left to the command of God: either He punishes them or He accepts their turning back.**"

**مُرْجَوْن** — root **ر-ج-و/ر-ج-أ**: **to postpone, to leave in suspense.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verse's own wording:** in enumerating three groups, the sūra **issues no ruling about the third.** *Immā … wa immā* — two possibilities are left open.

**And this coincides with the posture kept in the method (`STYLE.md`):** **a text can leave blank the place about which it has no ruling.** **The sūra applies this within itself** — and indeed in verse 101 it had said *lā taʿlamuhum*.

## 9/107-110 — Two foundations

*Wa'lladhīna'ttakhadhū masjidan ḍirāran wa kufran wa tafrīqan bayna'l-muʾminīna wa irṣādan li-man ḥāraba'llāha wa rasūlahū min qabl*

"**And those who took a mosque in order to do harm, and out of rejection, and to divide the believers, and as a watch-post for one who had before made war on God and His Messenger…**"

**Four grounds are enumerated, and all four state a *purpose*:** *ḍirāran*, *kufran*, *tafrīqan*, *irṣādan.*

**The root ض-ر-ر was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/231 (*wa lā tumsikūhunna ḍirāran li-taʿtadū*)** and it was recorded there: **the same act receives two different rulings by a difference of intention.** **I rely on that treatment** — and here the same thing is said of a **building**: the structure the same, the purpose different.

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse rules not on the building **itself** but on **what it was built for.** **And it comes from the same place as the line treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/35:** the form is kept, the function emptied out.

### أَفَمَنْ أَسَّسَ بُنْيَٰنَهُۥ عَلَىٰ تَقْوَىٰ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرِضْوَٰنٍ خَيْرٌ أَم مَّنْ أَسَّسَ بُنْيَٰنَهُۥ عَلَىٰ شَفَا جُرُفٍ هَارٍ فَٱنْهَارَ بِهِۦ فِى نَارِ جَهَنَّمَ (109)

**The likeness is built on two foundations, and its elements can be separated one by one:**

| Element | The first | The second |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | *Taqwā wa riḍwān* | *Shafā jurufin hār* |
| Ground | Firm | **Hollowed-out earth at the edge of a drop** |
| Outcome | — | *Fa'nhāra bihī* — **it collapsed with him** |

**جُرُف** — **a bank of earth whose underside has been scoured away by floodwater: the top still standing, the base emptied.** ***Hār* — on the point of collapse.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word itself:** a *juruf* is a ground that **looks firm from outside**; the hollow is **underneath.** **The likeness, that is, separates apparent firmness from the real support** — and this is the very difference between the mosque's form and its purpose.

**And the verb must be recorded: *fa'nhāra bihī* — "it collapsed with him."** **The structure does not merely fall; it takes its builder with it.**

**In `029-ankebut.md` at 29/41 the likeness of the spider's house was treated** (*awhana'l-buyūti la-baytu'l-ʿankabūt*) **and it was recorded there: the point is not that the web is useless but that it cannot be a *shelter*.** **The two likenesses cut at the same place: one an unsound wall, the other an unsound ground.**

## 9/111-112 — The transaction and nine attributes

*Inna'llāha'shtarā mina'l-muʾminīna anfusahum wa amwālahum bi-anna lahumu'l-janna*

"**God has bought from the believers their lives and their wealth, that theirs may be the garden.**"

**The root ش-ر-ي was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/207** (*wa mina'l-nāsi man yashrī nafsahu'btighāʾa marḍāti'llāh*) — there it was recorded that the word is one of the *aḍdād* (meaning both to sell and to buy). **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two verses can be compared:**

| Place | Verb | Who |
|---|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/207 | *Yashrī nafsahū* | **The person** — he sells himself |
| **al-Tawba 9/111** | *Ishtarā … anfusahum* | **The buying party** is stated |

**And the elements of the contract are enumerated one by one in the verse** — I record this as an observation: **what is sold** (lives and wealth), **the price** (the garden), **the document** (*waʿdan ʿalayhi ḥaqqan fi'l-Tawrāti wa'l-Injīli wa'l-Qurʾān*), **the confirmation** (*wa man awfā bi-ʿahdihī mina'llāh*). **The sentence, that is, is built with all the elements of a contract of trade.**

### ٱلتَّٰٓئِبُونَ ٱلْعَٰبِدُونَ ٱلْحَٰمِدُونَ ٱلسَّٰٓئِحُونَ ٱلرَّٰكِعُونَ ٱلسَّٰجِدُونَ ٱلْـَٔامِرُونَ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَٱلنَّاهُونَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ وَٱلْحَٰفِظُونَ لِحُدُودِ ٱللَّهِ (112)

**Nine attributes, all in the form of the active participle and all definite (*al-*).**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the form states **continuity** — not deeds performed but **attributes of the doer.**

**ٱلسَّٰٓئِحُون** — root **س-ي-ح**: **water flowing over the ground; and to travel.** **There is disagreement about what the word amounts to here:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Those who fast** — widely reported in the tradition |
| 2 | **Those who travel** — who set out for knowledge or for worship |

**Both readings are transmitted; I impose no preference.**

**And it is worth recording that the list may be compared with the lists of attributes in `023-muminun.md` 23/1-11 and `025-furkan.md` 25/63-77** — in those two files those lists were tabulated. **What is different in al-Tawba: all nine attributes are set out in a single verse and in a single form.**

## 9/113-116 — مَا كَانَ لِلنَّبِىِّ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَن يَسْتَغْفِرُوا۟ لِلْمُشْرِكِينَ وَلَوْ كَانُوٓا۟ أُو۟لِى قُرْبَىٰ مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا تَبَيَّنَ لَهُمْ أَنَّهُمْ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْجَحِيمِ

"**It is not for the Prophet and those who believe to ask forgiveness for those who associate partners with God, even if they be kin, after it has become clear to them that they are of the fire.**"

### The place of the qualification

**Two qualifications in the sentence must be recorded, and both are limiting:**

| Qualification | What it does |
|---|---|
| *Wa law kānū ulī qurbā* | **Kinship is no exception** |
| *Min baʿdi mā tabayyana lahum* | **Only after it has become clear** |

**Without the second qualification the ruling cannot be understood**, and I record this as my own reading: **the prohibition is placed not on praying itself but on its being continued *after the situation has become clear*.**

**And the next verse shows this by an example:**

**وَمَا كَانَ ٱسْتِغْفَارُ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ لِأَبِيهِ إِلَّا عَن مَّوْعِدَةٍ وَعَدَهَآ إِيَّاهُ فَلَمَّا تَبَيَّنَ لَهُۥٓ أَنَّهُۥ عَدُوٌّ لِّلَّهِ تَبَرَّأَ مِنْهُ** (114)

***Fa-lammā tabayyana lahū* — the same verb, a second time.** **Abraham's praying was the requirement of a word given; when the situation became clear he left off.**

**In `019-meryem.md` at 19/47 Abraham's saying to his father *sa-astaghfiru laka rabbī* was treated**, and it was recorded there: **the one driven away departs saying he will pray for the one who drove him away.** **This verse states the end of that word.** **Read together, the two sūras complete the picture.**

**إِنَّ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ لَأَوَّٰهٌ حَلِيمٌ** — ***awwāh*: root أ-و-ه — derived from the sound "āh"; the lexicographers explain it as one deeply moved, full of pity, praying with a groan.** **The word occurs also in `011-hud.md` at 11/75** (*inna Ibrāhīma la-ḥalīmun awwāhun munīb*) **and was treated there;** I rely on that treatment.

**I record this as an observation:** immediately after issuing the ruling to leave off praying, the verse describes **the one who leaves off praying as "deeply moved."** **The ruling, that is, does not come from a want of mercy.**

**وَمَا كَانَ ٱللَّهُ لِيُضِلَّ قَوْمًۢا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَىٰهُمْ حَتَّىٰ يُبَيِّنَ لَهُم مَّا يَتَّقُونَ** (115) — **and a third time the same root: *ḥattā yubayyina lahum*.**

***The root bayān* (ب-ي-ن) occurs three times in three verses** (113, 114, 115). **This is a repetition verifiable within the sūra**, and I record it as my own reading: **in all three places the ruling is tied to the condition of *clarity*** — no one is held responsible for what he does not know, and no one is left in an unclarified situation.

## 9/117-118 — لَّقَد تَّابَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَى ٱلنَّبِىِّ وَٱلْمُهَٰجِرِينَ وَٱلْأَنصَارِ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّبَعُوهُ فِى سَاعَةِ ٱلْعُسْرَةِ … وَعَلَى ٱلثَّلَٰثَةِ ٱلَّذِينَ خُلِّفُوا۟ حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا ضَاقَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْأَرْضُ بِمَا رَحُبَتْ وَضَاقَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ أَنفُسُهُمْ

"**God has turned in acceptance to the Prophet, to the emigrants and to the helpers, in the hour of hardship**… **And to the three who were left behind: until the earth, for all its breadth, closed in on them, and their own souls closed in on them.**"

### Two closings-in

**The sentence uses the same verb (*ḍāqat*) twice and ties it to two different subjects:**

| Subject | Expression |
|---|---|
| *al-Arḍ* | *Ḍāqat ʿalayhimu'l-arḍu **bimā raḥubat*** — **for all its breadth** |
| *Anfusuhum* | *Wa ḍāqat ʿalayhim **anfusuhum*** — **themselves upon themselves** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the qualification *bimā raḥubat*:** the first closing-in is **not a real narrowness** — the earth is wide. **What narrows is the person's relation to the earth.** **And the second clause carries this inward: the person's relation to himself.**

**And in `039-zumer.md` 39/10, `029-ankebut.md` 29/56 and `004-nisa.md` 4/97 the breadth of the earth (*arḍu'llāhi wāsiʿa*) was treated as a possibility of escape.** **In al-Tawba the same breadth becomes the measure of a state in which no escape is left.** **The two uses can be compared, and this is an opposition verifiable across sūras.**

**وَظَنُّوٓا۟ أَن لَّا مَلْجَأَ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّآ إِلَيْهِ** — "**They understood that there is no refuge from God except to Him.**"

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: *lā maljaʾa mina'llāhi illā ilayh* — the one fled from and the one taken refuge in are the same.**

**ثُمَّ تَابَ عَلَيْهِمْ لِيَتُوبُوٓا۟** — **and the order is worth recording: the acceptance is named *before* the turning back.** *Tāba ʿalayhim li-yatūbū* — **"He turned to them in acceptance so that they might turn back."** **I record this as an observation about the structure.**

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`):** the event related in the background of this verse is transmitted in detail in the classical sources. **In this commentary no personal name is given and no report is conveyed;** what the verse itself supplies as a frame is enough.

## 9/119-122 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَكُونُوا۟ مَعَ ٱلصَّٰدِقِينَ … وَمَا كَانَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ لِيَنفِرُوا۟ كَآفَّةً فَلَوْلَا نَفَرَ مِن كُلِّ فِرْقَةٍ مِّنْهُمْ طَآئِفَةٌ لِّيَتَفَقَّهُوا۟ فِى ٱلدِّينِ

"**O you who believe! Guard yourselves against God and be with the truthful**… **The believers are not to go out all together. Why should not a section from every community set out, to gain understanding in religion and to warn their people when they return?**"

### Two goings-out

**Throughout the sūra *nafīr* (going out on expedition) had been commanded** (38, 39, 41, 81). **The hundred and twenty-second verse uses the same verb for another work: *li-yatafaqqahū fi'l-dīn*.**

| Verse | What the *nafīr* is for |
|---|---|
| 38-41 | **The expedition** — all together |
| **122** | **Knowledge** — a section from every community |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the verb is the same:** the sūra uses the same word for two different mobilisations. **And in the second, *kāffatan* (all together) is openly rejected** — that is, a divided duty.

**فَقِهَ** — root **ف-ق-ه**: **to understand a thing in depth, to reach its fineness.** **Form V (*tafaqquh*) adds effort and process.**

**وَلِيُنذِرُوا۟ قَوْمَهُمْ إِذَا رَجَعُوٓا۟ إِلَيْهِمْ** — **and the purpose of the learning is stated: to return and to warn.** **Knowledge, that is, is not for staying where one went but for bringing back.**

### ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ لَا يُصِيبُهُمْ ظَمَأٌ وَلَا نَصَبٌ وَلَا مَخْمَصَةٌ فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ … إِلَّا كُتِبَ لَهُم بِهِۦ عَمَلٌ صَٰلِحٌ (120)

**Three hardships are enumerated:** *ẓamaʾ* (thirst), *naṣab* (weariness), *makhmaṣa* (hunger). **And all three are recorded as *a righteous deed*.**

**For *naṣab* and *lughūb* I rely on `035-fatir.md` 35/35;** there both were denied of the garden. **Here the same words occur as things for which a return is written in this world.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse enters into the ledger of deeds not **the work done** but **the hardship borne.**

## 9/123 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ قَٰتِلُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ يَلُونَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْكُفَّارِ وَلْيَجِدُوا۟ فِيكُمْ غِلْظَةً وَٱعْلَمُوٓا۟ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ

*Yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū qātilu'lladhīna yalūnakum mina'l-kuffāri wa'l-yajidū fīkum ghilẓa · Wa'ʿlamū anna'llāha maʿa'l-muttaqīn*

"**O you who believe! Fight those of the rejecters who are near you, and let them find firmness in you. And know that God is with those who guard themselves.**"

### The verse's own qualifications

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`), the verse must be read together with its own qualifications** — and these qualifications are not interpretation but **the elements of the sentence itself:**

| Qualification | Expression | What it limits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Alladhīna **yalūnakum*** | **Who** — those who are near, not a general category |
| 2 | *Wa'l-yajidū fīkum **ghilẓa*** | **How** — deterrence; an *attribute* that is to be found |
| 3 | *Wa'ʿlamū anna'llāha **maʿa'l-muttaqīn*** | **Condition** — the being-with is tied to guarding oneself |

The root **و-ل-ي**: **to be near, to be adjacent.** *Walī*, *awlā*, *mawlā* are from the same root. ***Yalūnakum* — "those adjacent to you", that is, those nearest in respect of distance.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word itself:** the verse states an **order** — beginning from what is near. **And this is consistent with the line the sūra has kept from the start:** from the ninth verse onward the one addressed is **a determinate party that made a treaty and broke it**; the fourth verse of the sūra openly excepted those who did not break their treaty, and the sixth the one who seeks refuge.

**غِلْظَة** — root **غ-ل-ظ**: **hardness, firmness, thickness.** **The root occurs in `009-tevbe.md` at 9/73 (*wa'ghluẓ ʿalayhim*) and in `003-al-i-imran.md` at 3/159 (*ghalīẓa'l-qalb*).**

**And the difference between the two uses is worth recording:** in Āl ʿImrān being *ghalīẓa'l-qalb* (hard of heart) is a **censured** attribute — *wa law kunta faẓẓan ghalīẓa'l-qalbi la'nfaḍḍū min ḥawlik.* **In al-Tawba, by contrast, *ghilẓa* is asked for as something the other side is to find.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the two do not contradict one another, because **the parties addressed are different** — one is a bearing towards one's own circle, the other a deterrence towards a party in a state of war. **And in `048-fetih.md` at 48/29 (*ashiddāʾu ʿala'l-kuffāri ruḥamāʾu baynahum*) this distinction was treated and a limit was laid down there;** I rely on that treatment.

### The limit drawn per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**No present-day political or military conclusion is drawn from this verse.** The frame of the verse is **a state of mutual war** which the sūra itself relates; and the limits of that state were enumerated one by one in the first sixteen verses of the sūra (the excepting of the one who keeps his treaty, the protecting of the one who seeks refuge and his being brought to a place of safety, the answering in kind when peace is inclined to — treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/61).

**And the last sentence of the verse leaves a measure: *inna'llāha maʿa'l-muttaqīn*.** **The being-with, that is, is tied not to the fighting itself but to guarding oneself** — and *taqwā* is the word that has been repeated since the second verse of the sūra.

## 9/124-127 — وَإِذَا مَآ أُنزِلَتْ سُورَةٌ فَمِنْهُم مَّن يَقُولُ أَيُّكُمْ زَادَتْهُ هَٰذِهِۦٓ إِيمَٰنًا فَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ فَزَادَتْهُمْ إِيمَٰنًا وَهُمْ يَسْتَبْشِرُونَ · وَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ فَزَادَتْهُمْ رِجْسًا إِلَىٰ رِجْسِهِمْ

"**When a sūra is sent down, some of them say, 'Which of you has this increased in faith?' As for those who believe, it has increased them in faith, and they rejoice. As for those in whose hearts is a sickness, it has added defilement to their defilement.**"

### The same text, two outcomes

**The sentence applies the same verb (*zādathum*) to both sides and changes its object:**

| Side | *Zādathum* |
|---|---|
| *Alladhīna āmanū* | *Īmānā* — **faith** |
| *Alladhīna fī qulūbihim maraḍ* | *Rijsan ilā rijsihim* — **defilement upon defilement** |

**I record this as my own reading:** the text is constant, **the outcome divides in two** — and the cause of the division is shown not in the text but **in the receiver** (*fī qulūbihim maraḍ*).

**The same structure was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/44** (*huwa li'lladhīna āmanū hudan wa shifāʾ … wa huwa ʿalayhim ʿamā*) **and occurred in `008-enfal.md` at 8/2 (*zādathum īmānā*).** **Three sūras record the same working.**

**أَوَلَا يَرَوْنَ أَنَّهُمْ يُفْتَنُونَ فِى كُلِّ عَامٍ مَّرَّةً أَوْ مَرَّتَيْنِ ثُمَّ لَا يَتُوبُونَ وَلَا هُمْ يَذَّكَّرُونَ** (126)

**A frequency is given: *fī kulli ʿāmin marratan aw marratayn*.**

**I record this as an observation:** it is said that the testing **is repeated** and that even so it produces no result. **In `029-ankebut.md` at 29/2 (*a-ḥasiba'l-nāsu an yutrakū … wa hum lā yuftanūn*) the inevitability of being tested was treated;** **al-Tawba gives the same root with a *number*.**

## 9/128-129 — The close of the sūra

*Laqad jāʾakum rasūlun min anfusikum ʿazīzun ʿalayhi mā ʿanittum ḥarīṣun ʿalaykum bi'l-muʾminīna raʾūfun raḥīm*

"**There has come to you an envoy from among yourselves: your falling into distress weighs heavy on him; he cares greatly for you; and to the believers he is compassionate and merciful.**"

### Three words

| Word | Root | What it states |
|---|---|---|
| *ʿAzīzun ʿalayhi mā ʿanittum* | ع-ن-ت | **Your distress weighs heavy on him** |
| *Ḥarīṣun ʿalaykum* | ح-ر-ص | **Deeply concerned for you** |
| *Raʾūfun raḥīm* | ر-أ-ف / ر-ح-م | **Compassionate and merciful** |

The root **ع-ن-ت**: **to fall into difficulty, hardship.** The lexicographers record the root's picture of "a bone's being hurt again as it knits after a break" — **that is, the renewed injuring of something that is in the course of mending.**

**I convey this as the lexicographers give it.**

The root **ح-ر-ص**: **to be anxious over a thing, to want it insistently.**

### The tension of the sūra

**And here the tension I recorded in the section on structure is completed.**

| Place | Expression |
|---|---|
| 9/1 | *Barāʾatun mina'llāhi wa rasūlihī* — **the severing of a relation** |
| **9/128** | *Raʾūfun raḥīm* — **compassion and mercy** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two ends of the sūra:** one and the same sūra opens with a **declaration of dissolution** and closes with a **description of mercy.** **And between the two ends, throughout the sūra, the same distinction has been at work: the one who broke his treaty and the one who did not, the one with an excuse and the one without, the one who gives and counts it a loss and the one who gives and counts it a nearness.**

**The sūra, that is, takes its stand not against a community but against a *behaviour*** — and this is the counterpart, in the sūra's own structure, of the clause of `STYLE.md` that says "what the verse describes are attributes".

**فَإِن تَوَلَّوْا۟ فَقُلْ حَسْبِىَ ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَهُوَ رَبُّ ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ** (129)

***Ḥasbiya'llāh* — treated in `008-enfal.md` at 8/62 (*fa-inna ḥasbaka'llāh*) and in `039-zumer.md` at 39/36 (*a-laysa'llāhu bi-kāfin ʿabdah*).** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the sūra closes a hundred and twenty-nine verses of dissolution, expedition and distinction with a sentence of trust.**

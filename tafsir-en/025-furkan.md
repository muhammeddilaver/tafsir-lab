# 25. Sūrat al-Furqān

**Seventy-seven verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the word **ٱلْفُرْقَان** in the first verse: **that which separates, that which makes distinct.**

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-9 | The Furqān and the four objections raised against it | *Fa-ḍallū fa-lā yastaṭīʿūna sabīlā* (9) |
| **II** | 10-34 | The objections met, and the scenes of the reckoning | *Wa aḥsana tafsīrā* (33-34) |
| **III** | 35-44 | The series of prophets, and the one who takes his *hawā* as a god | *Bal hum aḍallu sabīlā* (44) |
| **IV** | 45-62 | The shadow, the wind, the two seas, lineage — the proofs | *Wa khalaqa kulla shayʾin fa-qaddarahū taqdīrā* (2, 61-62) |
| **V** | 63-77 | **The servants of ٱلرَّحْمَٰن** — a list of attributes | *Fa-sawfa yakūnu lizāmā* (77) |

**The backbone of the sūra is a comparison:** in the first block those addressed object to the Prophet — **"what kind of messenger is this, who eats food and walks about in the markets"** (7). **And in its last block the sūra gives an indirect but complete answer to that objection: the attributes of the *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* likewise begin with walking** (63). **That is, the sūra closes the debate over "what a messenger should be" with a description of "how a servant lives".**

## 25/1-3 — تَبَارَكَ ٱلَّذِى نَزَّلَ ٱلْفُرْقَانَ عَلَىٰ عَبْدِهِۦ لِيَكُونَ لِلْعَٰلَمِينَ نَذِيرًا

"**Blessed is He who sent down the Furqān upon His servant, that he might be a warner to all the worlds!**"

### تَبَارَكَ

**Root ب-ر-ك: the core sense the lexicographers give is *baraka* — the kneeling of a camel, settling in a place and staying fixed there.** From this comes the sense of **baraka** (a good that lasts and increases). **The root was treated at `067-mulk.md` 67/1** (*tabāraka'lladhī bi-yadihi'l-mulk*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The word occurs three times in this sūra: 1, 10, 61.** This is a pattern that can be verified from the text, and the subjects of the three occurrences differ: **the book, the promise, the sky.**

### ٱلْفُرْقَان

The root **ف-ر-ق**: **to separate, to open a gap between two things.** The root was treated at `044-duhan.md` 44/4 (*yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm*) and in `077-murselat.md`.

***Furqān* — the pattern (*fuʿlān*) is counted by the lexicographers as a noun of action: "the act of separating", or "that which makes distinct".**

**I record this as an observation:** this name given to the book states not its content but its **function**. **And the whole of the sūra is that function put into practice: through the verses two parties, two sayings, two walks, two waters, two ends will be separated.**

**عَلَىٰ عَبْدِهِ** — "upon His servant". **The designation is worth recording: the objection that will come in the seventh verse will be aimed at exactly this point** — *mā li-hādha'r-rasūli yaʾkulu'ṭ-ṭaʿām.* **At the very outset the verse says that the one sent is a *servant*.**

**ٱلَّذِى لَهُۥ مُلْكُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ … وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ فَقَدَّرَهُۥ تَقْدِيرًا** — *taqdīr*: **the root ق-د-ر was treated at `097-kadr.md`, `054-kamer.md` 54/49 and `039-zumer.md` 39/67** — the core sense is **measure.** **I do not repeat it here.**

## 25/4-9 — Four objections

*Wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū in hādhā illā ifkuni'ftarāhu wa aʿānahū ʿalayhi qawmun ākharūn · Wa qālū asāṭīru'l-awwalīna'ktatabahā fa-hiya tumlā ʿalayhi bukratan wa aṣīlā*

"Those who disbelieve said: '**This is nothing but a falsehood he has invented; and another people has helped him with it.**' And they said: '**Legends of the ancients! He has had them written down, and they are recited to him morning and evening.**'"

### The table of the objections

**Four separate objections are set out across the block:**

| Verse | Objection | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | *Ifkuni'ftarāh wa aʿānahū … qawmun ākharūn* | **He got help** |
| 5 | *Asāṭīru'l-awwalīna'ktatabahā* | **He compiled it from old texts** |
| 7 | *Yaʾkulu'ṭ-ṭaʿāma wa yamshī fi'l-aswāq* | **He is an ordinary man** |
| 8 | *Law lā ulqiya ilayhi kanzun aw takūnu lahū jannatun* | **He is not rich** |

**Not one of the four has to do with the content.** I record this as my own reading: **none of the objections looks at what the text says** — they look at its source, at how it was put together, and at the condition of the one who carries it.

**تُمْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِ بُكْرَةً وَأَصِيلًا** — *bukratan wa aṣīlā* (early morning and late afternoon). **These two times were treated at `076-insan.md` 76/25 and `040-gafir.md` 40/55.**

**And this is worth recording:** there the same two times were named for **glorification**; **here they occur inside an accusation.** The objection describes a regular course of work.

### مَالِ هَٰذَا ٱلرَّسُولِ يَأْكُلُ ٱلطَّعَامَ وَيَمْشِى فِى ٱلْأَسْوَاقِ

**The objection in the seventh verse is the place in the sūra that will be dwelt on most** — because **it will be answered directly in the twentieth verse.**

**The logic of the objection must be recorded:** one who is a messenger ought to be far from human needs. **That is, what is expected is that the messenger should hold a visible privilege — an angel accompanying him (7), a treasure sent down to him (8), a garden given to him (8).**

## 25/10-19 — تَبَارَكَ ٱلَّذِىٓ إِن شَآءَ جَعَلَ لَكَ خَيْرًا مِّن ذَٰلِكَ … بَلْ كَذَّبُوا۟ بِٱلسَّاعَةِ وَأَعْتَدْنَا لِمَن كَذَّبَ بِٱلسَّاعَةِ سَعِيرًا

"**Blessed is He who, had He willed, would have given you better than that!**… **No — they have denied the Hour.**"

### The real matter beneath the objection

**The tenth verse answers the objection in the eighth (the treasure, the garden)** — and the form of the answer must be recorded: **the demand is not rejected; it is said to be within reach** (*in shāʾa jaʿala laka*).

**And the eleventh verse names the real source of the objection:** *bal kadhdhabū bi's-sāʿa* — **"no — they have denied the Hour."**

**The particle *bal* must be recorded: it lets go of what was said before and brings in the real cause.** I give this as my own reading: **the sūra says that beneath the objection that is voiced (wealth, an angel) there lies a refusal that is not voiced.** **That is, the dispute is not where it appears to be.**

### إِذَا رَأَتْهُم مِّن مَّكَانٍۭ بَعِيدٍ سَمِعُوا۟ لَهَا تَغَيُّظًا وَزَفِيرًا

"**When it sees them from a far place, they hear its raging growl and its roar.**"

**The subject of the verb must be recorded: *raʾathum* — the one that sees is the fire.** **That is, the act of seeing has been reversed.**

**تَغَيُّظ** — root **غ-ي-ظ**: **anger that boils up from within.** *Zafīr* — **a rasping breath drawn from deep down.**

**I record this as an observation:** both words have to do with **sound**, and both are sounds **belonging to a living thing**. **The figure of *tashkhīṣ* (personification) was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/30 (*hal imtalaʾti*);** I rely on that treatment.

**مُّقَرَّنِينَ دَعَوْا۟ هُنَالِكَ ثُبُورًا · لَّا تَدْعُوا۟ ٱلْيَوْمَ ثُبُورًا وَٰحِدًا وَٱدْعُوا۟ ثُبُورًا كَثِيرًا** (13-14)

**ثُبُور** — **destruction, annihilation.** The expression is a cry of the sort "woe is me, I am finished!"

**And the form of the answer is worth recording:** the cry is not forbidden — **it is told to be multiplied.** *Lā tadʿū … thubūran wāḥidan wa'dʿū thubūran kathīrā.* **I record this as an observation about the word order: the sentence answers by refusing to console.**

**قُلْ أَذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ أَمْ جَنَّةُ ٱلْخُلْدِ** (15) — and the block turns upon a question of comparison.

**وَيَوْمَ يَحْشُرُهُمْ وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ فَيَقُولُ ءَأَنتُمْ أَضْلَلْتُمْ عِبَادِى هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ أَمْ هُمْ ضَلُّوا۟ ٱلسَّبِيلَ** (17)

**The question is put to those who were served, and their answer is reported:** *subḥānaka mā kāna yanbaghī lanā an nattakhidha min dūnika min awliyāʾ.*

**The same reversal was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/5-6 and at `035-fatir.md` 35/14:** the party who was called upon refusing in the end. **I rely on that treatment.**

## 25/20 — وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَا قَبْلَكَ مِنَ ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ إِلَّآ إِنَّهُمْ لَيَأْكُلُونَ ٱلطَّعَامَ وَيَمْشُونَ فِى ٱلْأَسْوَاقِ وَجَعَلْنَا بَعْضَكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ فِتْنَةً أَتَصْبِرُونَ

"**All the messengers We sent before you likewise ate food and walked about in the markets. We have made you a trial for one another: will you endure?**"

### The form of the answer

**The verse repeats the words of the objection in the seventh verse exactly:** *yaʾkulūna'ṭ-ṭaʿāma wa yamshūna fi'l-aswāq.*

| Verse | Who says it | The expression |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Those who object** | *Yaʾkulu'ṭ-ṭaʿāma wa yamshī fi'l-aswāq* |
| 20 | **The text** | *La-yaʾkulūna'ṭ-ṭaʿāma wa yamshūna fi'l-aswāq* |

**The same expression, once as a defect and once as a rule.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** the answer does **not reject** the objection's description — it confirms it and generalises it. **That is, what is said is not "it is not so" but "it has always been so".** The thing objected to is set down not as an exception but as the rule.

**وَجَعَلْنَا بَعْضَكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ فِتْنَةً أَتَصْبِرُونَ** — "**We have made you a trial for one another.**"

**The root ف-ت-ن was treated in detail at `029-ankebut.md` 29/2-10** (the testing of gold in fire), and it was recorded there that the trial comes **at the hands of people** (*jaʿala fitnata'n-nāsi ka-ʿadhābi'llāh*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**The addition here must be recorded: the trial is *mutual*** — *baʿḍakum li-baʿḍ.* **That is, one is both the one who tests and the one tested.** And the question is tied to a single verb: *a-taṣbirūn.*

## 25/21-26 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَرْجُونَ لِقَآءَنَا لَوْلَآ أُنزِلَ عَلَيْنَا ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ أَوْ نَرَىٰ رَبَّنَا لَقَدِ ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ فِىٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ وَعَتَوْ عُتُوًّا كَبِيرًا

"Those who do not hope for the meeting with Us said: '**Should not angels have been sent down to us, or should we not see our Lord?**' **They have indeed made themselves great within themselves, and have gone very far in insolence.**"

### The demand grows

**In the seventh verse an angel's accompanying him was asked for; here the demand grows: *aw narā rabbanā*.**

| # | Verse | Demand |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | *Law lā unzila ilayhi malakun fa-yakūna maʿahū nadhīrā* | **An angel for the messenger** |
| **21** | *Law lā unzila ʿalayna'l-malāʾikata **aw narā rabbanā*** | **An angel for themselves — and direct sight** |

**I record this as an observation:** the demand moves from refusing the intermediary towards **making the absence of an intermediary a condition**.

**ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ فِىٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ** — "**they have made themselves great within themselves.**"

**The qualifier must be recorded: *fī anfusihim* — the self-magnifying is named not as an outward behaviour but as something taking place inside.** **The same placement was treated at `040-gafir.md` 40/56** (*in fī ṣudūrihim illā kibr*); I rely on that treatment.

**عَتَوْ عُتُوًّا كَبِيرًا** — root **ع-ت-و**: **to overstep the limit; to dry out and harden.** The lexicographers record for the root the sense of "a plant drying out and stiffening".

### وَقَدِمْنَآ إِلَىٰ مَا عَمِلُوا۟ مِنْ عَمَلٍ فَجَعَلْنَٰهُ هَبَآءً مَّنثُورًا (23)

"**We shall take up every deed they did and make of it scattered motes of dust.**"

**هَبَآء** — **the fine dust that floats in the air and is seen in a shaft of sunlight.**

**The word had occurred at `056-vakia.md` 56/6:** *fa-kānat **habāʾan** munbaththā* — there it was used of the **mountains**. **The two verses can be set side by side:**

| Place | What the *habāʾ* is | Qualifier |
|---|---|---|
| al-Wāqiʿa 56/6 | **The mountains** | *Munbath* — spread out |
| **al-Furqān 25/23** | **Deeds** | *Manthūr* — scattered |

**The same word, once for the heaviest body and once for the most abstract thing.** This is an overlap that can be verified across the sūras, and I record it as my own reading thus: **the word describes a thing that has not lost its visibility but has become impossible to hold** — that is, not annihilation but **not remaining in the hand.**

**أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْجَنَّةِ يَوْمَئِذٍ خَيْرٌ مُّسْتَقَرًّا وَأَحْسَنُ مَقِيلًا** (24) — *maqīl*: **the place where one rests at midday.** The word states the halt taken at the hottest hour of the day. **For an addressee who lives in the desert, this is the most concrete description of ease there is.**

## 25/27-29 — وَيَوْمَ يَعَضُّ ٱلظَّالِمُ عَلَىٰ يَدَيْهِ يَقُولُ يَٰلَيْتَنِى ٱتَّخَذْتُ مَعَ ٱلرَّسُولِ سَبِيلًا · يَٰوَيْلَتَىٰ لَيْتَنِى لَمْ أَتَّخِذْ فُلَانًا خَلِيلًا

"**On that day the wrongdoer will bite his hands and say: 'Would that I had taken a way together with the Messenger! Woe to me! Would that I had not taken so-and-so as a friend!'**"

**يَعَضُّ عَلَىٰ يَدَيْهِ** — **to bite one's hands: the bodily expression of regret.** In Arabic the idiom is named together with anger and helplessness.

**فُلَانًا** — **"so-and-so".** The word stands in for a pronoun, used of someone whose name is not spoken.

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the word itself:** the verse gives no name. **The sentence thereby becomes readable for any friendship at all** — and indeed the accounts of particular incidents transmitted in the classical commentaries do not remove that generality.

**خَلِيل** — root **خ-ل-ل**: the sense the lexicographers give is **love working its way into the interstices (*khilāl*) of the heart**. **That is, a *khalīl* is not a companion at the surface — he is a friend who has got inside.**

**The same reversal was treated at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/67** (*al-akhillāʾu yawmaʾidhin baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ʿaduww*), and it had also occurred at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/6. **Three sūras describe the same reversal: friendship turned inside out.**

**وَكَانَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ لِلْإِنسَٰنِ خَذُولًا** — *khadhūl*: **one who leaves another alone, who abandons him at the very moment of need.** An intensive form.

## 25/30 — وَقَالَ ٱلرَّسُولُ يَٰرَبِّ إِنَّ قَوْمِى ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ هَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ مَهْجُورًا

"**The Messenger said: 'My Lord! My people have left this Qurʾān abandoned.'**"

**مَهْجُور** — root **ه-ج-ر**: **to part, to abandon, to leave and go.** *Hijra* is from the same root. **The root was treated at `073-muzzemmil.md` 73/10 (*wa'hjurhum hajran jamīlā*) and at `074-muddessir.md` 74/5.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The word's use here is worth recording, and two readings of it are transmitted:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Abandoned** — not recited, not put into practice |
| 2 | **Spoken of with absurdities** — from the *hujr* (raving) branch of the root |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the one to whom the complaint is addressed must be recorded: the sentence is spoken from the Prophet's mouth, to his Lord.** Not to people. **I record this as an observation: in the sūra the objections (4-8) were voiced openly by people; the answer here is directed upward, as a complaint.**

## 25/31 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَا لِكُلِّ نَبِىٍّ عَدُوًّا مِّنَ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ وَكَفَىٰ بِرَبِّكَ هَادِيًا وَنَصِيرًا

"**Thus for every prophet We appointed an enemy from among the guilty. Your Lord suffices as a guide and as a helper.**"

**The verse comes immediately after the complaint in the thirtieth verse** (*inna qawmi'ttakhadhū hādha'l-qurʾāna mahjūrā*) **and is built as a consolation.**

**And the form of the consolation must be recorded: the situation is not removed — it is generalised.** *Li-kulli nabiyyin ʿaduwwā.*

**The same method was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/4 and at 35/25** (*wa in yukadhdhibūka fa-qad kudhdhibat rusulun min qablik*), and it had occurred at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/9 (*mā kuntu bidʿan mina'r-rusul*). **Three sūras build the same structure: what is being lived through is met by being set into a series.**

**وَكَفَىٰ بِرَبِّكَ هَادِيًا وَنَصِيرًا** — **two nouns: the one who guides and the one who helps.**

**I record this as an observation:** having stated that the enmity exists, the verse answers **two distinct needs** — the way (*hādī*) and support (*naṣīr*). **That is, both direction and strength.**

## 25/32-33 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَوْلَا نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ جُمْلَةً وَٰحِدَةً كَذَٰلِكَ لِنُثَبِّتَ بِهِۦ فُؤَادَكَ وَرَتَّلْنَٰهُ تَرْتِيلًا · وَلَا يَأْتُونَكَ بِمَثَلٍ إِلَّا جِئْنَٰكَ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَأَحْسَنَ تَفْسِيرًا

"Those who disbelieve said: '**Should not the Qurʾān have been sent down upon him all at once, as a single whole?**' **Thus — that We might make your heart firm by it; and We recited it slowly, in an ordered measure.** **Whenever they bring you an example, We bring you the truth and a finer exposition.**"

### The ground for the piecemeal descent

**The objection is a claim of deficiency: it did not come down as a whole.** **And the answer gives two grounds:**

| Ground | Expression |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Li-nuthabbita bihī fuʾādak* — **to make the heart firm** |
| 2 | *Wa rattalnāhu tartīlā* — **to recite slowly, in an ordered measure** |

**رَتَّلَ** — root **ر-ت-ل**: **the even, well-spaced set of the teeth.** From this comes the sense **ordered, spaced, unhurried.** **The root was treated at `073-muzzemmil.md` 73/4** (*wa rattili'l-qurʾāna tartīlā*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The comparison of the two verses is worth recording:**

| Place | Who does it |
|---|---|
| al-Muzzammil 73/4 | **The human being** — a command: recite slowly |
| **al-Furqān 25/32** | **The text** — a statement: We sent it down slowly |

**The same root, once as a command and once as a statement.** I record this as an observation: **the manner of the reciting resembles the manner of the descent.**

**وَأَحْسَنَ تَفْسِيرًا** — **the word *tafsīr* occurs in the Qurʾān only here.**

The root **ف-س-ر**: **to open, to lift the covering from a thing and make it plain.** The lexicographers record the root's relation to what is examined in order to learn a patient's condition (*tafsira*) — **that is, looking inside and bringing the condition to light.**

**I record this as an observation:** the word that gives this commentary its name occurs in the Qurʾān in a single place, and in the construct **"the finest exposition"**. **And the build of the sentence states a method: against every example brought, both *ḥaqq* and *tafsīr* are given** — that is, both what is true and the exposition of it.

## 25/34-42 — ٱلَّذِينَ يُحْشَرُونَ عَلَىٰ وُجُوهِهِمْ إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ … وَكُلًّا ضَرَبْنَا لَهُ ٱلْأَمْثَٰلَ وَكُلًّا تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

"**Those who will be gathered to Hell upon their faces**… **To each We gave examples, and each We shattered utterly.**"

### A short list of destructions

**The block names several peoples briefly:** the people of Noah (Nūḥ) (37), ʿĀd, Thamūd, **the Companions of al-Rass**, and *qurūnan bayna dhālika kathīrā* (38).

**There is no certain knowledge about the Companions of al-Rass** — **the same note was entered at `050-kaf.md` 50/12.** I rely on that treatment, and **I transmit no report.**

**وَكُلًّا ضَرَبْنَا لَهُ ٱلْأَمْثَٰلَ** — "to each We gave examples."

**I record this as an observation:** into the middle of the list of destructions is set **what was done before the destruction**. **And the same principle was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/24 (*wa in min ummatin illā khalā fīhā nadhīr*) and at `028-kasas.md` 28/59 (*wa mā kāna rabbuka muhlika'l-qurā ḥattā yabʿatha fī ummihā rasūlā*).** **Three sūras lay down the same condition.**

**تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا** — root **ت-ب-ر**: **to break into small pieces, to reduce to fragments.** *Tibr* — unworked, scattered particles of gold.

### وَلَقَدْ أَتَوْا۟ عَلَى ٱلْقَرْيَةِ ٱلَّتِىٓ أُمْطِرَتْ مَطَرَ ٱلسَّوْءِ أَفَلَمْ يَكُونُوا۟ يَرَوْنَهَا (40)

"**They have surely come upon the town that was rained upon with an evil rain. Did they not see it?**"

**The location of the proof is worth recording: the ruin lies on the road.**

**The same proof was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/27 (*mā ḥawlakum mina'l-qurā*) and at `040-gafir.md` 40/21, 40/82.** **The difference here: the verse says *they came upon it* — that is, they passed beside it.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the proof does not even require searching; **it is the act of seeing itself that is asked about.** *A-fa-lam yakūnū yarawnahā.*

### وَإِذَا رَأَوْكَ إِن يَتَّخِذُونَكَ إِلَّا هُزُوًا أَهَٰذَا ٱلَّذِى بَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ رَسُولًا (41)

**The sentence of mockery must be recorded: *a-hādha'lladhī baʿatha'llāhu rasūlā* — "Is this the one God has sent as a messenger?"**

**And the demonstrative pronoun (*hādhā*) is used.** **The objection in the seventh verse of the sūra had also begun with the same pronoun:** *mā li-**hādha**'r-rasūl.* **Both sentences of mockery are built on the same pointing.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**إِن كَادَ لَيُضِلُّنَا عَنْ ءَالِهَتِنَا لَوْلَآ أَن صَبَرْنَا عَلَيْهَا** (42) — "**had we not held fast to them, he would almost have turned us away from our gods.**"

**I record this as an observation:** in the sentence the word *ṣabr* occurs in the sense of **holding out**, and in the mouth of the opposing side. **And in the twentieth verse of the sūra the same root was a question directed at those addressed: *a-taṣbirūn*.** **The same word, on two sides and at two different jobs.**

## 25/43-44 — أَرَءَيْتَ مَنِ ٱتَّخَذَ إِلَٰهَهُۥ هَوَىٰهُ أَفَأَنتَ تَكُونُ عَلَيْهِ وَكِيلًا · أَمْ تَحْسَبُ أَنَّ أَكْثَرَهُمْ يَسْمَعُونَ أَوْ يَعْقِلُونَ إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا كَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ بَلْ هُمْ أَضَلُّ سَبِيلًا

"**Have you seen the one who has taken his own craving as his god? Will you then be a guardian over him?**"

**This sentence is almost identical with `045-casiye.md` 45/23, and it was treated in detail there** — in particular the two readings of the construct *ilāhahū hawāhu* (which of the two is the object) were set out in a table. **I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference here must be recorded:** in al-Jāthiya what followed the sentence went on to **the sealing**; **here what follows is a limit of responsibility:** *a-fa-anta takūnu ʿalayhi wakīlā.*

**The same word was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/41** (*wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl*). **And at `028-kasas.md` 28/56 the table of this limit across seven sūras was given; this verse is added to that table.**

**كَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ بَلْ هُمْ أَضَلُّ سَبِيلًا** — **the comparison was treated at `047-muhammed.md` 47/12**, and it was recorded there: **the animal's eating is not blamed; what is described is a human being's contenting himself with just that much.** I rely on that treatment.

**And the ground for the addition *bal hum aḍallu sabīlā* is worth recording:** an animal has no way from which it might stray. **The comparison looks at a capacity given and left unused.** I give this as my own reading.

## 25/45-50 — أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ كَيْفَ مَدَّ ٱلظِّلَّ وَلَوْ شَآءَ لَجَعَلَهُۥ سَاكِنًا ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَا ٱلشَّمْسَ عَلَيْهِ دَلِيلًا · ثُمَّ قَبَضْنَٰهُ إِلَيْنَا قَبْضًا يَسِيرًا

"**Have you not seen how your Lord stretched out the shadow? Had He willed, He would have made it still. Then We made the sun a proof for it; then We drew it in to Ourselves, little by little.**"

### The proof of the shadow

**The choice of the proof is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** a shadow is a thing that **has no existence of its own**. It cannot be grasped, it cannot be measured; **it exists only through the existence of something else.** The verse makes precisely this into a proof.

**وَلَوْ شَآءَ لَجَعَلَهُۥ سَاكِنًا** — "had He willed, He would have made it **still**."

***Sākin* — motionless.** **That is, the movement of the shadow is named not as something of itself but as the result of an ordering.**

**ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَا ٱلشَّمْسَ عَلَيْهِ دَلِيلًا** — "**We made the sun a proof for it.**"

**The build of the sentence looks inverted, and the lexicographers dwell on it:** what one expects is that the shadow should be the proof of the sun. **The verse says the reverse: the sun is the proof of the shadow.**

**I record this as an observation:** where a shadow falls is known by looking at where the sun is. **That is, when one wishes to read the shadow, the place to look is not the shadow itself — it is its source.**

**قَبْضًا يَسِيرًا** — "a drawing in slowly, by small degrees". ***Yasīr* — easy, little by little.** **The shadow's withdrawal over the course of the day is described not as a sudden removal but as a taking back that goes unnoticed.**

### وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا وَٱلنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًا وَجَعَلَ ٱلنَّهَارَ نُشُورًا

**Three words, three distinct functions:**

| Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Libās* | ل-ب-س | **A garment** — that which covers |
| *Subāt* | س-ب-ت | **A cutting off, a halt** — the description of sleep |
| *Nushūr* | ن-ش-ر | **Spreading out, rising to life** |

**This triad was treated at `078-nebe.md` 78/9-11** (*wa jaʿalnā nawmakum subātā wa jaʿalna'l-layla libāsā wa jaʿalna'n-nahāra maʿāshā*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference here must be recorded:** in al-Nabaʾ the day was named *maʿāsh* (livelihood); **in al-Furqān it is called *nushūr* (spreading out / resurrection).** **And *nushūr* is at the same time the name of the resurrection** — that is, the day is named as a small resurrection repeated every morning.

## 25/51-52 — وَلَوْ شِئْنَا لَبَعَثْنَا فِى كُلِّ قَرْيَةٍ نَّذِيرًا · فَلَا تُطِعِ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ وَجَٰهِدْهُم بِهِۦ جِهَادًا كَبِيرًا

"**Had We willed, We would have sent a warner to every town. So do not obey those who disbelieve, and strive against them with it, with a great striving.**"

### وَجَٰهِدْهُم بِهِ

**The pronoun in the sentence must be recorded: *bihī* — "with it".**

**The lexicographers generally give the Qurʾān as the referent of the pronoun** — and indeed its nearest referent is the book that has been spoken of since the thirty-second verse.

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is that a pronoun goes to its nearest referent:** what is shown as the instrument of the struggle is **the text itself**. **And the qualifier is kept large: *jihādan kabīrā*.**

**The root ج-ه-د was treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/6 and 29/69** (to expend strength, to go to the end of one's capacity) — it was set out in a table there that the root binds the two ends of that sūra. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And its connection with the fifty-first verse is worth recording:** that a separate warner was not sent to every town enlarges **the scope of a single warner's effort**. **Read together, the two verses make the ground for the qualifier "a great striving" visible.** I give this as my own reading.

## 25/53-54 — وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى مَرَجَ ٱلْبَحْرَيْنِ هَٰذَا عَذْبٌ فُرَاتٌ وَهَٰذَا مِلْحٌ أُجَاجٌ وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَهُمَا بَرْزَخًا وَحِجْرًا مَّحْجُورًا

"**It is He who let the two seas run free: this one is sweet and quenches thirst, that one is salt and bitter. Between the two He has set a barrier, a boundary not to be crossed.**"

**مَرَجَ** — root **م-ر-ج**: **to let go, to set free** (as one lets an animal loose in pasture); and **to mix.** **The lexicographers record both branches of the sense.**

**The same verb occurred at `055-rahman.md` 55/19-20** (*maraja'l-baḥrayni yaltaqiyān · baynahumā barzakhun lā yabghiyān*) and was treated there. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And at `035-fatir.md` 35/12 the same two waters had been named with the words *ujāj* and *ʿadhb furāt*;** there they were compared with al-Wāqiʿa 56/70. **Three sūras use the same phenomenon within three different frames:**

| Place | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| al-Raḥmān 55/19-20 | **Meeting and boundary** — *yaltaqiyān* / *barzakh* |
| Fāṭir 35/12 | **Not being equal, and a shared benefit** |
| **al-Furqān 25/53** | **Letting go, and a boundary not to be crossed** — *ḥijran maḥjūrā* |

**بَرْزَخ** — **the veil between two things, the zone that lies between.** **In the index the word is analysed here for the first time** (if it occurred in al-Raḥmān, the note there is relied on): the lexicographers state that the word is **a separator which comes between two things and prevents them from mixing.**

**حِجْرًا مَّحْجُورًا** — **two words from the same root** (ح-ج-ر): "a forbidden forbidding", that is, **absolutely impassable.** In Arabic this repetition states emphasis.

**The root was treated in `049-hucurat.md` on the occasion of the sūra's name** (to forbid, to separate; *ḥijr*, *ḥajar*, *ḥujra*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ مِنَ ٱلْمَآءِ بَشَرًا فَجَعَلَهُۥ نَسَبًا وَصِهْرًا** (54) — "**It is He who created a human being from water and gave him ties of lineage and of marriage.**"

**Two bonds must be recorded:** *nasab* — **kinship that comes by birth**; *ṣihr* — **kinship established by marriage.**

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the order of the verses:** the preceding verse described **two waters that do not mix**; this verse describes **two lines that are joined.** **The two verses stand side by side, the one ordering separation and the other union.**

## 25/55-57 — وَيَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا يَنفَعُهُمْ وَلَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَكَانَ ٱلْكَافِرُ عَلَىٰ رَبِّهِۦ ظَهِيرًا · وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَٰكَ إِلَّا مُبَشِّرًا وَنَذِيرًا · قُلْ مَآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ مِنْ أَجْرٍ إِلَّا مَن شَآءَ أَن يَتَّخِذَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِۦ سَبِيلًا

"**Setting God aside, they serve what can neither profit nor harm them**… **We sent you only as a bringer of good news and as a warner.** Say: '**I ask you no wage for this; only, let whoever wills take a way to his Lord.**'"

**ظَهِير** — root **ظ-ه-ر**: **the back; one who supports, who backs another up.**

**The lexicographers differ over the meaning of the construct:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | The one who disbelieves backs up (Satan, or falsehood) **against his Lord** |
| 2 | *Ẓahīr* here means "cast behind the back, disregarded" |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

### The form of the exception

**The fifty-seventh verse, in refusing the demand for a wage, sets up an exception, and the content of the exception is worth recording:**

*Illā man shāʾa an yattakhidha ilā rabbihī sabīlā* — **"only that whoever wills should take a way to his Lord."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the kind of thing the exception is:** what is asked for is **not something that leaves the hand of the one who gives it.** What is counted as the wage is to the benefit of the one who pays it. **That is, the exception, having refused the demand, turns it into a different kind of thing.**

**The same structure was treated at `042-sura.md` 42/23 (*lā asʾalukum ʿalayhi ajran illa'l-mawaddata fi'l-qurbā*), and the disagreement was set out in a table there.** I rely on that treatment; **both verses use the same pattern (*lā asʾalukum … illā*).**

**وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَٰكَ إِلَّا مُبَشِّرًا وَنَذِيرًا** — **two attributes: a bringer of good news and a warner.** **The same pair was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/12** (*li-yundhira … wa bushrā*): the same text does two distinct jobs according to the one addressed. **And the limit is drawn by these two jobs** — **a link in the line of responsibility set out in a table at `028-kasas.md` 28/56.**

## 25/58-60 — وَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱلْحَىِّ ٱلَّذِى لَا يَمُوتُ … ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ فَٱسْـَٔلْ بِهِۦ خَبِيرًا · وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ ٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلرَّحْمَٰنِ قَالُوا۟ وَمَا ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ

"**Put your trust in the Living who does not die**… **He is al-Raḥmān; ask one who knows about Him.** When they are told '**Prostrate yourselves to al-Raḥmān**', they say, '**And what is al-Raḥmān?**'"

**ٱلْحَىِّ ٱلَّذِى لَا يَمُوتُ** — the name *al-Ḥayy* was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/255 (*al-Ḥayyu'l-Qayyūm*). **The addition here must be recorded: *alladhī lā yamūt* — the attribute is reinforced by a negation.**

**And the choice of this attribute as the ground of trust is worth recording:** that what is trusted should be **lasting**. I give this as an observation: **the measure of the place one leans on is given not as its strength but as its duration.**

### وَمَا ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ — what the objection aims at

**The objection in the sixtieth verse is worth recording: the opposing side asks not about the prostration but about the name.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the form of the question:** *wa ma'r-Raḥmān* — "And what is al-Raḥmān?" **The question is built with *mā* (what), not with *man* (who).**

**In this part of the sūra the name *al-Raḥmān* gathers thickly** (59, 60, 63), **and in the sixty-third verse the description of the servants of that name will begin.** **That is, the sūra takes the name that has been refused and answers by describing its servants.** This is a structure that can be verified within the sūra.

**وَزَادَهُمْ نُفُورًا** — *nufūr*: **to take fright and flee.** **The root was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/42**, and it was recorded there: **the warning does not remain without effect; it has its effect in the opposite direction.** I rely on that treatment.

## 25/61-62 — تَبَارَكَ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ بُرُوجًا وَجَعَلَ فِيهَا سِرَٰجًا وَقَمَرًا مُّنِيرًا · وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ ٱلَّيْلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ خِلْفَةً لِّمَنْ أَرَادَ أَن يَذَّكَّرَ أَوْ أَرَادَ شُكُورًا

"**Blessed is He who set constellations in the sky and placed in it a lamp and an illuminating moon!** And it is He who made the night and the day **follow one after the other**: **for whoever wishes to take heed or wishes to give thanks.**"

**بُرُوج** — **treated in `085-buruc.md` on the occasion of the sūra's name** (the root's sense of "a visible, high structure"). **I do not repeat it here.**

**خِلْفَة** — root **خ-ل-ف**: **to come one after another, the one taking the other's place.** **The root was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/39** (*khalāʾif*).

**And the ground the sentence gives must be recorded: *li-man arāda an yadhdhakkara aw arāda shukūrā*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the repetition of the verb:** *arāda* (he willed) occurs **twice** — once for remembering, once for thanks. **That is, the alternation of night and day is offered as an opportunity: an order in which one who has missed it can make it up.** And both verbs state **will**: the order works the same for everyone, and the benefit from it is tied to wanting it.

## 25/63-67 — وَعِبَادُ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلَّذِينَ يَمْشُونَ عَلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ هَوْنًا وَإِذَا خَاطَبَهُمُ ٱلْجَٰهِلُونَ قَالُوا۟ سَلَٰمًا

"**The servants of al-Raḥmān are those who walk upon the earth in humility; and when the ignorant address them, they say, 'peace'.**"

### The opening of the list: walking

**From the sixty-third verse to the end the sūra gives a list of attributes**, and **the first item on the list is walking.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the seventh verse of the sūra:** there the objection raised against the Prophet was *wa yamshī fi'l-aswāq* (he walks about in the markets). **The sūra takes the very act the objection was about and makes it the first attribute of the servants of al-Raḥmān.** **And in the twentieth verse that same act had been set down as the rule for all messengers.**

| Verse | *Mashy* (walking) | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | *Yamshī fi'l-aswāq* | **Objection** — counted a defect in a messenger |
| 20 | *Yamshūna fi'l-aswāq* | **Rule** — all messengers |
| **63** | *Yamshūna ʿala'l-arḍi **hawnā*** | **Attribute** — the servants of al-Raḥmān |

**Three occurrences, one verb.** This is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.

**هَوْنًا** — root **ه-و-ن**: **softness, gravity of bearing; and lightness.** The lexicographers explain the word here as **a walk without arrogance, a calm walk.**

**The same field was treated at `031-lokman.md` 31/18-19** (*lā tamshi fi'l-arḍi maraḥā · wa'qṣid fī mashyik*). **Two sūras tie walking to a measure: the one as a prohibition, the other as an attribute.**

**قَالُوا۟ سَلَٰمًا** — **the same stance was treated at `028-kasas.md` 28/55** (*salāmun ʿalaykum lā nabtaghi'l-jāhilīn*), and the three-part answer was set out in a table there. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the difference here must be recorded: the answer has come down to a single word.** In al-Qaṣaṣ there were three clauses; **in al-Furqān only *salāmā*.**

### The list continues

| Verse | Attribute |
|---|---|
| 64 | Passing the night in prostration and in standing |
| 65-66 | Asking to be kept from the punishment of Hell |
| **67** | *Wa'lladhīna idhā anfaqū lam yusrifū wa lam yaqturū wa kāna bayna dhālika **qawāmā*** |

**The sixty-seventh verse is worth recording:** in spending, **both extremes** are refused at once — *isrāf* (going beyond the measure) and *iqtār* (stinting).

The root **ق-ت-ر**: **to narrow, to stint.** **The root occurred in `080-abese.md` (*wa wujūhun yawmaʾidhin ʿalayhā ghabaratun tarhaquhā qataratun*) in a different branch.**

**قَوَام** — root **ق-و-م**: **that which stands upright, that which holds the balance.** **The word states the point between the two extremes.**

**The root *qaṣd* (keeping to the middle way) was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/32 and at `031-lokman.md` 31/19, 31/32.** **This verse gives the same measure with a different root**, and it is read together with the measure set down at `002-bakara.md` 2/219 (*quli'l-ʿafw* — what is surplus to need).

## 25/68-71 — وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَدْعُونَ مَعَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَٰهًا ءَاخَرَ وَلَا يَقْتُلُونَ ٱلنَّفْسَ ٱلَّتِى حَرَّمَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَلَا يَزْنُونَ … إِلَّا مَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ عَمَلًا صَٰلِحًا فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ حَسَنَٰتٍ

"**They do not call upon another god together with God; they do not kill the soul God has made inviolable, save by right; and they do not commit fornication**… **Except for the one who repents, believes and does a righteous deed: for those, God changes their evil deeds into good ones.**"

### Three prohibitions and a single door

**Three grave acts are enumerated, and an exception follows them.** And the scope of the exception must be recorded: **it covers all three.**

**يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ حَسَنَٰتٍ** — "**He changes their evil deeds into good ones.**"

The root **ب-د-ل**: to put one thing in the place of another. **The root was treated at `056-vakia.md` 56/61** (*an nubaddila amthālakum*).

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence does not speak of **erasing** — it speaks of **exchanging.** That is, the past is not lifted away; **the column it is entered in is changed.**

**And the same line was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/53** (*lā taqnaṭū min raḥmati'llāhi inna'llāha yaghfiru'dh-dhunūba jamīʿā*). **The two verses are read together: the one forbids despair, the other states what the return receives in exchange.**

**وَمَن تَابَ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًا فَإِنَّهُۥ يَتُوبُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ مَتَابًا** (71) — **and the return itself is reinforced once more, by the absolute object.**

## 25/72-76 — وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَشْهَدُونَ ٱلزُّورَ وَإِذَا مَرُّوا۟ بِٱللَّغْوِ مَرُّوا۟ كِرَامًا · وَٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِّرُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِمْ لَمْ يَخِرُّوا۟ عَلَيْهَا صُمًّا وَعُمْيَانًا · وَٱلَّذِينَ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا هَبْ لَنَا مِنْ أَزْوَٰجِنَا وَذُرِّيَّٰتِنَا قُرَّةَ أَعْيُنٍ وَٱجْعَلْنَا لِلْمُتَّقِينَ إِمَامًا

"**They do not bear witness to falsehood; and when they pass by idle talk, they pass by with dignity.** When the signs of their Lord are recalled to them, they do not **fall down upon them deaf and blind.** And they say: '**Our Lord! Grant us, of our spouses and our offspring, a coolness of the eyes; and make us a leader for those who guard themselves.**'"

### مَرُّوا۟ كِرَامًا

**The root ل-غ-و was analysed at `056-vakia.md` 56/25**, and it occurred at `041-fussilet.md` 41/26 as a tactic and at `028-kasas.md` 28/55 as a response. **The fourth link is here.**

**And the verb here is worth recording: *marrū kirāmā* — "they passed by with dignity".**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse describes neither stopping nor quarrelling. **A qualifier is set beside the verb "to pass":** *kirām.* **That is, the matter is how one passes by idle talk.**

### لَمْ يَخِرُّوا۟ عَلَيْهَا صُمًّا وَعُمْيَانًا

**The build of the sentence is striking, and the lexicographers dwell on it:** what is negated is not **falling down** — it is **falling down deaf and blind.**

**That is, the verse does not refuse falling prostrate before the signs;** it enters the qualifier *ṣumman wa ʿumyānā*. **I record this as an observation about the word order: the acceptance is granted, but *how* it is made is laid down as a condition.**

### وَٱجْعَلْنَا لِلْمُتَّقِينَ إِمَامًا

**إِمَام** — **this word was treated at `028-kasas.md` 28/5 and 28/41**, and it was set out in a table there that the same word is used in two opposite directions (the weak being made leaders / leaders who call to the fire). **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference here must be recorded: it is inside a prayer that the word occurs** — what is asked for is leadership. **And a qualifier is entered: *li'l-muttaqīn* — a leader for those who guard themselves.**

**I record this as my own reading:** what is asked for is not a position but **a particular function within a particular community.** It was recorded in al-Qaṣaṣ that leadership on its own states no value; **this prayer carries that qualification within itself.**

**قُرَّةَ أَعْيُنٍ** — **"a coolness of the eyes".** The lexicographers explain the idiom by **the eye's coming to rest in one place and not straying elsewhere**: *qarra* — to settle, to become fixed. **That is, the eye's settling in peace upon one place.**

## 25/77 — قُلْ مَا يَعْبَؤُا۟ بِكُمْ رَبِّى لَوْلَا دُعَآؤُكُمْ

"Say: '**Were it not for your calling upon Him, why would my Lord give you any weight?**'"

**يَعْبَؤُا۟** — root **ع-ب-أ**: **to give weight to a thing, to take it into account.** The lexicographers record the word with the senses "to load a burden, to attach importance".

**The lexicographers differ over how the verse is to be understood:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Were it not for your calling upon Him**, He would give you no weight — the calling is the cause of the value |
| 2 | *Duʿāʾ* here means **service** |
| 3 | *Law lā* here does not mean "were it not for" but states a reproach |

**I transmit the disagreement; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the sūra's closing with this sentence is worth recording:** the sūra had opened with objections (4-8) — **the ordinariness of the messenger, the expectation of wealth and of an angel.** **At the close, the worth of a human being is tied not to anything he possesses but to his *calling*.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the two ends of the sūra.**

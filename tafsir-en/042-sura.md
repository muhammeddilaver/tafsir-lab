# 42. Sūrat al-Shūrā

Fifty-three verses. Meccan. It takes its name from a single word in verse 38: **وَأَمْرُهُمْ شُورَىٰ بَيْنَهُمْ** — *wa amruhum shūrā baynahum*, "their affair is consultation among themselves."

One thing about this name must be recorded at the outset, because it governs the expectation one brings to the sūra: **the word occurs in this one place alone in the Qurʾān**, it occurs not inside a command but in the middle of **a list of attributes**, and in the sentence where it occurs there is not even a verb. The sūra does not say "consult"; **it describes a community that consults.** The difference looks small but is not, and it will be taken up separately at verse 38.

The subject of the sūra is wider than its name, and can be summed up in one word: **division.** From beginning to end the sūra is occupied with the splitting apart of people — but it does this not as a complaint but as **a diagnosis**:

- Why do people divide? (13-14)
- When does division begin — before knowledge or after? (14)
- Once divided, how is the dispute closed? (15-16)
- How does a divided community manage its own affairs? (38)
- What does the one who is wronged do — repay, or pardon? (39-43)

**And nowhere is the sūra's answer "let all of you be the same."** The eighth verse says so outright: *"Had God willed, He would have made them a single community."* Plurality, then, is not an accident. The problem is not in the plurality but in the plurality's turning into **a rupture**.

### The three spines of the sūra

What follows are facts that can be counted from the text; the claim that they form an order is my own reading, and that will be marked separately.

**First: the ring of revelation.** Three times the sūra names revelation with the same pattern, and the three stand at three separate places in the text:

| Verse | Expression | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *kadhālika **yūḥī** ilayka wa ila'lladhīna min qablik* | The opening |
| **7** | *wa kadhālika **awḥaynā** ilayka qurʾānan ʿarabiyyā* | End of the first block |
| **52** | *wa kadhālika **awḥaynā** ilayka rūḥan min amrinā* | The close |

**All three begin with the same demonstrative: *kadhālika* — "thus".** The sūra opens with revelation, closes with revelation, and in the middle turns back to revelation once more.

**Second: the ring of dominion.** The first sentence of the fourth verse comes back **word for word** in the fifty-third:

| Verse | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **4** | *Lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ* |
| **53** | *…alladhī **lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ*** |

**This is not a repetition but a frame:** the same sentence stands at the beginning and at the end of the sūra, and everything that passes between remains inside it.

**Third: the ring of "affairs".** The fifteenth verse ends with *wa ilayhi'l-**maṣīr***; the fifty-third with *alā ila'llāhi **taṣīru'l-umūr***. The same root (ص-ي-ر), one a noun and one a verb. And in between, in verse 43, comes *ʿazmi'l-**umūr***. **The sūra's word for "affair" (*amr*), then, does three different pieces of work in three different places:** the affair people have among themselves (38), the affair that calls for resolve (43), and in the end the affair to which everything returns (53).

## The structure of the sūra

The sūra can be divided into ten blocks. Most of the block boundaries are marked either by a change of subject or by a pair of names.

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-6 | The letters, the source of revelation, the splitting of the heavens, the angels' asking forgiveness | *wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl* (6) |
| **II** | 7-9 | An Arabic Qurʾān, the mother of towns, the day of gathering, two parties | *ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr* (9) |
| **III** | 10-12 | Where disagreement is referred, the splitter and the maker, a sentence of transcendence, the keys of provision | *bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm* (12) |
| **IV** | 13-16 | The law, five prophets, the cause of division, standing straight, the closing of the dispute | *ʿadhābun shadīd* (16) |
| **V** | 17-19 | The Book and the balance, the nearness of the Hour, being subtle | *al-qawiyyu'l-ʿazīz* (19) |
| **VI** | 20-23 | Two harvests, an unlicensed law, two fears, the question of reward | *ghafūrun shakūr* (23) |
| **VII** | 24-28 | The charge of fabrication, the acceptance of repentance, the measure of provision, rain | *al-waliyyu'l-ḥamīd* (28) |
| **VIII** | 29-35 | Living creatures, affliction and pardon, ships, the stilling of the wind | *mā lahum min maḥīṣ* (35) |
| **IX** | 36-43 | The eight attributes of the believer, **shūrā**, the balance of repayment and pardon | *la-min ʿazmi'l-umūr* (43) |
| **X** | 44-53 | The outcome of going astray, being brought to the punishment, the call, the limit of conveying, children, the three ways of revelation | *taṣīru'l-umūr* (53) |

### The verse-ending

Looking at the ends of the fifty-three verses, a dominant pattern of sound appears: *ḥakīm, ʿaẓīm, raḥīm, wakīl, saʿīr, naṣīr, qadīr, unīb, baṣīr, ʿalīm, yunīb, murīb, maṣīr, shadīd, qarīb, baʿīd, ʿazīz, naṣīb, alīm, kabīr, shakūr, ṣudūr, tafʿalūn, shadīd, baṣīr, ḥamīd, qadīr, kathīr, naṣīr, aʿlām, shakūr, kathīr, maḥīṣ, yatawakkalūn, yaghfirūn, yunfiqūn, yantaṣirūn, ẓālimīn, sabīl, alīm, umūr, sabīl, khafiyy, muqīm, sabīl, nakīr, kafūr, al-dhukūr, ʿaqīman ʿalīmun qadīr, ʿaliyyun ḥakīm, mustaqīm, al-umūr.*

**The dominant pattern is the *faʿīl* form**, accompanied by *faʿūl* (*shakūr, kafūr, ḥamīd*) and by a few verb endings. **There is no order without exception** — it is not as in Sūrat Qāf, where all forty-five verses sit on the same sound. What is to be recorded is this: **the sound of this sūra is looser and longer-breathed than the sound of Qāf**, and this fits the length of its verses. In al-Shūrā there are long compound sentences; a short-breathed closing sound would not have sat on such sentences.

**This is an observation; no claim of calculation or number is being made.**

### The occasion of revelation and the historical setting

On the sūra's being Meccan there is a widespread agreement among the sources. **Reports are transmitted to the effect that certain verses (particularly 23-25 and 39-41) were revealed in Medina; since these carry no certainty I impose no preference here.**

The sūra is the third of the seven that open with *ḥā-mīm* (**al-ḥawāmīm**). What this group has in common — that the opening is followed immediately by mention of the Book or of revelation — was recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`. **So it is in al-Shūrā:** the third verse begins with *kadhālika yūḥī ilayk*.

**Points that support the Meccan character of the content:**

- Those addressed are **the mushrikūn**, and the argument is about tawḥīd and the source of revelation (9, 21, 24).
- A **limit of responsibility** is drawn for the Prophet again and again (6, 48) — this is the language of a period in which there is as yet no government and no established order.
- **Fighting (*qitāl*) does not occur.** The thirty-ninth verse speaks of answering a wrong, but what is at issue is an individual's taking of his right, not war.
- The People of the Book are named only once, and then at a distance (14).

**And this matters for the thirty-eighth verse.** The word *shūrā* comes as the attribute of a small community under pressure, one that has as yet no state, no army and no treasury. **The verse, that is, does not describe a form of government; it describes how a community works within itself.** This distinction will be treated in detail at verse 38.

The basmala was treated in earlier sections; the verses are numbered without it.

## 42/1-2 — حمٓ · عٓسٓقٓ

*Ḥā-mīm · ʿAyn-sīn-qāf*

"Ḥā-mīm. ʿAyn-sīn-qāf."

### First the general note — and it will not be repeated

**The full examination of the disconnected letters was made in `002-bakara.md` in the section on 2/1; it was repeated once more, and more heavily, in `050-kaf.md` at 50/1. I do not write it out again here.** Its summary is this:

- **The meaning of these letters is not known with certainty.** This is what must be said honestly and at the outset.
- One group of scholars refers the meaning to God (*tafwīḍ*); this is not a confession of ignorance but a choice to keep silent where the text gives no explanation.
- Among the views that do seek an explanation, the one that finds most support from within the text is the explanation that the letters, by pointing to **the raw material of the Qurʾān**, make the challenge meaningful — because most of the letter-groups are followed immediately by an expression that speaks of **the Book**.
- **And, as was written plainly there: no abjad, no counting of letters, no numerical calculation is done on these letters. That road is not taken in this commentary.**

**The one difference in al-Shūrā is this, and it is the only thing I treat: the splitting of the sequence in two.**

### The only disconnected-letter sequence in the Qurʾān that comes in two lines

This is a verifiable fact of the muṣḥaf and is peculiar to al-Shūrā.

Twenty-nine sūras in the Qurʾān open with letters. In these sūras the standing of the letters in the numbering of verses is of two kinds, and both can be verified from the muṣḥaf: **in some the letters count as a verse on their own** (*alif-lām-mīm* — al-Baqara 2/1; *ḥā-mīm* — al-Aḥqāf 46/1; *kāf-hā-yā-ʿayn-ṣād* — Maryam 19/1), **and in others the letters form a single verse together with the sentence that follows them** (*alif-lām-rā* — Yūnus 10/1; *qāf* — Qāf 50/1). **But in both cases the sequence of letters is not divided: however many the letters, they are read in one place, one after another.** *Kāf-hā-yā-ʿayn-ṣād* **is five letters and is not divided.**

**In al-Shūrā, however, five letters are divided into two separate verses:**

| | Maryam 19/1 | **al-Shūrā 42/1-2** |
|---|---|---|
| Number of letters | **Five**: كٓهيعٓصٓ | **Five**: حمٓ · عٓسٓقٓ |
| Number of verses | **One** | **Two** |
| Its arrangement | A single line | **Two lines** |

**The point, then, is not the number of the letters but that they are divided.** Another sequence of the same length is not divided; this one is.

**And one further detail must be recorded:** in six of the seven sūras that open with *ḥā-mīm*, the opening is a single *ḥā-mīm* and that is one verse. **Al-Shūrā is the sole exception within this group too** — because a second sequence follows the *ḥā-mīm*.

### What do we know about the reason for this division?

**There is no certain knowledge.** I am obliged to write this plainly, because this is one of the details about which most claims are produced, and most of the claims rest on letter-calculation — the road not taken in this commentary.

I give the explanations transmitted in the classical sources **not as a list of preferences but as an inventory**:

| Explanation | What it says | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **A division based on transmission (*tawqīfī*)** | The division of verses rests on the tradition of recitation and numbering; no reason is sought | Consistent with the general rule that verse boundaries are fixed by transmission. But it does not answer the question "why" |
| **Two sequences, two separate units** | *Ḥā-mīm* is the common opening of the group, *ʿayn-sīn-qāf* an addition peculiar to this sūra | Consistent with the textual data: *ḥā-mīm* is shared by seven sūras, *ʿayn-sīn-qāf* occurs only here. **But this is not an explanation; it is a restatement of the fact** |
| **A requirement of the verse-ending (*fāṣila*)** | Since the sounds of the two sequences differ, they were counted separately | In other sūras too there are differences of sound and there is no division there; so this is not sufficient |
| **Meanings assigned to the letters** | It is said that each letter points to a name or a word | As recorded in `002-bakara.md`, this is not systematic; applied to all the groups, the same method does not give consistent results |

**And what must be written above all of them is this: none of the explanations above suffices to say "this is the reason for the division."**

### What can be recorded

**I give this as an observation, as my own reading, and it is not binding:**

The one **measurable** effect the division produces is in the rhythm of recitation. Had it been a single verse, the five letters would have been read in one breath. Because they are two verses, there is a place to pause between them. **In its first two verses the sūra brings the reader to a stop twice.**

And then comes the third verse: *kadhālika yūḥī ilayka wa ila'lladhīna min qablik* — "thus does He reveal to you and to those before you." **The first thing that comes after the letters, that is, belongs once again to the field of revelation, in keeping with the pattern set out in `002-bakara.md`.** This says nothing on its own about the meaning of the letters; what it says is **what the letters attach to.**

**I go no further than this.**

## 42/3 — كَذَٰلِكَ يُوحِىٓ إِلَيْكَ وَإِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ

*Kadhālika yūḥī ilayka wa ila'lladhīna min qablika'llāhu'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm*

"Thus does He reveal to you and to those before you — God, the Mighty, the Wise."

### كَذَٰلِكَ — "thus"

**The sentence begins with a demonstrative, and what it points to is not stated.** *Kadhālika* — "like this", "thus". Like what?

The chief answers the exegetes give:

| Reading | What *kadhālika* points to |
|---|---|
| 1 | **To this sūra itself** — "just as you are now reading" |
| 2 | **To the revelation that came to earlier prophets** — "as it was to them" |
| 3 | **To a manner left unspecified** — to the modality of revelation; a modality that will be explained in verse 51 |

**The third reading is striking in point of arrangement, and I record it:** by saying *kadhālika* the sūra sets up a "thus", but does not say what the "thus" is until the fifty-first verse. In the fifty-first verse the three ways of revelation will be enumerated. **The sūra, that is, closes in the fifty-second verse the question it opened in the third** — and the fifty-second verse begins with the same word: *wa kadhālika awḥaynā ilayka.*

**I give this as my own reading; its ground is that the two verses open with the same word and that verse 51 comes between them.**

### يُوحِىٓ — an imperfect verb

**The root و-ح-ي was treated in detail in `053-necm.md`; I do not repeat it.** The finding there was this: the concrete sense of the root is **swift and hidden communication** — *speed* and *concealment*. And the word is not used only of the communication that comes to prophets; Zechariah's (Zakariyyā's) sign, what the bee knows, and the whispering of the satans are named with the same root.

**What belongs here is the form of the verb.** *Yūḥī* is imperfect — it conveys continuity and present time. Had it been perfect (*awḥā*) it would have described a finished act.

**And this takes on meaning when compared with the sūra's other two sentences about revelation:**

| Verse | Verb | Form | Agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3** | *yūḥī* | **Imperfect** — going on | *Allāhu'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm* (explicitly) |
| **7** | *awḥaynā* | **Perfect** — done | *We* (plural of majesty) |
| **52** | *awḥaynā* | **Perfect** — done | *We* |

**The third verse describes the process, the seventh and fifty-second the event.** And in the first the agent comes with its explicit name, in the other two with a pronoun. **I record this observation about the arrangement:** the sūra first sets down the owner of the act by name, then passes to a pronoun.

### وَإِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكَ — "and to those before you"

**This is the most important part of the sentence, and it prepares the whole argument of the sūra.**

The objection of those addressed — the objection this sūra addresses — is the objection that the revelation is **new** and **strange**. The answer is given in a few words: *wa ila'lladhīna min qablik.*

**What has come, that is, is not an exception but the continuation of a series.**

**This is the place where the same matter is said in a different word in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/9:** *mā kuntu bidʿan mina'r-rusul* — "I am no novelty among the messengers." There the word was *bidʿ* (that which has no precedent); here the pattern is a conjunction: *wa ilā.*

| | al-Aḥqāf 46/9 | al-Shūrā 42/3 |
|---|---|---|
| Who says it | **The Prophet** (with the command *qul*) | **The text itself** |
| How it is built | A negation: "I am no novelty" | An addition: "and to those before" |
| What it does | **Attaches** the claim to the chain | **Continues** the chain |

**And in the thirteenth verse this chain will be named:** Noah (Nūḥ), Abraham (Ibrāhīm), Moses (Mūsā), Jesus (ʿĪsā). The third verse prepares that list.

### ٱللَّهُ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ — a point of word order

**The agent of the sentence has been left to the end.** In an Arabic verbal sentence the ordinary order is verb-then-agent; here *ilayka wa ila'lladhīna min qablik* has come in between, and the agent comes last of all. And when it comes it does not come alone: it comes with two names.

**ٱلْعَزِيز** — root **ع-ز-ز**: to be firm, unassailable, superior. **ٱلْحَكِيم** — root **ح-ك-م**: to restrain and hold in place. **Both were treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/2**; the note there was this: one conveys **power**, the other **rightness of placement**; power is not named on its own.

**What belongs here is this addition, and I give it as my own reading:** compared with the pair of names at the end of the sūra, these two names form a line. The fifty-first verse ends with *innahu **ʿaliyyun ḥakīm***. **In both sentences the second name is the same (*ḥakīm*), the first has changed: *ʿazīz* → *ʿaliyy*.** In describing the source of revelation, then, the quality that stays constant is "rightness of placement", while the quality that changes shifts from "unassailability" to "height".

**This is an observation; I make no claim of a deliberate choice.**

## 42/4 — لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۖ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْعَظِيمُ

*Lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ, wa huwa'l-ʿaliyyu'l-ʿaẓīm*

"To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. He is the High, the Great."

### The arrangement of the sentence: *lahu* is fronted

**The ordinary order would have been "mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍi lahu".** The sentence is not built that way: the prepositional phrase (*lahu*) has been brought forward.

**In Arabic the fronting of an element (*taqdīm*) conveys specification:** "His alone". The same structure was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/2 and 57/5 (*lahu mulku's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*), and what was recorded there was this: the repetition is itself a piece of work.

**In al-Shūrā this sentence occurs twice — in the fourth verse and the fifty-third — with fifty verses in between.** As recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra, this is a frame.

**And there is a difference between the two occurrences, and it too must be recorded:**

| | 42/4 | 42/53 |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence | *Lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ* | *…alladhī lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ* |
| Its position | **An independent sentence** | **A relative clause** — "the path of God, which…" |
| What it attaches to | A statement standing on its own | *Ṣirāṭ Allāh* — a path |

**The same sentence, then, is first a statement and afterwards the description of a path.** I record this as an observation about the arrangement.

### ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْعَظِيمُ

**ٱلْعَلِىّ** — root **ع-ل-و**: height. **ٱلْعَظِيم** — root **ع-ظ-م**: greatness; the lexicographers relate the root to *ʿaẓm* (bone) — the largeness of a thing's skeleton, of its frame.

**These two names come side by side once more in the Qurʾān, and that place is the last sentence of the Verse of the Throne:** *wa huwa'l-ʿaliyyu'l-ʿaẓīm* (al-Baqara 2/255). **The structure of that verse was tabulated in `002-bakara.md`**, and what was recorded there was this: the verse widens from the inside outward — from the Essence to the attribute, from the attribute to dominion, from dominion to compass — **and closes at last with two names.**

**In al-Shūrā the same pair of names does the same work, but its place is different:** in al-Baqara it stands at **the end** of a long series, in al-Shūrā immediately **after** a single sentence. In al-Baqara the names are the summary of an accumulating description; here they are the seal of a single sentence about ownership.

**I record this as my own reading:** the shared work of the two names is to **separate** ownership from possession. The sentence "everything is His", standing alone, could place the owner on the same plane as what is owned — as a man has a field and stands beside the field. *ʿAliyy* breaks that plane: the owner is above and outside what is owned. **And this prepares for the eleventh verse, which comes seven verses later.**

---

## 42/5 — تَكَادُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ يَتَفَطَّرْنَ مِن فَوْقِهِنَّ ۚ وَٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّهِمْ وَيَسْتَغْفِرُونَ لِمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۗ أَلَآ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

*Takādu's-samāwātu yatafaṭṭarna min fawqihinna, wa'l-malāʾikatu yusabbiḥūna bi-ḥamdi rabbihim wa yastaghfirūna li-man fi'l-arḍ; alā inna'llāha huwa'l-ghafūru'r-raḥīm*

"The heavens are almost split apart above them. And the angels glorify their Lord with praise and ask forgiveness for those on the earth. Know well that God is the Forgiving, the Merciful."

### تَكَادُ — "almost"

***Kāda* in Arabic is one of the verbs of approximation (*afʿāl al-muqāraba*): it conveys that an act has come very close to happening but has not happened.**

**And this is the most important word in the sentence.** The verse does not say "the heavens split"; it says "they are on the point of splitting". **What is described, that is, is not an event but a tension.**

### يَتَفَطَّرْنَ — root ف-ط-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to split, to open lengthwise.** The root was treated in the corpus in the sections `082-infitar.md` and `084-insikak.md`; I do not repeat it. Its summary: *faṭr* is a thing's being split open; *ifṭār* (breaking a fast) and *fiṭra* (the first splitting, the first constitution) are from the same root.

**What belongs here is the root's second occurrence in this sūra.**

| Verse | Word | Who / what |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | *yatafaṭṭarna* — **on the point of splitting** | The heavens |
| **11** | *fāṭiru's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* — **the splitter, the first to split open** | God |

**The same root, six verses apart, in two opposite positions: what is split and who splits.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text. **I do not claim that it is a deliberate choice; what I record is that the root has been used at both ends within this sūra.**

**A note on the readings:** in some readings the word is read *yanfaṭirna* (Form VII). **The difference in meaning is not great** — one is "to be split piece by piece" (Form V, intensive), the other "to be split" (the reflexive, *muṭāwaʿa*). I record it, but since it does not change the meaning I do not dwell on it.

### مِن فَوْقِهِنَّ — "from above them"

**There is disagreement over this expression, and it turns on two questions: above what, and why?**

| Question | Views |
|---|---|
| **Above what?** | (a) Above the heavens themselves — that is, downward from the highest heaven; (b) Above the earth, that is, over those on the earth |
| **Why?** | (a) **Because of the mushrikūn's words** — from the weight of associating partners; (b) **Out of majesty** — from the awe of God's greatness |

**I do not impose a preference.** But a comparison can be made from within the Qurʾān, and that comparison appears to support the first option of the second question:

> *"The heavens are almost split **by it**, the earth split open and the mountains fall down in ruin — that they ascribed a child to al-Raḥmān."* (Maryam 19/90-91)

**There the cause is stated plainly: an ascription.** In al-Shūrā the cause is not stated. **And its not being stated is a choice of arrangement:** in al-Shūrā's context, immediately before this the source of revelation (3) and the owner of dominion (4) were set out; immediately after it will come the angels' asking of forgiveness. **The sentence, that is, stands between two things and can be attached to both.**

**As my own reading: by not stating the cause the verse leaves the tension inside the sentence itself.** In Maryam the cause is given and so the tension is resolved; here it is not given, and so it hangs.

### وَٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ يُسَبِّحُونَ … وَيَسْتَغْفِرُونَ لِمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ

**The second half of the sentence sets up a contrast with the first, and the contrast is striking.**

| | The upper half | The lower half |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | **The heavens** | **The angels** |
| State | On the point of splitting — **tension** | Glorification and asking forgiveness — **continuity** |
| Direction | From above downward | **Upward, for those below** |

**In the same sentence, then, there is both an overflowing tension and a work that calms it.** And the direction of the calming work must be recorded: the angels ask forgiveness not for themselves but **for those on the earth.**

**The pattern *sabbaḥa bi-ḥamdihi* was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/1:** *tasbīḥ* is a putting away (declaring free of deficiency), *ḥamd* is a drawing near (praise in return for a favour). Their coming together gathers two directions into one act.

**لِمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ — "for those on the earth".** The expression is **general**: no distinction is made between believer and unbeliever.

**This is a point that has been dwelt on, and two readings are transmitted:**

| Reading | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 1 | **General** — for everyone on the earth; asking forgiveness here means "asking for respite and mercy" |
| 2 | **Qualified** — it is qualified by the expression *"they ask forgiveness for those who believe"* in al-Muʾmin 40/7 |

**Both readings are found in the classical sources; I do not impose a preference.** What is to be recorded is this: **the wording of this verse is unqualified**, and the qualification is brought in from another verse.

### أَلَآ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

**The sentence uses three instruments of emphasis one upon another:** *alā* (the particle of attention), *inna* (emphasis), *huwa* (the pronoun of separation, *ḍamīr al-faṣl*).

**And this piling-up fits the place where the sentence stands:** immediately before, it was said that the heavens are on the point of splitting. **What follows is a sentence about forgiveness, emphasised three times over.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse does not set up the tension and then resolve it; **it sets forgiveness down beside the tension.** The two stand in the same verse and neither cancels the other. The balance that will be established between pardon and right at the end of the sūra (40-43) appears here for the first time, on the divine side.

## 42/6 — وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ أَوْلِيَآءَ ٱللَّهُ حَفِيظٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَمَآ أَنتَ عَلَيْهِم بِوَكِيلٍ

*Wa'lladhīna'ttakhadhū min dūnihi awliyāʾa'llāhu ḥafīẓun ʿalayhim wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl*

"As for those who have taken protectors besides Him — God is a watcher over them; you are no guardian over them."

### أَوْلِيَآء — root و-ل-ي

**The concrete sense of the root: nearness, adjacency, there being nothing else in between.** *Walī* — one who stands immediately beside a thing. *Walāya* — the authority that arises from that nearness. *Mawlā* — both master and freed slave; **the word works in both directions**, because nearness has two sides.

**The root occurs eight times in this sūra** and is its second densest root:

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *awliyāʾa* | What they have taken |
| **8** | *min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr* | What the wrongdoers do not find |
| **9** | *awliyāʾa* / *fa'llāhu huwa'l-waliyy* | **Both the claim and the answer in the same verse** |
| **28** | *wa huwa'l-waliyyu'l-ḥamīd* | After the rain |
| **31** | *min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr* | A repetition of verse 8 |
| **44** | *fa-mā lahu min waliyyin* | Of the one who has gone astray |
| **46** | *min awliyāʾa yanṣurūnahum* | Being without a helper |

**This distribution is a countable fact of the text.** And what it shows is this: **throughout the sūra the question "who has whom beside him" is kept continually open.**

### ٱللَّهُ حَفِيظٌ عَلَيْهِمْ — and the sharpest turn of a word in the sūra

**حَفِيظ** — root **ح-ف-ظ**: to guard, to watch over, to keep on record. **The root was treated in `050-kaf.md` and `082-infitar.md`**; the note there was this: the root holds both **guarding** and **keeping a record**, and the Qurʾān uses both.

**Now look at this word's second occurrence in the sūra:**

| Verse | Sentence | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *Allāhu **ḥafīẓun** ʿalayhim* | **God** is a watcher over them |
| **48** | *fa-mā arsalnāka ʿalayhim **ḥafīẓā*** | **You** were not sent as a watcher over them |

**The same word, with the same preposition (*ʿalayhim*), affirmed once and denied once. And the place where it is denied is the Prophet himself.**

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text and is worth recording.** In giving a task to God the sūra takes the same task away from the Prophet. And it does this twice more:

| What | Given to God | Withheld from the Prophet |
|---|---|---|
| **Watching over** | *ḥafīẓun ʿalayhim* (6) | *mā arsalnāka ʿalayhim ḥafīẓā* (48) |
| **Guardianship** | (the name *wakīl* is not used of God in this sūra) | *mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl* (6) |
| **The limit of responsibility** | — | *in ʿalayka illa'l-balāgh* (48) |

### وَمَآ أَنتَ عَلَيْهِم بِوَكِيلٍ

**وَكِيل** — root **و-ك-ل**. **It was treated in `067-mulk.md`**; the description there was this: to commit a matter to another, to appoint an agent; *wakīl* — the one to whom a matter is left. And the point recorded there is important: **tawakkul is not the refusal to act but the committing of the outcome.**

**The use here is the other face of the word:** a *wakīl* is one who takes on responsibility for a matter. The verse says to the Prophet: "you have not taken on their affair."

**And the *bi-* must be recorded:** *bi-wakīl* — the redundant *bāʾ* placed at the head of the predicate in a negation reinforces the negation. The nearest English equivalent: "you are in no way a guardian over them."

### The work of this sentence

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra as a whole:**

This verse establishes the **emotional** balance of the sūra. What will be recounted throughout the sūra will look like a failure — people divide, object, deny. **The sixth verse says at the outset on whose back this scene is not to be laid.**

**And this is a measure valid for anyone who calls:** the outcome is not the responsibility of the one who calls. The responsibility lies in *balāgh* — in conveying. The forty-eighth verse will say so outright.

### What this says to the present

The place where a person convinced he knows something rightly most often stumbles is in counting the other side's refusal to accept it as his own deficiency. This corrupts in both directions: either the person bends his words in order to persuade, or he grows angry at the one who does not accept.

**The verse closes the door on both** — but not by proposing indifference. Immediately afterwards, in the seventh verse, comes *li-tundhira*: "that you may warn". **The work goes on; but the outcome of the work is not on the back of the one who does it.**

## 42/7 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ قُرْءَانًا عَرَبِيًّا لِّتُنذِرَ أُمَّ ٱلْقُرَىٰ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا وَتُنذِرَ يَوْمَ ٱلْجَمْعِ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ ۚ فَرِيقٌ فِى ٱلْجَنَّةِ وَفَرِيقٌ فِى ٱلسَّعِيرِ

*Wa kadhālika awḥaynā ilayka qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan li-tundhira umma'l-qurā wa man ḥawlahā wa tundhira yawma'l-jamʿi lā rayba fīh; farīqun fi'l-jannati wa farīqun fi's-saʿīr*

"And thus We revealed to you an Arabic Qurʾān — that you may warn the mother of towns and those around it, and warn of the day of gathering, of which there is no doubt. One party in the Garden, and one party in the blaze."

### قُرْءَانًا عَرَبِيًّا — its coming indefinite

**Both words are indefinite (*nakira*):** *qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan* — "an Arabic Qurʾān". It does not say *al-qurʾāna'l-ʿarabī*.

**In Arabic indefiniteness sometimes conveys indeterminacy, sometimes *taʿẓīm* (magnification), sometimes *nawʿ* (kind).** Here most of the exegetes read it as **kind**: "a recitation that is in Arabic" — that is, the type of the text is being specified.

**The same construction occurs in several other places in the Qurʾān, and in all of them a clause of purpose accompanies it:**

| Place | Construction | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **Yūsuf 12/2** | *qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan* | *laʿallakum taʿqilūn* — "that you may reason" |
| **Fuṣṣilat 41/3** | *qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan* | *li-qawmin yaʿlamūn* — "for a people who know" |
| **al-Zukhruf 43/3** | *qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan* | *laʿallakum taʿqilūn* |
| **al-Shūrā 42/7** | *qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan* | ***li-tundhira*** — "that you may **warn**" |

**The difference is worth recording:** in the other places the purpose is **understanding** (*ʿaql*, *ʿilm*), here it is **warning**. The same quality (being Arabic), that is, is tied to a different work in different contexts. **In al-Shūrā the intelligibility of the language is set down as the ground for the warning's being able to arrive.**

**And this must be read together with the sixth verse:** there it was said that the Prophet is no guardian; here it is said what he is — **a warner.** The two are the halves of one sentence: you are not responsible, but you are charged.

### أُمَّ ٱلْقُرَىٰ — root أ-م-م

**One of the densest constructions in the sūra, and the root must be dwelt on.**

**The root أ-م-م.** In the corpus it was taken up in `002-bakara.md` at 2/78 and in `062-cuma.md` in connection with the word *ummī*; the note there was this: the root means **mother**, and *ummī*, in the classical explanation, is one who has remained in the state in which he was born of his mother, that is, one who has not learned to read and write.

**What belongs here are the root's other branches** — because they are what make the construction *ummu'l-qurā* intelligible:

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **أُمّ** (*umm*) | Mother; and the **origin, source, centre** of a thing |
| **أُمَّة** (*umma*) | A community; those gathered around something shared |
| **إِمَام** (*imām*) | The one who stands in front, who is followed |
| **أَمَام** (*amām*) | The front, the forward side |
| **أَمَّ** (*amma*) | **To head towards a place, to make for it** — *amma'l-bayt*: he made for the House |

**The picture beneath the root is this, and it is settled by the lexicographers' own descriptions: the point that is returned to, the point one comes out of.** A mother is both at once — the child comes out of her and returns to her. An *imām* is in front, and those behind him turn towards him. The verb *amma* means precisely "to head towards".

**For this reason a thing's central and original part is called *umm* in Arabic:**

- **أُمّ الرَّأْس** — the centre of the head; the membrane of the brain.
- **أُمّ الكِتَاب** — the origin of the Book. The Qurʾān uses this construction: Āl ʿImrān 3/7, al-Raʿd 13/39, al-Zukhruf 43/4.
- **أُمّ القُرَىٰ** — the mother of towns.

### The sense of the construction: why "mother"?

**The explanations given for this construction in the classical commentaries rest on the two branches of the root above:**

| Explanation | Which branch of the root | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin / source** | *umm* = mother, origin | The centre out of which the settlements around it came, to which they are attached |
| **The place headed for** | *amma* = to head towards | The place everyone **heads for** on account of the pilgrimage |
| **Centre** | *ummu'r-raʾs*, *ummu'l-kitāb* | The most important, indispensable part of a whole |

**The three do not exclude one another** and all three are transmitted in the classical sources. **I do not impose a preference.**

**There is a fact of language that must be recorded, and it is certain:** in Arabic the word *umm*, alongside the sense "mother", has a settled usage in the sense of **centre and origin**. The construction, that is, is not a figure but a pattern of the language itself.

### وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا — the widening of the circle

**The construction occurs twice in the Qurʾān, and in both the same addition comes with it:**

| Place | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **al-Anʿām 6/92** | *…**wa li-tundhira umma'l-qurā wa man ḥawlahā**; wa'lladhīna yuʾminūna bi'l-ākhirati yuʾminūna bih* |
| **al-Shūrā 42/7** | *…**li-tundhira umma'l-qurā wa man ḥawlahā**, wa tundhira yawma'l-jamʿ* |

**In both occurrences *wa man ḥawlahā* is present, and both come with the verb of warning.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text.

***Ḥawl* — root ح-و-ل: to turn, to go round something.** *Ḥawla* — the surroundings, the environs. *Ḥāl* (state) and *taḥawwul* (transformation) are from the same root; the shared picture is **turning**.

**And the arrangement draws this circle:**

| Step | What | Extent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Umma'l-qurā* | **A point** — the centre |
| 2 | *Wa man ḥawlahā* | **A ring** — the surroundings |
| 3 | *Wa tundhira **yawma'l-jamʿ*** | **A passage to time** — the circle of place closes |

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence widens in space, then leaves space and leaps to time. And the day named where it leaps to time is "the day of gathering" — that is, **the day on which all the circles of place are gathered in one place.** The widening ends with a joining at a single point.

### يَوْمَ ٱلْجَمْعِ — the day of gathering

**The root ج-م-ع: to bring together, to gather.** *Jumuʿa* is from the same root and was treated in `062-cuma.md`.

**This name is one of the names the Qurʾān uses for the day of resurrection.** In `050-kaf.md` a table was made of the three names Sūrat Qāf gives to the same day (*yawmu'l-waʿīd*, *yawmu'l-khulūd*, *yawmu'l-khurūj*). **The name al-Shūrā gives brings forward another aspect:** that day is the day on which **those who were divided are brought together.**

**And this attaches to the sūra as a whole.** The thirteenth verse will say *lā tatafarraqū* — "do not fall into division". The fourteenth will say *mā tafarraqū* — "they divided". **Division is the subject of the sūra; and the name given to the day on which that division will be closed is "gathering".**

**The fifteenth verse will use the same root once more:** *Allāhu **yajmaʿu** baynanā* — "God will gather us together."

| Verse | Word | Who is gathered |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *yawma'l-jamʿ* | Everyone |
| **15** | *yajmaʿu baynanā* | **The two disputing parties** |
| **29** | *jamʿihim* | The scattered living creatures |

### فَرِيقٌ فِى ٱلْجَنَّةِ وَفَرِيقٌ فِى ٱلسَّعِيرِ

**And immediately after the gathering comes a dividing.**

**فَرِيق — root ف-ر-ق.** It was treated in `075-kiyame.md`: to separate, to split, to open a space between; its derivatives are *farq*, *firqa*, *tafriqa*, *farīq*, *iftirāq*, *furqān*.

**Now look at the uses within this sūra — this is the most striking weave of a word in it:**

| Verse | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *farīqun… wa farīqun* | **The division in the outcome** — the Garden and the Fire |
| **13** | *wa lā **tatafarraqū** fīh* | **The division that is forbidden** — in religion |
| **14** | *wa mā **tafarraqū** illā min baʿdi…* | **The division that was made** — after knowledge came |

**The same root, in three separate verses, doing three separate pieces of work: prohibition, breach, outcome.**

**I record this as an observation and make no claim of a deliberate play on words.** What I record is what can be verified: in forbidding division, in reporting that division took place, and in describing the final separation, the sūra uses **the same root.**

**And a point of arrangement:** in the seventh verse *farīq* comes twice, both times indefinite — *farīqun… wa farīqun*. No number is given, no proportion. **Two parties; which is how large is not said.**

**ٱلسَّعِير** — root **س-ع-ر**: the kindling and rising of a flame. *Siʿr* (price) is from the same root — the lexicographers make the link by way of "rising".

## 42/8 — وَلَوْ شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَجَعَلَهُمْ أُمَّةً وَٰحِدَةً وَلَٰكِن يُدْخِلُ مَن يَشَآءُ فِى رَحْمَتِهِۦ ۚ وَٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ مَا لَهُم مِّن وَلِىٍّ وَلَا نَصِيرٍ

*Wa law shāʾa'llāhu la-jaʿalahum ummatan wāḥidatan wa lākin yudkhilu man yashāʾu fī raḥmatih; wa'ẓ-ẓālimūna mā lahum min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr*

"Had God willed, He would have made them a single community. But He admits whom He wills into His mercy. As for the wrongdoers — they have neither protector nor helper."

### أُمَّةً وَٰحِدَةً — and its link with the preceding verse

**In the seventh verse *ummu'l-qurā* occurred; in the eighth comes *ummatan wāḥidatan*. The same root (أ-م-م), two verses apart, in two different derivatives.**

**And what the sentence establishes is this: plurality is not an accident.** *Law shāʾa* — "had He willed". This pattern conveys an unfulfilled condition; that is, it means "He did not will it". **The state of not being a single community is not presented as an outcome that fell outside the divine will.**

**The same sentence is framed elsewhere in the Qurʾān as well, and there a ground is added:**

> *"Had your Lord willed, He would have made people a single community; but they go on differing."* (Hūd 11/118)

**In al-Shūrā no ground is added; in its place comes another sentence:** *wa lākin yudkhilu man yashāʾu fī raḥmatih.*

### وَلَٰكِن يُدْخِلُ مَن يَشَآءُ فِى رَحْمَتِهِۦ — a point of word order

**The construction of the sentence deserves attention. It does not say "He gives His mercy to whom He wills"; it says "He admits whom He wills into His mercy".**

| Construction | Who moves | Where the mercy is |
|---|---|---|
| "He gives His mercy to whom He wills" | The mercy comes to the person | Something carried |
| **"He admits whom He wills into His mercy"** (the text's construction) | **The person is taken into the mercy** | **A place entered** |

**Mercy here, that is, is framed not as an object but as a place.** *Yudkhilu… fī* — "admits into".

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement.** The same pattern occurs elsewhere in the Qurʾān too (*wa yudkhilahum fī raḥmatih*), so it is not peculiar to al-Shūrā; what I record is the pattern itself.

### وَٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ مَا لَهُم مِّن وَلِىٍّ وَلَا نَصِيرٍ

**This sentence occurs twice in the sūra, in almost the same wording:**

| Verse | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **8** | *wa'ẓ-ẓālimūna **mā lahum min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr*** |
| **31** | *wa **mā lakum min dūni'llāhi min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr*** |

**The difference: the third person in the eighth verse (*lahum*), the second person in the thirty-first (*lakum*).** The same sentence, that is, is said once of "them" and once directly to the one addressed.

**The difference between وَلِىّ and نَصِير** is set out by the lexicographers as follows:

| | **وَلِيّ** | **نَصِير** |
|---|---|---|
| Root | و-ل-ي — nearness | ن-ص-ر — help |
| What it does | **Stands beside**, takes on one's affair | **Helps**, adds strength |
| Kind of relation | A lasting bond | Support in a particular situation |

**Negating the two together removes two distinct kinds of support at once:** neither a permanent intimate nor a helper for the moment.

## 42/9 — أَمِ ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ أَوْلِيَآءَ ۖ فَٱللَّهُ هُوَ ٱلْوَلِىُّ وَهُوَ يُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

*Ami'ttakhadhū min dūnihi awliyāʾ, fa'llāhu huwa'l-waliyyu wa huwa yuḥyi'l-mawtā wa huwa ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr*

"Or have they taken protectors besides Him? God alone is the Protector; He gives life to the dead, and He has power over everything."

### أَمِ — the interrogative particle

***Am* does two things in Arabic:** either it brings in the second side of an alternative question ("this, or that?"), or **on its own it frames a question conveying reproach and astonishment** (*am munqaṭiʿa*). Here it is the second.

**And this particle occurs three times in the sūra**, doing the same work in all three: setting one of the other side's attitudes before it in the form of a question.

| Verse | Question | What it asks about |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | *Ami'ttakhadhū min dūnihi awliyāʾ* | **Taking a protector** |
| **21** | *Am lahum shurakāʾu sharaʿū lahum mina'd-dīn* | **Laying down a religion** |
| **24** | *Am yaqūlūna'ftarā ʿala'llāhi kadhibā* | **The charge of fabrication** |

**Three questions, three separate claims: to whom have you attached yourself, who laid down the rule, whose word is this.** This distribution is a fact that can be verified from the text.

### The claim and the answer in the same verse

**The structure of the verse is tight: the question and its answer are given in the same sentence, with the same word.**

- The question: *ittakhadhū… **awliyāʾ*** — "they took protectors"
- The answer: *fa'llāhu huwa'l-**waliyy*** — "the Protector is He"

**The answer holds three things at once:**

1. **Definiteness** — *al-waliyy*, with the article. In the question *awliyāʾ* was indefinite and plural.
2. **The pronoun of separation** — *huwa* (*ḍamīr al-faṣl*): "He alone".
3. **Singularity** — a singular against a plural.

**With three separate instruments, that is, the sentence does the same thing: it brings the plural down to a singular.**

### وَهُوَ يُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ — why this proof?

**The answer does not end there. A verb is brought in as the ground of protectorship: giving life to the dead.**

**Why this was chosen must be asked, and the answer is in the word itself:**

A *walī* is one who stands beside a person and takes on his affair. **The limit of a protector is as far as his power reaches.** The protectors a person takes — be they idols, persons or institutions — do their work up to a point within life. **Death is the boundary at which every protectorship stops.**

**The verse defines protectorship at exactly that boundary:** the protector is the one who does not stop where the others stop.

**I give this as my own reading**; its ground is that the proof has been placed immediately after the claim about the *walī*.

**And it is linked with what follows in the sūra:** in the twenty-eighth verse *al-waliyyu'l-ḥamīd* will come, and there too there will be a picture of bringing to life — dead ground revived by rain. **Two sentences about the protector, two revivals.**

## 42/10 — وَمَا ٱخْتَلَفْتُمْ فِيهِ مِن شَىْءٍ فَحُكْمُهُۥٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ ذَٰلِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ رَبِّى عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَإِلَيْهِ أُنِيبُ

*Wa ma'khtalaftum fīhi min shayʾin fa-ḥukmuhu ila'llāh; dhālikumu'llāhu rabbī ʿalayhi tawakkaltu wa ilayhi unīb*

"Whatever it is you differ about, its judgement belongs to God. That is God, my Lord; on Him I have relied, and to Him I turn."

### ٱخْتَلَفْتُمْ — the difference between *ikhtilāf* and *tafriqa*

**The root خ-ل-ف: to remain behind, to take someone's place, to come after him.** *Khalīfa*, *khilāf*, *ikhtilāf* and *mukhālafa* are from the same root. **Form VIII (*ikhtilāf*) conveys reciprocity:** being behind one another, standing behind one another in different places.

**Here I record an observation about the arrangement, and I give it as my own reading:**

**The sūra uses two different words and attaches two different verdicts to them.**

| Word | Where | What the sūra says |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱخْتِلَاف** (10) | *ma'khtalaftum fīhi min shayʾ* | **Not forbidden.** Its authority is pointed out: *fa-ḥukmuhu ila'llāh* |
| **تَفَرُّق** (13, 14) | *lā tatafarraqū fīh* | **Forbidden.** And in 14 it is reported to have been breached |

**The difference is visible in the senses of the words themselves:**

- ***Ikhtilāf*** — standing in different places from one another. **A difference of view.**
- ***Tafarruq*** — breaking away from one another, dispersing. **The severing of the bond.**

**The sūra, that is, accepts difference of view as a fact and points out where it is referred; the severing it forbids.** The three-verse distance between the tenth verse and the thirteenth carries this distinction.

**I stress this again: it is an inference, not a transmitted report.** Its ground is the lexical senses of the two roots and the sūra's two different verdicts.

### فَحُكْمُهُۥٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ — "its judgement belongs to God"

**حُكْم — root ح-ك-م:** as recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`, the concrete sense of the root is **to restrain and hold in place**; *ḥakama* (a horse's bridle) is from the same root. A *ḥukm* is a decision that sets a matter in its place and keeps it from scattering.

**The arrangement of the sentence must be recorded:** the preposition *ilā* is used — "towards God", "referred to God". Not *ʿinda* (with) or *li* (for). **The judgement, that is, is carried somewhere.**

**This is the manner in which the dispute is closed, and it will be repeated in the fifteenth verse:** *Allāhu yajmaʿu baynanā wa ilayhi'l-maṣīr.* In both verses the same structure: the matter is **sent** to an authority.

### ذَٰلِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ رَبِّى — the shift in the direction of address

**In the middle of the sentence the speaker changes, and this is an *iltifāt* (a shift in the direction of address).**

- The first half: *ma'khtalaf**tum*** — "you". A community addressed.
- The second half: *rab**bī**… tawakkal**tu**… un**ību*** — "I". The Prophet speaking.

**The sentence, that is, begins with a rule and ends with an avowal.** The rule is for everyone; the avowal is one person's.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence first sets down what it says as a **rule** and then shows it **on itself.** The one who says "the judgement belongs to God" says immediately afterwards "and He is the one I too rely on". **The rule includes the one who sets it.**

### أُنِيبُ — root ن-و-ب

**The root was treated in `050-kaf.md`** (in connection with *munīb* and *awwāb*). Its summary: *ināba* is **returning again and again** to a place; *nawba* (a turn, a thing done by turns) is from the same root. **In the root there is not a single return but a regular coming back.**

**And the verb is imperfect: *unīb* — "I turn", ongoing.** The verb beside it is perfect: *tawakkaltu* — "I have relied", done and settled.

| Verb | Form | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| *tawakkaltu* | **Perfect** | A decision taken once |
| *unīb* | **Imperfect** | **A repeated returning** |

**This is a point of word order and it fits the senses of the words:** reliance is a decision, and it is made; *ināba* is a habit, and it goes on.

**The root will come once more in the thirteenth verse:** *wa yahdī ilayhi **man yunīb*** — "and He guides to Himself whoever turns to Him." **What the Prophet says of himself in the tenth verse, that is, is set down in the thirteenth as the condition of guidance.**

## 42/11 — فَاطِرُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۚ جَعَلَ لَكُم مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَٰجًا وَمِنَ ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ أَزْوَٰجًا ۖ يَذْرَؤُكُمْ فِيهِ ۚ لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِۦ شَىْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْبَصِيرُ

*Fāṭiru's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ; jaʿala lakum min anfusikum azwājan wa mina'l-anʿāmi azwājā; yadhraʾukum fīh; laysa ka-mithlihi shayʾ; wa huwa's-samīʿu'l-baṣīr*

"The splitter into being of the heavens and the earth. He made for you mates from among yourselves, and mates among the livestock; by this He multiplies you. **There is nothing like unto Him.** He is the Hearing, the Seeing."

**This verse carries the densest sentence of transcendence (*tanzīh*) in the Qurʾān. I shall treat it in detail; but first the place where the sentence stands must be seen, because the sentence of transcendence does not come on its own.**

### فَاطِر — root ف-ط-ر

**In the fifth verse the same root occurred as *yatafaṭṭarna* (on the point of splitting). Here it comes as an active participle: *fāṭir* — the one who splits.**

**The concrete sense of the root is to split**, and in Arabic the sense of creating is derived from this: **to open a thing for the first time, when there is no precedent for it.** The distinction the lexicographers transmit is this:

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **خَلَقَ** (*khalaqa*) | خ-ل-ق | **To measure and cut** — to make according to a plan |
| **بَدَأَ** (*badaʾa*) | ب-د-أ | **To begin** — to be first |
| **فَطَرَ** (*faṭara*) | ف-ط-ر | **To split** — to open what is closed, to bring out what was not |

**The picture beneath the word is this: something closed splitting, and something coming out of it.** The cracking of a seed, the opening of a bud, the breaking of dawn.

**And since the word *fiṭra* is from the same root there is a link:** *fiṭra* is a being's state **at its first splitting open.**

**The link within the sūra was recorded in the section on its structure, and I repeat it here:** in the fifth verse the heavens were near to being split; in the eleventh the one who first split them is named. **The same root, six verses apart, passive and active.**

### جَعَلَ لَكُم مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَٰجًا — and the verse's real point of arrangement

**Now we come to a place that requires close attention, and it is the least treated side of the verse.**

**What is said immediately before the sentence of transcendence?** Pairing. Twice: mates from among human beings, mates among the livestock.

**And pairing is the source of *likeness* in the created world.** To be a pair is to be of the same kind; a mate is a person's **counterpart.** Indeed the word *zawj* in Arabic means "each one of a pair", and this side of the root was treated in `056-vakia.md` and `081-tekvir.md`.

**Then comes reproduction:** *yadhraʾukum fīh* — "by this He multiplies you". **That is, the multiplying of like by like.**

**And at exactly this point the sentence comes: *laysa ka-mithlihi shayʾ*.**

| Order | Sentence | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Jaʿala lakum… azwājan… azwājā* | **Pairing** — like with like |
| 2 | *Yadhraʾukum fīh* | **Multiplying** — the increase of likes |
| 3 | ***Laysa ka-mithlihi shayʾ*** | **And He has no like** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verse's own sequence:** the verse first shows that the created world exists **by pairing**, and then places the Creator **outside** that order. Having a mate is the condition of multiplying; what has no mate does not multiply, because it has no need to.

**In `112-ihlas.md` the sentence *"He has not begotten, and He has not been begotten"* was treated with this logic.** In al-Shūrā the same conclusion comes by another road: al-Ikhlāṣ denies begetting; al-Shūrā denies **likeness** — and begetting is the way likeness is produced.

### يَذْرَؤُكُمْ فِيهِ — the word and the pronoun

**The root ذ-ر-أ: to bring forth and spread, to multiply, to scatter as seed.** Its kinship with the word *dhurriyya* is debated by the lexicographers; in the root there is **dispersal by increase.**

**There is disagreement over the referent of the pronoun *fīh*:**

| Reading | What the pronoun refers to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To the pairing / to this order** | "By this He multiplies you" |
| 2 | **To the womb / to the field of creation** | "There He multiplies you" |
| 3 | **To the earth** | "There He multiplies you" |

**The first reading is the most widespread and accords with the context;** but I do not impose a preference.

**A note on the arrangement:** *fīh* is singular, whereas *azwāj* is plural. This is transmitted as a ground supporting the view that the pronoun refers not to the plural but **to the act itself** (the pairing).

### لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِۦ شَىْءٌ — the sentence itself

**Now I come to the construction of the sentence. There is an oddity in it that disappears in translation, and it is one of the most debated linguistic questions in the history of exegesis.**

**Two distinct instruments of comparison are used one upon the other in the sentence:**

- **كَ** (*kāf*) — the particle of comparison: "like".
- **مِثْل** (*mithl*) — a noun meaning likeness, counterpart.

**The word-for-word rendering, then, is this: "there is nothing like unto His like."**

**And this is unusual in Arabic.** Normally one says either *laysa ka-huwa shayʾ* ("there is nothing like Him") or *laysa mithlahu shayʾ* ("there is nothing that is His like"). The two coming together calls for explanation.

**The lexicographers and exegetes have explained this in three main ways. I give the three in a table; I impose no preference.**

| View | What it says | Its ground | The side found weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The *kāf* is redundant** | The *kāf* has been added for emphasis and has no sense of its own. The sentence means "*laysa mithlahu shayʾ*" | The use of a redundant particle for emphasis is a known fact of Arabic (*bi-*, *min*, *lā*) | Admitting a "superfluous letter" in the Qurʾān is disputed in general; the view that every letter does a work is widespread |
| **2. The *mithl* is redundant / a figure** | *Mithl* here is a figure meaning "the essence, the thing itself". In Arabic one says *"mithluka lā yafʿalu hādhā"*, meaning "you do not do this". The sentence means "*laysa ka-huwa shayʾ*" | This usage is settled in the Arabic language and its examples are many | To reckon *mithl* functionless meets the same objection as the first view |
| **3. Both are original — intensification** | "There is nothing like His like." If **even His like** has no like, then He Himself has none at all. The negation has been brought, by an indirect route, to its strongest form | The rhetoricians count this *intensification*: because the negation is made one step further back, it becomes more comprehensive | It is argued that there is a strain in the logic: to say "His like has no like" appears to presuppose that a like exists |

**A fourth explanation is also transmitted, and I record it separately:** the word *mithl* is used in Arabic in the sense of **"quality, attribute"** as well (*mathal* — a quality, a characterisation; indeed the Qurʾān says *mathalu'l-janna*, and what is meant there is "the description of the Garden"). On this reading the sentence comes to mean: **"there is no quality like His quality."** What is denied, that is, is not likeness of essence but likeness of **attribute.**

**None of these four explanations is certain, and all four have been defended in the classical sources.**

**But this must be noticed, and it is what the four explanations have in common:** whichever explanation is taken, **the conclusion the sentence reaches does not change** — nothing resembles Him. The disagreement is not over the conclusion but over which instrument of language brings one to it.

### شَىْءٌ — the scope of the sentence

**The subject of the sentence (the subject of *laysa*) has been left to the end: *shayʾ*.** And this word must be recorded.

***Shayʾ* is the most general noun in Arabic:** it covers everything that exists or can be conceived. **And when it comes in a negative sentence its scope becomes absolute** — an indefinite noun in a context of negation conveys universality.

**The sentence, then, does not say "no being", it says "no thing".** What exists, what is thought, what is conceived — all of it is inside.

**One further point of arrangement:** the predicate (*ka-mithlihi*) has been fronted, and the subject (*shayʾ*) left to the end. **In Arabic fronting conveys specification.** The weight of the sentence falls not on "no thing" but on "not being like Him".

### The notes on transcendence already set out in the corpus — and what is added to them

**This question was entered into in two places in the corpus. I do not repeat them; I add to them.**

**The first is `002-bakara.md` 2/255 (the Verse of the Throne).** What was recorded there was this: the verse consists from beginning to end of declarative sentences, with not a single imperative in it; the structure widens from the inside outward; and **a limit was set** — the word *kursī* cannot be understood so as to ascribe place, body or sitting to God, **because the same verse states that not even sleep can seize Him.** There, that is, the transcendence was drawn from the verse's **own internal consistency.**

**The second is `112-ihlas.md`.** There the difference between *aḥad* and *wāḥid* was tabulated, and this was recorded: *wāḥid* is numerical oneness, and numerical oneness is always open to a comparison; *aḥad* is "that beside which no other can be conceived" — an expression not of number but of **uniqueness.** And *al-Ṣamad*, coming without a connective, had been the unfolding of *aḥad*.

**Now I set the three side by side, and this comparison is what I wish to add:**

| | **al-Ikhlāṣ 112/1-4** | **al-Baqara 2/255** | **al-Shūrā 42/11** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Method | **Negative sentences** — three of its verses negative | **Affirmative sentences** — a series of attributes | **A single negative sentence**, in the middle of affirmative ones |
| What it denies | Begetting, being begotten, having an equal | Sleep, weariness, being encompassed | **Likeness itself** |
| The word of scope | *kufuwan **aḥad*** | *bi-**shayʾin** min ʿilmih* | ***shayʾ*** |
| Where it is placed | The whole sūra | Within a long series | **Between two affirmative sentences** |

**And the last row shows what is peculiar to al-Shūrā.** In al-Ikhlāṣ the transcendence is the **subject** of the text; in the Verse of the Throne it is **part of a series.** In al-Shūrā it is **a single sentence**, and both its sides are full: before it creation and multiplying, after it hearing and seeing.

**Al-Shūrā, that is, gives the transcendence not as a subject but as an *interposed sentence*.** And this is the ground of the point that follows.

### وَهُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْبَصِيرُ — the real point of the arrangement

**Here is the most striking side of the verse, and it must be dwelt on separately.**

**The sentence is this: "There is nothing like unto Him. And He is the Hearing, the Seeing."**

**Between the two sentences there is no transition at all. The same verse, between two full stops.**

**Let us look at what this does.**

**Transcendence is an operation of *putting at a distance*.** When you remove likeness, you also remove the possibility of comparison. And once the possibility of comparison is gone, **the possibility of relation is endangered too** — because a human being builds relation with the patterns he knows.

**When it is said of a being that "it resembles nothing", the natural question that arises is: then what has it to do with me?**

**The verse does not let that question be asked. It puts the answer in advance in the place where the question would be asked.**

| Half of the sentence | What it does | Its result |
|---|---|---|
| *Laysa ka-mithlihi shayʾ* | **Putting at a distance** — every likeness is removed | The possibility of analogy ends |
| *Wa huwa's-samīʿu'l-baṣīr* | **Drawing near** — hearing and seeing | **The relation continues** |

**And which two names are chosen is no accident.**

*Samīʿ* and *baṣīr* are, among the divine names, **the most turned towards the one addressed.** Because:

- **Hearing is the receiving of speech.** That someone hears when a person calls out is the most basic form of relation.
- **Seeing is the receiving of a state.** That a person is seen while he is in some condition is the second basic form of relation.

**Both are verbs that "turn towards": the one turning is He, the one turned towards is the human being.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse balances the distance the transcendence produces not by taking the transcendence back but **by declaring the relation in the same sentence.** The distance is not removed; what is said is that the distance **does not mean disconnection.**

**And this is the whole method of the sūra.** The same movement will be repeated throughout:

| Verse | What puts at a distance | What immediately draws near |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | The heavens on the point of splitting | *al-ghafūru'r-raḥīm* |
| **11** | *Laysa ka-mithlihi shayʾ* | *al-samīʿu'l-baṣīr* |
| **19** | *al-qawiyyu'l-ʿazīz* (at the end) | *Allāhu **laṭīfun bi-ʿibādihi*** (at the beginning) |
| **27** | Provision is sent down by measure | *innahu bi-ʿibādihi **khabīrun baṣīr*** |

**Note the fourth row: the name *baṣīr* occurs twice in the sūra, and in both places immediately after a sentence of limitation.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text.

### A limit — and it must be written plainly

**This verse has stood at the centre of the debate about the attributes in the history of theology.** One side, taking its start from the verse, turned all ascriptions of attributes into figures; another accepted the verse and held the attributes fixed without modality.

**No side is taken in this dispute in this commentary.** The same note was entered in `002-bakara.md` at 2/255 and in `057-hadid.md`.

**What is to be recorded is what the verse itself does:** the verse mentions two attributes **after** denying likeness. **The verse's own construction, that is, holds "there is no likeness" and "there are attributes" together.** That the text says the two together is the plainest proof that the two do not conflict — and this is what can be said without taking sides.

### What this says to the present

A human being knows what he does not know by likening it to what he does. This is not a defect but the way the mind works. But it has to stop somewhere.

**What this verse sets down is exactly that stopping-place.** And the stopping-place does not forbid thinking; it says **how far** thinking goes.

**And its practical consequence is this:** when a person's conception of God is left unchecked, sooner or later it begins to resemble him — he carries his own anger, his own partisanship, his own reckoning into it. In `112-ihlas.md` this was recorded by way of the command *qul*. Here the same thing is forbidden outright.

**But in doing this the verse does not sever the relation either.** Someone who hears and sees is still there. **Distance is not indifference** — and this is the balance that will be carried to the end of the sūra.

## 42/12 — لَهُۥ مَقَالِيدُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۖ يَبْسُطُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ وَيَقْدِرُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

*Lahu maqālīdu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ; yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu wa yaqdir; innahu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm*

"To Him belong the keys of the heavens and the earth. He spreads out provision for whom He wills, and He restricts it. He knows everything."

### مَقَالِيد — keys

**There is disagreement over the singular and the origin of the word, and I record it plainly.**

| View | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | Its singular is **مِقْلَاد** or **مِقْلِيد** — a key |
| 2 | Its singular is **إِقْلِيد** — a key; some lexicographers say that this word entered Arabic from another language |
| 3 | The word has no singular; it is used only in the plural |

**On the sense, however, there is little difference: "keys" or "treasuries".** The two are bound together; a key is the means of reaching a treasury.

**The construction occurs twice in the Qurʾān:** here and in al-Zumar 39/63 (*lahu maqālīdu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*). **In the same wording, in two places.**

### يَبْسُطُ … وَيَقْدِرُ — two verbs, one subject

**The sentence sets two opposite verbs side by side, and the agent of both is the same.**

**بَسَطَ — root ب-س-ط.** **It was treated in `071-nuh.md`**; the note there was this: to spread, to lay out, to open, to widen; *bisāṭ* — a rug spread on the ground; *basīṭ* — spread out, simple; and its opposite *qabḍ* (to gather in).

**قَدَرَ — root ق-د-ر.** **It was treated in detail in `097-kadr.md` in its three senses**, and that threefold table was used again in `054-kamer.md`. The table there was: **measure/determining**, **worth/honour**, **narrowness/fullness.** The verb *yaqdir* is here in the **third** sense: to narrow, to restrict the measure.

**And these two senses of the root — to measure and to narrow — are bound to one another:** to measure a thing is to set a limit to it, and setting a limit is narrowing. **This link was already made in `097-kadr.md`.**

**Now this verse must be read together with the twenty-seventh, because both use the same two roots:**

| | **42/12** | **42/27** |
|---|---|---|
| Verbs | *yabsuṭu… wa **yaqdir*** | *wa law **basaṭa**… yunazzilu bi-**qadar*** |
| To whom | *li-man yashāʾ* — **according to the person** | *li-ʿibādihi* — **to everyone** |
| Mood | Declarative — what is | **Conditional** — what has not been |
| What it says | The **distribution** differs | The **total** is measured |

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text: the same two roots, fifteen verses apart, answering two different questions.** The twelfth verse answers "how much to whom", the twenty-seventh "why not without limit".

**I record this distinction separately because confusing the two yields a false conclusion:** the twelfth verse is not explaining one person's straitness, it is saying that the distribution is from a single hand. It will be treated in detail at verse 27.

### إِنَّهُۥ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌ — and its link with the preceding verse

**The eleventh verse framed a negation with the word *shayʾ*: *laysa ka-mithlihi shayʾ*.**

**The twelfth frames an affirmation with the same word: *bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm*.**

| Verse | Sentence | What *shayʾ* does |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | *Laysa ka-mithlihi **shayʾ*** | **The field of likeness** — entirely empty |
| **12** | *Bi-kulli **shayʾin** ʿalīm* | **The field of knowledge** — entirely full |

**The same word, two verses apart, one leaving nothing in and the other leaving nothing out.**

**I record this as an observation; I do not claim that it is a deliberate choice.** What I record is what can be verified: in both verses the word works as a word of scope, once in negation and once in affirmation.

## 42/13 — شَرَعَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلدِّينِ مَا وَصَّىٰ بِهِۦ نُوحًا وَٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ وَمَا وَصَّيْنَا بِهِۦٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰٓ ۖ أَنْ أَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلدِّينَ وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ فِيهِ ۚ كَبُرَ عَلَى ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ مَا تَدْعُوهُمْ إِلَيْهِ ۚ ٱللَّهُ يَجْتَبِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يَشَآءُ وَيَهْدِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يُنِيبُ

*Sharaʿa lakum mina'd-dīni mā waṣṣā bihi Nūḥan wa'lladhī awḥaynā ilayka wa mā waṣṣaynā bihi Ibrāhīma wa Mūsā wa ʿĪsā, an aqīmu'd-dīna wa lā tatafarraqū fīh; kabura ʿala'l-mushrikīna mā tadʿūhum ilayh; Allāhu yajtabī ilayhi man yashāʾu wa yahdī ilayhi man yunīb*

"He has laid down for you as a way in religion what He enjoined upon Noah, and what We have revealed to you, and what We enjoined upon Abraham, Moses and Jesus: **uphold the religion, and do not fall into division in it.** What you call them to is hard upon the mushrikūn. God chooses for Himself whom He wills, and guides to Himself whoever turns to Him."

**One of the densest verses in the sūra. I treat it under three headings: the root, the list of names, and the shared foundation.**

### شَرَعَ — root ش-ر-ع

**This root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

**The concrete sense of the root is plain in the lexicographers' descriptions, and it carries a picture of water:**

> ***شَرِيعَة* (sharīʿa) — the place by which one goes down to a river or a spring; the descent cut into the bank, used by animals and people to reach the water.**

**The lexicographers record the features of this place too, and the features carry the sense of the word:**

| Feature | What it means |
|---|---|
| **It leads to water** | It is not itself the goal; it leads to the goal |
| **It is open** | Not a hidden trail; a place everyone knows and uses |
| **It goes down** | From above downward, towards the water |
| **It is not cut off** | As long as the water flows, the way is there too |

**The derivatives of the root widen this picture:**

- **شَرَعَ** (*sharaʿa*) — to open a way; and **to set about a thing, to begin.** The Turkish *şuru etmek* ("to set about") is this verb.
- **شَارِع** (*shāriʿ*) — **an open public road; a street.** In Arabic today this is the word for a street or avenue. The road everyone passes along, the road that is not closed.
- **شِرْعَة** (*shirʿa*) — the same sense, with a difference of pattern.
- **تَشْرِيع** (*tashrīʿ*) — the laying down of rules.
- **شَرَّعَ** — to raise something, to set it up; some lexicographers attach the word **شِرَاع** (a sail) to this root as well. **I do not give this link as certain; some of the lexicographers say so.**

**The Qurʾān uses the root in several places, and the uses support the concrete sense:**

> *"For each of you We appointed a **shirʿa** and a **minhāj**."* (al-Māʾida 5/48)

**This verse is worth recording, because *shirʿa* has *minhāj* set beside it** — and *minhāj* (root ن-ه-ج) too means **an open, clearly marked road.** **Both words are words for a road.**

### What follows from the concrete sense

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the lexicographers' description of the root:**

The connotation the word *sharīʿa* carries today is "a body of rules". **The root itself, however, describes not a list of rules but a *place of descent*.** And the difference between the two pictures is not trivial:

| | **A list of rules** | **A path down to water** |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Draws boundaries | **Brings you somewhere** |
| Where its value lies | In itself | **In where it arrives** |
| Who uses it | Those who must comply | **Those who are thirsty** |

**And the continuation of the verse supports this reading:** what is commanded? *An aqīmu'd-dīna wa lā tatafarraqū fīh* — not a list but **two verbs.** Uphold it; do not divide.

**I give this as an observation about the language; I do not claim that the root's original sense cancels present usage.** Languages widen the senses of words, and that is ordinary. What I record is the picture that stands beneath the root.

### Five prophets — and the order of the list

**Five names occur in the verse:**

1. **Noah**
2. **"What We revealed to you"** — Muhammad
3. **Abraham**
4. **Moses**
5. **Jesus**

**And the order is striking: at the second place, the sequence steps outside history.** The chronological order would be Noah–Abraham–Moses–Jesus–Muhammad. The text has put the last in the second place.

**The same five names occur together once more in the Qurʾān:**

> *"And when We took from the prophets their covenant — from **you**, from **Noah**, from **Abraham**, from **Moses** and from **Jesus** son of Mary."* (al-Aḥzāb 33/7)

**I set the two lists side by side — this is a fact that can be verified from the text:**

| Order | **al-Shūrā 42/13** | **al-Aḥzāb 33/7** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Noah** | **You** (*minka*) |
| 2 | **You** (*awḥaynā ilayka*) | **Noah** |
| 3 | Abraham | Abraham |
| 4 | Moses | Moses |
| 5 | Jesus | Jesus |

**The last three names are in the same order; the first two have changed places.** This is a fact worth recording.

**The explanations transmitted about the order:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Historical order** | Noah is the earliest, and the list begins with him |
| **Order of address** | In al-Aḥzāb the address is direct (*minka*), and so the one addressed is placed first |
| **Context** | What is emphasised in al-Shūrā is **continuity**; beginning with the earliest and placing the one addressed immediately after closes the chain |

**I do not impose a preference.** But I record an observation about the arrangement: **that in al-Shūrā the one addressed is placed not at the head of the list but immediately after the earliest sets "the most recent" beside "the most ancient".** The thousands of years in between, that is, are fitted into two words within the sentence.

**And there is a change of verb, and that too must be recorded:**

| To whom | Verb | Form / person |
|---|---|---|
| **Noah** | *waṣṣā bihi* | **Third person singular** — "He enjoined" |
| **To you** | *awḥaynā* | **First person plural** — "We revealed" |
| **Abraham, Moses, Jesus** | *waṣṣaynā bihi* | **First person plural** — "We enjoined" |

**The sentence, that is, begins in the third person and passes to the first plural — this is an *iltifāt* (a shift in the direction of address).** And the shift comes exactly at the point where the one addressed is reached.

**The verb changes as well:** for Noah, Abraham, Moses and Jesus **وصّى** (*to enjoin, to charge*), for the one addressed **أوحى** (*to reveal*). **The difference between the two verbs:**

- **وصّى** — root **و-ص-ي**: to bind one thing to another, to join them; and from there **charge, bequest.** The lexicographers record that the root holds **joining and continuity** (*waṣā'n-nabt* — the plants grew joined and dense).
- **أوحى** — treated in `053-necm.md`: **swift and hidden communication.**

**The root's sense of "joining" is particularly apt here, and I record this as my own reading:** a *tawṣiya* is something passed on by being **attached** to the next in line. The verse is speaking of a chain in any case.

### أَنْ أَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلدِّينَ وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ فِيهِ — the shared foundation

**What is enumerated as the shared foundation of five prophets is two verbs. And what these two verbs are is understood from what they are not.**

**What is not enumerated:** the forms of worship, the list of the lawful and the forbidden, the detail of the law. **The Qurʾān says elsewhere that these can differ:** *"For each of you We appointed a shirʿa and a minhāj"* (al-Māʾida 5/48).

**What is enumerated is two things:**

| Command | Root | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **أَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلدِّينَ** | ق-و-م | **Uphold the religion** |
| **وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ فِيهِ** | ف-ر-ق | **Do not break apart in it** |

**إِقَامَة — root ق-و-م.** It was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/255 in connection with *al-Qayyūm*: to stand, to stand over a matter and keep it going. **Form II (*iqāma*) is transitive: to hold something up, not to let it fall.**

**The command, then, is not "apply the religion" but "hold the religion up".** The word presupposes **the possibility of collapse**: what is held up is what would fall if it were let go.

**And the second command stands like the condition of the first.** I record this as my own reading: **holding something up is a collective work; those who have scattered cannot hold anything up.** That the two commands come side by side is meaningful for this reason.

### The link with `002-bakara.md` 2/213

**Why division arises was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/213. What was recorded there was this:**

> *"The verse frames a description of history, and its order is striking: 1. Unity — kāna'n-nāsu ummatan wāḥida. 2. Prophets and a Book — in order to settle the disagreement. 3. Disagreement — and this time among those who received the Book. The cause of division, that is, is not shown to be ignorance. The text says it plainly: min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu'l-bayyināt — after the clear proofs had come to them."*

**And the conclusion reached there was: "The sūra takes disagreement out of the class of a question of knowledge and makes it a question of character."**

**Al-Shūrā 42/13 gives the *imperative* side of that diagnosis.** Al-Baqara had recounted what happened; al-Shūrā says what is to be done. **And the next verse (14) will repeat al-Baqara's diagnosis in almost the same words.**

### كَبُرَ عَلَى ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ مَا تَدْعُوهُمْ إِلَيْهِ

**كَبُرَ — root ك-ب-ر.** It was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` and `074-muddessir.md`. **Here the verb means "it came heavy, it weighed on them"** — like the weight of a load.

**And what is interesting is this: what is it that comes heavy?** *Mā tadʿūhum ilayh* — "what you call them to". That is, **the two commands just enumerated**: upholding the religion and not dividing.

**I record this as an observation about the context:** in the first half of the verse unity is set down as the shared foundation of all the prophets; in the second half it is said that this foundation **comes heavy.** The reason for the heaviness is not given in the verse.

### ٱللَّهُ يَجْتَبِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يَشَآءُ وَيَهْدِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يُنِيبُ

**The verse closes with two verbs, and the objects of the two differ:**

| Verb | Root | Its object | Its condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **يَجْتَبِى** | ج-ب-ي | *man yashāʾ* — **whom He wills** | No condition |
| **يَهْدِى** | ه-د-ي | *man yunīb* — **whoever turns** | **There is a condition: turning** |

**اجْتِبَاء — root ج-ب-ي: to gather a thing, to pick out and bring together.** *Jibāya* — the collecting of taxes. **In Form VIII: to pick out and set apart for oneself.**

**The difference between the two sentences must be recorded, and it matters:**

- **Choosing** (*ijtibāʾ*) is not tied to a condition — this shows that a position given, such as prophethood, is owed to no one.
- **Guidance**, on the other hand, is tied to a condition: *man yunīb* — **the one who turns back.**

**And the verb *yunīb* is the verb the Prophet used of himself in the tenth verse:** *wa ilayhi **unīb***.

| Verse | Who | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | The Prophet, of himself | *wa ilayhi **unīb*** — "to Him I turn" |
| **13** | A general rule | *wa yahdī ilayhi **man yunīb*** — "He guides the one who turns" |

**The line set out from the third verse onward, then, is completed here:** what the Prophet says about himself is set down three verses later as a condition valid for everyone. **I record this as an observation about the arrangement.**

## 42/14 — وَمَا تَفَرَّقُوٓا۟ إِلَّا مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا جَآءَهُمُ ٱلْعِلْمُ بَغْيًۢا بَيْنَهُمْ ۚ وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةٌ سَبَقَتْ مِن رَّبِّكَ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى لَّقُضِىَ بَيْنَهُمْ ۚ وَإِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ أُورِثُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ مِنۢ بَعْدِهِمْ لَفِى شَكٍّ مِّنْهُ مُرِيبٍ

*Wa mā tafarraqū illā min baʿdi mā jāʾahumu'l-ʿilmu baghyan baynahum; wa lawlā kalimatun sabaqat min rabbika ilā ajalin musamman la-quḍiya baynahum; wa inna'lladhīna ūrithu'l-kitāba min baʿdihim la-fī shakkin minhu murīb*

"They fell into division only after knowledge had come to them, out of rivalry among themselves. Were it not for a word from your Lord that had gone before, for an appointed term, it would have been decided between them. And those who were made to inherit the Book after them are in a disquieting doubt about it."

### A point-by-point comparison with 2/213

**The overlap between this verse and `002-bakara.md` 2/213 is one of the clearest instances of the Qurʾān's interpreting the Qurʾān. I set the two texts side by side.**

> **al-Baqara 2/213:** *"…**wa ma'khtalafa fīhi** illa'lladhīna ūtūhu **min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu'l-bayyinātu baghyan baynahum**…"*
>
> **al-Shūrā 42/14:** *"**Wa mā tafarraqū** illā **min baʿdi mā jāʾahumu'l-ʿilmu baghyan baynahum**…"*

**What is shared:**

| Element | In both verses |
|---|---|
| The note of time | ***min baʿdi mā jāʾahum*** — "**after** it came" |
| The cause | ***baghyan baynahum*** — "out of rivalry among themselves" |
| The structure | *Mā… illā* — **restriction**: it did not happen otherwise |

**What differs — and the difference matters:**

| | **al-Baqara 2/213** | **al-Shūrā 42/14** |
|---|---|---|
| **The verb** | *ikhtilāf* — **difference of view** | *tafarruq* — **breaking away, dispersal** |
| **What came** | *al-bayyināt* — **clear proofs** (plural, concrete) | *al-ʿilm* — **knowledge** (singular, general) |
| **Who** | *alladhīna ūtūh* — those given the Book | Its subject comes from the preceding verse: **those commanded to uphold the religion** |
| **What follows** | God guided the believers to the truth | **The judgement is deferred**: *lawlā kalimatun sabaqat* |
| **Context** | A description of history — how it happened | **The breach of a prohibition** — the command of verse 13 broken |

**The last difference is the most important, and it is this:**

**In al-Baqara the disagreement is recounted as a *fact*. In al-Shūrā it is reported that something *forbidden* in the preceding verse has been done.**

| Verse | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **13** | *wa **lā tatafarraqū** fīh* — "**do not divide**" |
| **14** | *wa **mā tafarraqū** illā…* — "**they divided**" |

**The prohibitive and the perfect of the same root, in two consecutive verses.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text, and it adds a sharpness not found in `002-bakara.md`: **the command and the breach stand side by side.**

**And the second difference must be dwelt on too: *bayyināt* → *ʿilm*.**

*Bayyināt* are proofs that come from outside and can be pointed to. *ʿIlm* is **the state of knowing** — the result the proof produces in the other party. **Al-Shūrā, that is, goes a step beyond al-Baqara:** al-Baqara says "after the proofs came", al-Shūrā says "after the knowledge came".

**I record this as my own reading:** that a proof has come and that knowledge has formed are different things; the second narrows the room for excuse still further. The one to whom a proof has come may say "I did not understand"; the one in whom knowledge has formed cannot.

### بَغْيًۢا بَيْنَهُمْ — root ب-غ-ي

**The root was treated in detail in `049-hucurat.md` at 49/9; I do not repeat it.** The finding there was this:

> *"The root's original sense is **to want, to seek, to demand.** Ibtighāʾ — a seeking; bughya — the thing wanted… The transition the lexicographers record is this: the demand's **overstepping the bound.** A person wants what is his; when he begins to want what is not his, the act turns into baghy. Baghy, that is, is wanting in its boundary-crossing state."*

**And this too was recorded there:** al-Ḥujurāt is a sūra "woven of boundary words"; *fisq* was coming out of a husk, *baghy* was demand that crosses the bound, *ḥujra* was the boundary itself.

**Now let us look at the uses in al-Shūrā — the root occurs four times here:**

| Verse | Word | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **14** | *baghyan baynahum* | Those who divided | **The cause of the division** |
| **27** | *la-**baghaw** fi'l-arḍ* | People, if provision were spread without limit | **The consequence of abundance** |
| **39** | *idhā aṣābahumu'l-**baghy*** | What is done to the believers | **The wrong suffered** |
| **42** | *wa **yabghūna** fi'l-arḍi bi-ghayri'l-ḥaqq* | The wrongdoers | **Overstepping on the earth** |

**These four occurrences draw the spine of the sūra, and all four sit on the same core of meaning: the crossing of a bound.**

- **In 14** the bound is crossed *within religion* — dividing while knowledge is present.
- **In 27** the bound could be crossed *in wealth* — and so a measure is set.
- **In 39** the bound is crossed *in another's right* — from the victim's side.
- **In 42** the same thing *on the scale of the earth* — from the doer's side.

**I record this as my own reading:** *baghy* in al-Shūrā is both the cause of the division and the subject of the conflict. The balance of repayment and pardon the sūra will set up from the fortieth verse onward is built on this single word, named in the fourteenth.

**And a note on the language:** *baghyan* is in the accusative, and the grammarians count it a **object of purpose** (*mafʿūl lahu*): "**because of** rivalry". The sentence, that is, names the cause outright — not a lack of knowledge, but a state.

### وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةٌ سَبَقَتْ مِن رَّبِّكَ — the deferred judgement

***Lawlā* states the negative consequence of a condition that does not hold: "were it not for this, that would have happened."**

**كَلِمَةٌ سَبَقَتْ** — "a word that had gone before". **What it is, is not stated.** In the classical commentaries it is widely said that this is the decision to defer the reckoning to the day of resurrection; **I give this as a transmitted report, not as certain knowledge.**

***ilā ajalin musammā* — "for an appointed term".** This construction was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/3; the note there was this: one of the two qualifications placed upon creation is **a term** — not endless, but with a named appointment.

**And the sentence comes once more in the sūra, in almost the same wording:**

| Verse | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **14** | *wa lawlā **kalimatun sabaqat** min rabbika ilā ajalin musamman **la-quḍiya baynahum*** |
| **21** | *wa lawlā **kalimatu'l-faṣl** **la-quḍiya baynahum*** |

**The closing is word for word the same: *la-quḍiya baynahum*.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text and was recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra.

**The difference is in the name of the "word":**

| Verse | The word | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | *kalimatun sabaqat… ilā ajalin musammā* | **Time** — a deferral tied to a term |
| **21** | *kalimatu'l-faṣl* | **Separation** — the judgement itself |

### وَإِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ أُورِثُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ مِنۢ بَعْدِهِمْ لَفِى شَكٍّ مِّنْهُ مُرِيبٍ

***Ūrithū* — root و-ر-ث, passive: "they were made heirs".** In `057-hadid.md` at 57/10, in connection with *mīrāthu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*, the root's sense of "what remains after its owner" was treated.

**The choice of word carries a point of arrangement, and I give it as my own reading:** an inheritance is what comes **without being earned.** One who inherits the Book has done nothing to obtain it. **And the continuation of the sentence completes this: the heirs are in doubt.**

**شَكٍّ مِّنْهُ مُرِيبٍ — a word qualified by itself**

***Shakk* — doubt. *Murīb* — root ر-ي-ب, "that which casts into doubt".** That is, "a doubt that gives doubt".

**In Arabic the qualifying of a word by an adjective from its own root or from a close synonym conveys *intensification*.** The nearest English rendering: "a doubt that will not settle", "a doubt that leaves one uneasy".

**The difference between *rayb* and *shakk*** is set out by the lexicographers thus: *shakk* is the equality of two possibilities, a **mental** state; *rayb* adds **unease** to it — the doubt that troubles a person. **The construction, that is, adds to doubt the dimension of disquiet.**

**And a contrast with `002-bakara.md` 2/2 can be recorded:** there it was said of the Book *lā rayba fīh* — "there is no doubt in it". **Here those who inherit the Book are named as *fī shakkin minhu murīb* — "in a disquieting doubt about it".**

**The same root (ر-ي-ب), one about the text and one about its readers.** This is a verifiable fact. **What I record is this: the doubt is located not in the text but in those who took it over.**

## 42/15 — فَلِذَٰلِكَ فَٱدْعُ ۖ وَٱسْتَقِمْ كَمَآ أُمِرْتَ ۖ وَلَا تَتَّبِعْ أَهْوَآءَهُمْ ۖ وَقُلْ ءَامَنتُ بِمَآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ مِن كِتَٰبٍ ۖ وَأُمِرْتُ لِأَعْدِلَ بَيْنَكُمُ ۖ ٱللَّهُ رَبُّنَا وَرَبُّكُمْ ۖ لَنَآ أَعْمَٰلُنَا وَلَكُمْ أَعْمَٰلُكُمْ ۖ لَا حُجَّةَ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمُ ۖ ٱللَّهُ يَجْمَعُ بَيْنَنَا ۖ وَإِلَيْهِ ٱلْمَصِيرُ

*Fa-li-dhālika fa'dʿu wa'staqim kamā umirt; wa lā tattabiʿ ahwāʾahum; wa qul āmantu bimā anzala'llāhu min kitāb; wa umirtu li-aʿdila baynakum; Allāhu rabbunā wa rabbukum; lanā aʿmālunā wa lakum aʿmālukum; lā ḥujjata baynanā wa baynakum; Allāhu yajmaʿu baynanā; wa ilayhi'l-maṣīr*

"So call, then, and stand straight as you have been commanded; and do not follow their desires. Say: I believe in whatever Book God has sent down, and I am commanded to do justice among you. God is our Lord and your Lord. Our deeds are ours and your deeds are yours. Between us and you there is nothing to argue. God will gather us together; and to Him is the returning."

**The verse is one of the most tightly arranged sentences in the sūra: short clauses one after another, without connectives, with the rhythm of a list. I take them in order.**

### Structure: nine short sentences

| Order | Sentence | Turned towards |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Fa-li-dhālika fa'dʿu* | **A command** — to the Prophet |
| 2 | *Wa'staqim kamā umirt* | **A command** |
| 3 | *Wa lā tattabiʿ ahwāʾahum* | **A prohibition** |
| 4 | *Āmantu bimā anzala'llāhu min kitāb* | **An avowal** — about himself |
| 5 | *Wa umirtu li-aʿdila baynakum* | **A statement of task** |
| 6 | *Allāhu rabbunā wa rabbukum* | **Common ground** |
| 7 | *Lanā aʿmālunā wa lakum aʿmālukum* | **The field that divides** |
| 8 | *Lā ḥujjata baynanā wa baynakum* | **The closing of the argument** |
| 9 | *Allāhu yajmaʿu baynanā wa ilayhi'l-maṣīr* | **The pointing out of the authority** |

**The arrangement follows a direction:** first the commands turned upon himself (1-3), then the declaration of his own position (4-5), then what is shared with the other side (6), then what is separate (7), then the closing of the argument (8), then the authority (9).

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence is a sequence showing how an argument is to be ended — and in the sequence it is striking that **what is shared comes before what is separate.** First "our Lord is the same" is said, then "our deeds are separate".

### ٱسْتَقِمْ كَمَآ أُمِرْتَ — "as you have been commanded"

**اسْتِقَامَة — root ق-و-م, Form X: to be straight, to stand without swerving.** The same root as *aqīmu'd-dīn* in the thirteenth verse. **Two verses apart: hold the religion up (13), and you yourself stand straight (15).**

***Kamā umirt* — "as you have been commanded".** This qualification is worth recording: the measure of straightness is not the person's own conception but **a command that has been given.**

**The same expression occurs in Hūd 11/112:** *fa'staqim kamā umirta wa man tāba maʿak.* **The same pattern in both places.**

### وَقُلْ ءَامَنتُ بِمَآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ مِن كِتَٰبٍ

***min kitāb* — indefinite and with *min*.** This conveys universality: "whatever He has sent down of the kind 'Book'". **The avowal, that is, is not confined to a single Book.**

**And this is the natural consequence of the list of five prophets in the thirteenth verse:** if the same foundation was given to five prophets, then faith in the Books sent down to them is part of the avowal.

### وَأُمِرْتُ لِأَعْدِلَ بَيْنَكُمُ — the command of justice

**عَدْل — root ع-د-ل: to make a thing equal to its counterpart, to bring two sides level.** In the root there is **equivalence**; *ʿadl* and *ʿidl* (the counterweight of a load on the other side) are from the same root. The two sides of a camel's load must be equal, or the load falls.

**And note the object of the command: *baynakum* — "among you".** Those addressed are the other side. **Justice, that is, is set down as something required not within one's own community but between oneself and one's opponents.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pronoun:** despite all the "we/you" distinctions in the sentence, the object of the command of justice includes "you" as well.

### لَا حُجَّةَ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمُ — two readings

**This sentence is the most debated place in the verse.**

**حُجَّة — root ح-ج-ج.** It was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/139: **to bring a proof, to prevail in an argument.** And what was recorded there was this: the pattern is Form III (*mufāʿala*, *muḥājja*) — **a two-sided, reciprocal argument.**

**The wording of the sentence has been understood in two ways, and both are defended in the classical sources:**

| Reading | What it says | Its ground | Its result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. "There is no quarrel between us"** | *Ḥujja* here means **contention, dispute**. The sentence means "we will not wrangle with you" | The Form III pattern of the root conveys reciprocal disputation; and the continuation of the sentence (*Allāhu yajmaʿu baynanā*) refers the matter to an authority | **The argument is left**, the parties separate |
| **2. "There is no more need to bring proofs"** | The proof has already come and been set out; there is no longer a stage at which proofs are to be exchanged | The next verse (16) takes up exactly this point: *alladhīna yuḥājjūna fi'llāhi **min baʿdi mā ustujība lah*** — "those who argue **after** it has been accepted" | **The argument is over**, and the one who carries it on is in the wrong |

**I do not impose a preference. Both readings find support in the text.**

**There is a point that must be recorded, and it is shared by both readings:** the sentence does not **silence** the other side, it **closes** the argument. These are different things. To silence is to prevent the other from speaking; to close is to accept that the speaking will lead to no conclusion.

**And a note about the law is required:** in the classical sources this verse has been described as "the verse of mutual leaving-off" (*mutāraka*), and some authors have said that it was abrogated by rulings that came later. **Per the method (`USLUP.md`) no legal ruling is issued in this commentary; nor is any side taken on the claim of abrogation.** What I record is that the claim exists.

### The overlap with `002-bakara.md` 2/139

**Three sentences of this verse overlap almost exactly with al-Baqara 2/139. I set the two texts side by side — this is a verifiable fact:**

> **al-Baqara 2/139:** *"Qul **a-tuḥājjūnanā fi'llāhi wa huwa rabbunā wa rabbukum; wa lanā aʿmālunā wa lakum aʿmālukum**; wa naḥnu lahu mukhliṣūn."*
>
> **al-Shūrā 42/15-16:** *"…**Allāhu rabbunā wa rabbukum; lanā aʿmālunā wa lakum aʿmālukum**… wa'lladhīna **yuḥājjūna fi'llāhi**…"*

| Element | al-Baqara 2/139 | al-Shūrā 42/15-16 |
|---|---|---|
| *Rabbunā wa rabbukum* | **Present** | **Present** |
| *Lanā aʿmālunā wa lakum aʿmālukum* | **Present** | **Present** |
| *Yuḥājjūna fi'llāh* | **Present** (as a question, 139) | **Present** (as a statement, 16) |
| The close | *Wa naḥnu lahu mukhliṣūn* | *Allāhu yajmaʿu baynanā wa ilayhi'l-maṣīr* |

**Al-Shūrā 15-16, that is, sets up the elements of al-Baqara 2/139 again in the same order and gives them a different close.**

**What was recorded about that verse in `002-bakara.md` was this:**

> *"The objection inside the question is to the subject of the argument: fi'llāh — 'about God'. What is being argued over, that is, is not a ruling, a practice or a question of history; it is whom God belongs to. And the answer aims at exactly this point: 'He is our Lord and yours.' The premise of the argument is refused… The sentence turns the argument from belonging to action: what cannot be shared out is not the Lord but each person's own work."*

**What al-Shūrā adds is the close.** Al-Baqara ended with an avowal (*naḥnu lahu mukhliṣūn* — "we are sincerely His"); al-Shūrā ends with **a meeting**: *Allāhu yajmaʿu baynanā.*

### ٱللَّهُ يَجْمَعُ بَيْنَنَا — where the parted sides will meet

**And this is the place that connects with the sūra as a whole.**

**In the seventh verse the name of the day was given as *yawmu'l-jamʿ* — "the day of gathering".** Here the same root comes as a verb: *yajmaʿu baynanā.*

**And the point of arrangement is this: the verb takes *baynanā* — "what is between us".** What is gathered, that is, is not the two sides but **the distance between them.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence says that the division is not permanent, but it does not leave the joining to this world either. **The argument is closed; the matter is not deferred — it is transferred elsewhere.**

**And the last sentence of the verse seals that transfer: *wa ilayhi'l-maṣīr*.**

**مَصِير — root ص-ي-ر: to turn from one state to another, to become something.** The verb *ṣāra* in Arabic means "he became, he came to be". **A *maṣīr*, then, is not only a "place of arrival" but "the state one turns into".**

**And this word will come back as a verb in the last verse of the sūra:** *alā ila'llāhi **taṣīru**'l-umūr* (53). **As recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra, these two occurrences bind its two ends.**

### What this says to the present

Knowing at what point to leave an argument is harder than carrying it on. Most arguments lose their subject after a certain point and turn into each side's keeping of its position against the other.

**The verse names that point and gives a way out.** And the form of the way out is worth recording: not by declaring the other side in the wrong, but **by referring both sides to the same authority.** The sentence *Allāhu rabbunā wa rabbukum* preserves what is shared even at the place where the argument is closed.

## 42/16 — وَٱلَّذِينَ يُحَآجُّونَ فِى ٱللَّهِ مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا ٱسْتُجِيبَ لَهُۥ حُجَّتُهُمْ دَاحِضَةٌ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ وَعَلَيْهِمْ غَضَبٌ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ شَدِيدٌ

*Wa'lladhīna yuḥājjūna fi'llāhi min baʿdi ma'stujība lahu ḥujjatuhum dāḥiḍatun ʿinda rabbihim wa ʿalayhim ghaḍabun wa lahum ʿadhābun shadīd*

"As for those who argue about God after His call has been answered — their proof slips away with their Lord; upon them is anger, and for them a severe punishment."

### مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا ٱسْتُجِيبَ لَهُۥ — the third "after"

**Within three verses the sūra frames the pattern *min baʿdi mā* for the third time. This is a verifiable repetition:**

| Verse | After what came | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | *min baʿdi mā jāʾahumu'l-**ʿilm*** | **They divided** |
| **16** | *min baʿdi ma'**stujība** lah* | **They argue** |
| **(21)** | — | (The pattern changes) |

**This is the measure the sūra sets, and it is the same as the measure recorded in `002-bakara.md` at 2/213: the trouble arises not before knowledge but after it.**

***Ustujība lahu* is passive and its agent is not stated: "it was answered."** By whom? In the classical commentaries there are two readings:

| Reading | Who answered |
|---|---|
| 1 | **People** — there were those who responded to the call, and afterwards they began to argue |
| 2 | **God** — the Prophet's prayer or call was answered, and the matter was settled |

**I do not impose a preference.** What is to be recorded: the verb is passive, and the agent's not being stated throws the weight of the sentence onto **time** — onto the word "after".

### حُجَّتُهُمْ دَاحِضَةٌ — the slipping proof

**دَاحِضَة — root د-ح-ض.** And the concrete sense of the word is striking:

> ***Daḥaḍa'l-qadam* — the foot slipped. *Makānun daḥḍun* — a slippery place.**

**A *dāḥiḍa*, then, means not so much "invalid" as "**unable to hold, with its footing gone**".**

**The choice of word carries a picture, and I record it:** the proof is rejected not because it is false but because **one cannot stand on it.** Like a surface that slips when a foot is set on it.

**The Qurʾān uses the same root elsewhere in a similar sense:** *"…that they might drive out the truth with it (*li-yudḥiḍū bihi'l-ḥaqq*)…"* (al-Kahf 18/56; al-Muʾmin 40/5). **There the verb is transitive: to make the truth slip.**

**And its link with the fifteenth verse:**

| Verse | Sentence | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **15** | *Lā **ḥujjata** baynanā wa baynakum* | **The argument is closed** |
| **16** | *…**ḥujjatuhum** dāḥiḍatun ʿinda rabbihim* | **The proof of the one who carries it on does not hold** |

**The same word (*ḥujja*), in two consecutive verses: one is set aside, the other declared void.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text.

### غَضَبٌ and عَذَابٌ — two indefinites

**غَضَب — root غ-ض-ب.** As recorded in `048-fetih.md`, the root holds the idea of **swelling, intensifying.**

**Both words come indefinite: *ghaḍabun*, *ʿadhābun*.** In Arabic this can convey **magnification (*taʿẓīm*)** or **enlargement by leaving indefinite**: "an anger — and what an anger!"

**And the prepositions differ, which must be recorded too:**

| Construction | Preposition | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| *wa **ʿalayhim** ghaḍab* | *ʿalā* — upon | A state that **settles upon them** |
| *wa **lahum** ʿadhāb* | *li* — for | A recompense **set apart for them** |

**One upon them, the other for them.** This distinction is settled in Arabic, and the construction *al-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim* in `001-fatiha.md` uses the same preposition.

## 42/17 — ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنزَلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ ۗ وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ قَرِيبٌ

*Allāhu'lladhī anzala'l-kitāba bi'l-ḥaqqi wa'l-mīzān; wa mā yudrīka laʿalla's-sāʿata qarīb*

"It is God who sent down the Book with the truth, and the balance. And how do you know? Perhaps the Hour is near."

### ٱلْكِتَٰبَ … وَٱلْمِيزَانَ — a pair that occurs twice in the Qurʾān

**This pair occurs in two places in the Qurʾān, and in both with the same verb (*anzala*):**

| Place | Sentence | What is added |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Ḥadīd 57/25** | *wa **anzalnā maʿahumu'l-kitāba wa'l-mīzāna** li-yaqūma'n-nāsu bi'l-qisṭ* | **+ iron**; and the purpose: the upholding of justice |
| **al-Shūrā 42/17** | *anzala'**l-kitāba bi'l-ḥaqqi wa'l-mīzān*** | **+ *bi'l-ḥaqq***; and after it: the nearness of the Hour |

**This triad was treated in detail in `057-hadid.md` at 57/25.** The table there was: *kitāb* the **principle** of the measure, *mīzān* the **instrument** of the measure, *ḥadīd* the **protection** of the measure. And this was recorded there:

> *"The connection is that the measure has been given. If the balance were an instrument on which people agreed among themselves, the stronger party who broke the agreement would always come out right. By counting the mīzān among the things sent down, the verse places the measure outside the parties."*

**And the analysis of the word *mīzān* is there as well:** root **و-ز-ن**, a noun of instrument on the pattern *mifʿāl* — **an instrument for weighing.** I do not repeat it.

**What belongs here is the difference between the two verses, and it arises from their contexts:**

**Al-Ḥadīd is a sūra about *society*** — property, dominion, inheritance, debt, weapons. There the *mīzān* is tied to **the establishing of justice**: *li-yaqūma'n-nāsu bi'l-qisṭ*.

**Al-Shūrā, however, is a sūra about *division*.** And here what comes after the *mīzān* is not justice but **time**: *laʿalla's-sāʿata qarīb.*

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the continuations of the two verses:** the word for measure does the same work in both sūras — setting up an arbiter outside the parties. But al-Ḥadīd ties that arbiter to **the present**, al-Shūrā to **the end.** **And al-Shūrā's context requires this:** the fifteenth verse had closed the argument and referred the matter to the *yawmu'l-jamʿ*. The seventeenth says that **the measure of that referral has also been sent down.**

**A note on the arrangement:** whether the qualification *bi'l-ḥaqq* attaches to *kitāb* alone or to both has been debated among the grammarians. **The wording allows both; I impose no preference.**

### وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ — root د-ر-ي

**The root was treated in `090-beled.md`, `101-karia.md` and `104-humeze.md`.** The note there: *dirāya* is **knowing a thing by effort, by following a trail**; this is what distinguishes it from *ʿilm* — the verb *darā* carries the sense "he came to know by investigating".

**And in the Qurʾān there are two distinct patterns built on this root, and they do opposite work:**

| Pattern | Its meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***Wa mā adrāka*** | "What made you know?" — **an explanation follows** | *Wa mā adrāka ma'l-qāriʿa* (101/3) — and then it is explained |
| ***Wa mā yudrīka*** | "How do you know?" — **no explanation follows** | Here; and *wa mā yudrīka laʿalla's-sāʿata takūnu qarībā* (al-Aḥzāb 33/63) |

**This distinction is recorded in the classical sources and can be verified from the text:** the perfect pattern gives information, the imperfect pattern **leaves the information closed.**

**And here the imperfect pattern is used.** The sentence, that is, is framed not in order to give a piece of information but **to state that the information is not given.**

### The limit of knowledge at the two ends of the sūra

**The same root comes once more at the end of the sūra, and this was recorded in the section on its structure:**

| Verse | Sentence | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **17** | *Wa mā **yudrīka** laʿalla's-sāʿata qarīb* | **The knowledge of the time** — the Prophet does not know |
| **52** | *Mā kunta **tadrī** ma'l-kitābu wa la'l-īmān* | **The knowledge of the Book** — the Prophet did not know |

**Both sentences draw a limit; but their directions are opposite.** In the seventeenth the limit **still stands**; in the fifty-second the moment at which the limit **was crossed** is recounted. This will be treated in detail at verse 52.

### لَعَلَّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ قَرِيبٌ — a point of grammar

***Al-sāʿa* is a feminine noun; *qarīb* here comes masculine.** One would expect *qarība*.

**The grammarians explain this in several ways:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Al-sāʿa* here means **al-baʿth** (the resurrection) or **the time**; those words are masculine |
| 2 | The *faʿīl* pattern, when it carries **the sense of a passive participle**, may make no distinction of masculine and feminine |
| 3 | *Qarīb* stands here in an **adverbial** position (conveying place or time), and adverbials may come in this form |

**All three explanations are transmitted in the classical grammar books; I impose no preference.** What is to be recorded is that this is a known and much-discussed usage — not a breach of rule.

## 42/18 — يَسْتَعْجِلُ بِهَا ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهَا ۖ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ مُشْفِقُونَ مِنْهَا وَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّهَا ٱلْحَقُّ ۗ أَلَآ إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يُمَارُونَ فِى ٱلسَّاعَةِ لَفِى ضَلَٰلٍۭ بَعِيدٍ

*Yastaʿjilu biha'lladhīna lā yuʾminūna bihā; wa'lladhīna āmanū mushfiqūna minhā wa yaʿlamūna annaha'l-ḥaqq; alā inna'lladhīna yumārūna fi's-sāʿati la-fī ḍalālin baʿīd*

"Those who do not believe in it seek to hasten it. Those who believe are apprehensive of it and know that it is the truth. Know well that those who keep disputing about the Hour are in a far-gone error."

### Two attitudes, one subject

**The verse separates two groups by their reactions to the same thing:**

| | **Those who do not believe** | **Those who believe** |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | *Yastaʿjilūn* — **they seek haste** | *Mushfiqūn* — **they are apprehensive** |
| State of knowledge | *Lā yuʾminūna bihā* | *Yaʿlamūna annaha'l-ḥaqq* |
| Why so | Because they think it will not happen | Because they know it will |

**ISTIʿJĀL — root ع-ج-ل, Form X: to want a thing to come in haste.** *ʿAjala*, in the root's original sense, is **to want a thing before its time.**

**And the logic is plain: to want a thing to come in haste is a sign of not taking it seriously.** A person does not want a reckoning he truly believes will come to come quickly.

### مُشْفِقُون — root ش-ف-ق

**The root was treated in `052-tur.md`, `070-mearic.md` and `084-insikak.md`.** The description there: *shafaq* is the **intermediate light** between day and night; and *ishfāq* is **an anxiety** standing between fear and hope — not fear pure and simple.

**And the distinction the lexicographers record is this:** *khawf* drives away, whereas *ishfāq* **keeps the attachment alive.** A person is *mushfiq* for something he loves as well — indeed the Turkish *şefkat* ("compassion") is from the same root.

**This explains the word's work here:** the state of the believers is not flight but **attentiveness.**

**And the root occurs once more in the sūra — four verses later, but this time of another group:**

| Verse | Who | Why apprehensive |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | *alladhīna āmanū* | **Of the Hour** — because they know it is the truth |
| **22** | *al-ẓālimīn* | Of ***mā kasabū*** — of what they themselves have earned |

**The same word, two different groups, two different causes.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text.

**And the difference must be recorded:** the believers are apprehensive **of what is coming**, the wrongdoers **of what they have done.** The one's anxiety is in front of him, the other's behind.

### يُمَارُون — root م-ر-ي

***Mirāʾ* — to dispute, to wrangle.** The lexicographers give the root's original sense as **to milk** (*marā'ḍ-ḍarra* — he milked the udder) and explain the transition thus: the one who disputes tries to draw words out of the other.

**The root was treated in `054-kamer.md`** (*fa-tamāraw bi'n-nudhur*), and the reading recorded there was this: *tamārī* is "to render something ineffective by opening it to dispute".

**The use here does the same work:** to dispute about the Hour is to turn the Hour into **a subject of dispute** — and once a thing is turned into a subject of dispute, deciding about it can be deferred indefinitely.

***Ḍalālin baʿīd* — "a far-gone error".** The adjective *baʿīd* conveys not the **degree** of the straying but its **distance**: how far one has gone from the road.

## 42/19 — ٱللَّهُ لَطِيفٌۢ بِعِبَادِهِۦ يَرْزُقُ مَن يَشَآءُ ۖ وَهُوَ ٱلْقَوِىُّ ٱلْعَزِيزُ

*Allāhu laṭīfun bi-ʿibādihi yarzuqu man yashāʾ; wa huwa'l-qawiyyu'l-ʿazīz*

"God is subtle towards His servants; He provides for whom He wills. He is the Strong, the Mighty."

### لَطِيف — root ل-ط-ف

**The root was treated in `067-mulk.md`** (*wa huwa'l-laṭīfu'l-khabīr*). The note there: the concrete sense of the root is **fineness and hiddenness** — *laṭīf* is what is too fine to be told apart by the eye; and from there, **what can penetrate the narrowest place.**

**The word has two branches of meaning, and the lexicographers record both:**

| Branch | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fineness / hiddenness** | Too fine to be seen; working too hiddenly to be known |
| **Kindness / gentleness** | One who does good, who does not show harshness. *Luṭf* is from the same root |

**The two branches are bound to one another, and what binds them is this:** what is fine does its work **without being noticed.** Kindness too most often comes unseen.

### Arrangement: two names, two extremes

**The names at the two ends of the verse deserve attention:**

| The beginning of the verse | The end of the verse |
|---|---|
| *Allāhu **laṭīfun** bi-ʿibādih* | *wa huwa'l-**qawiyyu'l-ʿazīz*** |
| **Fineness, nearness, imperceptibility** | **Strength, unassailability** |

**In the same verse, nine words apart, two opposite extremes.**

**And this is a repetition of the pattern recorded at the eleventh verse** — there the transcendence and the hearing-and-seeing came in the same verse. **Here too what puts at a distance and what draws near stand in the same verse**, but the order is reversed: first nearness, then strength.

**I record this as my own reading:** the work of this sequence is to prevent the word *laṭīf* from being understood as **weakness.** What is fine is taken to be fragile; the verse places strength immediately after the fineness.

**قَوِىّ** — root **ق-و-ي**: power, strength; the root holds the idea of **ropes being twisted and made firm** (*quwā* — the strands of a rope). **In `057-hadid.md` at 57/25 the pair *qawiyyun ʿazīz* occurred in the same order.**

### يَرْزُقُ مَن يَشَآءُ — its link with the twelfth verse

**The twelfth verse said *yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu wa yaqdir*. The nineteenth says the same thing more briefly: *yarzuqu man yashāʾ*.**

**The difference: in the twelfth verse there were two verbs (spreads out / restricts), here there is one (provides).** And in the nineteenth a *laṭīf* has been placed at the head of the sentence.

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement:** the same information is given twice, and at the second giving a **quality** is added at its head. The twelfth verse stated the distribution; the nineteenth says what kind of one the distributor is.

## 42/20 — مَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ حَرْثَ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ نَزِدْ لَهُۥ فِى حَرْثِهِۦ ۖ وَمَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ حَرْثَ ٱلدُّنْيَا نُؤْتِهِۦ مِنْهَا وَمَا لَهُۥ فِى ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ مِن نَّصِيبٍ

*Man kāna yurīdu ḥartha'l-ākhirati nazid lahu fī ḥarthih; wa man kāna yurīdu ḥartha'd-dunyā nuʾtihi minhā wa mā lahu fi'l-ākhirati min naṣīb*

"Whoever wants the tillage of the hereafter, We increase for him his tillage. And whoever wants the tillage of this world, We give him of it; but in the hereafter he has no share."

### حَرْث — root ح-ر-ث

**The root was treated in two places in the corpus and I do not repeat the findings there:**

- **In `056-vakia.md` at 56/63**: *ḥarth* is what a person does — ploughing, sowing, watering; *zarʿ* is what happens inside the seed — splitting, taking root, coming up. **And the conclusion recorded there was: "the sowing is yours, the bringing forth is not."**
- **In `002-bakara.md` at 2/205 and 2/223**: that the root is "what reaches into the future"; the corrupter's destroying of the *ḥarth* (2/205), and this recorded at 2/223: ***ḥarth* in Arabic is what is valued, laboured over, protected; it is the farmer's source of living, not something neglected but something watched over.**

**In al-Shūrā the word is used in a third work, and this is the root's share in this sūra: *ḥarth* here is an *object of choice*.**

**And note: the same word is used on both sides.**

| | Construction |
|---|---|
| The hereafter | ***ḥartha'l-ākhirati*** |
| This world | ***ḥartha'd-dunyā*** |

**The verse, that is, does not present this world as without tillage and the hereafter as with it. Both are tillage.** The difference is in which is wanted.

### What the likeness says

**The sense of the word drawn out in `056-vakia.md` works here with its full weight. *Ḥarth* says three things at once:**

| The feature of sowing | Its counterpart in the verse |
|---|---|
| **It calls for labour** | Both sides do something — *yurīdu*, and the effort behind it |
| **Its result lies in the future** | A crop is not eaten at the moment it is sown |
| **Its result does not belong to the sower** | As recorded in `056-vakia.md`: the bringing forth is not a human work |

**The third item is especially important here, and I record it as my own reading:** the verse promises **a return** to both sides, but both returns are in the hand of the giver. *Nazid* and *nuʾtī* — the agent of both verbs is "We".

### Two verbs, two different acts — the difference itself

**And here is the sharpest place in the verse. Neither wish goes unanswered; but the answers are not of the same kind.**

| | **The one who wants the tillage of the hereafter** | **The one who wants the tillage of this world** |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | **نَزِدْ** — *nazid*: **We increase** | **نُؤْتِهِ** — *nuʾtihi*: **We give** |
| Root | ز-ي-د — to increase, to be added to | أ-ت-ي — to come; Form IV: to bring, to give |
| Its object | *fī ḥarthihi* — **within his own tillage** | *minhā* — **of it (a portion)** |
| What happens | **What is sown is made to grow** | **What is asked for is given** |
| Limit | Not stated | ***min*** — conveying partiality |
| His state on the other side | Not stated (his world is not cut off) | ***Mā lahu fi'l-ākhirati min naṣīb*** |

**The difference between the two verbs can be gathered into a single word: one is *increasing*, the other *giving*.**

**Giving is the delivery of what was asked for. Increasing is adding something on top of the delivery.**

**And this distinction is exactly the counterpart of the table of the two prayers recorded in `002-bakara.md`.**

### A comparison with `002-bakara.md` 2/200-202

**The table recorded there was this:**

> | Prayer | What is asked | Result |
> |---|---|---|
> | The first | This world only | *Mā lahu fi'l-ākhirati min **khalāq*** — no share in the hereafter |
> | The second | This world **and** the hereafter | Counted among those accepted |

**And the note entered there was important:**

> *"The point to notice: the prayer that is refused is not refused for asking for this world. **The second prayer also asks for this world.** What is refused is asking for this world **only.**"*

**Now I set the two texts side by side — the overlap is at the level of wording:**

| | **al-Baqara 2/200** | **al-Shūrā 42/20** |
|---|---|---|
| The end of the one who asks for this world only | *Mā lahu fi'l-ākhirati min **khalāq*** | *Mā lahu fi'l-ākhirati min **naṣīb*** |
| Pattern | *Mā lahu fi'l-ākhirati min…* | **Word for word the same** |
| The one word that changes | *khalāq* | *naṣīb* |

**This is a verifiable fact: in the two verses the sentence is the same, only the last word has changed.**

**The difference between the two words must be recorded:**

| Word | Root | Its meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **خَلَاق** (*khalāq*) | خ-ل-ق — to measure and cut | **An allotted portion.** Treated in `002-bakara.md` |
| **نَصِيب** (*naṣīb*) | ن-ص-ب — to set up, to fix in place | **A share set apart by being planted** — a portion marked out like a boundary stone |

**Both words mean "share"; but both stress that the share has been *set apart*.** *Khalāq* set apart by measuring, *naṣīb* by being planted.

**And there is a difference: in al-Baqara two prayers were being compared, in al-Shūrā two wishes.** Al-Baqara's second prayer asked for both this world and the hereafter. **In al-Shūrā, nothing at all is said about the worldly portion of the one who wants the tillage of the hereafter** — it is neither said to be given nor said to be withheld.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of the two texts:** al-Shūrā does not cut off the worldly portion of the one who wants the hereafter; **it leaves it in silence.** And the silence takes on meaning when read together with al-Baqara's second prayer: there both were asked for, and it was not refused.

### Heading off a misreading

**The following conclusion cannot be drawn from the verse: "the one who wants this world is given this world, therefore wealth is a sign of wanting this world."**

**This inference is not supported by the text, and for three reasons:**

1. **The verse states no quantity.** *Nuʾtihi minhā* — "We give him of it"; how much is not said. And *min* conveys partiality.
2. **The twelfth verse has already said that the distribution differs from person to person** (*yabsuṭu… wa yaqdir*); the amount of worldly goods is therefore no indicator.
3. **The twenty-seventh verse will open this further:** provision's not being spread without limit will be presented as **a protection.**

**The verse's measure looks not at property but at *the will*: *man kāna yurīd* — "whoever wants".** And the pattern *kāna yurīd* conveys a continuing want: not a momentary desire but **a life that has turned in a direction.**

### نَزِدْ — the three increases in the sūra

**The root ز-ي-د occurs three times in this sūra, and all three are in the same direction:**

| Verse | Sentence | What is added to |
|---|---|---|
| **20** | *na**zid** lahu fī ḥarthih* | **The tillage** |
| **23** | *na**zid** lahu fīhā ḥusnā* | **The good deed** |
| **26** | *wa ya**zīdu**hum min faḍlih* | **The answer** |

**In all three, what is increased is what the person himself has brought.** In the twentieth what he sowed, in the twenty-third what he earned, in the twenty-sixth what he asked for.

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text and I record it as a pattern of the sūra:** *ziyāda* is nowhere in the sūra something given from nothing; it always comes **on top of something.**

### What this says to the present

The distinction the verse sets up is not "this world is bad, the hereafter is good"; **both tillages are tillage.** The distinction is in **how far** the wanting reaches.

**And its practical consequence is this:** an effort that aims only at the visible outcome may well obtain that outcome. The verse does not deny it — *nuʾtihi minhā*, "We give", it says. What it says is that such an effort **brings its own limit with it**: what is received is as much as what was wanted.

**What increases, increases only in what is sown for the long term.** And this is the logic of the likeness of tillage itself: a field sown for harvest grows; what is gathered to be eaten at once comes to an end.

## 42/21 — أَمْ لَهُمْ شُرَكَٰٓؤُا۟ شَرَعُوا۟ لَهُم مِّنَ ٱلدِّينِ مَا لَمْ يَأْذَنۢ بِهِ ٱللَّهُ ۚ وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةُ ٱلْفَصْلِ لَقُضِىَ بَيْنَهُمْ ۗ وَإِنَّ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

*Am lahum shurakāʾu sharaʿū lahum mina'd-dīni mā lam yaʾdhan bihi'llāh; wa lawlā kalimatu'l-faṣli la-quḍiya baynahum; wa inna'ẓ-ẓālimīna lahum ʿadhābun alīm*

"Or have they partners who have laid down for them, as religion, what God has not permitted? Were it not for the word of separation, it would have been decided between them. And for the wrongdoers there is a painful punishment."

### شَرَعُوا۟ — the same verb, a different agent

**This verse is set face to face with the thirteenth by way of a single word. And this is the sharpest verbal opposition in the sūra:**

| Verse | Sentence | Agent | Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13** | ***Sharaʿa** lakum mina'd-dīni mā waṣṣā bihi Nūḥan…* | **God** | What was enjoined upon five prophets |
| **21** | *…**sharaʿū** lahum mina'd-dīni mā lam yaʾdhan bihi'llāh* | **The partners** | **What God has not permitted** |

**The same verb, the same preposition (*mina'd-dīn*), the same structure. The only thing that changes: who lays it down.**

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text and was recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra.** Eight verses apart, the same verb is used twice with two opposite agents.

**And the concrete sense of the root (treated at the thirteenth verse: *the path down to water*) makes this opposition still more visible.** On both sides there is a claim of "opening a way". The difference is in where the way arrives — and no information is given about where the second way arrives; it is only said to be **unpermitted.**

### مَا لَمْ يَأْذَنۢ بِهِ ٱللَّهُ — where the measure lies

**The sentence does not describe what the false way is; it gives the measure by which it is recognised: *permission*.**

**أَذِنَ — root أ-ذ-ن.** The concrete sense of the root is **the ear** (*udhun*); *idhn* is derived from "giving ear" — hearing a request and approving it.

**And this is worth recording in point of arrangement:** the measure looks not at the content of the rule laid down but at **its source.** That a rule seems reasonable or useful is not the subject of the measure.

**This measure will be used once more in the fifty-first verse:** *fa-yūḥiya **bi-idhnihi** mā yashāʾ* — what the messenger conveys is likewise tied to permission. **The same word, at the two ends: the unpermittedness of the false (21), the permittedness of the true (51).** This is a verifiable fact.

### كَلِمَةُ ٱلْفَصْلِ — the word of separation

**In the fourteenth verse *kalimatun sabaqat min rabbika ilā ajalin musammā* occurred. Here comes *kalimatu'l-faṣl*, and the close is the same: *la-quḍiya baynahum*.**

**فَصْل — root ف-ص-ل: to separate two things, to cut the connection between them.** *Faṣl* (a section), *fāṣila* (a verse-ending), *tafṣīl* (setting out in detail) are from the same root.

**The difference between the two constructions:**

| Verse | Construction | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | *Kalimatun sabaqat… ilā ajalin musammā* | **A note of time** — deferred |
| **21** | *Kalimatu'l-faṣl* | **A note of quality** — that which separates |

**In both verses the same outcome: the judgement is not given now.** And in both the cause is a "word". **The sūra, that is, names the decision of deferral twice, under two different names.**

## 42/22 — تَرَى ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ مُشْفِقِينَ مِمَّا كَسَبُوا۟ وَهُوَ وَاقِعٌۢ بِهِمْ ۗ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ فِى رَوْضَاتِ ٱلْجَنَّاتِ ۖ لَهُم مَّا يَشَآءُونَ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ ٱلْفَضْلُ ٱلْكَبِيرُ

*Tara'ẓ-ẓālimīna mushfiqīna mimmā kasabū wa huwa wāqiʿun bihim; wa'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti fī rawḍāti'l-jannāt; lahum mā yashāʾūna ʿinda rabbihim; dhālika huwa'l-faḍlu'l-kabīr*

"You will see the wrongdoers apprehensive of what they have earned — and it will fall upon them. Those who believed and did good works are in the meadows of the Gardens. Whatever they wish for is theirs with their Lord. That is the great bounty."

### مُشْفِقِينَ مِمَّا كَسَبُوا۟

**The *mushfiqūn* of the eighteenth verse returns here, and the difference was tabulated above: there the believers are apprehensive of what is coming, here the wrongdoers of what they have done.**

***Kasabū* — root ك-س-ب.** It was treated in `074-muddessir.md`: **to obtain, to earn, to gather and bring in.** And what was recorded there was this: **the root is used in the Qurʾān for both good and evil and is neutral.**

**In this use the word's neutrality produces a sharpness:** what a person fears is not a punishment coming from outside but **his own earnings.** The sentence puts "what they have earned" in the place of the thing feared.

**وَهُوَ وَاقِعٌۢ بِهِمْ — "and it will fall upon them".** *Wāqiʿ* is an active participle and conveys the future. **The root و-ق-ع was treated in `056-vakia.md` as the name of that sūra:** to fall, to come down, to come to pass.

**A point of arrangement: the sentence says the fear is not in vain.** Usually fear is felt about something that will not happen, and one is consoled. **Here it is the reverse: the fear is correct.**

### رَوْضَاتِ ٱلْجَنَّاتِ — "the meadows of the Gardens"

**رَوْضَة — root ر-و-ض.** The concrete sense of the word is striking:

> ***Rawḍa*, in the lexicographers' description: a hollow or a level place where water gathers and where, for that reason, the vegetation is thick.**

**A *rawḍa*, that is, is not just any garden but a place that holds water.** From the root come *riyāḍ* (its plural) and *riyāḍa* (the breaking in and training of an animal); the lexicographers link the second sense by way of "softening".

***Rawḍāt* is plural and occurs only here in the Qurʾān.** *Jannāt* is plural too. **The construction, then, is a plural of a plural: "the gardens of the gardens".**

**And the difference from the descriptions of the Garden recorded in `056-vakia.md` and `050-kaf.md` is this:** there **objects** were enumerated — fruit, shade, spouses, vessels. Here a **place** is named, and after it comes a single sentence: *lahum mā yashāʾūn* — "whatever they wish is theirs."

**No enumeration is made, that is.** I record this as an observation about the arrangement: instead of a list, an open end has been set down.

### لَهُم مَّا يَشَآءُونَ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ

**Up to this point in the sūra the verb *yashāʾ* has always been used of God** (8, 12, 13, 19, 21). **Here for the first time it is used of a human being: *mā yashāʾūn* — "what they wish".**

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text, and I record it.** And the qualification *ʿinda rabbihim* has been added: the wish is free, but where it stands is stated.

***Al-faḍlu'l-kabīr* — root ف-ض-ل: the part of a thing that is over and above what is needed; the surplus.** It was treated in `062-cuma.md` and `057-hadid.md`. **The root's original sense is "surplus"; *faḍl*, that is, is what is set on top of what is deserved.**

**And this stands on the same line as the verb *nazid* in the twentieth verse:** something added. **The twenty-sixth verse will join the two:** *wa **yazīduhum** min **faḍlih*** — increase and surplus in the same verse.

## 42/23 — ذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِى يُبَشِّرُ ٱللَّهُ عِبَادَهُ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ ۗ قُل لَّآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا إِلَّا ٱلْمَوَدَّةَ فِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ ۗ وَمَن يَقْتَرِفْ حَسَنَةً نَّزِدْ لَهُۥ فِيهَا حُسْنًا ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ

*Dhālika'lladhī yubashshiru'llāhu ʿibādahu'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāt; qul lā asʾalukum ʿalayhi ajran illa'l-mawaddata fi'l-qurbā; wa man yaqtarif ḥasanatan nazid lahu fīhā ḥusnā; inna'llāha ghafūrun shakūr*

"That is the good news God gives to His servants who believe and do good works. Say: **I ask you no wage for it — save affection in kinship.** Whoever earns a good deed, We add to it a further good for him. God is forgiving, and gives abundant return."

**This verse carries one of the most debated expressions in the Qurʾān. The disagreement is large, and it will be given without concealment and without partisanship.**

**And first I enter a note of method, because the subject requires it:** three readings will be set out in a table below. **In this commentary none of them is preferred, no superiority is attributed to any of them, and no insinuation about any group is framed.** Throughout history this verse has been the subject of different readings by different circles; **per the method (`USLUP.md`), no inter-school polemic is entered into.** What is done here is an inventory of the readings transmitted in the classical exegetical sources and of their grounds.

### مَوَدَّة — root و-د-د

**The root was treated in `085-buruc.md` in connection with the name *al-Wadūd*.** The note there was this:

> *"The sense of the root: to love. But in Arabic there is a difference the lexicographers record between *ḥubb* (love) and *wudd*: *wudd* is love in its **outwardly expressed, behaviour-become** state. Not an inclination felt only within; a nearness that reaches the other party."*

**This distinction is at work here and must be recorded:** what is asked for is not a **feeling** but a feeling turned into conduct. The pattern *mawadda* too (a *maṣdar mīmī*) gives not the act itself but **the name of the act.**

**The root's other derivatives:** *wudd* (love), *wadūd* (one who loves much / who is much loved), *Wadd* (the name of an idol known before Islam — in `085-buruc.md` it was recorded that no ruling is issued about this relation), *tawaddud* (to show affection).

### قُرْبَىٰ — root ق-ر-ب

**The root was treated in `050-kaf.md`, `056-vakia.md` and `090-beled.md`:** nearness; nearness of distance and nearness of kinship.

***Qurbā* is a noun on the pattern *fuʿlā*, and it is used in Arabic in two senses:**

| Sense | Example of its use in the Qurʾān |
|---|---|
| **Kinship** | *Dhi'l-qurbā* — "the possessor of nearness", that is, a relative (al-Baqara 2/83, 2/177; al-Nisāʾ 4/36 and elsewhere) |
| **Drawing near / a state of nearness** | *Qurbatan ʿinda'llāh* (al-Tawba 9/99) — a means of nearness with God |

**And the particle *fī* in the verse is decisive as well:** *al-mawaddata **fi'l**-qurbā*. **This particle can be understood in three ways:**

- **Locative**: "**within** the kinship" — the field in which the affection is to be found.
- **Causal**: "**because of** the kinship" — the ground of the affection.
- **Directional**: "**towards** nearness" — the aim of the affection.

**How the sentence is to be understood depends largely on how these two indeterminacies (the sense of the word and the work of the particle) are resolved.**

### إِلَّا — the kind of exception

**There is a third indeterminacy, and it is at the centre of the dispute: what kind of exception is *illā* here?**

| Kind | What it means | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Connected exception (*muttaṣil*)** | What is excepted is of the same kind as what it is excepted from | "I ask no wage — **except this wage**" |
| **Disconnected exception (*munqaṭiʿ*)** | What is excepted is not of the preceding kind | "I ask no wage — **but I do ask this**" (which is not a wage) |

**This distinction is critical**, because in other places the Qurʾān repeats in absolute terms that the prophets asked for no wage:

> *"Say: whatever wage I have asked of you, **let it be yours**; my reward is with God alone."* (Sabaʾ 34/47)
>
> *"Say: I ask you no wage for it — **except that whoever wishes may take a way to his Lord.**"* (al-Furqān 25/57)

**Al-Furqān 25/57 is particularly striking**, because there too the same structure is present: a refusal of wage plus an exception with *illā* — and what is excepted is something that is **to the other party's own benefit.**

**This is an internal textual indication supporting the disconnected reading, and I record it.** But it does not settle the verse by itself, because whose benefit *al-mawadda fi'l-qurbā* is remains disputed.

### Three main readings

**The chief readings transmitted for this expression in the classical exegetical sources are these. The table is not a list of preferences but an inventory.**

| | **Reading** | **How it is understood** | **Its grounds** | **The objection raised against it** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | **Affection on account of the tie of kinship / not doing harm** | *Qurbā* = "the kinship between us". The sentence: "I ask no wage; **at the very least, on account of the kinship between us, do not show me enmity, show affection**" | **1.** The sūra is Meccan and those addressed are Quraysh. **2.** *Fī* is read as causal. **3.** That there were ties of kinship with the branches of Quraysh is historically known. **4.** *Illā* is counted disconnected: what is asked is not a wage but the removal of an obstacle | The words "between us" are not in the wording; they must be supplied |
| **(b)** | **Affection for the Prophet's kin** | *Qurbā* = "my kin". The sentence: "I ask no wage; **except that you love my kin**" | **1.** The word *al-qurbā* is widely used in the Qurʾān in the sense of **relatives** (*dhi'l-qurbā*). **2.** *Fī* is read as locative: the field in which the affection is to lie. **3.** There are reports transmitted in this direction | It is said that a difficulty is seen in the sūra's being Meccan and those addressed being the mushrikūn; some for that reason transmit that the verse is Medinan |
| **(c)** | **Deeds that bring one near to God** | *Qurbā* = "nearness, drawing near". The sentence: "I ask no wage; **except that you have affection for what brings one near to God**" — that is, something that is to the other party's own benefit | **1.** The word *qurbā*/*qurba* is used in the Qurʾān in the sense of "nearness with God". **2.** The continuation of the verse appears to support this: *wa man yaqtarif ḥasanatan nazid lahu fīhā ḥusnā* — immediately afterwards **earning a good deed** is spoken of. **3.** It sits on the same line as Sabaʾ 34/47 and al-Furqān 25/57: what is asked is to the other party's benefit | The use of *al-qurbā* with the definite article in the sense of "nearness to God" has fewer parallels than the word's common usage |

**All three of these readings occur in the classical exegetical sources. I do not impose a preference.**

### Why the disagreement grew so large

**I record an observation, and this is not a verdict but a finding about the structure of the disagreement:**

**Three indeterminacies have come together in the verse:**

| Indeterminacy | How many possibilities |
|---|---|
| The sense of the word *qurbā* | At least two (kinship / nearness) |
| The work of the particle *fī* | At least three (locative / causal / directional) |
| The kind of *illā* | Two (connected / disconnected) |

**The combination of three indeterminacies allows, at the level of language, more than one consistent reading.** And this explains why the disagreement has not been settled: **none of the parties breaks a rule of the language.**

**This accords with the method recorded in `002-bakara.md`:** where a text allows more than one reading, to declare one of the readings "the right one" is to do more than the text itself does.

### What is common to all three readings

**And this needs recording, because it is overshadowed by the dispute: all three of the three readings accept the first half of the same sentence.**

***Lā asʾalukum ʿalayhi ajrā*** — **"I ask you no wage."**

**This sentence comes again and again in the Qurʾān from the mouths of prophets** — the same pattern is used in the stories of Noah, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lot (Lūṭ) and Shuʿayb (one after another in Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ). **Being unpaid, that is, is one of the defining attributes of prophethood in the Qurʾān.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the disagreement is about the **exception** in the sentence; not about its **principal ruling.** And the principal ruling covers a wider field than the part disputed: the call has no price.

### وَمَن يَقْتَرِفْ حَسَنَةً نَّزِدْ لَهُۥ فِيهَا حُسْنًا

***Iqtirāf* — root ق-ر-ف, Form VIII.** The concrete sense of the root: **to peel or scrape the bark off a thing** (*qirf* — the bark of a tree). From this the sense **to earn, to acquire** is derived.

**And one feature of the word must be recorded:** in Arabic *iqtirāf* is most often used for the acquiring of **bad** things — *iqtarafa dhanban* (he committed a sin). Indeed in other places in the Qurʾān it occurs in that direction (al-Anʿām 6/113, 6/120).

***Here, however, it is used of a good deed: yaqtarif ḥasanatan.***

**I record this as an observation about the word.** And the concrete sense of the word says one further thing: to obtain by peeling off the bark is **to obtain by effort.** A good deed, that is, is named here not as something that happens by chance but as something **earned.**

***Nazid lahu fīhā ḥusnā* — the *nazid* of the twentieth verse returns here.** And the arrangement is the same: *nazid lahu **fī** ḥarthih* (20) / *nazid lahu **fīhā** ḥusnā* (23). **The same verb, the same particle, the same structure — three verses apart.**

### غَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ

**غَفُور — root غ-ف-ر: to cover.** It was treated in `058-mucadele.md` in comparison with *al-ʿAfuww*: *ghufrān* covers, *ʿafw* erases.

**شَكُور — root ش-ك-ر.** And the use of this name of God must be dwelt on.

**The concrete sense of the root *shukr*** is attached by the lexicographers to two things: *shakara'd-dābba* — the animal grew fat on little fodder; and *shakarati'sh-shāt* — the ewe's milk increased. **The shared picture: giving much in return for little.**

**And this picture explains the name's use of God:** *Shakūr* is the one who gives a great return for a servant's small work. **The word conveys not feeling need and giving thanks, but giving back more than in full.**

**And the name's place in the verse must be recorded:** immediately before it, *nazid lahu fīhā ḥusnā* was said — "We increase". **The name *Shakūr* is the name of that increase.**

**And the root will occur once more in the sūra, this time of a human being:**

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **23** | *ghafūrun **shakūr*** | **God** |
| **33** | *li-kulli ṣabbārin **shakūr*** | **A human being** |

**The same pattern (*faʿūl*), two parties.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text and was recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra. **When it is a human being's attribute it means not "one who gives much for little" but "one who returns what is given"; the pattern is the same, the direction reversed.**

## 42/24 — أَمْ يَقُولُونَ ٱفْتَرَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ كَذِبًا ۖ فَإِن يَشَإِ ٱللَّهُ يَخْتِمْ عَلَىٰ قَلْبِكَ ۗ وَيَمْحُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْبَٰطِلَ وَيُحِقُّ ٱلْحَقَّ بِكَلِمَٰتِهِۦٓ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ عَلِيمٌۢ بِذَاتِ ٱلصُّدُورِ

*Am yaqūlūna'ftarā ʿala'llāhi kadhibā; fa-in yashaʾi'llāhu yakhtim ʿalā qalbik; wa yamḥu'llāhu'l-bāṭila wa yuḥiqqu'l-ḥaqqa bi-kalimātih; innahu ʿalīmun bi-dhāti'ṣ-ṣudūr*

"Or do they say, 'He has forged a lie against God'? Had God willed, He would have sealed your heart. God erases falsehood and establishes the truth by His words. He knows what is in the breasts."

### The third *am*

**After the ninth and twenty-first verses, this is the third *am* question.** And the subject changes: taking a protector (9), laying down a religion (21), **forging a word** (24).

**افْتَرَىٰ — root ف-ر-ي: to cut and shape something, to slit a hide.** The verb *farā* means "he slit the hide, he cut it"; and from it the sense **to forge** is derived. **The transition the lexicographers record is this: to forge is to cut and shape speech into something that never was.**

**From the same root *fariyyā* (Maryam 19/27) — "an unheard-of thing, a thing cut and shaped".**

### فَإِن يَشَإِ ٱللَّهُ يَخْتِمْ عَلَىٰ قَلْبِكَ — a difficult sentence

**What this sentence says must be dwelt on, because translated directly it seems to say something unexpected.**

**The readings transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | How it is understood |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A hypothetical condition** — "If you really had forged it, God, if He willed, would seal your heart and cut off the revelation." That is, the impossibility of the charge of forgery is shown: were it a forgery, its source would be cut off |
| 2 | **The context of patience** — "If God wills, He seals your heart", that is, He binds it to patience, makes it able to bear hardship. *Khatm* here means "making firm" |
| 3 | **A general statement of power** — what is at issue is not a threat but the expression of the limitlessness of power |

**All three readings are transmitted; I impose no preference.**

***Khatm* — root خ-ت-م: to seal, to close the mouth of a thing and set a seal on it.** It was treated in `083-mutaffifin.md` in connection with *raḥīqin makhtūm*. **Two things are in the root: closing and marking.**

### وَيَمْحُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْبَٰطِلَ — a note on the orthography of the muṣḥaf

**This verb is written in the codices without the final *wāw*: وَيَمْحُ.** Its recitation is *wa yamḥu*.

**This is a known feature of the *rasm* of the muṣḥaf and has parallels in the Qurʾān** (such as *sanadʿu* — al-ʿAlaq 96/18). **I record it; I draw from it no meaning and no calculation.** It is a fact belonging to the tradition of writing.

**مَحْو — root م-ح-و: to erase, to obliterate without leaving a trace.** And the distinction recorded in `058-mucadele.md` is at work here: *ghufrān* covers, *maḥw* erases. **Falsehood is not covered; it is erased.**

### وَيُحِقُّ ٱلْحَقَّ بِكَلِمَٰتِهِۦ

***Yuḥiqqu* (Form IV) — "to make real, to establish".** From the same root as *ḥaqq*.

**Two verbs set up a contrast:**

| Verb | Object | The act |
|---|---|---|
| *Yamḥū* | *al-bāṭil* | **Erases** |
| *Yuḥiqqu* | *al-ḥaqq* | **Establishes** |

**And the instrument is the same: *bi-kalimātihi* — "by His words".** Both acts, that is, are done at the level of speech. **This is connected with the charge at the head of the verse:** the charge was about a **word** (did he forge it?); and the answer too is given by way of a word.

***ʿAlīmun bi-dhāti'ṣ-ṣudūr* — "knowing what is in the breasts".** The construction was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/6. ***Dhāt* here means "what is possessed, what is within"; *dhātu'ṣ-ṣudūr* — the contents of the breasts.**

**And its placement is meaningful:** the charge is a charge about intention ("he forged it"). **The answer closes with who it is that knows intention.**

## 42/25 — وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى يَقْبَلُ ٱلتَّوْبَةَ عَنْ عِبَادِهِۦ وَيَعْفُوا۟ عَنِ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا تَفْعَلُونَ

*Wa huwa'lladhī yaqbalu't-tawbata ʿan ʿibādihi wa yaʿfū ʿani's-sayyiʾāti wa yaʿlamu mā tafʿalūn*

"It is He who accepts repentance from His servants, erases evil deeds, and knows what you do."

### يَقْبَلُ ٱلتَّوْبَةَ عَنْ عِبَادِهِ — a point about a particle

**The sentence uses not *min* but *ʿan*: *yaqbalu't-tawbata ʿan ʿibādih*.**

**This is a detail the grammarians have dwelt on.** *Min* conveys "from" (a source), whereas *ʿan* conveys "from, off, on behalf of" and carries a sense of **moving away** or **taking off from.**

**The explanations transmitted:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | *ʿAn* here stands for *min*; the two particles can be used in place of one another |
| 2 | *ʿAn* means "off": the repentance is **taken off** the servant, that is, its burden is lifted |
| 3 | *ʿAn* means "for the benefit of, on behalf of": the acceptance takes place **to the servant's advantage** |

**I do not impose a preference.** The second and third explanations rest on the idea of direction the word carries and are worth recording as a point of language.

***Tawba* — root ت-و-ب: to turn.** And the word's being used both of the servant and of God is striking: the servant *tāba ila'llāh* (turned to God), God *tāba ʿala'l-ʿabd* (turned to the servant). **The same verb, working in two directions.**

### وَيَعْفُوا۟ عَنِ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ — the four pardons in the sūra

**The root ع-ف-و was treated in `058-mucadele.md`.** The note there was this: **the concrete sense of the root is to erase, to wipe out a trace; like the wind erasing footprints in sand.** And its difference from *ghufrān*: **what is covered is still there but unseen; what is erased is no longer there.**

**And now let us look at the root's distribution in this sūra — this is one of the most important weaves in it:**

| Verse | Sentence | Who pardons | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **25** | *wa **yaʿfū** ʿani's-sayyiʾāt* | **God** | Evil deeds |
| **30** | *wa **yaʿfū** ʿan kathīr* | **God** | **Much** of what is earned |
| **34** | *wa **yaʿfu** ʿan kathīr* | **God** | **Much** of what is earned |
| **40** | *fa-man **ʿafā** wa aṣlaḥ* | **A human being** | What was done to him |

**Three of the four occurrences are God's, one a human being's.** And all four are from the same form of the same root.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this distribution:** before commanding pardon of a human being, the sūra shows pardon **three times as a divine act.** That the recompense of the one who pardons in the fortieth verse is left to God (*fa-ajruhu ʿala'llāh*) accords with this sequence — the human being turns out to have done something that is being done in any case.

**This is an inference; it is not a transmitted report.**

### وَيَعْلَمُ مَا تَفْعَلُونَ — and the change of address

**The first two parts of the sentence come in the third person (*ʿibādih*), the last in the second person plural (*tafʿalūn*).**

**This is an *iltifāt***, and its effect is worth recording: the sentence begins as a general statement and ends by turning to the one addressed. **The sentence "He accepts repentance from His servants", that is, comes directly upon the reader with "and He knows what you do".**

## 42/26 — وَيَسْتَجِيبُ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَيَزِيدُهُم مِّن فَضْلِهِۦ ۚ وَٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ شَدِيدٌ

*Wa yastajību'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti wa yazīduhum min faḍlih; wa'l-kāfirūna lahum ʿadhābun shadīd*

"He answers those who believe and do good works, and gives them more of His bounty. As for those who reject, for them is a severe punishment."

### يَسْتَجِيبُ — root ج-و-ب

**The root was treated in `089-fecr.md`** (*jābu'ṣ-ṣakhra bi'l-wād* — "they hewed the rocks in the valley"). The note there: **the concrete sense of the root is to cleave, to cut through.**

**And the sense *ijāba* (answering) is derived from this, and the transition is instructive:** an answer is the **filling of the gap** a question or a call opens, the cutting through of the distance between. **The word "answer" in Arabic, that is, is a verb of *reaching*.**

**The root occurs four times in this sūra, and all four stand on its spine:**

| Verse | Word | Who answers | Whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **16** | *ustujība **lahu*** | (passive) | The call |
| **26** | *ya**stajīb**u'lladhīna āmanū* | **God** | Those who believe |
| **38** | *i**stajāb**ū li-rabbihim* | **Those who believe** | Their Lord |
| **47** | *i**stajīb**ū li-rabbikum* | **A command** — to those addressed | Their Lord |

**The arrangement sets up this order:** first it is reported that the call has been answered (16), then that God answers (26), then that the servants answer (38), and at the last answering is commanded (47).

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text.**

### A grammatical disagreement

**There are two readings of how the verb *yastajīb* takes *alladhīna āmanū*:**

| Reading | How | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Alladhīna* is the **object** — the verb transitive | "**God** answers those who believe" |
| 2 | *Alladhīna* is the **agent** | "**Those who believe** answer Him" |

**The first reading is the widespread one** and accords with the continuation of the sentence: *wa **yazīduhum** min faḍlih* — "and increases for them"; the agent there is plainly God. **The agent of the second clause, that is, should be the agent of the first as well.**

**I record this ground but impose no preference;** the second reading is also transmitted.

### وَيَزِيدُهُم مِّن فَضْلِهِ

**The *nazid* of the twentieth and twenty-third verses comes here for the third time** — and this time together with the word *faḍl*.

**In the twenty-second verse *al-faḍlu'l-kabīr* occurred; root ف-ض-ل: surplus, what is over.** **In the sentence, then, two words of increase come one upon the other: *yazīduhum* + *min faḍlihi*.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement:** "He gives them more of His more." The same idea is reinforced by two words.

## 42/27 — وَلَوْ بَسَطَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِعِبَادِهِۦ لَبَغَوْا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَٰكِن يُنَزِّلُ بِقَدَرٍ مَّا يَشَآءُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ بِعِبَادِهِۦ خَبِيرٌۢ بَصِيرٌ

*Wa law basaṭa'llāhu'r-rizqa li-ʿibādihi la-baghaw fi'l-arḍ; wa lākin yunazzilu bi-qadarin mā yashāʾ; innahu bi-ʿibādihi khabīrun baṣīr*

"Had God spread out provision (without limit) for His servants, they would have overstepped on the earth. But He sends down by a measure what He wills. He is aware of His servants and sees them."

### بَسَطَ — root ب-س-ط

**The root was treated in `071-nuh.md`:** to spread, to lay out, to open, to widen; *bisāṭ* — a rug spread on the ground; its opposite *qabḍ*.

**And in the twelfth verse the same root occurred as *yabsuṭu*.** The difference between the two verses was tabulated above; I repeat it here because the right understanding of the verse depends on it:

| | **42/12** | **42/27** |
|---|---|---|
| Mood | **Declarative** — what is | **Conditional** (*law*) — **what has not been** |
| To whom | *li-man yashāʾ* — according to the person | *li-ʿibādihi* — **to His servants**, general |
| What it says | The distribution is from one hand | **No limitless spreading took place** |

***Law* conveys an unfulfilled condition.** The verse, that is, speaks not of **something that has happened** but of **a possibility that has not.** This is the same as the finding recorded for the same pattern in `056-vakia.md` at 56/65: *law nashāʾu* — "had We willed" — an unfulfilled condition.

### لَبَغَوْا۟ — and the root's place in the sūra

**The root *baghy* was treated at the fourteenth verse and grounded on `049-hucurat.md` 49/9: wanting in its boundary-crossing state.**

**And here the logic of the root works exactly:**

*Baghy* is a word that passes from "to want" to "to overstep". **Provision's being spread without limit removes the material bound that stands before wanting.** Once the bound is gone, nothing is left to limit the wanting itself.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the semantic transition of the root:** the verse does not say that the human being is evil; **it says that wanting does not limit itself.** The bound comes from outside.

### وَلَٰكِن يُنَزِّلُ بِقَدَرٍ مَّا يَشَآءُ — measure

***Qadar* — root ق-د-ر.** **It was treated in detail in `097-kadr.md` in its three senses:**

| Sense | Which aspect of the Night in Sūrat al-Qadr |
|---|---|
| **Measure / determining** | What was done |
| **Worth / honour** | What it is worth |
| **Narrowness / fullness** | What it is filled with |

**And in `054-kamer.md` at 54/49 (*innā kulla shayʾin khalaqnāhu bi-qadar*) the same pattern — *bi-qadar* — was treated.** The note there was this: in al-Qamar the root is **distributed over two different words** — *bi-qadar* carries the branch of measure, *muqtadir* the branch of power; and this makes it easier to read the forty-ninth verse independently of the debate about divine decree.

**In al-Shūrā there is the same separation, and I record it:**

| Word | Verse | Which branch |
|---|---|---|
| *yaqdir* | **12** | **Narrowness** — to restrict |
| *bi-qadar* | **27** | **Measure** |
| *qadīr* | **9, 29, 50** | **Power** |

**The sūra, that is, has given the three branches of the root to three different words.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text and has the same structure as the distribution in al-Qamar.

***Bi-qadar* here is in the same sense as in al-Qamar 54/49: measure, quantity.** And as recorded in `054-kamer.md`, this commentary does not enter into the theological debate about divine decree; it looks at what the word says.

***Yunazzilu* — Form II, *tafʿīl*.** As recorded in `057-hadid.md` at 57/9, this form conveys sending down **piece by piece, repeatedly.** **The measure, that is, is not only in the quantity but in the time as well: not all at once, but at intervals.**

### إِنَّهُۥ بِعِبَادِهِۦ خَبِيرٌۢ بَصِيرٌ

**The verse closes with two names, both of them words of knowing; but from different sides:**

| Name | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **خَبِير** | خ-ب-ر | **Inner knowledge** — knowing the hidden side of a thing. The lexicographers relate the root to *khabr* (broken ground; ploughed land): knowledge that has been gone into, turned over |
| **بَصِير** | ب-ص-ر | **Outer knowledge** — seeing |

**The two coming together covers both sides of knowledge: the seen and the unseen.**

**And its placement must be recorded:** the verse spoke of a **restriction** (provision comes down limited). **Immediately after the restriction, it is said that the one who restricts knows His servants.**

**This is a repetition of the pattern recorded at the eleventh verse:** a name that draws near is set after a sentence that puts at a distance. **And the name *baṣīr* occurs twice in the sūra, in both places in this position** — 11 and 27. This is a verifiable fact.

### Heading off a misreading — and this matters

**The following conclusion cannot be drawn from this verse: "since poverty is a protection, there is no need to try to better anyone's condition."**

**This inference is not supported by the text, for three reasons:**

1. **The subject of the verse is not one person's provision but *the total*.** *Li-ʿibādihi* — "for His servants", plural and general. The verse speaks of the absence of a limitless spreading; it does not explain any one person's straitness.
2. **The Qurʾān at the same time commands the seeking of provision:** *"…disperse in the land and **seek** of God's bounty"* (al-Jumuʿa 62/10) — treated in `062-cuma.md`. **With the same root (ب-غ-ي, *ibtighāʾ*),** and there in a positive sense.
3. **The sūra itself commands spending** (38): *wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn*. **A text cannot both command sharing and defend straitness.**

**What is to be recorded is this:** the verse states that there is a measure; it issues no verdict about where the measure lies for any individual. **And the word *baghy* is a warning — whom it is aimed at is not stated in the verse; it covers everyone whose means increase.**

### What this says to the present

The verse's finding rests on a testable human observation: **an increase in means brings an increase in wanting; and wanting does not, together with the means, satisfy itself.**

This is said not as a rule of morals but as a **working.** And the word itself carries it: *baghy*, at its root, is "to want". **The problem, that is, is not wanting; it is that wanting does not carry its own bound.**

**What carries the bound is, according to the verse, outside: *bi-qadar*.** And this is the first instance of the balance that will be set up in the fortieth verse of the sūra — there too a right will be granted, but by measure: *sayyiʾatun **mithluhā***.

## 42/28 — وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُنَزِّلُ ٱلْغَيْثَ مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا قَنَطُوا۟ وَيَنشُرُ رَحْمَتَهُۥ ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلْوَلِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ

*Wa huwa'lladhī yunazzilu'l-ghaytha min baʿdi mā qanaṭū wa yanshuru raḥmatah; wa huwa'l-waliyyu'l-ḥamīd*

"It is He who sends down the rain after they have despaired, and spreads out His mercy. He is the Protector, the Praiseworthy."

### ٱلْغَيْث — which rain

**There are many words for rain in Arabic, and each brings a different aspect forward. The particularity of the word *ghayth* must be recorded.**

**كوك غ-ي-ث: to rush to help, to come to the rescue.** *Ighātha* — to help; *mughīth* — one who comes to the rescue. **Indeed *istighātha* (calling for help) is from the same root.**

**A *ghayth*, then, is not "falling water" but water that comes to the rescue.** The word itself names the rain as a help.

**For comparison:**

| Word | Root | What it brings forward |
|---|---|---|
| **مَطَر** (*maṭar*) | م-ط-ر | The event of raining itself; in most places in the Qurʾān in a context of **punishment** (*amṭarnā ʿalayhim*) |
| **غَيْث** (*ghayth*) | غ-ي-ث | **Coming to the rescue** |
| **وَدْق** (*wadq*) | و-د-ق | The grains of rain, the drops |
| **صَيِّب** (*ṣayyib*) | ص-و-ب | A downpour that finds its mark — treated in `002-bakara.md` |

**And the placement in the verse confirms the word: *min baʿdi mā qanaṭū* — "after they had despaired".** Help becomes "rescue" only when hope has been given up.

### قَنَطُوا۟ — root ق-ن-ط

***Qunūṭ* — the complete cutting off of hope.** The lexicographers count it more severe than *yaʾs* (despair): *yaʾs* is the ending of hope, *qunūṭ* is the collapse that comes with the ending of hope.

**And the verb is plural: *qanaṭū* — "they despaired". Its subject is not stated outright**, but from the context it is understood to be human beings.

**A point of arrangement: the pattern *min baʿdi mā* comes for the third time in the sūra.**

| Verse | *Min baʿdi mā* | What happened after |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | *jāʾahumu'l-ʿilm* | **They divided** — negative |
| **16** | *ustujība lah* | **They argued** — negative |
| **28** | *qanaṭū* | **The rain came down** — **positive** |

**The same pattern, three times; the first two describe an evil a person does after a piece of knowledge, the third a good that comes after a person's hope has ended.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text.

**I record this as my own reading:** the pattern is a pattern of "after", and in all three uses **what is unexpected** is recounted — twice in the bad direction, once in the good.

### وَيَنشُرُ رَحْمَتَهُۥ

***Nashr* — root ن-ش-ر: to spread, to open; to unroll and lay out what has been folded.** From the same root *nashr* (rising and spreading out at the resurrection), *muntashir* (spread out) — treated in `054-kamer.md`.

**And the object of the verb is not "rain" but ***mercy***.** The sentence, that is, gives first the concrete (rain), then the abstract (mercy), and joins the two in the same sentence.

### وَهُوَ ٱلْوَلِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ

**In the ninth verse *fa'llāhu huwa'l-waliyy* occurred, and there the proof of protectorship was "He gives life to the dead".**

**Here the same name comes again, and with its proof:** dead ground revived by rain.

| Verse | Name | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | *al-waliyy* | *Yuḥyi'l-mawtā* — **He gives life to the dead** |
| **28** | *al-waliyyu'l-ḥamīd* | **Rain** — after hope has been given up |

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text and was pointed to at the ninth verse.** Two sentences about the protector, two pictures of revival.

**حَمِيد — root ح-م-د.** It was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/24: *ḥamīd* is an intensive pattern in the sense of "the praised" (*maḥmūd*). **And what was recorded there was this: had *ghanī* been said on its own it would have set up a distance; *ḥamīd* balances it — the one who needs nothing is at the same time worthy of praise, that is, the relation continues.**

**Here the same balance is struck with a different pair: *walī* + *ḥamīd*.** Protectorship conveys nearness, *ḥamīd* that the return is deserved.

## 42/29 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ خَلْقُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَثَّ فِيهِمَا مِن دَآبَّةٍ ۚ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ جَمْعِهِمْ إِذَا يَشَآءُ قَدِيرٌ

*Wa min āyātihi khalqu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa mā baththa fīhimā min dābba; wa huwa ʿalā jamʿihim idhā yashāʾu qadīr*

"Among His signs are the creation of the heavens and the earth and the living creatures He has scattered in the two of them. And He has the power to gather them when He wills."

### وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِ — the beginning of a series

**This pattern occurs three times in the sūra and builds a series of proofs:**

| Verse | The proof |
|---|---|
| **29** | The creation of the heavens and the earth + **living creatures** |
| **32** | The **ships** on the sea |
| **(33)** | The stilling of the wind — the continuation of 32 |

**And the same method recorded in `050-kaf.md` is at work:** the proof is not a miracle but **what is seen every day.** The same finding was made in `002-bakara.md` at 2/163-164: *"The verse does not offer a miracle as proof — it offers the familiar."*

### بَثَّ — root ب-ث-ث

***Bathth* — to scatter a thing, to spread it, to strew it.** The lexicographers record that in the root there is **a dispersal that appears uncontrolled**; indeed from the same root *bathth* also means "to pour out one's heart, to spread out one's trouble" (Yūsuf 12/86: *innamā ashkū **baththī** wa ḥuznī ila'llāh*).

**And the choice of word is worth recording:** *baththa* conveys not an orderly placing but **a strewing.** For the dispersal of living creatures over the earth this word accords with observation.

### دَآبَّة — root د-ب-ب

***Dābba*, in the root's original sense, means one that stirs on the ground, that moves.** *Dabba* — he walked slowly, he stirred.

**And the definition is wide: the word covers every moving creature.** The Qurʾān says so outright: *"There is no *dābba* on the earth but its provision is upon God"* (Hūd 11/6).

**There is a detail in the verse that the exegetes dwell on:** *mā baththa **fīhimā*** — "**in the two of them**". That is, it is said that living creatures are in the heavens too.

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | The pronoun refers to both; **there are living creatures in the heavens too** |
| 2 | Though the pronoun says "the two", what is meant is the earth; in Arabic what belongs to one of two things may be ascribed **to both together** (*taghlīb*) |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **And no "scientific miracle" is drawn from this** — per the method (`USLUP.md`), modern knowledge is not forced onto a verse in this commentary. What is recorded is what the wording says and what the exegetes understood.

### وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ جَمْعِهِمْ إِذَا يَشَآءُ قَدِيرٌ

**The sentence brings in the opposite of the verb *baththa* (scattering): *jamʿ* (gathering).**

| Verb | The act |
|---|---|
| *Baththa* | **To scatter** — to disperse |
| *Jamʿ* | **To gather** — to bring together |

**And the word *jamʿ* comes for the third time in the sūra:**

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *yawma'l-jamʿ* | Everyone |
| **15** | *yajmaʿu baynanā* | The two disputing parties |
| **29** | *jamʿihim* | **The scattered living creatures** |

**In all three occurrences the same work: bringing together what has been dispersed.** This is directly connected with the sūra's main theme of "division" and is a verifiable fact.

***Idhā yashāʾ* — "when He wills".** Not *in* (a condition, a possibility) but *idhā* (a time); **in Arabic *idhā* is used for what will come to pass.** The gathering, that is, is set down not as a possibility but as a **time.**

## 42/30 — وَمَآ أَصَٰبَكُم مِّن مُّصِيبَةٍ فَبِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِيكُمْ وَيَعْفُوا۟ عَن كَثِيرٍ

*Wa mā aṣābakum min muṣībatin fa-bimā kasabat aydīkum wa yaʿfū ʿan kathīr*

"Whatever affliction strikes you is for what your own hands have earned. **And He pardons much.**"

**This verse must be treated carefully, because misread it turns into a sentence that accuses people — and the verse itself does not do that.**

### مُّصِيبَة — root ص-و-ب

**The root was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/155.** The note there was this:

> *"Root ص-و-ب: **to strike, to find its mark.** From the same root come *iṣāba* and *ṣawāb* (correct). A *muṣība*, then, in the word's own logic, is **'what strikes home'** — not what falls at random but what finds its mark. The word says in advance that there is a direction inside the event."*

**This note holds here too and I do not repeat it.**

### فَبِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِيكُمْ — the wording of the sentence

***Kasabat aydīkum* — "what your hands have earned".** The use of the word "hand" is a figure: it stresses that the act was **done**, that it came out into the open.

**The pattern occurs three times in the sūra:**

| Verse | Sentence | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | *bimā **kasabat aydīkum*** | Affliction |
| **34** | *bimā **kasabū*** | The sinking of ships |
| **48** | *bimā **qaddamat aydīhim*** | An evil that touches a person |

**The same structure in all three, with the word "hand" in two.** This is a verifiable fact.

### وَيَعْفُوا۟ عَن كَثِيرٍ — the second half of the verse and the note that must be brought forward

**This is the half of the verse most often dropped, and when it is dropped the sense of the verse changes.**

**The sentence is: *wa yaʿfū ʿan kathīr* — "**and He pardons much**".**

**This says three things:**

| What it says | How |
|---|---|
| **The amount pardoned is much** | *Kathīr* — "much". Not little |
| **What comes, then, is not the whole of what is earned** | The greater part of what is earned is left without return |
| **And this is a continuing act** | *Yaʿfū* is imperfect — without interruption |

**I stress this separately because when the first half of the sentence is read alone something else is understood.** The first half sets up a connection; the second says that the connection is **not proportional**: what is earned is much, what comes is little.

**And the root's distribution in the sūra was tabulated at the twenty-fifth verse:** the verb *yaʿfū* occurs four times in the sūra and three of them are God's pardon. **This verse is the second of those three.**

### What cannot be drawn from this verse — and this must be written plainly

**The following generalisation cannot be drawn from the verse: "every affliction is the return for a sin; therefore a person to whom something happens is in that state on account of his sin."**

**This generalisation is not supported by the text, and I set out the grounds:**

**First: the verse itself sets a *proportion*.** The sentence *wa yaʿfū ʿan kathīr* says that there is **no** one-to-one correspondence between what comes and what is earned. Were the correspondence one-to-one, the sentence about pardon would be meaningless.

**Second: the Qurʾān names the same fact elsewhere as a trial. It was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/155:**

> *"We shall surely **test** you with something of fear and hunger, and with loss of wealth, lives and fruits. Give good news to those who are patient."*

**And what was recorded there was this:** the root is *b-l-w* — **to test by wearing down**; and the Qurʾān says this is not one-directional: *"We test you with evil and with good"* (al-Anbiyāʾ 21/35). **The five things enumerated in the list in al-Baqara — fear, hunger, loss of wealth, of lives, of fruits — are named outright as *balāʾ* (a testing)**, not as punishment.

**Third: in `002-bakara.md` at 2/214 it is said plainly whom the hardship strikes.** What was recorded there was this:

> *"Who says these words is stated plainly in the verse: **al-rasūlu wa'lladhīna āmanū maʿahu** — the messenger and those who believed with him… The verse reports these words not as an instance of weakness but as the measure of the point to which the hardship reached."*

**By the Qurʾān's own data, that is, among those who suffer hardship to the point of being shaken are prophets.** A generalisation cannot leave this datum out.

**Fourth: whom the address is to.** The verse says *aṣāba**kum*** — second person plural. **Those addressed, that is, are a particular community, and the sentence is said to them.** It is not a structure that can be used for one person to pass a verdict about another.

### The balance of consolation and responsibility

**I record what the verse does as my own reading, and its ground is the two halves of the sentence:**

**The first half of the sentence states a *responsibility*:** it says that what befalls one is not from outside, causeless, arbitrary. This takes a person out of passivity — what is done has a consequence.

**The second half of the sentence states a *consolation*:** what comes is not the whole of what is deserved. This keeps a person from being crushed.

| Half | What it gives | What it removes |
|---|---|---|
| *Fa-bimā kasabat aydīkum* | **Responsibility** | The sense of arbitrariness |
| *Wa yaʿfū ʿan kathīr* | **Consolation** | Crushing weight |

**When the two are not read together the balance is broken:** read the first half alone and an accusing sentence comes out; read the second alone and responsibility disappears.

**And this is the balance repeated throughout the sūra** — in the eleventh verse transcendence and relation, in the fifth tension and forgiveness, in the fortieth right and pardon all stood in the same sentence.

### What this says to the present

**A note must be entered, and it concerns the use of the verse.**

The verse opens a door for a person to take account of **his own** condition. But this door is **one-way**: for a person to ask, about what has befallen him, "what did I do that this came?" accords with the wording of the verse.

**To ask the same question about someone else does not follow from the wording of the verse** and plainly conflicts with its second half: one who knows that what comes is a small part of what is earned cannot compute what was earned by looking at what came.

**And this too must be recorded:** to say to a person in pain "this is from what you did" is not consolation but the adding of a burden. **When the sūra has brought pardon forward even in this verse, turning the verse into an instrument of accusation breaks the text's own balance.**

## 42/31 — وَمَآ أَنتُم بِمُعْجِزِينَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۖ وَمَا لَكُم مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مِن وَلِىٍّ وَلَا نَصِيرٍ

*Wa mā antum bi-muʿjizīna fi'l-arḍ; wa mā lakum min dūni'llāhi min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr*

"You cannot thwart Him on the earth. And you have, apart from God, neither protector nor helper."

### مُعْجِزِين — root ع-ج-ز

***ʿAjz*, in the root's original sense, is to be left behind in a matter, to fail to keep up.** *ʿAjuz* — the hind part of a thing, the rear. The lexicographers make the link thus: the one who cannot do a thing is left behind it.

***Muʿjiz* (an active participle of Form IV) — "one who renders another powerless".** The sentence, that is, does not say "you are not powerless"; **it says "you cannot render Him powerless".**

**And the redundant *bi-* reinforces the negation** — as with *bi-wakīl* in the sixth verse.

### The repetition of the eighth verse

**The second half of the sentence is the sentence of the eighth verse. I set the two side by side:**

| Verse | Sentence | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *wa'ẓ-ẓālimūna **mā lahum min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr*** | **They** — third person |
| **31** | *wa **mā lakum** min dūni'llāhi **min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr*** | **You** — second person |

**The same sentence, with two different persons, twenty-three verses apart.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text and was recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** a verdict spoken in the third person is about someone standing at a distance; spoken in the second person it comes upon the reader. **The sūra gives the same verdict twice, from two different distances.**

**And the qualification *min dūni'llāh* has been added:** in the eighth verse this qualification was not there. The sentence, that is, leaves an exception here: there is none **apart from God** — God Himself stands outside this.

## 42/32 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِ ٱلْجَوَارِ فِى ٱلْبَحْرِ كَٱلْأَعْلَٰمِ

*Wa min āyātihi'l-jawāri fi'l-baḥri ka'l-aʿlām*

"Among His signs are the ships that run on the sea like mountains."

### ٱلْجَوَارِ — the word itself

***Al-jawār* is the plural of *jāriya*; root ج-ر-ي: to flow, to run.** And the use of this word for a ship is striking: a ship is named not "that which floats" but **"that which flows"**.

**The word, that is, brings forward not the ship's own movement but its going along with the water.** The next verse will supply the counterpart of this choice: when the wind stops, the ships stop.

**In the muṣḥaf the word is written as *al-jawār*, with the final *yāʾ* dropped** — a known feature of the spelling of nouns on the *jār* pattern in Arabic. I record it; I draw no meaning from it.

### كَٱلْأَعْلَٰم — root ع-ل-م

**And here there is a point about a word, because the root turns up in an unexpected place.**

***Aʿlām* is the plural of *ʿalam*. And *ʿalam* in Arabic means this: a sign that makes a place known — a waymark, a boundary stone, and a mountain.**

**The branching of the root:**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **عِلْم** (*ʿilm*) | Knowledge |
| **عَلَم** (*ʿalam*) | **A sign; a mountain; a flag; a banner** |
| **عَلَامَة** (*ʿalāma*) | A mark |
| **مَعْلَم** (*maʿlam*) | A landmark by which a place is recognised |

**The shared core: what *makes a thing known*.** A mountain is the most reliable sign for finding one's way in the desert; hence it is called an *ʿalam*.

**And the work of the comparison is worth recording:** ships are compared to mountains. **The comparison points two ways**, and the exegetes record both:

| Aspect | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Size** | A ship stands on the sea like a mountain |
| **Visibility** | A ship is like a mark picked out from afar |

**The second aspect rests on the sense in the root of the word, and I record it.** An *ʿalam* is first a "sign", and then a "mountain".

## 42/33 — إِن يَشَأْ يُسْكِنِ ٱلرِّيحَ فَيَظْلَلْنَ رَوَاكِدَ عَلَىٰ ظَهْرِهِۦٓ ۚ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَٰتٍ لِّكُلِّ صَبَّارٍ شَكُورٍ

*In yashaʾ yuskini'r-rīḥa fa-yaẓlalna rawākida ʿalā ẓahrih; inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr*

"If He wills, He stills the wind, and they lie motionless on its back. In that are signs for every steadfast and thankful one."

### يُسْكِنِ ٱلرِّيحَ — where the proof is built

**The proof is built not on the ship's going but on its being able to stop.**

**And this is the same structure of proof recorded in `056-vakia.md` at 56/65.** It was written there:

> *"A person supposes the coming up of the crop to be his own work because **it comes up every year.** By recalling that its not coming up is also possible, the verse shows that the ordinary is **no guarantee.** The proof is built not against a miracle but against **the taking of an order for a permanence.**"*

**In al-Shūrā the same structure: the ship goes because the wind blows. That the wind blows is no guarantee.**

**And the pattern *in yashaʾ* must be recorded:** not *law* but *in*. **In `056-vakia.md`, *law nashāʾu* conveyed an unfulfilled condition; here *in* is used**, and *in* conveys a possible condition. **The stilling of the wind, that is, is not an unrealised possibility but a state that can occur.**

### رَوَاكِد — root ر-ك-د

***Rākid* — motionless, still.** The root **ر-ك-د**: to stand, to stay fixed. In Arabic still water is called *māʾun rākid*.

**The word is plural and comes in the feminine plural pattern** (*fa-yaẓlal**na***) — the ships are counted feminine, because the word *jawār* is feminine.

***ʿAlā ẓahrihi* — "on its back".** The back of the sea. **This is a settled usage in Arabic**, and calling the surface of the sea a "back" makes the ship appear as a load that is carried.

**I record this as my own reading:** the word says that the ship **does not go by itself.** It is on the back of something; if the back moves it goes, if it stops it stops.

### لِّكُلِّ صَبَّارٍ شَكُورٍ — a pair of attributes

**Both words are in intensive patterns:**

| Word | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **صَبَّار** | *faʿʿāl* | **Much enduring** |
| **شَكُور** | *faʿūl* | **Much thanking** |

**The root *ṣabr* was treated in `050-kaf.md` and `049-hucurat.md`:** the root's original sense is **to hold a thing in a place, to confine it**; patience is holding the self upon a state.

**The root *shukr* was treated at the twenty-third verse:** to give much in return for little.

**Why these two as a pair? The answer is in the verse itself, and I give it as my own reading:**

**The verse recounts two states: the ship's going and its stopping.**

| State | Which attribute is at work |
|---|---|
| The ship goes (32) | ***Shakūr*** — returning what is given |
| The ship stops (33) | ***Ṣabbār*** — holding one's place in a state |

**The two attributes, then, correspond to the two situations the verse recounts.** And both are in a pattern of **muchness**: not once, but continually.

**The same pair occurs in several other places in the Qurʾān** (Ibrāhīm 14/5, Luqmān 31/31, Sabaʾ 34/19) **and in all of them in the context of a *sign*.** This is a verifiable fact.

**And its link with the twenty-third verse:** there *shakūr* was a name of God. **Here it is an attribute of a human being.** The same pattern, two parties — recorded in the section on the structure.

## 42/34 — أَوْ يُوبِقْهُنَّ بِمَا كَسَبُوا۟ وَيَعْفُ عَن كَثِيرٍ

*Aw yūbiqhunna bimā kasabū wa yaʿfu ʿan kathīr*

"Or He destroys them for what they have earned — and He pardons much."

### يُوبِقْ — root و-ب-ق

***Wabaqa* — he perished; in Form IV *awbaqa* — he destroyed.** From the same root **مَوْبِق** (*mawbiq*) — a place of destruction; it occurs in the Qurʾān at al-Kahf 18/52.

**The verb describes the sinking of ships** and, together with the stopping in the preceding verse, sets up two possibilities:

| Verse | What happens | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **33** | The ships **stop** | Light |
| **34** | The ships **are destroyed** | Heavy |

**And the arrangement goes from light to heavy.** This is the structure of *gradation* recorded for *sina* and *nawm* in `002-bakara.md` at 2/255.

### وَيَعْفُ عَن كَثِيرٍ — the repetition of the thirtieth verse

**And this is the place that must be recorded: the sentence *wa yaʿfū ʿan kathīr* occurs twice in the sūra — in the thirtieth and thirty-fourth verses.**

| Verse | What precedes | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | *Mā aṣābakum min muṣībatin fa-bimā kasabat aydīkum* | ***wa yaʿfū ʿan kathīr*** |
| **34** | *Aw yūbiqhunna bimā kasabū* | ***wa yaʿfu ʿan kathīr*** |

**The same note of pardon, in two different contexts, four verses apart.** This supports the finding made at the thirtieth verse: **the note of pardon is placed beside the "earning–consequence" link by the sūra deliberately and again and again.**

**A note on grammar:** in the thirty-fourth verse the verb comes in the jussive as *yaʿfu* (*wa yaʿfu*, not *yaʿfū*), because it is attached to the preceding conditional sentence. **In some readings it is read in the indicative (*wa yaʿfū*), and on that reading the sentence is independent of the condition, a statement standing on its own.** The difference in meaning is small but real: on the attached reading the pardon is particular to that situation; on the independent reading it is a general statement. **Since the variant reading touches the meaning I record it; I impose no preference.**

## 42/35 — وَيَعْلَمَ ٱلَّذِينَ يُجَٰدِلُونَ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِنَا مَا لَهُم مِّن مَّحِيصٍ

*Wa yaʿlama'lladhīna yujādilūna fī āyātinā mā lahum min maḥīṣ*

"And that those who dispute about Our signs may know that there is no escape for them."

### يُجَٰدِلُون — root ج-د-ل

***Jadal* — to dispute, to wrangle.** The concrete sense of the root is striking: ***jadala'l-ḥabl*** — **to twist a rope tightly, to plait it.** *Jadīl* — a firmly twisted rope.

**The transition the lexicographers record is this:** two disputing parties plait one another's words by twisting them; each pulls to his own side. **The word, that is, carries the picture of a tug-of-war with a rope.**

**And the Form III pattern (*jidāl*) conveys reciprocity** — not a one-sided speaking.

**The root is close in sense to *yumārūn* in the eighteenth verse, and the two do the same work in the sūra:**

| Verse | Word | Subject | The root's picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18** | *yumārūna fi's-sāʿa* | **The Hour** | Milking — drawing words out |
| **35** | *yujādilūna fī āyātinā* | **The signs** | Twisting — a tug-of-war with a rope |

**Both words are rendered "to dispute", but they carry different pictures.** This is a verifiable fact of language.

### مَحِيص — root ح-ي-ص

***Ḥayṣ* — to swerve away from something and escape, to slip out.** *Maḥīṣ* — a place of escape.

**The word occurred in `050-kaf.md` in the series of verse-endings of Sūrat Qāf** (*hal min maḥīṣ*, 50/36), and it was used there in the context of past communities seeking a way out.

**The difference in the use here: there past communities, here those who dispute.** And what they have in common is that the word is a word of **escape.**

### A point of grammar

The word *wa yaʿlama* comes in the **subjunctive**, and this is a point the grammarians have dwelt on. The explanations transmitted:

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | It is subjunctive by an unexpressed *an*: "…so that they may know" — a clause of purpose |
| 2 | It is coordinated with the apodosis before it |

**The sense is close in either case: the knowing is set down as the purpose or the outcome of the two preceding possibilities (stopping and sinking).**

## 42/36-39 — The attributes of the believer: the building of a list

**From the thirty-sixth verse to the thirty-ninth, four verses work as a single sentence. The expression that gives the sūra its name stands in the middle of this list. The list must first be seen as a whole, and then I shall take it verse by verse.**

**The list is built as follows:**

- **Verse 36** sets a **condition**: *li'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿalā rabbihim yatawakkalūn* — "for those who believe and rely upon their Lord".
- **Verses 37, 38 and 39** begin with *wa'lladhīna…* and **add attributes** to the same group.

**Grammatically, then, the four verses are the attributes of a single noun.** This is a verifiable structural fact.

**Counted out, eight attributes emerge:**

| # | Attribute | Verse | Field |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Āmanū* — they believed | 36 | **Inward** |
| 2 | *ʿAlā rabbihim yatawakkalūn* — they rely upon their Lord | 36 | **Inward** |
| 3 | *Yajtanibūna kabāʾira'l-ithmi wa'l-fawāḥish* — they keep clear of grave sins | 37 | **Conduct — avoidance** |
| 4 | *Wa idhā mā ghaḍibū hum yaghfirūn* — when they are angry they forgive | 37 | **Relation — the moment of anger** |
| 5 | *Istajābū li-rabbihim wa aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāh* — they answered their Lord and upheld the prayer | 38 | **Worship** |
| 6 | ***Wa amruhum shūrā baynahum*** — their affair is consultation among themselves | 38 | **Decision within the community** |
| 7 | *Wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn* — they spend of what We have provided | 38 | **Property** |
| 8 | *Idhā aṣābahumu'l-baghyu hum yantaṣirūn* — when wronged they take their right | 39 | **Outward** |

**And the order of the list follows a direction: from inside outward.** Faith and reliance (inward) → avoidance (one's own conduct) → anger (close relations) → worship → decision within the community → property → a wrong coming from outside.

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the subjects of the attributes themselves.**

## 42/36 — فَمَآ أُوتِيتُم مِّن شَىْءٍ فَمَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا ۖ وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ

*Fa-mā ūtītum min shayʾin fa-matāʿu'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā; wa mā ʿinda'llāhi khayrun wa abqā li'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿalā rabbihim yatawakkalūn*

"Whatever you have been given is the provision of the life of this world. What is with God is better and more lasting, for those who believe and rely upon their Lord."

### مَتَاع — root م-ت-ع

***Matāʿ*, in the lexicographers' description: what is used for a time and then comes to an end.** The provision a traveller takes with him, the household goods, merchandise — all are *matāʿ*.

**Two things are in the root: *benefit* and *duration*.** A thing's being *matāʿ* does not mean it is useless; it means it is **not lasting.**

**And the verse says this with the word itself, and then sets *abqā* against it.**

### خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ — two comparative patterns

**Both words are in the *afʿal* (comparative) pattern:**

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **خَيْر** | خ-ي-ر | **Better** — quality |
| **أَبْقَىٰ** | ب-ق-ي | **More lasting** — duration |

**And their coming together is worth recording:** had only "better" been said, it would have been a comparison of value and could be argued about. **"More lasting", by contrast, is a measurable note** and sets up a direct contrast with the word *matāʿ*.

**The same pair occurs in several other places in the Qurʾān** and was treated in `087-ala.md` in connection with al-Aʿlā 87/17 (*wa'l-ākhiratu **khayrun wa abqā***).

### يَتَوَكَّلُون — root و-ك-ل

**The root occurred in the sixth verse as *wakīl* and was grounded on `067-mulk.md`.** The note there is worth repeating, because this is the second attribute in the list:

> *"Tawakkul is not the refusal to act but the committing of the outcome. The one who appoints an agent does not abandon the matter — he shares the responsibility. This distinction is central to the Qurʾān's understanding of tawakkul and is very open to misunderstanding."*

**And this distinction is confirmed by the continuation of the list:** in the thirty-eighth verse prayer, consultation and spending will be enumerated; in the thirty-ninth, the taking of one's right against a wrong. **A list that begins with reliance, that is, says three verses later "they conduct their affairs by consultation among themselves".**

**I record this as my own reading:** that the same list contains both "they rely upon their Lord" and "they conduct their affairs by consultation" shows from the text itself that reliance is not the refusal to decide. **The two attributes stand two verses apart, as the description of the same group.**

## 42/37 — وَٱلَّذِينَ يَجْتَنِبُونَ كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ وَٱلْفَوَٰحِشَ وَإِذَا مَا غَضِبُوا۟ هُمْ يَغْفِرُونَ

*Wa'lladhīna yajtanibūna kabāʾira'l-ithmi wa'l-fawāḥisha wa idhā mā ghaḍibū hum yaghfirūn*

"And those who keep clear of grave sins and of indecencies, and who forgive when they are angry."

### يَجْتَنِبُون — root ج-ن-ب

**The concrete sense of the root: *the side*.** *Janb* — the side of a thing, the flank. **In Form VIII (*ijtināb*): to take something to one's side, that is, to stand off to the side of it — to keep clear of it.**

**And the choice of verb carries a point of arrangement and must be recorded:** *ijtināb* is not "not doing" but **"not passing near"**. **It is wider than abandoning:** a person may not do a thing and yet stand close to it. The word takes distance into account as well.

### كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ وَٱلْفَوَٰحِش

**This pair was treated in `053-necm.md` at 53/32** (*alladhīna yajtanibūna kabāʾira'l-ithmi wa'l-fawāḥisha illa'l-lamam*) **and was tabulated there:**

> | | **كَبَٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ** | **ٱلْفَوَاحِش** |
> |---|---|---|
> | What | The grave among sins | Those whose ugliness is open |

**And what was recorded there:** *fuḥsh* — ugliness that oversteps, excess; *fāḥisha*, plural *fawāḥish* — an act whose ugliness is plain. **I do not repeat it.**

**What belongs here is this: in al-Najm the expression was followed by an exception (*illa'l-lamam* — except small lapses); in al-Shūrā there is no exception.** In its place comes another sentence: the moment of anger.

**This is a verifiable difference and I record it as an observation.**

### وَإِذَا مَا غَضِبُوا۟ هُمْ يَغْفِرُونَ — the hardest attribute in the list

**The construction of the sentence must be read carefully, because a detail is lost in translation.**

***Idhā mā ghaḍibū* — "when they are angry".** **Anger, that is, is not *forbidden*; that they grow angry is accepted.**

**The sentence does not say "they do not grow angry". This is a fact that must be recorded and follows directly from the wording.**

**غَضَب — root غ-ض-ب.** As recorded in `048-fetih.md`, the root holds **swelling, intensifying.**

**And *hum yaghfirūn* — a point of arrangement:** the pronoun (*hum*) has been repeated. Grammatically it is unnecessary; *yaghfirūn* already carries its plural agent. **In Arabic this repetition conveys specification and emphasis:** "**they** forgive" — not anyone else, and at exactly that moment.

**The same structure is in the thirty-ninth verse as well:** *hum yantaṣirūn.* **In both verses the pronoun is repeated, and the two verses recount opposite acts: one forgiving, the other taking one's right.**

| Verse | Sentence | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **37** | *wa idhā mā ghaḍibū **hum yaghfirūn*** | **They forgive** |
| **39** | *wa idhā aṣābahumu'l-baghyu **hum yantaṣirūn*** | **They take their right** |

**The same pattern of emphasis, two different behaviours — and both are attributes in the same list.** This is a verifiable fact and the first sign of the balance that will be set up in the fortieth verse.

**I record this as my own reading:** the difference between the two sentences lies in the trigger.

| | Verse 37 | Verse 39 |
|---|---|---|
| What triggers it | ***Ghaḍab*** — one's own anger | ***Baghy*** — a wrong coming from outside |
| The response | **Forgiveness** | **Answering it** |

**The list, that is, sets forgiveness against anger and the taking of one's right against a wrong.** The two are not confused: anger is an inward state, *baghy* an outward act.

## 42/38 — وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَجَابُوا۟ لِرَبِّهِمْ وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَأَمْرُهُمْ شُورَىٰ بَيْنَهُمْ وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ

*Wa'lladhīna'stajābū li-rabbihim wa aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāta wa amruhum shūrā baynahum wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn*

"And those who answer their Lord, and uphold the prayer, and **whose affair is consultation among themselves**, and who spend of what We have provided them."

**The verse that gives the sūra its name.**

### شُورَىٰ — root ش-و-ر

**This root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus, and it needs to be given in detail.**

**The concrete sense of the root is plain in the lexicographers' descriptions, and it comes from an unexpected place:**

> ***شَارَ الْعَسَلَ*** (*shāra'l-ʿasal*) — **he drew the honey out of the hive, he took it.**

**Honey is inside the hive; it is unseen, it is closed in, and getting it out calls for both labour and care.** The verb *shāra* is **to take that honey from there and set it out.**

**The root's other derivatives carry the same core:**

| Derivative | Meaning | The shared aspect |
|---|---|---|
| **شَارَ** (*shāra*) | To take honey from the hive | **Drawing out** |
| **أَشَارَ** (*ashāra*) | **To point**, to indicate with the hand | **Making a thing visible** |
| **إِشَارَة** (*ishāra*) | Pointing out, indicating | The same |
| **شَارَ الدَّابَّة** | To run an animal so as to show it to a buyer; **to bring out its qualities** | **Bringing out into the open what is hidden** |
| **مَشْوَار** (*mishwār*) | The place or ground where an animal is shown | The same |
| **شَوَار** (*shiwār*) | Goods on display, household furnishings | **What has been set out** |
| **مَشُورَة / شُورَىٰ** | **Consultation** | **The bringing out of an opinion** |
| **اسْتِشَارَة** | Asking for counsel | The same |

**The shared core is this, and it is settled by the lexicographers' own descriptions: to take a thing out of where it is and set it out.**

### The picture that comes out of the root — and what consultation is

**Now I make the link between this concrete sense and consultation. I give it as my own reading; its ground is the lexicographers' description of the root.**

**The honey is in the hive and no one sees it.** Without opening the hive, how much there is and what it is like is not known. **Without the work of drawing it out, the honey, *while it exists, is as though it did not.***

**That the name of consultation is this word says this: within a community there are opinions, but until they are drawn out there are none.**

**And this says three things at once:**

| The picture of the honey | Its counterpart in consultation |
|---|---|
| The honey is **inside** | Opinion is inside people; until spoken it is not known |
| The honey **must be drawn out** | Opinion does not come out unless it is asked for |
| Drawing it out **takes labour** | Consultation is not something that happens of itself; it is work that is done |

**And the *ishāra* branch completes this:** *ashāra* is to point with the hand — **to make visible what is not seen.** The same root both draws out the honey and points with the finger.

**I give this as an analysis of a word. I do not claim that this sense of the root is a deliberate choice; what I record is the picture that stands beneath the word.**

### The شُورَىٰ pattern — and its being a nominal sentence

**And here is the most overlooked side of the verse.**

***Shūrā* is a noun on the pattern *fuʿlā*** — like *bushrā* (good news), *dhikrā* (a reminder), *ḥusnā* (goodness). **It is a verbal-noun pattern.**

**And the sentence is: *wa amruhum shūrā baynahum.***

| Element of the sentence | What |
|---|---|
| *Amruhum* | **Subject** — "their affair" |
| *Shūrā* | **Predicate** — "is consultation" |
| *Baynahum* | **Adverbial** — "among themselves" |

**There is no verb in the sentence, that is. It is a nominal sentence.**

**This yields three consequences, and I record all three:**

**First: not a command but a description.** The sentence does not say "consult"; it says "their affair is consultation". **And this accords with the list as a whole** — what has been running since the thirty-sixth verse is a list of attributes.

**Second: continuity.** In Arabic a nominal sentence conveys fixity and continuity; a verbal sentence occurrence and renewal. **This distinction was recorded in `112-ihlas.md`:** *"a nominal sentence conveys fixity and continuity… not an event but a state."*

**Consultation, that is, is described not as a procedure resorted to in extraordinary situations but as the community's *continuing state*.**

**Third: the owner of the affair is the community.** *Amru**hum*** — "**their** affair". And *bayna**hum*** — "**among themselves**". **Both pronouns point to the community.**

**The sentence says that the authority to be consulted is not outside.**

### The three occurrences of this root in the Qurʾān

**The root occurs in this sense in three places in the Qurʾān, and in three different patterns. This is a verifiable fact:**

| Place | Word | Pattern | Who | Subject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **al-Baqara 2/233** | *wa **tashāwur*** | **Verbal noun** (Form VI) | **Mother and father** | Weaning a child |
| **Āl ʿImrān 3/159** | ***shāwirhum** fi'l-amr* | **Imperative** (Form III) | **The Prophet → the community** | In "the affair" |
| **al-Shūrā 42/38** | ***shūrā** baynahum* | **A noun** | **A community among themselves** | "Their affair" |

**Comparing the three occurrences is instructive:**

- **In al-Baqara 2/233** the consultation is **between two people** — mother and father. And the subject is a concrete decision about a child's care. **The word, that is, holds at the smallest unit as well.**
- **In Āl ʿImrān 3/159** it is a **command**, and the one addressed is a single person: *shāwirhum* — "consult them". **One who has the authority to decide, that is, is commanded to consult.**
- **In al-Shūrā 42/38** it is neither a command nor singular; **it is the attribute of a community.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the three occurrences show the word working at three different scales — family, leadership, community. **And in none of them is an institution, a procedure or a method described.**

**This last point matters and must be written plainly:** **the verse does not describe a form of government.** Neither how those to be consulted are determined, nor whether the outcome is binding, nor any procedure is stated. **These are things the verse does not say, and to present what is unsaid as said is not done in this commentary, per the method (`USLUP.md`).**

**The sūra's being Meccan supports this too:** as recorded in the section on the occasion of revelation, the word comes as the attribute of a community that has as yet no state, no army and no treasury.

### The verse's place in the arrangement — between prayer and spending

**And now I come to the most striking structural feature of the verse.**

**The expression *shūrā* has been set in the middle of the verse, between two acts of worship.**

| Order | Element | Field | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Istajābū li-rabbihim* | **Answering God** | Verb (perfect) |
| 2 | *Wa aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāh* | **Prayer** | Verb (perfect) |
| 3 | ***Wa amruhum shūrā baynahum*** | **Decision within the community** | **Nominal sentence** |
| 4 | *Wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn* | **Property** | Verb (imperfect) |

**This table shows two things, and both can be verified from the text:**

**First — position.** *Shūrā* stands between prayer and spending. **And these two are the two acts of worship most often named together in the Qurʾān** (*yuqīmūna'ṣ-ṣalāta wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn* — in al-Baqara 2/3 and in many places). **The sūra, that is, separates a pair that customarily comes together and sets consultation between them.**

**This is a verifiable fact of arrangement.**

**Second — pattern.** Three of the four elements are verbal sentences, one a nominal sentence. **And the nominal one is exactly in the middle.**

**I record this as my own reading, and it is not binding:** a nominal sentence set between two verbs breaks the rhythm of the list. **And the break comes exactly where the community's own affair is reached.** The other three are things they "do"; this is what they "are".

**One further comparison can be added, and it too is verified by the text:** the same list will continue in the thirty-ninth verse with a verbal sentence (*hum yantaṣirūn*). **The nominal sentence, that is, stands alone in the list.**

### وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ يُنفِقُون

***min* twice:** *mimmā* = *min mā*. **This pattern was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/7:** *min* conveys partiality — "some of it". **Not the whole.**

**And *razaqnāhum* — "what We have provided".** The source of what is spent is not the one who spends. **In `057-hadid.md` this was treated in detail by way of the word *mustakhlafīn*, and I do not repeat it.**

**What belongs here is this: all four of the elements contain a "giving".**

| Element | What is given |
|---|---|
| *Istajābū* | **An answer** |
| *Aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāh* | **Time** |
| *Shūrā baynahum* | **Speech / opinion** |
| *Yunfiqūn* | **Property** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared structure of the four elements:** four things a person has in hand are enumerated in the list, and all four are passed to the other side. **And *shūrā* is, in this series, the turn of "speech".**

### What this says to the present

**What the verse states is not a procedure but a habit.** And this distinction matters in practice: a procedure is set up and operated; a habit is noticed in its absence.

**For a community's "affair to be consultation" does not mean holding consultative meetings.** The measure the verse sets is simpler and rests on the word *amruhum*: **whose affair is the affair counted to be?** If within a community the affair is counted the affair of one person or one circle, then even if consultation takes place it is not *shūrā*; if the affair is counted the community's own, the form may vary.

**And here the picture of the root works once more:** the honey is in the hive. **Honey not drawn out is honey that is not there.** So too an opinion not spoken within a community — it exists and yet does not.

## 42/39 — وَٱلَّذِينَ إِذَآ أَصَابَهُمُ ٱلْبَغْىُ هُمْ يَنتَصِرُونَ

*Wa'lladhīna idhā aṣābahumu'l-baghyu hum yantaṣirūn*

"And those who, when a wrong strikes them, help one another and take their right."

### ٱلْبَغْى — with the definite article

**The root was treated at the fourteenth verse and grounded on `049-hucurat.md` 49/9: wanting in its boundary-crossing state.**

**In this use the word has taken the definite article: *al-baghy*.** Indefinite, it would have been "a wrong"; being definite, it is "that known overstepping" — that is, the thing the sūra has already named.

**And note: the verb is *aṣāba* — the same root as *aṣābakum* in the thirtieth verse (ص-و-ب).**

| Verse | What strikes | Whom |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | *muṣība* | Those addressed — and its cause their own earning |
| **39** | *al-baghy* | The believers — and its cause **another's act** |

**This distinction is worth recording and touches the question treated at the thirtieth verse:** the sūra itself shows that not everything that strikes comes from a person's own earning. **In the thirty-ninth verse what strikes is plainly another's overstepping.**

**I record this as an observation about the text, and it supports the limitation set out at the thirtieth verse.**

### يَنتَصِرُون — root ن-ص-ر, Form VIII

***Naṣr* — to help.** And **Form VIII (*intiṣār*)** conveys two things:

| Meaning | How |
|---|---|
| **To help oneself** | To bring the help about upon oneself — that is, to take one's right |
| **To help one another** | Reciprocity |

**And there are two readings of the word's sense here:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **They take their right** — they do not submit to the wrong |
| 2 | **They help one another** — they stand by the one who is wronged |

**Both readings are transmitted in the classical sources and they do not exclude one another.** The pattern allows both.

**And the word will occur twice more in the sūra:**

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **39** | *hum **yantaṣirūn*** | The believers — as an attribute |
| **41** | *wa la-mani'**ntaṣara** baʿda ẓulmih* | The one wronged — as a ruling |
| **(46)** | *awliyāʾa **yanṣurū**nahum* | Being without a helper — in the negative |

**This is a verifiable fact, and the forty-first verse will state the ruling of the thirty-ninth outright.**

### The placing of this attribute in the list

**And this must be recorded, because it is decisive for the list as a whole:**

**In the thirty-seventh verse it was said "when they are angry they forgive". Two verses later it says "when a wrong strikes them they take their right".**

**These two attributes are in the same list and do not cancel one another.** How this is so will be explained in the fortieth verse — and this is the finest balance in the sūra.

## 42/40-43 — Repayment, pardon and right: the balance four verses set up

**These four verses must be read together, because taken singly each leads to a different conclusion. First I take each of them singly, and then I shall show as a whole the balance they set up.**

---

## 42/40 — وَجَزَٰٓؤُا۟ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِّثْلُهَا ۖ فَمَنْ عَفَا وَأَصْلَحَ فَأَجْرُهُۥ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ

*Wa jazāʾu sayyiʾatin sayyiʾatun mithluhā; fa-man ʿafā wa aṣlaḥa fa-ajruhu ʿala'llāh; innahu lā yuḥibbu'ẓ-ẓālimīn*

"The return for an evil is an evil like it. But whoever pardons and sets right, his reward is with God. He does not love wrongdoers."

### سَيِّئَةٌ مِّثْلُهَا — the return too being named "an evil"

**And this is the most striking side of the verse.**

**The sentence names the return one has a right to *sayyiʾa* as well.** The lawful return, that is, is named not "a good" but **with the same word.**

**This is a known usage in rhetoric: *mushākala* — naming the return with the name of what is being returned.** And there are examples of it in the Qurʾān (*wa jazāʾu sayyiʾatin sayyiʾatun mithluhā*; *fa-mani'tadā ʿalaykum fa'tadū ʿalayhi bi-mithli ma'tadā ʿalaykum* — al-Baqara 2/194).

**But the choice of the word is not merely a matter of style, and I record this as my own reading:**

**Calling the return "an evil" shows that exercising a right and virtue are not the same thing.** A person takes his right and is not blamed for it — the forty-first verse will say so outright. **But what he takes is not named a fine thing.**

**And this prepares the sentence that comes immediately after:** *fa-man ʿafā wa aṣlaḥa fa-ajruhu ʿala'llāh.*

**My ground is the difference of naming between the two sentences:**

| Sentence | What is done | How it is named |
|---|---|---|
| *Jazāʾu sayyiʾatin **sayyiʾatun** mithluhā* | **Making a return** | ***Sayyiʾa*** — an evil |
| *Fa-man ʿafā wa aṣlaḥa fa-**ajruhu** ʿala'llāh* | **Pardoning** | ***Ajr*** — a reward |

### مِّثْلُهَا — the note of measure

***Mithl* — like, equal.** Root **م-ث-ل**; treated in `047-muhammed.md`.

**And this word is the sentence's most important limiter:** the return must be **equal.** More than that is wrongdoing.

**And the verse says so plainly at the end: *innahu lā yuḥibbu'ẓ-ẓālimīn*.** A limit, that is, has been set beside what is permitted.

**The pattern recorded in `002-bakara.md` at 2/223 is at work here too:** *"The form repeated in the sūra: a note of responsibility beside a permission."*

**And the word *mithl* occurs for the second time in the sūra** — in the eleventh verse in the expression *laysa ka-**mithli**hi shayʾ*. **The same root in two different works: one denies likeness, the other makes equivalence a condition.** This is a verifiable fact; I make no claim of a deliberate choice.

### فَمَنْ عَفَا وَأَصْلَحَ — two conditions, one reward

**And there is a detail here that must be recorded: pardon on its own is not held sufficient. A second verb is added beside it.**

| Verb | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| **عَفَا** | ع-ف-و | **To erase** — to give up one's right |
| **أَصْلَحَ** | ص-ل-ح | **To set right** — to repair what is broken |

***Iṣlāḥ* — root ص-ل-ح.** It was treated in `049-hucurat.md` at 49/9-10: to bring what is broken **back into usable condition.** *Ṣāliḥ* — serviceable, unspoiled.

**The coming together of the two verbs determines what kind of pardon this is, and I record it as my own reading:**

**Pardon on its own may be a *withdrawal*** — a person does not take his right, cuts the relation, moves away. **Technically that is pardon, but it does not repair what is broken.**

**The condition of *iṣlāḥ* ties the pardon to a repair.** What is expected, that is, is not the abandoning of a right but **the setting right of the relation.**

**My ground is the sentence itself: the two verbs are joined with *wa*, and the reward is given for the two together** — *fa-ajruhu*, a singular pronoun, answers to both.

### فَأَجْرُهُۥ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ — the reward left unmeasured

**And this is the sharpest point of the arrangement.**

**In the first half of the sentence the return is measured: *sayyiʾatun mithluhā* — as much as its like.**

**In the second half the reward is unmeasured: *fa-ajruhu ʿala'llāh* — the amount is not stated.**

| | **Making a return** | **Pardoning** |
|---|---|---|
| Who gives the return | **The person himself** | **God** |
| Amount | ***Mithluhā*** — determined | **Not stated** |
| When | Now | Not specified |
| Its name | *Sayyiʾa* | ***Ajr*** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of the two halves:**

**The one who takes his right takes what is due to him himself, and how much he will take is settled. The one who pardons takes what is due to him from another, and how much it is is not settled.**

**And the preposition *ʿalā* must be recorded:** *ajruhu **ʿalā**'llāh* — "**upon** God". In Arabic *ʿalā* conveys obligation. **The sentence ties the pardoner's due to an undertaking.**

**In `057-hadid.md` at 57/11 the same structure was treated in the discussion of *qarḍ ḥasan* (lending God a good loan):** what a person gives is named a loan and its return is promised multiplied. **Here the same structure is built on a right rather than on property.**

## 42/41 — وَلَمَنِ ٱنتَصَرَ بَعْدَ ظُلْمِهِۦ فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ مَا عَلَيْهِم مِّن سَبِيلٍ

*Wa la-mani'ntaṣara baʿda ẓulmihi fa-ulāʾika mā ʿalayhim min sabīl*

"As for those who take their right after being wronged — there is no way against them."

### بَعْدَ ظُلْمِهِ — the referent of the pronoun

**There are two readings of the pronoun in the construction *ẓulmihi*, and both are transmitted:**

| Reading | Construction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **An objective genitive** — "the wrong done to him" | "**After having been wronged**" |
| 2 | **A subjective genitive** — "the wrongdoer's wrong" | "**After the wrongdoer's wrong**" |

**Both readings arrive at the same result: the one who takes his right has first suffered a wrong.** I do not impose a preference.

### مَا عَلَيْهِم مِّن سَبِيلٍ — not being blamed

***Sabīl* — a way.** And in Arabic the idiom *"ʿalayhi sabīl"* means **"a way against him, a case, a liability"**. **The sentence, then: there is no proceeding, no blame, no charge against them.**

**And the *la-* must be recorded:** *wa **la**-mani'ntaṣara* — the lām of emphasis. **The sentence has been reinforced.**

**I record this as my own reading:** immediately after the fortieth verse has praised pardon, the forty-first **defends** the one who does not pardon. This sequence matters: had pardon been praised and left there, the one who takes his right would have been indirectly blamed. **The verse closes that door.**

### سَبِيل — the four occurrences in the sūra

**The word occurs four times in the sūra, twice in a positive and twice in a negative direction:**

| Verse | Sentence | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **41** | *mā ʿalayhim min **sabīl*** | **The one who takes his right** — no way against him |
| **42** | *innam**a's-sabīl**u ʿala'lladhīna yaẓlimūn* | **The wrongdoer** — there is a way against him |
| **44** | *hal ilā maraddin min **sabīl*** | **The one who sees the punishment** — is there a way back |
| **46** | *fa-mā lahu min **sabīl*** | **The one who strays** — no way |

**The forty-first and forty-second verses set up a direct opposition:** the same word, in two consecutive verses, on two opposite sides. **This is a fact that can be verified from the text.**

## 42/42 — إِنَّمَا ٱلسَّبِيلُ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ يَظْلِمُونَ ٱلنَّاسَ وَيَبْغُونَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ ٱلْحَقِّ ۚ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

*Innama's-sabīlu ʿala'lladhīna yaẓlimūna'n-nāsa wa yabghūna fi'l-arḍi bi-ghayri'l-ḥaqq; ulāʾika lahum ʿadhābun alīm*

"The way is only against those who wrong people and overstep on the earth without right. For them there is a painful punishment."

### إِنَّمَا — the particle of restriction

***Innamā* in Arabic conveys restriction (*qaṣr*): "only, solely".** The sentence, that is, does not say "the way is also against the wrongdoers"; **it says "it is against them alone".**

**Together with the preceding verse, the two sentences set up an equation:**

| Verse | Against whom there is / is not a way |
|---|---|
| **41** | The one who takes his right — **none** |
| **42** | The one who wrongs and oversteps — **there is, and against him alone** |

### يَظْلِمُونَ ٱلنَّاسَ — its object is "people"

**And a note on the arrangement: the object of the verb is *al-nās* — definite, general.** It does not say "those who wrong the believers"; **it says "those who wrong people".**

**I record this, because it accords directly with the principle written in the method:** *"No blanket ruling is issued about an ethnic or religious group; what the verse describes are attributes, and whoever bears that attribute is included in it."*

**The verse does exactly this here: the verdict is attached not to a group but to an act.** And the identity of the victim is not specified either — *al-nās*, everyone.

### وَيَبْغُونَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ ٱلْحَقِّ

**The fourth and last occurrence of the root *baghy* in the sūra.** The end of the series tabulated at the fourteenth verse:

| Verse | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | Those who divided | **In religion** |
| **27** | People, if provision were spread | **On the earth** |
| **39** | What is done to the believers | (the victim's side) |
| **42** | The wrongdoers | **On the earth** |

**And the twenty-seventh and forty-second verses use the same construction: *fi'l-arḍ*.** The twenty-seventh said *la-baghaw **fi'l-arḍ***; the forty-second says *wa yabghūna **fi'l-arḍ***.

**This is a verifiable fact.** And it shows this: **what is named in the twenty-seventh verse as a *possibility* is named in the forty-second as a *fact*.** What was said would happen were provision spread without limit has, even with limited provision, come about in some people.

**The qualification *bi-ghayri'l-ḥaqq*:** "without right". This qualification reinforces the sense of wrongfulness already present in the word itself.

## 42/43 — وَلَمَن صَبَرَ وَغَفَرَ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ لَمِنْ عَزْمِ ٱلْأُمُورِ

*Wa la-man ṣabara wa ghafara inna dhālika la-min ʿazmi'l-umūr*

"And whoever endures and forgives — that is among the matters that call for resolve."

### The same pattern as the forty-first verse

**Note: the two verses begin with the same structure.**

| Verse | The opening | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **41** | *wa **la-man**i'ntaṣara baʿda ẓulmihi* | **The one who takes his right** |
| **43** | *wa **la-man** ṣabara wa ghafara* | **The one who endures and forgives** |

**The same lām of emphasis, the same conditional pattern, two opposite behaviours.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text and is the proof of the balance at the level of arrangement: **the two behaviours are presented in the same grammatical form.**

### عَزْمِ ٱلْأُمُورِ — the matters that call for resolve

***ʿAzm* — root ع-ز-م.** It occurred in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/35 in connection with *ulu'l-ʿazm*. **The sense of the root: to resolve firmly upon a matter, to make one's intention firm.** The lexicographers record that the root holds **certainty and firmness.**

**The construction *ʿazmu'l-umūr* occurs in three places in the Qurʾān, and the context of all three is the same:**

| Place | What precedes it |
|---|---|
| **Āl ʿImrān 3/186** | *"If you **endure** and guard yourselves — that is among the ʿazmu'l-umūr"* |
| **Luqmān 31/17** | *"…**endure** what befalls you. That is among the ʿazmu'l-umūr"* |
| **al-Shūrā 42/43** | *"Whoever **endures** and forgives — that is among the ʿazmu'l-umūr"* |

**In all three occurrences the construction comes together with endurance.** This is a verifiable fact.

### The work of the word

**And the work of the construction here must be recorded, because it is the last piece of the balance.**

**When a person gives up his right, an onlooker can read it in two ways: as virtue or as inability.**

**The construction *ʿazmu'l-umūr* closes the second reading.** The word *ʿazm* conveys **decision** and **will.** **Pardon, that is, is named not "not being able to" but "deciding not to".**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the lexical sense of the word.**

### The balance the four verses set up — as a whole

**Now, by tabulating together the four verses from the fortieth to the forty-third, I show how the balance is built. This is a structure of arrangement that can be verified from the text.**

| Verse | What is granted | What is encouraged | The limit set |
|---|---|---|---|
| **39** | The one wronged **takes his right** — and this is counted an **attribute** of the believer | — | — |
| **40** | A return may be made **in like measure** | **Pardon + setting right** → the reward with God | *Lā yuḥibbu'ẓ-ẓālimīn* — more than that is wrongdoing |
| **41** | The one who takes his right **is not blamed** | — | — |
| **42** | — | — | The way turns **only** against the wrongdoer |
| **43** | — | **Endurance + forgiveness** = *ʿazmu'l-umūr* | — |

**The two ends of the balance are these, and both are the text's own expressions:**

**One end: not submission.** The thirty-ninth verse counts taking one's right as an attribute; the forty-first plainly defends the one who takes his right. **The sūra does not praise submitting to a wrong.**

**The other end: not locking oneself into retaliation.** The fortieth verse ties pardon to an unmeasured reward; the forty-third names pardon a **work of will.** **The sūra does not present making a return as the only way either.**

**And the passage between the two ends has been secured, at the level of arrangement, as follows:**

| Instrument | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The same pattern** | *wa la-man…* in 41 and 43 | Presents the two behaviours **at the same grammatical level** |
| **The difference of naming** | *sayyiʾa* / *ajr* in 40 | Sets the return down as lawful but **not fine** |
| **The source of the reward** | *ʿala'llāh* in 40 | Takes the price of pardon **out from between the parties** |
| **The note of limit** | *lā yuḥibbu'ẓ-ẓālimīn* at the end of 40 | Prevents the return from **overflowing** |
| **The fixing of direction** | *innamā* in 42 | Lays the responsibility **only** on the one who began |

### My own reading

**I record this as my own reading, and it is not binding:**

**These four verses reject two false solutions at once.**

**The first false solution: "pardon in every case."** This makes wrongdoing sustainable, because no cost is left in front of the wrongdoer. **The sūra closes this with the thirty-ninth and forty-first verses.**

**The second false solution: "always make a return."** This turns a relationship into a series of accounts and carries the constant risk of overstepping the *mithl* limit. **The sūra balances this with the note at the end of the fortieth verse and with the forty-third.**

**And what must be noticed is this: the sūra imposes no preference.** Both ways are left open. What is said is that **both are lawful and that the recompense of one will come from elsewhere.**

**One further observation:** in the fortieth verse the condition of pardon is two verbs — *ʿafā* **and** *aṣlaḥa*. **The pardon the sūra praises, that is, is the pardon that repairs a relationship.** A giving-up that does not repair does not fully fall under the wording of the verse.

### What this says to the present

The counsel given to a person who has been wronged usually falls into one of two extremes: either "do not dwell on it" is said and the wrong becomes normal, or "pursue your right to the end" is said and the matter fills the person's whole life.

**These four verses do neither.** They grant the right, set its limit, encourage pardon, but do **not make pardon obligatory** and do not blame the one who does not pardon.

**And in practice the most useful note is the sentence at the end of the fortieth verse:** *innahu lā yuḥibbu'ẓ-ẓālimīn.* **The possibility of turning into a wrongdoer while making a return is recalled in the very sentence that grants the right.** This is the place where the exercise of a right most often slips: the return's not stopping at the limit of *mithl*.

## 42/44 — وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِن وَلِىٍّ مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ ۗ وَتَرَى ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ لَمَّا رَأَوُا۟ ٱلْعَذَابَ يَقُولُونَ هَلْ إِلَىٰ مَرَدٍّ مِّن سَبِيلٍ

*Wa man yuḍlili'llāhu fa-mā lahu min waliyyin min baʿdih; wa tara'ẓ-ẓālimīna lammā raʾawu'l-ʿadhāba yaqūlūna hal ilā maraddin min sabīl*

"Whomever God leads astray has no protector after Him. You will see the wrongdoers, when they see the punishment, saying: 'Is there any way back?'"

### مَرَدّ — root ر-د-د

***Radd* — to turn back, to return.** *Maradd* — a place of return or the possibility of return; a noun of place or a verbal noun.

**And the word will come once more three verses later, this time in the negative:**

| Verse | Sentence | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| **44** | *Hal ilā **maraddin** min sabīl* | **The wrongdoers** — as a question |
| **47** | *Yawmun lā **maradda** lahu mina'llāh* | **The text** — as a statement |

**The question, that is, is answered before it is asked** — the forty-seventh verse closes the question asked in the forty-fourth. **And at the level of arrangement the order is reversed: the question first, the answer after; but the answer is given to the one addressed in this world.**

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement.**

### وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ — a note

***Iḍlāl* — root ض-ل-ل, Form IV: to lead astray.**

**This pattern has been debated in the history of theology, and this commentary's stance was recorded in `069-hakka.md`, `089-fecr.md` and `054-kamer.md`: no theological side is taken and the question of divine decree is not settled beneath a single verse.**

**I enter the same note here.** What is to be recorded is the verse's context: the sentence comes after the ruling given about the wrongdoers in the preceding group of verses (39-43). **Straying, that is, is positioned in the verse's arrangement as the outcome of a series of acts.**

## 42/45 — وَتَرَىٰهُمْ يُعْرَضُونَ عَلَيْهَا خَٰشِعِينَ مِنَ ٱلذُّلِّ يَنظُرُونَ مِن طَرْفٍ خَفِىٍّ ۗ وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِنَّ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَأَهْلِيهِمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ ۗ أَلَآ إِنَّ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ فِى عَذَابٍ مُّقِيمٍ

*Wa tarāhum yuʿraḍūna ʿalayhā khāshiʿīna mina'dh-dhulli yanẓurūna min ṭarfin khafiyy; wa qāla'lladhīna āmanū inna'l-khāsirīna'lladhīna khasirū anfusahum wa ahlīhim yawma'l-qiyāma; alā inna'ẓ-ẓālimīna fī ʿadhābin muqīm*

"You will see them brought before it, shrunken with humiliation, looking with a furtive glance. And those who believed will say: the losers are those who have lost themselves and their families on the day of resurrection. Know well that the wrongdoers are in a lasting punishment."

### يُنظُرُونَ مِن طَرْفٍ خَفِىٍّ — the finest description in the sūra

**This expression is one of the most careful sentences of observation in the Qurʾān and must be dwelt on.**

***Ṭarf* — root ط-ر-ف.** It was treated in `055-rahman.md`. **The concrete sense of the root: the tip or edge of a thing.** *Ṭarf* is specifically **the edge of the eyelid**, and from there **the eye, the glance.**

***Khafiyy* — root خ-ف-ي: hidden, concealed, faint.**

**The construction, then: "they look from a hidden, narrowed corner of the eye."**

**And what the description conveys must be recorded:** a person looks this way at something he does not want to look at but cannot help looking at — **not directly, but from the corner of the eye, stealing a glance.**

**This is the description of a state in which fear and curiosity are present at once.** And it accords with the measure recorded in `081-tekvir.md`: in describing a state the Qurʾān most often gives **its counterpart in the body.**

**A point of arrangement:** three states come one upon another in the sentence — *khāshiʿīna* (shrunken), *mina'dh-dhulli* (with humiliation), *yanẓurūna min ṭarfin khafiyy* (looking from the corner of the eye). **All three are things visible from outside.** The inner state is not described at all.

**خُشُوع — root خ-ش-ع:** in the lexicographers' description, **the lowering of the voice and the shrinking of the body.** The word is used both of the voice and of the body.

***Dhull* — root ذ-ل-ل:** treated in `067-mulk.md` and `002-bakara.md`: **a thing's state of being submissive, made tractable.** And the root has a positive branch as well (*dhalūl* — a docile, serviceable animal). **The use here is the negative branch: humiliation.**

### إِنَّ ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَأَهْلِيهِمْ

**خُسْر — root خ-س-ر:** treated in `103-asr.md`. **The concrete sense of the root is commercial: the diminishing of capital, loss.**

**And the construction of the sentence carries a *restriction* (*ḥaṣr*):** *inna'l-khāsirīna **alladhīna** khasirū* — "the losers **are these**". **Other losses, that is, are not counted losses.**

**And what is lost is two things: *anfusahum wa ahlīhim* — themselves and their families.**

**The choice of this pair is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** a commercial root is used, but what is lost is not property. **What is counted as capital is the person himself and those close to him.**

**The word *ahl* in Arabic means "family" and "those who belong to a place".** Two readings are transmitted: (a) their families in this world, (b) the kin who would have been given them had they been in the Garden. **I do not impose a preference.**

### عَذَابٍ مُّقِيمٍ

***Muqīm* — root ق-و-م, an active participle of Form IV: standing, settled, continuous.**

**And the root occurs for the third time in the sūra:**

| Verse | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| **13** | *aqīmu'd-dīn* | **Uphold the religion** — a command |
| **38** | *aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāh* | **They upheld the prayer** — an attribute |
| **45** | *ʿadhābin **muqīm*** | **A punishment that stands** — an outcome |

**The same root, in three different works.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text; I make no claim of a deliberate choice.

## 42/46 — وَمَا كَانَ لَهُم مِّنْ أَوْلِيَآءَ يَنصُرُونَهُم مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِن سَبِيلٍ

*Wa mā kāna lahum min awliyāʾa yanṣurūnahum min dūni'llāh; wa man yuḍlili'llāhu fa-mā lahu min sabīl*

"Nor were there for them any protectors to help them apart from God. And whomever God leads astray has no way."

### The meeting of two roots

**The verse gathers the sūra's two key roots into one sentence: و-ل-ي and ن-ص-ر.**

**And this pair had occurred twice before in the sūra, as nouns** (8 and 31: *min waliyyin wa lā naṣīr*). **Here one comes as a noun and the other as a verb: *awliyāʾa yanṣurūnahum*.**

| Verse | Form |
|---|---|
| **8, 31** | *Waliyy* + *naṣīr* — **two nouns** |
| **46** | *Awliyāʾ* + *yanṣurūna* — **noun + verb** |

**At the third occurrence, that is, protectorship is described by a verb: a protector is one who helps.** This accords with the proof given for *walī* in the ninth verse (giving life to the dead).

### مِن سَبِيلٍ — the word's last occurrence

**In the forty-first verse it was said *mā ʿalayhim min sabīl* (there is no way against them); here it says *fa-mā lahu min sabīl* (there is no way for him).**

| Verse | Construction | Preposition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **41** | *mā **ʿalayhim** min sabīl* | *ʿalā* — against | **No blame** |
| **46** | *fa-mā **lahu** min sabīl* | *li* — for | **No way out** |

**The same construction, two opposite senses with a single letter's difference.** This is a verifiable fact of language and an instance of the basic distinction in Arabic between *ʿalā* and *li* (against / for).

## 42/47 — ٱسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِرَبِّكُم مِّن قَبْلِ أَن يَأْتِىَ يَوْمٌ لَّا مَرَدَّ لَهُۥ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ۚ مَا لَكُم مِّن مَّلْجَإٍ يَوْمَئِذٍ وَمَا لَكُم مِّن نَّكِيرٍ

*Istajībū li-rabbikum min qabli an yaʾtiya yawmun lā maradda lahu mina'llāh; mā lakum min maljaʾin yawmaʾidhin wa mā lakum min nakīr*

"Answer your Lord before there comes a day that God will not turn back. On that day you will have no refuge, and no denial."

### ٱسْتَجِيبُوا۟ — the root's last occurrence, in the imperative

**The series tabulated at the twenty-sixth verse is completed here:**

| Verse | Who answers | Form |
|---|---|---|
| **16** | (passive) | Perfect passive |
| **26** | **God** | Imperfect |
| **38** | **The believers** | Perfect |
| **47** | **The one addressed** | **IMPERATIVE** |

**After four steps the sūra arrives at a command.** This is a fact of arrangement that can be verified from the text.

**And the qualification *li-rabbikum*: the one to be answered is not "us" but "your Lord".** It was the same in the thirty-eighth verse: *istajābū **li-rabbihim***. **The owner of the call, that is, is not the Prophet.** This accords with the limit of responsibility drawn in the sixth and forty-eighth verses.

### مَلْجَأ — root ل-ج-أ

***Lajaʾa* — to take refuge, to flee somewhere for protection.** *Maljaʾ* — a place of refuge.

**The word is rare in the Qurʾān; it occurs in the same sense in al-Tawba 9/118** (*wa ẓannū an lā **maljaʾa** mina'llāhi illā ilayh* — "they realised that there is no refuge from God except with Him"). **This verse is worth recording, because it shows the direction of refuge.**

### نَكِير — two readings

***Nakīr* — root ن-ك-ر: not to know, not to recognise, to deny.** *Munkar* — the unrecognised, the unaccepted; *inkār* is from the same root.

**Two readings of the word's sense here are transmitted:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | ***Nakīr* = *inkār***: "on that day you cannot deny what you did" — the record is out in the open |
| 2 | ***Nakīr* = the power to change, to object**: "you will have no means of altering the situation" |

**Both readings are transmitted in the classical sources; I impose no preference.**

**An observation about the arrangement:** the sentence gives two negations in the same pattern — *mā lakum min maljaʾ* / *mā lakum min nakīr*. **One is a place (nowhere to flee), the other speech (nothing to say).** Neither with the body nor with the tongue, that is, is any way out left.

## 42/48 — فَإِنْ أَعْرَضُوا۟ فَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَٰكَ عَلَيْهِمْ حَفِيظًا ۖ إِنْ عَلَيْكَ إِلَّا ٱلْبَلَٰغُ ۗ وَإِنَّآ إِذَآ أَذَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِنَّا رَحْمَةً فَرِحَ بِهَا ۖ وَإِن تُصِبْهُمْ سَيِّئَةٌۢ بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ فَإِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ كَفُورٌ

*Fa-in aʿraḍū fa-mā arsalnāka ʿalayhim ḥafīẓā; in ʿalayka illa'l-balāgh; wa innā idhā adhaqna'l-insāna minnā raḥmatan fariḥa bihā; wa in tuṣibhum sayyiʾatun bimā qaddamat aydīhim fa-inna'l-insāna kafūr*

"If they turn away, We did not send you as a watcher over them. Upon you is only the conveying. When We let the human being taste a mercy from Us, he rejoices in it; and if an evil befalls them for what their own hands have sent ahead, then indeed the human being is ungrateful."

### فَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَٰكَ عَلَيْهِمْ حَفِيظًا — the return to the sixth verse

**The verbal return recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra is completed here:**

| Verse | Sentence | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *Allāhu **ḥafīẓun** ʿalayhim* | **God** — a watcher |
| **48** | *mā arsalnāka ʿalayhim **ḥafīẓā*** | **You** — not a watcher |

**And what was said in the sixth verse with the word *wakīl* is repeated here with *ḥafīẓ*.**

**After a gap of forty-two verses, the sūra draws the same limit a second time.** This is a verifiable fact.

### إِنْ عَلَيْكَ إِلَّا ٱلْبَلَٰغُ — the name of the task

***Balāgh* — root ب-ل-غ: to reach, to arrive at.** It was treated in `046-ahkaf.md`. ***Balāgh* is "conveying"** — the word's arriving at the one addressed.

**And the word's limit is within itself, and I record this as my own reading:** *balāgh* is **to convey**, not **to get accepted.** The delivery of a letter does not guarantee its being read.

**The structure of the sentence reinforces this too:** *in… illā* — a pattern of restriction. "Upon you is **only** this."

### Two states of the human being — a description

**The second half of the verse changes subject and passes to a general description of the human being. Two situations, two reactions:**

| Situation | Verb | Reaction | Word |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A mercy is given to taste** | *adhaqnā* | **He rejoices** | *fariḥa bihā* |
| **An evil strikes** | *tuṣibhum* | **He is ungrateful** | *kafūr* |

**And there is an asymmetry between the two sentences, which must be recorded too:**

| | The sentence of mercy | The sentence of evil |
|---|---|---|
| Is the source stated | **Yes** — *minnā*, "from Us" | **No** — instead of a source, a **cause**: *bimā qaddamat aydīhim* |
| Person | Singular — *al-insāna… fariḥa* | **It passes to the plural** — *tuṣibhum… aydīhim* |

**Good, that is, comes "from Us", evil "from your own hands".** This is a verifiable fact of arrangement and is the same as the structure in the thirtieth verse.

***Adhāqa* — root ذ-و-ق: to taste.** The root occurred in `054-kamer.md` and `051-zariyat.md`. And the choice of verb is worth recording: **to taste is to take a very little.** The Qurʾān uses this verb both of favour and of punishment, and in both it conveys that **only a portion is given.**

***Kafūr* — root ك-ف-ر, the *faʿūl* intensive pattern: deeply ungrateful.** **The root's original sense is to cover** (*kāfir* — the farmer who covers the seed, the darkness that covers the night), and it was treated in `109-kafirun.md` and `076-insan.md`. **Ingratitude is covering over the good one has received.**

**And the pattern is the same measure as *shakūr* in the twenty-third and thirty-third verses (*faʿūl*).**

| Verse | Word | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **23** | *shakūr* | God | **One who gives return** |
| **33** | *shakūr* | The believer | **Much thanking** |
| **48** | *kafūr* | The human being | **Deeply ungrateful** |

**Three words, the same pattern, in three separate places in the sūra.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text and makes the opposition of *shukr* and *kufr* visible at the level of pattern.

### فَإِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ كَفُورٌ — a general ruling?

**A note must be entered.**

**The sentence says *al-insān* — with the definite article.** In Arabic this pattern conveys **the kind**: "the being called man". **Not a ruling about each individual human being, that is, but the description of a tendency.**

**And the sūra itself shows this:** the list running from the thirty-sixth verse to the thirty-ninth described another state of the same human being. **That two descriptions occur within the same sūra shows that the second is not an unqualified ruling.**

**This accords with the principle written in the method: what the verse describes are attributes.**

## 42/49-50 — لِّلَّهِ مُلْكُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۚ يَخْلُقُ مَا يَشَآءُ ۚ يَهَبُ لِمَن يَشَآءُ إِنَٰثًا وَيَهَبُ لِمَن يَشَآءُ ٱلذُّكُورَ · أَوْ يُزَوِّجُهُمْ ذُكْرَانًا وَإِنَٰثًا ۖ وَيَجْعَلُ مَن يَشَآءُ عَقِيمًا ۚ إِنَّهُۥ عَلِيمٌ قَدِيرٌ

*Li'llāhi mulku's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ; yakhluqu mā yashāʾ; yahabu li-man yashāʾu ināthan wa yahabu li-man yashāʾu'dh-dhukūr · Aw yuzawwijuhum dhukrānan wa ināthā; wa yajʿalu man yashāʾu ʿaqīmā; innahu ʿalīmun qadīr*

"To God belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth. He creates what He wills. **He bestows daughters on whom He wills, and He bestows sons on whom He wills.** Or He gives them in pairs, both male and female. And He makes whom He wills childless. He is knowing, powerful."

### يَهَبُ — root و-ه-ب

**The root is analysed here for the first time in the corpus.**

***Wahaba* — to give without return, to bestow.** *Hiba* — what is given without price. **And this is the root's distinguishing side: a *hiba* is a giving that has no return.**

**For comparison:**

| Verb | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **أَعْطَىٰ** (*aʿṭā*) | ع-ط-و | To give — general |
| **رَزَقَ** (*razaqa*) | ر-ز-ق | To provide — according to need |
| **وَهَبَ** (*wahaba*) | و-ه-ب | **To bestow without return** |

**And the verb is repeated twice in the verse: *yahabu… wa yahabu*.** The same verb, that is, is used for both sides.

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement:** a child is named in the verse neither a **right**, nor a **result**, nor an **achievement.** It is named a **gift.** **And the same word is used for both sexes.**

### The naming of daughters first

**And this is the most striking side of the verse.**

**The order in the sentence is this: first *ināthan* (daughters), then *al-dhukūr* (sons).**

**This is the reverse of the addressed society's ordering. And what that ordering was is recorded in the Qurʾān's own data.**

**In `081-tekvir.md` at 81/8-9 (*al-mawʾūda*) this subject was treated in detail, and what was tabulated there was this:**

> | Verse | What it says | The motive |
> |---|---|---|
> | *al-Naḥl 16/58-59* | The face of the father who receives news of a daughter turns black, he hides, he thinks of burying her | **Shame, disgrace** |
> | *al-Isrāʾ 17/31* | *"Do not kill your children out of fear of poverty"* | **Fear of poverty** |

**And the distinction recorded there was important:** al-Isrāʾ 17/31 and al-Anʿām 6/151 say "your children" and specify no sex; **the killing of daughters out of shame is a separate fact, and the word *mawʾūda* is feminine and names it.**

**It was also recorded in `081-tekvir.md` that both exaggerations about how widespread it was are to be avoided** — both "everyone was doing it" and "it never happened" were rejected. **I do not repeat that note; I rely on it.**

**Now the wording of al-Naḥl 16/58 must be noticed:**

> *"When one of them is given good news of **a daughter**, his face turns black and he is filled with rage."*

**The matter, that is, is before killing a question of *ordering*: news of a daughter is counted bad news.**

**And al-Shūrā 42/49 turns exactly that ordering around.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of the two texts:** the verse does not defend the daughter, does not praise her, does not advance a thesis. **It merely puts her first in the order.** And it does this without opening an argument and without giving a reason.

**There is one further detail of arrangement, and it must be recorded too:**

| Word | Its state |
|---|---|
| *ināthan* | **Indefinite** |
| *al-dhukūr* | **Definite** (with the article) |

**The exegetes have dwelt on this difference and several explanations are transmitted:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | The definiteness answers the **known** preference in the minds of those addressed: "those sons you want" |
| 2 | The indefiniteness here conveys **kind**; the definiteness also conveys kind; there is no difference of meaning between them |
| 3 | The word came in this form as a requirement of the verse-ending (*fāṣila*) |

**There is no certain knowledge and I impose no preference.** What is to be recorded is that the difference **exists.**

### Four possibilities — and a complete division

**Read together, the two verses enumerate every possible case. This is a fact that can be verified from the text:**

| # | Case | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daughters only** | *Yahabu li-man yashāʾu **ināthā*** |
| 2 | **Sons only** | *Wa yahabu li-man yashāʾu'**dh-dhukūr*** |
| 3 | **Both together** | *Aw **yuzawwijuhum** dhukrānan wa ināthā* |
| 4 | **Neither** | *Wa yajʿalu man yashāʾu **ʿaqīmā*** |

**Four cases, the whole of a totality.** There is no other possibility.

**And all four are within the same sentence; all four are attached to the same verb (*yashāʾ*); all four stand under the sentence *li'llāhi mulku's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*.**

***Yuzawwijuhum* — root ز-و-ج, Form II: to make into pairs, to pair.** **The root occurred in the eleventh verse as *azwāj*.** There are two readings of its sense here:

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **"He gives the two together"** — both a son and a daughter |
| 2 | **"He gives them in turn"** — a son and a daughter, one after the other |

**I do not impose a preference; the first reading is the more widespread.**

### عَقِيم — and how it is named

***ʿAqīm* — root ع-ق-م.** **The root was treated in `051-zariyat.md` at 51/29** (*ʿajūzun ʿaqīm*). The note there: **at the centre of the root are dryness, unfruitfulness, a thing's being cut off.**

**And here one must proceed carefully, because the word names a human condition.**

**Let us look at what the verse does — and this is read directly from the text:**

**First: the fourth case is named *in the same sentence* as the other three.** It is not put in a separate sentence, a separate verse or a separate context. **Having no child, that is, is counted not as a fault outside the order but as a state within the order.**

**Second: the verb is the same kind.** In the other three *yahabu* and *yuzawwiju*; here *yajʿalu* — "He makes". **All four are attached to the same subject.**

**Third: the verse states no ground, no cause and no defect.** *Man yashāʾ* — "whom He wills". **The same expression is present in the other three.**

**Fourth: the verse closes with two names — *ʿalīmun qadīr*.** And the name *ʿalīm* (knowing) stands in a place worth recording: **after the enumeration of the four cases it is said "He is knowing".**

**I record this as my own reading, and it is not binding:** the name *ʿalīm* set at the end of the four cases says that the distribution is not ignorant or random. **And this is said, for one who is in the fourth case, in the same sentence as for those in the other three.**

**A note of limit — and I write it plainly:** the verse issues no ruling about a person who has no child, shows no cause, ascribes no deficiency. **To draw such a conclusion from the verse runs against the verse's having listed the four cases in the same sentence and in the same structure.**

### What this says to the present

**Two separate aspects can be recorded, and both follow from the wording of the verse.**

**First: the ordering.** The verse names the daughter first, and does so not as a thesis but as an order. **As recorded in `081-tekvir.md`, the motive the verse describes — the counting of a daughter as a burden and a disgrace — has not been buried in history.** Sex-selective practices have produced measurable population imbalances in some countries and have become the subject of legal regulation. **This is written not as a note of "scientific miracle" but as a record that the attitude the verse opposes persists.**

**Second: the manner in which childlessness is named.** In a community, people who have no children are most often put either where they are not spoken of or where they are pitied. **The verse does neither:** it neither keeps silent nor sets them apart. **It enumerates the four cases in one breath.**

## 42/51 — وَمَا كَانَ لِبَشَرٍ أَن يُكَلِّمَهُ ٱللَّهُ إِلَّا وَحْيًا أَوْ مِن وَرَآئِ حِجَابٍ أَوْ يُرْسِلَ رَسُولًا فَيُوحِىَ بِإِذْنِهِۦ مَا يَشَآءُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ عَلِىٌّ حَكِيمٌ

*Wa mā kāna li-basharin an yukallimahu'llāhu illā waḥyan aw min warāʾi ḥijābin aw yursila rasūlan fa-yūḥiya bi-idhnihi mā yashāʾ; innahu ʿaliyyun ḥakīm*

"It is not for a mortal that God should speak to him except by revelation, or from behind a veil, or by sending a messenger who conveys by His permission what He wills. He is high, wise."

### لِبَشَر — the choice of word

***Bashar* — root ب-ش-ر.** In `054-kamer.md` a reading was drawn from the choice of this word. **The concrete sense of the root is *bashara* — skin, flesh. *Bashar* is the word that names the human being on the side of his body and skin.**

**And its difference from *insān* is set out by the lexicographers thus:**

| Word | Root | What it brings forward |
|---|---|---|
| **إِنْسَان** | أ-ن-س (or ن-س-ي) | Familiarity, the forming of closeness; or forgetting |
| **بَشَر** | ب-ش-ر | **The body, the skin, the visible side** |

**That *bashar* is used in the sentence is a point of arrangement, and I record it:** the impossibility of speech is tied to the human being's being **embodied.** **The obstacle, that is, is neither moral nor a matter of knowledge; it is a matter of the kind of being.**

### The three ways of revelation

**The verse enumerates three ways, and all three come in a different grammatical form. This is a point the grammarians have dwelt on and is a verifiable fact.**

| # | Expression | Grammatical form | What happens | What comes in between |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | ***وَحْيًا*** | **An accusative noun** (verbal noun / circumstantial) | **Direct communication** — without an intermediary | **None** |
| **2** | ***أَوْ مِن وَرَآئِ حِجَابٍ*** | **A prepositional phrase** (adverbial) | **The speech is heard, the speaker is not seen** | **A veil** |
| **3** | ***أَوْ يُرْسِلَ رَسُولًا فَيُوحِىَ بِإِذْنِهِۦ*** | **A verbal sentence** | **A messenger is sent, and he conveys by permission** | **A messenger** |

**That the three ways come in three different grammatical forms is explained by the grammarians thus:** the third requires a **verb** and an **agent** (the act of sending and the one sent), and so it is built as a verbal sentence; in the first two there is no separate agent.

**I give this explanation as a transmitted report.** What I record is the difference of form itself.

### What the three ways have in common

**And there is something that must be noticed: in all three the word "waḥy" or its root is present.**

- The first is directly *waḥyan*.
- In the third the messenger's task too is *fa-**yūḥiya*** — "he reveals".
- The second does not carry the word but is inside the same list.

**The word *waḥy*, that is, is used both in a narrow and in a wide sense:** in the narrow sense the first way, in the wide sense the shared name of the three.

**This accords with the finding recorded in `053-necm.md`:** the concrete sense of the root is **swift and hidden communication**, and its field of use is wide. **The verse displays that width within itself.**

### مِن وَرَآئِ حِجَابٍ

***Ḥijāb* — root ح-ج-ب.** It was treated in `083-mutaffifin.md`. The note there: **the concrete sense is to come between two things and prevent them from seeing one another**; *ḥājib* — a doorkeeper, the official who stands between a ruler and the people; and **the eyebrow** — what stands above the eye.

**And in `083-mutaffifin.md` the word occurred in a context of deprivation** (*innahum ʿan rabbihim yawmaʾidhin **la-maḥjūbūn***). **In al-Shūrā the same root becomes the name of a mode of communication.**

| Place | Word | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Muṭaffifīn 83/15** | *la-maḥjūbūn* | **Barring** — deprivation of sight |
| **al-Shūrā 42/51** | *min warāʾi ḥijāb* | **Mediating** — speech from behind a veil |

**The same root, one a cutting off and one a letting through.** This is a verifiable fact.

**And a point of arrangement: the construction *min warāʾi ḥijāb* says that the barrier is not removed.** The speech takes place not in spite of the veil but **by means of the veil.** **The veil, that is, is the condition of the speech.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the eleventh verse:** the sentence *laysa ka-mithlihi shayʾ* had removed likeness. **The veil is the counterpart, in communication, of that removed likeness** — without something set in between there is no transmission.

### بِإِذْنِهِ — the return to the twenty-first verse

**As recorded at the twenty-first verse, the word *idhn* occurs twice in the sūra, in two opposite directions:**

| Verse | Sentence | What |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | *mā **lam yaʾdhan** bihi'llāh* | **Without permission** — a false religion |
| **51** | *fa-yūḥiya **bi-idhnihi*** | **With permission** — the messenger's conveying |

**This is a fact that can be verified from the text.** And what it shows is this: **even the messenger's conveying is not authoritative on its own; it is tied to permission.** The measure is the same in both places.

***ʿAliyyun ḥakīm* shares a root with *al-ʿaliyyu'l-ʿaẓīm* in the fourth verse (ع-ل-و), and shares a name with *al-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm* in the third (*ḥakīm*).** I complete here the observation recorded at the third verse:

| Verse | The pair of names |
|---|---|
| **3** | *al-**ʿazīzu**'l-**ḥakīm*** |
| **4** | *al-**ʿaliyyu**'l-ʿaẓīm* |
| **51** | ***ʿaliyyun ḥakīm*** |

**The pair in the fifty-first verse is made of one part from each of the pairs in the third and fourth verses.** This is a verifiable fact; I make no claim of a deliberate choice.

## 42/52 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ رُوحًا مِّنْ أَمْرِنَا ۚ مَا كُنتَ تَدْرِى مَا ٱلْكِتَٰبُ وَلَا ٱلْإِيمَٰنُ وَلَٰكِن جَعَلْنَٰهُ نُورًا نَّهْدِى بِهِۦ مَن نَّشَآءُ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا ۚ وَإِنَّكَ لَتَهْدِىٓ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ

*Wa kadhālika awḥaynā ilayka rūḥan min amrinā; mā kunta tadrī ma'l-kitābu wa la'l-īmān; wa lākin jaʿalnāhu nūran nahdī bihi man nashāʾu min ʿibādinā; wa innaka la-tahdī ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm*

"And thus We revealed to you a spirit from Our command. You did not know what the Book was, nor what faith was; but We made it a light by which We guide whom We will of Our servants. And you indeed guide to a straight path."

### وَكَذَٰلِكَ — for the third time

**The triad recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra is completed here:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **3** | *Kadhālika **yūḥī** ilayka* |
| **7** | *Wa kadhālika **awḥaynā** ilayka **qurʾānan ʿarabiyyā*** |
| **52** | *Wa kadhālika **awḥaynā** ilayka **rūḥan min amrinā*** |

**The seventh and fifty-second verses use the same verb in the same pattern; what changes is the name of what is revealed.**

| Verse | The name of what is revealed | What it brings forward |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *Qurʾānan ʿarabiyyā* | **Language and form** — what is recited, what is in Arabic |
| **52** | *Rūḥan min amrinā* | **Function** — what gives life |

**This is a verifiable fact.** The same thing is named twice, and the two names give two different aspects.

**And the open question recorded at the third verse closes here:** what did *kadhālika* point to? **The fifty-first verse enumerated the three ways; the fifty-second says "thus".**

### رُوحًا مِّنْ أَمْرِنَا — root ر-و-ح

**The root was treated in detail in `056-vakia.md` at 56/89 (*rawḥ wa rayḥān*). The table there was this:**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Rīḥ* | Wind |
| *Rūḥ* | Life, spirit |
| *Rawḥ* | Ease, relief, a breeze |
| *Rayḥān* | A sweet-smelling plant; and provision |
| *Rāḥa* | Rest |

**And the shared core recorded there was this:** *"the lexicographers generally give it as 'moving air': the wind blows, the breath goes in and out, scent is carried on the air, relief is the opening of what was tight."*

**I do not repeat it. What belongs here is the word's use for revelation.**

***Rūḥ* is what separates the living from the lifeless.** And revelation's being named with this word is seen elsewhere in the Qurʾān too:

> *"He sends down the angels **with a spirit from His command** upon whom He wills of His servants."* (al-Naḥl 16/2)
>
> *"He casts **the spirit from His command** upon whom He wills of His servants."* (al-Muʾmin 40/15)

**In all three verses there is the same construction: *min amrih* / *min amrinā*.** This is a verifiable fact.

**And the word *al-amr* has been understood here in two ways:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | ***Amr* = affair, command** — "a spirit from Our command" |
| 2 | ***Amr* = the divine sphere of action** — the same usage as *quli'r-rūḥu min amri rabbī* in al-Isrāʾ 17/85 |

**I impose no preference.**

### What is "rūḥ" here?

**There are two main readings of what the word answers to in this verse:**

| Reading | What *rūḥ* is | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The revelation / the Qurʾān itself** | The continuation of the sentence: *mā kunta tadrī **ma'l-kitāb**…* and *jaʿalnā**hu** nūrā* — the pronoun refers to what was revealed |
| 2 | **Gabriel** | The word *rūḥ* being used in the Qurʾān of an angel as well (al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/193: *al-rūḥu'l-amīn*) |

**The first reading is the more widespread and the pronoun structure of the sentence supports it.** But I impose no preference.

### What follows from the choice of word

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sense of the root drawn out in `056-vakia.md`:**

**Revelation is named here neither "knowledge" nor "news". It is named *rūḥ*.**

**And the difference between *rūḥ* and *ʿilm* is this:** knowledge is added, spirit **brings to life.** Knowledge is set upon a thing; spirit is what makes that thing be.

**And the continuation of the sentence supports this:** *jaʿalnāhu nūrā* — "We made it a light". **Light too is not something added; it is the condition of visibility.**

**The two words — *rūḥ* and *nūr* — do the same work: both are themselves unseen but make other things possible.**

### مَا كُنتَ تَدْرِى مَا ٱلْكِتَٰبُ وَلَا ٱلْإِيمَٰنُ

**This sentence must be dwelt on carefully, and comparing it with `046-ahkaf.md` 46/9 gives the right frame.**

**First I set the two verses side by side:**

| | **al-Aḥqāf 46/9** | **al-Shūrā 42/52** |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence | *wa **mā adrī** mā yufʿalu bī wa lā bikum* | *wa **mā kunta tadrī** ma'l-kitābu wa la'l-īmān* |
| Root | **د-ر-ي** | **د-ر-ي** |
| Time | **Imperfect** — "I do not know", **now** | ***Kāna* + imperfect** — "you did not know", **in the past** |
| Who says it | **The Prophet** (with the command *qul*) | **The text** — addressing him |
| Subject | **The outcome** — what will happen to him | **The Book and faith** |
| The state of the limit | **It stands** | **It has been crossed** |

**And this comparison shows a line. What was recorded for that verse in `046-ahkaf.md` was this:**

> *"This sentence is one of the most limiting expressions the Qurʾān puts into its prophet's mouth, and it must be treated honestly… What is to be recorded is this: the sentence states a limit… And immediately afterwards it is said where the source is: **the knowledge is not from himself.**"*

**Al-Shūrā 42/52 says the same thing but changes the tense.** In al-Aḥqāf the limit **still stands**; in al-Shūrā the moment at which the limit **was crossed** is recounted — and the one who crossed it was not himself.

**Together, then, the two verses draw this line:**

| Time | What is known | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Before the revelation** | The Book and faith — **were not known** (42/52) | — |
| **After the revelation** | The Book and faith — **were made known** | *Awḥaynā… jaʿalnāhu nūrā* |
| **Now and after** | The outcome — **still not known** (46/9) | The limit stands |

**This is a verifiable fact and I record it: the limit is not lifted, it moves.**

### وَلَا ٱلْإِيمَٰنُ — a disputed word

**And the second object of the sentence (*al-īmān*) has been dwelt on, because taken at its wording it touches a sensitive matter.**

**First I record what the wording says: the sentence does not say *"you were not a believer"*. It says *"you did not know"*** — the verb is *darā*, not *āmana*.

**The chief readings transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | What *al-īmān* is | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The details of faith, the foundations of the law** | What these were could not be known before the revelation came |
| 2 | **The faith of people** | "You did not know who would believe" |
| 3 | **Faith itself as a matter of knowledge** | Something whose content cannot be known unless made known by revelation |
| 4 | **The Qurʾān being named "faith"** | The word's coming together with *al-kitāb* is seen as supporting this |

**All four readings are transmitted in the classical sources. I impose no preference.**

**And I enter a note of method:** the state of the prophets before revelation is a heading that has been debated in the history of theology. **This commentary does not enter into that debate.** What is to be recorded is that the wording of the verse speaks of **knowledge.**

**And the discussion at `093-duha.md` 93/7 (*wa wajadaka ḍāllan fa-hadā*) was treated within the same frame; I rely on it and do not repeat it.**

### وَإِنَّكَ لَتَهْدِىٓ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ

**The sentence is built with two instruments of emphasis: *inna* + *la-*.** And what it says sets up a contrast with the sentence immediately before it:

| Sentence | What it says |
|---|---|
| *Mā kunta tadrī* | **You did not know** |
| *Wa innaka la-tahdī* | **And you guide** |

**In the same verse, about the same person: he did not know, and he guides.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the two sentences standing side by side is enough to show the source of the guiding. **A person who did not know turning into a guide recounts what happened in between without stating it.**

**And the object of the verb must be recorded: *tahdī ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm* — "you guide towards a straight path".** What one is brought to, that is, is the path itself. **Guidance here is not arrival but setting out on the road.**

**And the next verse will give the name of that path.**

**The word *ṣirāṭ* was treated in detail in `001-fatiha.md`** (*ihdina'ṣ-ṣirāṭa'l-mustaqīm*) **and occurred in `047-muhammed.md` as well; I do not repeat it.** Its summary: a *ṣirāṭ* is the wide, open main road that everyone travels; the origin of the word is related to **swallowing** (*sariṭa*) — the road that takes the traveller into itself.

## 42/53 — صِرَٰطِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلَّذِى لَهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۗ أَلَآ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ تَصِيرُ ٱلْأُمُورُ

*Ṣirāṭi'llāhi'lladhī lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ; alā ila'llāhi taṣīru'l-umūr*

"The path of God, to whom belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Know well that to God all affairs return."

### صِرَٰطِ ٱللَّهِ — an appositive

**The word is an appositive substitute for the last word of the preceding verse (*ṣirāṭin mustaqīm*) — its explanation.** The verse thus completes the preceding sentence: **which straight path? The path of God.**

**And the definition is given by a relative clause: *alladhī lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ.***

### The frame of the sūra closes

**This sentence is the sentence of the fourth verse. It was recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra; here it is completed:**

| Verse | Sentence | Its position |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | *Lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ* | **An independent statement** |
| **53** | *…alladhī **lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ*** | **The description of a path** |

**Fifty verses apart, word for word the same sentence.** This is a verifiable fact.

**And the difference, as recorded at the fourth verse, is in its position:** a statement at the beginning, a **description** at the end. **The sūra closes the frame it opened with a sentence about dominion by making that same sentence the name of a path.**

**I record this as my own reading, and it is not binding:** in the fourth verse the sentence was said as a **fact**; in the fifty-third as a **direction.** The same information, first as a state, then as a bearing.

### أَلَآ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ تَصِيرُ ٱلْأُمُورُ

**The last sentence of the sūra, and it closes three rings at once.**

**First: the root ص-ي-ر.** In the fifteenth verse *wa ilayhi'l-**maṣīr*** occurred; here the same root comes as a verb: *ta**ṣīru**'l-umūr*.

| Verse | Word | Form |
|---|---|---|
| **15** | *al-maṣīr* | **A noun** — the place of arrival |
| **53** | *taṣīru* | **A verb** — it returns |

**Second: the word *al-amr*.** It occurs three times in the sūra:

| Verse | Construction | Whose affair |
|---|---|---|
| **38** | *wa **amruhum** shūrā baynahum* | **The community's** affair |
| **43** | *la-min ʿazmi'l-**umūr*** | The affairs that **call for resolve** |
| **53** | *taṣīru'l-**umūr*** | **All** affairs |

**Three occurrences, three scales: community, person, the whole.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text.

**I record this as my own reading, and it is not binding:** the sūra uses the word "affair" three times, and in all three there is a question of ownership — **whose affair is it?** In the thirty-eighth verse it is the community's, in the forty-third the person's, and in the fifty-third the place to which all of them arrive is stated. **And the three do not conflict:** the affair is in the human being's hands, the human being decides it, but where it goes is settled.

**Third: the particle *alā*.** It occurs four times in the sūra, and all four are a sign of warning:

| Verse | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **5** | *Alā inna'llāha huwa'l-ghafūru'r-raḥīm* |
| **18** | *Alā inna'lladhīna yumārūna fi's-sāʿati la-fī ḍalālin baʿīd* |
| **45** | *Alā inna'ẓ-ẓālimīna fī ʿadhābin muqīm* |
| **53** | *Alā ila'llāhi taṣīru'l-umūr* |

**Four occurrences; and the last, unlike the other three, does not take *inna*.** In its last sentence the sūra lets go of one instrument of emphasis.

**I record this as an observation about the arrangement.**

### What this says to the present

The sūra opened as a sūra about division: people divide, argue, take one another's rights. **And its last sentence binds all of this to a single place.**

**But what must be noticed is this:** the sentence does not say "all of it returns to God, so there is nothing to do." **The sūra itself established the opposite:** uphold the religion (13), stand straight (15), do justice (15), consult (38), take your right (39), pardon (40), spend (38).

**The work of the last sentence is to take the outcome of these acts off the back of the human being** — just as the sixth and forty-eighth verses took it off the back of the Prophet.

**The work is done; where the work arrives is known elsewhere.**

## The sūra as a whole — looking back

### What the sūra did

**Al-Shūrā is a sūra about division, and it takes division up at three separate levels:**

| Level | Where | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Belief** | 13-16 | Division arises **after** knowledge; the cause is *baghy* |
| **Community** | 36-39 | The attributes of an undivided community — **shūrā** stands in the middle of this list |
| **The individual** | 39-43 | What the one who is wronged does: right, pardon, and the balance of the two |

**And at no level does the sūra say "let there be no difference."** The eighth verse rejects that outright: *"Had God willed, He would have made them a single community."* **What is opposed is not plurality but *tafarruq* — rupture.**

**The distinction between the *ikhtilāf* of the tenth verse and the *tafarruq* of the thirteenth carries the whole argument of the sūra:** for one an authority is pointed out, the other is forbidden.

### Structure

| Block | Verses | The work | The close |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-6 | The letters, the source of revelation, the limit of responsibility | *Wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl* |
| **II** | 7-9 | An Arabic Qurʾān, the mother of towns, two parties | *ʿAlā kulli shayʾin qadīr* |
| **III** | 10-12 | Where disagreement is referred, **the sentence of transcendence**, the keys of provision | *Bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm* |
| **IV** | 13-16 | **The law, five prophets, the cause of division**, the closing of the argument | *ʿAdhābun shadīd* |
| **V** | 17-19 | The Book and the balance, the nearness of the Hour | *Al-qawiyyu'l-ʿazīz* |
| **VI** | 20-23 | **Two tillages**, an unlicensed law, **the question of reward** | *Ghafūrun shakūr* |
| **VII** | 24-28 | The charge of fabrication, repentance, **the measure of provision**, rain | *Al-waliyyu'l-ḥamīd* |
| **VIII** | 29-35 | Living creatures, **affliction and pardon**, ships | *Mā lahum min maḥīṣ* |
| **IX** | 36-43 | **The attributes of the believer, shūrā, the balance of return and pardon** | *La-min ʿazmi'l-umūr* |
| **X** | 44-53 | Being brought to the punishment, the call, the limit of conveying, **children, the three ways of revelation** | *Taṣīru'l-umūr* |

### My own readings

**The following are mine, are not transmitted reports, and are not binding:**

The reading that the three spinal rings (revelation, dominion, "affairs") form an order; the note that the only measurable effect of the division of the disconnected letters is in the rhythm of recitation; the reading that the *kadhālika* of the third verse closes in the fifty-second; the line between the pairs of names in the third and fifty-first verses; the reading that in the fourth verse the name *ʿaliyy* completes the sentence about ownership by a "difference of plane";

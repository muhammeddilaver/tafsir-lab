# 38. Sūrat Ṣād

**Eighty-eight verses.** It is widely reported to have come down in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the single letter in its first verse: **ص**.

It is one of the three sūras in the Qurʾān that open with a single letter — **Ṣād, Qāf, Nūn**. The three were compared in `050-kaf.md`, and what was recorded there was this: in all three, the first thing that follows the letter belongs to **the field of writing**. Sūrat al-Qalam names the pen; Ṣād and Qāf name the text.

**The subject of the sūra can be said in a single word: *judgment*.** But this is not the sūra of saying "judgment will be passed on the Day of Resurrection". What the sūra does is more interesting: **it tells of the work of judging by putting it into the hands of individual people.**

- In the twenty-second verse two litigants ask a prophet for a judgment.
- In the twenty-sixth verse the measure of judging is announced to that prophet: *bi'l-ḥaqq*, and *lā tattabiʿi'l-hawā*.
- In the twenty-seventh verse a judgment is passed about the creation of heaven and earth: not *bāṭilan*.
- In the twenty-eighth verse a question is asked: are the two groups **to be treated alike**?
- From the forty-ninth verse onward two recompenses are set side by side and nothing is asked of the reader — they are merely shown.
- And in the seventy-fifth verse Iblīs is asked: *mā manaʿaka* — what held you back?

**Throughout the sūra, that is, someone is continually deciding about something.** Some decide rightly, some are in haste, some trust their own analogy and are wrong.

And the opening and the close of the sūra set this in a frame: **in the first verse an oath is sworn by the Qurʾān, and in the eighty-eighth it is said, *"you will surely know its report after a time."*** The eighty-six verses between stand in this interval in which knowing is deferred.

**This section will first make the skeleton of the sūra visible, and then come down to the verses.**

---

## The structure of the sūra

The sūra consists of eleven blocks. The boundaries of the blocks are drawn mostly either by a change of personal name or by a repeated pattern.

| Block | Verses | Subject | What ends the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-11 | The letter, the oath, the inventory of the objection | *jundun mā hunālika mahzūmun mina'l-**aḥzāb*** (11) |
| **II** | 12-16 | The list of those who cried lies, and the single cry | *ʿajjil lanā qiṭṭanā qabla yawmi'l-**ḥisāb*** (16) |
| **III** | 17-26 | **David** (Dāwūd) — kingdom, wisdom, the suit, the measure of judgment | *bimā nasū yawma'l-**ḥisāb*** (26) |
| **IV** | 27-29 | That the creation is not in vain, and the ground of the Book | *wa li-yatadhakkara ulu'l-**albāb*** (29) |
| **V** | 30-40 | **Solomon** (Sulaymān) — the horses, the prayer, the wind, the giving | *wa inna lahū ʿindanā la-zulfā wa ḥusna **maʾāb*** (40) |
| **VI** | 41-44 | **Job** (Ayyūb) — affliction, healing, the oath, patience | *niʿma'l-ʿabd innahū **awwāb*** (44) |
| **VII** | 45-48 | Six names: Abraham (Ibrāhīm), Isaac (Isḥāq), Jacob (Yaʿqūb), Ishmael (Ismāʿīl), Elisha (al-Yasaʿ), Dhū'l-Kifl | *wa kullun mina'l-**akhyār*** (48) |
| **VIII** | 49-64 | The two recompenses side by side, and the quarrel in the Fire | *la-ḥaqqun takhāṣumu ahli'n-**nār*** (64) |
| **IX** | 65-70 | "I am only a warner" | *illā annamā ana nadhīrun **mubīn*** (70) |
| **X** | 71-85 | **Iblīs** — the command to prostrate, the analogy, the expulsion, the term | *wa mimman tabiʿaka minhum **ajmaʿīn*** (85) |
| **XI** | 86-88 | The close: no wage, no contriving, but a term | *wa la-taʿlamunna nabaʾahū baʿda **ḥīn*** (88) |

**There is something in the placement of the blocks that must be recorded:** blocks III, V, VI and VII — that is, the exact middle of the sūra, the thirty-two verses from verse 17 to verse 48 — are given over to **the accounts of the prophets**. And these accounts stand between the polemic that precedes them and the scene that follows them.

---

### The clearest datum of the sūra: the verse-ending changes at the sixty-seventh verse

**This is a countable fact, and it can be verified one by one.**

**Of the eighty-eight verses, the first sixty-six, from 1 to 66, end without exception in a long *ā* sound followed by a single consonant:**

*dhikr, shiqāq, manāṣ, kadhdhāb, ʿujāb, yurād, ikhtilāq, ʿadhāb, al-Wahhāb, al-asbāb, al-aḥzāb, al-awtād, al-aḥzāb, ʿiqāb, fawāq, al-ḥisāb, awwāb, al-ishrāq, awwāb, al-khiṭāb, al-miḥrāb, al-ṣirāṭ, al-khiṭāb, anāb, maʾāb, al-ḥisāb, al-nār, al-fujjār, al-albāb, awwāb, al-jiyād, al-ḥijāb, al-aʿnāq, anāb, al-Wahhāb, aṣāb, ghawwāṣ, al-aṣfād, ḥisāb, maʾāb, ʿadhāb, sharāb, al-albāb, awwāb, al-abṣār, al-dār, al-akhyār, al-akhyār, maʾāb, al-abwāb, sharāb, atrāb, al-ḥisāb, nafād, maʾāb, al-mihād, ghassāq, azwāj, al-nār, al-qarār, al-nār, al-ashrār, al-abṣār, al-nār, al-Qahhār, al-Ghaffār.*

**Then in the sixty-seventh verse the sound changes, and it never returns:**

*ʿaẓīm, muʿriḍūn, yakhtaṣimūn, mubīn, ṭīn, sājidīn, ajmaʿūn, al-kāfirīn, al-ʿālīn, ṭīn, rajīm, al-dīn, yubʿathūn, al-munẓarīn, al-maʿlūm, ajmaʿīn, al-mukhlaṣīn, aqūl, ajmaʿīn, al-mutakallifīn, li'l-ʿālamīn, ḥīn.*

**Twenty-two verses, this time with a long *ī* or *ū* sound.**

**And where exactly is the break?** At the sixty-seventh verse. And that verse is this:

> **قُلْ هُوَ نَبَؤٌا۟ عَظِيمٌ** — *"Say: it is a mighty report."*

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the list above:** for sixty-six verses the sūra speaks in a single sound; then it says "**a mighty report**" and changes its voice. **In the ear, the sound changes before the subject does.**

**One further note:** the sound pattern of the second section (*ī/ū* + a single consonant) is the pattern of the **whole** of Sūrat Qāf, as recorded in `050-kaf.md`. Ṣād, that is, passes over into the sound of Qāf in its last twenty-two verses. **I give this as an observation; I am not building a claim on it.**

### The backbone of the sūra: ذ-ك-ر

**In the first verse the sūra describes the Qurʾān as *dhi'dh-dhikr* (possessor of the reminder), and it closes in the eighty-seventh verse by saying *dhikrun li'l-ʿālamīn* (a reminder for all the worlds).** In between the word returns six more times:

| Verse | Expression | In whose mouth |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *wa'l-Qurʾāni **dhi'dh-dhikr*** | The text |
| 8 | *a-unzila ʿalayhi'**dh-dhikru** min bayninā* | The objectors |
| 8 | *bal hum fī shakkin min **dhikrī*** | The text |
| 32 | *ʿan **dhikri** rabbī* | Solomon |
| 43 | *wa **dhikrā** li-uli'l-albāb* | The text |
| 46 | ***dhikra'**d-dār* | The text |
| 49 | *hādhā **dhikr*** | The text |
| 87 | *in huwa illā **dhikrun** li'l-ʿālamīn* | The text |

**What is to be recorded is this:** the same word is at once the name of the Book (1, 87), the subject of the objection (8), the thing a prophet says he turned away from (32), and the function of the accounts that are told (43, 46, 49).

**The forty-ninth verse is the knot of this chain:** when the thirty-two verses of the prophets are finished, the text says *hādhā dhikr* — "**this is a reminder**". What has been recounted, that is, is named after the recounting is over.

---

### A second countable strand: و-ه-ب

**The root occurs four times, and its distribution is worth noticing:**

| Verse | Expression | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | *raḥmati rabbika'l-ʿazīzi'**l-Wahhāb*** | The name is **asserted** — in the middle of the polemic |
| **30** | *wa **wahabnā** li-Dāwūda Sulaymān* | The name **at work** — a son |
| **35** | *wa **hab** lī mulkan… innaka anta'**l-Wahhāb*** | The name **called upon** — a prayer |
| **43** | *wa **wahabnā** lahū ahlahū* | The name **at work again** — a family |

**I record this as my own reading:** the attribute uttered in the middle of a dispute in the ninth verse (*al-Wahhāb* — the one who gives abundantly) is displayed over the next forty verses in **three separate events**. The sūra states the attribute first and then exemplifies it.

---

### Third strand: أ-و-ب and مَآب

**These two words come from the same root** (أ-و-ب: to return) and occur eight times in all in the sūra.

| Verse | Word | Who / what |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | *awwāb* | David |
| 19 | *awwāb* | The birds and the mountains (or all of them) |
| 24 | *anāb* | David (from the root ن-و-ب, the same picture) |
| 25 | *ḥusna **maʾāb*** | The place David will come to |
| 30 | *awwāb* | Solomon |
| 34 | *anāb* | Solomon |
| 40 | *ḥusna **maʾāb*** | The place Solomon will come to |
| 44 | *awwāb* | Job |
| 49 | *la-ḥusna **maʾāb*** | The God-fearing |
| 55 | *la-sharra **maʾāb*** | The transgressors |

**And there is a verifiable equivalence here:** the construct *ḥusna maʾāb* comes in exactly the same wording for David (25) and for Solomon (40); then in the forty-ninth verse it is repeated for **the whole body of the God-fearing**.

**What was given to two prophets, that is, opens out to a class in the forty-ninth verse.** And immediately afterwards the opposite is set down: *sharra maʾāb*.

***Maʾāb* means "the place one returns and arrives at".** The cast of the sūra is full of *awwāb*s (those who turn back often); the name of the place arrived at is from the same root. **The one who returns and the place returned to are named with one word.**

---

### Fourth strand: ع-ز-ز

**The root occurs five times, and all five are at a different work:**

| Verse | Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | *fī **ʿizzatin** wa shiqāq* | The state of those who disbelieve — **resistance** |
| 9 | *rabbika'**l-ʿAzīzi**'l-Wahhāb* | A name of God — **invincibility** |
| 23 | ***ʿazzanī** fi'l-khiṭāb* | The litigant's complaint — **to overbear in speech** |
| 66 | *al-**ʿAzīzu**'l-Ghaffār* | A name of God — **invincibility** |
| 82 | *fa-bi-**ʿizzatika** la-ughwiyannahum* | Iblīs's oath — **swearing by the might of the other party** |

**The occurrence in the verses of the eighties must be recorded in particular:** Iblīs swears **by the might** of the being he is defying. I shall take this up below in its place.

---

### Fifth strand: خ-ص-م — litigation

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | *nabaʾu'l-**khaṣm*** | The two litigants |
| 22 | ***khaṣmāni** baghā baʿḍunā ʿalā baʿḍ* | The two litigants |
| 64 | *ta**khāṣum**u ahli'n-nār* | Those in the Fire |
| 69 | *al-malaʾu'l-aʿlā idh **yakhtaṣimūn*** | The assembly on high |

**Four occurrences, three separate places:** in a miḥrāb on earth (21-22), in the Fire (64), and in the place where the angels are (69). **The same root, on three levels.**

**And the order is worth recording:** the sūra gives litigation first in an example that **is settled** (David issues a judgment), then in one that **is not settled** (in the Fire no one can convince anyone), and last of all in the example that stands **at the beginning** (the objection of Iblīs).

---

### Sixth strand: the same command, two different objects

**This is one of the finest placements in the sūra, and it rests entirely on the wording.**

| Verse | Who says it | Command | Steadfast in what |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | The leading men of Mecca | *wa'**ṣbirū*** | *ʿalā **ālihatikum*** — to your gods |
| **17** | God | *i**ṣbir*** | *ʿalā **mā yaqūlūn*** — to what they say |

**The same command, from the same root and in the same mood, eleven verses apart, in the mouths of two opposed sides.** The other side tells its own body: hold out. The text tells its envoy: endure.

**And in the forty-fourth verse the root comes a third time, this time as a verdict:** *innā wajadnāhu **ṣābirā*** — "we found him patient." **The command is given twice, that is, and once the outcome is announced.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the three words are from one root:** the sūra does not show patience as the monopoly of one side. **Holding out is something both sides can do; what divides them is what is held out for.**

---

### Seventh strand: هَٰذَا — the demonstrative changes hands

**The most frequently repeated word in the sūra is *hādhā* ("this"). And its distribution falls into two.**

**First the other side uses it — three times, all three dismissive:**

| Verse | Expression | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | *inna **hādhā** la-shayʾun ʿujāb* | "This is an astonishing thing" |
| 6 | *inna **hādhā** la-shayʾun yurād* | "There is a purpose behind this" |
| 7 | *in **hādhā** illā ikhtilāq* | "This is nothing but a fabrication" |

**Then the text takes it back — six times, from the forty-ninth verse onward:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| 49 | ***hādhā** dhikr* |
| 53 | ***hādhā** mā tūʿadūna li-yawmi'l-ḥisāb* |
| 54 | *inna **hādhā** la-rizqunā* |
| 55 | ***hādhā*** (and what follows: *wa inna li'l-ṭāghīna la-sharra maʾāb*) |
| 57 | ***hādhā** fa'l-yadhūqūh* |
| 59 | ***hādhā** fawjun muqtaḥimun maʿakum* |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the distribution of the word:** the side that says "this" at the beginning of the sūra and pushes it away with the back of its hand hears the same word **about itself** at the end. To the man who says *"this astonishing thing"* in the fifth verse, it is said in the fifty-seventh: *"here it is — taste it."*

**In the thirty-ninth verse the same word occurs once more, and it stands at the gentlest place in the sūra:** *hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā* — "this is our giving."

---

### The occasion of revelation and the historical frame

**On the sūra's being Meccan there is a state of near-unanimity.**

**For verses four to eight a report about the occasion of revelation is transmitted in the classical works of exegesis and ḥadīth:** the leading men of Quraysh come to Abū Ṭālib and complain about his nephew, they speak with the Prophet, and when they hear nothing from him but "*lā ilāha illā'llāh*" they rise from the gathering and leave, and say the words in the sixth verse.

**I give this report in the language of "it is transmitted", and I issue no ruling about its chain.** This much can be said: **the wording of the verses is self-sufficient enough for a reader who does not know the report to follow it.** The words *wa'nṭalaqa'l-malaʾu minhum* in the sixth verse ("their leading men rose and went off") describe a departure from a gathering; the report makes this concrete, but does not make it necessary.

The basmala was treated in earlier sections; the verses are counted without it.

## 38/1-3 — صٓ وَٱلْقُرْءَانِ ذِى ٱلذِّكْرِ · بَلِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ فِى عِزَّةٍ وَشِقَاقٍ · كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا مِن قَبْلِهِم مِّن قَرْنٍ فَنَادَوا۟ وَّلَاتَ حِينَ مَنَاصٍ

*Ṣād · Wa'l-Qurʾāni dhi'dh-dhikr · Bali'lladhīna kafarū fī ʿizzatin wa shiqāq · Kam ahlaknā min qablihim min qarnin fa-nādaw wa-lāta ḥīna manāṣ*

"Ṣād. **By the Qurʾān, possessor of the reminder.** No; those who disbelieve are in a **resistance** and a **rift**. How many generations We destroyed before them; they cried out, but **it was no longer the hour of escape.**"

### صٓ — the letter

**The disconnected letters were treated at length in `002-bakara.md` at 2/1, and in `050-kaf.md` at 50/1 by way of an opening that may be counted this sūra's sibling. I rely on what was recorded there and do not repeat it here.**

**The summary is this, and it holds here exactly: the meaning of these letters is not known with certainty.** One body of scholars refers the meaning to God (*tafwīḍ*); among the views that seek an explanation, the one that finds the most support from within the text is that the letters point to the **raw material** of the Qurʾān and so give the challenge its force.

**And the note entered in bold in the discussion of Qāf must be entered here too:** **no numerical reckonings, no abjad, no letter-counting are carried out** on these letters. That road is not taken in this commentary.

**For the letter ṣād there are guesses transmitted in the classical sources** — it is said to be the first letter of words such as *ṣādiqun* (one who speaks truly) or *ṣadaqa* (he spoke truly). **I convey these but adopt none of them,** because, as was recorded in the discussion of al-Baqara, the method is not systematic: applied to all the letter-groups alike, it does not yield consistent results.

**There is no certain knowledge. No meaning is manufactured for the letter in this commentary.**

**The only thing that can be verified from the text is the pattern recorded in the discussion of Qāf:** what comes after the letter is the Book, and here nothing at all intervenes.

> **صٓ** وَ**ٱلْقُرْءَانِ** ذِى ٱلذِّكْرِ

### ذِى ٱلذِّكْرِ — the attribute given to the Qurʾān here

**What is sworn by is the Qurʾān, and it is named with an attribute: *dhi'dh-dhikr*.**

**ذِكْر — root ذ-ك-ر.** To mention, to remember, to remind; and from there: **admonition, warning, that by which a people is spoken of — honour, name.**

**The construct has been understood in two ways, and the disagreement is real:**

| Reading | What it means | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **"Possessor of the reminder"** | The Qurʾān that contains admonition, that reminds | This is the commonest use of the word *dhikr* in the Qurʾān; verses 49 and 87 of this sūra also use the same word in this sense |
| **"Possessor of honour"** | The Qurʾān that wins its holder a name and a standing | In Arabic *dhikr* means "being spoken of, name"; the Qurʾān uses this openly: *"It is surely a **dhikr** for you and for your people"* (al-Zukhruf 43/44) |
| **"Reminder"** | Reminding of the earlier scriptures and of known truths | Consistent with the Qurʾān's naming of itself as *muṣaddiq* (confirming) |

**I do not impose a preference.** But a datum from within the sūra can be recorded: **the word occurs seven more times in the sūra** (tabulated above), and its last occurrence is *dhikrun li'l-ʿālamīn* (87) — where the sense is plainly "admonition". **The sūra supports the first sense from within itself.**

**The comparison with Qāf must be recorded:**

| Sūra | The attribute of the Qurʾān | What the attribute conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **Ṣād 38/1** | *dhi'dh-**dhikr*** | **Function** — what it does |
| **Qāf 50/1** | *al-**Majīd*** | **Quality** — what it is |

**In `050-kaf.md` the picture of "breadth and abundance" in the root of *majīd* was treated; I rely on that treatment.** The two sūras use the same oath pattern and choose two different attributes: one names the compass of the text, the other its work.

### Where is the answer to the oath? — the disagreement over the *jawāb al-qasam*

**In Arabic an oath (*qasam*) requires an answer (*jawāb al-qasam*): "…by such and such, it is so."** Here there is no explicit answer — exactly as in Qāf.

**The same question was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/1 and four views were tabulated there. I rely on that; here I give the table particular to Ṣād, because in Ṣād the spread of views is wider than in Qāf.**

| View | What the answer is | Ground | Its weak side |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The answer is elided (*maḥdhūf*)** | A sentence such as "…truly this Qurʾān is the truth" / "…truly you are among the envoys" is supplied | In Arabic, where the indication is clear, elision of the answer is common; the context requires it as well | The supplied wording is wholly conjecture; no two supply the same sentence |
| **The answer is verse 2** | *Bali'lladhīna kafarū fī ʿizzatin wa shiqāq* | It is held possible for the particle *bal* to come after an oath | It is not customary for a sentence conveying *iḍrāb* (a turning away) to be the answer to an oath |
| **The answer is in verse 3** | *Kam ahlaknā min qablihim min qarn* — "truly, how many generations We destroyed" | In meaning it is a declarative sentence suited to the oath | There is no *lām* or *inna* at the head of the answering clause |
| **The answer is verse 14** | *In kullun illā kadhdhaba'r-rusula fa-ḥaqqa ʿiqāb* | The sentence's *in… illā* pattern is a pattern used in answers to oaths | Twelve verses intervene |
| **The answer is verse 64** | *Inna dhālika la-ḥaqqun takhāṣumu ahli'n-nār* | It is reinforced with *inna* + *lām* — the typical form of an oath's answer | Its distance is found forced |
| **The answer is verse 88** | *Wa la-taʿlamunna nabaʾahū baʿda ḥīn* | **In wording this is the most fitting candidate:** *lām* + the *nūn* of emphasis is the most established form of an oath's answer in Arabic | It stands at the very end of the sūra; eighty-six verses intervene |

**In this disagreement I do not impose a preference.**

**But an observation like the one made in the discussion of Qāf can be made here too, and I record it as my own reading:** all six views look for the answer around **a confirmation** — either that the Qurʾān is true, or that the one who cries lies meets his recompense, or that the report will be known sooner or later. **The wording of the answer is disputed; its direction is not.**

**And the force of the last view in point of wording must be recorded:** if the answer is verse 88, then the whole of the sūra is taken inside a single oath. **This is a reading that makes the beginning and the end of the sūra one sentence** — it is not binding, but in terms of Arabic construction it is the option that strains least.

### بَل — the particle of turning

***Bal* (بل) is the particle of *iḍrāb*: leaving off the preceding sentence and passing to something else.** It was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/2; it was recorded there that it works in two ways — either it cancels what precedes, or it leaves what precedes and leaps to the real matter.

**Here it is the second, and its effect is the same as in Qāf:** an oath is sworn, no answer is given, and with *bal* the subject shifts at once to the state of the other side. **The text does not begin to defend its own claim; it makes the objection itself the subject.**

**And *bal* comes four times across the sūra:** 2 (*bali'lladhīna kafarū*), twice in 8 (*bal hum fī shakk*, *bal lammā yadhūqū ʿadhāb*), 60 (*bal antum lā marḥaban bikum*). **All four make a correction: they put the true name to what has been said.**

### عِزَّة — root ع-ز-ز

**The concrete sense of the root: hardness, firmness, resistance to wear.** The lexicographers say *al-arḍu'l-ʿazāz*: **hard, barren ground that a mattock will not enter.** All the derivatives of the word come out of this picture:

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **عَزَّ** (*ʿazza*) | It grew strong, it prevailed; it became rare and hard to come by |
| **عِزَّة** (*ʿizza*) | Invincibility, refusal to bow; and **proud resistance** |
| **عَزِيز** (*ʿazīz*) | The one not overcome; and the precious, the rarely found |
| **أَعَزَّ** (*aʿazza*) | He made mighty |
| **عَزَّ عَلَىٰ** | It came hard upon someone, it bore down on him |

**There is one thing at the centre of the root: *that a thing cannot be penetrated*.** A mattock does not enter hard ground; a hand cannot be laid on one who is *ʿazīz*.

**And the word works in two directions:**

| | **The side of praise** | **The side of blame** |
|---|---|---|
| In whom | God (*al-ʿAzīz*, 38/9, 66) | Those who disbelieve (*fī ʿizzatin*, 38/2) |
| What it means | Not to be overcome — **a fact** | Refusal to bow — **a claim** |
| Its source | From His own Essence | Supposed to come from oneself |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra's own distribution of the word:** the same root is used of the other side in the second verse and of God in the ninth — **seven verses apart.** Immediately after naming the other side's claim to might, that is, the text states where might actually lies.

**One further note:** the particle **فِى** in the construct *fī ʿizzatin* matters. It does not say "they are mighty"; it says **"they are *inside* a might"**. In Arabic *fī* conveys being inside a vessel. **Might is given here, that is, not as their attribute but as a state they have entered.** A posture, a position.

### شِقَاق — root ش-ق-ق

**The root was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/52, and what was recorded there was this:** *"the root ش-ق-ق: to split, to cleave in two… *Shiqāq* is the falling of two sides into **separate halves**."* **I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**What is to be added here is that the two words have been set side by side: *ʿizza* and *shiqāq*.**

| Word | What it conveys | Its direction |
|---|---|---|
| **عِزَّة** | Not to bow | **Inward** — holding one's own position |
| **شِقَاق** | To fall into the other half | **Outward** — widening the gap with the other side |

**I record this as my own reading:** the two together give the two faces of a single posture — **hardness and severance.** And this description explains every dialogue in the rest of the sūra: from the fourth verse on, what the other side says is always a derivative of these two words.

**In Fuṣṣilat the word *shiqāq* came with the adjective *baʿīd* (far)** — there the **distance** of the rift was being named. **Here there is no adjective; in its place the word is paired with another word.** The two sūras complete the same word with two different things.

### كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا — a multitude not put into numbers

**كَمْ** here is *khabariyya* — not interrogative but conveying **multitude**: "how many…" No number is given; a multitude is given.

**قَرْن — root ق-ر-ن:** to join, to set side by side. *Qarn* — **a body of people who lived at the same time, adjoining one another**; a generation. The picture at the root of the word is that people are **together in point of time.**

**When "how many generations" is said, then, what is named is not the name of a people but a stretch of time.** The sūra gives no names yet; the names will come in the twelfth verse.

### فَنَادَوا۟ — the cry

**Root ن-د-ي:** to call, to call out loudly. **The verb *nādaw* — they called, they cried out.**

**And its object is not given.** Whom they called, what they said, is not stated. **This is not an omission but a choice:** what is being told is not the content of the call but **that its hour had passed.**

### وَلَاتَ حِينَ مَنَاصٍ — one of the most striking constructions in the Qurʾān

**لَاتَ** is a rare particle in Arabic. It is the negative *lā* with a *tāʾ* added to it, and it is used **only with words denoting time**. Its function: **"it is no longer that hour"**.

**The grammarians differ over how this particle works, and although the disagreement is technical it must be recorded:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **It works like *laysa*** | Its subject is elided and its predicate is *ḥīna manāṣ*; the supplied reading: *wa-lāta'l-ḥīnu ḥīna manāṣin* |
| **It works like the *lā* of generic negation** | *Ḥīna* is in the accusative and is its subject; its predicate is elided |
| **It governs nothing and merely emphasises** | Some grammarians have said this |

**The meaning is the same on all three views, and I do not impose a preference.**

**مَنَاص — root ن-و-ص.** And the concrete sense of the root is worth recording: **to flee, to slip away and escape, to draw back.** *Nāṣa yanūṣu* — he fled, he withdrew. *Manāṣ* — a place of escape, a possibility of escape.

**The lexicographers record that the word was first used of an animal:** the startled beast's springing back and bolting. **There is a sudden shying and a movement of flight, that is, inside the word.**

**The picture the sentence builds is this:** there is crying out, there is no place to flee, **and the reason there is none is not place but time.** It does not say "there was no place to flee"; it says **"it was no longer the *hour* of escape."**

### What this says to the present

**Three verses set up a sequence, and the sequence announces the whole of the sūra in advance:**

| Verse | What is done |
|---|---|
| 1 | The text is set down — **what is in hand** |
| 2 | The state of the other side is diagnosed — **what is now** |
| 3 | The outcome of the same state in the past is shown — **what has been** |

**And what the third verse says is not a threat but a piece of information about *time*.** People crying out are described; they obtain nothing not because they objected but **because they were late.**

**I record this as my own inference:** what the verse dwells on is not remorse itself but **the timing of remorse.** It is said that the same words serve at one hour and do not serve at another. This must be read together with the phrase *baʿda ḥīn* ("after a time") at the end of the sūra: the sūra opens with a notion of **term** and closes with a notion of **term**.

## 38/4-5 — وَعَجِبُوٓا۟ أَن جَآءَهُم مُّنذِرٌ مِّنْهُمْ وَقَالَ ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ هَٰذَا سَٰحِرٌ كَذَّابٌ · أَجَعَلَ ٱلْءَالِهَةَ إِلَٰهًا وَٰحِدًا إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَشَىْءٌ عُجَابٌ

*Wa ʿajibū an jāʾahum mundhirun minhum wa qāla'l-kāfirūna hādhā sāḥirun kadhdhāb · A-jaʿala'l-ālihata ilāhan wāḥidā, inna hādhā la-shayʾun ʿujāb*

"**They were astonished that a warner from among themselves had come to them**, and the disbelievers said: 'This is a sorcerer, a great liar. **Has he made the gods one single god? This is truly an astonishing thing.**'"

### عَجِبُوا۟ — and the real matter here

**The root ع-ج-ب was treated in detail in `050-kaf.md` at 50/2. I rely on that and do not repeat the analysis of the root.**

**The finding there was this, and it explains the whole polemical part of this sūra:**

> ***"Astonishment has no evidential value, because astonishment is a measure not of knowledge but of familiarity.** A person is astonished at what he is not used to. And habit says nothing whatever about whether a thing is possible."*

**And the table drawn up in Qāf holds here exactly:** a proof rests on a premise and can be tested; astonishment rests on habit and contains nothing to test.

**Now I pass to what is particular to Ṣād, because Ṣād carries this diagnosis a step beyond Qāf.**

**In Qāf the object of astonishment was single: being raised after death.** In Ṣād the astonishment is directed at **three separate things in succession**, and all three are set out between the fourth and the seventh verses:

| Verse | What astonishes | The habit the astonishment rests on |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | That the warner is **from among themselves** | "An envoy cannot be someone familiar" |
| **5** | That the gods are **reduced to one** | "Many gods; it has been so since the forefathers" |
| **7** | That they have **not heard** such a thing before | "There is nothing we have not heard" |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of these three verses:** all three objections have the same structure — **something falls outside the customary, and is rejected for that reason.** In none of them is a proof advanced; in all three the measure is the hearer's own past experience.

### مِّنْهُمْ — the real knot of the objection

**In describing what astonishes them, the verse bears down on a single word: *minhum* — from among themselves.**

**The object of the astonishment, that is, is not that a warner came.** The idea of a warner was not strange to them; they knew the reports of the peoples that were destroyed. **What astonishes them is that the warner is *familiar*.**

**And in the Qurʾān this objection will turn into an explicit sentence in the eighth verse:** *a-unzila ʿalayhi'dh-dhikru min bayninā* — "has the reminder been sent down on him, **from among us**?"

**In `054-kamer.md` this objection was treated under the heading "the objection to the choice", and what was recorded there is worth noting:** the same sentence occurs in the mouth of Thamūd as well (al-Qamar 54/25), **and the wording is almost exactly the same.** The sūra, that is, makes its hearers listen to their own sentence in the mouth of an earlier people. **In `043-zuhruf.md` at 43/31 there is another form of the same objection:** *"Should this Qurʾān not have been sent down on some great man of the two cities?"*

**Read together, what the three places yield is this, and I give it as my own reading:** the objection is made not to the content of the message but to **its manner of distribution**. The other side takes the question of **to whom** revelation comes as a matter of status; the text takes it as a matter of **office**.

### سَٰحِرٌ كَذَّابٌ — two accusations, two patterns

**The accusation is built with two words, and the two words are on different patterns. The difference is meaningful:**

| Word | Pattern | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **سَاحِر** (*sāḥir*) | **فَاعِل** — active participle | The one who does the thing; **an attribute**, but conveying no degree |
| **كَذَّاب** (*kadhdhāb*) | **فَعَّال** — intensive | **One who lies much**; who has made the thing a habit |

**The second word is thus heavier than the first.** "Sorcerer" is said, but "liar" is not — what is said is **"one who has made lying his trade"**.

**سِحْر — root س-ح-ر.** The lexicographers dwell on its concrete sense: **to show a thing otherwise than it is, to turn the eye and the apprehension.** *Saḥar* — the moment of daybreak when dark and light are mingled; the place where two things run into one another.

**I record this as my own reading:** this side of the root shows why the accusation was chosen. **The other side does not deny that the speech is effective; it ascribes the effect to a confusion.** "This speech does something, but what it does is not real." This is a rejection that concedes the power of the speech.

**And the two accusations contradict one another:** a sorcerer is one who shows what he sees as something else; a liar is one who says what is not. **The one performs an operation on the real, the other does not use the real at all.** To say both of the same person in the same sentence is not a consistent diagnosis but **the piling of two separate grounds of rejection one on top of the other.**

**I give this as an observation; the verse does not itself stress the contradiction.**

### أَجَعَلَ ٱلْءَالِهَةَ إِلَٰهًا وَٰحِدًا — the form of the question

**The sentence is built as a question but expects no answer. In Arabic this is called *istifhām inkārī*: rejection in the form of a question.**

**And the construction must be recorded:** *al-āliha* (definite plural: **the known gods**) → *ilāhan wāḥidā* (indefinite singular: **one single god**).

| | Object | Grammar | What it calls up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Point of departure | ٱلْءَالِهَة | Definite, plural | **Settled, recognised, countable** |
| Point of arrival | إِلَٰهًا وَٰحِدًا | Indefinite, singular | **New, unrecognised, single** |

**I record this as my own reading:** the objection frames a language of loss. The other side sees *tawḥīd* as a **subtraction** — an operation that comes down from many to few. The whole answer of the text is that this is not a subtraction but a **correction**; and the sūra will give that answer with examples over ninety verses.

**And the choice of verb is striking as well: *jaʿala* — he made, he rendered.** The other side presents *tawḥīd*, that is, not as a **claim about the truth** but as **an act** of the envoy's: "he has reduced the gods to one." **As if the number of the gods were something somebody decides.**

### عُجَابٌ — the intensive form

**The word is on the *fuʿāl* pattern and, as was recorded in `050-kaf.md`, conveys intensity: not "astonishing" but "most astonishing".**

**In Qāf the other side said *shayʾun ʿajīb* (50/2); here it says *shayʾun ʿujāb*.** The same root, one degree higher.

**In `072-cin.md` these two occurrences were named together**, and the comparison drawn there should be recalled here too: the jinn hear the Qurʾān and say *"qurʾānan ʿajabā"* and believe; the Meccans use the same root and cry lies. **The word is the same, the verb that follows it different.**

**I record this because it is decisive for the method of the sūra:** astonishment is in itself neither faith nor denial. **Astonishment is a starting point; what it turns out to be is settled by what is done after it.**

## 38/6-8 — وَٱنطَلَقَ ٱلْمَلَأُ مِنْهُمْ أَنِ ٱمْشُوا۟ وَٱصْبِرُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ ءَالِهَتِكُمْ إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَشَىْءٌ يُرَادُ · مَا سَمِعْنَا بِهَٰذَا فِى ٱلْمِلَّةِ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ إِنْ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا ٱخْتِلَٰقٌ · أَءُنزِلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلذِّكْرُ مِنۢ بَيْنِنَا بَلْ هُمْ فِى شَكٍّ مِّن ذِكْرِى بَل لَّمَّا يَذُوقُوا۟ عَذَابِ

*Wa'nṭalaqa'l-malaʾu minhum ani'mshū wa'ṣbirū ʿalā ālihati-kum, inna hādhā la-shayʾun yurād · Mā samiʿnā bi-hādhā fi'l-millati'l-ākhira, in hādhā illā'khtilāq · A-unzila ʿalayhi'dh-dhikru min bayninā, bal hum fī shakkin min dhikrī, bal lammā yadhūqū ʿadhāb*

"Their leading men rose and went off: '**Walk on, hold fast to your gods; there is a purpose behind this.** We have not heard of this in the last religion; this is nothing but **a fabrication**. Has the reminder been sent down on him, from among us?' No, they are in doubt about My reminder. No, **they have not yet tasted My punishment.**"

### وَٱنطَلَقَ ٱلْمَلَأُ — the setting of the scene

**ٱنطَلَقَ — root ط-ل-ق.** Its concrete sense: **the loosing of a tie, being set free.** *Ṭalāq* is from the same root; *iṭlāq* — to let go. **Form VII (*infiʿāl*) here means "to set out, to rise and go".**

**The choice of verb builds a scene:** people **rise** from a place where they had been sitting, and speak as they disperse. This is the end of a meeting.

**ٱلْمَلَأ — root م-ل-أ:** to fill. *Malaʾ* — **the leading men who fill an assembly**; the company that fills the eye when it is seen. The picture at the root of the word is a place being filled by them.

**What must be recorded is who is speaking.** In the fourth and fifth verses the speaker was *al-kāfirūn* (general); **here the speaker is *al-malaʾ*, those in the seat of decision.**

**And what they do must be recorded: they are addressing their own body.** The one addressed is no longer the envoy but **their own base.** This is a change of stage in the dispute: talking to one another has been abandoned, and **an internal mobilisation** has begun.

### ٱمْشُوا۟ وَٱصْبِرُوا۟ — two commands

**Two commands come in succession, and both are ordinary words. Their ordinariness is meaningful.**

**ٱمْشُوا۟ — root م-ش-ي:** to walk. The imperative: "walk on".

**The exegetes have understood this command in two ways:**

| Reading | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | "Get up, leave this place" — a call to quit the meeting |
| **Figurative** | "Keep on your road, do not swerve from the road you have taken" — a call to persist |

**The two do not exclude one another** and both uses of the word are common in Arabic. I do not impose a preference.

**وَٱصْبِرُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ ءَالِهَتِكُمْ — and the most notable verbal equivalence in the sūra begins here.**

**It was tabulated above in the section on structure; here I open it out.** The same command, from the same root and in the same mood, will come from the other side eleven verses later:

| Verse | Speaker | Wording | Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | The leading men of Mecca | **وَٱصْبِرُوا۟** | *ʿalā **ālihatikum*** |
| **17** | God | **ٱصْبِرْ** | *ʿalā **mā yaqūlūn*** |

**This is a datum that can be verified from the text.** What is interpretation is the conclusion to be drawn from it; that I give as my own reading:

**Patience is given in the sūra as a strength both sides possess.** Endurance is nobody's monopoly. What divides them is not the act itself but **what the act is attached to**: one leans on its idols, the other on words.

**And the grammar supports this:** in both sentences the verb comes with the same preposition — **عَلَىٰ**. In Arabic *ṣabara ʿalā* means "to stand upon a thing, to bear with it". Both, that is, are standing **upon** something; the difference is the ground beneath them.

### إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَشَىْءٌ يُرَادُ — "there is a purpose behind this"

**This sentence is one of the places in the sūra that must be handled with the greatest care.**

**يُرَادُ — root ر-و-د.** Its concrete sense: **to go back and forth after a thing, to seek.** *Rāʾid* — the scout who seeks pasture and water for the tribe. *Irāda* — to want a thing and to turn towards it. **The verb here is passive (*yurād*): "it is wanted, it is intended" — but the one who wants is not named.**

**The gap left by the passive produces the whole effect of the sentence.** Who wants it? It is not stated. **The nearest English rendering: "there is something behind this."**

**I record step by step how the sentence works:**

| Step | Expression | Its function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *inna hādhā* | It reduces the subject to a "thing" — it does not name the content |
| 2 | *la-shayʾun* | It leaves it indefinite — what it is, is not said |
| 3 | *yurād* | It hides the agent — who wants it, is not said |

**All three steps produce indefiniteness. And it is precisely through this indefiniteness that the sentence becomes undiscussable.**

**I record this as my own reading:** this is not a claim but an **insinuation**. Were it claimed, it could be tested — had it been said "he wants such and such, because such and such is the evidence", the evidence would be discussed. **When it is insinuated, nothing is left to test.** Whatever the one addressed says, the sentence stands: what he says can itself be counted part of "the thing that is wanted".

**And this belongs to the same family as the diagnosis of *ʿajab* in the fourth verse:**

| | *ʿAjab* (4-5) | *Yurād* (6) |
|---|---|---|
| What it rests on | On habit | On the assumption of an intent |
| Can it be tested | No | No |
| What it does | It closes the subject | It makes the subject **undiscussable** |

**No side is taken in present-day politics in this commentary, and none is taken here.** But what the verse describes is a **form of speech**, and forms of speech are not bounded by a time. **The note is this, and it concerns the attribute, not persons:** to reject a view without looking at its content, by saying that a hidden purpose lies behind it, is exactly the posture the verse reports. **Whoever does this is included in the attribute** — at the beginning of the sūra as at its end, the verdict is attached to attributes and not to persons.

**And that the verse records this as a *defect* is clear from where the sentence is placed:** all four objections between four and seven are given in the same section, in the same order, and with no evidence accompanying any of them.

### مَا سَمِعْنَا بِهَٰذَا فِى ٱلْمِلَّةِ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ — not having heard it

**مِلَّة — root م-ل-ل.** On the root of the word the lexicographers make a connection with *imlāʾ* (to dictate): *milla*, **a dictated road that is followed; the religion a community has adopted.**

**ٱلْءَاخِرَة — "last", "final".** And the construct is disputed:

| View | What *al-millatu'l-ākhira* means | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **The religion of their own age** | "There is no such thing in the religion we hold today" | *Ākhir* = the latest, the most recent; that is, what they inherited from their fathers |
| **Christianity** | As the last of the known religions | It was the most recent revealed religion known in that region |
| **The religion/belief concerning the hereafter** | That they meant belief in the resurrection | Held weak; the wording does not admit of it |

**I do not impose a preference.** But the **structure** of the objection is the same on all three views, and that is the real matter: **"we have not heard this" is advanced as evidence.**

**I record this as my own reading:** this is the *ʿajab* of the fourth verse cast into the form of knowledge. Astonishment says "I am not used to it"; this sentence says the same thing as "it is not in our records". **In both, the measure is the speaker's own store.**

### ٱخْتِلَٰق — fabrication, and the two branches of the root

**Root: خ-ل-ق.** And this is the most instructive point of vocabulary in the sūra — because **the same root will be used for "to create" in the seventy-first verse** (*innī khāliqun basharan min ṭīn*).

**In `068-kalem.md` at 68/4 the *khalq* (outward form) and *khuluq* (character, inward form) branches of this root were treated; in `059-hasr.md` and `082-infitar.md` the concrete sense of the root was given. I rely on that and do not repeat it here.** The summary was this: **the original sense of the root is to cut and shape to a measure** — *khalaqa'l-adīma*, he measured and cut the hide before cutting it out.

**Now the third branch, which must be added here:**

**The lexicographers record that a second use came out of the root's sense of "cutting and shaping": اِخْتَلَقَ (*ikhtalaqa*) — to *fabricate* a saying, to construct and put forward something with no basis.** *Ikhtilāq* — a fabrication. From the same root *khalq* — both creation and (in some uses) **a made-up saying.**

**The relation between the two branches, as the lexicographers record it, is this:**

| Use | Agent | Material | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **خَلَقَ** (*khalaqa*) — to create | God | Matter brought into being | **A real thing** |
| **ٱخْتَلَقَ** (*ikhtalaqa*) — to fabricate | Man | Something non-existent | **A thing that is not real** |

**The common point:** in both, something is put forward **by cutting and shaping**; in both there is a **work of making**. **The point of divergence:** in the one, what comes out really exists; in the other it exists only in the saying.

**The lexicographers record this second branch from the usage of Arabic itself**, and the Qurʾān uses both branches: in the verse *"…and you fabricate a falsehood"* (al-ʿAnkabūt 29/17 — *wa takhluqūna ifkā*) the same root carries the sense "to fabricate".

**Now I come to the link within the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:**

**In the seventh verse the other side calls the envoy *mukhtaliq* (a fabricator). In the seventy-first verse God names Himself *khāliq* (creator).** The two words are from one root.

**And the sūra puts thirty-two verses of prophets between these two words** — that is, the content of the thing called "fabrication". **The accusation first, the material after.**

**I record this as an observation about a pattern; I am not claiming that the sūra sets up a deliberate play on words.** What I record is that the same root has been used in the sūra for two opposed works.

### بَلْ هُمْ فِى شَكٍّ مِّن ذِكْرِى — the correction of the diagnosis

**And in the second half of the eighth verse the text takes the floor. Two *bal*s come one on top of the other:**

| Expression | What it corrects |
|---|---|
| ***Bal** hum fī shakkin min dhikrī* | The matter is not who the envoy is — it is **doubt about the text itself** |
| ***Bal** lammā yadhūqū ʿadhāb* | Nor is the matter doubt — **they have not yet tasted anything** |

**The first correction changes the subject of the objection.** The other side had asked "why him?"; the answer says the question is really "is this it?" **The dispute about the envoy, that is, is diagnosed as a cover for the dispute about the text.**

**The second correction goes further still, and must be read with care.**

**لَمَّا** — in Arabic this differs from *lam*: *lam* says "it did not happen", while **لَمَّا** says **"it has not happened yet, but it is expected"**. The grammarians record the difference explicitly: in *lammā* there is a **continuation and an expectation.**

**The sentence, that is, does not say "they did not taste the punishment"; it says "they have not yet tasted it."** The time-note is inside it.

**And the verb *yadhūqū* — root ذ-و-ق: to taste.** The concrete sense of the word is tasting with the tongue; **that is, the most immediate and least disputable form of knowledge.** Sight can be deceived, hearing can err; taste is direct.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two words were chosen together:** the sentence traces the source of the doubt to **a lack of experience.** Astonishment (4), insinuation (6), not having heard (7) and doubt (8) — all four come out at the same place: **the other side does not yet know the thing it is passing judgment on.**

**And this joins with the last verse of the sūra:** *wa la-taʿlamunna nabaʾahū baʿda ḥīn* — "you will surely know its report **after a time**." **What is called "not yet" in the eighth verse becomes "after a time" in the eighty-eighth.**

**عَذَابِ** — the *yāʾ* at the end of the word has dropped (*ʿadhābī*, not *ʿadhāb*). **The same dropping occurs in the fourteenth verse too: *ʿiqāb*.** In `050-kaf.md` it was recorded that this dropping occurs twice in Qāf (*waʿīd*); **in Ṣād too it occurs twice, and both times at the end of a verse.** This is a conformity to the verse-ending: the word has been set into the sound pattern of the sūra.

## 38/9-11 — أَمْ عِندَهُمْ خَزَآئِنُ رَحْمَةِ رَبِّكَ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْوَهَّابِ · أَمْ لَهُم مُّلْكُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا فَلْيَرْتَقُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَسْبَٰبِ · جُندٌ مَّا هُنَالِكَ مَهْزُومٌ مِّنَ ٱلْأَحْزَابِ

*Am ʿindahum khazāʾinu raḥmati rabbika'l-ʿazīzi'l-Wahhāb · Am lahum mulku's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa mā baynahumā, fa'l-yartaqū fi'l-asbāb · Jundun mā hunālika mahzūmun mina'l-aḥzāb*

"**Or are the treasuries of the mercy of your Lord — the mighty, the great giver — with them?** Or is the dominion of the heavens and the earth and what is between them theirs? **Then let them climb up the ropes!** They are a ragtag army, routed there."

### أَمْ — the question handed back

***Am* in Arabic means "or", and it attaches to a preceding question.** Here it attaches to the question in the eighth verse: *a-unzila ʿalayhi'dh-dhikru min bayninā* — "has the reminder been sent down on him, from among us?"

**And the answer hands the question back to the other side.** Thus:

| Step | Who | What is said |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | They | "Why was he chosen?" |
| **9** | The text | "With whom does the power to choose lie? **Are the treasuries with you?**" |
| **10** | The text | "Is the dominion yours? **Then climb up.**" |

**I record this as my own reading:** the objection had been made to a decision about distribution. The answer asks **whose** the distribution is. The dispute, that is, is turned from "is the choice right?" to "with whom does the power of choosing lie?"

**This is another form of the diagnosis recorded in `040-gafir.md` at 40/56:** it was said there that beneath the dispute lay *kibr*, and that this pride was a thing **not to be reached** (*mā hum bi-bālighīh*). **Here there is the same structure: a claim to authority, and the showing that the authority is not in fact in their hands.**

### خَزَآئِن — the treasuries

**Root خ-ز-ن:** to conceal a thing, to store and guard it. *Khāzin* — the one who holds the treasury, its keeper. *Khizāna* — the place where things are kept.

**And note the construct: *khazāʾinu raḥmati rabbik* — "the treasuries of the mercy of your Lord".** Not the treasury of wealth, but of mercy.

**I record this because this is what is under dispute:** prophethood, in the language of the verse, is not a fortune or an office but is counted in the ledger of **mercy**. The other side was speaking of it as a status; the text names it as a gift.

### ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْوَهَّابِ — two names, two works

**Two names come in succession, and both matter in this sūra.**

**ٱلْعَزِيز — root ع-ز-ز.** It was treated at the second verse: hard, impenetrable, not to be overcome. **It is the answer, seven verses later, to the *ʿizza* of the other side (2).**

**ٱلْوَهَّاب — root و-ه-ب:** to give without return. **Pattern: فَعَّال — intensity and continuity.** *Hiba* is from the same root: what is given without expecting a return.

**This is the most distinctive side of the root, and the lexicographers record it plainly:** *wahb* is giving **without a price**. Not a sale, not a reward, not a loan — **without return.**

**The two names standing side by side set up a balance:**

| Name | What it conveys | Were it left alone |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلْعَزِيز** | No one can wrest anything from Him | A picture of unreachable power |
| **ٱلْوَهَّاب** | He gives without return | A picture of measureless abundance |

**I record this as my own reading:** the two together sum up the whole logic of the sūra. **A thing that cannot be seized is a thing that can be given.** The other side supposes prophethood to be something to be "wrested"; the verse says it is something "given".

**And a sequence of the same kind was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/3** — there five attributes came in succession and mercy and punishment were given in one breath. **Here the structure is a pair, but its function is the same: the naming of two directions together.**

**And this name will recur in the sūra: in the thirty-fifth verse Solomon will call on it in his prayer — *innaka anta'l-Wahhāb*.** **A name that occurs in a sentence of polemic, that is, comes back twenty-six verses later in the prayer of a prophet.** It was tabulated above.

### فَلْيَرْتَقُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَسْبَٰبِ — "let them climb up the ropes"

**The sentence is in the imperative but is not a command. In Arabic this is called *taʿjīz*: showing the limit of the other side's power by commanding what cannot be done.**

**ٱرْتَقَىٰ — root ر-ق-ي:** to rise, to climb. *Ruqyā* — a ladder. **Form VIII (*iftiʿāl*) conveys that the act is done by the agent's own effort:** to go up by one's own exertion.

**أَسْبَاب — its singular is *sabab*.** And the concrete sense of the root is very clear: **سَبَب — a rope, a cable; especially a rope used to reach somewhere.** The rope let down into a well, the rope tied to a tent.

**From here the abstract sense arose: *sabab* — the means that brings one to a thing.** The word passed into Turkish as *sebep*, "cause", and in Turkish the rope behind it has been forgotten.

**Now the picture of the sentence comes out:** "If the dominion is yours, **take hold of the ropes and climb.**"

**And this uses exactly the same word as the scene of Pharaoh treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/36-37:**

| Verse | Who | Expression | What is wanted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ghāfir 40/36** | Pharaoh | *laʿallī ablughu'**l-asbāb*** | To have a tower built and reach the ways of the heavens |
| **Ṣād 38/10** | The leading men of Mecca | *fa'l-yartaqū fi'**l-asbāb*** | (They are told they cannot do it) |

**This is a shared wording that can be verified.** And the conclusion recorded in the discussion of Ghāfir holds here too: **a claim to authority is always told through an attempt to ascend, and it is always unreached.**

### جُندٌ مَّا هُنَالِكَ مَهْزُومٌ — a belittling construction

**Every word of the sentence carries a belittlement, and its construction is striking in Arabic.**

**جُنْد — root ج-ن-د:** a body gathered and brought together; an army. The word is **indefinite (with tanwīn)** — "an army", any army.

**مَّا** — here, on the view of the majority of grammarians, it is **زَائِدَة**, that is, a particle that has come for emphasis, and it conveys **belittlement (*taḥqīr*)**. The nearest English rendering: **"what they call an army", "that broken-down army over there".**

**هُنَالِكَ** — "there". A far demonstrative. **Not a near place but a distant one is being pointed at.**

**مَهْزُوم — root ه-ز-م.** And the concrete sense of the root must be recorded: **to break a thing, to make it cave in; the collapse of something hollow.** The lexicographers say *hazama'l-siqāʾa* — **he caved in the waterskin (because it had emptied).** The sound of a dry branch snapping is also called *hazīm*.

**To be defeated, that is, is in Arabic a *caving-in*** — losing one's shape not under a blow from outside but **because one has emptied inside.**

**ٱلْأَحْزَاب — its singular *ḥizb*: a separated part of a body of people, a faction, a party.** The word will come again in this sūra at the thirteenth verse: *ulāʾika'l-aḥzāb* — "those are the factions."

**Now the construction is complete, and the time-note matters: *mahzūm* is a passive participle — that is, not "will be routed" but "routed".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pattern of the word:** the sentence gives no news of the future; it makes a **statement of state**. The army is counted beaten before it has fought — because the picture in the root *hazm* is not a blow from outside but **the collapse of what is hollow.**

**And the eleventh verse builds a bridge to the twelfth:** *mina'l-aḥzāb* — "of the factions". Which factions? **The next verse will count them.** The sūra, that is, uses the word first and gives its list afterwards.

### What this says to the present

**The three verses from nine to eleven do a single work: they ask whether a claim has anything behind it.**

**And the form of the question is worth recording.** The verse does not say "you are mistaken"; it asks **"what have you got?"** Are there treasuries, is there dominion, will you be able to climb?

**I record this as my own inference:** this is a methodical answer to the objections between the fourth and eighth verses. **The other side had framed sentences that cannot be tested** (astonishment, insinuation, "we have not heard"). **The answer asks a question that can be tested:** with whom does the authority in fact lie?

**And this distinction — between the untestable sentence and the testable question — will go on working through the rest of the sūra.** In the twenty-second verse two litigants will lay an event before a judge and ask for a measure; those quarrelling in the Fire (59-64), on the other hand, will trade untestable accusations and reach no conclusion at all.

## 38/12-14 — كَذَّبَتْ قَبْلَهُمْ قَوْمُ نُوحٍ وَعَادٌ وَفِرْعَوْنُ ذُو ٱلْأَوْتَادِ · وَثَمُودُ وَقَوْمُ لُوطٍ وَأَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْأَيْكَةِ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلْأَحْزَابُ · إِن كُلٌّ إِلَّا كَذَّبَ ٱلرُّسُلَ فَحَقَّ عِقَابِ

*Kadhdhabat qablahum qawmu Nūḥin wa ʿĀdun wa Firʿawnu dhu'l-awtād · Wa Thamūdu wa qawmu Lūṭin wa aṣḥābu'l-ayka, ulāʾika'l-aḥzāb · In kullun illā kadhdhaba'r-rusula fa-ḥaqqa ʿiqāb*

"Before them the people of Noah (Nūḥ) cried lies, and ʿĀd, and **Pharaoh, lord of the stakes**; and Thamūd, and the people of Lot (Lūṭ), and **the people of al-Ayka**. **Those are the factions.** Every one of them did nothing but cry lies to the envoys; **and My punishment fell due.**"

### The form of the list

**Six peoples are counted, divided across two verses.** And a similar list was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/12-14 — there were **eight** peoples there. **Comparing the two lists shows that the selection is not random.**

| | **Qāf 50/12-14** | **Ṣād 38/12-13** |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Eight | Six |
| Those in common | Noah, al-Rass, Thamūd, ʿĀd, Pharaoh, Lot, al-Ayka, Tubbaʿ | Noah, ʿĀd, Pharaoh, Thamūd, Lot, al-Ayka |
| Absent in Ṣād | The people of al-Rass, the people of Tubbaʿ | — |
| The close | *fa-ḥaqqa **waʿīd*** (the threat came true) | *fa-ḥaqqa **ʿiqāb*** (the punishment came true) |

**The difference between the two closes must be recorded, and it is fine:**

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **وَعِيد** (Qāf) | و-ع-د | **What was promised** — as yet only in words |
| **عِقَاب** (Ṣād) | ع-ق-ب | **The requital that follows after** — what has actually come |

**The concrete sense of the root ع-ق-ب was treated in `047-muhammed.md`:** the heel, that which comes after. *ʿĀqiba* — what comes behind a matter. **That is, *ʿiqāb* stresses the punishment's quality of "coming from behind".**

**I record this as my own reading:** Qāf ends its list with **the fulfilment of a threat**, Ṣād ends the same list with **the arrival of a requital**. The two name the same event from two different sides: the one, the keeping of a word; the other, the outcome of a deed.

**And the *yāʾ* at the end of the word *ʿiqāb* has dropped here too** (*ʿiqābī*, not *ʿiqāb*) — like *ʿadhāb* in the eighth verse. **Both are for conformity to the verse-ending.**

### فِرْعَوْنُ ذُو ٱلْأَوْتَادِ — lord of the stakes

**This construct was treated in detail in `089-fecr.md` at 89/10, and a table of four views was given there. I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**The summary of the analysis of the root there:** **و-ت-د** — *watid/watad*: a stake, a nail, a post driven into the ground; its plural is *awtād*. **The work of a stake is one thing only: to hold something in place.** A stake is not driven for its own sake; it is driven so that something else will not move.

**And in the discussion of al-Fajr this too was recorded:** in al-Nabaʾ 78/7 the same word is used of the mountains (*wa'l-jibāla awtādā*) — that is, **the idea of fixing is not in itself bad;** what is bad is the confusion about whose the authority to fix is.

**I convey the table of the disagreement from al-Fajr in summary; the detail is there:**

| View | What it means | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **Soldiers, armies** | The power that holds up a reign — like the stakes that hold a tent | The metaphor of "stakes" for support in Arabic; the word's coming in the plural |
| **A firm, settled reign** | A rule whose roots are driven into the ground, unshakeable | The stake's real function is to fix; the metaphor is direct |
| **Buildings, monuments** | Edifices built to last | The monumental architecture of Egypt |
| **An instrument of torture** | The stakes on which he stretched people | Some exegetes have understood it so; I do not go into the details |

**The note in the discussion of al-Fajr holds here too: I do not impose a firm preference, and the first three views come out at the same place** — a power thought to be fixed and unshakeable.

**What is particular to Ṣād is where the construct stands, and this too was named in the discussion of al-Fajr:**

| | **al-Fajr 89/6-10** | **Ṣād 38/12-13** |
|---|---|---|
| Length of the list | Three (ʿĀd, Thamūd, Pharaoh) | Six |
| Pharaoh's place | **The end of the list** — a descent from crowds to a single man | **The middle of the list** — inside the enumeration |
| The function of the epithet | To mark the summit | To make recognition possible |

**In the discussion of al-Fajr the following was recorded for this construct, and it matters:** *"the expression 'lord of the stakes' is **a settled designation** the Qurʾān uses for Pharaoh; it is not an invention peculiar to al-Fajr. This shows that the expression was something the hearers understood."* **The usage in Ṣād is the ground of that finding.**

### أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْأَيْكَةِ — the people of al-Ayka

**أَيْكَة — dense woodland, thicket, grove.** This people was named in `050-kaf.md` at 50/14, and as was recorded there the Qurʾān mentions them in several places (al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/176; al-Ḥijr 15/78) and **associates them with the peoples to whom Shuʿayb was sent.**

**I do not go into the detail:** whether the people of al-Ayka and the people of Midian are one people or two has been disputed in the classical sources, and **no preference is adopted in this commentary.**

**What is to be recorded is this:** five of the six peoples on the list are named by a prophet's name or by a people's name; **the people of al-Ayka, however, by the name of a *place*** — by a wood. The list, that is, varies the modes of recognition: some by their leader (Noah, Lot), some by their lineage (ʿĀd, Thamūd), some by their ruler (Pharaoh), some by their country (al-Ayka).

### أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلْأَحْزَابُ — the return of the word

**In the eleventh verse *mina'l-aḥzāb* ("of the factions") had been said, and which factions was not stated. The thirteenth verse gives the answer: *ulāʾika'l-aḥzāb* — "those are the factions."**

**The distance between the two verses is two verses, and the link is verbal; it can be verified.**

**I record this as my own reading:** in the eleventh verse the present opponents had been named "a routed army". **The thirteenth verse says who that army is by counting from the past.** The present group, that is, is placed as the continuation of the groups of the past — not as an accusation but **as a classification.**

### إِن كُلٌّ إِلَّا كَذَّبَ ٱلرُّسُلَ — a plural that comes down to a singular

**The construction of the sentence is striking.**

**إِن… إِلَّا** — a strong pattern of restriction in Arabic: "nothing other than…". In English it is carried by "nothing but", "only".

**And the object is plural: *al-rusul* — the envoys.** Yet each people had had one envoy come to it.

**The exegetes explain this plural in two ways:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **A collective view** | The six peoples are named collectively; taken all together, the envoys who were cried lies to are many |
| **To cry lies to one envoy is to cry lies to them all** | What the envoys brought is one in its principle; whoever rejects one has rejected the principle |

**The second view finds support in other verses of the Qurʾān** — as in the saying of the people of Noah that *"they cried lies to the envoys"* (al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/105); yet the envoy who came to them was one. **I do not impose a preference, but the second explanation accords better with the usage elsewhere in the text.**

## 38/15-16 — وَمَا يَنظُرُ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ إِلَّا صَيْحَةً وَٰحِدَةً مَّا لَهَا مِن فَوَاقٍ · وَقَالُوا۟ رَبَّنَا عَجِّل لَّنَا قِطَّنَا قَبْلَ يَوْمِ ٱلْحِسَابِ

*Wa mā yanẓuru hāʾulāʾi illā ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan mā lahā min fawāq · Wa qālū rabbanā ʿajjil lanā qiṭṭanā qabla yawmi'l-ḥisāb*

"These too await nothing but **a single cry**; **and it has no pause.** And they said: '**Our Lord! Hasten our portion to us before the Day of Reckoning.**'"

### مَا يَنظُرُ — "they do not await"

**Root ن-ظ-ر:** to look; and **to wait.** The two senses come from one root: to look at a thing is to watch for its coming.

**The verb here has the sense "to wait", and the pattern contains a restriction:** *mā yanẓuru… illā* — "they await nothing but…"

**And the point here is this:** they are not in fact waiting for anything; **their not waiting comes out at the same place as their waiting for one single thing to come.** In Arabic this is an ironic usage: to say of a person who takes no notice of a thing, "he awaits only this."

### صَيْحَة — a single cry

**Root ص-ي-ح:** to raise the voice, to shout. *Ṣayḥa* — a loud sound uttered once. **And the word is reinforced with an adjective of oneness: *wāḥida*.**

**The same word was used of the Resurrection in `050-kaf.md` at 50/42** (*yawma yasmaʿūna'ṣ-ṣayḥata bi'l-ḥaqq*), and was treated there.

**What is added here is the qualifying clause that follows.**

### مَّا لَهَا مِن فَوَاقٍ — "it has no pause"

**فَوَاق — root ف-و-ق.** And the concrete sense of the word is very sharp and very local:

**The lexicographers describe *fawāq* thus: the interval allowed while a she-camel is being milked, so that her udder may gather milk a second time.** The milker draws a little, leaves off, waits for the milk to come down, then draws again. **The name of that interval of waiting is *fawāq*.**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **فَوَاق / فُوَاق** | The interval between two milkings; a short respite |
| **أَفَاقَ** (*afāqa*) | He came to, he recovered his senses — **consciousness returned** |
| **إِفَاقَة** | Coming to, recovery; coming to oneself after illness |
| **فَوْق** | Above — the same root, from the branch of "coming back / rising" |

**At the centre of the root is this: a thing's coming back, its returning to itself.** The milk comes back to the udder; consciousness comes back to the person.

**Now what the verse says becomes clear: *mā lahā min fawāq* — this cry has no "interval of return".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the lexicographers' description of the root:** the verse does not say "this cry is very violent"; it says **"it has no interval."** It will not break off and begin again, and it will leave no gap in which one might come to oneself.

**And the fifteenth verse must be read together with the third:** there it was said *wa-lāta ḥīna manāṣ* — "it was no longer the hour of escape". **Both announce the absence of a *time*:** the one, that there is no time to flee; the other, that there is no interval in which to come to.

**Some of the exegetes have also understood the word as "return, repetition"** — that is, "there is no turning it back". **Both readings come out of the same root and do not exclude one another.**

### عَجِّل لَّنَا قِطَّنَا — "hasten our portion"

**قِطّ — root ق-ط-ط.** Its concrete sense: **to cut crosswise, to slice.** *Qiṭṭ* — a cut piece; and from there: **a written document, the slip of paper on which a person's allotted share is written, a deed.**

**The word has two branches of meaning, and the exegetes differ:**

| View | What *qiṭṭ* means | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **The record of deeds / a written document** | The page on which a person's own record is written | "Give us our record at once, let us not wait for the Day of Reckoning" — a mocking demand |
| **A portion, a share** | The lot allotted to a person | "Give us at once the punishment that falls to our share" — a challenge |
| **A reward** | The recompense to be given | From the same root's sense of "the document given" |

**I do not impose a preference.** On all three readings the **posture** of the sentence is the same: **mockery and haste.**

**عَجِّل — root ع-ج-ل:** to hurry. **Form II: to bring a thing forward, to hasten it.** **That the verb comes in the form of a prayer must be recorded:** they call out *rabbanā* and then say *ʿajjil*. A mockery is being framed, that is, **in the shape of a prayer.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse shows a stage of denial. **The other side is no longer arguing — it is jeering.** And jeering is the point the objections between the fourth and eighth verses arrive at: astonishment (4), insinuation (6), "we have not heard" (7), doubt (8), and finally **mockery (16)**.

**And there are other examples of this posture in the Qurʾān:** *"If this is the truth from You, then rain down stones on us from heaven"* (al-Anfāl 8/32). **The same structure: asking for a threat to be carried out in order to show that one does not take it seriously.**

### يَوْمِ ٱلْحِسَابِ — the day the sūra names for the first time

**This root will occur four times in the sūra: as the construct at 16, 26, 53; and at 39 as *bi-ghayri ḥisāb*.** And its distribution is worth recording:

| Verse | Expression | For whom |
|---|---|---|
| **16** | *qabla yawmi'l-ḥisāb* | The mockers — they want the day **brought forward** |
| **26** | *bimā nasū yawma'l-ḥisāb* | Those who go astray — they have **forgotten** the day |
| **39** | *fa'mnun aw amsik **bi-ghayri ḥisāb*** | Solomon — an authority to give **without reckoning** |
| **53** | *hādhā mā tūʿadūna li-yawmi'l-ḥisāb* | The God-fearing — the day is **the address of the promise** |

**I record this as an observation:** the same word is used at four different works — hastened, forgotten, lifted and awaited. **And the construct *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* ("without reckoning") in the thirty-ninth verse is the word's only positive use in the sūra.**

## 38/17-20 — ٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ وَٱذْكُرْ عَبْدَنَا دَاوُۥدَ ذَا ٱلْأَيْدِ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ · إِنَّا سَخَّرْنَا ٱلْجِبَالَ مَعَهُۥ يُسَبِّحْنَ بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِشْرَاقِ · وَٱلطَّيْرَ مَحْشُورَةً كُلٌّ لَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ · وَشَدَدْنَا مُلْكَهُۥ وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُ ٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَفَصْلَ ٱلْخِطَابِ

*Iṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūna wa'dhkur ʿabdanā Dāwūda dha'l-ayd, innahū awwāb · Innā sakhkharna'l-jibāla maʿahū yusabbiḥna bi'l-ʿashiyyi wa'l-ishrāq · Wa'ṭ-ṭayra maḥshūra, kullun lahū awwāb · Wa shadadnā mulkahū wa ātaynāhu'l-ḥikmata wa faṣla'l-khiṭāb*

"**Endure what they say**, and remember **our servant David, possessor of strength**; he was one who turned back often. We made the mountains subservient with him: they gave praise **at evening and at the rising of the sun**. **And the birds, gathered** — all of them turning back to him. **We made his kingdom firm, and gave him wisdom and the parting of speech.**"

### ٱصْبِرْ — and the answer to the command eleven verses earlier

**It was tabulated above; here I complete it.** In the sixth verse the leading men of Mecca had said to their own body *wa'ṣbirū ʿalā ālihatikum*. **Here the same root, the same preposition, a different object: *iṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn*.**

**And the difference in the object is worth recording as my own reading:**

| | Verse 6 | Verse 17 |
|---|---|---|
| Steadfast in what | *ālihatakum* — **objects** | *mā yaqūlūn* — **words** |
| The content of the patience | Not to let a thing go | To bear with a thing |
| Its outcome | (The sūra said this in verse 11: rout) | (The sūra will say this in verse 44: *niʿma'l-ʿabd*) |

**The same command occurred in the same wording in `050-kaf.md` at 50/39:** *fa'ṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn*. **The two sūras give the same command to the envoy in the same words.** This is a repetition that can be verified.

### وَٱذْكُرْ — the verb that opens the accounts

**And now the longest section of the sūra begins. Its structure rests on repetition:**

| Verse | Command | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **17** | *wa'**dhkur** ʿabdanā* | David |
| **41** | *wa'**dhkur** ʿabdanā* | Job |
| **45** | *wa'**dhkur** ʿibādanā* | Abraham, Isaac, Jacob |
| **48** | *wa'**dhkur*** | Ishmael, Elisha, Dhū'l-Kifl |

**The same command four times.** And Solomon is not on this list — because he enters in the thirtieth verse as something **given** to David (*wa wahabnā li-Dāwūda Sulaymān*), and not by a separate command.

**And the verb is from the backbone root of the sūra: ذ-ك-ر.** As was tabulated above, the word occurs eight times in the sūra. **In the first verse the Qurʾān was named *dhi'dh-dhikr*; here a command from the same root arrives; and in the forty-ninth verse the whole of what has been recounted will be named *hādhā dhikr*.**

**The sūra, that is, names its own section of accounts with its own opening epithet. This is a frame that can be verified.**

### عَبْدَنَا — "our servant"

**This construct occurs four times in the sūra** (17, 30 *niʿma'l-ʿabd*, 41, 44 *niʿma'l-ʿabd*) **and appears once more in the eighty-third verse in the mouth of Iblīs: *ʿibādaka minhumu'l-mukhlaṣīn*.**

**What is to be recorded is this: in this sūra the prophets are first named *servant*, and only then is it said what was given to them.** The order is the same each time:

| Verse | First | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | *ʿabdanā Dāwūd* | *dha'l-ayd* — possessor of strength |
| 30 | *niʿma'l-ʿabd* | (36-39: the wind, the jinn, the authority to give) |
| 41 | *ʿabdanā Ayyūb* | (42-43: healing and family) |

**I record this as my own reading:** in the middle of a polemic — ten verses after the question "why was he chosen?" — the sūra defines prophethood as a **servanthood**. **The other side saw an office; the text says a duty.**

### ذَا ٱلْأَيْدِ — possessor of strength

**أَيْد — root أ-ي-د:** power, strength, support. *Ayyada* — he supported, he strengthened; *taʾyīd* — support.

**This word was treated in detail in `051-zariyat.md` at 51/47, and Ṣād 38/17 was used there as the decisive indication.** The finding there was this:

> *"Ṣād 38/17 is a decisive indication, because there the word occurs without tanwīn and in a construct (**dha'l-ayd**), so there is no possibility of its being the plural of *yad* (hand) — it is plainly in the sense of 'strength'."*

**I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**What is to be added here is the word's relative within this sūra. In the forty-fifth verse this will come: *uli'l-aydī wa'l-abṣār* — "those possessed of hands and eyes".** There the word is the plural of *yad* (hand), and I shall treat it in its own place. **The two constructs look alike; they are not:**

| Verse | Wording | Root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **17** | ذَا ٱلْأَيْد | أ-ي-د | **Strength** |
| **45** | أُو۟لِى ٱلْأَيْدِى | ي-د-ي | **Hands** (and, figuratively, strength) |

**The difference between the two is visible in the writing: in verse 45 there is a *yāʾ* at the end (ٱلْأَيْدِى), in verse 17 there is not (ٱلْأَيْد).**

### إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ — the sūra's repeated verdict

**The root أ-و-ب was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/32.** The note there: *"Awwāb is on the *faʿʿāl* pattern: one who returns often… the word conveys not a single act of returning but the having made returning a habit."* **I rely on that.**

**What is particular to Ṣād is the frequency of the word: three times for three prophets** (17 David, 30 Solomon, 44 Job), **and once more in the nineteenth verse.**

**And as was recorded in `088-gasiye.md`, in Qāf 50/32 the same epithet occurs as a general attribute:** *li-kulli awwābin ḥafīẓ*. **The epithet given to three prophets in Ṣād, that is, opens out to everyone in Qāf.**

**I record this as my own reading:** all three prophets are named with the same single epithet. And the epithet has nothing to do with the kingdom, the strength or the patience given to them; it has to do with **turning back**. **The sūra, that is, shows as the common point of three separate lives not what was obtained but the place that is returned to.**

### إِنَّا سَخَّرْنَا ٱلْجِبَالَ — the making subservient of the mountains

**سَخَّرَ — root س-خ-ر.** And the root has two branches; they were treated in `045-casiye.md` and `043-zuhruf.md`:

| Branch | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **تَسْخِير** (*taskhīr*) | Form II | To **bind** a thing to a task, to press it into service |
| **سُخْرِيَّة** (*sukhriyya*) | — | **To mock**, to make a laughing-stock of someone |

**The common point of the two branches, as the lexicographers record it, is the putting of a thing to a task against its own will.**

**And both branches occur in this sūra:** here *sakhkharnā* (*taskhīr*, 18 and 36), and in the sixty-third verse *ittakhadhnāhum **sukhriyyā*** (mockery). **The same root, the one a favour and the other a fault.**

### يُسَبِّحْنَ بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِشْرَاقِ — two hours

**سَبَّحَ — root س-ب-ح.** Its concrete sense: **to move swiftly and without hindrance through water or air.** *Sabaḥa* — he swam. **And *tasbīḥ* is to hold God far from all deficiency** — its link with the picture of "unhindered passage" in the root: a thing's **passing away from** what does not befit it.

**On the question of the praise of the mountains and the birds two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Literal** | Every existing thing has a praise proper to itself; the Qurʾān says this explicitly elsewhere: *"There is nothing that does not praise Him with His praise, but you do not understand their praising"* (al-Isrāʾ 17/44) |
| **An event particular to David** | The mountains' and the birds' joining in the praise together with him is told as a sign given to him |

**The two do not exclude one another** and I do not impose a preference. **The verse al-Isrāʾ 17/44 supports the first view from the text;** while the verse's note here, *maʿahū* ("together with him"), may be pointing to the second.

**بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِشْرَاقِ — two hours are named: late afternoon, and the hour of the sun's rising.**

**إِشْرَاق — root ش-ر-ق:** to rise, to shine. *Sharq* — the direction in which the sun rises. **The verbal noun of Form IV: the sun's climbing and the spreading of its light** — that is, the moment just after sunrise, when the light covers everything.

**A similar pair was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/55:** *bi'l-ʿashiyyi wa'l-ibkār* — late afternoon and early morning. **And the note there holds here too: "By naming the two extreme hours, what lies between them is taken in."**

**The two sūras use the same word (*al-ʿashiyy*), and the second word changes:**

| Sūra | The pair | What the second word conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **Ghāfir 40/55** | *al-ʿashiyy* + *al-ibkār* | **Earliness** — the first moment of the day |
| **Ṣād 38/18** | *al-ʿashiyy* + *al-ishrāq* | **Shining** — the moment the sun climbs |

**And the word *al-ʿashiyy* will occur once more in this sūra, at the thirty-first verse — this time as the hour at which Solomon's horses were brought before him.** **The same hour, two prophets, two separate scenes: in the one, praise; in the other, a delay.** I record this as an observation; I shall come back to it below in its place.

### وَٱلطَّيْرَ مَحْشُورَةً — the gathered birds

**مَحْشُورَة — root ح-ش-ر:** to gather together, to drive and heap in one place. **In the Qurʾān the word is used most often of the gathering at the Resurrection** (*ḥashr*).

**Here it is a passive participle: gathered, brought together.** The birds come not of themselves but **as things gathered.**

**كُلٌّ لَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ** — "all of them turning back to him."

**To whom does the pronoun (*lahū*) refer? The exegetes differ:**

| View | To whom the pronoun refers | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **To God** | *Kullun lahū awwāb* — all of them turn back **to Him** | Praise is in any case to God; and in verse 17 the word meant turning back to God |
| **To David** | When David begins to praise, the mountains and the birds turn **to him**, repeating with him | The note *maʿahū* fits this; here *awwāb* would mean "responding, repeating" |

**I do not impose a preference.** The second view rests on the picture of "coming back" in the root sense of the word: an echo answering a sound is also a kind of return.

### وَشَدَدْنَا مُلْكَهُۥ — the making firm of the kingdom

**شَدَّ — root ش-د-د:** to bind a thing tightly, to draw it taut, to make it firm. *Shadīd* — firm, severe. **Its concrete sense is the tightening of a knot.**

**And the object of the verb is *mulk*.** What is given, that is, is not the kingdom itself — the kingdom was already there; **what is given is the firmness of the kingdom.**

**Worth recording:** in the eleventh verse the other side had been named *mahzūm* (caved in) — and the picture of the root ه-ز-م was the collapse of something emptied inside. **Here the exact opposite: *shadadnā* — we drew tight, we made firm.** The two roots are contraries of one another: the one, slackening and collapse; the other, tautening and holding.

**I record this as an observation; the sūra does not itself stress the contrast.**

### وَفَصْلَ ٱلْخِطَابِ — "the parting of speech"

**And now we come to one of the most important terms in the sūra.**

**فَصْل — root ف-ص-ل. The root was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/3, and what was recorded there was this:** *"The concrete sense of the root: to separate two things from one another, to set a distance between them."* **I rely on that and do not repeat the analysis of the root.**

**In Fuṣṣilat the word was in Form II (*tafṣīl* — to separate a whole into its parts, to set out in detail).** **Here the verbal noun comes in its bare form: *faṣl* — to separate.**

| Pattern | Word | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Form II** | *tafṣīl / fuṣṣilat* | To detail, to separate out the subjects | Fuṣṣilat 41/3 |
| **Verbal noun** | *faṣl* | To separate, to cut apart; **to give judgment** | Ṣād 38/20 |
| **Construct** | *yawmu'l-faṣl* | The Day of Separation | al-Mursalāt 77/13; al-Nabaʾ 78/17 |

**خِطَاب — root خ-ط-ب:** to speak turning towards someone. *Khuṭba* — an address directed at a gathering; *khiṭāb* — speech directed at the one addressed. **There is a *turning* at the root of the word: speech is aimed at a target.**

**Now the construct: *faṣlu'l-khiṭāb* — "the parting of speech".** And the exegetes explain it in several ways:

| View | What it means | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **The capacity to give a decisive judgment in a suit** | To separate the words of two sides from one another and determine who is in the right | The root *faṣl* is used in the Qurʾān in the sense of judgment; and verses 21-26 are precisely a scene of judgment |
| **To speak clearly and decisively** | To speak without prolonging or circling, separating out the matter | The sense of *khiṭāb* as "speech" |
| **To know where to begin speaking** | To set up the right order of a discourse | The explanation of some exegetes |

**The first view is the one most in accord with the sūra's own movement, and its ground is in the text:** the next verse (21) opens a scene of litigation, and the twenty-sixth verse explicitly contains the command to judge (*fa'ḥkum bayna'n-nāsi bi'l-ḥaqq*). **But this is an observation and not a preference; the other explanations are transmitted as well, and they do not exclude one another.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the concrete sense of the root:** *faṣl* is **to separate** two things. Giving judgment is named in the verse not as an exercise of power but as **an act of separating**. The judge separates what has been mixed. **And this is exactly what will not be possible to do in the next scene** — David will judge having heard only one of two speeches; that is, without having taken one of the two things he is to separate into his hand.

**And the word *khiṭāb* inside the construct will return in the twenty-third verse:** the litigant will say *wa ʿazzanī **fi'l-khiṭāb*** — "he overbore me in speech."

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **20** | *faṣla'l-**khiṭāb*** | What was given to David |
| **23** | *ʿazzanī fi'l-**khiṭāb*** | The litigant's complaint |

**Two words, three verses apart and from the same root.** To the one has been given the capacity to part speech; the other complains of being overborne in speech. **This is a verbal link that can be verified, and it plays a part in the setting of the scene.**

## 38/21-22 — وَهَلْ أَتَىٰكَ نَبَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَصْمِ إِذْ تَسَوَّرُوا۟ ٱلْمِحْرَابَ · إِذْ دَخَلُوا۟ عَلَىٰ دَاوُۥدَ فَفَزِعَ مِنْهُمْ قَالُوا۟ لَا تَخَفْ خَصْمَانِ بَغَىٰ بَعْضُنَا عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ فَٱحْكُم بَيْنَنَا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَلَا تُشْطِطْ وَٱهْدِنَآ إِلَىٰ سَوَآءِ ٱلصِّرَٰطِ

*Wa hal atāka nabaʾu'l-khaṣmi idh tasawwaru'l-miḥrāb · Idh dakhalū ʿalā Dāwūda fa-faziʿa minhum, qālū lā takhaf, khaṣmāni baghā baʿḍunā ʿalā baʿḍ, fa'ḥkum baynanā bi'l-ḥaqqi wa lā tushṭiṭ, wa'hdinā ilā sawāʾi'ṣ-ṣirāṭ*

"**Has the report of the litigants reached you? When they climbed the wall into the miḥrāb.** When they came in upon David he **started back** from them. They said: '**Do not fear. We are two litigants; one of us has wronged the other. Judge between us with the truth, do not deal unjustly, and guide us to the middle of the way.**'"

### On the handling of this account — a note on method

**By the rule of `STYLE.md`, which is binding in this commentary, moral imputations drawn from reports of Israelite provenance are not entered into in the accounts of the prophets.**

**These verses are one of the places around which the largest body of reports has gathered in the classical literature of exegesis.** Some of those reports ascribe to David acts **unbefitting a prophet** and read this account as the punishment for those acts.

**My note is this, and it is plain:**

1. **The source of those reports is not the Qurʾān.** There is not a single word in the wording of the verses that speaks of such an event.
2. **A considerable body of the classical exegetes rejected these reports**, giving as their ground the impeccability (*ʿiṣma*) of the prophets and the fact that the wording of the verse does not admit of that narrative. **I record this in the language of "it is transmitted".**
3. **Those narratives will not be entered into in this commentary, and their details will not be given.** To be silent where the text is silent is the attitude applied in `002-bakara.md` at 2/35-36 to the species of the forbidden tree as well: *"Where the text is silent, one must be silent."*

**What follows is limited to what the wording of the verses says, and no more.**

### هَلْ أَتَىٰكَ — the form of the opening

**It opens with the form of a question: *hal atāka* — "has it reached you?"**

**This pattern was treated in `088-gasiye.md`**, and Ṣād 38/21 was named there as one of its instances. **I rely on that.** The function of the pattern is not to ask for information; **it is to gather attention and to announce in advance the weight of what is to be told.**

**And the object: *nabaʾu'l-khaṣm* — "the report of the litigants".**

**نَبَأ — root ن-ب-أ.** It was treated in detail in `078-nebe.md`: **not an ordinary piece of news but a great and weighty report that has consequences.** The lexicographers define *nabaʾ* as "a report whose benefit is great and by which knowledge or a preponderant supposition is produced".

**And this word will occur twice more in the sūra:** 67 (*huwa nabaʾun ʿaẓīm*) and 88 (*wa la-taʿlamunna nabaʾahū*). **All three occurrences turn on the *hearing* of something.**

### ٱلْخَصْم — litigant

**Root خ-ص-م:** to contend, to go to law. **The word is used for both singular and plural** — which is why the verse's saying *al-khaṣm* (a singular form) and then *tasawwarū* (a plural verb) is not a mismatch. **The lexicographers record this plainly: because *khaṣm* comes from the origin of a verbal noun, it does not change for number.**

**And in the twenty-second verse the dual comes: *khaṣmāni* — two litigants.**

**The root is among the sūra's weaving words; it was tabulated above** (21, 22, 64, 69).

### إِذْ تَسَوَّرُوا۟ ٱلْمِحْرَابَ — to come over the wall

**تَسَوَّرَ — root س-و-ر.** And here the concrete sense of the word builds the whole scene.

**سُور (*sūr*) — the wall that encircles a city or a building.** The word was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/13 (*fa-ḍuriba baynahum bi-sūrin lahū bāb* — a wall with a gate in it is set between them).

**تَسَوَّرَ — Form V: to get up onto the wall, to enter by going over the wall.**

**The verse, that is, describes a manner of entry, and the manner is out of the ordinary:**

| | The ordinary entry | The entry here |
|---|---|---|
| The way | The door | **The wall** |
| Permission | Is asked | **Was not asked** |
| Its effect | Is expected | **It startles** |

**مِحْرَاب — root ح-ر-ب.** And the root of the word is surprising: **حَرْب — war.** The lexicographers dwell on the link of *miḥrāb* with this root and several explanations are transmitted: the place where a person makes war on Satan or on his own soul; or the most protected, hardest-to-enter part of a building.

**The word's use in the Qurʾān indicates a private chamber set apart for worship and retreat** (Āl ʿImrān 3/37, 39; Maryam 19/11). **The miḥrāb of the mosques of today is a later settled use of the word; it is not the sense in the verse.**

**What is to be recorded is this:** the *miḥrāb* is the **innermost, most protected** place in a building. **And the two litigants entered it not by the door but over the wall.**

### فَفَزِعَ مِنْهُمْ — starting back

**فَزِعَ — root ف-ز-ع.** And the word differs from *khawf* (fear); the lexicographers record the difference:

| Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| **فَزَع** (*fazaʿ*) | ف-ز-ع | **A sudden start** — a leap at something unexpected |
| **خَوْف** (*khawf*) | خ-و-ف | **Fear** — the expectation of a harm |

***Fazaʿ* is not a stretch of time but an instant.** It is the jolt a sound or a sight that comes without warning gives a person.

**And the verse gives this with two words together:** *fa-faziʿa* (he started back) and then the litigants' saying *lā takhaf* (do not fear). **Two separate roots: the one tells what happened, the other the words that were spoken.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse does not record David's reaction **as a fault**. **That a man should start back when his miḥrāb is entered over the wall is the natural reaction being described.** There is not one word in the verse that censures this reaction.

### خَصْمَانِ بَغَىٰ بَعْضُنَا عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ — the laying of the suit

**بَغَىٰ — root ب-غ-ي.** The root was treated in `045-casiye.md` and `049-hucurat.md`. **Its concrete sense: to seek a thing, to want it; and from there: to want beyond one's bounds, to lay a hand on another's right.**

**The lexicographers record another branch of the root as well: *baghā'l-jarḥ* — the wound festered, it swelled.** There is a picture of **overflowing, of swelling** in the root, that is: something that spills past its limit.

**And the subject of the verb must be recorded: *baghā baʿḍunā ʿalā baʿḍ* — "one of us against the other".**

**Who it is, is not stated.** In laying out the matter the litigants do not set themselves apart as parties: they say "one" and "the other". **This is an impartial manner of presentation.**

**And the same verb will come back in the twenty-fourth verse — this time in David's mouth and as a general verdict:** *wa inna kathīran mina'l-khulaṭāʾi **la-yabghī** baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ.* **The same root, the same pattern (*baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ*), three verses later.**

### فَٱحْكُم بَيْنَنَا بِٱلْحَقِّ — what is asked for

**And now the litigants' request comes; it is made up of three parts, and all three must be recorded:**

| Order | Request | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *fa'ḥkum baynanā **bi'l-ḥaqq*** | A judgment — and its **measure** is specified |
| 2 | *wa **lā tushṭiṭ*** | Do not deal unjustly — a **negative** note |
| 3 | *wa'hdinā ilā **sawāʾi'ṣ-ṣirāṭ*** | Show us the way — **what comes after the judgment** |

**حَكَمَ — root ح-ك-م.** Its concrete sense was treated in `002-bakara.md` and `047-muhammed.md`: **the root's original picture is the bit put into an animal's mouth (*ḥakama*)** — the thing that keeps it from moving without order. **A *ruling*, that is, is to hold a thing in its place, to draw its limit.** *Ḥikma* is from the same root: to put a thing where it belongs.

**And what was given to David in the twentieth verse was *al-ḥikma*.** **What is asked for in the twenty-second verse is *fa'ḥkum* — from the same root.** This is a link between two verses that can be verified.

**لَا تُشْطِطْ — root ش-ط-ط.** It was treated in `072-cin.md` at 72/4, and this verse was explicitly named there. **The note there:** *"Its concrete sense: to go far off, to go to excess, to spill past a limit. The most concrete residue of the root is geographical: **شَطّ (*shaṭṭ*)** — the bank of a river, its edge… The picture of the root, that is, is an **edge**: the place where a thing ends."*

**I rely on that.**

**What is to be added here is this: the two requests complete one another.**

| Request | Its direction | Its picture |
|---|---|---|
| *bi'l-ḥaqq* | **Positive** — according to what judgment is to be given | A measure |
| *lā tushṭiṭ* | **Negative** — where it is not to go | An **edge** |

**I record this as my own reading:** the litigants ask two things of a judge — a centre (*ḥaqq*) and a limit (that he not commit *shaṭaṭ*). **And these two will also be the two parts of the measure of judgment given to David in the twenty-sixth verse:** *bi'l-ḥaqq* (the centre) and *lā tattabiʿi'l-hawā* (the direction not to be swerved into).

**سَوَآءِ ٱلصِّرَٰطِ — root س-و-ي:** to be even, to be level. *Sawāʾ* — the **middle** of a thing, the point where the two sides are equal.

**And *ṣirāṭ* — root ص-ر-ط:** a wide, open road. It was treated in `001-fatiha.md`.

**The construct: "the middle of the way".** Not its edge, that is, but its middle. **And this is the other face of the same picture as the request *lā tushṭiṭ*:** the one says do not go to the edge, the other says bring us to the middle.

## 38/23-24 — إِنَّ هَٰذَآ أَخِى لَهُۥ تِسْعٌ وَتِسْعُونَ نَعْجَةً وَلِىَ نَعْجَةٌ وَٰحِدَةٌ فَقَالَ أَكْفِلْنِيهَا وَعَزَّنِى فِى ٱلْخِطَابِ · قَالَ لَقَدْ ظَلَمَكَ بِسُؤَالِ نَعْجَتِكَ إِلَىٰ نِعَاجِهِۦ وَإِنَّ كَثِيرًا مِّنَ ٱلْخُلَطَآءِ لَيَبْغِى بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَقَلِيلٌ مَّا هُمْ وَظَنَّ دَاوُۥدُ أَنَّمَا فَتَنَّٰهُ فَٱسْتَغْفَرَ رَبَّهُۥ وَخَرَّ رَاكِعًا وَأَنَابَ

*Inna hādhā akhī lahū tisʿun wa tisʿūna naʿjatan wa liya naʿjatun wāḥidatun fa-qāla akfilnīhā wa ʿazzanī fi'l-khiṭāb · Qāla laqad ẓalamaka bi-suʾāli naʿjatika ilā niʿājih, wa inna kathīran mina'l-khulaṭāʾi la-yabghī baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ, illa'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti wa qalīlun mā hum, wa ẓanna Dāwūdu annamā fatannāhu fa'staghfara rabbahū wa kharra rākiʿan wa anāb*

"'**This is my brother; he has ninety-nine ewes and I have one single ewe. He said, "Make her over to me," and he overbore me in speech.**' David said: '**He has surely wronged you in asking for your ewe to add to his own ewes.** Indeed most partners wrong one another, save those who believe and do righteous deeds — **and how few they are.**' **And David understood that We were testing him; he asked forgiveness of his Lord, fell down bowing, and turned back.**"

### The event that is told

**The litigant lays out the matter, and the telling is short and concrete. It has three elements:**

| Element | Expression | Its function |
|---|---|---|
| **The party** | *hādhā **akhī*** — this is my brother | It states the relation between them |
| **The number** | *tisʿun wa tisʿūna* / *wāḥida* | It shows the disproportion |
| **The demand and the pressure** | *akfilnīhā* / *ʿazzanī fi'l-khiṭāb* | It states the act and the manner |

**نَعْجَة — a ewe.** Its plural is *niʿāj*. **The word occurs four times in the scene** (*naʿjatan*, *naʿjatun*, *naʿjatika*, *niʿājihī*) — that is, the same word four times in so short an account.

**تِسْعٌ وَتِسْعُونَ — ninety-nine.** And the choice of number is worth recording: **not a round number, but one short of a hundred.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the figure makes the logic of the demand visible. **That a man who has ninety-nine should ask for the one cannot be explained by need.** Had the number been a hundred, the idea of need would never have arisen; by choosing ninety-nine, it is intimated that the demand springs from **the wish to complete.** **This is an interpretation; the verse does not state the motive.**

**أَكْفِلْنِيهَا — root ك-ف-ل.** Its concrete sense: **to take a thing upon oneself, to assume responsibility for it; to take someone under one's protection.** *Kafīl* — one who takes on another's burden. *Kifl* — a portion, a share.

**The verb is in Form IV and means "make her over to me, put her in my charge".** **The demand, that is, is not framed in the plain language of seizure;** it is spoken in the language of protection and transfer.

**This is worth recording:** *akfilnīhā* is a courteous word. **But the litigant at once adds a second thing.**

### وَعَزَّنِى فِى ٱلْخِطَابِ — "he overbore me in speech"

**عَزَّنِى — root ع-ز-ز.** It was treated at the second verse as *ʿizza*: hardness, impenetrability.

**When the verb is used transitively its sense is: *he got the better of me, he beat me, he overbore me.***

**And the adverbial phrase: *fi'l-khiṭāb* — "in speech", "in the talking".**

**The second part of the complaint, that is, is not the demand itself but the way the demand was pressed:**

| Part of the complaint | About what |
|---|---|
| *akfilnīhā* | **The demand** — what he wanted |
| *ʿazzanī fi'l-khiṭāb* | **The manner** — how he wanted it |

**And now the link with the twentieth verse is complete. It was tabulated above:**

| Verse | Expression | Whose |
|---|---|---|
| **20** | *wa ātaynāhu'l-ḥikmata wa **faṣla'l-khiṭāb*** | What was **given** to David |
| **23** | *wa ʿazzanī **fi'l-khiṭāb*** | The litigant's **complaint** |

**This is a shared wording that can be verified, and I rest my own reading on it:** the suit is opened in exactly the field of the capacity given to David. **A man overborne in speech comes to a man given the capacity to part speech.** The matter told begins as a matter of a ewe, but the second half of the complaint is a matter of **speech**.

### قَالَ لَقَدْ ظَلَمَكَ — the giving of the judgment

**And now we come to the place in the verse that must be read with the greatest care.**

**David gives judgment. And the wording of the judgment is decisive:** *laqad ẓalamak* — **"he has surely wronged you."** Reinforced twice, with *lām* + *qad*.

**Now I set out what can be verified from the text:**

| Order | What the verse says | What the verse does not say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One litigant laid out his matter (23) | That the other litigant said anything |
| 2 | David gave judgment (24) | That the other party was heard |
| 3 | David understood that he was being tested (24) | What the test was about |

**The second line is the core of this account, and it rests entirely on the wording: there is not a single word in the verse about the second litigant's speaking.**

**And the form of David's sentence shows this further:** the judgment contains no **condition**. It does not say "if it is as you have told it"; it says outright *laqad ẓalamak*.

**The classical commentaries dwell on this point, and two attitudes are transmitted:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **The judgment was given on the account of one side alone** | This is the test (*fitna*): the skipping of the order of judgment. There is no speech of the second party in the wording of the verse |
| **The judgment is conditional** | The note "if what you say is true" is there implicitly; in Arabic the condition in judgments of this kind is sometimes left unsaid |

**I convey both views and do not impose a preference.** **But this must be recorded: both views agree that the test has to do with the *procedure of judgment*.** There is no wording in the verse that ties the test to any other subject.

**And this principle of judicial procedure is among the settled maxims of the literature of Islamic law:** a judge does not give judgment without hearing both sides. **That this principle has been connected with this verse is transmitted in the classical sources. I do not enter into the legal detail.**

### وَإِنَّ كَثِيرًا مِّنَ ٱلْخُلَطَآءِ — the widening of the judgment

**خُلَطَاء — its singular *khalīṭ*. Root خ-ل-ط: to mix.** *Khalīṭ* — **one whose property has been mixed with another's, a partner.** The lexicographers record that the word is used in particular of those **whose flocks are mixed with another's.**

**The choice of word suits the scene:** the suit is a suit about sheep, and *khulaṭāʾ* are precisely those who pasture their flocks together.

**And David's sentence passes from a single event to a general verdict:**

| Order | Sentence | Its scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *laqad **ẓalamak*** | A single event |
| 2 | *wa inna **kathīran** mina'l-khulaṭāʾi la-yabghī* | **The majority** |
| 3 | *illa'lladhīna **āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāt*** | The exception |
| 4 | *wa **qalīlun** mā hum* | **The fewness of the exception** |

**I record this as my own reading:** the judgment is given in one sentence and generalised in three. And the fourth sentence (*wa qalīlun mā hum* — "and how few they are") is a sentence of regret.

**And the particle *mā* here is of the same kind as the *jundun mā* of the eleventh verse** — a particle conveying emphasis and feeling. **There, belittlement; here, the stress on fewness.**

**And the expression *la-yabghī baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ* is the exact repetition of the wording the litigants used in the twenty-second verse** (*baghā baʿḍunā ʿalā baʿḍ*). **The same pattern, two verses apart: the one a complaint, the other a general verdict.**

### وَظَنَّ دَاوُۥدُ أَنَّمَا فَتَنَّٰهُ — the moment of understanding

**ظَنَّ — root ظ-ن-ن.** The word works in two directions in Arabic: **a weak supposition (*ẓann*)** and **a conviction close to certainty**. The Qurʾān uses both. **The context here requires the second** — the verbs that follow (asking forgiveness, falling into prostration) are the outcome of a conviction, not of a hesitation.

**This is why rendering the word as "understood" rather than "supposed" is the choice of the majority of the exegetes.** The contrary view is transmitted as well; I do not impose a preference.

**فَتَنَّٰهُ — root ف-ت-ن.** And the concrete sense of the root was treated in `085-buruc.md` and `051-zariyat.md`: **to put gold into the fire and separate the pure from the dross.** *Fitna* — that fire, that assay.

**The picture of the root falls exactly into place here, and I record it as my own reading:** *fitna* is not a punishment but an act of **separation** — the fire does not destroy the gold, it shows what is in it.

**And there is a link of words here:** in the twentieth verse David had been given *faṣla'l-khiṭāb* (the capacity to **separate**). **In the twenty-fourth verse David himself is tried with a *fitna* — that is, with an act of separating.** The two words come from different roots (ف-ص-ل and ف-ت-ن) but both do the work of separating. **I give this as an observation; it is a link of sense, not of wording.**

**أَنَّمَا** — "only", "nothing but". What he understands, that is, is this: **what is happening is nothing but a test.** The litigants, the suit, the ewe — all are part of a test.

### فَٱسْتَغْفَرَ … وَخَرَّ رَاكِعًا وَأَنَابَ — three verbs

**Three verbs come in succession, and their order must be recorded:**

| Order | Verb | Root | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *fa'staghfara rabbah* | غ-ف-ر | **He asked forgiveness** — with the tongue |
| 2 | *wa kharra rākiʿan* | خ-ر-ر / ر-ك-ع | **He fell down bowing** — with the body |
| 3 | *wa anāb* | ن-و-ب | **He turned back** — with his direction |

**خَرَّ — root خ-ر-ر:** to fall from a height, to come down as if collapsing. **This is in the sound of the word as well:** *harīr* — the sound water makes as it runs. **The verb tells not of a controlled bending but of a fall.**

**رَاكِعًا — root ر-ك-ع:** to bend, to bow the back. **This root was treated in `077-murselat.md` at 77/48 and this verse was named there** (*"And he fell down bowing"* — Ṣād 38/24). **I rely on that.**

**And the coming of the two words together is dwelt on:** *kharra* (to fall down) generally comes with *sājidan* (prostrating); here it comes with *rākiʿan*. **The exegetes explain this by the wide sense of the word *rukūʿ* (submission)** — as was recorded in the discussion of al-Mursalāt, the root is used for "submission in general" as well.

**أَنَابَ — root ن-و-ب.** It was treated in `050-kaf.md`: *"*ināba* is **to return again and again** to a place; *nawba* (a turn, work done by turns) is from the same root. In the root there is not a single act of returning but a regular coming back."* **I rely on that.**

**And in the seventeenth verse David had already been named *awwāb*.** **Here the verb of the same attribute comes: *anāb*.** **The attribute, that is, is announced first, and then shown at work.**

### The question of the prostration

**In `096-alak.md` at 96/19, when the prostrations of recitation were treated, this verse was named, and what was recorded there was this:** *"The disagreement is chiefly over two places: whether the prostration at Ṣād 38/24 is a prostration of recitation or a prostration of thanksgiving…"*

**I rely on that and do not enter into the legal detail.** What is to be recorded is that there is a difference of view among the schools about the nature of the prostration in this verse.

## 38/25-26 — فَغَفَرْنَا لَهُۥ ذَٰلِكَ وَإِنَّ لَهُۥ عِندَنَا لَزُلْفَىٰ وَحُسْنَ مَـَٔابٍ · يَٰدَاوُۥدُ إِنَّا جَعَلْنَٰكَ خَلِيفَةً فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَٱحْكُم بَيْنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ ٱلْهَوَىٰ فَيُضِلَّكَ عَن سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَضِلُّونَ عَن سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ شَدِيدٌۢ بِمَا نَسُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْحِسَابِ

*Fa-ghafarnā lahū dhālik, wa inna lahū ʿindanā la-zulfā wa ḥusna maʾāb · Yā Dāwūdu innā jaʿalnāka khalīfatan fi'l-arḍi fa'ḥkum bayna'n-nāsi bi'l-ḥaqqi wa lā tattabiʿi'l-hawā fa-yuḍillaka ʿan sabīli'llāh, inna'lladhīna yaḍillūna ʿan sabīli'llāhi lahum ʿadhābun shadīdun bimā nasū yawma'l-ḥisāb*

"**We forgave him that.** With Us he has a **nearness** and **a good place of return**. '**O David! We have made you a viceroy on the earth; so judge between people with the truth, and do not follow desire — it will lead you astray from the way of God.** Those who go astray from the way of God have a severe punishment, because they forgot the Day of Reckoning.'"

### فَغَفَرْنَا لَهُۥ ذَٰلِكَ — what is forgiven

**غَفَرَ — root غ-ف-ر.** Its concrete sense was treated in `047-muhammed.md` and `074-muddessir.md`: **to cover a thing over, to close it.** *Mighfar* is from the same root — the helmet that covers the head.

**And the object must be recorded: *dhālika* — "that", "that thing".**

**An indefinite demonstrative is used.** What was forgiven is not named.

**I record this plainly:** the verse does not say what was forgiven. **To manufacture a content where it has not spoken is a thing that will not be done in this commentary.** As was written in the note on method above, reports that fill this gap are transmitted in the classical commentaries; **that they are of Israelite provenance and that a considerable body of the exegetes rejected them is also transmitted.** I do not go into the details.

**What can be drawn from the text is this, and it is limited:** in the twenty-fourth verse it was said that there was a *fitna* (a test), that David understood it, that he asked forgiveness and turned back. **The twenty-fifth verse announces that what was asked for was given.**

**And the construction matters: *fa-ghafarnā* — joined with *fāʾ*.** In Arabic *fāʾ* conveys consequence and **following without delay.** **The forgiveness, that is, comes directly on the heels of the turning back.**

### زُلْفَىٰ — nearness

**Root ز-ل-ف.** It was treated in `081-tekvir.md` and this verse was named there: *"Zulfa — nearness, rank. Zulfā — nearness: 'For him there is a nearness with Us' (Ṣād 38/25)."* **I rely on that.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to draw near, to step forward.** *Muzdalifa* is from the same root — the place of drawing near.

**And the construct: *ʿindanā la-zulfā* — "a nearness with Us".**

### حُسْنَ مَـَٔابٍ — a good place of return

**مَـَٔاب — root أ-و-ب.** From the same root as the *awwāb* of the seventeenth verse. **A noun of place: the place one returns and arrives at.**

**As was tabulated above, the word *maʾāb* occurs four times in the sūra** (25, 40, 49, 55) **and two of them are word for word the same:**

| Verse | Expression | For whom |
|---|---|---|
| **25** | *wa inna lahū ʿindanā la-**zulfā wa ḥusna maʾāb*** | **David** |
| **40** | *wa inna lahū ʿindanā la-**zulfā wa ḥusna maʾāb*** | **Solomon** |
| 49 | *wa inna li'l-muttaqīna la-**ḥusna maʾāb*** | The God-fearing |
| 55 | *wa inna li'l-ṭāghīna la-**sharra maʾāb*** | The transgressors |

**The twenty-fifth and the fortieth verses are identical word for word.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it closes the two sections on the prophets with the same sentence.

**And in the forty-ninth verse the same construct opens out to an unnamed group: *al-muttaqīn*.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra then ties what was given to two prophets to an **attribute**. What is recounted, that is, does not remain the privilege of two men; **it is turned into a measure that can be carried.**

### إِنَّا جَعَلْنَٰكَ خَلِيفَةً فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ — the viceroyalty

**خَلِيفَة — root خ-ل-ف:** to come after, to take the place of. **The word was treated in detail in `002-bakara.md` at 2/30. I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**The difference between the use here and the one in al-Baqara must be recorded, and it can be verified:**

| | **al-Baqara 2/30** | **Ṣād 38/26** |
|---|---|---|
| The one addressed | **The angels** — an announcement | **David** — an address to one person |
| Who is viceroy | *fi'l-arḍi **khalīfa*** — indefinite | *jaʿalnā**ka** khalīfa* — a definite person |
| What follows | The angels' question and the names | **A command: judge** |

**I record this as my own reading:** in al-Baqara the viceroyalty is announced as a **position**; in Ṣād it is given as a **description of a duty**. **And the duty is stated in a single sentence: *fa'ḥkum bayna'n-nāsi bi'l-ḥaqq*.**

**In this verse, that is, the viceroyalty is identified with the work of judging.** This is consistent with the whole subject of the sūra.

### فَٱحْكُم بَيْنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلْحَقِّ — the measure of judgment

**And now we come to the central sentence of the sūra.**

**In the twenty-second verse the litigants had said *fa'ḥkum baynanā bi'l-ḥaqq*. In the twenty-sixth verse God says *fa'ḥkum bayna'n-nāsi bi'l-ḥaqq*.**

**The only difference between the two sentences is the widening of the field of those addressed:**

| Verse | Who asks | Between whom |
|---|---|---|
| **22** | The two litigants | *baynanā* — **between us** |
| **26** | God | *bayna'n-nās* — **between people** |

**This is a verbal widening that can be verified, and I rest my own reading on it:** what is lived through in a single suit turns four verses later into a general principle. **The sūra does not give the rule in the abstract; it first makes an event happen, and then states the rule.**

**بِٱلْحَقِّ — and the qualification.** The qualification *bi'l-ḥaqq* was treated in detail in `045-casiye.md` at 45/22 and `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/3. **I rely on that.** What was recorded there was this: that the creation is *bi'l-ḥaqq* is **a qualification placed on the creating itself** — not a quality added afterwards.

**The same qualification is placed here on judgment.** **And in the next verse (27) the opposite of the same word will come: *bāṭilā*.**

**Within three verses, that is, the same measure is used in two fields:**

| Verse | Field | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **26** | **Judgment** | *bi'l-ḥaqq* |
| **27** | **Creation** | *mā khalaqnā… **bāṭilā*** (not in vain) |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the adjacency of the two verses:** the sūra names the measure of the judgment a man gives and the measure on which the universe is built **with the same word**. Judgment is required to be with *ḥaqq*, because creation is not with *bāṭil*.

### وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ ٱلْهَوَىٰ — the source of straying

**هَوَىٰ — root ه-و-ي.** It was treated in `045-casiye.md` at 45/23, and the two branches of the root were given there on the basis of `053-necm.md`: **to fall downward; and desire, inclination.** **I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**The finding in the discussion of al-Jāthiya was this, and it connects here directly:** there the man *"who takes his hawā as his god"* is described, and this was explained as **"determining what to choose as a god according to one's own desire".**

**And in `079-naziat.md` and `053-necm.md` this verse (Ṣād 38/26) was explicitly named:** *"Do not follow hawā; it will lead you astray from the way of God."*

**What is to be added here is the construction of the sentence, and it is fine.**

**The verse does not say "do not follow your desire". ٱلْهَوَىٰ — definite and unpossessed: "do not follow desire".** There is no pronoun.

**I record this as my own reading:** the word has not been personalised. What is forbidden is not David's desire but **the use of desire as a measure.** This lifts the rule out of being peculiar to one person.

**And *fa-yuḍillaka* — "it will then lead you astray".** Joined with *fāʾ*: the straying is the **consequence** of following *hawā*, not a separate event.

**ضَلَّ — root ض-ل-ل:** to leave the road, to be lost. **And that the verb is attributed here to *hawā* must be recorded:** what leads astray is not the person himself but the thing he follows. **That is, *hawā* is described as something that gives direction** — something that can be followed, and something that takes one somewhere when it is followed.

**Joined with the "falling downward" branch of the root, this yields the following as my own reading:** *hawā* takes a person somewhere — but the direction it goes is downward. The two branches of the word (falling / desire) build one single picture in this sentence.

### بِمَا نَسُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْحِسَابِ — forgetting

**نَسِىَ — root ن-س-ي:** to forget; and **to abandon, to disregard.** The Qurʾān uses the word mostly in the second sense — not dropping out of knowledge, but **not being taken into account.**

**And the chain the sentence sets up must be recorded:**

| Step | Expression |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Nasū yawma'l-ḥisāb* — they **did not take the Day of Reckoning into account** |
| 2 | (And so) *ittibāʿu'l-hawā* — they followed desire |
| 3 | *Yaḍillūna ʿan sabīli'llāh* — they strayed from the way |
| 4 | *Lahum ʿadhābun shadīd* — the requital |

**The verse gives this chain in the reverse order:** it states the punishment first and then its cause (*bimā nasū*). **At the end of the sentence, that is, stands the starting point.**

**And *yawmu'l-ḥisāb* had occurred in the sixteenth verse** — there the mockers wanted that day **brought forward.** **Here the same day is named as forgotten by those who go astray.** It was tabulated above.

**I record this as my own reading:** the two postures look opposed but come out at the same place. **To ask mockingly for a thing to come is also not to take it into account.** The demand in the sixteenth verse is another form of the forgetting in the twenty-sixth.

### What this says to the present

**The stretch from the twenty-first to the twenty-sixth verse is the most concrete part of the sūra, and it recounts a procedure of judgment.**

**Three things emerge from the text and are beyond dispute:**

1. **Judgment is bound to a measure:** *bi'l-ḥaqq*. Not the judge's own conviction, but the truth itself.
2. **What corrupts judgment is an inclination:** *ittibāʿu'l-hawā*. Not a bribe or a pressure — **the following of desire.** The verse names the finest of the dangers: not a corrupter that comes from outside, but a leaning that comes from within.
3. **And the ordering of verses twenty-three and twenty-four shows that giving judgment on one-sided knowledge is a matter of testing.**

**I record this as my own inference, and I give it not as a sermon but as an observation:** what goes wrong in the account is not an ill intention. **The judgment recounted is in all likelihood the right judgment** — that a man with ninety-nine ewes should ask for the one is called *ẓulm* in the wording of the verse itself. **What is tested is not the rightness of the outcome but the road by which it was reached.**

**And this distinction — between a right outcome and a right procedure — is not only a matter of adjudication.** To settle on what one person has told you and decide, in any matter whatever, has the same structure. **The sentence *inna hādhā la-shayʾun yurād* in the sixth verse of the sūra was built in exactly this way:** the passage from a one-sided reading to a verdict.

**This resemblance between the two scenes is a link I make; the sūra does not make it separately.** But both are inside the same sūra, and what is missing in both is the same thing: **the word of the other side.**

## 38/27-28 — وَمَا خَلَقْنَا ٱلسَّمَآءَ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا بَٰطِلًا ذَٰلِكَ ظَنُّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ فَوَيْلٌ لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلنَّارِ · أَمْ نَجْعَلُ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ كَٱلْمُفْسِدِينَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أَمْ نَجْعَلُ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ كَٱلْفُجَّارِ

*Wa mā khalaqna'l-samāʾa wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā bāṭilā, dhālika ẓannu'lladhīna kafarū, fa-waylun li'lladhīna kafarū mina'n-nār · Am najʿalu'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti ka'l-mufsidīna fi'l-arḍ, am najʿalu'l-muttaqīna ka'l-fujjār*

"**We did not create the heaven and the earth and what is between them in vain.** That is the supposition of those who disbelieve. **So woe to those who disbelieve, from the Fire!** Or shall We treat those who believe and do righteous deeds like those who work corruption in the earth? **Or shall We treat the God-fearing like the dissolute?**"

### Context — the passage from account to principle

**The twenty-sixth verse ended with a measure of judgment (*bi'l-ḥaqq*). The twenty-seventh applies the same measure to the universe.**

**And the transition is silent: no conjunction comes between, no sign is given that the subject has changed.** The moment the account of David ends, the ground of the creation comes.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the adjacency of the two verses:** immediately after the verse that requires judgment to be given among people, the sūra says that **the universe too was not built without judgment.** **The two measures are tied to the same place.**

### بَٰطِلًا — in vain

**Root ب-ط-ل.** Its concrete sense: **a thing's going for nothing, remaining void, yielding no result.** *Bāṭil* — that by which one arrives nowhere. The lexicographers count the word the opposite of *ḥaqq*.

**And the word's grammatical place here must be recorded: *bāṭilan* is in the accusative, and the grammarians explain this in two ways:**

| View | Explanation | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **A circumstantial accusative (*ḥāl*)** | The state of the things created | "We did not create them as a void thing" |
| **An accusative of purpose (*mafʿūl li-ajlih*)** | The ground of the creating | "We did not create them in vain" — that is, not without purpose |

**Both explanations are transmitted and the sense is close. I do not impose a preference.**

**And the qualification *bi'l-ḥaqq*, treated in `045-casiye.md` at 45/22 and `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/3, comes here in its negative. I rely on that.**

**Set the three verses side by side and what emerges can be verified:**

| Verse | Expression | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Aḥqāf 46/3** | *mā khalaqna'l-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā illā **bi'l-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā*** | **Positive** — truth and a term |
| **al-Jāthiya 45/22** | *wa khalaqa'llāhu'l-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa **bi'l-ḥaqq*** | **Positive** — truth |
| **Ṣād 38/27** | *wa mā khalaqna'l-samāʾa wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā **bāṭilā*** | **Negative** — not void |

**The same sentence-frame, in three sūras in three different forms.** al-Aḥqāf adds the term, al-Jāthiya the measure, and Ṣād gives it by negating the opposite.

**I record this as my own reading:** the choice of the negative pattern is fitting in this sūra, because **Ṣād is a sūra of objections.** The other side was rejecting something; the verse answers by rejecting a rejection. **And it names that rejection at once: *dhālika ẓannu'lladhīna kafarū*.**

### ذَٰلِكَ ظَنُّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ — a diagnosis

**And this sentence completes the diagnoses of the polemical part of the sūra.**

**In the eighth verse it was said *bal hum fī shakkin min dhikrī* — doubt. Here it says *ẓann*.**

| Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| **شَكّ** (*shakk*) | ش-ك-ك | **Irresolution** between two possibilities |
| **ظَنّ** (*ẓann*) | ظ-ن-ن | **The weighing down of one side** — a conviction |

**In the eighth verse, that is, irresolution; in the twenty-seventh, a conviction.** And the content of the conviction is stated: **that the universe has been built in vain.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse describes denial not as a lack of knowledge but as **an assumption.** And the subject of the assumption is not God's existence — it is **whether the universe has an outcome.**

**And in the twenty-fourth verse the same root was used of David: *wa ẓanna Dāwūdu annamā fatannāh*.**

| Verse | Whose supposition | About what | Its outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **24** | David | "This is a test" | A turning back and forgiveness |
| **27** | Those who disbelieve | "This is in vain" | *Waylun… mina'n-nār* |

**The same root, three verses apart, two opposite outcomes.** I record this as an observation. **The word itself is neither right nor wrong; what is decisive is what it is directed at.**

### أَمْ نَجْعَلُ — two questions

**And the twenty-eighth verse asks the same matter in the field of morals.**

**Two questions come in succession, and both begin with *am najʿalu*. The pairs compared are different:**

| Question | One side | The other side | The axis of comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The first** | *alladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāt* | *al-mufsidīna **fi'l-arḍ*** | **What they do** — building / corrupting |
| **The second** | *al-muttaqīn* | *al-fujjār* | **Their inward posture** — guarding / plunging |

**I record this as my own reading:** the first question is framed by a measure that **looks outward** (what one does on the earth), the second by a measure that **looks inward** (how one stands). **The two together take in both a person's act and his posture.**

**فُجَّار — root ف-ج-ر.** Its concrete sense: **to split a thing open, to cleave a spring so that it gushes.** *Infijār* — a bursting forth; *fajr* — the light that breaks out through the darkness of the night. **And from there: to break through a limit, to acknowledge no measure.**

**This root was treated in `083-mutaffifin.md`** (*kitāba'l-fujjāri la-fī sijjīn*), and the "splitting" picture of the root was given there. **I rely on that.**

**مُتَّقِين — root و-ق-ي:** to guard oneself, to put something between oneself and a thing. **The two words are exact opposites:**

| | **مُتَّقِى** | **فَاجِر** |
|---|---|---|
| The picture of the root | **To take cover** — to hold something in between | **To break through** — to leave nothing in between |
| Its posture towards a limit | It stops on the near side of the limit | It splits the limit |

**This opposition is drawn from the root senses the lexicographers record, and I give it as my own reading:** the two words are not randomly chosen opposites; **both describe a relation to a limit, in contrary directions.**

### The common work of the two verses

**The twenty-seventh verse says the universe is not in vain. The twenty-eighth asks whether two groups will be treated alike.**

**And the logical link between them is direct, and I record it as my own reading:**

**If the two groups arrived at the same outcome, what a person does would have no outcome. And a thing that has no outcome is *bāṭil* — that is, the very thing the twenty-seventh verse rejects.**

**The second verse, that is, is not the proof of the first but its application.** That the universe is not void means that the moral distinction is real.

**And the same link was made in `045-casiye.md` at 45/22:** there too the creation's being *bi'l-ḥaqq* was joined with the ground that *"every soul may be recompensed for what it has earned"*. **I rely on the note there: "The ground of the distinction is given not as a desire to punish but as a qualification placed on the creating itself."**

## 38/29 — كِتَٰبٌ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ إِلَيْكَ مُبَٰرَكٌ لِّيَدَّبَّرُوٓا۟ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ وَلِيَتَذَكَّرَ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ

*Kitābun anzalnāhu ilayka mubārakun li-yaddabbarū āyātihī wa li-yatadhakkara ulu'l-albāb*

"**A blessed Book We have sent down to you** — that they may **ponder** its verses and that those who have a kernel may **take admonition**."

### The place of the verse within the sūra

**This verse stands near the exact middle of the sūra, and it is a sentence of explanation: it says what the text in your hands is and what it was sent down for.**

**And its position must be recorded:** verses twenty-one to twenty-six told an account; verses twenty-seven and twenty-eight laid down a principle; **the twenty-ninth verse says how these are to be read.**

### مُبَٰرَك — root ب-ر-ك

**The root was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/9 and in `044-duhan.md` at 44/3. I rely on that.**

**The concrete sense of the root is worth recording, because it is surprising:** **بَرَكَ (*baraka*) — the camel's kneeling down, its settling and staying in a place.** *Birka* — a pool, the place where water gathers and stands.

**The picture of the root, that is, is a *settling and a staying*.** *Baraka* — not first the increase of a thing but its **remaining**; the not drying up of the source of increase.

**And *mubārak* is a passive participle: given blessing.** The quality is not of itself, that is, but placed in it.

**The Qurʾān uses this epithet of itself in several places** (al-Anʿām 6/92, 155; al-Anbiyāʾ 21/50; al-Dukhān 44/3). **What is particular to the use in Ṣād is that a ground follows immediately upon the epithet.**

### لِّيَدَّبَّرُوٓا۟ — *tadabbur*

**Root د-ب-ر. It was treated in detail in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/24, and this verse (Ṣād 38/29) was explicitly named there. I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**The summary of the findings there:**

> *"The concrete sense of the root carries the whole weight of the word: **the back, the back of a thing.**… **تَدَبُّر (*tadabbur*): to get behind a thing, to think about its end.**… That is, *tadabbur* is not something that happens at a glance. The pattern of the word says that the work **takes time** and **demands labour**."*

**And in the discussion of Sūrat Muḥammad a particular note was entered for this verse:**

> *"**And Ṣād 38/29 is striking:** there *tadabbur* is given as the **ground of the Book's being sent down** — *li-yaddabbarū*. The text, that is, ties its own reason for existing to this verb."*

**I rely on that note and add only two things here.**

**One: the difference of mood between the two sūras.**

| Sūra | Expression | Mood | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Muḥammad 47/24** | *a-fa-lā **yatadabbarūna**'l-Qurʾān* | A question — negative | **Reproach**: why do they not do it |
| **Ṣād 38/29** | ***li-yaddabbarū** āyātih* | A ground with *lām* | **Purpose**: it was sent down for this |

**The same verb, at two different works in two sūras: in the one its absence is questioned, in the other it is set down as the reason for existing.** This can be verified from the wording of the two verses.

**Two: the difference of object.** In Sūrat Muḥammad the object is *al-Qurʾān* (the whole); **here it is *āyātih* (the parts).**

**I record this as my own reading:** in Ṣād the object of *tadabbur* is **the verses one by one.** And joined with the root's picture of "getting behind", what comes out is this: it is asked that one get behind each verse separately — not a collective impression.

### وَلِيَتَذَكَّرَ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ — the second ground

**Two grounds in succession, both joined with *lām*. But their subjects differ:**

| Ground | Subject | Who |
|---|---|---|
| *li-yaddabbarū* | An implied plural pronoun | **Everyone** — indefinite |
| *li-yatadhakkara* | *ulu'l-albāb* | **Those who have a kernel** — definite |

**I record this as my own reading:** the first verb is a **work** and is asked of everyone; the second is an **outcome** and is tied to a particular attribute. **That is, *tadabbur* is something anyone can do, and *tadhakkur* is its fruit.**

**أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰب — its singular *lubb*.** And the concrete sense of the word must be recorded: **لُبّ — the kernel of a thing, its inner part; the edible part inside the shell of a walnut or an almond.**

**The rendering "those of understanding" therefore falls short.** *Ulu'l-albāb* means **those who have a kernel** — those who have got past the shell, who are full inside.

**And the word will occur once more in this sūra, at the forty-third verse: *wa dhikrā li-uli'l-albāb* (at the end of the account of Job).**

| Verse | Expression | After what |
|---|---|---|
| **29** | *wa li-yatadhakkara **ulu'l-albāb*** | The ground of the Book's being sent down |
| **43** | *wa **dhikrā** li-uli'l-albāb* | The giving back of Job's family to him |

**In both occurrences the same root (ذ-ك-ر) is named together with the same group.** This is a repetition that can be verified.

**I record this as my own reading:** the twenty-ninth verse says of the Book as a whole that admonition is to be taken from it; the forty-third describes **a single event** with the same word. **The accounts, that is, are instances of the function the Book describes for itself.**

### A note on the word order

**The ordering of the verse is worth recording:** *kitābun anzalnāhu ilayka **mubārakun*** — the adjective comes **after** the verbal clause.

**The ordinary order in Arabic would be "we sent down a blessed Book".** Here comes first *kitābun* (indefinite — "a Book"), then *anzalnāhu ilayk* (a qualifying clause), then *mubārak* (a second adjective).

**The grammarians explain this thus: the intervening clause brings the second adjective forward.** The emphasis, that is, falls on *mubārak*.

**And I record this as an observation:** in the seventh verse the other side had said of this Book *in hādhā illā **ikhtilāq*** — "nothing but a fabrication." **The twenty-ninth verse says three things about the same Book: it has been sent down, it is blessed, and it has a purpose.** The objection is not answered directly; **what the Book is, is stated, and the comparison is left to the reader.**

## 38/30 — وَوَهَبْنَا لِدَاوُۥدَ سُلَيْمَٰنَ نِعْمَ ٱلْعَبْدُ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ

*Wa wahabnā li-Dāwūda Sulaymān, niʿma'l-ʿabd, innahū awwāb*

"**And We bestowed Solomon on David.** How excellent a servant! He was one who turned back often."

### وَهَبْنَا — and the answer to the ninth verse

**The root و-ه-ب occurred in the ninth verse as *al-Wahhāb* and was treated there: to give without return.**

**It was tabulated above; here I complete it. This is the name's first actual answer within the sūra:**

| Verse | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | *al-**Wahhāb*** — the name | God |
| **30** | *wa **wahabnā*** — the verb | Solomon to David |

**And what is given is not a kingdom or a capacity — it is a son.**

**I record this as my own reading:** in the ninth verse the other side had been met with the question "are the treasuries of mercy with you?" **The thirtieth verse shows that something from that treasury has in fact been given.** And what is given is a thing of a kind a man cannot obtain by his own effort. **This accords with the word's sense of "giving without return".**

### نِعْمَ ٱلْعَبْدُ — the pattern of praise

**نِعْمَ — in Arabic a verb of praise (*fiʿl al-madḥ*): a verb that conveys praise and does not inflect.** Its opposite is *biʾsa* (blame).

**This pattern was treated in `077-murselat.md` at 77/23 and this verse was named there:** *"And 'How excellent a servant' (Ṣād 38/30, 44) — *niʿma'l-ʿabd*."* **I rely on that.**

**And the pattern occurs four times in the sūra — twice in praise, twice in blame:**

| Verse | Pattern | Who/what |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | *niʿma'l-ʿabd* | Solomon |
| **44** | *niʿma'l-ʿabd* | Job |
| **56** | *fa-biʾsa'l-mihād* | Hell — "how evil a bed" |
| **60** | *fa-biʾsa'l-qarār* | "How evil a place to stay" |

**This is a placement that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the same grammatical pattern works in opposite directions in the two halves of the sūra. **Two praises in the section on the prophets, two blames in the section on the Fire.**

**And the object of the praise must be recorded: *al-ʿabd* — the servant.** Not the kingdom, not the wisdom, not the wind. **The list of what was given to Solomon will come six verses later; the praise comes before what was given, and is built on a single word.**

### إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ — the shared epithet of three prophets

**In the seventeenth verse for David, here for Solomon, in the forty-fourth for Job.**

**All three uses are on the same pattern: *innahū awwāb*.** Word for word the same.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra introduces the three prophets by three different things — David by strength and judgment, Solomon by kingdom and rule, Job by affliction and patience. **But it closes all three with the same sentence.** What differs is what was given; what is shared is the returning.

## 38/31-33 — إِذْ عُرِضَ عَلَيْهِ بِٱلْعَشِىِّ ٱلصَّٰفِنَٰتُ ٱلْجِيَادُ · فَقَالَ إِنِّىٓ أَحْبَبْتُ حُبَّ ٱلْخَيْرِ عَن ذِكْرِ رَبِّى حَتَّىٰ تَوَارَتْ بِٱلْحِجَابِ · رُدُّوهَا عَلَىَّ فَطَفِقَ مَسْحًۢا بِٱلسُّوقِ وَٱلْأَعْنَاقِ

*Idh ʿuriḍa ʿalayhi bi'l-ʿashiyyi'ṣ-ṣāfinātu'l-jiyād · Fa-qāla innī aḥbabtu ḥubba'l-khayri ʿan dhikri rabbī ḥattā tawārat bi'l-ḥijāb · Ruddūhā ʿalayya, fa-ṭafiqa masḥan bi's-sūqi wa'l-aʿnāq*

"**When there were presented to him in the late afternoon the noble racing horses that stand poised on the tip of one hoof.** He said: '**I have loved the love of the good out of (/for) the remembrance of my Lord**' — until **it was hidden behind the veil.** '**Bring them back to me.**' And **he set to stroking/striking their legs and their necks.**"

### A note on method — again

**These three verses are the place in the sūra over which there has been the greatest divergence.** And the note at the twenty-first verse holds here too:

**A great many reports have gathered around this scene in the classical commentaries, and some of them ascribe to Solomon acts unbefitting a prophet.** **Those narratives are not entered into in this commentary.** It is transmitted that a considerable body of the classical exegetes rejected these reports.

**Below only the wording of the verses will be dwelt on, the disagreement will be given in a table, and no preference will be imposed.**

### ٱلصَّٰفِنَٰتُ ٱلْجِيَادُ — the description of the horses

**They are described with two words, and both are technical terms.**

**صَافِنَات — root ص-ف-ن.** The lexicographers describe the word by a very distinct stance: **صَفَنَ الفَرَسُ — the horse stood on three legs and set the fourth on the tip of its hoof.**

**This is a stance known in horsemanship, and it shows two things:** that the animal is **fresh** (a tired horse plants all four legs) and that it is **trained.**

| Term | Root | About what |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلصَّافِنَات** | ص-ف-ن | **Stance** — ready even at rest |
| **ٱلْجِيَاد** | ج-و-د | **Running** — swift, noble |

**جِيَاد — its singular *jawād*. Root ج-و-د:** to be good, to be generous. *Jawād* — both a generous man and a swift horse. **The common point: the generous man does not hold back his hand when he gives, and the swift horse does not hold itself back when it runs.**

**The two epithets together, that is, say one single thing: these are not ordinary horses.**

**Some exegetes have also understood *ṣāfināt* not as "raising one foot" but as "drawn up in rows"** (from the closeness of the roots ص-ف-ن and ص-ف-ف). **Both explanations are transmitted.**

### بِٱلْعَشِىِّ — the hour

**And this word occurs for the second time in the sūra. In the eighteenth verse it was the hour at which the mountains gave praise together with David: *bi'l-ʿashiyyi wa'l-ishrāq*.**

| Verse | Hour | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | *bi'l-**ʿashiyy**i wa'l-ishrāq* | David — **praise** |
| **31** | *bi'l-**ʿashiyy*** | Solomon — **the presenting of the horses** |

**This is a verbal repetition that can be verified.** The one who makes the link is I, and I record it as my own reading: **the same hour, two prophets, two different occupations.** The sūra does not interpret this further; it sets the two scenes side by side and leaves them.

### إِنِّىٓ أَحْبَبْتُ حُبَّ ٱلْخَيْرِ عَن ذِكْرِ رَبِّى — the disagreement over the sentence

**The sentence is disputed on account of three words: *aḥbabtu*, *al-khayr*, and *ʿan*.**

**First the lexicon:**

**ٱلْخَيْر — root خ-ي-ر:** that which is good, that which is preferred. **And the word has a particular use in Arabic which the lexicographers record: *al-khayr* is used in the sense of horses.** This is a settled use in the language.

**عَنْ** — in Arabic it basically conveys **moving away**: "from". But it is used in other works as well.

**Now the table of the disagreement:**

| Reading | What *al-khayr* is | What *ʿan* conveys | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The reading of preference** | **The love of property / of horses** | **Moving away** — "on account of, in preference to" | "I have loved this love **so as to pass beyond** the remembrance of my Lord" |
| **2. The reading of ground** | **The horses** | **In the sense of *li-ajli*** — "on account of, for" | "I have loved these horses **because of the remembrance of my Lord**" — that is, they are for a duty |
| **3. The reading of preoccupation** | **The horses** | Moving away | "Being occupied with them held me back from the remembrance" |

**The first and third readings take it as the confession of a fault; the second as a justification.**

**I do not impose a preference.** This much can be recorded, and it rests on the wording: **there is not one word in the sentence conveying regret or a plea for forgiveness.** In the thirty-fourth verse *thumma anāb* (then he turned back) will come, but that sentence follows a separate event (the throne and the body).

**And *aḥbabtu ḥubba* — the verbal noun coming together with the verb conveys emphasis in Arabic:** "I loved, and what a loving."

### حَتَّىٰ تَوَارَتْ بِٱلْحِجَابِ — what was hidden

**تَوَارَتْ — root و-ر-ي:** to cover, to conceal. **Form VI (*tafāʿul*): to hide oneself, to pass behind something.**

**حِجَاب — root ح-ج-ب:** a veil, a barrier; the thing standing between. **This word was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/5** (the other side's saying *wa min bayninā wa baynika **ḥijāb***).

**And the subject of the verb is not stated in the verse. This is the source of the disagreement:**

| View | What was hidden | Ground | Its weak side |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The sun** | The sun set | The indication of *bi'l-ʿashiyy* (late afternoon); in Arabic the sun can be indicated by a pronoun without being named — the lexicographers record this | It has not been named in the wording |
| **The horses** | The horses ran off and passed out of sight | **This is the feminine plural actually named in the wording** (*al-ṣāfināt*); it is the norm for a pronoun to refer to its nearest antecedent | The word *ḥijāb* is unusual for horses |

**I do not impose a preference.** The two readings give the following verse different senses: on the first, what is meant by "bring them back" is the horses and the event takes place after sunset; on the second, the horses had gone off and are called back.

### فَطَفِقَ مَسْحًۢا بِٱلسُّوقِ وَٱلْأَعْنَاقِ — what the *masḥ* is

**And this is the most disputed half-sentence in the sūra.**

**طَفِقَ — in Arabic it is among the verbs that convey "to begin, to set about a thing"** (*afʿāl al-shurūʿ*). **The word that follows states the work begun.**

**مَسْح — root م-س-ح.** And the original sense of the root: **to pass the hand over a thing, to stroke it, to wipe it.** The wiping (*masḥ*) of the head in the ablution is from this root (al-Māʾida 5/6).

**And the lexicographers record a second use of the root as well: *masaḥahū bi'l-sayf* — he struck him with the sword, he cut him.** This use is derived from the picture of the sword **passing over** its target.

**Now the table of the disagreement:**

| View | What the *masḥ* is | What has happened | Its ground |
|---|---|---|---|
| **To stroke / to caress** | To lay a hand on | Solomon had the horses brought back and **stroked their legs and their necks** — affection, appreciation, attention | **This is the original sense of the root**, and the word's other uses in the Qurʾān go this way |
| **To cut** | To strike with the sword | He slaughtered the horses — he freed himself of what was holding him back, or he consecrated them | The use *masaḥahū bi'l-sayf* recorded by the lexicographers; and the resemblance of the construct *al-sūq wa'l-aʿnāq* (legs and necks) to the places of slaughter |
| **To brand / to mark** | To pass the hand over and set a mark | He assigned the horses to a task | The explanation of some exegetes; it rests on the "passing over" sense of the root *masḥ* |

**In this disagreement I certainly do not impose a preference, and I write my reason out plainly: the wording of the verse admits of all three readings and contains no indication that excludes any of them.**

**This much can be recorded:**

- **The original and common sense of the root supports the first view.** The second sense is figurative and less used.
- **The indication supporting the second view is the naming of the parts that are cut** (*sūq* — legs; *aʿnāq* — necks).
- **The indication supporting the first view is the command *ruddūhā ʿalayya* (bring them back to me):** to have a thing brought back is an act of drawing near.

**And I do not enter into the detailed narratives that have gathered around the second view in the classical commentaries;** it is transmitted that the source of these narratives is report, and that they have been disputed among the exegetes.

**سُوق — its singular *sāq*: the shank, the leg.** **أَعْنَاق — its singular *ʿunuq*: the neck.**

**I record this as an observation:** the two parts are where the horse's two basic qualities reside — **the leg carries the running, the neck the stance.** The two epithets of the thirty-first verse (*ṣāfināt* — stance, *jiyād* — running) correspond exactly to these two parts. **This is an equivalence of wording; I establish no ruling as to what it means.**

## 38/34-35 — وَلَقَدْ فَتَنَّا سُلَيْمَٰنَ وَأَلْقَيْنَا عَلَىٰ كُرْسِيِّهِۦ جَسَدًا ثُمَّ أَنَابَ · قَالَ رَبِّ ٱغْفِرْ لِى وَهَبْ لِى مُلْكًا لَّا يَنۢبَغِى لِأَحَدٍ مِّنۢ بَعْدِىٓ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْوَهَّابُ

*Wa laqad fatannā Sulaymāna wa alqaynā ʿalā kursiyyihī jasadan thumma anāb · Qāla rabbi'ghfir lī wa hab lī mulkan lā yanbaghī li-aḥadin min baʿdī, innaka anta'l-Wahhāb*

"**And We tested Solomon too, and cast a body upon his throne; then he turned back.** He said: '**My Lord! Forgive me, and bestow on me a kingdom that will not befit anyone after me. You are indeed the great giver.**'"

### وَلَقَدْ فَتَنَّا سُلَيْمَٰنَ — the second test

**The root ف-ت-ن was used of David in the twenty-fourth verse and was treated there: to put gold into the fire and separate out the pure.**

**And the two sentences must be compared:**

| Verse | Expression | Who knows |
|---|---|---|
| **24** | *wa **ẓanna Dāwūdu** annamā fatannāhu* | **David understood** — the test is perceived from inside |
| **34** | *wa laqad **fatannā** Sulaymān* | **The text announces it** — the test is reported from outside |

**This is a difference of construction that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** in the account of David it is David who perceives the test; in the account of Solomon it is the text itself that announces the test to the reader. **Two tellings give the same event from two different places.**

### وَأَلْقَيْنَا عَلَىٰ كُرْسِيِّهِۦ جَسَدًا — a plain note of disagreement

**There is no certain knowledge about what this sentence recounts, and I write that plainly.**

**A great many explanations are transmitted for this half-verse in the classical commentaries. These explanations:**

- **have no common ground;**
- **rest for the most part on report, and the soundness of the reports is disputed;**
- **some of them go back to narratives held to be of Israelite provenance.**

**The detail of those explanations is not entered into in this commentary, and no preference is made among them.** The note entered in `050-kaf.md` at 50/1 for the "Mount Qāf" explanation holds here too: **to convey and to adopt are two different things, and an explanation with no ground is not elaborated even while it is being conveyed.**

**I keep within what the wording says:**

**كُرْسِىّ — root ك-ر-س:** to pile a thing up and make it firm; and from there **a throne, a seat.** There is a **fixedness** and a **being piled up** at the root of the word.

**جَسَد — root ج-س-د:** a body, a frame. **And the distinguishing side of the word is recorded by the lexicographers: *jasad* is a body without a soul.** For a living body *jism* or *badan* is used; *jasad* conveys what is lifeless or motionless. **The Qurʾān uses this word of the image of the calf as well** (al-Aʿrāf 7/148; Ṭāhā 20/88 — *ʿijlan jasadan lahū khuwār*).

**أَلْقَيْنَا — root ل-ق-ي.** In `050-kaf.md` the four different works of this root in Sūrat Qāf were tabulated. **Form IV: to throw, to cast.**

**ثُمَّ أَنَابَ** — *thumma* here conveys **sequence and an interval**: "then". **And the verb is the very one used of David in the twenty-fourth verse: *anāb*.**

| Verse | Who | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| **24** | David | *wa **anāb*** |
| **34** | Solomon | *thumma **anāb*** |

**The same word, at the knot of both accounts.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.

### رَبِّ ٱغْفِرْ لِى — the first half of the prayer

**The prayer is made up of two requests, and their order must be recorded:**

| Order | Request | About what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *ighfir lī* | **Forgiveness** |
| 2 | *hab lī mulkan…* | **A kingdom** |

**Forgiveness comes first.**

**And a comparison with the account of David can be made:**

| | **David (24-25)** | **Solomon (34-35)** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb | *fa'st**aghfar**a rabbah* — **he asked forgiveness** | *rabbi'**ghfir** lī* — **the wording of the prayer is given** |
| The answer | *fa-**ghafarnā** lahū dhālik* (25) | (Not separately announced in the verse; 36-40 count what was given) |

**The same root (غ-ف-ر) is at the knot of both accounts.** And **the root will come once more in the sūra, in the sixty-sixth verse, as a name: *al-ʿAzīzu'l-Ghaffār*.**

### وَهَبْ لِى مُلْكًا لَّا يَنۢبَغِى لِأَحَدٍ مِّنۢ بَعْدِى — the second request

**This sentence calls for careful reading, because at first glance it looks like a request for a privilege.**

**لَا يَنۢبَغِى — root ب-غ-ي.** And this is the very root that occurs in the twenty-second and twenty-fourth verses of the sūra (*baghā*, *la-yabghī* — to wrong).

**Form VII (*infiʿāl*): *inbaghā* — to be fitting, to befit, to be suitable.** **And in the negative: *lā yanbaghī* — it does not befit, it is not fitting.**

**The relation between the two branches of the root must be recorded:**

| Pattern | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Form I** | *baghā* | **To seek** → to seek beyond one's bounds |
| **Form VII** | *inbaghā* | **To be fitting** — for the seeking of it to be in place |

**The same root, that is, conveys "to seek out of place" in the one and "to be in place" in the other.** The common point is a **measure of fittingness**: *baghy* is to seek by going outside the measure; *inbighāʾ* is to be inside it.

**And this is a weave that can be verified within the sūra:**

| Verse | Wording | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | *baghā baʿḍunā ʿalā baʿḍ* | The litigants |
| 24 | *la-yabghī baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ* | Partners (a general verdict) |
| **35** | *lā **yanbaghī** li-aḥadin min baʿdī* | Solomon's prayer |

**The same root, in three occurrences, in two opposite directions.**

**Now I come to the meaning of the prayer, and I record the disagreement:**

| View | What *lā yanbaghī li-aḥadin min baʿdī* means |
|---|---|
| **Let it be given to no one after me** | Understood as a request for a monopoly; some exegetes explain it as a sign of prophethood, in the form of a request for a miracle |
| **Of a kind that will not befit anyone after me** | What is asked for is a kingdom of a quality that would not be fitting in another's hands — that is, a qualification about its nature, not a wish that it be given to no one |
| **That no one can seize** | A kingdom whose firmness is guaranteed, that no one afterwards can usurp and take over |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But an observation can be recorded, and it rests on the wording:** the **first** request of the prayer is forgiveness, and its **last** sentence is *innaka anta'l-Wahhāb*. **The request, that is, is enclosed by a qualification on both sides:** before it a plea for pardon, after it the name of the giver.

**And this note joins with the ninth verse of the sūra:** there the other side was asked "are the treasuries of mercy with you?" **Here a prophet asks, knowing that the treasury is not with him.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses use the same name:** the question in the ninth verse was a question of authority. **The prayer in the thirty-fifth verse is a prophet's answer to that question: the authority is not mine but Yours — and that is why I ask.**

**The difference between asking and laying claim stands between these two verses.**

## 38/36-40 — فَسَخَّرْنَا لَهُ ٱلرِّيحَ تَجْرِى بِأَمْرِهِۦ رُخَآءً حَيْثُ أَصَابَ · وَٱلشَّيَٰطِينَ كُلَّ بَنَّآءٍ وَغَوَّاصٍ · وَءَاخَرِينَ مُقَرَّنِينَ فِى ٱلْأَصْفَادِ · هَٰذَا عَطَآؤُنَا فَٱمْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ · وَإِنَّ لَهُۥ عِندَنَا لَزُلْفَىٰ وَحُسْنَ مَـَٔابٍ

*Fa-sakhkharnā lahu'r-rīḥa tajrī bi-amrihī rukhāʾan ḥaythu aṣāb · Wa'sh-shayāṭīna kulla bannāʾin wa ghawwāṣ · Wa ākharīna muqarranīna fi'l-aṣfād · Hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā fa'mnun aw amsik bi-ghayri ḥisāb · Wa inna lahū ʿindanā la-zulfā wa ḥusna maʾāb*

"**And We made the wind subservient to him:** it ran at his command, **gently**, wherever he directed. **And the satans too** — every builder and diver — **and others bound together in fetters.** '**This is Our giving; so bestow or withhold — without reckoning.**' And with Us he has a **nearness** and **a good place of return**."

### فَسَخَّرْنَا لَهُ ٱلرِّيحَ — and the answer to the eighteenth verse

**The same verb (*sakhkharnā*) was used of David in the eighteenth verse: *sakhkharna'l-jibāla maʿahū*.**

**The difference between the two sentences lies in the particles, and it is fine:**

| Verse | Expression | Particle | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18** | *sakhkharna'l-jibāla **maʿahū*** | **مَعَ** — with | The mountains give praise **together with** David |
| **36** | *sakhkharnā **lahu**'r-rīḥ* | **لَ** — for | The wind is **at Solomon's command** |

**This is a difference that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** what is given to David is a work done together; what is given to Solomon is a power tied to command. **Both are *taskhīr*; the form of the relation differs.**

### رُخَآءً — gently

**Root ر-خ-و:** to be soft, to be slack. *Rikhwa* — slackness; *rakhāʾ* — breadth, plenty (a relative from the same root).

**The choice of the word is worth recording.** In the Qurʾān the wind is mostly named with force and violence (*rīḥan ṣarṣarā* — Fuṣṣilat 41/16; al-Qamar 54/19). **Here the opposite epithet: *rukhāʾ* — gentle.**

**I record this as my own reading:** this conveys that the power given is **controlled.** The wind is at his command, but not harsh. **The power is given in a consistency that will not batter what it carries.**

**حَيْثُ أَصَابَ — root ص-و-ب:** to hit a thing, to find the right target. *Ṣawāb* — that which hits the mark; *muṣība* — that which strikes (in the negative direction).

**The phrase is rendered "wherever he wished"; but the root of the word is not "he wished" but "he hit the mark".** The lexicographers record that in this use the verb *aṣāba* carries the sense "to aim at, to make for".

### وَٱلشَّيَٰطِينَ كُلَّ بَنَّآءٍ وَغَوَّاصٍ — two works

**Two names of trades are given, and both are on the *faʿʿāl* pattern — that is, the pattern that conveys a trade:**

| Word | Root | Work | Its direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **بَنَّاء** | ب-ن-ي | **Builder** | **Upward** — construction |
| **غَوَّاص** | غ-و-ص | **Diver** | **Downward** — diving |

**I record this as an observation:** two works, two opposite directions. **One raises up, the other goes down.** And both are works of **extraction**: the one takes stone from its place and sets it in a building, the other brings something up from the bottom of the sea.

**غَاصَ — root غ-و-ص:** to dive into water. The lexicographers record that the word is used in particular for **bringing up pearls.**

### مُقَرَّنِينَ فِى ٱلْأَصْفَادِ — those bound in fetters

**مُقَرَّنِين — root ق-ر-ن:** to bind two things to one another, to bring them side by side. **A passive participle of Form II: bound together.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to yoke two animals to the same yoke.** *Qarīn* — the one bound alongside; this word was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/23 and 27.

**أَصْفَاد — its singular *ṣafad*. Root ص-ف-د:** a tie, a chain, a shackle. **The lexicographers describe the *ṣafad* as "a bond that ties the hands to the neck".**

**And three groups are counted:**

| Group | What it does |
|---|---|
| The wind | It carries |
| The satans (builder, diver) | **They work** |
| The other satans | **They are bound** |

**I record this as my own reading:** the power given is not only the power to make things serve but also the power to **hold in check.** Some of them work, some are held fast. **There is an order inside the kingdom, that is: not everything is loose.**

### هَٰذَا عَطَآؤُنَا فَٱمْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ — the condition of the giving

**And now the answer to the prayer of the thirty-fifth verse comes.**

**عَطَاء — root ع-ط-و:** to give, to hold out. *Aʿṭā* — he gave. **The concrete sense of the root is *handing a thing from hand to hand*.**

**فَٱمْنُنْ — root م-ن-ن.** And the word has two branches:

| Branch | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **To do a kindness, to make a gift** | *Manna ʿalayhi* — he showed him favour |
| **To cast a gift in someone's face** | *Mann* — to throw what one has given in another's face |

**The sense here is the first: "give, bestow".** The Qurʾān uses the second sense too, and forbids it (al-Baqara 2/264 — *lā tubṭilū ṣadaqātikum bi'l-manni wa'l-adhā*).

**أَمْسِكْ — root م-س-ك:** to hold, to keep in hand, not to let go.

**The two commands are opposites: *imnun* (give) and *amsik* (withhold). And both are left free: "bestow or withhold."**

**بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ — "without reckoning".** And the construct has been understood in two ways:

| View | What it attaches to | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **To the giving/withholding** | *fa'mnun aw amsik bi-ghayri ḥisāb* | "You will not be called to account for what you do" — you are free in this disposal |
| **To the *ʿaṭāʾ*** | *hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā… bi-ghayri ḥisāb* | "This giving is without limit" — it conveys the abundance of what is given |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And as was tabulated above, this is one of the four occurrences of the word *ḥisāb* in the sūra, and the only positive one.** In the sixteenth verse the day was wanted brought forward, in the twenty-sixth it had been forgotten, in the fifty-third it will be the address of a promise. **Here, however, it is lifted.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sūra's central word (*reckoning*) is suspended inside an authority given to a prophet. **And this must be read together with the qualification placed on David in the twenty-sixth verse** — there the authority to judge was bound to a measure (*bi'l-ḥaqq*, *lā tattabiʿi'l-hawā*); **here the authority to give is left free.**

| Verse | Who | Authority | Qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **26** | David | **To judge** | *bi'l-ḥaqq* — **bound** |
| **39** | Solomon | **To give/withhold** | *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* — **unbound** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of the two verses:** two different authorities are given to two prophets, and the one is tightly bound, the other free. **And the difference comes from whom the authority touches:** a judgment touches another man's right, a gift comes out of one's own portion.

### وَإِنَّ لَهُۥ عِندَنَا لَزُلْفَىٰ وَحُسْنَ مَـَٔابٍ

**It is the exact repetition of the twenty-fifth verse. It was tabulated above.**

**And that the two accounts close with the same sentence must be recorded:** the one after a test and a forgiveness, the other after a kingdom and an authority. **What was given differs; the place arrived at is the same.**

## 38/41-44 — وَٱذْكُرْ عَبْدَنَآ أَيُّوبَ إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُۥٓ أَنِّى مَسَّنِىَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ بِنُصْبٍ وَعَذَابٍ · ٱرْكُضْ بِرِجْلِكَ هَٰذَا مُغْتَسَلٌۢ بَارِدٌ وَشَرَابٌ · وَوَهَبْنَا لَهُۥٓ أَهْلَهُۥ وَمِثْلَهُم مَّعَهُمْ رَحْمَةً مِّنَّا وَذِكْرَىٰ لِأُو۟لِى ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ · وَخُذْ بِيَدِكَ ضِغْثًا فَٱضْرِب بِّهِۦ وَلَا تَحْنَثْ إِنَّا وَجَدْنَٰهُ صَابِرًا نِّعْمَ ٱلْعَبْدُ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ

*Wa'dhkur ʿabdanā Ayyūba idh nādā rabbahū annī massaniya'sh-shayṭānu bi-nuṣbin wa ʿadhāb · Urkuḍ bi-rijlika, hādhā mughtasalun bāridun wa sharāb · Wa wahabnā lahū ahlahū wa mithlahum maʿahum raḥmatan minnā wa dhikrā li-uli'l-albāb · Wa khudh bi-yadika ḍighthan fa'ḍrib bihī wa lā taḥnath, innā wajadnāhu ṣābirā, niʿma'l-ʿabd, innahū awwāb*

"**And remember our servant Job.** When he called to his Lord: '**Satan has touched me with weariness and torment.**' '**Strike the ground with your foot: here is a cool washing-place and a drink.**' **And We bestowed on him his family and the like of them with them** — as a mercy from Us and an admonition for those who have a kernel. '**Take a handful of twigs in your hand, strike with it, and do not break your oath.**' **We found him patient. How excellent a servant! He was one who turned back often.**"

### Two commands, two prophets

**For the third time the sūra says *wa'dhkur ʿabdanā*. And what distinguishes this account from the earlier ones is that it begins not with what was given but with what was taken away.**

| Account | What stands at the opening |
|---|---|
| **David (17)** | *dha'l-ayd* — **strength** |
| **Solomon (30)** | *wa wahabnā* — **a bestowal** |
| **Job (41)** | *massaniya'sh-shayṭānu bi-nuṣbin wa ʿadhāb* — **an affliction** |

**I record this as my own reading:** in the first two accounts a test came after what was given; in the third the test stands at the beginning. **The sūra shows that the same epithet (*awwāb*) is the same in all three states.**

### إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُۥ — the manner of the calling

**نَادَىٰ — root ن-د-ي:** to call in a loud voice. **And this root occurred in the third verse:** *fa-nādaw wa-lāta ḥīna manāṣ* — "they cried out, but it was no longer the hour of escape."

| Verse | Who | When | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3** | The generations destroyed | **After the hour had passed** | *wa-lāta ḥīna manāṣ* |
| **41** | Job | **While the affliction lasted** | An answer comes (42) |

**This is a shared wording that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the same verb in two places, with two opposite outcomes. **What divides them is not the cry itself but its time.** The third verse set up a question of **hour**; the forty-first shows the other face of that question.

### مَسَّنِىَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ بِنُصْبٍ وَعَذَابٍ — the wording of the complaint

**مَسَّ — root م-س-س:** to touch. **And the distinguishing side of the word is its lightness:** *mass* is to come into contact with a thing — not to strike, not to hit.

**نُصْب — root ن-ص-ب.** This word was treated in `088-gasiye.md` at 88/3 and in `094-insirah.md`, and this verse was named there: *"Satan has touched me with weariness and torment"* (Ṣād 38/41).

**The concrete sense of the root: to set a thing up, to hold it upright.** *Naṣb* — a standing stone; *manṣib* — the place where a person is set up. **And from there: *naṣab/nuṣb* — to grow weary, exhaustion.** The link the lexicographers make is this: **standing upright for a long time wearies.**

The word conveys not "pain", that is, but **exhaustion**.

| Word | What |
|---|---|
| **نُصْب** | **Exhaustion, being spent** — the withdrawal of strength |
| **عَذَاب** | **Distress, affliction** — what comes from outside |

**The two words together take in two directions: the exhaustion within and the distress without.**

### To whom the complaint is made and to what it is ascribed

**And here there is a construction that must be read with care.**

**Job ascribes the affliction to Satan: *massaniya'sh-shayṭān*.** But the one he **calls to** is his Lord: *nādā rabbah*.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the separation of the subjects of the sentence:**

| Element | Who |
|---|---|
| The one called to | **The Lord** |
| The one the affliction is ascribed to | **Satan** |

**There is no reproach in the sentence, that is.** Job calls to his Lord but does not ascribe the affliction to Him. **And this prepares the verdict in the forty-fourth verse (*innā wajadnāhu ṣābirā*).**

**Satan's role here has been disputed in the classical commentaries, and the disagreement is real:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Whispering** | Satan's touch is not the sickness but the whispering he gave during the sickness |
| **The distress itself** | An effect of Satan's, by God's leave, is in question; the verse says this in its wording |
| **Ascription out of courtesy** | Job ascribed the affliction, out of courtesy, to Satan rather than to his Lord |

**I convey all three and do not impose a preference.** **And in this discussion I do not enter into details resting on report — the kind of the illness, its duration, what befell his family;** the verse says none of these things.

### ٱرْكُضْ بِرِجْلِكَ — the form of the answer

**رَكَضَ — root ر-ك-ض.** Its concrete sense: **to strike with the foot, to kick.** The lexicographers record that the word is used in particular for **a rider's striking with his foot to spur his horse.** From here comes the sense "to run, to go swiftly" as well (al-Anbiyāʾ 21/12 — *idhā hum minhā yarkuḍūn*).

**And the character of the command must be recorded: an *act* is asked for.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the answer is not a sentence of glad tidings but a **command to move.** A sick and exhausted man is told to strike the ground with his foot. **The healing, that is, comes after the person's own movement.**

**And this is a pattern that has a parallel in the Qurʾān:** Mary too is given a command — *wa huzzī ilayki bi-jidhʿi'n-nakhlati tusāqiṭ ʿalayki ruṭaban janiyyā* (Maryam 19/25): she is asked to shake the trunk of the palm towards herself. **In both places a small act is asked for in a situation that looks impossible.** This link is one I make; the verses do not refer to one another.

### هَٰذَا مُغْتَسَلٌۢ بَارِدٌ وَشَرَابٌ — what is given

**مُغْتَسَل — root غ-س-ل:** to wash. **A noun of place or of instrument of Form VIII: a place to wash in, or the thing one washes with (water).**

**بَارِد — root ب-ر-د:** cold, cool.

**And two uses are given: to wash and to drink.**

| Use | To what |
|---|---|
| *mughtasal* | **Outward** — the body |
| *sharāb* | **Inward** — drinking |

**I record this as an observation:** in the forty-first verse the affliction was given with two words (*nuṣb* — exhaustion from within, *ʿadhāb* — distress from without). **In the forty-second the answer comes in a pair as well: drinking and washing.** Two words fall to two afflictions.

**And the epithet *bārid* must be recorded:** in the fifty-seventh verse of the sūra the drink given to those in the Fire will be *ḥamīm* (boiling water). **Two drinks in the same sūra: the one cool, the other boiling.** This is a contrast that can be verified from the text.

### وَوَهَبْنَا لَهُۥٓ أَهْلَهُۥ وَمِثْلَهُم مَّعَهُمْ

**And the root و-ه-ب comes for the fourth and last time.** It was tabulated above: *al-Wahhāb* (9) → *wahabnā* (30) → *hab lī* (35) → *wahabnā* (43).

**وَمِثْلَهُم مَّعَهُمْ — "and the like of them with them".**

**The exegetes explain this expression in two ways:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **His family was given back and as many again were added** | An increase in number |
| **His family and their offspring** | The later generations are included as well |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**رَحْمَةً مِّنَّا وَذِكْرَىٰ لِأُو۟لِى ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ — and two grounds are given:**

| Ground | To whom |
|---|---|
| *raḥmatan **minnā*** | **To Job** — the event itself |
| *wa **dhikrā** li-uli'l-albāb* | **To the reader** — the telling of the event |

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse gives both the reason the event was lived and the reason it is **told.** **And the second ground uses exactly the same words as the ground for the Book's being sent down in the twenty-ninth verse:** *wa li-yatadhakkara **ulu'l-albāb***. It was tabulated above.

### وَخُذْ بِيَدِكَ ضِغْثًا فَٱضْرِب بِّهِۦ وَلَا تَحْنَثْ — the question of the oath

**ضِغْث — root ض-غ-ث.** And the lexicographers describe the word thus: **a bundle of mixed grass, twigs or stalks, as much as a hand can grasp.** A handful. There is a **mixedness** and a **being held together** at the root of the word; from the same root *aḍghāthu aḥlām* — confused dreams (Yūsuf 12/44).

**تَحْنَثْ — root ح-ن-ث.** Its concrete sense: **a sin, a grave offence; and the breaking of an oath.** *Ḥinth* — to break an oath. **The original sense at the root of the word is "a heavy burden, a sin"**, and it passed to this meaning because the breaking of an oath was counted a sin.

**Now the disagreement. The verse gives a command but does not state the background of the command (what the oath was).**

| Question | Views |
|---|---|
| **What the oath was** | The verse does not say. It is transmitted in the classical commentaries that an oath had been sworn and that this command was its fulfilment; **I do not enter into the narratives about the content of the oath, because they have no support from the text** |
| **What is to be struck** | The verse does not say. The person or thing to be struck is not named in the wording |
| **What the *ḍighth* is** | (1) A handful of grass / a bundle of thin twigs — **the description of the majority of the lexicographers**; (2) A bundle of ears of grain; (3) Any mixed handful held together |
| **The scope of the ruling** | (1) **It is a dispensation particular to Job**, from which no general ruling is derived; (2) **It is a general dispensation**: the wording of the oath is counted fulfilled by a single collective stroke. This verse has been discussed in the literature of jurisprudence and there is a difference of view among the schools. **I issue no legal ruling; I confine myself to recording that the disagreement exists** |

**In this question I certainly do not impose a preference, and my reason is this: the verse does not state two of the three elements of the matter (the content of the oath and the one it concerns).** Where the text is silent I am silent.

**The only thing verifiable in the wording is this: there is an oath, it is asked that it not be broken, and the manner of its fulfilment has been made lighter.**

**I record this as an observation:** the choice of the word *ḍighth* — a handful of thin twigs — conveys **lightness.** **What is recounted in the verse, that is, is not the cancellation of an obligation but the lessening of its weight.** The oath stands; the manner of carrying it changes.

### إِنَّا وَجَدْنَٰهُ صَابِرًا — patience as a verdict

**And now the sūra's chain of patience closes. It was tabulated above; here I complete it.**

| Verse | Who | Mood | Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | The leading men of Mecca | **Command** | *ʿalā ālihatikum* |
| **17** | God → to the envoy | **Command** | *ʿalā mā yaqūlūn* |
| **44** | God → about Job | **Verdict** | — |

**Two commands and one verdict. And the form of the verdict must be recorded: *innā wajadnāhu ṣābirā* — "we found him patient."**

**وَجَدَ — root و-ج-د:** to find. **The verb is the pattern that announces the outcome of a test:** something sought has been found where it was sought.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** the verse does not say "he was patient"; it says **"we found him patient."** **Patience, that is, is given not as something claimed but as something established after a testing.**

**And this must be read together with the forty-first verse.** Job had complained — *massaniya'sh-shayṭānu bi-nuṣbin wa ʿadhāb*. **To have spoken one's affliction, that is, is no bar to being found patient.**

**I record this plainly as my own inference, because it corrects a widespread misunderstanding:** the patience the verse describes is not silence. **Job speaks, takes his affliction to his Lord, receives an answer — and is found patient.** No contradiction is set up between these two in the verse.

**نِّعْمَ ٱلْعَبْدُ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ** — the exact repetition of the thirtieth verse. **Two prophets (Solomon and Job) close with the same two sentences.**

## 38/45-47 — وَٱذْكُرْ عِبَٰدَنَآ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَإِسْحَٰقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ أُو۟لِى ٱلْأَيْدِى وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرِ · إِنَّآ أَخْلَصْنَٰهُم بِخَالِصَةٍ ذِكْرَى ٱلدَّارِ · وَإِنَّهُمْ عِندَنَا لَمِنَ ٱلْمُصْطَفَيْنَ ٱلْأَخْيَارِ

*Wa'dhkur ʿibādanā Ibrāhīma wa Isḥāqa wa Yaʿqūba uli'l-aydī wa'l-abṣār · Innā akhlaṣnāhum bi-khāliṣatin dhikra'd-dār · Wa innahum ʿindanā la-mina'l-muṣṭafayna'l-akhyār*

"**And remember our servants Abraham, Isaac and Jacob — those possessed of hands and eyes.** We purified them with a pure thing — **the remembrance of the abode**. With Us they are **among the chosen, the best.**"

### The passage to a list

**The sūra passes from three accounts told one by one to a list of names.** And the transition must be recorded:

| Section | Who | Manner of telling |
|---|---|---|
| 17-26 | David | **A scene** — one event |
| 30-40 | Solomon | **A scene** — two events |
| 41-44 | Job | **A scene** — one event |
| **45-48** | **Six names** | **A list** — no event, an attribute |

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra drops the detail and quickens. And in doing so it announces that **those not recounted belong to the same class.** **The matter, that is, is not the accounts themselves but the attribute the accounts display.**

**And *ʿibādanā* is plural** — in the three previous places it was singular (*ʿabdanā*). **This is a change of pattern that can be verified.**

### أُو۟لِى ٱلْأَيْدِى وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرِ — hand and eye

**And this construct is one of the densest expressions in the sūra.**

**أَيْدِى — its singular *yad*: hand. Root ي-د-ي.**

**And it must not be confused with the *dha'l-ayd* of the seventeenth verse.** It was tabulated above: there the root is أ-ي-د (strength), here ي-د-ي (hand). **The difference is visible in the writing: here there is a *yāʾ* at the end.**

**أَبْصَار — its singular *baṣar*: sight, the eye. Root ب-ص-ر.** It was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/22 and in `067-mulk.md`.

**And the construct is figurative; the exegetes agree on this:**

| Limb | What it stands for | The picture of the root |
|---|---|---|
| **يَد** — hand | **Strength, doing, deed** | What is held in the hand has been obtained |
| **بَصَر** — eye | ***Baṣīra*, insight, right seeing** | What is seen has been known |

**Some exegetes have read *aydī* here too as "strength" (the root أ-ي-د).** The two readings meet in sense; **I do not impose a preference.**

**Now I come to the work of the construct, and I record it as my own reading.**

**The naming of the two together sets up a completion:**

| Alone | What happens |
|---|---|
| **The hand alone** | To do without seeing — **strength with no direction** |
| **The eye alone** | To see without doing — **knowledge with no answering act** |

**The construct gives the presence of the two together as the quality of a class.**

**And this can be linked with the polemic at the beginning of the sūra, and the link is one I make:** in the objections between the fourth and eighth verses there was neither hand nor eye. **To be astonished, to insinuate, to say "we have not heard" — none of these is either a work or a seeing.** The forty-fifth verse offers the other side a measure: by what is a person named?

**And these two limbs will meet us once more in the sūra — this time in the opposite direction:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **45** | *uli'l-**aydī** wa'l-**abṣār*** | Three prophets — **those who possess them** |
| **63** | *am zāghat ʿanhumu'l-**abṣār*** | Those in the Fire — **those whose eyes have swerved** |
| **75** | *limā khalaqtu **bi-yadayy*** | God — **what I created with My two hands** |

**Three occurrences, in three different places, and all three around the same two limbs.** I record this as an observation about a pattern.

### أَخْلَصْنَٰهُم بِخَالِصَةٍ — purifying

**خَلَصَ — root خ-ل-ص.** Its concrete sense: **a thing's being separated from other things and becoming pure.** *Khāliṣ* — that which has no admixture; the lexicographers use the word of milk and of butter: **separated from its dregs, clear.**

**And the verb is in Form IV: *akhlaṣa* — he purified, he made pure.**

**In the sentence the verbal noun and the verb come together: *akhlaṣnāhum bi-khāliṣa* — emphasis.**

**And what the *khāliṣa* is, is said at once: *dhikra'd-dār*.**

**A variant reading is transmitted:** when *bi-khāliṣatin* is read with tanwīn, *dhikra'd-dār* becomes its explanation; when it is read as a construct (*bi-khāliṣati dhikra'd-dār*), the sense "the purity of remembering the abode" comes out. **The difference of sense is not great; because I cannot state with certainty which of the imams of the readings held which preference, I give no names.**

### ذِكْرَى ٱلدَّارِ — "the remembrance of the abode"

**And the construct is short but dense.**

**ٱلدَّار — root د-و-ر:** to turn, to revolve. *Dār* — **an enclosed place, a house, an abode.** The picture in the root is that a thing's surroundings are encircled.

**And the word is definite: *al-dār* — "that abode".** Which abode is not stated, but the construct *al-dāru'l-ākhira* (the abode of the hereafter) is settled in the Qurʾān and the majority of the exegetes understand that here.

| View | What *al-dār* is | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **The abode of the hereafter** | The place to be arrived at | The settled usage in the Qurʾān; and the density of the word *maʾāb* in the sūra |
| **Simply "the abode"** | The place of arrival in general | The word's being left indefinite |

**The first view is widespread; I do not impose a preference.**

**And *dhikrā* — from the root ذ-ك-ر, the backbone word of the sūra.** It was tabulated above: this is the sixth occurrence of the root.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the construction of the word:** the content of the three prophets' having been "purified" is not a list of deeds but **a remembering**. **In the verse, that is, the definition of purity is the continual keeping in mind of the place to be arrived at.**

**And this joins with the hand-and-eye pair of the forty-fifth verse:** the hand does, the eye sees, **and the remembrance sets the direction.** The three together give how a person is described.

### ٱلْمُصْطَفَيْنَ ٱلْأَخْيَارِ — two epithets

**مُصْطَفَىٰ — root ص-ف-و:** to be pure, to be clear. **Form VIII: *iṣṭafā* — to pick out and select, to take the best of a thing.** And the root is of the same family as *khāliṣ*: **both have to do with purity.**

**In the forty-sixth verse *akhlaṣnāhum*, that is, and in the forty-seventh *al-muṣṭafayn* — the same idea said with two roots.**

**أَخْيَار — its singular *khayr* or *khayyir*.** Root خ-ي-ر: that which is good, that which is **chosen.** And the original sense of the root is **choice**: *ikhtiyār* — to choose. **What is "good", that is, is what is worth choosing.**

**Worth recording:** three words — *khāliṣ*, *muṣṭafā*, *akhyār* — **all three convey a work of *choosing and sifting*.** Three separate roots, one picture.

## 38/48 — وَٱذْكُرْ إِسْمَٰعِيلَ وَٱلْيَسَعَ وَذَا ٱلْكِفْلِ وَكُلٌّ مِّنَ ٱلْأَخْيَارِ

*Wa'dhkur Ismāʿīla wa'l-Yasaʿa wa Dha'l-Kifl, wa kullun mina'l-akhyār*

"**And remember Ishmael, Elisha and Dhū'l-Kifl. All are of the best.**"

### Three names

**The fourth and last *wa'dhkur* command of the sūra.** And this time there is no note of *ʿabdanā / ʿibādanā* — the names are given directly.

**ٱلْيَسَع — Elisha.** He occurs in two places in the Qurʾān: here and al-Anʿām 6/86. **In both he is inside a list.**

**ذَا ٱلْكِفْل — Dhū'l-Kifl.** He occurs in two places in the Qurʾān: here and al-Anbiyāʾ 21/85. **In the occurrence in al-Anbiyāʾ he is named together with Ishmael and Idrīs and is described as *mina'ṣ-ṣābirīn* (among the patient).**

**The meaning of the name is dwelt on: *kifl* — root ك-ف-ل.** And this root occurred in the twenty-third verse: *akfilnīhā* — "make her over to me, put her in my charge."

**The senses of the root: a portion, a share; and taking upon oneself, standing surety.**

| View | What *Dhū'l-Kifl* means |
|---|---|
| **Possessor of a portion** | One given a double share |
| **The one who takes on** | One who takes a responsibility upon himself, who gives his word and keeps it |

**Various views about his identity are transmitted in the classical sources, and there is no agreement among them.** **I do not go into the detail:** the Qurʾān gives no information about this person beyond his name and two descriptions of him (*mina'l-akhyār* here, *mina'ṣ-ṣābirīn* in al-Anbiyāʾ 21/85). **Where the text is silent I am silent.**

### وَكُلٌّ مِّنَ ٱلْأَخْيَارِ — the repeated close

**The forty-seventh verse ended with *al-muṣṭafayna'l-akhyār*; the forty-eighth ends with *mina'l-akhyār*.**

**The same word, in two consecutive verses.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it serves as a seal closing the section on the prophets.

**And *kullun* — "all".** No distinction is made, that is: the one whose account is told and the one whose name alone occurs are in the same class.

### A summing-up of the section on the prophets

**From the seventeenth verse to the forty-eighth, nine prophets are named in thirty-two verses:** David, Solomon, Job, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Ishmael, Elisha, Dhū'l-Kifl.

**And the structure of the section shows a narrowing:**

| Verses | How many persons | How many verses | Per person |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17-26 | 1 (David) | 10 | 10 |
| 30-40 | 1 (Solomon) | 11 | 11 |
| 41-44 | 1 (Job) | 4 | 4 |
| 45-47 | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| 48 | 3 | 1 | **1/3** |

**This is a countable fact.** And I record it as my own reading: **the section ends by quickening.** The first three accounts are detailed, the last six names are almost a single breath. **The telling closes by turning into a list — and immediately afterwards, in the forty-ninth verse, the whole section is named with a single word: *hādhā dhikr*.**

**And no list of miracles is given for any of the nine names.** For David the mountains and the birds are named, for Solomon the wind and the jinn — but these are not the attributes counted at the close of the section: **hand, eye, remembrance of the abode, chosenness, goodness.**

## 38/49-54 — هَٰذَا ذِكْرٌ وَإِنَّ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ لَحُسْنَ مَـَٔابٍ · جَنَّٰتِ عَدْنٍ مُّفَتَّحَةً لَّهُمُ ٱلْأَبْوَٰبُ · مُتَّكِـِٔينَ فِيهَا يَدْعُونَ فِيهَا بِفَٰكِهَةٍ كَثِيرَةٍ وَشَرَابٍ · وَعِندَهُمْ قَٰصِرَٰتُ ٱلطَّرْفِ أَتْرَابٌ · هَٰذَا مَا تُوعَدُونَ لِيَوْمِ ٱلْحِسَابِ · إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَرِزْقُنَا مَا لَهُۥ مِن نَّفَادٍ

*Hādhā dhikr, wa inna li'l-muttaqīna la-ḥusna maʾāb · Jannāti ʿadnin mufattaḥatan lahumu'l-abwāb · Muttakiʾīna fīhā yadʿūna fīhā bi-fākihatin kathīratin wa sharāb · Wa ʿindahum qāṣirātu'ṭ-ṭarfi atrāb · Hādhā mā tūʿadūna li-yawmi'l-ḥisāb · Inna hādhā la-rizqunā mā lahū min nafād*

"**This is a reminder.** And for the God-fearing there is surely **a good place of return**: the gardens of ʿAdn, **their gates opened wide to them.** Reclining there, they **call for abundant fruit and drink.** Beside them are **spouses of modest gaze, of like age.** '**This is what you are promised for the Day of Reckoning.**' **This is Our provision; it has no exhausting.**"

### هَٰذَا ذِكْرٌ — the naming of the section

**Two words, and they change the whole structure of the sūra.**

**As was tabulated above, the root ذ-ك-ر is the backbone of the sūra.** And here the root **names thirty-two verses of narrative with a single word.**

| Verse | Expression | What it names |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *wa'l-Qurʾāni **dhi'dh-dhikr*** | **The whole Book** |
| **49** | *hādhā **dhikr*** | **The section on the prophets** |
| **87** | *in huwa illā **dhikrun** li'l-ʿālamīn* | **The whole Book, again** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the ordering of these three occurrences:** the sūra gives to a section within itself the name it gives to its own Book. **The accounts, that is, turn out to have been told not to give information but to remind** — and the forty-third verse had already said this (*wa dhikrā li-uli'l-albāb*).

**And the word *hādhā* here is the first link of the "the demonstrative changes hands" strand tabulated above.** In the fifth, sixth and seventh verses the other side had used *hādhā* to reject. **From the forty-ninth verse onward the same word comes six times in the mouth of the text.**

### لِلْمُتَّقِينَ لَحُسْنَ مَـَٔابٍ — the widening promise

**It was tabulated above:** the same construct was used of David in the twenty-fifth verse and of Solomon in the fortieth. **Here it opens out to a class.**

**And the difference must be recorded:**

| Verse | Who | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| 25, 40 | David, Solomon | *inna **lahū ʿindanā** la-zulfā wa ḥusna maʾāb* |
| **49** | The God-fearing | *inna **li'l-muttaqīna** la-ḥusna maʾāb* |

**The notes *ʿindanā* (with Us) and *zulfā* (nearness) are absent here;** *ḥusna maʾāb* stands exactly as it was.

**I record this as an observation; it shows that what is peculiar to two prophets and what is open to everyone are kept apart within the text.**

### جَنَّٰتِ عَدْنٍ — the gardens of ʿAdn

**عَدْن — root ع-د-ن:** to **stay, to settle, not to leave** a place. *Maʿdin* is from the same root — the place where a substance is found lodged in the earth.

***ʿAdn*, that is, is not a place-name but a quality: permanence.**

**This construct occurred in `040-gafir.md` at 40/8** (*jannāti ʿadnini'llatī waʿadtahum*) **and was there named together with spouses and children; the note in the discussion of `056-vakia.md` was relied on.** I rely on that.

### مُّفَتَّحَةً لَّهُمُ ٱلْأَبْوَٰبُ — the opened gates

**فُتِّحَ — root ف-ت-ح:** to open. **Form II (*tafʿīl*) here conveys multiplicity: all the gates, opened one by one.**

**And the construction is dwelt on:** *mufattaḥatan lahumu'l-abwāb* — "the gates being opened to them". **The gates come at the end of the sentence.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence does not set up a scene of **waiting** or of **asking leave**. The gates have been opened beforehand. **And this can be compared with the scene in the twenty-first verse:** there two litigants entered a place **by climbing the wall**, because they could not enter by the door.

| Verse | Place | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | The miḥrāb | **Over the wall** — the door was not used |
| **50** | The Garden | **The gates are open** — there is no need to climb |

**This link is one I make; the sūra does not make it separately.** But the two scenes are inside the same sūra, and in both an **entry** is being recounted.

### مُتَّكِـِٔينَ — reclining

**Root و-ك-أ:** to lean on a thing, to recline. **Form VIII: *ittakaʾa* — he reclined.**

**This word was treated in `056-vakia.md` and `076-insan.md` in descriptions of the Garden.** I rely on that.

**What is to be recorded is what the posture conveys: to recline is the posture of being safe and unhurried.**

### يَدْعُونَ فِيهَا بِفَٰكِهَةٍ كَثِيرَةٍ وَشَرَابٍ — asking

**دَعَا — root د-ع-و:** to call, to ask for.

**And the choice of verb must be recorded:** it does not say "it is given to them", it says **"they ask"**. **A state of demand, that is, is being described, not a passivity.**

**And two things are asked for: *fākiha* (fruit) and *sharāb* (drink).**

**فَاكِهَة — root ف-ك-ه.** And the distinguishing side of the root is recorded by the lexicographers: **from the same root *fukāha* — jesting, pleasant conversation.** What makes the link is that both are **not a basic need but a pleasure.** **Fruit is eaten not to fill the belly but for its taste.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the things counted in the verse are not necessities. **In the description of the Garden hunger is not being relieved; pleasantness is being named.**

**And the word *sharāb* had occurred in the forty-second verse too — the cool drink given to Job.** This is a verbal repetition that can be verified.

### قَٰصِرَٰتُ ٱلطَّرْفِ أَتْرَابٌ

**This construct was treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/35-37, and this verse (Ṣād 38/52) was named there:** *"In Ṣād 38/52 the same word occurs again in a description of the Garden, and so it does in al-Nabaʾ 78/33. The word is settled in this context, that is."* **I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**The summary is this: قَاصِرَات — root ق-ص-ر: to shorten, to limit a thing.** *Ṭarf* — the corner of the eye, the glance. **The construct: those who have limited their glance** — those, that is, whose eye is not on another.

**أَتْرَاب — its singular *tirb*.** Root ت-ر-ب — the same root as **earth** (*turāb*). The lexicographers make the link thus: **those who grew up playing in the same dust at the same time — that is, those of like age.**

**The picture at the root of the word is having grown up together.**

### هَٰذَا مَا تُوعَدُونَ لِيَوْمِ ٱلْحِسَابِ — the shift in the direction of address

**And there is an *iltifāt* (a shift in the direction of address) in the construction:** from forty-nine to fifty-two the telling was in the third person (*lahum*, *ʿindahum*); **in the fifty-third verse it turns suddenly to the second: *tūʿadūn* — "what you are promised".**

**I record this as my own reading:** the scene being told stops being told and is set before the one addressed. **And this stands at the very middle of the sūra's *hādhā* strand.**

**ٱلْحِسَاب — the fourth and last occurrence.** It was tabulated above.

### إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَرِزْقُنَا مَا لَهُۥ مِن نَّفَادٍ — inexhaustibility

**نَفَاد — root ن-ف-د:** to be exhausted, to run out, to come to an end. *Nafida'l-māʾ* — the water ran out.

**And the pattern of negation is strong: *mā lahū min nafād* — with *min* intervening.** In Arabic this generalises the negation **as to kind**: "it has no exhausting whatever."

**And that the word *rizq* was chosen must be recorded. Root ر-ز-ق:** what is given to a living thing, what it takes benefit from. **The original sense of the root is the giving of a portion.**

**I record this as an observation, and it is a link within the sūra:** in the thirty-ninth verse it was said to Solomon *hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā* ("this is Our giving") and the note *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* (without reckoning) was entered. **In the fifty-fourth verse it says *hādhā la-rizqunā* ("this is Our provision") and the note *mā lahū min nafād* (it has no exhausting) is entered.**

| Verse | Expression | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **39** | *hādhā **ʿaṭāʾunā*** | *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* — **beyond counting** |
| **54** | *inna hādhā la-**rizqunā*** | *mā lahū min nafād* — **inexhaustible** |

**The two sentences are on the same pattern** (*hādhā* + a first-person plural possessive + a negative note of limit). **This is an equivalence that can be verified from the text.**

## 38/55-58 — هَٰذَا وَإِنَّ لِلطَّٰغِينَ لَشَرَّ مَـَٔابٍ · جَهَنَّمَ يَصْلَوْنَهَا فَبِئْسَ ٱلْمِهَادُ · هَٰذَا فَلْيَذُوقُوهُ حَمِيمٌ وَغَسَّاقٌ · وَءَاخَرُ مِن شَكْلِهِۦٓ أَزْوَٰجٌ

*Hādhā, wa inna li'l-ṭāghīna la-sharra maʾāb · Jahannama yaṣlawnahā fa-biʾsa'l-mihād · Hādhā fa'l-yadhūqūhu ḥamīmun wa ghassāq · Wa ākharu min shaklihī azwāj*

"**This.** And for the transgressors there is surely **an evil place of return**: Hell. They enter it; **how evil a bed it is!** **Here it is — let them taste it: boiling water and pus.** And **others of its kind, in pairs.**"

### هَٰذَا — a division in one word

**The verse begins with a single word, and that word closes the preceding section: *hādhā* — "this".**

**The grammarians count this a sign of transition:** a short pause as the telling passes from one side to the other.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra neither joins the two tableaux nor compares them. **It simply passes from one to the other** — and marks the passage with a single word.

### ٱلطَّٰغِين — the transgressors

**Root ط-غ-ي:** to exceed a bound, to overflow. **And the concrete sense of the root was treated in `096-alak.md` and `089-fecr.md`:** *ṭughyān* — water's overflowing its vessel, going past the limit.

**What was recorded in the discussion of al-Fajr was this, and it connects here:** *"the furthest point of *ṭughyān* is to put oneself in the position of a lord."*

**And that the word set against the God-fearing is *ṭāghīn* must be recorded:**

| | **مُتَّقِى** (49) | **طَاغِى** (55) |
|---|---|---|
| The picture of the root | **To take cover** | **To overflow** |
| Relation to the limit | To stop on its near side | To cross it |

**And in the twenty-eighth verse the opposition took the form *muttaqī / fujjār*.** **Here it is *muttaqī / ṭāghī*.** **All three words convey a relation to a limit:** one takes cover, one splits through (ف-ج-ر), one overflows (ط-غ-ي).

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the lexicographers' descriptions of the three roots.**

### لَشَرَّ مَـَٔابٍ — the opposite construct

**It is the exact opposite of the forty-ninth verse:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **49** | *wa inna li'l-muttaqīna la-**ḥusna** maʾāb* |
| **55** | *wa inna li'l-ṭāghīna la-**sharra** maʾāb* |

**The same pattern, one word different.** *Ḥusn* → *sharr*. **This is a symmetry that can be verified from the text.**

**And the word *maʾāb* occurs here for the fourth and last time.** It was tabulated above: David (25), Solomon (40), the God-fearing (49), the transgressors (55).

**I record this as my own reading:** *maʾāb* is "the place one returns and arrives at", and the root is أ-و-ب — the prophets of the sūra were named *awwāb* (those who return often). **The sūra says, that is, that everyone returns somewhere; what differs is the place returned to.**

### فَبِئْسَ ٱلْمِهَادُ — "how evil a bed"

**مِهَاد — root م-ه-د:** to level and spread a thing out, to make up a bed. *Mahd* — a cradle. **This word was treated in `078-nebe.md` at 78/6** (*a-lam najʿali'l-arḍa **mihādā***) — there the earth was named a "bed".

**And its use here is a reversal:**

| Verse | Word | For what |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Nabaʾ 78/6** | *mihād* | **The earth** — as a favour |
| **Ṣād 38/56** | *al-mihād* | **Hell** — as a reproach |

**The same word, in two opposite contexts.** I record this as an observation.

**And *biʾsa* — the verb of blame, the opposite of *niʿma*.** It was tabulated above: in the sūra two *niʿma* (30, 44), two *biʾsa* (56, 60).

### حَمِيمٌ وَغَسَّاقٌ — two drinks

**حَمِيم — root ح-م-م:** to grow hot, to burn. *Ḥamīm* — **boiling water.** It was treated in `078-nebe.md`, `056-vakia.md` and `044-duhan.md`.

**غَسَّاق — root غ-س-ق.** **It was treated in detail in `078-nebe.md` at 78/25, and the two wings of the root were given in `113-felak.md`. I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**The summary of the findings in the discussion of al-Nabaʾ:**

> *"The root **غ-س-ق** has two wings of meaning — *dense darkness* and *a thick, heavy, flowing thing* — and the place where the two meet is **a flowing, seeping, spreading thickness**… The renderings of the word in the commentaries: pus, the fluid that runs from the wounds of the people of the Fire, a very cold and putrid drink… The drink, that is, carries darkness inside its own name."*

**And *ghassāq* is on the فَعَّال pattern — intensive.**

**A note on a variant reading was entered in the discussion of al-Nabaʾ as well:** it is transmitted that the word is also read without the doubling, as **غَسَاق**; the difference of sense is not great. **The same note holds here.**

**What is particular to Ṣād is the difference of construction between the two sūras:**

| Sūra | Expression | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Nabaʾ 78/24-25** | *lā yadhūqūna fīhā bardan wa lā sharābā · **illā** ḥamīman wa ghassāqā* | **Exception** — "no drink, except…" |
| **Ṣād 38/57** | *hādhā **fa'l-yadhūqūhu** ḥamīmun wa ghassāq* | **Command** — "here it is, let them taste" |

**The kind of that exception was discussed in the treatment of al-Nabaʾ** (whether *muttaṣil* or *munqaṭiʿ*) and two views were given there with no preference imposed. **In Ṣād there is no pattern of exception; in its place there is an imperative.**

**And the verb is the same: *yadhūqū* — to taste.** **In the eighth verse it was said *bal lammā yadhūqū ʿadhāb* ("they have not yet tasted the punishment").**

| Verse | Expression | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *bal lammā **yadhūqū** ʿadhāb* | **Not yet** |
| **57** | *hādhā **fa'l-yadhūqūh*** | **Now** |

**This is a verbal link that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** what is called "they have not yet tasted" at the beginning of the sūra is given to be tasted in the fifty-seventh verse. **The expectation contained in the *lammā* of the eighth verse is met here.**

### وَءَاخَرُ مِن شَكْلِهِۦٓ أَزْوَٰجٌ — leaving it indefinite

**شَكْل — root ش-ك-ل:** to resemble; the form or kind of a thing. *Mushkil* — that which cannot be told apart because things resemble one another (and from there: hard, confused).

**أَزْوَاج — its singular *zawj*: a pair, a mate; and a kind, a sort.**

**The sentence makes no enumeration: it says "there are others of its kind" and gives no detail.**

**A variant reading is transmitted:** *wa ākharu* (singular) or *wa ukharu* (plural). **The difference of sense is not great; because I cannot state with certainty which imam held which preference, I give no names.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence has been deliberately left open. **Two things were counted, and the third was passed over by saying "and the like of them."** This accords with the general manner of the sūra: in the description of the Garden in the fifty-first verse too it said *fākihatin **kathīra*** (abundant fruit), and made no count.

## 38/59-61 — هَٰذَا فَوْجٌ مُّقْتَحِمٌ مَّعَكُمْ لَا مَرْحَبًۢا بِهِمْ إِنَّهُمْ صَالُوا۟ ٱلنَّارِ · قَالُوا۟ بَلْ أَنتُمْ لَا مَرْحَبًۢا بِكُمْ أَنتُمْ قَدَّمْتُمُوهُ لَنَا فَبِئْسَ ٱلْقَرَارُ · قَالُوا۟ رَبَّنَا مَن قَدَّمَ لَنَا هَٰذَا فَزِدْهُ عَذَابًا ضِعْفًا فِى ٱلنَّارِ

*Hādhā fawjun muqtaḥimun maʿakum, lā marḥaban bihim, innahum ṣālu'n-nār · Qālū bal antum lā marḥaban bikum, antum qaddamtumūhu lanā, fa-biʾsa'l-qarār · Qālū rabbanā man qaddama lanā hādhā fa-zidhu ʿadhāban ḍiʿfan fi'n-nār*

"'**This is a company plunging in along with you. No welcome for them; they are the ones to enter the Fire.**' They said: '**No — no welcome to you! It is you who sent this ahead for us. How evil a place to stay!**' They said: '**Our Lord! Whoever sent this ahead for us, increase his punishment twofold in the Fire.**'"

### The setting of the scene — who is speaking?

**Who is speaking is not stated explicitly in the verse, and here is where the disagreement lies:**

| View | Who says 59 | Who says 60 |
|---|---|---|
| **The keepers and those who came first** | The keepers of Hell, announcing the newcomers to those who entered first | The newcomers, answering those who came first |
| **The leaders and the followers** | The leaders who entered first, saying "no welcome" of the followers coming behind | The followers answer |
| **The angels and all of them** | An announcement by an angel | Those entering, among themselves |

**I do not impose a preference.** **But on all three views the structure of the scene is the same: a group arrives, the others do not want it, the arrivals answer back.**

### فَوْجٌ مُّقْتَحِمٌ — "a company plunging in"

**فَوْج — root ف-و-ج:** a body moving together, a troop. **The word occurs in `110-nasr.md` at 110/2** (*yadkhulūna fī dīni'llāhi **afwājā***).

**مُقْتَحِم — root ق-ح-م.** And this word was treated in detail in `090-beled.md` at 90/11. **I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**The summary of the finding there:**

> *"The concrete sense of the root: **to plunge into a thing without thinking, without reckoning, with the eyes shut.** To throw oneself into a dangerous place… **The pattern: افتعال (*iftiʿāl*).** In Arabic this pattern conveys that the act is done **by the agent's own effort and taken upon himself**. That is, *iqtaḥama* is not 'he plunged' but **'he threw himself into it by force'**."*

**And this verse (Ṣād 38/59) had already been named in `090-beled.md`:** *"of a company cast into Hell it is said 'this is a company **plunging in** along with you' (Ṣād 38/59); there too the word describes a mass being pushed into a place and plunging in."*

**In al-Balad the same root was used at a positive work:** *fa-la'qtaḥama'l-ʿaqaba* — "he did not assault the steep pass." **Here at a negative one.**

| Sūra | Expression | What is plunged into |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Balad 90/11** | *fa-la'qtaḥama'l-**ʿaqaba*** | **The steep pass** — the good that is hard |
| **Ṣād 38/59** | *fawjun **muqtaḥim** maʿakum* | **The Fire** |

**The same verb, two opposite targets.** I record this as an observation: **the root itself is neither praise nor blame; what is decisive is where the headlong plunge goes.**

### لَا مَرْحَبًۢا بِهِمْ — the refusal of welcome

**مَرْحَبًا — root ر-ح-ب:** to be wide, to be ample. *Raḥb* — a spacious place.

**And the Arabic greeting *marḥaban* is exactly an expression of making room:** "may you find a wide place", "there is room for you here." The Turkish greeting *merhaba* comes from this root and is in origin a sentence of hospitality.

**Its negative says exactly the opposite: *lā marḥaban bikum* — "there is no room for you."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sense of the root:** the first words in the scene of the Fire are **a refusal of hospitality.** **And this can be read together with the second verse of the sūra:** there those who disbelieve were described as being in *fī ʿizzatin wa **shiqāq*** (a rift). ***Shiqāq* was the falling of two sides into separate halves.** Here those same sides are splitting within themselves.

**And this link between *shiqāq* and *lā marḥaban* is one I make; the sūra does not make it separately.**

### أَنتُمْ قَدَّمْتُمُوهُ لَنَا — the passing on of the guilt

**قَدَّمَ — root ق-د-م:** to go forward, to put forward. **Form II: to offer a thing forward, to send it ahead.**

**And the verb is used twice over in two verses: *qaddamtumūhu* (60), *man qaddama* (61).**

**I record this as my own reading:** the subject of the dispute turns into a question of **order** — who did it first, who put it forward. **And this can be compared with the *al-malaʾ* scene at the beginning of the sūra (6):** there too the leading men went ahead and told those behind them, "walk on".

| Verse | Who | Verb | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | *al-malaʾ* | *wa'nṭalaqa* / *imshū* | **Goes ahead and calls** |
| **60** | The followers | *antum **qaddamtumūhu** lanā* | **Accuses the one who went ahead** |

**This link is one I make, and I give it as an observation about a pattern.**

### فَبِئْسَ ٱلْقَرَارُ — "how evil a place to stay"

**قَرَار — root ق-ر-ر:** to **stand, to settle, to be fixed** in a place. *Qarār* — to become stable. *Mustaqarr* — the place settled in.

**And the word is of the same family as *al-mihād* (bed) in the fifty-sixth verse of the sūra:** both convey **a place stayed in.**

| Verse | Word | The picture of the root |
|---|---|---|
| **56** | *al-**mihād*** | A bed spread out |
| **60** | *al-**qarār*** | A place settled in, stayed in |

**Both come with *biʾsa*.** This is a repetition that can be verified.

### فَزِدْهُ عَذَابًا ضِعْفًا — a prayer and its answer

**And in the sixty-first verse a prayer is made: *rabbanā*.**

**I record this as my own reading:** this is the first place in the scene of the Fire where God is called on. **And the content of the call is not a request for escape but for another's punishment to be increased.**

**ضِعْف — root ض-ع-ف:** a fold, a multiple; and **weakness.** The word has two branches and the lexicographers record this: *ḍiʿf* — the multiple of a thing; *ḍaʿf* — feebleness. **The common point is a thing's being doubled or divided in two.**

### A comparison with the scene in Sūrat Ghāfir

**The scene of dispute in the Fire was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/47-50, and a chain of three stages was tabulated there. I rely on that.**

**The table in Ghāfir was this:**

| Who to whom | What is wanted | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| The weak → those who were arrogant | To share the burden | "We are all in it" |
| All of them → the keepers | A lightening | "Did the proofs not come?" |
| The keepers → all of them | — | *Fa'dʿū wa mā duʿāʾu'l-kāfirīna illā fī ḍalāl* |

**Now I set the scenes of the two sūras side by side. This comparison can be verified from the text:**

| | **Ghāfir 40/47-50** | **Ṣād 38/59-64** |
|---|---|---|
| **The parties** | *Ḍuʿafāʾ* (the weak) ↔ *alladhīna'stakbarū* (those who were arrogant) | *Fawj* (the arriving company) ↔ those who entered first |
| **The first words** | *innā kunnā lakum tabaʿā* — "we followed you" | *lā marḥaban bihim* — "no room for them" |
| **What is asked** | **The sharing of the burden** — *fa-hal antum mughnūna ʿannā* | **Nothing** — accusation and imprecation |
| **A third party** | **The keepers** — they answer with a question | (On one view the keepers; but the speech is between the parties) |
| **Any looked for and missing** | None | **Yes (62-63)** — "why do we not see those men?" |
| **The closing verdict** | *wa mā duʿāʾu'l-kāfirīna illā fī ḍalāl* — **the emptiness of the prayer** | *inna dhālika la-ḥaqqun takhāṣumu ahli'n-nār* — **the reality of the dispute** |

**The difference between the two closes must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:**

**Ghāfir closes the scene with the futility of what is done (the prayer). Ṣād closes the scene by confirming the reality of what happens: *inna dhālika la-ḥaqq*.**

**And this difference accords with the backbones of the two sūras:**

- **The backbone of Ghāfir is the root ج-د-ل (to dispute)**, and as was recorded there the sūra treated denial as *"a posture of disputation"*. **The scene shows that the dispute arrives nowhere.**
- **The backbone of Ṣād is judgment and *ḥaqq*.** And the sixty-fourth verse describes the quarrel of those in the Fire as *ḥaqq* — that is, as **something that will really happen.**

**One further note:** in Ghāfir the dispute turns on the axis of **status** (the weak / the arrogant); **in Ṣād on the axis of order** (who put it forward first). **This too accords with the difference between the polemical parts of the two sūras:** in Ghāfir the matter was *kibr* (40/56), in Ṣād it is *ʿizza* and *shiqāq* (38/2).

## 38/62-64 — وَقَالُوا۟ مَا لَنَا لَا نَرَىٰ رِجَالًا كُنَّا نَعُدُّهُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَشْرَارِ · أَتَّخَذْنَٰهُمْ سِخْرِيًّا أَمْ زَاغَتْ عَنْهُمُ ٱلْأَبْصَٰرُ · إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ لَحَقٌّ تَخَاصُمُ أَهْلِ ٱلنَّارِ

*Wa qālū mā lanā lā narā rijālan kunnā naʿudduhum mina'l-ashrār · Attakhadhnāhum sukhriyyan am zāghat ʿanhumu'l-abṣār · Inna dhālika la-ḥaqqun takhāṣumu ahli'n-nār*

"And they said: '**What is it with us that we do not see men we used to count among the evil? Did we take them for a mockery, or have our eyes swerved away from them?**' **This is the truth: the quarrelling of the people of the Fire.**"

### The most striking moment of the scene

**And now, in the middle of the scene of the Fire, something quite different happens: some of them look about and search for someone.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the position of the verse:** between the fifty-ninth and the sixty-first verses there was accusation and imprecation. **In the sixty-second the subject changes all at once:** no one is accusing anyone any longer; **an absence is noticed.**

### رِجَالًا كُنَّا نَعُدُّهُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَشْرَارِ — a counting

**نَعُدُّ — root ع-د-د:** to count. *ʿAdad* — a number. **The verb is not "to suppose" but to count** — to place in a list.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** the verse does not say "we supposed them to be evil"; it says **"we used to *count* them among the evil."** **There was not a conviction, that is, but a classification.** People had been put on a list.

**أَشْرَار — its singular *sharr*.** And the word is the exact opposite of *al-akhyār* (the best) in the forty-seventh and forty-eighth verses.

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **47, 48** | *al-**akhyār*** | The prophets — **God's classification** |
| **62** | *al-**ashrār*** | The men looked for — **people's classification** |

**This is a verbal opposition that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the sūra sets two lists of the same kind against one another. **And the sixty-second verse shows that the people's list does not hold.**

**And the word *rijāl* is indefinite: "certain men".** No name is given.

### أَتَّخَذْنَٰهُمْ سِخْرِيًّا أَمْ زَاغَتْ عَنْهُمُ ٱلْأَبْصَٰرُ — two possibilities

**The sentence offers a question and two possibilities. And both possibilities are about themselves:**

| Possibility | What it means |
|---|---|
| *Attakhadhnāhum **sukhriyyā*** | "We took them for a mockery" — **the fault was in us** |
| *Am **zāghat** ʿanhumu'l-abṣār* | "Our eyes have swerved away from them" — **now we cannot see** |

**سِخْرِيّ — root س-خ-ر.** It was treated at the eighteenth verse: the two branches of the root — *taskhīr* (to press into service) and *sukhriyya* (mockery). **Here the second branch.**

**And this is an opposition within the sūra that can be verified:**

| Verse | Word | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18** | *sakhkharnā* | God → David | **The mountains were pressed into service** |
| **36** | *sakhkharnā* | God → Solomon | **The wind was pressed into service** |
| **63** | *sukhriyyā* | People → the believers | **They were made an object of mockery** |

**The same root, three occurrences: two a favour, one a fault.**

**زَاغَتْ — root ز-ي-غ:** to swerve, to slip, to depart from the straight. **This root was treated in `053-necm.md` at 53/17** (*mā zāgha'l-baṣaru wa mā ṭaghā* — "the eye neither swerved nor overstepped"). **I rely on that.**

**And the comparison is striking and can be verified from the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Najm 53/17** | *mā **zāgha**'l-baṣaru wa mā ṭaghā* | The envoy's eye — **it did not swerve** |
| **Ṣād 38/63** | *am **zāghat** ʿanhumu'l-abṣār* | The eyes of those in the Fire — **have they swerved?** |

**The same verb, the same subject (*baṣar*), two opposite verdicts.**

**And the word *abṣār* had occurred in the forty-fifth verse: *uli'l-aydī wa'l-**abṣār***. It was tabulated above.**

| Verse | *Abṣār* | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **45** | *uli'l-aydī wa'l-**abṣār*** | Three prophets — **those who possess them** |
| **63** | *zāghat ʿanhumu'l-**abṣār*** | Those in the Fire — **those whose eyes have swerved** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these two occurrences:** in the forty-fifth verse the sūra defined a class as "those possessed of eyes". **In the sixty-third there are men asking whether their eyes have swerved.**

### That the question is not answered

**And this is the most striking side of the verse: a question is asked and no answer is given.**

**The sūra does not answer this question.** Where the men looked for are is not stated.

**I record this as my own reading:** the answer had in fact already been given between the forty-ninth and the fifty-fourth verses. **The reader knows where the men asked about are — because that tableau was shown ten verses earlier.** Those who ask do not know.

**This is a consequence of the sūra's having set the two tableaux side by side, and it can be verified from the construction:** *hādhā… wa inna li'l-muttaqīna la-ḥusna maʾāb* (49) → *hādhā wa inna li'l-ṭāghīna la-sharra maʾāb* (55) → **and in the sixty-second verse those in the first tableau are being looked for.**

### إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ لَحَقٌّ تَخَاصُمُ أَهْلِ ٱلنَّارِ — the seal of the section

**And the section closes with a sentence of confirmation.**

**تَخَاصُم — root خ-ص-م, Form VI: to go to law against one another.** It was tabulated above: the root occurs four times in the sūra (21, 22, 64, 69).

**And now the chain becomes visible:**

| Verse | Expression | Where | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **21-22** | *al-khaṣm* / *khaṣmāni* | **In the miḥrāb** | **A judgment was given** |
| **64** | *takhāṣumu ahli'n-nār* | **In the Fire** | **No outcome at all** |
| **69** | *al-malaʾu'l-aʿlā idh **yakhtaṣimūn*** | **On high** | (To be recounted in 71-85) |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these three occurrences:** the sūra shows the same verb on three levels. **And the middle one — the quarrel of those in the Fire — is the only one without an outcome.** The suit on earth ended in a judgment; those in the Fire arrive nowhere.

**لَحَقٌّ — and the word is among the central words of the sūra.**

| Verse | Expression | About what |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | *fa'ḥkum baynanā **bi'l-ḥaqq*** | **A request** |
| 26 | *fa'ḥkum bayna'n-nāsi **bi'l-ḥaqq*** | **A command** |
| 27 | (*bāṭilā* — its opposite) | **Creation** |
| **64** | *inna dhālika **la-ḥaqq*** | **Reality** |
| 84 | *fa'l-**ḥaqqu** wa'l-**ḥaqqa** aqūl* | **God's own word** |

**Five occurrences, four different works: as a measure, as a command, as a reality, and as a quality of speech.**

**And *la-ḥaqq* here confirms the reality of a scene.** **I record this as my own reading:** what is recounted is not left as a description or a likeness. **The sūra breaks in and says "this is true"** — and considering how ordinary the things happening in the scene are (accusation, imprecation, looking about), this is a note worth attention.

## 38/65-66 — قُلْ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ مُنذِرٌ وَمَا مِنْ إِلَٰهٍ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ ٱلْوَٰحِدُ ٱلْقَهَّارُ · رَبُّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْغَفَّٰرُ

*Qul innamā ana mundhir, wa mā min ilāhin illa'llāhu'l-Wāḥidu'l-Qahhār · Rabbu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa mā baynahuma'l-ʿAzīzu'l-Ghaffār*

"**Say: I am only a warner. There is no god but God, the One, the All-Subduing.** The Lord of the heavens and the earth and what is between them; **the mighty, the ever-forgiving.**"

### قُلْ — and the sūra changes direction

**In the sixty-fifth verse the sūra turns to the envoy and gives a command: *qul* — "say".**

**And the command comes three times in the sūra: 65, 67, 86.** All three carry a short sentence and all three announce a **limit**:

| Verse | Command | What is to be said | What it limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **65** | *qul* | *innamā ana mundhir* | **His office** |
| **67** | *qul* | *huwa nabaʾun ʿaẓīm* | **The greatness of the report** |
| **86** | *qul* | *mā asʾalukum ʿalayhi min ajr* | **Its price** |

### إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ مُنذِرٌ — the answer to the fourth verse

**And now a link is made with the fourth verse of the sūra. There it had been said:**

> *wa ʿajibū an jāʾahum **mundhirun** minhum* — "they were astonished that a **warner** from among themselves had come to them."

**The same word, sixty-one verses later, this time in the envoy's own mouth.**

| Verse | Word | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | *mundhir* | **The text** — reporting their astonishment |
| **65** | *innamā ana **mundhir*** | **The envoy** — defining himself |
| **70** | *innamā ana **nadhīrun mubīn*** | **The envoy** — again |

**This is a chain of wording that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the other side had been astonished that a *mundhir* should come. **The sūra gives that word back as the envoy's own definition of himself** — and limits it with *innamā* ("only"): no more than this.

**إِنَّمَا** — in Arabic it is the particle of **ḥaṣr** (restriction): "only, nothing but". **The sentence, that is, does not announce an office; it announces the limit of an office.**

**And a similar restriction was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/45:** *wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-jabbār* — "you are not a tyrant over them." **The note there was this:** *"Compulsion is taken out of the envoy's hand and something is given in its place: the text."*

**In Ṣād there is the same structure, but the order differs:**

| Sūra | First | Then |
|---|---|---|
| **Qāf 50/45** | Authority is **negated** (*mā anta… bi-jabbār*) | A command is given (*fa-dhakkir bi'l-Qurʾān*) |
| **Ṣād 38/65** | The office is **limited** (*innamā ana mundhir*) | **A sentence of *tawḥīd*** follows |

### ٱلْوَٰحِدُ ٱلْقَهَّارُ — the answer to the fifth verse

And the second link: in the fifth verse the other side had said *a-jaʿala'l-ālihata **ilāhan wāḥidā*** — "has he made the gods one single god?"

**The sixty-fifth verse uses the same word: *al-Wāḥid*.**

**And the difference between the two sentences lies in the grammatical state of the word:**

| Verse | Expression | Definiteness | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | *ilāhan **wāḥidā*** | **Indefinite** — "one single god" | In the other side's language: **something new** |
| **65** | *Allāhu'**l-Wāḥid*** | **Definite** — "the One" | In the text's language: **a known reality** |

**This is a difference of construction that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the other side had spoken of *tawḥīd* as an indefinite proposal; the verse gives it as a definite name. **The answer, that is, does not defend the claim — it names it.**

**ٱلْقَهَّار — root ق-ه-ر:** to prevail and subdue, to become such that one cannot be resisted. **The pattern is *faʿʿāl* — intensive.**

**This name was named in `112-ihlas.md` and `093-duha.md` and its occurrences in the Qurʾān were listed** (Yūsuf 12/39; al-Raʿd 13/16; Ṣād 38/65; al-Zumar 39/4; Ghāfir 40/16). **And it is striking: in almost every place the name occurs it comes together with *al-Wāḥid*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that pairing:** the two names together set up an inference. **Were there more than one, one would prevail over the other.** To be *Qahhār* requires that there be no other above; and that requires being *wāḥid*. **The two names complete one another.**

### ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْغَفَّٰرُ — and the return of the ninth verse

**The sixty-sixth verse gives a second pair of names, and both had occurred earlier in the sūra:**

| Name | Its earlier occurrence in the sūra |
|---|---|
| **ٱلْعَزِيز** | 9 (*rabbika'l-**ʿAzīzi**'l-Wahhāb*) |
| **ٱلْغَفَّار** | The root: 25 (*fa-**ghafarnā***), 35 (*rabbi'**ghfir** lī*) |

**And setting the two pairs side by side, a balance is seen:**

| Verse | Pair of names | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | *al-ʿAzīz* + *al-**Wahhāb*** | Might + **giving without return** |
| **66** | *al-ʿAzīz* + *al-**Ghaffār*** | Might + **forgiving abundantly** |

***Al-ʿAzīz* is fixed in both; the second name changes.** And the two changing names are on the same pattern (*faʿʿāl*) and both convey a **giving**: the one of goods, the other of pardon.

**I record this as an observation; its ground is the structure of the two constructs.**

**And it can be compared with the sequence of five attributes treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/3:** there too power and mercy were given in one breath. **The two sūras set up the same balance with different numbers of names.**

## 38/67-70 — قُلْ هُوَ نَبَؤٌا۟ عَظِيمٌ · أَنتُمْ عَنْهُ مُعْرِضُونَ · مَا كَانَ لِىَ مِنْ عِلْمٍۭ بِٱلْمَلَإِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰٓ إِذْ يَخْتَصِمُونَ · إِن يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ إِلَّآ أَنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ

*Qul huwa nabaʾun ʿaẓīm · Antum ʿanhu muʿriḍūn · Mā kāna liya min ʿilmin bi'l-malaʾi'l-aʿlā idh yakhtaṣimūn · In yūḥā ilayya illā annamā ana nadhīrun mubīn*

"**Say: it is a mighty report. You are turning away from it.** **I had no knowledge of the assembly on high** when they were disputing. It is revealed to me only **that I am a clear warner**."

### The sūra's change of sound is here

**It was tabulated above in the section on structure, and I recall it here because it is a datum that can be verified:** **from the first verse to the sixty-sixth every verse ends in a long *ā* + a single consonant. In the sixty-seventh the sound changes: *ʿaẓīm*.** And it never comes back.

**The sūra's break of sound, that is, is exactly at this verse.**

### نَبَؤٌا۟ عَظِيم — a mighty report

**This construct was treated in detail in `078-nebe.md` and Ṣād 38/67 was explicitly discussed there. I rely on that.**

**What was recorded in the discussion of al-Nabaʾ was this:**

> *"Ṣād 38/67-68: 'Say: it is a mighty report; you are turning away from it.' The same construct is used there of the Qurʾān… But that the construct in Ṣād 38/67 is word for word the same is a datum worth recording and must not be passed over. Set the two verses side by side and what comes out is this: **the Qurʾān can use the expression 'mighty report' both of itself and of the resurrection itself.** This is no contradiction — because the core of the report the Qurʾān carries is the resurrection in any case."*

**And this was recorded separately at the close of the discussion of al-Nabaʾ.**

**What is to be added here is its referent within Ṣād. To whom does the pronoun *huwa* (it) refer?**

| View | What the pronoun refers to | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **The Qurʾān** | The message of *tawḥīd* in the preceding verse, and the Book itself | The sūra had named the Book in verse 29; verse 87 will say *in huwa illā dhikrun* — the same pronoun |
| **The report of *tawḥīd*** | The sentence in verses 65-66 | This is the nearest antecedent |
| **The Resurrection / the raising** | The subject denied at the beginning of the sūra | The usage in al-Nabaʾ 78/2 |

**I do not impose a preference.** But an indication within the sūra can be recorded: **in the eighty-seventh verse the same pronoun is used, and there its referent is plain: *in huwa illā dhikrun li'l-ʿālamīn* — the Book.** This is a datum supporting the first view; it is not binding.

### أَنتُمْ عَنْهُ مُعْرِضُونَ — a verse of one sentence

**It is the shortest verse in the sūra: three words.**

**أَعْرَضَ — root ع-ر-ض.** Its concrete sense: **the breadth of a thing, its side.** *ʿArḍ* — width. **Form IV (*iʿrāḍ*): to turn one's face away, to turn one's side** — that is, to show the one addressed not one's face but one's **side**.

**This root was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/4** (*fa-aʿraḍa aktharuhum fa-hum lā yasmaʿūn*), and what was recorded there was this: **not hearing is set down as the consequence of turning away — not as its cause.** I rely on that.

**And the construction of the sentence must be recorded: *antum* (you) has been brought forward.**

**In Arabic, fronting the subject in a nominal sentence conveys *takhṣīṣ* (specification).** **That is, not "you are turning away" but "it is you who turn away".**

**I record this as my own reading:** the report is mighty (67), and the response given to it is named in a single word (68). **There is no conjunction at all between the two verses — two sentences set side by side.** The comparison is made by the reader.

### مَا كَانَ لِىَ مِنْ عِلْمٍۭ بِٱلْمَلَإِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰٓ — the source of the knowledge

**And now the sūra makes its passage to the next section.**

**ٱلْمَلَإِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ — "the assembly on high".** The word *malaʾ* had occurred in the sixth verse: **the leading men of Mecca.**

**And now the same word comes with an epithet: *al-aʿlā* — high, the one above.**

| Verse | *Malaʾ* | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | *al-malaʾ minhum* | **The leading men of Mecca** | They rose and went off, saying "walk on" |
| **69** | *al-malaʾu'l-**aʿlā*** | **The assembly above** | *Yakhtaṣimūn* — **they dispute** |

**This is a shared wording that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the sūra uses the same word on two levels. **The *malaʾ* below leaves a gathering and decides; the *malaʾ* above is inside a dispute.**

**And the views as to who is meant:**

| View | Who *al-malaʾu'l-aʿlā* are |
|---|---|
| **The angels** | The angels will be named in verses 71-73; the most widespread explanation |
| **The angels and Iblīs** | The dispute (*ikhtiṣām*) occurs in 74-76; these are the parties |
| **The scene in al-Baqara 2/30** | The angels' question *a-tajʿalu fīhā man yufsidu fīhā* is counted a dispute |

**None of the three excludes the others; I do not impose a preference.**

**إِذْ يَخْتَصِمُونَ — and the root خ-ص-م comes for the fourth and last time.** It was tabulated above.

### إِن يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ إِلَّآ أَنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ — the character of the knowledge

**The sentence says two things, and both must be recorded:**

**One: the knowledge is not from himself.** *Mā kāna liya min ʿilm* — "I had no knowledge." That is, what is to be recounted is not the product of a personal access.

**Two: the ground of the revelation is announced.** *In yūḥā ilayya illā annamā ana nadhīrun mubīn.*

**And the construction of the sentence has been disputed among the grammarians:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| **Ground** | "It is revealed to me only **because** I am a warner" |
| **Content** | "It is only **revealed** to me that I am a clear warner" |

**Both explanations are transmitted.** The second reduces the content of the revelation to an announcement of office; the first states the reason for the revelation. **I do not impose a preference.**

**نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ — and the third occurrence of the word *mundhir*.** It was tabulated above (4, 65, 70).

**مُبِين — root ب-ي-ن:** to be separate, to be clear. **The active participle of Form IV: *mubīn* — both "that which is clear" and "that which makes clear".** The word works in both directions in Arabic (intransitive and transitive).

**I record this as my own reading:** the epithet announces that the warning is not **hidden knowledge.** **And this joins with the sentence "I had no knowledge" in the sixty-ninth verse:** what is to be recounted is neither the envoy's own discovery nor a concealed secret.

**Now the sūra will recount what happened in that "assembly on high".**

## 38/71-74 — إِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى خَٰلِقٌۢ بَشَرًا مِّن طِينٍ · فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ · فَسَجَدَ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ · إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ ٱسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ

*Idh qāla rabbuka li'l-malāʾikati innī khāliqun basharan min ṭīn · Fa-idhā sawwaytuhū wa nafakhtu fīhi min rūḥī fa-qaʿū lahū sājidīn · Fa-sajada'l-malāʾikatu kulluhum ajmaʿūn · Illā Iblīs, istakbara wa kāna mina'l-kāfirīn*

"When your Lord said to the angels: '**I am creating a man from clay. When I have shaped him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down before him in prostration.**' **All the angels prostrated, every one of them — except Iblīs. He grew proud and was among the disbelievers.**"

### The treatment of this account in al-Baqara

**This account was treated in detail in `002-bakara.md` at 2/30-34. I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**A summary of what was treated in al-Baqara:**

- **That the prostration is not a prostration of worship** is widely agreed; the one who gave the command is God, and so obedience or disobedience is directed to Him. **"What Iblīs rejected was not Adam (Ādam) but the command."**
- ***Istikbār* is not being great but counting oneself great.** Form X (*istifʿāl*) conveys seeking a thing or reckoning it so. **"The word itself says that pride is not a reality but a supposition."**
- **Iblīs's being counted a disbeliever is not from a lack of knowledge:** *"disbelief is not a lack of knowledge but the covering of what is known… Iblīs knows and rejects nonetheless."*
- **Whether Iblīs is an angel:** on the basis of al-Kahf 18/50 the majority of the exegetes say he is a jinn.

**Now I pass to what is particular to Ṣād. And the difference is, verifiably, large.**

### The difference between al-Baqara and Ṣād

| | **al-Baqara 2/30-34** | **Ṣād 38/71-85** |
|---|---|---|
| **The opening sentence** | *innī jāʿilun fi'l-arḍi **khalīfa*** — viceroyalty | *innī **khāliqun** basharan min ṭīn* — creation |
| **The angels' speech** | **Present** — *a-tajʿalu fīhā man yufsidu fīhā* | **Absent** |
| **The names** | **Present** — *wa ʿallama Ādama'l-asmāʾa kullahā* | **Absent** |
| **Iblīs's ground** | **Not given** (it is in al-Aʿrāf 7/12) | **Given** — *ana khayrun minhu* |
| **The dialogue** | Short | **Long** — 75-85, eleven verses |
| **The name of the matter** | *abā wa'stakbara* — refusal and pride | ***Qiyās*** and **istikbār** |

**The two sūras, that is, take hold of the same event from two different places.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above:** al-Baqara tells the account by way of **what man is** (viceroy, the one who knows the names). **Ṣād tells it by way of what Iblīs did** — and the name of what he did is **an error of reasoning**.

**And this accords with the whole subject of the sūra:** Ṣād is a sūra of **giving judgment**. David judged on one-sided knowledge; those in the Fire quarrelled without outcome; **Iblīs judges by an analogy of his own.**

### إِنِّى خَٰلِقٌۢ بَشَرًا مِّن طِينٍ — and the return of the seventh verse

**And now we come to one of the most striking returns of a word in the sūra. It was treated above in the discussion of 38/7; here I complete it.**

**In the seventh verse the other side had said:** *in hādhā illā **ikhtilāq*** — "this is nothing but **a fabrication**."

**In the seventy-first verse God names Himself thus:** *innī **khāliqun** basharan min ṭīn* — "I am **creating** a man from clay."

**The two words are from one root: خ-ل-ق.** The two branches of the root (to create / to fabricate) were treated above as the lexicographers record them.

**And the root occurs five times in the sūra:**

| Verse | Word | Who | Sense |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7** | *ikhtilāq* | The objectors | **Fabrication** |
| **27** | *mā **khalaqnā**'s-samāʾa* | The text | **Creation** |
| **71** | *innī **khāliqun** basharā* | God | **Creation** |
| **75** | *limā **khalaqtu** bi-yadayy* | God | **Creation** |
| **76** | ***khalaqtanī** min nār… **khalaqtahū** min ṭīn* | Iblīs | **Creation** |

**One root, five occurrences, and only the first in the sense of "fabrication".** This is a distribution that can be verified from the text.

**بَشَر — root ب-ش-ر.** Its concrete sense: **skin, the outer surface of the hide.** *Bashara* — the skin. **And from there: *bashar* — man, the being whose skin is visible.** The Qurʾān uses this word for man's **material side**; *insān* and *ādam* name other sides.

**طِين — clay:** earth mixed with water.

**Worth recording:** in telling of the creation, the material is named. **And Iblīs's analogy will be built on exactly this material (76).**

### فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى — two stages

**سَوَّىٰ — root س-و-ي:** to make even, to set straight, to complete the form of. **This word was treated in `082-infitar.md` at 82/7 and in `091-sems.md`.**

**What was recorded in the discussion of al-Infiṭār was this, and this verse was named there:**

> *"In telling of the creation of man the Qurʾān follows the order **first creating, then *taswiya*, then the giving of a capacity** (al-Sajda 32/9, al-Ḥijr 15/29, Ṣād 38/72, al-Aʿlā 87/2). That is, *taswiya* is the stage in which the raw material is brought into working order."*

**I rely on that.**

**نَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى — and this expression was treated in `066-tahrim.md` at 66/12.** The note there: *"This expression is used in the Qurʾān of the creation of Adam as well (al-Ḥijr 15/29; Ṣād 38/72). The same verb, the same construct."* **I rely on that.**

**A note of *tanzīh* is necessary, and the note entered for the throne in `040-gafir.md` at 40/7 holds here too:** the expression "I breathed of My spirit" cannot be understood in a way that ascribes body, part or division to God. **Two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries for expressions of this kind:** to accept the wording as it stands and refer its manner to God (*tafwīḍ*), or to say that the construct conveys **honour and attribution** (*iḍāfat al-tashrīf*) — as in the construct "the House of God" (*baytu'llāh*). **I convey both and do not impose a preference.**

### فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ — the form of the command

**وَقَعَ — root و-ق-ع:** to fall, to come down somewhere. **The imperative: *qaʿū* — fall, go down.**

**And the choice of verb must be recorded:** it does not say "prostrate", it says **"fall down as prostrators"**. **Both a movement (falling) and a state (*sājidīn*) are commanded together, that is.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the command carries an immediacy that leaves no room for hesitation. **And it is joined with *fāʾ*: the moment the breathing is over.**

### فَسَجَدَ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ — a threefold emphasis

**Three separate reinforcements one upon another in the sentence:**

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| *al-malāʾika* | The subject — definite |
| *kulluhum* | **"All of them"** — no exception |
| *ajmaʿūn* | **"Altogether"** — at one moment, not one by one |

**In Arabic the use of these two emphatics (*kull* + *ajmaʿ*) together closes off exception entirely.**

**And immediately afterwards an exception comes: *illā Iblīs*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this construction:** the sentence first shuts every door, then opens a single exception. **This is a construction that makes the singularity and the strangeness of the exception visible.**

### ٱسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ

**These two verbs were treated in al-Baqara 2/34. I rely on that.**

**The construction in al-Baqara: *abā wa'stakbara wa kāna mina'l-kāfirīn* — three verbs.**
**The construction in Ṣād: *istakbara wa kāna mina'l-kāfirīn* — two verbs.**

***Abā* (he refused) is absent in Ṣād.** **This is a difference that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** al-Baqara counts the act and its ground separately; **Ṣād begins directly with the ground.** Because Ṣād will shortly give that ground in Iblīs's own mouth as well.

**And the diagnosis treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/56 connects here directly. The note there was this:**

> *"The verse names not the **apparent subject** of the dispute but **the wish behind it**… *in fī ṣudūrihim illā kibrun mā hum bi-bālighīh* — in their breasts there is nothing but a greatness they will not attain."*

**Ghāfir had said that beneath those who dispute lies *kibr*. Ṣād shows the same thing with an account:** **in the first instance of dispute, what lies beneath is *istikbār*.**

**And the table set up in Ghāfir gains one further link here:**

| Verse | Who | What he wants to reach | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ghāfir 40/36** | Pharaoh | *al-asbāb* — the ways of the heavens | By a tower — he did not reach |
| **Ghāfir 40/56** | Those who dispute | *Kibr* | *Mā hum bi-bālighīh* |
| **Ṣād 38/10** | The leading men of Mecca | *al-asbāb* | *Fa'l-yartaqū* — they cannot |
| **Ṣād 38/74-75** | **Iblīs** | *al-ʿālīn* — to be of the exalted | *Fa'khruj minhā* — he was cast out |

**Four occurrences, the same structure: a claim of ascent and its non-fulfilment.** This table is one I set up; the verses do not refer to one another. **But all four are inside the same two sūras, and their wordings are partly shared (*asbāb*, *bulūgh*, *ʿālīn*).**

## 38/75-76 — قَالَ يَٰٓإِبْلِيسُ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَن تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَىَّ أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ أَمْ كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلْعَالِينَ · قَالَ أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ خَلَقْتَنِى مِن نَّارٍ وَخَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن طِينٍ

*Qāla yā Iblīsu mā manaʿaka an tasjuda limā khalaqtu bi-yadayy, astakbarta am kunta mina'l-ʿālīn · Qāla ana khayrun minhu, khalaqtanī min nārin wa khalaqtahū min ṭīn*

"He said: '**Iblīs! What held you back from prostrating to what I created with My two hands? Did you grow proud, or were you of the exalted?**' He said: '**I am better than he; You created me from fire and created him from clay.**'"

### The form of the question

**And here there is something striking: a question is asked.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verse itself:** what Iblīs did is already known (the seventy-fourth verse said it). **Nonetheless a question is asked and a ground demanded.**

**And the question comes with two alternatives: *astakbarta am kunta mina'l-ʿālīn?***

| Alternative | What it means |
|---|---|
| *Astakbarta* | **Did you count yourself great** — a supposition |
| *Am kunta mina'l-ʿālīn* | **Were you of the exalted** — a claim about reality |

**The difference between the two alternatives is the same as what was recorded for *istikbār* in the discussion of al-Baqara:** Form *istifʿāl* conveys **reckoning a thing so**; *al-ʿālīn*, on the other hand, are those who are in fact high.

**I record this as my own reading:** the question offers Iblīs two options — **a supposition or a fact?** And Iblīs's answer chooses neither of them: **it brings an analogy.**

### مَا مَنَعَكَ — "what held you back"

**مَنَعَ — root م-ن-ع:** to prevent, to hold back.

**And the choice of verb must be recorded: it does not say "why did you not prostrate?"** It says **"what *prevented* you?"**

**I record this as my own reading:** the question assumes that prostration is **the ordinary thing.** Had there been no obstacle, prostration would have followed. **The question asks Iblīs, that is, not for a ground but for an *obstacle*.**

**And Iblīs can show no obstacle; in its place he brings a claim of superiority. The two are not the same thing.**

### لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَىَّ — "to what I created with My two hands"

**A note of *tanzīh* is necessary, and it is the same as the note entered above for *rūḥī*.**

**This expression cannot be understood in a way that ascribes an organ, a limb or corporeality to God.** **Two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Attitude | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Tafwīḍ** | The wording is accepted as it stands and its manner referred to God; no likening is fallen into |
| **Taʾwīl** | The construct conveys **power**, **direct concern** or **particular care**; in Arabic "I made it with my own hand" describes taking a work upon oneself in person |

**I convey both and do not impose a preference.**

**What must be recorded is the expression's function in the scene, and this is beyond dispute: the construct conveys a distinction.** The being Iblīs is asked to prostrate to is not presented as an ordinary being.

**And the use of the dual (*yadayy* — two hands) is dwelt on as well:** the Qurʾān uses the singular (*yaduhū*) and the plural (*aydīnā* — Yāsīn 36/71) elsewhere. **I say nothing definite about the weight of meaning in this difference.**

### أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ — the framing of the analogy

**And now we come to the real subject of this section of the sūra: Iblīs makes an *analogy*.**

**In `002-bakara.md` at 2/34 this sentence was treated by way of al-Aʿrāf 7/12, and there the following was recorded:**

> *"**Pride is born of a comparison.** Iblīs makes no claim of worth on his own account; he compares himself with another and comes out superior. Pride is not seeing oneself high but **seeing another low**; its measure is always another. And the comparison itself is faulty: to set fire against earth and declare fire superior is an assumption. Fire burns and consumes; earth carries and brings forth. Iblīs has invented a hierarchy and put himself at the top of it."*

**I rely on that and do not repeat it here.**

**Here I add three things belonging to Ṣād's own construction.**

**One: the logical form of the sentence.** What Iblīs says is, in form, a complete inference:

| Step | Expression | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| Premise 1 | *khalaqtanī min **nār*** | **True** — the verse confirms it as well |
| Premise 2 | *wa khalaqtahū min **ṭīn*** | **True** — verse 71 says the same |
| Conclusion | *ana **khayrun** minh* | **Does not follow from the premises** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the three lines above:** both of Iblīs's premises are true and agree with the text itself. **What is false is the assumption that the conclusion follows from them.** The names of the two materials have been given; **nowhere has any information been given about which is superior.** Iblīs has added that step himself.

**And this form of error belongs to the same family as the objections at the beginning of the sūra:**

| Verse | Who | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **4-5** | The Meccans | They take habit for a measure (*ʿajab*) |
| **7** | The Meccans | They take not having heard for a measure |
| **76** | Iblīs | **He takes his own analogy for a measure** |

**In all three the same thing is missing: where the measure comes from is not stated.**

**Two: the *command* is not mentioned at all in the sentence.** The question put to Iblīs concerned not obeying a command. **The answer says nothing about the command; it speaks of the material.**

**I record this as my own reading:** Iblīs changes the subject. **The question was about obedience; the answer is about worth.** And as was recorded in al-Baqara, what was rejected is not Adam but the command — while Iblīs in his answer speaks only of Adam.

**Three: a verbal link within the sūra.** In the forty-seventh and forty-eighth verses the prophets were named *al-**akhyār*** (the best). **The root is خ-ي-ر.**

**And in the seventy-sixth verse Iblīs uses the same root of himself: *ana khayrun minh*.**

| Verse | Word | Who | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **47, 48** | *al-**akhyār*** | The prophets | **God** |
| **76** | *ana **khayrun** minh* | Iblīs | **Iblīs himself** |

**This is a shared wording that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the same root, in the one an attribute **given**, in the other an attribute **claimed**. **The sūra thereby draws attention to who it is that says a thing is "good".**

**And the original sense of the root supports this: خ-ي-ر — *ikhtiyār*, to choose.** In the forty-seventh verse the prophets were also named *al-**muṣṭafayn*** (the chosen). **To be "good", that is, was tied in the forty-seventh verse to a *being chosen*. Iblīs chooses himself.**

## 38/77-85 — قَالَ فَٱخْرُجْ مِنْهَا فَإِنَّكَ رَجِيمٌ · وَإِنَّ عَلَيْكَ لَعْنَتِىٓ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ · قَالَ رَبِّ فَأَنظِرْنِىٓ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ · قَالَ فَإِنَّكَ مِنَ ٱلْمُنظَرِينَ · إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْوَقْتِ ٱلْمَعْلُومِ · قَالَ فَبِعِزَّتِكَ لَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ · إِلَّا عِبَادَكَ مِنْهُمُ ٱلْمُخْلَصِينَ · قَالَ فَٱلْحَقُّ وَٱلْحَقَّ أَقُولُ · لَأَمْلَأَنَّ جَهَنَّمَ مِنكَ وَمِمَّن تَبِعَكَ مِنْهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ

*Qāla fa'khruj minhā fa-innaka rajīm · Wa inna ʿalayka laʿnatī ilā yawmi'd-dīn · Qāla rabbi fa-anẓirnī ilā yawmi yubʿathūn · Qāla fa-innaka mina'l-munẓarīn · Ilā yawmi'l-waqti'l-maʿlūm · Qāla fa-bi-ʿizzatika la-ughwiyannahum ajmaʿīn · Illā ʿibādaka minhumu'l-mukhlaṣīn · Qāla fa'l-ḥaqqu wa'l-ḥaqqa aqūl · La-amlaʾanna jahannama minka wa mimman tabiʿaka minhum ajmaʿīn*

"He said: '**Go out from there; you are driven away. And My curse is upon you until the Day of Judgment.**' He said: '**My Lord! Then grant me respite until the day they are raised.**' He said: '**You are among those granted respite — until the day of the known time.**' He said: '**Then by Your might, I shall lead them all astray — save such of Your servants among them as are purified.**' He said: '**This is the truth — and I speak the truth: I shall fill Hell with you and with those of them who follow you, all together.**'"

### The structure of the dialogue

**In nine verses there are five turns of speech, and all of them open with *qāla*:**

| Verse | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 77-78 | God | **Expulsion and curse** |
| 79 | Iblīs | **A request for respite** |
| 80-81 | God | **The granting of the respite and its limit** |
| 82-83 | Iblīs | **An oath and an exception** |
| 84-85 | God | **The last word** |

**I record this as my own reading:** exchanged speech had been seen twice before in the sūra — in the miḥrāb (21-26) and in the Fire (59-64). **This is the third and the longest.**

**And setting the three scenes side by side, an order emerges:**

| Scene | Verses | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **The miḥrāb** | 21-26 | **A judgment was given** — and a measure was announced to the one judging |
| **The Fire** | 59-64 | **No outcome at all** — the quarrel goes on |
| **On high** | 71-85 | **A judgment was given** — and a respite granted |

**I give this as an observation about the structure.**

### فَٱخْرُجْ مِنْهَا فَإِنَّكَ رَجِيمٌ — the expulsion

**رَجِيم — root ر-ج-م:** to stone, to throw stones. **And from there: driven off, cast out by stoning.**

**The concrete sense of the word is a community's driving someone beyond its bounds by throwing stones.** This is a form of exclusion known in old Arab society.

**فَٱخْرُجْ مِنْهَا — "go out from there".** To what does the pronoun (*minhā*) refer?

| View | Where |
|---|---|
| **The Garden / the station he occupied** | As a place |
| **The company of the angels** (*al-malaʾu'l-aʿlā*) | As the class he belonged to |
| **That state in general** | As a position |

**I do not impose a preference.** **On all three views what is recounted is a *putting outside*** — and this accords with the picture in the word *rajīm*.

### إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ — the first name of the term

**دِين — root د-ي-ن:** debt, requital, judgment and submission. **At the centre of the root is a *relation of requital*:** *dayn* — a debt; *dāna* — he judged, he requited.

**And *yawmu'd-dīn* — "the day of requital".** This construct was treated in `001-fatiha.md`.

### فَأَنظِرْنِىٓ — the request for respite

**أَنْظَرَ — root ن-ظ-ر.** It occurred in the fifteenth verse as *mā yanẓuru* (they do not await): to look and to wait.

**Form IV: *anẓara* — to grant someone a term, to allow him to wait.** In the language of commerce: **to defer a debt.** The Qurʾān uses it in this sense too (al-Baqara 2/280 — *fa-naẓiratun ilā maysara*).

**And the root's three occurrences in the sūra are worth recording:**

| Verse | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| **15** | *mā **yanẓuru** hāʾulāʾi illā ṣayḥatan wāḥida* | **To await** — a single cry |
| **79** | *fa-**anẓirnī*** | **To ask for a term** |
| **80** | *mina'l-**munẓarīn*** | **Those granted a term** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these three occurrences:** the same root conveys a waiting at the beginning of the sūra and a respite at its end. **And in the fifteenth verse those who wait were not asking for a term — Iblīs asks, and receives.**

**That Iblīs addresses Him as *rabbi* must be recorded.** As was recorded in the discussion of al-Baqara, Iblīs does not deny God's existence; **he speaks with Him, asks, and his request is granted.** This dialogue in Ṣād is the plainest ground of that finding.

### إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْوَقْتِ ٱلْمَعْلُومِ — the limit of the term

**Iblīs asked *ilā yawmi yubʿathūn* (until the day they are raised). The answer was *ilā yawmi'l-waqti'l-maʿlūm* (until the day of the known time).**

**Are the two expressions the same? The exegetes differ:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **They are the same** | The request has been granted; the expression is merely given in other words |
| **They differ** | What was asked was the day of the raising; what was given is **a known time** — which may be the first blast of the trumpet, and is before the raising. **The request, that is, was not met in full** |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**This much rests on the wording and can be recorded: the two expressions are not given in the same words.** The request is *yawmi yubʿathūn*, the answer *yawmi'l-waqti'l-maʿlūm*. **And the second carries a note announcing who holds the knowledge: *maʿlūm* — known.** By whom it is known is not stated.

### فَبِعِزَّتِكَ لَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ — and the most striking oath in the sūra

**And now we return to the second verse of the sūra. The ع-ز-ز strand was tabulated above; here I complete it.**

**Iblīs swears an oath. And what he swears by must be recorded: *bi-ʿizzatika* — "by Your might".**

| Verse | *ʿIzza* | Whose | How it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | *fī **ʿizzatin** wa shiqāq* | Of those who disbelieve | A state — **resistance** |
| **9, 66** | *al-**ʿAzīz*** | Of God | A name |
| **23** | ***ʿazzanī** fi'l-khiṭāb* | Of the litigant's opponent | A verb — **to overbear** |
| **82** | *fa-bi-**ʿizzatika*** | **Of God** | **Iblīs's oath** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this distribution:** Iblīs swears by the might of the being he defies. **He uses the reality of what he rejects, that is, as the measure in his own oath.**

**And this is a further ground for the finding in the discussion of al-Baqara:** *"Iblīs assents to the whole list and yet stands on the other side."*

**And a link can be made with the second verse, and I give it as my own reading:** in the second verse of the sūra those who disbelieve were described as *fī ʿizzatin* — with a resistance they supposed to be their own. **In the eighty-second verse Iblīs knows where might lies, and swears by it.** The difference between the two postures is not a difference of knowledge — **it is a difference of doing or not doing what the known requires.**

**أَغْوَىٰ — root غ-و-ي:** to go astray, to lose the way. **Form IV: to lead astray.** *Ghayy* — error, going far from the right road.

**And the verb comes with a double emphasis: *la-ughwiyanna* — *lām* + the *nūn* of emphasis.** **This is the strongest pattern of an oath's answer in Arabic.**

### إِلَّا عِبَادَكَ مِنْهُمُ ٱلْمُخْلَصِينَ — the exception

**Iblīs puts an exception inside his own oath.**

**مُخْلَص — root خ-ل-ص.** It was treated at the forty-sixth verse: **to become pure, to be separated from one's dregs.**

**And the pattern is a passive participle: *mukhlaṣ* — purified, made pure.** **It differs from the active participle *mukhliṣ* (sincere, one who purifies himself):**

| Pattern | Word | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| **Active participle** | *mukhliṣ* | **The person** — the one who purifies himself |
| **Passive participle** | *mukhlaṣ* | **God** — the one who has been purified |

**A variant reading is transmitted: it is said the word is also read *mukhliṣīn* (active).** Because I cannot state with certainty which imam held which preference, I give no names.

**And a link can be made with the forty-sixth verse, and it is a link of wording:**

| Verse | Wording | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **46** | *innā **akhlaṣnāhum** bi-khāliṣa* | Three prophets |
| **83** | *ʿibādaka minhumu'l-**mukhlaṣīn*** | Those Iblīs cannot touch |

**The same root, the same family of patterns.** **I record this as my own reading:** in the forty-sixth verse what the purifying consisted in was stated — *dhikra'd-dār*, the remembrance of the abode. **In the eighty-third verse Iblīs concedes that those who bear that attribute are outside his own reach.**

**And the word *ʿibādaka* (Your servants) must be recorded.** In the sūra the word *ʿabd/ʿibād* had been used of the prophets (17, 30, 41, 44, 45). **Iblīs uses the same word.**

### فَٱلْحَقُّ وَٱلْحَقَّ أَقُولُ — the last word

**And the sūra's chain of *ḥaqq* closes here. It was tabulated above.**

**The construction of the sentence has been disputed among the grammarians, and the disagreement is technical:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **The first is an oath, the second an object** | *fa'l-ḥaqqu* — "by the truth"; *wa'l-ḥaqqa aqūl* — "and I speak the truth" |
| **The first is a subject (*mubtadaʾ*) whose predicate is elided** | "The truth is this…" |
| **Both are objects** | A repetition for emphasis |

**A variant reading is also transmitted:** it is said the first word is read in the nominative (*al-ḥaqqu*) or in the accusative (*al-ḥaqqa*). **Because I cannot state with certainty which imam held which preference, I give no names; the difference of sense is not great.**

**The sense is close on all three views and I do not impose a preference.**

**What is to be recorded is this: the word comes twice over.** And in the sixty-fourth verse of the sūra the quarrel of those in the Fire had been described as *la-ḥaqq*. **Now the last word too is framed with the same word.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra's ح-ق-ق chain:** in the twenty-second verse two litigants had asked a man for judgment *bi'l-ḥaqq*. **In the eighty-fourth verse the one who judges announces the quality of His own speech with the same word.** The sūra names for its own speech, at the end, the measure it asked of man.

### لَأَمْلَأَنَّ جَهَنَّمَ — the answering oath

**مَلَأَ — root م-ل-أ:** to fill. **And this root had occurred twice more in the sūra: *al-malaʾ* (6) and *al-malaʾu'l-aʿlā* (69) — "the company that fills".**

| Verse | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *al-**malaʾ*** | Those who fill an assembly |
| **69** | *al-**mala**ʾu'l-aʿlā* | The company above |
| **85** | *la-am**la**ʾanna jahannam* | **To fill Hell** |

**The same root, three occurrences.** I record this as an observation about a pattern; I am not claiming that the sūra sets up a deliberate play on words.

**And the answering structure of the two oaths must be recorded:**

| Verse | Who | Oath | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **82** | Iblīs | *la-ughwiyannahum* | *ajmaʿīn* — **all of them** |
| **83** | Iblīs | (an exception) | *illā… al-mukhlaṣīn* |
| **85** | God | *la-amlaʾanna* | *minka wa **mimman tabiʿaka**… ajmaʿīn* |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two sentences:** Iblīs says "all of them" and enters an exception. **The answering sentence, however, does not say "all" — it says *mimman tabiʿaka*: "of those who *follow* you".**

**The scope, that is, is not general as Iblīs claimed; it is tied to an act: *tabiʿaka* — to follow.**

**And this joins with the twenty-sixth verse of the sūra:** there David was told *wa lā **tattabiʿi**'l-hawā* — the same root (ت-ب-ع).

| Verse | Expression | What is followed |
|---|---|---|
| **26** | *lā **tattabiʿi**'l-hawā* | **Desire** |
| **85** | *wa mimman **tabiʿaka*** | **Iblīs** |

**This is a shared wording that can be verified, and I record it as my own reading:** the sūra uses in the eighty-fifth verse, as a measure, the very act it forbade in the twenty-sixth. **The verdict is tied not to who Iblīs is but to who follows him.**

## 38/86-88 — قُلْ مَآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ مِنْ أَجْرٍ وَمَآ أَنَا۠ مِنَ ٱلْمُتَكَلِّفِينَ · إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا ذِكْرٌ لِّلْعَٰلَمِينَ · وَلَتَعْلَمُنَّ نَبَأَهُۥ بَعْدَ حِينٍۭ

*Qul mā asʾalukum ʿalayhi min ajrin wa mā ana mina'l-mutakallifīn · In huwa illā dhikrun li'l-ʿālamīn · Wa la-taʿlamunna nabaʾahū baʿda ḥīn*

"**Say: I ask you no wage for this, and I am not one of those who contrive.** It is nothing but a reminder for all the worlds. **And you will surely know its report after a time.**"

### مَآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ مِنْ أَجْرٍ — no wage

**أَجْر — root أ-ج-ر:** the return for a work, a wage. **And the concrete sense of the root: the knitting of a broken bone** (*ajara'l-ʿaẓmu*). The lexicographers make the link thus: **a wage is what "mends" the labour spent.**

**And the pattern of negation is strong: *mā asʾalukum… min ajr* — with *min* intervening, generalising as to kind: "no wage whatever".**

**The Qurʾān repeats this sentence in the mouths of many envoys** (al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/109, 127, 145, 164, 180; Yūnus 10/72; Hūd 11/29, 51, and others). **This, that is, is the common declaration of envoyship.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the ninth and tenth verses of the sūra:** there the other side was asked "are the treasuries with you, is the dominion yours?" **In the eighty-sixth verse the envoy says that on his own side too there is no expectation of a treasury or a dominion.** The sūra closes the question of authority on both sides.

### ٱلْمُتَكَلِّفِين — those who contrive something of their own

**تَكَلَّفَ — root ك-ل-ف.** Its concrete sense: **to load a thing on oneself by force, to make it one's own burden.** *Kulfa* — trouble; *takalluf* — **to make a show of having what one has not, to force a thing.**

**The word conveys two things in Arabic:**

| Sense | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Straining** | To try to do what is not in one's power |
| **Affectation** | To appear other than one is; to add something of one's own |

**And the two senses meet in this verse.**

**Now a link is made with the seventh verse of the sūra, and this link is my own reading:**

**In the seventh verse the other side had said:** *in hādhā illā **ikhtilāq*** — "this is nothing but a fabrication."

**In the eighty-sixth verse the envoy says:** *wa mā ana mina'l-**mutakallifīn*** — "I am not one of those who contrive something of their own."

**The two words are from different roots (خ-ل-ق and ك-ل-ف) but they take up the same accusation.** **And the sūra answers the accusation eighty verses later.**

**Worth recording: the answer is not a defence but a *refusal*.** "I do not fabricate" — and no ground is given. **The ground is offered in the next verse as the Book itself.**

### إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا ذِكْرٌ لِّلْعَٰلَمِينَ — the last name of the Book

**And the backbone root of the sūra comes for the last time: ذ-ك-ر.** It was tabulated above — the last of eight occurrences.

**And the ring closes:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **1** | *wa'l-Qurʾāni **dhi'dh-dhikr*** — by the Qurʾān, possessor of the reminder |
| **49** | *hādhā **dhikr*** — this is a reminder |
| **87** | *in huwa illā **dhikrun** li'l-ʿālamīn* — it is a reminder for all the worlds |

**This is a frame that can be verified from the text.**

**And the note *li'l-ʿālamīn* is new:** in the first verse the Book was named only *dhi'dh-dhikr*; **here those it addresses are stated: all the worlds.**

**عَالَمِين — root ع-ل-م:** to know. *ʿĀlam* — that by which something is known; a set of existing things. **This word was treated in `001-fatiha.md`.**

**I record this as my own reading:** through the sūra those addressed had been narrow — the leading men of Mecca, a community, an envoy. **In the last verses the compass opens.** And this widening is the exact counterpart of the objection in the fourth verse: there it was said *min bayninā* ("from among us") — a narrow frame. **Here *li'l-ʿālamīn* — the widest frame.**

### وَلَتَعْلَمُنَّ نَبَأَهُۥ بَعْدَ حِينٍۭ — the last sentence of the sūra

**And the sūra closes with a sentence about knowing.**

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: *la-taʿlamunna* — *lām* + the *nūn* of emphasis.**

**As was recorded above in the table of the *jawāb al-qasam*, this pattern is the most established form of an oath's answer in Arabic.** **And some exegetes find the answer to the oath of the first verse here.** I tabulated that view there; **I do not impose a preference, but I record the force of the wording once more.**

**نَبَأ — and the word comes for the third and last time in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Report of what |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | *hal atāka **nabaʾu**'l-khaṣm* | **Of the litigants** — an account |
| **67** | *huwa **nabaʾun** ʿaẓīm* | **A mighty report** |
| **88** | *wa la-taʿlamunna **nabaʾahū** baʿda ḥīn* | **Its report** |

**Three occurrences, and all three together with a verb of *hearing / knowing*:** *atāka* (has it reached you), *qul* (say), *la-taʿlamunna* (you will know).

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra first offers the report with a question (21), then announces its greatness (67), and at the end ties the knowing of it to a time (88).

**بَعْدَ حِينٍ — "after a time"**

**حِين — root ح-ي-ن:** an indefinite stretch of time. **And this is the distinguishing side of the word: its length is not specified.** It may be a moment, or it may be a long age.

**And the word occurs for the second time in the sūra. In the third verse it was said:** *wa-lāta **ḥīna** manāṣ* — "it was no longer the hour of escape."

| Verse | Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *wa-lāta **ḥīna** manāṣ* | **The hour had passed** — being late |
| **88** | *baʿda **ḥīn*** | **The hour will come** — waiting |

**This is a shared wording that can be verified, and it binds the beginning of the sūra to its end.**

**And it joins with the eighth verse as well:** *bal **lammā** yadhūqū ʿadhāb* — "they have not yet tasted the punishment." **There *lammā* conveyed an expectation; here that expectation is given its name: *baʿda ḥīn*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of these three verses (3, 8, 88):** from beginning to end the sūra dwells on an **interval of time.** **Knowing is not refused; it is deferred.** And the mood of the sentence says this plainly: *la-taʿlamunna* — **"you will surely know".** Whether it will be known is not the matter in dispute; **when it will be known is.**

### On the last sentence of the sūra

**And one last observation.**

**The sūra does not end with a threat.** There is a warning in the last sentence, but the predicate of the sentence is not *ʿadhāb* or *ʿiqāb* — it is **to know.**

**This can be compared with the close recorded in `050-kaf.md` at 50/45:** there too the sūra does not end with a frightening, and the last command was a reminding.

| Sūra | The predicate of the last sentence | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **Qāf 50/45** | *fa-**dhakkir** bi'l-Qurʾān* | **A command** — to the envoy |
| **Ṣād 38/88** | *wa la-**taʿlamunna** nabaʾah* | **A report** — to the one addressed |

**The two sūras look in two different directions: Qāf tells the envoy what he is to do, Ṣād tells the one addressed what will happen.**

## The sūra as a whole — looking back

**In eighty-eight verses Sūrat Ṣād does three separate works and binds all three together with the same words:** it draws up an inventory of an objection, it names nine prophets, and it closes with a scene of judgment.

**By the time it is done, it has brought back nearly every one of the words it used at its opening.**

### The verifiable rings of the sūra

**What follows are data of the text; what is interpretation is the claim that they form an order, and I offer that as an observation.**

| Opening | Close |

### The method of the sūra

**As it is read in this commentary, the method of Ṣād is this:**

**One. The objection is given first by being inventoried (4-11).** Four separate objections are counted, and all four share the same lack: **in none of them is there any evidence.** Astonishment, insinuation, "we have not heard" and doubt — all four take the speaker's own store for a measure. **And as was recorded in `050-kaf.md` at 50/2, astonishment is a measure not of knowledge but of familiarity.**

**Two. The answer is given not by argument but by example (17-48).** Over thirty-two verses nine prophets are named. **And this section does not answer the other side's objection directly;** in its place it shows what the thing called "fabrication" is.

**Three. When the section is finished it is named with a single word (49): *hādhā dhikr*.** The sūra says what the thing it has recounted is after it has recounted it.

**Four. Two tableaux are set side by side (49-58) and no comparison is made.** The passage between them takes a single word: *hādhā* (55).

**Five. A scene is shown (59-64) and its being real is separately recorded: *inna dhālika la-ḥaqq*.**

**Six. And last of all, the first instance of the matter is recounted (71-85).** The sūra reverses the chronology: the oldest event is placed at the very end.

### What stands at the centre of the sūra

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra's distribution of words:**

**Ṣād is a sūra of *measure*.** And the question it asks is this: **on what do you rest when you give judgment?**

**And the sūra shows four separate wrong answers to this question:**

| Verse | Who | What his measure is | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4-5** | The Meccans | **Habit** — what I have not seen before does not happen | *Shayʾun ʿujāb* |
| **6** | *al-malaʾ* | **An assumed intent** — there is a purpose behind it | *Shayʾun yurād* |
| **23-24** | (A scene) | **The account of one side** | *Fatannāhu* — a test |
| **76** | Iblīs | **His own analogy** — fire is superior to clay | *Fa'khruj minhā* |

**And it gives one right answer, twice and in the same words:**

> *fa'ḥkum bayna'n-nāsi **bi'l-ḥaqqi** wa lā tattabiʿi'l-hawā* (26)

**The measure is not the person himself; it is something outside him and prior to him.**

### Two lists

**And the sūra makes two lists.**

**The first is God's list:** *al-muṣṭafayna'l-**akhyār*** (47), *wa kullun mina'l-**akhyār*** (48).

**The second is people's list:** *rijālan kunnā **naʿudduhum** mina'l-**ashrār*** (62).

**And in the sixty-second verse it is seen that the second list does not hold.** Those who counted cannot find those they counted.

**I record this as my own reading and regard it as the most concrete lesson of the sūra:** **people classify one another.** The sūra does not forbid this; **it only says that whether a list holds will become clear somewhere else.**

**And this agrees with the principle recorded in `STYLE.md`:** *"No wholesale ruling is issued about an ethnic or religious group; what the verse describes are attributes, and whoever carries that attribute is included in it."*

**The sūra itself behaves in exactly this way:** in the eighty-fifth verse the verdict is tied not to a person but to an act — *wa mimman **tabiʿaka***.

### Where the sūra leaves us

**The sūra ends not with a question but with a report. But the content of the report is not given.**

> *wa la-taʿlamunna **nabaʾahū** baʿda ḥīn*

**What will be known is not stated. Nor is it stated when it will be known** — *ḥīn* is a stretch of time of indefinite length.

**The one thing stated is that it will be known.**

**And the third verse of the sūra had already given the counterpart of this sentence:** there were people crying out there, and *wa-lāta ḥīna manāṣ* — it was no longer the hour of escape. **There is a moment of knowing, that is, and that moment may be too late to do anything.**

**This is what the sūra leaves with the one addressed, and I record it as a question — the text does not ask it, I ask it:**

**What does it say about "now" that one is going to learn "later" whether a thing is true?**

**And the twenty-ninth verse of the sūra had already given the shortest answer that can be given to this question:**

> *kitābun anzalnāhu ilayka mubārakun **li-yaddabbarū** āyātih*

**The reason for existing that the Book announces for itself is framed so as not to wait for that "later": to get behind a thing, to think about its end.**

**And as was recorded in `047-muhammed.md`, *tadabbur* is, by the picture in its root, getting behind a thing and looking at its end. That is, the knowledge deferred in the last verse of the sūra is tied to an act in the twenty-ninth.**

**The term is granted — and what is to be done within the term is stated as well.**

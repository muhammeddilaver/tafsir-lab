# 46. Sūrat al-Aḥqāf

**Thirty-five verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the word **ٱلْأَحْقَاف** in the twenty-first verse: the **sand dunes** named as the homeland of ʿĀd.

The sūra is the last of the seven sūras that open with *ḥā-mīm*. The shared feature of this group is that the Book is spoken of immediately after the opening; al-Aḥqāf opens that way too.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-14 | The demand for evidence, the objections, standing straight | *Jazāʾan bimā kānū yaʿmalūn* (14) |
| **II** | 15-20 | Two portraits of a human being: the one who gives thanks and the one who refuses | *Bimā kuntum tafsuqūn* (20) |
| **III** | 21-28 | The story of ʿĀd — those who greeted the cloud with joy | *Wa dhālika ifkuhum wa mā kānū yaftarūn* (28) |
| **IV** | 29-35 | The listening of the jinn, and the close | *Fa-hal yuhlaku illā'l-qawmu'l-fāsiqūn* (35) |

**The backbone of the sūra is a question, and it is asked openly in the fourth verse: "have you any evidence in hand?"** Every block after that treats one side of this question — those who demand evidence (I), the two stances taken before evidence (II), those who do not recognise the evidence when it comes (III), those who recognise it the moment they hear it (IV).

---

## 46/1-3 — حمٓ · تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَكِيمِ · مَا خَلَقْنَا ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَآ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَأَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى

*Ḥā-mīm · Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥakīm · Mā khalaqna's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā illā bi'l-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā*

"Ḥā-mīm. The sending down of this Book is from God, the Mighty, the Wise. We created the heavens, the earth and what is between them only **in truth and for a determined term**."

### حمٓ

**The general note on the disconnected letters has already been entered elsewhere in this commentary** (`068-kalem.md` and `050-kaf.md`); I do not repeat it here. **The summary is this: there is no certain knowledge about the meaning of these letters; many explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries and none of them makes a claim to certainty.** Per the method (`STYLE.md`), claims resting on letter-and-number reckoning are not entered into.

**What can be recorded is a fact of word order that can be verified:** in almost every sūra that opens with *ḥā-mīm*, the Book is named right after the opening. So here too: the second verse begins with *tanzīlu'l-kitāb*.

### تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ — how the sentence is built

**The sentence is not built as "we sent down the Book."** Its subject is a **verbal noun**: *tanzīl* — "the being sent down". That is, what is placed first is not the verb but **the act itself**.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** at the centre of the sentence stands not the act but **where the act comes from** — *mina'llāh*. The sūra settles the source of the Book in its first sentence; for that is precisely what will be in dispute from the fourth verse on.

**ٱلْعَزِيز** — root **ع-ز-ز**: to be firm, unreachable, above others. The concrete core of the word's meaning is explained by the lexicographers by way of **"hard, impassable ground"**. **ٱلْحَكِيم** — root **ح-ك-م**: to hold back and keep in place; the root's connection with *ḥakama* (the bit of a bridle) was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/269.

**That the two names come together is worth recording:** one conveys **power**, the other **right placement**. Power is not named on its own.

### بِٱلْحَقِّ وَأَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى — two qualifications

Two qualifications are placed upon the creating:

| Qualification | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| **بِٱلْحَقّ** | **Not in vain** — upon a reality and a purpose |
| **أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى** | **Not endless** — with a term whose name has been set |

**Together the two draw a limit.** I record this as my own reading: before it enters the discussion of the hereafter, the sūra says that **the universe has a term**. The assumption that "everything will go on like this" is thereby removed at the very outset.

***Musammā* — passive participle: "named".** The term is not merely determined; **its name has been set** — and who knows that name is not said. The same pattern was used in al-Wāqiʿa 56/50 for *yawmin maʿlūm* (a known day) and was treated there: **not indeterminate, but closed to man.**

## 46/4 — قُلْ أَرَءَيْتُم مَّا تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ أَرُونِى مَاذَا خَلَقُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ أَمْ لَهُمْ شِرْكٌ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ ٱئْتُونِى بِكِتَٰبٍ مِّن قَبْلِ هَٰذَآ أَوْ أَثَٰرَةٍ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ

*Qul araʾaytum mā tadʿūna min dūni'llāhi arūnī mādhā khalaqū mina'l-arḍi am lahum shirkun fi's-samāwāt · Iʾtūnī bi-kitābin min qabli hādhā aw athāratin min ʿilmin in kuntum ṣādiqīn*

"Say: have you seen those you call upon apart from God? Show me: what have they created of the earth? Or have they a share in the heavens? **Bring me a Book sent down before this, or a remnant of knowledge** — if you are telling the truth."

### The verse is a demand for evidence — and the form of the demand must be recorded

**The sentence has three steps:**

1. **Show me** — *arūnī mādhā khalaqū* (a work visible to the eye)
2. **The claim of partnership** — *am lahum shirkun fi's-samāwāt*
3. **A written or transmitted support** — *iʾtūnī bi-kitābin … aw athāratin min ʿilm*

**That is, the verse asks for two kinds of evidence: observation and transmitted report.** It leaves no third way.

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the verse itself:** what is demanded is not feeling, habit or numbers. **And the grounds the other side will put forward throughout the sūra will be exactly these** — the forefathers (in the example of ʿĀd at 21-22), social precedence (11), habit.

### أَثَٰرَةٍ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ — "a remnant of knowledge"

The root **أ-ث-ر**: **a trace, a mark left behind.** *Athar* — a footprint, a remnant, a report handed down. The root has occurred at several places in this commentary (`087-ala.md`, `079-naziat.md`, `059-hasr.md`).

**The lexicographers transmit differing readings of what the word answers to here:** a crumb of knowledge left over from the past, a report handed down, or a fragment of a written document.

**The point they share is this, and it shows what the verse is doing: what is asked for is not even a whole Book — a *remnant*.** **The demand is presented reduced to its minimum.** I record this as an observation: the request for evidence is framed not so as to press the other side hard but **from the lowest threshold**.

## 46/5-6 — وَمَنْ أَضَلُّ مِمَّن يَدْعُوا۟ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَن لَّا يَسْتَجِيبُ لَهُۥٓ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ وَهُمْ عَن دُعَآئِهِمْ غَٰفِلُونَ · وَإِذَا حُشِرَ ٱلنَّاسُ كَانُوا۟ لَهُمْ أَعْدَآءً وَكَانُوا۟ بِعِبَادَتِهِمْ كَٰفِرِينَ

"Who is more astray than one who calls, apart from God, upon that which cannot answer him until the Day of Resurrection? And they are **unaware** of the calling of these."

### The two layers of unansweredness

The verse describes the state of the one called upon in two degrees:

| Degree | Expression | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Lā yastajību lahū ilā yawmi'l-qiyāma* | **It cannot answer** — with a time limit set: until the Resurrection |
| 2 | *Wa hum ʿan duʿāʾihim ghāfilūn* | **It does not even know of it** |

**The second degree is heavier than the first, and the order is deliberate:** to be unable to answer is a weakness; to be unaware means **that the relation was never established at all**.

**وَإِذَا حُشِرَ ٱلنَّاسُ كَانُوا۟ لَهُمْ أَعْدَآءً** — and in the end the relation does not merely break, it **turns into its opposite.** The same idea occurs in Sūrat Maryam and Sūrat al-Furqān as well; **what is to be recorded here is that the sūra begins to work the idea of "the thing you took for a support" on its very first page.** In the twenty-sixth verse the same idea will be built not for an idol but for **the organs of sense**.

## 46/7-8 — وَإِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتُنَا بَيِّنَٰتٍ قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لِلْحَقِّ لَمَّا جَآءَهُمْ هَٰذَا سِحْرٌ مُّبِينٌ

"When Our verses are recited to them as clear signs, those who disbelieve say of the truth when it comes to them: **'this is plain magic'**."

### Two words set face to face

**بَيِّنَٰت** (clear) and **مُّبِين** (plain) — **both from the same root: ب-ي-ن.**

| Who | Word | Of what |
|---|---|---|
| The text | *bayyināt* | Of the verses |
| The other side | *mubīn* | Of "magic" |

**The same root, in the same sentence, in two opposed verdicts.** I record this as an observation about the word order: **the clarity is granted by both sides; the dispute is over *what* the clear thing is.** The other side does not say "we do not understand"; it grants the effect and rejects the source.

**The same stance had been recorded at al-Qamar 54/2 as well** (*wa in yaraw āyatan yuʿriḍū wa yaqūlū siḥrun mustamirr*); it was treated in `054-kamer.md`.

**أَمْ يَقُولُونَ ٱفْتَرَىٰهُ** — the second objection: "he made it up". **The answer leaves the consequence upon the one who objects:** *qul ini'ftaraytuhū fa-lā tamlikūna lī mina'llāhi shayʾā* — "if I have made it up, you cannot protect me against God."

## 46/9 — قُلْ مَا كُنتُ بِدْعًا مِّنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ وَمَآ أَدْرِى مَا يُفْعَلُ بِى وَلَا بِكُمْ

*Qul mā kuntu bidʿan mina'r-rusuli wa mā adrī mā yufʿalu bī wa lā bikum*

"Say: I am not **an unprecedented one** among the messengers. Nor do I know what will be done with me or with you."

### بِدْع — root: ب-د-ع

**The sense of the root: to bring forth for the first time something that has had no prior example.** *Badīʿ*, *ibdāʿ* and *bidʿa* are from the same root. The root occurred in `057-hadid.md`.

**What the sentence does:** it says that what has come is **not a new thing**, but the continuation of a chain. **Here is the answer to the demand for "an earlier Book" made in the fourth verse:** the claim places itself not outside the earlier Books but along their line.

### وَمَآ أَدْرِى مَا يُفْعَلُ بِى وَلَا بِكُم

**This sentence is one of the most limiting statements the Qurʾān has its Prophet say, and it must be treated honestly.**

**What is said is plain: that he does not know what will befall him in this world.** The scope of this statement has been debated in the classical commentaries:

| Reading | Scope |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The outcome in this world** — what will befall him, what sort of life he will live |
| 2 | **Knowledge of particulars** — the particular items beyond what has been made known in general |

**I do not impose a preference.** What is to be recorded is this: **the sentence conveys a limit, and this limit agrees with the frame set in the third verse** — the term has been given a name, but it is not man who set the name.

**إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ** — and immediately after, where the source lies is stated: **the knowledge is not from himself.**

## 46/10 — وَشَهِدَ شَاهِدٌ مِّنۢ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ عَلَىٰ مِثْلِهِۦ فَـَٔامَنَ وَٱسْتَكْبَرْتُمْ

"A witness from the Children of Israel bore witness to the like of it and believed; but you were arrogant."

**The verse gives the second answer to the demand for "an earlier Book" in the fourth verse:** the testimony of someone who knows that Book.

**Who the witness is is not said in the verse.** Various names are transmitted in the classical commentaries; **I do not pass these on as certain knowledge.** Since the verse itself gives no name, I record **that the verdict is tied not to a name but to a stance**: *fa-āmana wa'stakbartum* — one believed, the others grew proud.

**ٱسْتَكْبَرْتُمْ** — Form X: **to see oneself as great, to lay claim to greatness.** Its opposite is not faith but **surrender.** The verse ties the ground of the refusal not to a lack of knowledge but to **a stance** — and read together with the fourth verse of the sūra this is a consistency: when evidence comes to the side that demanded evidence, what appears is not a discussion of evidence.

---

## 46/11 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَوْ كَانَ خَيْرًا مَّا سَبَقُونَآ إِلَيْهِ

*"Those who disbelieve said of those who believe: **had it been anything good, they would not have reached it before us.**"*

### The logic of the objection

**This is the most striking objection in the sūra, because it does not touch the content at all.**

**Its structure is this:** what is good comes first to the one who is superior. If these people received it before us, then it is not something good. **That is, the measure is not what has come but who received it.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the objection is not an item of evidence but an **assumption about rank**. And this assumption falls under neither of the two kinds of evidence asked for in the fourth verse (observation and transmitted report).

**Other examples of this stance have been treated in this commentary:** in `080-abese.md`, the turning towards the man of standing while the blind man who came was left unattended; in `104-humeze.md`, the amassing of wealth being taken for superiority. **The shared point: reading worth off the position of the one who carries it.**

**وَإِذْ لَمْ يَهْتَدُوا۟ بِهِۦ فَسَيَقُولُونَ هَٰذَآ إِفْكٌ قَدِيمٌ** — "since they have not found guidance by it, they will say 'this is an old fabrication'."

**إِفْك** — root **أ-ف-ك**: to turn a thing away from its direction, to turn it inside out. **That is, the word used for "fabrication" means "turned about".** The word will come back in the twenty-eighth verse, and there it will describe **their own condition**: *wa dhālika ifkuhum* — "that was their fabrication."

## 46/12 — وَمِن قَبْلِهِۦ كِتَٰبُ مُوسَىٰٓ إِمَامًا وَرَحْمَةً وَهَٰذَا كِتَٰبٌ مُّصَدِّقٌ لِّسَانًا عَرَبِيًّا لِّيُنذِرَ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ وَبُشْرَىٰ لِلْمُحْسِنِينَ

"Before it there was the Book of Moses (Mūsā), **as a leader and a mercy**. And this is a confirming Book, **in the Arabic tongue**: to warn those who do wrong, and as good news for those who do good."

**إِمَامًا** — root **أ-م-م**: to go in front; the direction intended. *Imām* — **the one who goes ahead, the one by looking at whom others walk.** The word's sense of road and direction comes from this root.

**That the Book is qualified as a "leader" is worth recording:** the Book is named not as a source of knowledge but as **something that walks in front**.

**And the verse is part of the answer given to the demand in the fourth verse.** There *bi-kitābin min qabli hādhā* — "a Book before this" — had been asked for. **Here that Book is named, and the relation of this text to it is stated: *muṣaddiq* — confirming.**

**لِّسَانًا عَرَبِيًّا** — **the core of the root ع-ر-ب, "clarity, not concealing what one means", was analysed in `056-vakia.md` at 56/37 (*ʿurub*); I rely on that treatment.** The name of the language is named together with the sense of clarity in its root.

**And the last two words of the verse separate two distinct tasks:** *li-yundhira* (to warn) and *bushrā* (good news). **The same text does two distinct jobs according to whom it addresses.**

## 46/13-14 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"Surely **for those who say 'our Lord is God' and then stand straight** there is no fear; nor shall they grieve."

### قَالُوا۟ … ثُمَّ ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟

**The connective between the two verbs is worth recording: *thumma*.**

In Arabic *thumma*, unlike *fāʾ*, conveys **a distance between the two.** That is, there is a stretch of time between the saying and the standing straight: saying is done in a moment, standing straight lasts.

**ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟** — Form X, root **ق-و-م**: **to stand straight, to continue without bending.** The root's connection with *qayyūm* was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/255: to stand, and to hold standing.

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse sets two conditions, and the second is spread over time. **This is also what will be related through the rest of the sūra:** when ʿĀd saw a cloud (24), when a man reaches the age of forty (15), when the jinn heard a text (29) — all of them are about **what is done beyond the first moment**.

**فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ** — this construction occurs in many places in the Qurʾān, and **it closes off both directions of time:** *khawf* belongs to the future, *ḥuzn* to the past. **That is, neither what waits ahead nor what is left behind.**

## 46/15 — وَوَصَّيْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ إِحْسَٰنًا حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُۥ كُرْهًا وَوَضَعَتْهُ كُرْهًا وَحَمْلُهُۥ وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ ثَلَٰثُونَ شَهْرًا

*Wa waṣṣayna'l-insāna bi-wālidayhi iḥsānā · Ḥamalathu ummuhū kurhan wa waḍaʿathu kurhā · Wa ḥamluhū wa fiṣāluhū thalāthūna shahrā*

"We have **counselled** man to do good to his parents. His mother carried him **with hardship** and gave birth to him **with hardship**. His carrying and his weaning are thirty months."

### The second block begins: two portraits

**The first block had closed at the fourteenth verse.** From the fifteenth verse to the twentieth the sūra sets up two pictures of a human being, and both are described **by way of the same relationship**: the relation with one's parents.

**This is the most striking choice in the structure of the sūra, and I record it as my own reading:** the first block related those who demanded evidence and did not take it. **The second block picks the piece of evidence that lies nearest: a person's own birth.** The same method will be at work in the twenty-sixth verse too — the ears, the eyes and the hearts that had been given to ʿĀd.

### وَصَّيْنَا — root: و-ص-ي

**The root: to bind one thing to another, to join them; and to advise, to bequeath.** The lexicographers record the root's sense of "joining" (*waṣā'n-nabt* — the twining of a plant about itself).

**The root has occurred in this commentary in `103-asr.md` and `090-beled.md`** (*tawāṣaw bi'l-ḥaqq*, *tawāṣaw bi'ṣ-ṣabr*) — there it was in the **reciprocal** form (Form VI). **Here it is Form II, and the agent is God: *waṣṣaynā*.**

**The choice of form is worth recording:** the ruling is given not with the verb "we commanded" but with the verb **"we counselled"**.

### كُرْهًا — twice

**The word is repeated twice:** *ḥamalathu ummuhū **kurhan** wa waḍaʿathu **kurhā**.*

The root **ك-ر-ه**: dislike, hardship, heaviness. The root was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/216 (*wa huwa kurhun lakum*) and at 2/256 (*lā ikrāha fi'd-dīn*).

**What the repetition does is this: two distinct hardships are counted separately** — the carrying and the giving birth. **They are not merged and passed over in a single word.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse gives the ground of the counsel not in an abstract language of rights but **through the description of a bodily hardship**. And the side that bears the hardship is named: *ummuhū*. The counsel is for the two of them (*wālidayh*); the detail is for the mother.

### ثَلَٰثُونَ شَهْرًا — thirty months

**A number is given: the carrying and the weaning together are thirty months.**

**It is transmitted that in the classical works of law this verse is read together with al-Baqara 2/233 (*ḥawlayni kāmilayn* — two full years of suckling), and that a conclusion about the minimum term of pregnancy is drawn from the difference between the two.**

**This is an inference and must be marked as one:** the verse does not make this calculation itself. **I pass it on as a transmitted inference, not as the literal ruling of the verse.** Per the method (`STYLE.md`), I do not establish a legal conclusion.

### حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ وَبَلَغَ أَرْبَعِينَ سَنَةً قَالَ رَبِّ أَوْزِعْنِىٓ أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ

"Then, **when he reaches his full strength and reaches forty years**, he says: My Lord! **Move me** … to give thanks for Your favour."

**أَشُدّ** — root **ش-د-د**: strength, firmness. *Ashudd* is the age at which the strength of body and of mind is complete.

**The age of forty is named explicitly only here in the Qurʾān.** It is transmitted that in the classical commentaries this is named as the age at which maturity is complete; **I do not draw a firm ruling about a limit from it.**

**أَوْزِعْنِىٓ** — root **و-ز-ع**: the sense given by the lexicographers is **to take hold of something and steer it, to rein it in, to make a task dear to someone and turn him towards it**. The word's connection with the sense of "keeping an army in order" is recorded.

**The content of the prayer is worth recording:** the man does not ask for wealth, for long life or for success — he asks **to be able to give thanks**. That is, what is asked for is not a thing given but **the stance to be taken towards what is given.**

**وَأَصْلِحْ لِى فِى ذُرِّيَّتِىٓ** — "and set right for me my offspring." **The prayer reaches out beyond himself.** The fifteenth verse had begun with the parents; the man of forty names his children. **The two ends of the chain meet in a single verse.**

## 46/16 — أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ نَتَقَبَّلُ عَنْهُمْ أَحْسَنَ مَا عَمِلُوا۟ وَنَتَجَاوَزُ عَن سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ

"These are the ones from whom We accept **the best of what they did** and whose evil deeds We **pass over**."

### أَحْسَنَ مَا عَمِلُوا۟ — "the best of it"

**The sentence does not say "we accept what they did": *aḥsana mā ʿamilū* — the best of what they did.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the measure is taken not from the average but from **the peak**. **And in answer to it comes the second verb: *natajāwazu ʿan sayyiʾātihim* — the passing over of the evil deeds.**

**ت-ج-ا-و-ز** — root **ج-و-ز**: to pass by a place and go on. **From Form VI, *tajāwuz*: to go past without stopping over it.** The word conveys not so much erasing as **not stopping**.

**What the two verbs build together is worth recording:** one takes the best, the other goes past the worst. **That is, the reckoning is set up in the person's favour from both ends.**

**وَعْدَ ٱلصِّدْقِ ٱلَّذِى كَانُوا۟ يُوعَدُونَ** — "the **promise of truth** that they were promised." The construct *waʿdu'ṣ-ṣidq* conveys not the content of the promise but **its quality**: the promise that will be kept.

---

## 46/17 — وَٱلَّذِى قَالَ لِوَٰلِدَيْهِ أُفٍّ لَّكُمَآ

*Wa'lladhī qāla li-wālidayhi uffin lakumā a-taʿidāninī an ukhraja wa qad khalati'l-qurūnu min qablī*

"And the one who says to his parents, '**ugh to you both!** Do you threaten me that I shall be brought out of the earth, when so many generations have passed away before me?'…"

### The counter-portrait — and the same relationship

**This verse carries the same structure as the portrait in the fifteenth verse, and the two can be compared item by item:**

| | 46/15 | 46/17 |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship | Parents | Parents |
| The words | *Rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura* | *Uffin lakumā* |
| Direction | A prayer for thanks and for his offspring | Objection and refusal |
| Evidence | His own birth | *Qad khalati'l-qurūnu min qablī* — the generations past |

**The evidence the second portrait puts forward is striking:** "many generations passed before me" — **that is, none came back.** This is the demand for evidence in the first block (46/4) built the other way round: **there evidence was being asked for; here *absence* is offered as evidence.**

### أُفّ

**The word is a noun standing for a verb (*ism al-fiʿl*): a sound conveying weariness, disgust and constriction.** The lexicographers give differing explanations of its origin; the point they share is that the word carries **not a meaning but a state**.

**The same word is forbidden towards parents at al-Isrāʾ 17/23** (*fa-lā taqul lahumā uff*). **What is seen when the two verses are read together is this:** the smallest utterance forbidden there comes here as the first sentence of the portrait.

**وَهُمَا يَسْتَغِيثَانِ ٱللَّهَ وَيْلَكَ ءَامِنْ** — "and the two of them, calling on God for help, say 'woe to you, believe!'"

***Istighātha*** — root **غ-و-ث**: the cry for rescue of one who is drowning or in straits. **The choice of the word shows the position of the parents: like someone trying not to persuade but to save.**

**فَيَقُولُ مَا هَٰذَآ إِلَّآ أَسَٰطِيرُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ** — the answer comes out at the same door as *ifkun qadīm* (an old fabrication) in the eleventh verse: **to invalidate a thing by counting it as belonging to the past.**

## 46/18 — أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْقَوْلُ فِىٓ أُمَمٍ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِن قَبْلِهِم مِّنَ ٱلْجِنِّ وَٱلْإِنسِ

"These are the ones against whom the word has been fulfilled, among **communities of jinn and of men** that had passed away before them."

**حَقَّ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْقَوْلُ** — "the word became due against them." The root **ح-ق-ق**: to be fixed, to settle into place. **That is, the verdict given has found its place in their case.**

**فِىٓ أُمَمٍ قَدْ خَلَتْ** — and the person is not named on his own: **he is placed inside the communities that passed away before him.**

**This turns back upon itself the evidence of the portrait in the seventeenth verse.** There the man had said *qad khalati'l-qurūnu min qablī* — "many generations passed before me, and none came back." **The verse takes the same expression (*qad khalat*) and ties it somewhere else: those who have passed away are not an item of evidence but a line the person himself belongs to.**

**This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra** — and, one verse apart, two opposed uses of the same verbal root.

**مِّنَ ٱلْجِنِّ وَٱلْإِنسِ** — and the communities are named from the two kinds. **In the fourth block of the sūra (29-32) the listening of a group of jinn, and their turning back, will be related.** What is seen when the two verses are read together is this: **the same two kinds occur in one place as those held responsible, in another as the first to recognise.**

## 46/19-20 — وَلِكُلٍّ دَرَجَٰتٌ مِّمَّا عَمِلُوا۟ · أَذْهَبْتُمْ طَيِّبَٰتِكُمْ فِى حَيَاتِكُمُ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱسْتَمْتَعْتُم بِهَا

"For everyone there are degrees according to what they did… **In your life of this world you used up all your good things and took your enjoyment of them.**"

### أَذْهَبْتُمْ طَيِّبَٰتِكُمْ

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: *ṭayyibātikum* — "your good things".** That is, it is granted that they were given; **it is not said that they were taken unjustly.**

**The reproach is directed not at their having been taken but at their having been *used up*.** The verb is *adhhaba* — **Form IV: to take away, to bring to an end.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the choice of words:** the verse does not forbid the good things of this world; it speaks of **the whole of the portion having been spent in one place**. The same measure was set up in al-Baqara 2/200-202 by way of two prayers — there too it was said that the one who asks only for this world has no portion left in the hereafter. **The two verses meet at the same point.**

**بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَسْتَكْبِرُونَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ ٱلْحَقِّ وَبِمَا كُنتُمْ تَفْسُقُونَ** — the ground is tied to two verbs: **growing proud** and **stepping outside the bounds.** The same root had occurred in the tenth verse (*wa'stakbartum*). **The second block closes with the diagnosis laid down in the first block.**

## 46/21-25 — ʿĀd: the cloud greeted with joy

*Wa'dhkur akhā ʿĀd · Idh andhara qawmahū bi'l-Aḥqāf*

"Remember the brother of ʿĀd: when he warned his people **in al-Aḥqāf**…"

### ٱلْأَحْقَاف — the name of the sūra

**The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.** Its singular is *ḥiqf*.

The root **ح-ق-ف** — **it is analysed here for the first time in this commentary.** The sense the lexicographers give: **a long, curving dune of sand that the wind has driven and heaped up.** The root carries a sense of **bending and curving**; *iḥqawqafa* — it bent, it curved.

**Whether the word is a place name or a description of terrain is debated in the classical commentaries;** the majority transmit that it points to a sandy region in the south of the Arabian peninsula. **I make no firm geographical determination.**

**But the word itself is worth recording, and it is directly connected with the story:** *aḥqāf* means sand **heaped up by the wind**. **And what will destroy the people is wind.** That is, the name of the homeland carries the material of what is to come.

**I record this as my own reading; its support is the dictionary sense of the word, not an association built after the fact.**

### فَلَمَّا رَأَوْهُ عَارِضًا مُّسْتَقْبِلَ أَوْدِيَتِهِمْ قَالُوا۟ هَٰذَا عَارِضٌ مُّمْطِرُنَا

"When they saw it as **a cloud** coming towards their valleys, they said, 'this is a cloud that will bring us rain'."

**عَارِض** — root **ع-ر-ض**: to come broadside, to stretch right across the horizon. The word is used for **a cloud that covers the horizon**. The root has occurred in `002-bakara.md`, `057-hadid.md` and `054-kamer.md`.

**The construction of the scene is striking, and it advances in three steps:**

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | **They see** — a cloud on the horizon |
| 2 | **They interpret** — "it will bring us rain" |
| 3 | **They are corrected** — *bal huwa mā'staʿjaltum bih* — "no, it is what you asked to be hastened" |

**That is, the destruction does not come as something unrecognised: it comes in the guise of the thing most gladly welcomed.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the people's own sentence:** for a community living in the desert, a cloud on the horizon **is life itself.** The verse relays this joy of theirs and shows where the error lies: **what they saw was true; the verdict they gave was false.** The cloud really was coming.

**This overlaps with the demand for evidence in the fourth verse of the sūra:** seeing is not enough; **one must know what the thing seen is.**

### رِيحٌ فِيهَا عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ · تُدَمِّرُ كُلَّ شَىْءٍۭ بِأَمْرِ رَبِّهَا

**تَدْمِير** — root **د-م-ر**: to destroy, to level to the ground. **The root occurs here for the first time in this commentary.** The lexicographers also record the root's sense of "entering a place without permission" (*damara ʿalayhim*): **to get inside and turn everything upside down.**

**كُلَّ شَىْءٍۭ بِأَمْرِ رَبِّهَا** — "everything, by the command of its Lord." And immediately after: **فَأَصْبَحُوا۟ لَا يُرَىٰٓ إِلَّا مَسَٰكِنُهُمْ** — "**they became such that nothing was to be seen but their dwellings.**"

**The apparent tension between "destroying everything" and "their dwellings remained" is explained by the lexicographers as follows:** the expression *kullu shayʾ* is limited by its context in Arabic — everything within the area the destruction reached. **I pass this explanation on as it is transmitted.**

**That what remained were *dwellings* is worth recording:** what is left standing is what man made; what has gone is man himself.

---

## 46/26 — وَلَقَدْ مَكَّنَّٰهُمْ فِيمَآ إِن مَّكَّنَّٰكُمْ فِيهِ وَجَعَلْنَا لَهُمْ سَمْعًا وَأَبْصَٰرًا وَأَفْـِٔدَةً فَمَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُمْ سَمْعُهُمْ وَلَآ أَبْصَٰرُهُمْ وَلَآ أَفْـِٔدَتُهُم مِّن شَىْءٍ

"We had given them means We have not given you. We gave them hearing, eyes and **hearts**. But neither their hearing nor their eyes nor their hearts availed them anything."

### Three organs, twice

**The structure of the verse is built upon a repetition:** the same three words are counted first in the sentence of **giving**, then in the sentence of **being of no use**.

| Order | Organ | Its work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Samʿ* — hearing | **To hear** |
| 2 | *Abṣār* — eyes | **To see** |
| 3 | *Afʾida* — hearts | **To grasp** |

**The order itself carries information:** it runs from the outside inwards — what is heard, what is seen, and at last what is weighed within.

**أَفْـِٔدَة** — root **ف-ء-د**: the core the lexicographers record is **the burning of an ember**; *fuʾād* is used for the **kindled, moving** side of the heart. **How this root differs from *qalb* was treated in `056-vakia.md` and in `053-necm.md`; I do not repeat it here.**

**فَمَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُمْ … مِّن شَىْءٍ** — "availed them nothing at all."

**I record this as my own reading, and its support is the verse's own repetition:** the instruments were given complete. **The verse does not speak of an impossibility; it speaks of a means left unused.** In the twenty-fourth verse they **had seen** the cloud — the eye was working.

**إِذْ كَانُوا۟ يَجْحَدُونَ بِـَٔايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ** — and the cause is stated: **جحود** — root **ج-ح-د**: to deny a thing knowingly. The lexicographers also record the root's sense of "the ground being barren and dry" (*arḍun jaḥād*). **That is, denial is described not as ignorance but as a ground that does not give back what it is given.**

## 46/27-28 — The ruins nearby

*"We destroyed the towns around you, and We **turned the signs about and about** in the telling — that they might return."*

**صَرَّفْنَا** — root **ص-ر-ف**: to turn, to revolve, to change the direction of. **Form II here conveys repetition and variation:** the same thing told by different routes, from different faces.

**And once again the evidence is chosen from near at hand:** *mā ḥawlakum mina'l-qurā* — **"the towns around you".** Not a distant history but ruins on the roadside.

**The sūra's understanding of evidence is completed here:**

| Verse | Where the evidence comes from |
|---|---|
| 46/4 | **Observation and transmitted report** — what is demanded |
| 46/15 | **His own birth** |
| 46/26 | **His own senses** |
| 46/27 | **The ruins around him** |

**All four are within a person's reach.** I record this as my own reading: **throughout the sūra not one item of evidence is fetched from somewhere far from the one addressed.**

**فَلَوْلَا نَصَرَهُمُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ قُرْبَانًا ءَالِهَةًۢ** — "if only those gods they took, apart from God, as **a means of nearness** had helped them!"

**قُرْبَان** — root **ق-ر-ب**: nearness. **The word means the thing by which nearness is sought.** That is, the verse names the **purpose** of the things they took: to draw near.

**بَلْ ضَلُّوا۟ عَنْهُمْ** — "rather, they went astray from them." **The picture set up in the fifth and sixth verses is completed here:** the side called upon could not answer, and knew nothing of it; and now it is **not there at all.**

**وَذَٰلِكَ إِفْكُهُمْ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ يَفْتَرُونَ** — and the word **إِفْك** comes back. In the eleventh verse they had called the Qurʾān *ifkun qadīm* (an old fabrication). **Seventeen verses later the sūra gives the same word back to them.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.

## 46/29-32 — The listening of the jinn

*"And when We turned towards you a company of the jinn, to listen to the Qurʾān. When they came to it they said, '**be silent**'. And when it was finished they returned to their people **as warners**."*

### The scene of listening

**The scene is built with five verbs, and the order of the verbs is worth recording:**

| Order | Verb | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ṣarafnā* | **They were turned** |
| 2 | *Anṣitū* | **They fell silent** — saying it to one another |
| 3 | *Quḍiya* | **It was finished** |
| 4 | *Wallaw … mundhirīn* | **They returned** — as warners |
| 5 | *Qālū yā qawmanā* | **They told** |

**Between hearing and turning back to tell there is no stage at all.** I record this as my own reading, and its support is that the verbs are linked one after another with *fāʾ* and *wāw*: **in the first block of the sūra there were those who demanded evidence and put it off for months; here the matter is settled in a single listening.**

**And the contrast looks deliberate in the structure of the sūra:** in the tenth verse **one person** from the Children of Israel bore witness, and those facing him grew proud. In the twenty-ninth verse **a company that is not seen** turns back the moment it hears.

### إِنَّا سَمِعْنَا كِتَٰبًا أُنزِلَ مِنۢ بَعْدِ مُوسَىٰ مُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ

"We have listened to a Book **sent down after Moses**, confirming what came before it."

**The sentence of the jinn is the third answer given to the demand in the fourth verse of the sūra:** there it had been said, "bring a support from an earlier Book". **Here those who listen define what they have listened to by relating it to the earlier Book.**

**And inside the definition there is a chain:** after Moses, confirming what came before. **It says the same thing as the expression *mā kuntu bidʿan mina'r-rusul* (I am not an unprecedented one) in the ninth verse** — the one from the mouth of the Prophet, the other from the mouths of those who listened.

**يَٰقَوْمَنَآ أَجِيبُوا۟ دَاعِىَ ٱللَّهِ** — the call is not "believe us": **"answer the caller of God."** They do not place themselves in between.

**The detailed analysis of the subject of the jinn was made in `072-cin.md`** — the root sense of the word (**ج-ن-ن**: to cover, to conceal), invisibility, and the limit of certain knowledge about these beings were treated there. **I do not repeat it here, and I keep the same limit: no description is produced beyond what the text states.**

## 46/33 — أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَلَمْ يَعْىَ بِخَلْقِهِنَّ بِقَٰدِرٍ عَلَىٰٓ أَن يُحْۦِىَ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ

"Have they not seen that God, who created the heavens and the earth and **was not wearied by creating them**, is able to give life to the dead?"

**وَلَمْ يَعْىَ** — root **ع-ي-ي**: to grow weary, to be unable, to be at a loss. The lexicographers also record the root's sense of "not knowing how a task is to be done".

**The same idea occurs at Qāf 50/38 with another word** (*wa mā massanā min lughūb*) and was treated in `050-kaf.md`. **At al-Baqara 2/255 it came from the same place:** *wa lā yaʾūduhū ḥifẓuhumā* — the keeping of the two does not weigh upon Him.

**Three verses, three different words, the same work:** *lughūb*, *ʿayy*, *awd*. **All three are words that convey a bodily limit, and all three are denied.**

**The logic of the argument is consistent with the rest of the sūra:** having done the greater is counted as evidence of being able to do the lesser. **And once again the evidence comes out of observation** — *a-wa-lam yaraw* (have they not seen).

## 46/34-35 — The close: patience and the term

*"…Be patient, as the **messengers of firm resolve** were patient. And do not seek to hasten it for them. **On the day they see what they are promised, it will be as though they had not stayed more than an hour of a day.** This is a delivering. Is any destroyed but the people who have stepped outside the bounds?"*

### أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْعَزْمِ مِنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ

**عَزْم** — root **ع-ز-م**: to resolve firmly upon a matter, firmness of resolve; a sense of "cutting off" is also recorded in the root.

**Who these messengers are is not enumerated in the verse.** Differing lists are transmitted in the classical commentaries; **I pass none of them on as certain knowledge.** The only thing the verse gives is **an attribute: those of firm resolve.**

**And the form of the command is worth recording: *kamā ṣabara* — "as they were patient".** Patience is commanded together with its example; **nothing new is asked for.** This lies on the same line as *mā kuntu bidʿan mina'r-rusul* in the ninth verse: **to be the continuation of the chain.**

### لَمْ يَلْبَثُوٓا۟ إِلَّا سَاعَةً مِّن نَّهَارٍ

**"As though they had not stayed more than an hour of a day."**

**The measure is worth recording: *sāʿatan min nahār* — a piece of the daytime.** Not of the night but of the day: **that is, of the time a person passes awake and aware.**

**And what the sentence does joins with the third verse of the sūra:** there it was said that the universe was created with *ajalin musammā* (a term whose name has been set). **Here it is said how long that term looks when it is seen from its end.** The sūra declares the term at the beginning and measures it at the end.

### بَلَٰغٌ

**A sentence of one word: *balāgh* — "it is a delivering".**

The root **ب-ل-غ**: to arrive at a place, to reach; *bulūgh*, *tablīgh* and *balāgha* are from the same root. **The word passes a verdict about the sūra itself: the work of this text is to convey.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra began by demanding evidence (4), set the items of evidence out in order (15, 26, 27, 33), and at the end limited its own task to a single word. **To compel, to take charge of the outcome, or to call to account is not the work the text assigns itself.**

**فَهَلْ يُهْلَكُ إِلَّا ٱلْقَوْمُ ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ** — and the close ties the measure of destruction to **an act**: *fāsiqūn* — those who step outside the bounds. **No lineage, no people and no name of a community is mentioned; an attribute is mentioned.** This is the line kept throughout the sūra.

## The sūra as a whole — looking back

| Word / root | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **أ-ف-ك** | *ifkun qadīm* (11) — *ifkuhum* (28) | The charge returning to the one who made it |
| **ك-ب-ر** | *wa'stakbartum* (10) — *tastakbirūn* (20) | The same diagnosis, in two blocks |
| **ع-ر-ض** | *ʿāriḍan* (24, the narrator's word) — *ʿāriḍun mumṭirunā* (24, their word) | Destruction greeted with joy |
| **ح-ق-ف** | *al-Aḥqāf* (21) | The name of the homeland, sand heaped up by the wind |
| **Not growing weary** | *lam yaʿya* (33) | The same line as al-Baqara 2/255 and Qāf 50/38 |
| **The distance of the evidence** | 4, 15, 26, 27, 33 | All of it within the reach of the one addressed |
| **The term** | *ajalin musammā* (3) — *sāʿatan min nahār* (35) | The two ends of the sūra |

**Disagreements where no preference is adopted:** the meaning of the letters *ḥā-mīm* (1); the scope of the expression *mā adrī mā yufʿalu bī* (9); the legal conclusion drawn from the thirty months in the fifteenth verse; the geographical location of al-Aḥqāf (21); who the *ulū'l-ʿazm* messengers are (35).

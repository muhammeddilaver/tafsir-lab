# 39. Sūrat al-Zumar

**Seventy-five verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the word **زُمَرًا** in verses 71 and 73: **in troops, in groups.**

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-10 | Unmixed religion, the proofs of creation, two human beings | *Innamā yuwaffa'ṣ-ṣābirūna ajrahum bi-ghayri ḥisāb* (10) |
| **II** | 11-21 | The command of *ikhlāṣ* and two outcomes | *Inna fī dhālika la-dhikrā li-uli'l-albāb* (21) |
| **III** | 22-35 | The effect of the Book and two parables | *Wa yajziyahum ajrahum bi-aḥsani'lladhī kānū yaʿmalūn* (35) |
| **IV** | 36-52 | The question "is He not enough?", the forbidding of despair | *Wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn* (52) |
| **V** | 53-75 | The call of mercy and two processions | *Wa qīla'l-ḥamdu li'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn* (75) |

**The backbone of the sūra is *ikhlāṣ* — the making of religion unmixed.** The word occurs **three times** near the beginning of the sūra (2, 3, 11), and the rest of the sūra turns around this single concept.

**And the sūra is built out of pairs:** two human types (8-9), two layers (16), two parables (29), two groups (71 and 73), two blasts (68). **The name of the sūra too conveys not a multitude but a *division*.**

## 39/1-3 — تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْحَكِيمِ · إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَآ إِلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ بِٱلْحَقِّ فَٱعْبُدِ ٱللَّهَ مُخْلِصًا لَّهُ ٱلدِّينَ · أَلَا لِلَّهِ ٱلدِّينُ ٱلْخَالِصُ

"The sending down of this Book is from God, the Mighty, the Wise. We sent the Book down to you **with the truth**; so serve God, **making religion unmixedly His**. **Know well: unmixed religion belongs to God alone.**"

### خلص — the picture the root carries

The root **خ-ل-ص**: **a thing's separating out from what has been mixed into it and coming clear.** *Khalaṣa* — it was delivered, it was set apart, it stayed pure. *Khulāṣa* — the essence itself.

**The concrete use of the word is worth recording:** the straining of oil or of milk, its separation from its dregs, is described with this root. **That is, *ikhlāṣ* is not adding something — it is sorting out.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the root itself:** what the verse asks is not that a quality be added to service, but **that what has been mixed into it be taken out.**

**And it must be recorded that the word comes in the first three verses of the sūra in two different patterns:**

| Verse | Word | Pattern | To whom it belongs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | *Mukhliṣan* | **Active participle** — one who makes unmixed | **Man's work** |
| 3 | *Al-Khāliṣ* | **Adjective** — that which is unmixed | **Religion itself** |

**In the second verse there is a command, in the third a ruling:** man is to make it unmixed, because religion already belongs to God as something unmixed.

### مَا نَعْبُدُهُمْ إِلَّا لِيُقَرِّبُونَآ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ زُلْفَىٰٓ

*"We serve them only that they may bring us nearer to God."*

**The verse relays the other side's reasoning from its own mouth — and the reasoning is worth recording.**

**I record this as an observation:** in the words relayed, **God is not denied.** On the contrary: the goal is to draw nearer to Him. **The dispute is not over existence but over the *intermediary*.**

**زُلْفَىٰ** — root **ز-ل-ف**: to draw near, nearness. **The root was treated in `056-vakia.md` and in `050-kaf.md` at 50/31 (*uzlifati'l-jannatu li'l-muttaqīn*); I rely on that treatment.**

**And the verse's answer does not argue with the reasoning — it states the ruling:** *inna'llāha yaḥkumu baynahum fī mā hum fīhi yakhtalifūn.* **That is, the matter is named as a disagreement, and its ruling is counted as deferred.** This lies on the same line as the thread of disagreement treated in `045-casiye.md` at 45/17 and in `002-bakara.md` at 2/213.

## 39/4 — لَّوْ أَرَادَ ٱللَّهُ أَن يَتَّخِذَ وَلَدًا لَّٱصْطَفَىٰ مِمَّا يَخْلُقُ مَا يَشَآءُ سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْوَٰحِدُ ٱلْقَهَّارُ

"**Had God willed to take a child, He would have chosen what He willed from among what He creates.** He is far above that. He is God, the One, the **Qahhār**."

### What the conditional sentence does

**The sentence is built with the conditional *law* (if)**, and in Arabic this particle conveys a condition that **has not come about**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the sentence:** before rejecting the claim outright, the verse **shows the claim's outcome within the claim's own logic** — had there been such a thing, the one chosen would still have been one of **the created**. **That is, the notion of "child" cannot step outside the category of createdness.**

**ٱصْطَفَىٰ** — root **ص-ف-و**: to purify, to choose by sifting. **That the root lies in the same field as *ikhlāṣ* is worth recording:** the first three verses of the sūra were built with the word *khāliṣ* (**خ-ل-ص**), and here **ص-ف-و** comes. **Both roots carry the picture of separating out from a mixture.**

**ٱلْوَٰحِدُ ٱلْقَهَّار** — these two names were treated together in `040-gafir.md` at 40/16. **I rely on that treatment.**

## 39/5-6 — يُكَوِّرُ ٱلَّيْلَ عَلَى ٱلنَّهَارِ وَيُكَوِّرُ ٱلنَّهَارَ عَلَى ٱلَّيْلِ … يَخْلُقُكُمْ فِى بُطُونِ أُمَّهَٰتِكُمْ خَلْقًا مِّنۢ بَعْدِ خَلْقٍ فِى ظُلُمَٰتٍ ثَلَٰثٍ

"**He winds the night over the day, and He winds the day over the night**… He creates you in your mothers' wombs, **within three darknesses**, one creation after another."

### يُكَوِّر — root: ك-و-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to make a thing round by folding it and winding it.** *Kawwara'l-ʿimāma* — he wound the turban. *Kūr* — a rounded heap.

**The root was analysed in `081-tekvir.md` in connection with the name of that sūra** (*idha'sh-shamsu kuwwirat*), and the word's picture of being wound and rolled up was treated there. **I do not repeat it here.**

**The use here is worth recording, and it runs in two directions:** *yukawwiru'l-layla ʿala'n-nahāri wa yukawwiru'n-nahāra ʿala'l-layl* — **each is wound over the other.**

**Per the method (`USLUP.md`) I draw a limit:** I derive from this word no firm conclusion about the shape of the earth or its motion. **The dictionary sense of the word is winding and folding; what the verse describes is night and day taking each other's place without a break.** To produce a modern cosmology out of this would be to make the text say what it does not say.

### فِى ظُلُمَٰتٍ ثَلَٰثٍ

**"Three darknesses."** Differing explanations of what these are are transmitted in the classical commentaries, and **none of them is presented as certain knowledge.**

**What the verse itself gives is this, and that much I record:** the forming takes place somewhere **unseen**, and it takes place **by stages** — *khalqan min baʿdi khalq.*

**In `040-gafir.md` at 40/67 the same staged forming had been set out in six steps**, and the limit set by the method (`USLUP.md`) was drawn there: **these expressions are not to be matched one-to-one with the terms of modern embryology.** I keep the same limit here.

**How the proof works is plain:** man's most basic forming is completed in a place hidden from his own eye. **The same line as the one treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/58-59: the separation of man's share from the outcome.**

## 39/7 — وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ

"**No bearer of sin bears the burden of another.**"

**The root و-ز-ر and this principle were analysed in detail in `053-necm.md` at 53/38** (the weight carried on the back; *wazīr* is from the same root). **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is where the verse stands.** The sentence comes in the middle of a verse that speaks of ingratitude: *wa in tashkurū yarḍahu lakum* — "if you are thankful, He is pleased with it for you."

**And what comes before the sentence is worth recording:** *wa lā yarḍā li-ʿibādihi'l-kufr* — **"He is not pleased with ingratitude for His servants."**

**I record this as an observation:** immediately before saying that responsibility is personal, the verse says **what it is that is not wanted.** Together the two sentences set up a balance: the burden is not shared, but neither is it wanted that anyone should come under it.

## 39/8-9 — وَإِذَا مَسَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ ضُرٌّ دَعَا رَبَّهُۥ مُنِيبًا إِلَيْهِ ثُمَّ إِذَا خَوَّلَهُۥ نِعْمَةً مِّنْهُ نَسِىَ مَا كَانَ يَدْعُوٓا۟ إِلَيْهِ مِن قَبْلُ · أَمَّنْ هُوَ قَٰنِتٌ ءَانَآءَ ٱلَّيْلِ سَاجِدًا وَقَآئِمًا … قُلْ هَلْ يَسْتَوِى ٱلَّذِينَ يَعْلَمُونَ وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

"When harm touches man, he calls upon his Lord, **turning to Him**. Then, when He grants him a favour from Himself, **he forgets what he was calling upon Him for before**… Or is he who is devout **through the hours of the night, prostrating and standing**… **Say: are those who know and those who do not know equal?**"

### Two portraits

**Here the sūra sets two human beings side by side, and the very portrait treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/49-51 stands first:**

| Place | Portrait |
|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/49-51 | He does not weary of asking for good; when evil touches him he despairs; when favour is given he draws his side aside |
| **Al-Zumar 39/8** | When harm touches him he calls, turning to Him; when favour is given he **forgets** |

**The two sūras end the same behaviour with two different verbs: the one drawing aside, the other forgetting.**

**خَوَّلَهُ** — root **خ-و-ل**: to give someone something to look after and to use. **The lexicographers record the sense of the word as "given in trust, given together with the responsibility for it".** The choice of word is worth recording: what is given is named not as **property** but as **something to be looked after.**

**قَٰنِتٌ ءَانَآءَ ٱلَّيْل** — *qānit*: root **ق-ن-ت**, to stand in reverent awe and in unbroken obedience. *Ānāʾ* — the hours of the night.

**And the verse closes with a question:** *hal yastawi'lladhīna yaʿlamūna wa'lladhīna lā yaʿlamūn.*

**The placing of the question must be recorded:** the opposition between knowing and not knowing comes **immediately after the description of worship.** **That this opposition is the whole axis of `045-casiye.md` I set out in a table there;** here the same opposition is joined to **deed.**

## 39/10 — قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ … وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ إِنَّمَا يُوَفَّى ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ أَجْرَهُم بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ

"…**God's earth is wide.** Those who endure are paid their reward **without reckoning.**"

**وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ** — the sentence reminds one who is hemmed in that a change of place is open to him. **It is transmitted that in the classical commentaries this is connected with the emigration.**

**بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ** — "without reckoning". **Words for measure, weighing and requital will occur often throughout the sūra** (39/47, 39/69-70). **That here, in a sūra whose very subject is reckoning, the reckoning is lifted for one single group, is worth recording.**

**And that group is *al-ṣābirūn*.** I record this as an observation: **that endurance is not a measurable operation is answered by its reward too being given without measure.**

## 39/11-16 — قُلْ إِنِّىٓ أُمِرْتُ أَنْ أَعْبُدَ ٱللَّهَ مُخْلِصًا لَّهُ ٱلدِّينَ · … فَٱعْبُدُوا۟ مَا شِئْتُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ

"Say: '**I have been commanded to serve God, making religion unmixedly His**'… **Then serve what you will besides Him!**"

**In five verses the command *qul* (say) comes four times.** And what is said forms a series: I have been commanded (11), I have been commanded to be the first to submit (12), I fear (13), I serve Him (14).

**فَٱعْبُدُوا۟ مَا شِئْتُم مِّن دُونِهِ** — "then serve what you will."

**This sentence is in the imperative but is not a command.** In Arabic this use is called **threat and abandonment (*tahdīd*, *takhliya*)**: leaving the other side alone with its own choice.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sentence that follows:** *qul inna'l-khāsirīna'lladhīna khasirū anfusahum wa ahlīhim yawma'l-qiyāma.* **That is, the letting-go comes together with a statement of the outcome.**

**In `041-fussilet.md` at 41/5 the other side had said the same thing:** *fa'ʿmal innanā ʿāmilūn* — "you work, we are working." **The same sentence of parting: there from the other side, here from this one.** And it will come once more in the thirty-ninth verse of the sūra: *yā qawmi'ʿmalū ʿalā makānatikum innī ʿāmil.*

**لَهُم مِّن فَوْقِهِمْ ظُلَلٌ مِّنَ ٱلنَّارِ وَمِن تَحْتِهِمْ ظُلَلٌ** — "above them **canopies** of fire, and below them canopies."

**That the word *ẓulal* (canopies) is used of fire does the same work as *ẓillin min yaḥmūm* (a shade of black smoke), treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/43:** a word of mercy is hollowed out. **I rely on that treatment.**

## 39/17-18 — فَبَشِّرْ عِبَادِ · ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَمِعُونَ ٱلْقَوْلَ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ أَحْسَنَهُۥٓ

"**Give good news to My servants: those who listen to the word and follow the best of it.**"

### The most striking description in the sūra

**The description consists of two verbs, and both are worth recording:**

| Verb | Pattern | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Yastamiʿūn* | **Form VIII** — to lend an ear, to listen attentively | They **listen** to the word |
| *Yattabiʿūn* | **Form VIII** — to go after | They **follow** the best of it |

**And the object is left unspecified: *al-qawl* — "the word".** Which word is not said.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the generality of the word:** the description leaves out both **rejecting without listening** and **following everything one hears.** There is a work of sorting here: listening is open, following is selective.

**And this description is the exact opposite of the tactic treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/26:** there it was said *lā tasmaʿū li-hādha'l-qurʾāni wa'lghaw fīh* — "do not listen, make noise." **Set the two verses side by side and it is seen that the two sūras hold the two ends of the same matter.**

**أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمْ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ** — *albāb* is the plural of *lubb*: **the kernel of a thing, its inside;** the edible part of a fruit, the part separated from its husk. **That is, the intellect is named as the capacity to throw away the husk and take the kernel** — and the description matches exactly the work of sorting done one verse earlier.

## 39/19-20 — أَفَمَنْ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ كَلِمَةُ ٱلْعَذَابِ أَفَأَنتَ تُنقِذُ مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ · لَٰكِنِ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّقَوْا۟ رَبَّهُمْ لَهُمْ غُرَفٌ مِّن فَوْقِهَا غُرَفٌ مَّبْنِيَّةٌ

"One against whom the word of punishment has come true… **will you rescue the one who is in the Fire?** But for those who guard themselves against their Lord there are **chambers built one above another.**"

**أَفَأَنتَ تُنقِذُ** — the question is directed at the addressee, and the pronoun is brought forward: ***a-fa-anta*** — "is it **you**?"

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence draws a limit of responsibility. **In `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/35 the same limit had been set with the word *balāgh* (delivery);** here it comes in the form of a question. **And it will be said plainly in the forty-first verse of the sūra: *wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl*.**

**غُرَفٌ مِّن فَوْقِهَا غُرَفٌ مَّبْنِيَّة** — "chambers with chambers above them".

**The choice of image is worth recording:** the reward is described not with a garden or a river but **with a building of storeys.** The adjective *mabniyya* (built) is set down as well.

**The language of the descriptions of the Garden, and their being chosen from the life of the one addressed, were treated in detail in `056-vakia.md`; I rely on that treatment.** **This much may be recorded in addition here: a house of storeys is an image that carries value in a city, not in the desert.**

## 39/21 — أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ أَنزَلَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءً فَسَلَكَهُۥ يَنَٰبِيعَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ يُخْرِجُ بِهِۦ زَرْعًا مُّخْتَلِفًا أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ ثُمَّ يَهِيجُ فَتَرَىٰهُ مُصْفَرًّا ثُمَّ يَجْعَلُهُۥ حُطَٰمًا

"Have you not seen that God sends water down from the sky and **drives it into springs** in the earth, then brings forth with it **crops of differing colours**, then it **dries and turns yellow**, then He makes it **broken chaff**?"

### A single sentence in five stages

**The verse follows the path of the water from beginning to end and gives each stage with its own verb:**

| Stage | Verb | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Anzala* | **He sent down** — from the sky |
| 2 | *Salaka* | **He drove it along** — into the springs underground |
| 3 | *Yukhriju* | **He brings forth** — a crop of differing colours |
| 4 | *Yahīju … muṣfarran* | **It dries, it turns yellow** |
| 5 | *Yajʿaluhū ḥuṭāmā* | **He makes it broken chaff** |

**سَلَكَ** — root **س-ل-ك**: to put a thing into a path, to pass it through. **The word describes the water's movement underground as a "driving along".**

**حُطَٰم** — **analysed in `056-vakia.md` at 56/65** (a dry plant broken up and crumbled; the same root as *al-ḥuṭama*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the comparison with the context in al-Wāqiʿa is worth recording:**

| Place | How *ḥuṭām* comes |
|---|---|
| Al-Wāqiʿa 56/65 | *Law nashāʾu la-jaʿalnāhu ḥuṭāmā* — a disaster that **could have happened but has not** |
| **Al-Zumar 39/21** | *Thumma yajʿaluhū ḥuṭāmā* — **the last step of the ordinary cycle** |

**That is, the same word: in one sūra an interruption, in the other a completion.** I record this as my own reading: **in al-Zumar the withering is set out not as a punishment but as the process itself** — and the verse ends this with *dhikrā li-uli'l-albāb* (a reminder for those who possess the kernel).

## 39/22-23 — أَفَمَن شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُۥ لِلْإِسْلَٰمِ فَهُوَ عَلَىٰ نُورٍ مِّن رَّبِّهِۦ … ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ كِتَٰبًا مُّتَشَٰبِهًا مَّثَانِىَ تَقْشَعِرُّ مِنْهُ جُلُودُ ٱلَّذِينَ يَخْشَوْنَ رَبَّهُمْ ثُمَّ تَلِينُ جُلُودُهُمْ وَقُلُوبُهُمْ إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ

"**One whose breast God has opened to Islam**… God has sent down **the fairest of discourse**, a Book whose parts resemble one another and which comes **in pairs**. The skins of those who fear their Lord **shiver at it**; then their skins and their hearts **soften to the remembrance of God.**"

### شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُ

The root **ش-ر-ح**: **to cut open, to widen.** The root was analysed in `094-insirah.md` in connection with the name of that sūra (*a-lam nashraḥ laka ṣadrak*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**What is set opposite it is worth recording:** *fa-waylun li'l-qāsiyati qulūbuhum min dhikri'llāh* — **those whose hearts have hardened.**

**That is, the opposition is not *open / closed* but *opened / hardened*.** Both are the result of a process.

### أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيث

**That the word *ḥadīth* (speech, discourse) is used of the Book must be recorded.** **The same word was treated in `045-casiye.md` at 45/6 (*fa-bi-ayyi ḥadīthin baʿda'llāhi wa āyātihī yuʾminūn*) and in `056-vakia.md` at 56/81 (*a-fa-bi-hādha'l-ḥadīthi antum mudhinūn*).** **In all three places the Book is named as "speech"** — and here it takes a superlative: *aḥsanu'l-ḥadīth.*

### مُّتَشَٰبِهًا مَّثَانِى

**Two adjectives side by side, and both are disputed:**

**مُتَشَابِه** — root **ش-ب-ه**: to resemble. **Its use here differs from the distinction *muḥkam / mutashābih* treated in `002-bakara.md`:** there the verses whose meaning is closed were meant; here **the whole of the Book** is named with this adjective. **The lexicographers give the sense here as "its parts holding together, consistent in style and in content".**

**مَثَانِى** — root **ث-ن-ي**: **to fold, to double, to repeat.** There is disagreement over what the word answers to here:

| Reading | What *mathānī* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Repeated** — the recurrence of the narratives, the rulings, the warnings |
| 2 | **Paired** — its coming in twos, such as Garden and Fire, good news and warning |
| 3 | **Folded** — coming section by section, one upon another |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The second reading is consistent with the structure of this sūra** — the sūra is built out of pairs from beginning to end, and I set this out in a table in the section on the structure. **I record this as a supporting indication, not as a preference.**

### تَقْشَعِرُّ ثُمَّ تَلِين

**The verse describes a bodily reaction in two stages:**

**ق-ش-ع-ر** — a four-letter root; **the shivering of the skin, the standing on end of the hair.** **The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**ل-ي-ن** — to soften.

| Stage | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Taqshaʿirru* — **shivering** | **The skin** |
| 2 | *Talīnu* — **softening** | **The skin and the heart** |

**The order of the two stages and their scope are worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the first reaction is in the skin alone, and it is a **start of alarm.** At the second stage **the heart joins in**, and the movement acquires a direction: *ilā dhikri'llāh.*

**That is, the text first affects the body, then gives a direction.** And this is the exact opposite of the adjective *qāsiya* (hardened) in the twenty-second verse: **the hardening heart and the softening heart are set against one another within the same block.**

---

## 39/24-28 — أَفَمَن يَتَّقِى بِوَجْهِهِۦ سُوٓءَ ٱلْعَذَابِ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ … قُرْءَانًا عَرَبِيًّا غَيْرَ ذِى عِوَجٍ لَّعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ

"**One who tries to shield himself with his face against the worst of the punishment on the Day of Resurrection**… This is an Arabic Qurʾān, **with no crookedness in it.**"

### يَتَّقِى بِوَجْهِه

**The scene is bodily and worth recording: the person tries to shield himself *with his face*.**

The root **و-ق-ي** — the same root as *taqwā*: **to guard oneself, to take cover.** And one whose hands are bound can turn nothing but his face against what comes at him.

**I record this as my own reading:** by joining the act of shielding to the **least protected** organ of what is to be shielded, the verse describes helplessness. **And that the root is the same as *taqwā* carries the sentence onto a second level: the one who did not guard himself with *taqwā* in this world tries to guard himself with his face there.**

### غَيْرَ ذِى عِوَجٍ

The root **ع-و-ج**: **crookedness, warping.** *ʿIwaj* is used for crookedness the eye does not see; *ʿawaj* for crookedness that is seen — **the lexicographers record this distinction.**

**And in the verse *ʿiwaj* is used:** that is, it is the **unseen** crookedness that is negated.

**I transmit this as the lexicographers record it, and I add the following as my own reading:** while "not crooked" would have been enough to say of a text, **the choice of the word that negates unseen crookedness** carries the claim one step further.

**وَلَقَدْ ضَرَبْنَا لِلنَّاسِ فِى هَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ مِن كُلِّ مَثَلٍ** — "We have set forth for people in this Qurʾān **every kind of example.**"

**And in the next verse (29) one of those examples will come: the parable of the two slaves.** **That is, the sentence announces what comes after it.**

---

## 39/29 — ضَرَبَ ٱللَّهُ مَثَلًا رَّجُلًا فِيهِ شُرَكَآءُ مُتَشَٰكِسُونَ وَرَجُلًا سَلَمًا لِّرَجُلٍ هَلْ يَسْتَوِيَانِ مَثَلًا

"God has set forth an example: **a man in the hands of partners quarrelling with one another**, and **a man surrendered wholly to one man.** Are the two of them equal as an example?"

### How the parable is built

**The example is taken from the life of that day: a slave with more than one owner, and a slave with a single owner.**

**مُتَشَٰكِسُون** — root **ش-ك-س**: **ill-tempered, hard to get on with, quarrelling.** **Form VI: mutual quarrelling.** The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.

**The work the parable does is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the verse does not argue the **theoretical** wrongness of multiplicity — it shows its **practical outcome.** A man with more than one owner cannot know whom to act by; each wants something different, and he can fully satisfy none of them.

**That is, the matter is taken out of being a dispute about belief and made a matter of *direction*.** And this matches the command in the second verse of the sūra: *mukhliṣan lahu'd-dīn* — **the sorting out of the mixture.**

**سَلَمًا لِّرَجُلٍ** — "surrendered to one man". **The root س-ل-م** — the practical counterpart of the concept of *ikhlāṣ* at the centre of the sūra is given here with the word **surrender.**

---

## 39/30-31 — إِنَّكَ مَيِّتٌ وَإِنَّهُم مَّيِّتُونَ · ثُمَّ إِنَّكُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ عِندَ رَبِّكُمْ تَخْتَصِمُونَ

"**You too will die, and they too will die.** Then, on the Day of Resurrection, before your Lord, you will **go to law against one another.**"

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: death is said first to the addressee.** *Innaka mayyitun* — and only after that is the other side mentioned.

**تَخْتَصِمُون** — root **خ-ص-م**, Form VIII: **to bring suit against one another, to become adversaries.**

**The dispute in the Fire (*yataḥājjūn*) was treated in detail in `040-gafir.md` at 40/47**, and it was recorded there that the argument goes on even there. **Here the same idea is given with another root, and its addressee is wider: *innakum* — all of you.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse says that the dispute of this world is not closed, only that it **changes place.**

---

## 39/32-35 — فَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن كَذَبَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ وَكَذَّبَ بِٱلصِّدْقِ إِذْ جَآءَهُۥ · وَٱلَّذِى جَآءَ بِٱلصِّدْقِ وَصَدَّقَ بِهِۦٓ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُتَّقُونَ

"Who does greater wrong than **one who invents a lie against God and denies the truth when it comes to him**? And **the one who brings the truth and those who confirm it** — they are the ones who guard themselves."

### Two verses, two sides, the same root

**Both verses are built on the same root: ص-د-ق.**

| Verse | Verb | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | *Kadhdhaba **bi'ṣ-ṣidq*** — he denied the truth | The first side |
| 33 | *Jāʾa **bi'ṣ-ṣidqi** wa **ṣaddaqa** bih* — he brought the truth and confirmed it | The second side |

**And in the second verse there are two distinct acts: bringing, and confirming.** The lexicographers dwell on whom this pair refers to and transmit differing explanations. **I do not impose a preference.**

**But the structure is plain and worth recording:** **for a word to pass into life two things are needed — one who says it and one who accepts it.** The verse gathers both under the same ruling: *ulāʾika humu'l-muttaqūn.*

**لِيُكَفِّرَ ٱللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ أَسْوَأَ ٱلَّذِى عَمِلُوا۟ وَيَجْزِيَهُمْ أَجْرَهُم بِأَحْسَنِ ٱلَّذِى كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ** — "**that God may cover over the worst of what they did, and give them their reward according to the best of what they used to do.**"

**The two extremes must be recorded: *aswaʾa* (the worst) is covered over, *aḥsana* (the best) is taken as the measure.**

**The same structure was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/16** (*nataqabbalu ʿanhum aḥsana mā ʿamilū wa natajāwazu ʿan sayyiʾātihim*) — **the two sūras set up the same two-ended reckoning.** I rely on that treatment.

---

## 39/36-40 — أَلَيْسَ ٱللَّهُ بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُۥ وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ … قُلْ يَٰقَوْمِ ٱعْمَلُوا۟ عَلَىٰ مَكَانَتِكُمْ إِنِّى عَٰمِلٌ

"**Is God not enough for His servant?** Yet they frighten you with those besides Him… Say: '**O my people! Work where you stand; I too am working.**'"

**أَلَيْسَ ٱللَّهُ بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُ** — a declaration of sufficiency in the form of a question. The root **ك-ف-ي**: to suffice, to meet a need.

**وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ** — "they frighten you". **The verb is Form II: the act of *instilling* fear.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse names the instrument the other side uses — **not evidence, but fear.** And through the fourth block of the sūra this instrument will be mentioned several times.

**ٱعْمَلُوا۟ عَلَىٰ مَكَانَتِكُمْ إِنِّى عَٰمِلٌ** — **the second occurrence of the sentence of parting that came in the fifteenth verse** (*fa'ʿbudū mā shiʾtum min dūnih*). **And it is in the same pattern as the sentence *fa'ʿmal innanā ʿāmilūn*, which came from the mouth of the other side in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/5.**

| Place | Who says it |
|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/5 | **The other side** — to close the argument |
| Al-Zumar 39/15, 39/39 | **This side** — stating the outcome |

**The two sūras put the same sentence into two mouths.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and I add the following as my own reading: **in both mouths the sentence conveys a parting; what makes the difference is the statement of outcome appended to it.**

---

## 39/41 — إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ لِلنَّاسِ بِٱلْحَقِّ فَمَنِ ٱهْتَدَىٰ فَلِنَفْسِهِۦ وَمَن ضَلَّ فَإِنَّمَا يَضِلُّ عَلَيْهَا وَمَآ أَنتَ عَلَيْهِم بِوَكِيلٍ

"We sent the Book down to you, **for people**, with the truth. Whoever finds the way, it is to his own good; whoever strays, strays to his own harm. **You are not a guardian over them.**"

**لِلنَّاسِ** — "for people". **The addressee is not narrowed.**

**وَمَآ أَنتَ عَلَيْهِم بِوَكِيل** — *wakīl*: root **و-ك-ل**, one who takes a matter upon himself and carries it through, one who bears the responsibility.

**The limit is drawn plainly here**, and it settles into a ruling the question of the nineteenth verse (*a-fa-anta tunqidhu man fi'n-nār*). **The same limit was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/35 (*balāgh*) and in `050-kaf.md` at 50/45 (*wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-jabbār*).** **Three sūras say the same thing with three different words: delivery, not being a compeller, not being a guardian.**

---

## 39/42 — ٱللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى ٱلْأَنفُسَ حِينَ مَوْتِهَا وَٱلَّتِى لَمْ تَمُتْ فِى مَنَامِهَا فَيُمْسِكُ ٱلَّتِى قَضَىٰ عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ وَيُرْسِلُ ٱلْأُخْرَىٰٓ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى

"God takes the souls at the time of their death; **and the one that has not died, in its sleep.** Then **He holds back the one for which He has decreed death**, and **releases the other until an appointed term.**"

### Sleep and death tied to the same verb

**The verse sets out two states with a single verb: *yatawaffā* — root و-ف-ي, "to take in full, to receive complete".**

**And the distinction is made after the verb:**

| Verb | To whom | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Yumsiku* | The one for which death is decreed | **He holds it back** |
| *Yursilu* | The other | **He releases it** — *ilā ajalin musammā* |

**Both verbs are concrete: to hold and to let go.** A thing held in the hand is either not released or released.

**I record this as my own reading:** by putting something lived through every night (sleep) into the same category as death, the verse **brings the proof down into daily life.** Every night a person is left without consciousness and comes back; **the coming back is itself set out as a permission.**

**أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى** — "a term that has been named". **The same construction was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/3** (of the term of the universe) — **here the same expression is used of a single human being.**

---

## 39/43-44 — أَمِ ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ شُفَعَآءَ قُلْ أَوَلَوْ كَانُوا۟ لَا يَمْلِكُونَ شَيْـًٔا وَلَا يَعْقِلُونَ · قُل لِّلَّهِ ٱلشَّفَٰعَةُ جَمِيعًا

"'Have they taken intercessors besides God?' Say: '**Even though they have power over nothing and do not reason?**' Say: '**Intercession belongs wholly to God.**'"

**The answer to the reasoning of the third verse of the sūra — that the intermediaries are counted as bringing near — is completed here.**

**And the form of the answer is worth recording: intercession is not denied — its owner is named.** *Li'llāhi'sh-shafāʿatu jamīʿā.* **The same line was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/255 (*man dha'lladhī yashfaʿu ʿindahū illā bi-idhnih*):** the door is not closed, it is made subject to permission. I rely on that treatment.

---

## 39/45 — وَإِذَا ذُكِرَ ٱللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ ٱشْمَأَزَّتْ قُلُوبُ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَإِذَا ذُكِرَ ٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ إِذَا هُمْ يَسْتَبْشِرُونَ

"**When God alone is mentioned**, the hearts of those who do not believe in the hereafter **shrink in revulsion**. And when those besides Him are mentioned, at once **they are full of cheer.**"

**ٱشْمَأَزَّتْ** — a four-letter root, **ش-م-أ-ز**: **to shrink, to feel disgust, to draw back in revulsion.** **The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**The verb set opposite it: *yastabshirūn* — Form X, to rejoice as at good news.**

**When each of the two verbs comes must be recorded:**

| What is mentioned | Reaction |
|---|---|
| *Allāhu **waḥdahū*** — **alone** | *Ishmaʾazzat* — shrinking |
| *Alladhīna min dūnih* | *Yastabshirūn* — rejoicing |

**What sets it off is not that God is mentioned — but that He is mentioned *alone*.** I record this as my own reading; its ground is the presence of the word *waḥdahū* in the sentence. **And this joins with the reasoning of the third verse:** there it was relayed that the intermediaries were counted as "bringing near". **The objection is not to God but to being without an intermediary.**

---

## 39/46-48 — قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ فَاطِرَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ عَٰلِمَ ٱلْغَيْبِ وَٱلشَّهَٰدَةِ أَنتَ تَحْكُمُ بَيْنَ عِبَادِكَ فِى مَا كَانُوا۟ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ

"Say: '**O God! Originator of the heavens and the earth, Knower of the seen and the unseen!** It is **You who will judge** between Your servants in the matters over which they differed.'"

**فَاطِر** — root **ف-ط-ر**: **to split open, to bring forth for the first time.** The lexicographers record together the branches of the root for "breaking a fast" (*ifṭār*) and "being split open" (*infiṭār*). **The root was treated in `082-infitar.md`; I rely on that treatment.**

**That the verse is built as a prayer is worth recording:** the matter disputed throughout the sūra — disagreement — is here **taken out of dispute and its ruling deferred**, and this matches the sentence in the third verse: *inna'llāha yaḥkumu baynahum fī mā hum fīhi yakhtalifūn.* **The same expression twice: once as a statement, once as a prayer.**

**وَلَوْ أَنَّ لِلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ مَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ جَمِيعًا وَمِثْلَهُۥ مَعَهُۥ لَٱفْتَدَوْا۟ بِهِۦ** — "if all that is in the earth were theirs, and as much again with it, **they would offer it all as ransom to escape the punishment.**"

**The measure must be recorded: the whole of the earth *and as much again*.** That is, a quantity that goes beyond what exists. **And even so it is not accepted.**

**وَحَاقَ بِهِم مَّا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ** — **the root ح-ي-ق was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/45:** a snare turning back on the one who set it and closing around him. **Here what closes around them is the very thing they made an object of mockery.**

---

## 39/49 — فَإِذَا مَسَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ ضُرٌّ دَعَانَا ثُمَّ إِذَا خَوَّلْنَٰهُ نِعْمَةً مِّنَّا قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أُوتِيتُهُۥ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍۭ

"When harm touches man, he calls upon Us. Then, when We grant him a favour from Us, he says: '**This was given to me only by virtue of a knowledge I have.**'"

### The repetition of the eighth verse — and its difference

**This verse begins with almost the same words as the eighth verse of the sūra.** The two verses may be set side by side:

| | 39/8 | 39/49 |
|---|---|---|
| When harm touches | *Daʿā rabbahū munīban ilayh* | *Daʿānā* |
| When favour is given | *Nasiya mā kāna yadʿū ilayhi min qabl* — **he forgets** | *Qāla innamā ūtītuhū ʿalā ʿilm* — **he says "I earned it by my knowledge"** |

**The same pattern, forty-one verses apart, with two different outcomes.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** in the first there is **forgetting**, in the second **claiming ownership.** **That is, the verse shows two stages of the same behaviour: first forgetting the source, then claiming to be the source oneself.**

**عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ** — "upon a knowledge". **The same construction occurred in `045-casiye.md` at 45/23** (*wa aḍallahu'llāhu ʿalā ʿilmin*), and the disagreement over whose knowledge it is was set out in a table there. **Here the construction is plainly the person's own knowledge: a claim of desert.**

**And the verse meets this with a single sentence:** *bal hiya fitnatun wa lākinna aktharahum lā yaʿlamūn* — **"no, it is a trial."** The thing given is not counted as the measure.

---

## 39/50-52 — قَدْ قَالَهَا ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ فَمَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُم مَّا كَانُوا۟ يَكْسِبُونَ

"**Those before them said this too;** but what they had earned availed them nothing."

**قَدْ قَالَهَا** — "they had said it". **The pronoun goes back to the sentence in the forty-ninth verse:** *innamā ūtītuhū ʿalā ʿilm* — "this was given to me by my knowledge."

**That is, the verse ties a sentence to history:** this saying is not new; **it is a saying that recurs.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sūra brings no evidence to refute the claim — **it shows the end of those who made the same claim.** The same method was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/27: *mā ḥawlakum mina'l-qurā* — the ruins around you.

**أَوَلَمْ يَعْلَمُوٓا۟ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَبْسُطُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ وَيَقْدِرُ** — *basṭ* (to spread out and widen) and *qadr* (to give by measure).

**The two verbs must be recorded together:** both the abundance and the scarcity of what is given are tied, in the same sentence, to **the same will.** **That is, abundance is no proof of desert, and straitness no proof of having fallen from favour** — and this is the direct answer to the claim of the forty-ninth verse (I earned it by my knowledge).

---

## 39/53-55 — قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا

"Say: '**O My servants who have gone to excess against themselves! Do not despair of God's mercy.** God forgives all sins.'"

### How the address is built

**The first word of the sentence must be recorded: *yā ʿibādī* — "O My servants".**

**That is, the address begins by calling even those who have gone to excess servants.** The description comes after: *alladhīna asrafū ʿalā anfusihim.*

**أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ** — root **س-ر-ف**: to overstep the measure, to squander. **And the particle *ʿalā* — "against themselves".** That is, the excess is named as something done not to another but **to oneself.**

**لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟** — root **ق-ن-ط**. **This root was analysed in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/49:** there man, when evil touches him, was described as *qanūṭ* (one who gives up hope). **I rely on that treatment.**

**The comparison of the two verses is worth recording:**

| Place | *Qunūṭ* |
|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/49 | **Description** — such is man |
| **Al-Zumar 39/53** | **Prohibition** — do not be so |

**The one describes the state, the other forbids that state.** I record this as my own reading: **the Qurʾān first establishes man's tendency, then issues a command against it.**

**وَٱتَّبِعُوٓا۟ أَحْسَنَ مَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُم مِّن رَّبِّكُم** — "**follow the best** of what has been sent down to you from your Lord."

**In the eighteenth verse the servants given good news had been described as *fa-yattabiʿūna aḥsanahū* (those who follow the best of it).** **Thirty-seven verses later the same expression turns into a command.**

| Verse | Form |
|---|---|
| 18 | *Yattabiʿūna aḥsanahū* — **description** |
| 55 | *Wa'ttabiʿū aḥsana mā unzila* — **command** |

**This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

**أَن تَقُولَ نَفْسٌ يَٰحَسْرَتَىٰ عَلَىٰ مَا فَرَّطتُ فِى جَنۢبِ ٱللَّهِ** — "**before a soul says, 'Alas for me, for what I failed in on the side of God!'**"

**حَسْرَة** — root **ح-س-ر**: **to strip off what lies over a thing and lay it bare;** from this come the senses of **weariness** and **regret**. **The root was treated in `067-mulk.md`, `069-hakka.md` and `002-bakara.md`.**

**فِى جَنۢبِ ٱللَّه** — *janb*: the side. **The lexicographers dwell on the meaning of the construction and give renderings such as "in the matter of God's due", "on the side of nearness to Him". I do not impose a firm preference, and I enter the note that the construction carries no bodily sense** — the care for *tanzīh* kept consistently throughout this corpus holds here as well.

## 39/56-66 — أَن تَقُولَ نَفْسٌ … أَوْ تَقُولَ لَوْ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ هَدَىٰنِى لَكُنتُ مِنَ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ · أَوْ تَقُولَ حِينَ تَرَى ٱلْعَذَابَ لَوْ أَنَّ لِى كَرَّةً فَأَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ

"Before **a soul** says this… or says '**Had God guided me, I would have been among those who guard themselves**'; or, when it sees the punishment, says '**Had I one more turn, I would be among those who do good**'…"

### Three excuses

**The verses set out three separate sentences, and all three come in the pattern *an taqūla nafsun* / *aw taqūla*:**

| Order | Saying | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (56) | *Yā ḥasratā ʿalā mā farraṭtu fī janbi'llāh* | **Regret** |
| 2 (57) | *Law anna'llāha hadānī* | **Transfer of responsibility** |
| 3 (58) | *Law anna lī karratan* | **The wish to return** |

**The order is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** first regret, then the laying of the blame on another, then the demand to make good. **And the second cuts off the third:** had no guidance been given, returning too would have no meaning.

**And the answer is given directly to the second excuse (59):** *balā qad jāʾatka āyātī fa-kadhdhabta bihā wa'stakbarta* — **"No! My signs came to you, and you denied them and grew proud."**

**بَلَىٰ** — in Arabic, the particle of answer used to refute a negative sentence. **That is, the negative on which the excuse rests ("I was not guided") is rejected.**

**وَٱسْتَكْبَرْتَ** — and the cause is named as **self-magnification.** **That *kibr* lies beneath the dispute was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/56;** here the same root is set against the excuse.

### قُلْ أَفَغَيْرَ ٱللَّهِ تَأْمُرُوٓنِّىٓ أَعْبُدُ أَيُّهَا ٱلْجَٰهِلُونَ

"Say: '**Is it other than God that you command me to serve, O you ignorant ones!**'"

**The verb must be recorded: *taʾmurūnnī* — "do you command me?"** That is, what the other side is doing is named not as an **offer** but as a **command.**

**وَلَقَدْ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْكَ وَإِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكَ لَئِنْ أَشْرَكْتَ لَيَحْبَطَنَّ عَمَلُكَ** — **the root ح-ب-ط was analysed in `049-hucurat.md` at 49/2** (an animal that eats a harmful plant, swells and dies — growth on the outside, rot within). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The addressee of the conditional sentence is worth recording:** the ruling is given as directed **to the prophets as well** (*wa ila'lladhīna min qablik*). **That is, the rule is laid down without exception.**

**بَلِ ٱللَّهَ فَٱعْبُدْ وَكُن مِّنَ ٱلشَّٰكِرِينَ** — and in the word order **the object is brought forward**: *Allāha fa'ʿbud* — "serve **God**." In Arabic this fronting conveys restriction: **and no other.** **The command *mukhliṣan lahu'd-dīn* in the second verse of the sūra is thereby repeated here by way of word order.**

## 39/67 — وَمَا قَدَرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ حَقَّ قَدْرِهِۦ وَٱلْأَرْضُ جَمِيعًا قَبْضَتُهُۥ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ وَٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ مَطْوِيَّٰتٌۢ بِيَمِينِهِۦ سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ وَتَعَٰلَىٰ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

"**They did not measure God with the measure due to Him.** On the Day of Resurrection the earth is wholly in His grasp, and the heavens are **rolled up in His right hand.** He is far above what they associate with Him, and exalted beyond it."

### A note that must be entered

**The note of *tanzīh* kept consistently throughout this corpus holds for the expressions in this verse too, and repeating it is obligatory:** these expressions cannot be understood in a way that ascribes **an organ, a body or a place** to God.

**And the strongest ground for this is the verse itself:** the sentence ends with *subḥānahū wa taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn* — **that is, the *tanzīh* has been set down by the verse's own hand immediately after the image.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the verse:** a likeness is drawn, and then, **within the same verse**, the likeness is passed beyond. **The verse enters its own qualification itself.**

**Two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries:** accepting the wording as it stands and leaving its manner to God (*tafwīḍ*), or interpreting it in the sense of power and complete disposal (*taʾwīl*). **I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

**وَمَا قَدَرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ حَقَّ قَدْرِهِ** — **the root ق-د-ر was treated in `097-kadr.md` and in `054-kamer.md` at 54/49:** the core sense is **measure.** **The sentence here conveys an error of measurement: they could not measure Him with the measure due to Him.**

**And the rest of the verse brings a scale:** the earth a handful, the heavens rolled up. **That is, the error of measure is met again in the language of measure.**

## 39/68 — وَنُفِخَ فِى ٱلصُّورِ فَصَعِقَ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ إِلَّا مَن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ نُفِخَ فِيهِ أُخْرَىٰ فَإِذَا هُمْ قِيَامٌ يَنظُرُونَ

"**The trumpet is blown**; whoever is in the heavens and on the earth falls senseless — **except whom God wills.** Then it is blown again; and there they are, **standing and looking on.**"

**The two blasts are plainly separated, and *thumma* is set between them** — the particle that conveys an interval.

**إِلَّا مَن شَآءَ ٱللَّه** — **the exception must be recorded: the sentence issues a comprehensive ruling, then adds the exception itself.** Who is excepted is not said; **various explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries, none of them is certain knowledge, and I do no more than relay them.**

**فَإِذَا هُمْ قِيَامٌ يَنظُرُونَ** — *fa-idhā*: in Arabic this is used for **what happens all at once.** **After the second blast no stage at all is described: they are straightway standing and looking on.**

**The distinction treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/68 works here too:** man's forming runs by intervals, the coming to pass of the ruling is instantaneous.

## 39/69-70 — وَأَشْرَقَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ بِنُورِ رَبِّهَا وَوُضِعَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ وَجِا۟ىٓءَ بِٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ وَٱلشُّهَدَآءِ وَقُضِىَ بَيْنَهُم بِٱلْحَقِّ وَهُمْ لَا يُظْلَمُونَ · وَوُفِّيَتْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ مَّا عَمِلَتْ

"**The earth shone with the light of its Lord**; the record was laid down; the prophets and the witnesses were brought; judgement was given between them with truth, and they were not wronged. **Every soul was paid in full for what it had done.**"

### Five verbs, all of them passive

**Five verbs come one after another in the verse, and apart from *ashraqat* all of them are passive:** *wuḍiʿa* (was laid down), *jīʾa* (were brought), *quḍiya* (judgement was given), *wuffiyat* (was paid in full).

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** in none of them is the agent named. **The scene is built out of what happens alone, without showing who does it** — and this is a manner of narration that describes the courtroom itself.

**وَأَشْرَقَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ بِنُورِ رَبِّهَا** — the root **ش-ر-ق**: to rise, the spreading of light (*sharq*, *ishrāq* are from the same root).

**A note per the method (`USLUP.md`):** this expression cannot be understood in a way that ascribes a bodily light to God. **It is transmitted that in the classical commentaries the word *nūr* is here interpreted in the sense of justice, or of the judgement coming into the open; the attitude of accepting the wording as it stands and leaving its manner is transmitted as well.** I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.

**وَوُفِّيَتْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ مَّا عَمِلَتْ** — *tawfiya*: **to pay in full.** Root **و-ف-ي** — **the same root as *yatawaffa'l-anfus* (to take the souls in full) in the forty-second verse.**

| Verse | Verb | What is taken / given |
|---|---|---|
| 42 | *Yatawaffā* | **Souls** — taken in full |
| 70 | *Wuffiyat* | **Requital** — given in full |

**The same root at the two ends: what is taken and what is given.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and together with *yuwaffa'ṣ-ṣābirūna ajrahum* in the tenth verse it occurs three times.

## 39/71-75 — وَسِيقَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ زُمَرًا … وَسِيقَ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّقَوْا۟ رَبَّهُمْ إِلَى ٱلْجَنَّةِ زُمَرًا

"Those who disbelieve are driven **in troops** to Hell… and those who guard themselves against their Lord are led **in troops** to the Garden."

### زُمَر — the name of the sūra

The root **ز-م-ر** — **it is analysed here for the first time in this corpus.** *Zumra*: **a company that moves together, a troop.** The lexicographers record the root's connection with the sense of "sound" (*zamr* — the sound of a wind instrument) and build the link by way of **the murmur of a crowd.** **I relay this as a connection made by the lexicographers.**

**That the word is used of both groups must be recorded:** the same word, the same verb (*sīqa* — they were driven), two opposite directions. **The sūra ties the distinction not to the content but to the direction.**

### The difference between the two scenes — and the matter of the *wāw*

**The two passages are almost symmetrical; but there is one difference, and the lexicographers have dwelt on it at length:**

| Group | Arrival at the gates |
|---|---|
| Hell (71) | *Ḥattā idhā jāʾūhā **futiḥat** abwābuhā* |
| The Garden (73) | *Ḥattā idhā jāʾūhā **wa futiḥat** abwābuhā* |

**In the second sentence there is one extra *wāw*.** The lexicographers give differing explanations of this difference:

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The *wāw* conceals the answering clause of the sentence** — the expression of a joy that cannot be told; the answer has been left unsaid |
| 2 | **The gates of the Garden are open beforehand** — they are not opened when they arrive; they are found already open |
| 3 | **The *wāw* here is a feature of style** and no extra meaning should be loaded onto it |

**All three explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **But that the difference is there in the text is certain, and it can be verified.**

**And there is a difference in the content as well:** in the scene of Hell the keepers ask a question (*a-lam yaʾtikum rusulun minkum*); in the scene of the Garden the keepers **give the greeting of peace** (*salāmun ʿalaykum ṭibtum fa'dkhulūhā khālidīn*).

**That peace is given as the requital was treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/25-26 and 56/91;** I rely on that treatment: **on the good side the last word said is again peace.**

**وَتَرَى ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةَ حَآفِّينَ مِنْ حَوْلِ ٱلْعَرْشِ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّهِمْ وَقُضِىَ بَيْنَهُم بِٱلْحَقِّ وَقِيلَ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ**

**The sūra closes with the second verse of al-Fātiḥa:** *al-ḥamdu li'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn.*

**And the verb is passive: *wa qīla* — "it was said".** Who said it is not stated.

**I record this as an observation:** after seventy-five verses of *ikhlāṣ*, of parting, of two groups and of reckoning, **the last sentence is not a ruling but a sentence of praise** — and its speaker has not been named.

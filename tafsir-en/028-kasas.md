# 28. Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ

**88 verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the word **ٱلْقَصَص** in verse 25: **the account that is related, the story.**

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-6 | The order Pharaoh built, and the promise given | *Wa nuriya Firʿawna wa Hāmāna wa junūdahumā minhum mā kānū yaḥdharūn* (6) |
| **II** | 7-28 | **The life of Moses (Mūsā)** — the chest, the palace, the killing, Midian | *Wa'llāhu ʿalā mā naqūlu wakīl* (28) |
| **III** | 29-42 | The call in the valley, and Pharaoh's tower | *Wa yawma'l-qiyāmati hum mina'l-maqbūḥīn* (42) |
| **IV** | 43-60 | The Book, guidance, and the secure land | *A-fa-lā taʿqilūn* (60) |
| **V** | 61-88 | The scene of the reckoning, **Qārūn**, and the close | *Kullu shayʾin hālikun illā wajhah* (88) |

### The backbone of the sūra: ع-ل-و

**The sūra opens with one root and closes with the same root:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | *Inna Firʿawna **ʿalā** fi'l-arḍ* | **Pharaoh** — he exalted himself in the land |
| 83 | *Li'lladhīna lā yurīdūna **ʿuluwwan** fi'l-arḍi wa lā fasādā* | **The abode of the hereafter** — for those who do not want exaltation |

**And the same pair occurs in both verses: *ʿuluww* and *fasād*.** The fourth verse describes Pharaoh as *mina'l-mufsidīn* (one of those who work corruption); the eighty-third gives the abode to those who want neither *ʿuluww* nor *fasād*. **This is a link that can be verified from the text, and it frames every story the sūra tells.**

## 28/1-6 — طسٓمٓ … إِنَّ فِرْعَوْنَ عَلَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَجَعَلَ أَهْلَهَا شِيَعًا يَسْتَضْعِفُ طَآئِفَةً مِّنْهُمْ يُذَبِّحُ أَبْنَآءَهُمْ وَيَسْتَحْىِۦ نِسَآءَهُمْ · وَنُرِيدُ أَن نَّمُنَّ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتُضْعِفُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَنَجْعَلَهُمْ أَئِمَّةً وَنَجْعَلَهُمُ ٱلْوَٰرِثِينَ

"Ṭā-sīn-mīm… **Pharaoh exalted himself in the land and divided its people into factions: he was making one group among them weak, slaughtering their sons and sparing their women.** But We were willing **to show favour to those made weak in the land, to make them leaders, and to make them the heirs.**"

**The note on the disconnected letters has been entered many times in this series; I do not repeat it here.**

### شِيَعًا — the method of the order

The root **ش-ي-ع**: **to spread, to attach oneself to one side.** *Shīʿa* — the body of partisans that gathers around a person. **The plural comes here as *shiyaʿ*: factions, separate groups.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse describes what Pharaoh did **with three verbs**, and the first of them is dividing. **That is, oppression does not begin with direct force; it begins by breaking the community into pieces** — and only in a divided community can one section be set apart and made *mustaḍʿaf*.

**يَسْتَضْعِفُ** — root **ض-ع-ف**, Form X: **to reckon someone weak and to make him weak.** **The pattern matters: weakness is not something that happens of itself but something that is *done*.**

**And the verse sets two verbs side by side:** *yudhabbiḥu abnāʾahum wa yastaḥyī nisāʾahum* — **to kill the sons, to spare the women.** The second looks at first sight like mercy; **but within the whole of the sentence it stands as a method for cutting off the continuation of a people.**

### وَنُرِيدُ أَن نَّمُنَّ

**The fifth verse is set directly opposite the fourth, and the tense must be recorded: *wa nurīdu* — the imperfect.**

**That is, the imperfect had been used for Pharaoh's acts as well** (*yudhabbiḥu*, *yastaḥyī*). **Both sides are shown within a work that is still going on.** I record this as an observation about the word order: **the sūra brings two wills onto the stage at the same time and in the same tense.**

**أَئِمَّة** — the plural of *imām*; root **أ-م-م**: to go in front, one who is followed. **The root was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/12 (*kitābu Mūsā imāman*).**

**And the content of the promise is worth recording:** those made weak are not merely **rescued** — they are made **leaders and heirs**. **That is, the promise is a reversal of position.**

## 28/7-13 — وَأَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَىٰٓ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰٓ أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ فَإِذَا خِفْتِ عَلَيْهِ فَأَلْقِيهِ فِى ٱلْيَمِّ وَلَا تَخَافِى وَلَا تَحْزَنِىٓ … وَأَصْبَحَ فُؤَادُ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰ فَٰرِغًا إِن كَادَتْ لَتُبْدِى بِهِۦ لَوْلَآ أَن رَّبَطْنَا عَلَىٰ قَلْبِهَا

"We made this known to the mother of Moses: '**Suckle him. When you fear for him, cast him into the water. Do not fear and do not grieve; We shall return him to you.**'… **And the heart of Moses' mother was left quite empty; had We not steadied her heart, she would almost have disclosed him.**"

### The order of the commands

**There are five things in what was made known to the mother, and their order is worth recording:**

| Order | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Arḍiʿīh* | **Suckle him** — the ordinary act |
| 2 | *Fa-idhā khifti ʿalayh* | **If you fear** — the condition |
| 3 | *Fa-alqīhi fi'l-yamm* | **Cast him into the water** — the act that looks most dangerous |
| 4 | *Wa lā takhāfī wa lā taḥzanī* | **Two prohibitions** |
| 5 | *Innā rāddūhu ilayk* | **The promise** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order:** the riskiest command — casting the child into the water — stands in the middle, and **before it comes the condition, after it the consolation and the promise.** That is, the command is not left bare.

**وَلَا تَخَافِى وَلَا تَحْزَنِى** — **the two verbs look to two different times:** *khawf* to what is coming, *ḥuzn* to what has passed. **This pair was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/13 and in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/30;** I rely on that treatment.

### فَٰرِغًا — the emptied heart

**فُؤَاد** — root **ف-ء-د**: the burning of embers; the **kindled, moving** side of the heart. **The root was treated in `056-vakia.md`, `053-necm.md` and in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/26.**

**فَارِغ** — **empty.** The lexicographers give differing explanations for this construction: emptied of everything so as to hold nothing but the child, or left without the strength to bear it.

**رَبَطْنَا عَلَىٰ قَلْبِهَا** — *rabṭ*: **to bind, to fasten tight.** The idiom means **holding the heart in its place, keeping it from scattering.**

**I record this as an observation:** the two words together build one picture — **a vessel that empties and a heart that is bound fast.** And *fuʾād* and *qalb* occur in the same verse as two distinct words: **the one empties, the other is bound.**

**وَحَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِ ٱلْمَرَاضِعَ مِن قَبْلُ** — "**We had already forbidden him the wet-nurses.**"

**And the knot of the story is here:** that the child would take no wet-nurse becomes **the cause that carries him back to his mother.** **That is, the promise (*innā rāddūhu ilayk*) comes about through an ordinary chain of causes.** I record this as my own reading.

## 28/14-21 — وَدَخَلَ ٱلْمَدِينَةَ عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا فَوَجَدَ فِيهَا رَجُلَيْنِ يَقْتَتِلَانِ … فَوَكَزَهُۥ مُوسَىٰ فَقَضَىٰ عَلَيْهِ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِنْ عَمَلِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ … قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّى ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِى فَٱغْفِرْ لِى

"**He entered the city at a moment when its people were unaware** and found two men there fighting… **Moses struck him with his fist and finished him. 'This is of Satan's doing,' he said… 'My Lord! I have wronged myself; forgive me,' he said.**"

### How the story is handled

**The event is told without its outcome being hidden**, and Moses' own assessment of it is transmitted as well: *hādhā min ʿamali'sh-shayṭān*, *innī ẓalamtu nafsī*.

**I record this as an observation:** the text does not **cover over** a grave event in the life of a prophet, and it has the assessment made **by him**. **And the character of the event is named in the verse: not a deliberate killing but the unlooked-for result of an intervention.** *Wakaza* — **to strike with the fist**; *fa-qaḍā ʿalayh* — he finished him.

**And the meeting with the same man on the following day is worth recording (18-19):** the one who asks for help is once again in a fight. **What Moses says to him stands in the text:** *innaka la-ghawiyyun mubīn* — **"you are plainly one gone astray."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the verses:** the story shows that the one who stands on the side of the wronged does not pass untested either. **That is, being "made weak" does not of itself bring rightness of conduct** — and this is a note set beside the promise in the fifth verse of the sūra.

**وَجَآءَ رَجُلٌ مِّنْ أَقْصَا ٱلْمَدِينَةِ يَسْعَىٰ** (20) — "**and a man came running from the far end of the city.**"

**The same pattern occurs in `036-yasin.md` at 36/20 as well** (*wa jāʾa min aqṣa'l-madīnati rajulun yasʿā*). **In both places someone who is not named comes running and brings word of something.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

## 28/22-28 — Midian: water, shade, and an offer of work

*Wa lammā tawajjaha tilqāʾa Madyana qāla ʿasā rabbī an yahdiyanī sawāʾa's-sabīl*

"When he turned towards Midian (Madyan) he said: '**It may be that my Lord will guide me to the even path.**'"

### The details of the scene

**The story is told with small and concrete details:**

| Detail | Expression |
|---|---|
| The crowd | *Ummatan mina'n-nāsi yasqūn* — a body of people watering |
| The two women | *Wa wajada min dūnihimu'mraʾatayni tadhūdān* — **holding their animals back** |
| The reason | *Lā nasqī ḥattā yuṣdira'r-riʿāʾ* — we do not water until the shepherds withdraw |
| The father | *Wa abūnā shaykhun kabīr* — our father is very old |
| Moses | *Fa-saqā lahumā thumma tawallā ila'ẓ-ẓill* — he watered for them, then withdrew to the shade |

**تَذُودَان** — root **ذ-و-د**: **to keep away, to drive back.** The word describes holding the animals off from the water and making them wait.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse has the two women give the reason for their waiting **in their own words**, and the reason is practical: until the crowd disperses. **That is, the scene is built as a scene of daily life before it gives any moral lesson.**

**ثُمَّ تَوَلَّىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلظِّلِّ فَقَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّى لِمَآ أَنزَلْتَ إِلَىَّ مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَقِيرٌ** — "**My Lord! I stand in need of whatever good You may send down to me.**"

**The prayer is worth recording: nothing is asked for — a need is stated.** **The root ف-ق-ر was analysed at `035-fatir.md` 35/15** (*faqar* — the spine; *faqīr* — one whose spine is broken). **I rely on that treatment.**

### قَالَتْ إِحْدَىٰهُمَا يَٰٓأَبَتِ ٱسْتَـْٔجِرْهُ إِنَّ خَيْرَ مَنِ ٱسْتَـْٔجَرْتَ ٱلْقَوِىُّ ٱلْأَمِينُ

"One of the two said: '**Father, hire him. The best of those you might hire is the one who is strong and trustworthy.**'"

**The sentence gives a measure, and the measure is made of two words:**

| Attribute | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| *al-Qawiyy* | ق-و-ي | **Strength** — being able to do the work |
| *al-Amīn* | أ-م-ن | **Trustworthiness** — not turning the work to ill use |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two attributes come together:** the one states **capacity**, the other **safety**, and **neither is enough on its own.** The one who is strong but not to be trusted, and the one who is trustworthy but not strong, both fail the measure.

**And who it is that speaks must be recorded:** the one who sets the measure is the one who has watched the scene from the beginning. **That is, the judgement rests on observation** — and indeed both attributes had been seen in the scene: drawing water from the well (strength) and his coming with his eyes lowered (trustworthiness).

**I give this as a reading; the verse does not make the connection explicitly, but the order of the scene lends itself to it.**

## 28/29-35 — The call in the valley

*Fa-lammā qaḍā Mūsa'l-ajala wa sāra bi-ahlihī ānasa min jānibi'ṭ-Ṭūri nārā*

"When Moses had completed the term and set out with his family, **he made out a fire on the side of the Mount (al-Ṭūr)**… When he came to it, he was called **from the tree in the blessed spot on the right side of the valley**: '**O Moses! I am God, the Lord of all beings.**'"

**ءَانَسَ** — root **أ-ن-س**: **to catch sight of and make out, to grow warm to.** From the same root come *ins* (human being) and *uns* (closeness). **The lexicographers record for the word the sense of sensing a light from a distance.**

**And what the fire was wanted for is stated in the verse:** *laʿallī ātīkum minhā bi-khabarin aw jadhwatin mina'n-nāri laʿallakum taṣṭalūn* — **word, or an ember to warm yourselves by.** **That is, he goes for an ordinary need.**

**I record this as an observation:** the call comes not in a place that had been sought out — **but on the road, at a point turned aside to for a daily need.**

### Two signs and a fear

**The verses give two signs, and fear is named with both:**

| Sign | Expression | Reaction |
|---|---|---|
| The staff | *Fa-lammā raʾāhā tahtazzu ka-annahā jānnun **wallā mudbiran wa lam yuʿaqqib*** | **He fled without looking back** |
| The hand | *Usluk yadaka fī jaybika takhruj bayḍāʾa min ghayri sūʾ* | — |

**وَلَمْ يُعَقِّبْ** — *taʿqīb*: **to turn back and look, to come back.** **That is, the flight is a complete flight.**

**وَٱضْمُمْ إِلَيْكَ جَنَاحَكَ مِنَ ٱلرَّهْبِ** — "**draw your wing in to yourself against the fear.**"

***Janāḥ* (wing), when it is used of a human being, means "the arm, the side"** and the lexicographers explain this idiom as **a person's gathering himself together, coming out of a shudder.** **They rest it on the picture of a bird that spreads its wings when startled and folds them again when it settles.**

**I transmit this as a connection the lexicographers make.**

### وَأَخِى هَٰرُونُ هُوَ أَفْصَحُ مِنِّى لِسَانًا فَأَرْسِلْهُ مَعِىَ رِدْءًا يُصَدِّقُنِىٓ

"**My brother Aaron (Hārūn) is clearer of tongue than I am; send him with me as a support so that he may confirm me.**"

**رِدْء** — root **ر-د-أ**: **a support, a helper who holds from behind.** The lexicographers explain the word by **a prop set behind something so that it does not topple over.**

**I record this as an observation:** the request is not that the burden be shared — **it is to be confirmed.** *Yuṣaddiqunī.* And the ground given is practical: that the tongue is clearer.

**سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ بِأَخِيكَ** — "**We shall strengthen your arm with your brother.**" *ʿAḍud* — **the upper arm.** **Again a bodily picture: support is told as the strengthening of an arm.**

## 28/36-42 — Pharaoh's tower, and the word turned around

*Wa qāla Firʿawnu yā ayyuha'l-malaʾu mā ʿalimtu lakum min ilāhin ghayrī fa-awqid lī yā Hāmānu ʿala'ṭ-ṭīni fa'jʿal lī ṣarḥan laʿallī aṭṭaliʿu ilā ilāhi Mūsā*

"Pharaoh said: '**O chiefs! I know of no god for you other than myself. Hāmān! Kindle a fire for me over the clay** (bake brick) **and build me a tower; perhaps I may climb up to the god of Moses.**'"

### Comparison with Ghāfir

**The same demand occurred at `040-gafir.md` 40/36-37 as well, and was treated there.** **The two verses can be set side by side:**

| | Ghāfir 40/36 | **al-Qaṣaṣ 28/38** |
|---|---|---|
| The command | *Ibni lī ṣarḥā* | *Awqid lī … ʿala'ṭ-ṭīni fa'jʿal lī ṣarḥā* |
| The detail | None | **The baking of the clay** — the production of the building material |
| The ground | *Laʿallī ablughu'l-asbāb* | *Laʿallī aṭṭaliʿu ilā ilāhi Mūsā* |

**In al-Qaṣaṣ the stage of production is named as well.** I record this as an observation: **the demand is set out in detail as a work of architecture** — and what was recorded in `040-gafir.md` becomes still sharper here: **the claim is turned into a problem of engineering.**

**مَا عَلِمْتُ لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرِى** — "**I know of no god for you other than myself.**"

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded: *mā ʿalimtu* — "I do not know".** The claim is built not as a judgement about what exists but **upon the speaker's own knowledge**. **The same structure was treated at `040-gafir.md` 40/29:** *mā urīkum illā mā arā* — "I show you only what I myself see."

### وَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ أَئِمَّةً يَدْعُونَ إِلَى ٱلنَّارِ (41)

**And the sharpest point of word order in the sūra is here:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | *Wa najʿalahum **aʾimma*** | **Those made weak** — they are to be made leaders |
| 41 | *Wa jaʿalnāhum **aʾimmatan** yadʿūna ila'n-nār* | **Pharaoh and those around him** — leaders who call to the fire |

**The same word, the same verbal pattern, two opposite directions.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** the word *imāma* (leadership) states no value on its own. **To be in front is a position; what is decisive is the place it calls to.**

## 28/43-51 — The Book and "you were not there"

*Wa mā kunta bi-jānibi'l-gharbiyyi idh qaḍaynā ilā Mūsa'l-amra wa mā kunta mina'sh-shāhidīn*

"**You were not on the western side when We decreed the command to Moses, nor were you among those who witnessed it.**"

### How the proof is built

**The same pattern is repeated three times through the section:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| 44 | *Wa mā kunta bi-jānibi'l-gharbiyy* |
| 45 | *Wa mā kunta thāwiyan fī ahli Madyan* |
| 46 | *Wa mā kunta bi-jānibi'ṭ-Ṭūri idh nādaynā* |

**Three times, "you were not there".** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.

**I record this as an observation:** the proof is built upon **the source of the knowledge**. **And the same method had been treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/48** (*wa mā kunta tatlū min qablihī min kitāb*) — there it was the absence of reading and writing that was made the proof. **The two sūras build the same proof by two different routes: the one from the absence of schooling, the other from the absence of witnessing.**

**وَلَٰكِنَّآ أَنشَأْنَا قُرُونًا فَتَطَاوَلَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْعُمُرُ** — *tatāwala ʿalayhimu'l-ʿumur*: **a long time passed over them.**

**I give this as a record:** the verse states the distance between plainly. **That is, the knowledge cannot have come by an unbroken transmission** — and the weight of the proof is born from this.

**وَلَقَدْ وَصَّلْنَا لَهُمُ ٱلْقَوْلَ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ** (51) — the root **و-ص-ل**, Form II: **to convey without interruption, by joining one thing to another.**

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** the word was conveyed not at one stroke but **link joined to link**. **And it stands in the same field as the concept of *taṣrīf* (turning a matter about and about in the telling) treated in `043-zuhruf.md` and `045-casiye.md`** — the one states repetition, the other continuity.

## 28/52-55 — وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا۟ ٱللَّغْوَ أَعْرَضُوا۟ عَنْهُ وَقَالُوا۟ لَنَآ أَعْمَٰلُنَا وَلَكُمْ أَعْمَٰلُكُمْ سَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْكُمْ لَا نَبْتَغِى ٱلْجَٰهِلِينَ

"…**They are given their reward twice over for their patience; they repel evil with good** and spend of what We have provided for them. **When they hear idle talk they turn away from it and say: 'Our deeds are ours and your deeds are yours. Peace be upon you; we do not seek out the ignorant.'**"

### وَيَدْرَءُونَ بِٱلْحَسَنَةِ ٱلسَّيِّئَةَ

**This construction was treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/34** (*idfaʿ bi'llatī hiya aḥsan*) and had been carried into the field of argument at `029-ankebut.md` 29/46. **The third link is here.**

| Place | Verb | Field |
|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/34 | *Idfaʿ* — د-ف-ع | **Enmity** |
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/46 | *Lā tujādilū illā bi'llatī hiya aḥsan* | **Argument** |
| **al-Qaṣaṣ 28/54** | *Yadraʾūn* — د-ر-أ | **Evil** |

The root **د-ر-أ**: **to push, to drive back, to ward off.** The lexicographers use the word especially for **getting in front of something and blocking it.**

**Three sūras, three distinct roots, one method.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

### ٱللَّغْو and the form of the answer

**The root ل-غ-و was analysed in detail at `056-vakia.md` 56/25** (speech that is not reckoned with, that goes for nothing) and had occurred at `041-fussilet.md` 41/26 as a **tactic** (*wa'lghaw fīh* — make a noise over it). **I do not repeat it here.**

**Here the same word comes in the context of a response, and the answer given is made of three parts:**

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| *Lanā aʿmālunā wa lakum aʿmālukum* | **Separation** — it divides the accounts |
| *Salāmun ʿalaykum* | **Peace** — it does not set up enmity |
| *Lā nabtaghi'l-jāhilīn* | **A boundary** — it does not seek the relationship |

**I record this as my own reading:** the answer is neither silence nor retaliation — **it is a third way.** And the *salām* in the middle of it keeps the separation from turning into enmity.

**The use of *salāmun ʿalaykum* in this context stands on the same line as `043-zuhruf.md` 43/89 (*fa'ṣfaḥ ʿanhum wa qul salām*) — there too a separation is sealed with a greeting of peace.**

---

## 28/56 — إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِى مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَهْدِى مَن يَشَآءُ

"**You cannot guide whom you love; but God guides whom He wills.**"

### The sharpest expression of the limit

**This limit has been treated in many places in this series, and each sūra used a different word:**

| Place | Expression | What it limits |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/35 | *Balāgh* | **The task** — it is delivery |
| al-Zumar 39/41 | *Wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl* | **Responsibility** |
| Qāf 50/45 | *Wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-jabbār* | **The authority to compel** |
| Fāṭir 35/8 | *Fa-lā tadhhab nafsuka ʿalayhim ḥasarāt* | **Grief** |
| Luqmān 31/23 | *Fa-lā yaḥzunka kufruh* | **Grief** |
| **al-Qaṣaṣ 28/56** | *Lā tahdī **man aḥbabta*** | **Love** — the closest bond |

**Six sūras, six distinct words.** And what is worth recording in al-Qaṣaṣ: **the limit is set upon love.** *Man aḥbabta* — "the one you love."

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of the verb:** in the other verses it was an authority or a feeling that was being limited; **here it is said that even the strongest bond does not produce the result.** And this agrees with the general line of the sūra: **the mother had cast her child into the water, and what brought about his return was not her love but the order of causes.**

## 28/57-60 — "If we follow the guidance together with you, we shall be snatched away from our land"

*Wa qālū in nattabiʿi'l-hudā maʿaka nutakhaṭṭaf min arḍinā · A-wa-lam numakkin lahum ḥaraman āminan yujbā ilayhi thamarātu kulli shayʾ*

"'**If we follow the guidance with you, we shall be snatched away from our land**,' they said. **Have We not settled them in a safe sanctuary to which the fruits of all things are gathered?**"

### Comparison with al-ʿAnkabūt

**The same root (خ-ط-ف) and the same proof were treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/67. The two verses can be set side by side, and the relation between them is worth recording:**

| Place | Expression | Who is snatched away |
|---|---|---|
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/67 | *Wa **yutakhaṭṭafu'n-nāsu** min ḥawlihim* | **Those around them** — they themselves are safe |
| **al-Qaṣaṣ 28/57** | *Nutakhaṭṭaf min arḍinā* | **They themselves** — the thing they fear |

**The same verb: in the one a fact that has come about, in the other a possibility that is feared.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**I record this as my own reading:** the objection is built not upon belief but **upon a concern for security** — and the answer is given on the same plane: **who established the place in which you are already safe?** That is, protection is tied back to the source of the very thing that would be given up for the sake of protection.

**وَمَا كَانَ رَبُّكَ مُهْلِكَ ٱلْقُرَىٰ حَتَّىٰ يَبْعَثَ فِىٓ أُمِّهَا رَسُولًا** (59) — **the condition of destruction: that a warner has been sent.**

**The same principle had been recorded at `035-fatir.md` 35/24** (*wa in min ummatin illā khalā fīhā nadhīr*). **The two sūras state the same principle from two directions: the one that a warner has been sent, the other that there is no destruction until one is sent.**

**وَمَآ أُوتِيتُم مِّن شَىْءٍ فَمَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَزِينَتُهَا وَمَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰٓ** (60) — and the block closes with a sentence that sets the measure back in its place.

## 28/61-70 — Three times, "He calls to them"

*Wa yawma yunādīhim fa-yaqūlu ayna shurakāʾiya'lladhīna kuntum tazʿumūn*

"**On that day He will call to them and say: 'Where are those you claimed were My partners?'**"

### The repeated pattern

**The same sentence occurs three times in the sūra, and each time a different question is asked:**

| Verse | Question | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| 62 | *Ayna shurakāʾiya'lladhīna kuntum tazʿumūn* | **Where are the partners** |
| 65 | *Mādhā ajabtumu'l-mursalīn* | **What answer did you give the messengers** |
| 74 | *Ayna shurakāʾiya'lladhīna kuntum tazʿumūn* | **Where are the partners** (again) |

**Three callings, two questions — and the first and the third are word for word the same.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**تَزْعُمُون** — root **ز-ع-م**: **to claim without ground.** The lexicographers record that the word is used of **speech whose truth is uncertain**. **That is, the question takes as its subject not the existence of the partners but the claim itself.**

**فَعَمِيَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْأَنۢبَآءُ يَوْمَئِذٍ فَهُمْ لَا يَتَسَآءَلُونَ** (66) — "**on that day the tidings will be closed off from them; they will not even be able to ask one another.**"

**عَمِيَتْ** — root **ع-م-ي**: blindness. **The subject of the verb is *al-anbāʾ* (the tidings): what goes blind is not the person but the tidings themselves.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence makes the knowledge unreachable — and makes the tidings the subject of the verb. **And the result is that solidarity is cut off as well: *fa-hum lā yatasāʾalūn* — they cannot ask one another.**

**At `040-gafir.md` 40/47 it was treated that the dispute continues in the fire;** here what is told is **that speech is cut off**. **The two scenes do not contradict each other — they tell of different moments.**

### وَرَبُّكَ يَخْلُقُ مَا يَشَآءُ وَيَخْتَارُ مَا كَانَ لَهُمُ ٱلْخِيَرَةُ (68)

"**Your Lord creates what He wills and chooses; they have no choice.**"

**The lexicographers dwell on the scope of the sentence, and the disagreement must not be concealed:**

| Reading | What *mā kāna lahumu'l-khiyara* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The choosing of prophets** — it is not they who determine who is to be a messenger |
| 2 | **General** — in the matter of creating and decreeing, the choice does not belong to them |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**The first reading is closer to the context** — because in this part of the sūra it is the messengers and the answer given to them that is being spoken of (65). **I record this as a supporting indication, not as a preference.**

**And the same objection had been treated at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/31-32** (*law lā nuzzila hādha'l-qurʾānu ʿalā rajulin mina'l-qaryatayni ʿaẓīm*) — there too the measure of the choosing was in dispute. **The two sūras take up the same matter from two different places.**

## 28/71-73 — سَرْمَدًا: unceasing night, unceasing day

*Qul a-raʾaytum in jaʿala'llāhu ʿalaykumu'l-layla **sarmadan** ilā yawmi'l-qiyāmati man ilāhun ghayru'llāhi yaʾtīkum bi-ḍiyāʾ … Qul a-raʾaytum in jaʿala'llāhu ʿalaykumu'n-nahāra **sarmadan** … man ilāhun ghayru'llāhi yaʾtīkum bi-laylin taskunūna fīh*

"Say: '**If God had made the night unceasing over you until the Day of Resurrection, what god other than God would bring you light?**' Say: '**If God had made the day unceasing over you until the Day of Resurrection, what god would bring you a night in which to rest?**'"

### Two symmetrical questions

**سَرْمَد** — **unceasing, continuing without a break.** The word occurs in the Qurʾān only in these two verses.

**The two questions are exactly symmetrical, and what answers to each must be recorded:**

| What continues | What is left missing | The word |
|---|---|---|
| **Night** | Light | *Ḍiyāʾ* |
| **Day** | Rest | *Laylin **taskunūna** fīh* |

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** what is asked for against the night is **light** — that is, sight; what is asked for against the day is **stillness** — that is, rest. **Two distinct needs, in two distinct words.**

**And the expression *taskunūna fīh* is worth recording: the root س-ك-ن (*sakīna*) was treated at `048-fetih.md` 48/4.** **Here the same root comes as the function of the night: coming to rest.**

**وَمِن رَّحْمَتِهِۦ جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلَّيْلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ لِتَسْكُنُوا۟ فِيهِ وَلِتَبْتَغُوا۟ مِن فَضْلِهِۦ** (73) — and the two questions close with a sentence that joins the two functions: **rest and earning.**

---

## 28/74-75 — وَنَزَعْنَا مِن كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ شَهِيدًا فَقُلْنَا هَاتُوا۟ بُرْهَٰنَكُمْ

"**We shall draw out a witness from every community and say: 'Bring your decisive proof!'** Then they will know that the truth belongs to God, and that what they used to invent has vanished from them."

**نَزَعْنَا** — root **ن-ز-ع**: **to pull out, to tear out.** **The harshness of the verb is worth recording:** the witness is not summoned, **he is drawn out.**

**بُرْهَان** — **decisive proof.** The lexicographers describe the word as the proof that binds the other side and puts an end to the dispute.

**And the form of the demand must be recorded: *hātū burhānakum* — "bring your proof".**

**This demand has been treated in several places in this series, and in all of them it comes from the same place:**

| Place | The demand |
|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/4 | *Iʾtūnī bi-kitābin min qabli hādhā aw athāratin min ʿilm* |
| Luqmān 31/11 | *Fa-arūnī mādhā khalaqa'lladhīna min dūnih* |
| Ghāfir 40/35, 40/56 | *Bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum* |
| **al-Qaṣaṣ 28/75** | *Hātū burhānakum* |

**Four sūras, one measure: a ground to rest on.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

**And the time of the demand is worth recording:** the question asked in this world (al-Aḥqāf, Luqmān) is asked here **on the day of the reckoning**. **That is, the measure does not change — what changes is the place where it is put.** I record this as my own reading.

**وَضَلَّ عَنْهُم مَّا كَانُوا۟ يَفْتَرُونَ** — "**what they used to invent vanished from them.**"

**The same reversal was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/28** (*bal ḍallū ʿanhum*): **the side that is called upon disappears.** I rely on that treatment.

## 28/76-82 — Qārūn

*Inna Qārūna kāna min qawmi Mūsā fa-baghā ʿalayhim wa ātaynāhu mina'l-kunūzi mā inna mafātiḥahū la-tanūʾu bi'l-ʿuṣbati uli'l-quwwa*

"Qārūn was of the people of Moses; **he behaved outrageously towards them.** We had given him treasures **whose very keys a band of strong men could hardly carry.**"

### How the measure is given

**The size of the wealth is not stated directly — it is given through one detail: *mafātiḥahū la-tanūʾu bi'l-ʿuṣbati uli'l-quwwa*.**

The root **ن-و-أ**: **to straighten up with difficulty under a weight, to be burdened in rising.** **That is, what is heavy is not the treasure — but *its keys*.**

**I record this as an observation:** the measure is given from the most indirect place there is. **And the reader is left obliged to imagine the treasure for himself.**

### قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أُوتِيتُهُۥ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ عِندِىٓ

"**This was given me only because of a knowledge that is mine.**"

**This sentence occurred at `039-zumer.md` 39/49 in almost the same words** (*innamā ūtītuhū ʿalā ʿilm*) and was treated there: **forgetting the source, and then claiming to be the source oneself.**

| Place | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/49 | *Innamā ūtītuhū ʿalā ʿilm* | **General** — a type of person |
| **al-Qaṣaṣ 28/78** | *Innamā ūtītuhū ʿalā ʿilmin **ʿindī*** | **Qārūn** — with *ʿindī* (that I have) added |

**In al-Qaṣaṣ there is one word more: *ʿindī*.** I record this as my own reading: **the addition raises the ownership of the claim by one further degree** — knowledge is named not merely as the cause but as a cause **belonging to him**.

### فَخَسَفْنَا بِهِۦ وَبِدَارِهِ ٱلْأَرْضَ

The root **خ-س-ف**: **to sink into the ground, to cave in.** **The root was treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/40 as one of four forms of destruction.**

**And the form of the destruction is worth recording:** *bihī wa bi-dārihī* — **himself and his house.** **That is, the place where the display was made is named along with him** — for indeed in verse 79 it had been said *fa-kharaja ʿalā qawmihī fī zīnatih* (he went out before his people in his finery).

**وَأَصْبَحَ ٱلَّذِينَ تَمَنَّوْا۟ مَكَانَهُۥ بِٱلْأَمْسِ يَقُولُونَ وَيْكَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَبْسُطُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ** (82) — "**those who the day before had wished themselves in his place began the next morning to say…**"

**I record this as an observation:** the verse sets the wish of one day and the words of the next side by side. **And the only thing that has changed in between is that the event has been seen.**

## 28/83-88 — The close of the sūra

*Tilka'd-dāru'l-ākhiratu najʿaluhā li'lladhīna lā yurīdūna ʿuluwwan fi'l-arḍi wa lā fasādā wa'l-ʿāqibatu li'l-muttaqīn*

"**That is the abode of the hereafter! We give it to those who want neither exaltation in the land nor corruption.** The outcome belongs to those who guard themselves."

### The closing of the ring

**This verse corresponds exactly to the fourth verse of the sūra** — I set it out in a table in the section on the structure:

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| 4 | *Inna Firʿawna **ʿalā** fi'l-arḍi … innahū kāna mina'l-**mufsidīn*** |
| 83 | *Li'lladhīna lā yurīdūna **ʿuluwwan** fi'l-arḍi wa lā **fasādā*** |

**Both words are the same: *ʿuluww* and *fasād*.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**And the choice of the verb is worth recording: *lā yurīdūna* — "those who do not want".** **That is, the measure is not the deed done — but the thing wanted.** I give this as my own reading; its ground is that the verb states an act of will.

**مَن جَآءَ بِٱلْحَسَنَةِ فَلَهُۥ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهَا وَمَن جَآءَ بِٱلسَّيِّئَةِ فَلَا يُجْزَى ٱلَّذِينَ عَمِلُوا۟ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ إِلَّا مَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ** (84) — **the asymmetry between the two sides:**

| Side | The return |
|---|---|
| The one who brings good | *Fa-lahū **khayrun minhā*** — **better than what he brought** |
| The one who brings evil | *Fa-lā yujzā … illā mā kānū yaʿmalūn* — **only as much as he did** |

**On the one side an increase, on the other exact equivalence.** This is a difference that can be verified from the text.

**إِنَّ ٱلَّذِى فَرَضَ عَلَيْكَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ لَرَآدُّكَ إِلَىٰ مَعَادٍ** (85) — "**He who made the Qurʾān binding upon you will surely bring you back to a place of return.**"

**What the word *maʿād* refers to has been disputed in the classical commentaries:** the city of his birth, the hereafter, or the place of return in general. **I transmit the disagreement; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the verb is worth recording: *la-rādduka* — "will bring you back".** **The same root (ر-د-د) had occurred in the seventh verse of the sūra, in the promise given to the mother:** *innā **rāddūhu** ilayk* — "We shall return him to you."

| Verse | Expression | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | *Innā rāddūhu ilayk* | **To the mother** — the child will come back |
| 85 | *La-rādduka ilā maʿād* | **To the Prophet** — he will reach the place of return |

**The same root, at the two ends of the sūra, for two distinct returns.** I record this as my own reading: **the sūra begins with a promise of return and ends with a promise of return.**

**كُلُّ شَىْءٍ هَالِكٌ إِلَّا وَجْهَهُۥ** (88) — "**Everything is perishing except His face.**"

**The note kept in this series about the word *wajh* (face) holds here too:** the expression cannot be understood in a way that ascribes a limb or a body to God. **The same note had been entered at `002-bakara.md` 2/115 (*fa-thamma wajhu'llāh*);** I rely on that treatment. **It is transmitted that in the classical commentaries *wajh* is explained in the sense of "the essence" or "His good pleasure".**

**هَالِك** — **an active participle: "perishing".** The lexicographers record that the pattern here states **a state already in progress**: not what will perish, but what is perishing.

# 27. Sūrat al-Naml

**Ninety-three verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the scene in verse 18: **وَادِ ٱلنَّمْل** — *wādi'n-naml*, **the valley of the ants** — where, as the army of Solomon (Sulaymān) approaches a valley, an ant calls out to its own community, and Solomon hears what it says and smiles.

**The sūra takes its name from the smallest speaker in its narratives.** This is striking among the names of the sūras: al-Naml carries the name neither of a prophet nor of a people nor of a concept — but of **an ant that speaks once, in a single scene.** I record this as an observation; it will be treated separately below, at verses 18-19.

The basmala was treated in earlier sections; the verses are numbered without it.

---

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-6 | The letters, the book, two kinds of hearer | *min ladun ḥakīmin ʿalīm* (6) |
| **II** | 7-14 | **Moses** (Mūsā) — the fire, the nine signs, denial against knowledge | *kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-mufsidīn* (14) |
| **III** | 15-19 | **David and Solomon** (Dāwūd, Sulaymān) — knowledge, the army, **the valley of the ants** | *fī ʿibādika'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn* (19) |
| **IV** | 20-44 | **The hoopoe and the queen of Sabaʾ** | *aslamtu maʿa Sulaymāna li'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn* (44) |
| **V** | 45-53 | **Thamūd** — two factions and nine men | *wa anjaynā'lladhīna āmanū wa kānū yattaqūn* (53) |
| **VI** | 54-58 | **Lot** (Lūṭ) | *fa-sāʾa maṭaru'l-mundharīn* (58) |
| **VII** | 59-64 | Praise, greeting, and **a sequence of proof in five questions** | *hātū burhānakum in kuntum ṣādiqīn* (64) |
| **VIII** | 65-81 | The unseen, the objection to resurrection, and the limit of the messenger | *fa-hum muslimūn* (81) |
| **IX** | 82-93 | Scenes of the Last Day, and the close | *wa mā rabbuka bi-ghāfilin ʿammā taʿmalūn* (93) |

### The spine of the sūra: ع-ل-م

**From beginning to end this is a sūra about knowledge, and that is a countable fact.** The root occurs in every block of the sūra, and each time it marks a position:

| Verse | Expression | In whom |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | *min ladun ḥakīmin **ʿalīm*** | **In the source** — where the book comes from |
| 14 | *wa'sta**yqan**athā anfusuhum* | **In the denier** — who rejects what he knows |
| 15 | *wa laqad ātaynā Dāwūda wa Sulaymāna **ʿilmā*** | **In two prophets** — given |
| 16 | ***ʿullimnā** manṭiqa'ṭ-ṭayr* | **In Solomon** — taught |
| 22 | ***aḥaṭtu** bimā lam **tuḥiṭ** bih* | **In the bird** — exceeding the king's |
| 40 | *alladhī ʿindahū **ʿilmun** mina'l-kitāb* | **In a man who is not named** |
| 42 | *wa ūtīna'l-**ʿilma** min qablihā* | **In the queen** — given beforehand |
| 52 | *la-āyatan li-qawmin **yaʿlamūn*** | **In those who look** — who take the lesson |
| 61 | *bal aktharuhum lā **yaʿlamūn*** | **Absent** — in the majority |
| 65 | *lā **yaʿlamu** man fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi'l-ghayba illā'llāh* | **In God alone** — the unseen |
| 84 | *wa lam **tuḥīṭū** bihā **ʿilmā*** | **Absent** — in those who deny |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence above:** the sūra passes knowledge from prophet to bird, from bird to queen, from queen to reader — **and at verse 65 it ties off the end of the sequence.** That is, the sūra exalts knowledge and then draws its limit.

### The second spine: و-ز-ع

**The root occurs three times in the sūra, and all three fall in different blocks:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | *fa-hum **yūzaʿūn*** | **The armies of Solomon** — held in order |
| 19 | *rabbi **awziʿ**nī an ashkura* | **Solomon** — dispose me to give thanks |
| 83 | *fa-hum **yūzaʿūn*** | **The deniers, gathered** — held in order |

**Verses 17 and 83 are word for word the same: *fa-hum yūzaʿūn*.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**The sense of the root was worked out in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/15:** *to take hold of something and steer it, to rein it in; to hold an army in order.* **I do not repeat it here.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the three occurrences:** the same root names first the order of an earthly army, then the order a prophet asks for inside himself, and at the end the order of those gathered on the day of reckoning. **The word thus travels from outside to inside, and from inside back out again.**

### The third spine: وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

| Verse | Expression | Who does not notice |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | *wa hum lā yashʿurūn* | **Solomon and his armies** — of what the ant said |
| 50 | *wa hum lā yashʿurūn* | **The nine men of Thamūd** — of their own plot |
| 65 | *wa mā yashʿurūna ayyāna yubʿathūn* | **Everyone** — of when they will be raised |

**The same verb three times, three different failures to notice.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and each case will be treated separately below in its place.

## 27/1-3 — طسٓ · تِلْكَ ءَايَٰتُ ٱلْقُرْءَانِ وَكِتَابٍ مُّبِينٍ · هُدًى وَبُشْرَىٰ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · ٱلَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَهُم بِٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ هُمْ يُوقِنُونَ

*Ṭā-sīn · tilka āyātu'l-qurʾāni wa kitābin mubīn · hudan wa bushrā li'l-muʾminīn · alladhīna yuqīmūna'ṣ-ṣalāta wa yuʾtūna'z-zakāta wa hum bi'l-ākhirati hum yūqinūn*

"Ṭā-sīn. **These are the verses of the Qurʾān and of a clear Book: a guidance and glad tidings for those who believe.** They perform the prayer, they give the alms, and **they are certain of the hereafter.**"

### The disconnected letters

**This note has been entered many times in this series — at length in `002-bakara.md` at 2/1, and in summary in `050-kaf.md` at 50/1. I do not repeat it here.** The summary was this: the meaning of the letters is **not known with certainty**; one group of scholars refers the meaning to God; among the views that seek an explanation, the one that finds most support from within the text is that the letters, by pointing to the **raw material** of the Qurʾān, make the challenge meaningful. **And, as was written there plainly: no numerical calculation, no abjad reckoning and no counting of letters is carried out on these letters.**

### Standing back to back with al-Qaṣaṣ: طسٓ / طسٓمٓ

**The two sūras stand side by side in the order of the codex, and their openings are almost the same. The comparison can be verified from the text:**

| | **al-Naml 27/1** | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/1-2 |
|---|---|---|
| The letters | *Ṭā-sīn* — **two letters** | *Ṭā-sīn-mīm* — **three letters** |
| The demonstrative | *tilka* | *tilka* |
| Verses of what | *āyātu'l-**qurʾāni** wa kitābin mubīn* | *āyātu'l-**kitābi**'l-mubīn* |
| What follows | **For whom it is** — *hudan wa bushrā li'l-muʾminīn* | **What will be done** — *natlū ʿalayka min nabaʾi Mūsā* |

**Two differences are worth recording, and both are countable data.**

**First:** in al-Naml **two** names are given — *al-Qurʾān* and *kitābun mubīn*; in al-Qaṣaṣ there is **one**. **Qurʾān**, from the root ق-ر-أ, names what is **recited**; **kitāb**, from the root ك-ت-ب, names what is **written**. **The opening of al-Naml thus names the text in both its modes at once: as sound and as script.**

**Second:** the opening of al-Naml goes on by describing **the one addressed** (*li'l-muʾminīn*, and then three attributes), while al-Qaṣaṣ goes on by announcing **what is to be narrated**. **This is a difference of method between the two sūras: one begins by defining its reader, the other by announcing its subject.** I record this as my own reading; its ground is the sentences that continue the two openings.

**And as was recorded in `028-kasas.md`, no conclusion is drawn from the way the disconnected letters are arranged across sūras.** That the two sūras are neighbours is a datum; **I draw no inference from it about the meaning of the letters.**

### The verse that coincides exactly with Luqmān

**The third verse is word for word the same as 31/4 in `031-lokman.md`:** *alladhīna yuqīmūna'ṣ-ṣalāta wa yuʾtūna'z-zakāta wa hum bi'l-ākhirati hum yūqinūn.*

**And the openings of three sūras can be set side by side:**

| | al-Baqara 2/2-3 | Luqmān 31/3-4 | **al-Naml 27/2-3** |
|---|---|---|---|
| For whom | *hudan li'l-**muttaqīn*** | *hudan wa raḥmatan li'l-**muḥsinīn*** | *hudan wa **bushrā** li'l-**muʾminīn*** |
| The second word | — | *raḥma* | **bushrā** |
| The attributes | Faith in the unseen, prayer, spending | Prayer, alms, certainty of the hereafter | **Prayer, alms, certainty of the hereafter** |

**The lists of attributes in Luqmān and in al-Naml are the same; the difference lies in the second word: there *raḥma*, here *bushrā*.** This is an overlap and a difference that can be verified from the text.

**بُشْرَىٰ** — root **ب-ش-ر**: the concrete sense of the root is **skin, the outer surface of the flesh** (*bashara*). **Glad tidings come from this root, because good news shows first in the face** — the colour of the face changes. **The root was treated in `043-zuhruf.md`, `048-fetih.md` and `037-saffat.md`; I do not repeat it here.**

**I record this as an observation:** al-Naml is a sūra whose narratives end in joy — Solomon smiles (19), the queen submits (44), those who are delivered are delivered (53, 57). **The *bushrā* of the opening agrees with the sūra's choice of narrative.** I record this as an overlap, not as a claim.

**يُوقِنُونَ** — root **ي-ق-ن**: the concrete sense the lexicographers give is **water settling and running clear, the sediment sinking to the bottom**. *Yaqīn* is knowledge from which the cloudiness has gone. **The root was treated in `056-vakia.md` and `044-duhan.md`.**

**And the word will occur once more in this sūra — at verse 82, in exactly the opposite direction:** *anna'n-nāsa kānū bi-āyātinā lā yūqinūn.* **The sūra defines the one it addresses by certainty, and ties off its close with the absence of certainty.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

## 27/4-6 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ زَيَّنَّا لَهُمْ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ فَهُمْ يَعْمَهُونَ · أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَهُمْ سُوٓءُ ٱلْعَذَابِ وَهُمْ فِى ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ هُمُ ٱلْأَخْسَرُونَ · وَإِنَّكَ لَتُلَقَّى ٱلْقُرْءَانَ مِن لَّدُنْ حَكِيمٍ عَلِيمٍ

*inna'lladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi'l-ākhirati zayyannā lahum aʿmālahum fa-hum yaʿmahūn · ulāʾika'lladhīna lahum sūʾu'l-ʿadhābi wa hum fi'l-ākhirati humu'l-akhsarūn · wa innaka la-tulaqqa'l-qurʾāna min ladun ḥakīmin ʿalīm*

"**As for those who do not believe in the hereafter: We have made their deeds seem fair to them, and so they wander on in bewilderment.** Theirs is the worst of the punishment, and **in the hereafter they are the greatest losers.** **And you are receiving the Qurʾān from One who is wise and knowing.**"

### زَيَّنَّا لَهُمْ — and the question of the verb's agent

The root **ز-ي-ن**: to adorn, to make something look fair. **The pattern is Form II, and it conveys that the act is performed upon something.**

**The use of this root in the Qurʾān was recorded in `057-hadid.md`, and it applies directly here:** the Qurʾān uses the act of "making deeds seem fair" in three distinct ways.

| Agent | Example | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **Satan** | *wa zayyana lahumu'sh-shayṭānu aʿmālahum* — in this sūra, **27/24** | The agent is named |
| **Passive** | *zuyyina lahum sūʾu ʿamalihim* (Fāṭir 35/8) | The agent is not stated |
| **God** | *zayyannā lahum aʿmālahum* — in this sūra, **27/4** | As a settled way of acting (*sunna*) |

**And both agents occur within this sūra itself: *zayyannā* in verse 4, *zayyana lahumu'sh-shayṭān* in verse 24.** This is a pairing that can be verified within the sūra.

**This question was treated in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/14, and the resolution recorded there was this: the two usages do not contradict each other** — one names **the one who does the act directly**, the other **the one who lays down the law under which it happens**. **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat the discussion.**

**And the verse's own qualification must not be missed: the sentence opens with a condition — *alladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi'l-ākhira*.** **The adorning is thus named as the consequence of not believing in the hereafter, not as its cause.** I record this as an observation about the word order: in the sentence, the disbelief comes first and the adorning second.

### يَعْمَهُونَ

The root **ع-م-ه**: **to be unable to find one's way although the eye sees, to flounder about in bewilderment.** **The lexicographers compare the root with ع-م-ي (blindness) and record the difference:** *ʿamā* is the eye's not seeing, whereas *ʿamah* is **the loss not of sight but of direction.**

**The distinction works here, and I record this as my own reading:** the verse does not call the man whose deeds have been made fair to him blind. It calls him **one who sees and does not know where to go.** **The adorning does not damage his sight; it damages his bearing.**

**And the root comes once more, at verse 66 of the sūra, this time alongside the other one: *bal hum minhā ʿamūn* — ع-م-ي.** Two roots in two separate places in the same sūra; **I claim no derivational link between them: they are two distinct roots.** What I record is only the nearness of meaning.

### ٱلْأَخْسَرُونَ

The root **خ-س-ر**: **to diminish, to lose one's capital.** *Khusrān* — the loss of the principal in trade. **The word comes here on the *afʿal* pattern and with the article: *al-akhsarūn* — "those who lose most."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** loss is given here as a degree, not as an absolute verdict. **And a comparison is set up implicitly: everyone loses something; these are the ones who lose most.**

### لَتُلَقَّى ٱلْقُرْءَانَ

The root **ل-ق-ي**, **not in Form V but in the passive of Form II**: *tulaqqā* — **"it is being given to you, it is being conveyed to you."**

**This pattern was recorded in `073-muzzemmil.md`**, and the root's branches — "to let go from the hand / to meet" — were worked out there. **I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is the tense of the verb, and it must be recorded: *tulaqqā* is imperfect (*muḍāriʿ*) — an act in progress.** **The sentence does not say "it was given to you"; it says "it is being given to you".** I record this as an observation about the word order: the sūra speaks at a moment when the sending down is still going on.

**مِن لَّدُنْ حَكِيمٍ عَلِيمٍ** — *ladun* means "from the presence of", and the lexicographers record that it conveys a more inward nearness than *ʿinda*.

**The choice of the two names and their order is worth recording:** *Ḥakīm* (the one who acts with wisdom) and *ʿAlīm* (the one who knows). **And every narrative in the sūra will take place between these two names:** knowledge given (15), a language taught (16), knowledge that exceeds (22), the one who brings out what is hidden (25), the knowing of the One who knows (65). **The opening thus names the subject of the sūra in advance, with two names.** I give this as my own reading; its ground is the ع-ل-م table above.

## 27/7-8 — إِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِأَهْلِهِۦٓ إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ نَارًا سَـَٔاتِيكُم مِّنْهَا بِخَبَرٍ أَوْ ءَاتِيكُم بِشِهَابٍ قَبَسٍ لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَصْطَلُونَ · فَلَمَّا جَآءَهَا نُودِىَ أَنۢ بُورِكَ مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا وَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

*idh qāla Mūsā li-ahlihī innī ānastu nārā, sa-ātīkum minhā bi-khabarin aw ātīkum bi-shihābin qabasin laʿallakum taṣṭalūn · fa-lammā jāʾahā nūdiya an būrika man fi'n-nāri wa man ḥawlahā wa subḥāna'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn*

"When Moses said to his family: '**I have made out a fire. I shall bring you news from it, or bring you a burning brand, so that you may warm yourselves.**' **When he came to it, a voice called out: 'Blessed is the one in the fire and the one around it. And glory be to God, the Lord of all the worlds.'**"

### A comparison with al-Qaṣaṣ — one scene, two tellings

**This scene was treated in `028-kasas.md` at 28/29-35, and the root أ-ن-س was worked out there:** *to catch sight of, to make out; to grow warm;* from the same root come *ins* (human being) and *uns* (intimacy). **The lexicographers record the sense "to sense a light from a distance".** **I do not repeat the analysis of the root; I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is the difference between the two tellings, and all of it can be verified from the text:**

| | **al-Naml 27/7-8** | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/29-30 |
|---|---|---|
| The making out | *innī **ānastu** nārā* | *ānasa min jānibi'ṭ-Ṭūri nārā* |
| Statement of place | **None** — no place is named | ***min jānibi'ṭ-Ṭūr*** — the side of Mount Ṭūr |
| What is to be brought | *bi-khabarin* **aw** *bi-**shihābin qabasin*** | *bi-khabarin* **aw** *bi-**jadhwatin** mina'n-nār* |
| The purpose | *laʿallakum **taṣṭalūn*** | *laʿallakum **taṣṭalūn*** |
| The call | ***nūdiya an būrika man fi'n-nāri wa man ḥawlahā*** | *nūdiya min shāṭiʾi'l-wādi'l-aymani fi'l-buqʿati'l-mubārakati mina'sh-shajarati* |
| The content of the call | **A declaration of blessing** + *subḥāna'llāh* | **A declaration of identity** — *innī ana'llāhu rabbu'l-ʿālamīn* |

**Three differences are worth recording.**

**First — the name of the ember.** In al-Qaṣaṣ, *jadhwa*; in al-Naml, *shihābun qabas*.

- **جَذْوَة** (*jadhwa*) — a piece of wood with its end alight; **the log itself.**
- **شِهَاب** (*shihāb*) — a shaft of light that shines and blazes. **This word was treated in `072-cin.md`**, and it was recorded there that the root is used both for a bright trail in the sky and for a flame from a fire.
- **قَبَس** (*qabas*) — root **ق-ب-س**: **a piece taken from a fire.** *Iqtibās* — taking and taking from someone; the word's present-day sense of "quotation" comes from here.

**In al-Qaṣaṣ, then, it is the wood itself that is named; in al-Naml, *the piece taken from a fire*.** I record this as an observation: **the word al-Naml chooses contains the act of taking.**

**Second — al-Naml gives no place name.** Al-Qaṣaṣ names Mount Ṭūr, the right bank of the valley and the tree; **al-Naml says only "a fire".** This is a difference in the two tellings' choice of detail, and it is a countable datum.

**Third — the content of the call is altogether different.** In al-Qaṣaṣ the one who calls **identifies himself**; in al-Naml the first thing said is **a declaration of blessing**: *būrika man fi'n-nāri wa man ḥawlahā.* **In al-Naml the declaration of identity comes one verse later, at verse 9.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two texts:** al-Naml opens the scene with **the hallowing of the place**, al-Qaṣaṣ with **the introduction of the speaker.** The two sūras cut into the same moment from two different points.

### بُورِكَ مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا

The root **ب-ر-ك** was worked out in `025-furkan.md` at 25/1 and in `067-mulk.md` at 67/1: **a camel's kneeling down, settling in a place and staying fixed there;** and from this, **blessing** — a good that abides and increases. **I do not repeat it here.**

**The indefiniteness of the sentence must be recorded, and it is a datum of the text: the particle *man* ("who") is used, and who is meant is not said.** More than one reading of this expression is transmitted in the classical commentaries:

| Reading | Who *man fi'n-nār* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Moses** — the one standing at the place where the fire is |
| 2 | **The angels** — those who are reported to have been present there |
| 3 | **Light** — the divine manifestation that appeared as fire, the manner of which is not known |

**I do not impose a preference in this disagreement.** And I do draw one limit: **the verse cannot be read in a way that would mean God is inside the fire** — and indeed *subḥāna'llāh* (He is free of every deficiency) follows immediately upon the sentence. **That this clause of transcendence is placed at exactly this point is a datum of the word order, and it blocks the mistaken reading above.** I record this as an observation about the word order.

### The ordinariness of the scene

**The observation recorded in `028-kasas.md` holds here too, and rather than repeat it I add something to it:** the call comes not at a place that was sought out, but **on a road, at a point turned aside to for an everyday need.**

**What is peculiar to al-Naml is this addition, and I give it as my own reading:** the two things asked for in al-Naml — **news** and **warmth** — are the sūra's own subjects. **News will be carried throughout the sūra** (the hoopoe brings *nabaʾin yaqīn* at 27/22; the letter is carried at 27/28), **and the sūra will turn on the question "who brings news".** The first word Moses speaks as he turns towards the fire is *bi-khabarin*. **I record this as an overlap, not as a claim about intent.**

## 27/9-11 — يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَنَا ٱللَّهُ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ · وَأَلْقِ عَصَاكَ فَلَمَّا رَءَاهَا تَهْتَزُّ كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ وَلَّىٰ مُدْبِرًا وَلَمْ يُعَقِّبْ يَٰمُوسَىٰ لَا تَخَفْ إِنِّى لَا يَخَافُ لَدَىَّ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · إِلَّا مَن ظَلَمَ ثُمَّ بَدَّلَ حُسْنًۢا بَعْدَ سُوٓءٍ فَإِنِّى غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

*yā Mūsā innahū ana'llāhu'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm · wa alqi ʿaṣāk, fa-lammā raʾāhā tahtazzu ka-annahā jānnun wallā mudbiran wa lam yuʿaqqib, yā Mūsā lā takhaf innī lā yakhāfu ladayya'l-mursalūn · illā man ẓalama thumma baddala ḥusnan baʿda sūʾin fa-innī ghafūrun raḥīm*

"'**O Moses! It is I: God, the Mighty, the Wise. Cast down your staff!**' When he saw it writhing as though it were a serpent, **he turned and fled without looking back.** '**O Moses! Do not fear; in My presence the messengers do not fear. Except one who has done wrong and then, after evil, has turned it to good — for I am much-forgiving, most merciful.**'"

### The same two words as in al-Qaṣaṣ

**These two verbs were treated in `028-kasas.md` at 28/31:** *wallā mudbiran wa lam yuʿaqqib* — **he fled without turning to look behind him.** The root **ع-ق-ب**: **the heel;** *taʿqīb* — to go after someone, to turn back and look. **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

**What is peculiar to al-Naml is verse 11, added to this passage, and it is worth recording.**

### إِلَّا مَن ظَلَمَ ثُمَّ بَدَّلَ حُسْنًۢا بَعْدَ سُوٓءٍ

**Verse 11 sets an exception upon the sentence in verse 10:** *innī lā yakhāfu ladayya'l-mursalūn* — "in My presence the messengers do not fear" — **illā man ẓalama…**

**What the clause attaches to has been debated in the classical commentaries, and the disagreement is not concealed:**

| Reading | What the exception attaches to | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A disjunctive (*munqaṭiʿ*) exception** — not from the messengers but from people in general | The messengers do not fear; **as for others**, the one who does wrong fears, but if he repents he is forgiven |
| 2 | **A connected (*muttaṣil*) exception** — to a lapse within the messengers' own lives | If a messenger has had a lapse, his fear is lifted once he turns it to good |

**Both readings are transmitted in the classical commentaries; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record one indication, not a preference:** in the narrative of al-Qaṣaṣ, Moses says in his own words *innī ẓalamtu nafsī* ("I have wronged myself") — **treated in `028-kasas.md` at 28/16.** **The same root (ظ-ل-م) occurs here, in the clause of exception.** This is a verbal overlap between the two sūras that can be verified; **it is an indication in support of the second reading, but it is not a proof.**

**And the structure of the verse is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the sentence is built in three steps — **wrongdoing** (*ẓalama*), **exchange** (*baddala*), **forgiveness** (*ghafūrun raḥīm*). **The middle step is not erasure but exchange:** *baddala ḥusnan baʿda sūʾin* — "he turned it to good after evil." **The past is not treated as though it had not happened; something is set on top of it.**

**بَدَّلَ** — root **ب-د-ل**, Form II: **to put something else in the place of a thing.** *Tabdīl* — causing an exchange of places. **The verb conveys not the erasure of a thing but the filling of its place.**

## 27/12 — وَأَدْخِلْ يَدَكَ فِى جَيْبِكَ تَخْرُجْ بَيْضَآءَ مِنْ غَيْرِ سُوٓءٍ فِى تِسْعِ ءَايَٰتٍ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ وَقَوْمِهِۦٓ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ قَوْمًا فَٰسِقِينَ

*wa adkhil yadaka fī jaybika takhruj bayḍāʾa min ghayri sūʾin fī tisʿi āyātin ilā Firʿawna wa qawmih, innahum kānū qawman fāsiqīn*

"'**Put your hand into the fold of your garment; it will come out white, without blemish. These are among nine signs to Pharaoh and his people. They are a people gone out of the way.**'"

### تِسْعِ ءَايَٰتٍ — the nine signs

**The Qurʾān counts the signs given to Moses as *nine* in two places: here and al-Isrāʾ 17/101.** **This is a fact that can be verified from the text**, and it was recorded in `054-kamer.md` at 54/41-42: **Sūrat al-Qamar gathered the same signs into a single word, *kullihā* ("all of them").**

**The method of the three sūras can be set side by side:**

| Place | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| al-Qamar 54/42 | *kadhdhabū bi-āyātinā **kullihā*** | **Gathering** — it gives no number |
| **al-Naml 27/12** | *fī **tisʿi** āyāt* | **Counting** — nine |
| al-Isrāʾ 17/101 | *tisʿa āyātin **bayyināt*** | **Counting** + a quality |

**Which signs the nine are has been counted in different ways in the classical commentaries, and the list is not entirely fixed.** The staff, the hand, famine, the loss of crops, the flood, the locusts, the vermin, the frogs and the blood — these items are transmitted; **there is disagreement over the order and over some of the items. I do not impose a definite list.**

**بَيْضَآءَ مِنْ غَيْرِ سُوٓءٍ** — "white, without blemish." **The note matters: the phrase *min ghayri sūʾ* states plainly that the whiteness is not the mark of a disease.** The text itself adds a gloss, so that the sign will not be read as a defect.

**And the word *sūʾ* had already occurred in the preceding verse: *baddala ḥusnan baʿda sūʾin*.** **The same word in two consecutive verses, in two different domains: one moral, the other bodily.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra; **I build no link of meaning on it; I record only the repetition.**

## 27/13-14 — فَلَمَّا جَآءَتْهُمْ ءَايَٰتُنَا مُبْصِرَةً قَالُوا۟ هَٰذَا سِحْرٌ مُّبِينٌ · وَجَحَدُوا۟ بِهَا وَٱسْتَيْقَنَتْهَآ أَنفُسُهُمْ ظُلْمًا وَعُلُوًّا فَٱنظُرْ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْمُفْسِدِينَ

*fa-lammā jāʾathum āyātunā mubṣiratan qālū hādhā siḥrun mubīn · wa jaḥadū bihā wa'stayqanathā anfusuhum ẓulman wa ʿuluwwā, fa'nẓur kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-mufsidīn*

"**When Our signs came to them, laid open to sight, they said: 'This is plain sorcery.' They denied them out of wrongdoing and self-exaltation, though their own selves were certain of them. Look, then, how the end of those who spread corruption came about!**"

### مُبْصِرَةً — a point about the pattern

The root **ب-ص-ر**: to see. **The word here is *mubṣira* — an active participle from Form IV: "that which makes one see, that which opens the eye."**

**A point about the word order must be recorded:** the adjective used of the signs is not "visible" but **"making visible"**. **The signs themselves furnish a seeing.** And this makes the denial that follows immediately upon it sharper still: **not to see, in the face of what makes one see.**

**The same pattern will occur once more, at verse 86 of this sūra: *wa'n-nahāra mubṣiran* — "and the day, giving sight."** **The same adjective, once for the signs and once for the day.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I shall return to it there.

### وَجَحَدُوا۟ بِهَا وَٱسْتَيْقَنَتْهَآ أَنفُسُهُمْ

The root **ج-ح-د** was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/26, and the core recorded there was this: **to deny what one knows;** and the concrete sense the lexicographers record — ***arḍun jahād*: ground that is barren and dry.** **Denial is thus described not as ignorance but as a ground that does not give back what it takes in. I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs to al-Naml is that this verse defines *juḥūd* in a single sentence — and the definition is in the text itself:**

| Element | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| What is outside | *wa jaḥadū bihā* | **Denial** — with the tongue, in the open |
| What is inside | *wa'stayqanathā **anfusuhum*** | **Certain knowledge** — within |
| Cause 1 | *ẓulman* | **Wrongdoing** |
| Cause 2 | *wa ʿuluwwan* | **Self-exaltation** |

**ٱسْتَيْقَنَتْهَآ** — root **ي-ق-ن**, Form X. **The same root as the *yūqinūn* of the sūra's third verse.** **And the subject of the verb is *anfusuhum* (their own selves): it does not say "they knew", it says "their own selves knew it with certainty".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of subject:** the sentence divides the person in two — **the one who denies with his tongue, and the inner self that knows.** **The verse thus describes denial not as a lack of knowledge but as a division.**

**And its link with the third verse of the sūra is worth recording:** there the believers were defined as *yūqinūn* (they are certain). **Here the same root is found inside those who deny.** **The difference does not lie in whether the knowledge is present — but in whether it comes to speech.** This is a repetition of a root that can be verified within the sūra.

### ظُلْمًا وَعُلُوًّا — the ring formed with al-Qaṣaṣ

The root **ع-ل-و** was set out in a table in `028-kasas.md` as the spine of that sūra:

| Place | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/4 | *inna Firʿawna **ʿalā** fi'l-arḍ* | **Pharaoh** — he exalted himself in the land |
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/83 | *lā yurīdūna **ʿuluwwan** fi'l-arḍi wa lā fasādā* | **To whom the abode of the hereafter is given** |
| **al-Naml 27/14** | *ẓulman wa **ʿuluwwā*** | **The people of Pharaoh** — the cause of the denial |
| **al-Naml 27/31** | *allā **taʿlū** ʿalayya wa'tūnī muslimīn* | **Solomon's letter** — what is forbidden |

**Four occurrences, two sūras.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

**And the closing of the ring must be recorded:** in al-Qaṣaṣ, *ʿuluww* and *fasād* stood side by side in the same two verses (4 and 83). **Verse 14 of al-Naml carries the same pair:** *ʿuluwwā*, and immediately after it *ʿāqibatu'l-**mufsidīn***.

| | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/4 | **al-Naml 27/14** |
|---|---|---|
| The first word | *ʿalā* — ع-ل-و | *ʿuluwwan* — ع-ل-و |
| The second word | *mina'l-mufsidīn* — ف-س-د | *ʿāqibatu'l-mufsidīn* — ف-س-د |

**The two sūras use the same pair in the same scene.** I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above: **al-Naml repeats in a single verse the frame built in al-Qaṣaṣ, and then passes on to its own narratives.**

**And the word *ʿuluww* will occur once more in this sūra, at verse 31, in Solomon's letter — this time as a prohibition.** **The sūra thus uses the same word once as the cause of denial and once as the first condition of a letter of embassy.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I shall return to it at verse 31.

## 27/15-16 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَا دَاوُۥدَ وَسُلَيْمَٰنَ عِلْمًا وَقَالَا ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى فَضَّلَنَا عَلَىٰ كَثِيرٍ مِّنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · وَوَرِثَ سُلَيْمَٰنُ دَاوُۥدَ وَقَالَ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ عُلِّمْنَا مَنطِقَ ٱلطَّيْرِ وَأُوتِينَا مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ ٱلْفَضْلُ ٱلْمُبِينُ

*wa laqad ātaynā Dāwūda wa Sulaymāna ʿilmā, wa qāla'l-ḥamdu li'llāhi'lladhī faḍḍalanā ʿalā kathīrin min ʿibādihi'l-muʾminīn · wa waritha Sulaymānu Dāwūd, wa qāla yā ayyuha'n-nāsu ʿullimnā manṭiqa'ṭ-ṭayri wa ūtīnā min kulli shayʾ, inna hādhā la-huwa'l-faḍlu'l-mubīn*

"**We gave David and Solomon a knowledge, and both of them said: 'Praise be to God, who has favoured us above many of His believing servants.'** **Solomon inherited from David, and said: 'O people! We have been taught the speech of birds, and we have been given of everything. This is indeed a plain favour.'**"

### The first thing given: عِلْم

**The narrative opens not with kingship but with knowledge, and this is a datum of the word order.** *Ātaynā… ʿilman* — the object of the first sentence is *knowledge*.

**And that the word comes indefinite is worth recording: *ʿilman* — "a knowledge."** **Of what it is knowledge is not said.** I record this as an observation about the word order: the indefiniteness leaves the scope open, and one instance of it is given in the next verse (*manṭiqu'ṭ-ṭayr*), but no definition is given.

**The section on David and Solomon was treated in detail in `038-sad.md`** (the horses, the prayer, the wind, the repetition of the word *awwāb* for three prophets) **and what was given to the two of them was set out in a table in `034-sebe.md` at 34/10-14** (the softening of iron, the flowing of copper, the wind, the labour of the jinn, and the death of Solomon). **I rely on these two files; I do not repeat the analyses there.**

**What is peculiar to al-Naml is this, and it is a countable difference:**

| Sūra | What is named first in the David–Solomon passage |
|---|---|
| Ṣād 38/17-40 | **The attribute of servanthood** — *niʿma'l-ʿabd, innahū awwāb* |
| Sabaʾ 34/10-13 | **Materials and resources** — iron, copper, wind |
| **al-Naml 27/15** | ***Knowledge*** — and after it, **praise** |

**Three sūras name the same two men through three different doors.** I record this as an observation.

### فَضَّلَنَا عَلَىٰ كَثِيرٍ — a qualification

**The content of the praise must be read carefully, and it carries two qualifications:**

1. ***ʿalā kathīrin*** — "above many", **not above all.**
2. ***min ʿibādihi'l-muʾminīn*** — the group above which they are set is **His believing servants.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence makes no claim of absolute superiority — **it is bounded on both sides.** And the comparison is drawn not with those who deny but **with the believers.**

**And the verb of the sentence must be recorded: *faḍḍala* — "He favoured", not a passive, and its agent is God.** **The superiority is thus named not as something attained but as something given.** Indeed the sentence opens with *al-ḥamdu li'llāh*: **the subject of the utterance is stated at the outset.**

### وَوَرِثَ سُلَيْمَٰنُ دَاوُۥدَ

The root **و-ر-ث**: to come after someone in his place, to take possession of what was his.

**What was inherited is not said in the verse**, and this has been debated in the classical commentaries:

| Reading | What *waritha* covers |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Prophethood and knowledge** — not property; that the passage opens with knowledge is taken as an indication |
| 2 | **Kingship and rule** — the government passing from father to son |
| 3 | **Both together** — the general sense of the word is left as it stands |

**I do not impose a preference.** **I record one indication, not a preference:** the chain of verses opened in the preceding verse with *ʿilman*, and in the next sentence Solomon speaks of something **taught** (*ʿullimnā*). **The context runs along the axis of knowledge; but the verse does not say so explicitly.**

### عُلِّمْنَا مَنطِقَ ٱلطَّيْرِ

The root **ن-ط-ق**: **to bring forth speech with the voice, to speak.** ***Manṭiq*** — speech, utterance; **and from this, in later centuries, it took on the sense of a method of thought (the science of logic).** **The root was treated in `053-necm.md` at 53/3 (*wa mā yanṭiqu ʿani'l-hawā*) and in `077-murselat.md`; in both places this verse was already cited.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/21 the same root occurred for the making of the limbs speak on the day of reckoning** (*anṭaqanā'llāhu'lladhī anṭaqa kulla shayʾ*). **In the last block of al-Naml the same root will turn the other way: at verse 85, *fa-hum lā yanṭiqūn* — "and they will not speak."**

**Within the sūra the root occurs twice, and the two directions are the reverse of one another:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | *ʿullimnā **manṭiqa**'ṭ-ṭayr* | **Solomon** — the speech of what is reckoned speechless is understood |
| 85 | *fa-hum lā **yanṭiqūn*** | **Those who deny** — the one who speaks falls silent |

**This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.** And I record this as my own reading: **the sūra opens speech as a capacity and closes it as a deprivation.**

**I draw a limit, and per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write it out plainly:** I derive no scientific claim about animal communication from the phrase *manṭiqu'ṭ-ṭayr*. **The verse reports a particular understanding given to Solomon; it does not say that it opens a general door to knowledge.** Indeed the verb is passive, and its agent is evident: *ʿullimnā* — "we were taught."

**وَأُوتِينَا مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ** — "we have been given of everything."

**The word order must be recorded: the particle *min* is there.** The sentence does not say *ūtīnā kulla shayʾ* ("we were given everything") — ***min kulli shayʾ*: "of everything [a share]."** **The giving thus conveys not the extent but the variety.**

**And the same phrase will be used once more in this sūra, at verse 23, of the queen of Sabaʾ: *wa ūtiyat min kulli shayʾ*.** **The same construction, once of a prophet and once of a queen.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I shall return to it there.

## 27/17 — وَحُشِرَ لِسُلَيْمَٰنَ جُنُودُهُۥ مِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ وَٱلْإِنسِ وَٱلطَّيْرِ فَهُمْ يُوزَعُونَ

*wa ḥushira li-Sulaymāna junūduhū mina'l-jinni wa'l-insi wa'ṭ-ṭayri fa-hum yūzaʿūn*

"**Solomon's armies of jinn and men and birds were gathered before him, and they were held in order.**"

### حُشِرَ — and the word's second place

The root **ح-ش-ر**: **to gather from a place and drive forward.** The lexicographers record the sense "to drive into a narrow place" — and indeed the Qurʾān uses this word mostly of the day of reckoning.

**And the sūra uses this word twice:**

| Verse | Expression | Who is gathered |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | *wa **ḥushira** li-Sulaymāna junūduh* | **The armies of Solomon** — in this world |
| 83 | *wa yawma **naḥshuru** min kulli ummatin fawjā* | **Those who deny** — on the day of reckoning |

**In both verses ح-ش-ر and و-ز-ع occur together.** That is, the clause *fa-hum yūzaʿūn* is the same in both places. **This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra and is fixed word for word** — it was set out in the table in the section on the spines above.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two words the two verses share:** the sūra describes the order of a prophet's army and the order of those gathered on the day of reckoning **with the same two verbs.** **The difference lies in who is gathered, and in the direction the gathering moves.**

### يُوزَعُونَ

The root **و-ز-ع** was worked out in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/15: **to take hold and steer, to rein in; to hold an army in order.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The lexicographers explain the verb thus: *wazaʿa'l-jaysha* — to halt those at the head of the army so that those behind can come up, and to keep the ranks from breaking apart.** **The word describes not the advance but the holding together of what advances.**

**I record this as an observation, and give it as preparation for the next verse:** verse 17 reports an order. **In verse 18 this ordered army will approach a valley, and an ant will say that it may crush something without noticing.** This transition between the two verses is the text's own sequence.

### مِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ وَٱلْإِنسِ وَٱلطَّيْرِ

**Three groups are enumerated, and their order is worth recording: jinn, men, birds.**

**The jinn's working for Solomon was treated in detail in `034-sebe.md` at 34/12-14**, and a qualification was entered there: **the verse ties this labour to a permission (*bi-idhni rabbih*) and to a command; it does not present it as an unbounded power of disposal.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the root analysis of the word *jinn* was carried out in `072-cin.md`** (ج-ن-ن — to cover, to conceal). **I do not repeat it here.**

**A limit, per the method (`STYLE.md`):** I build no claim, on the basis of this verse, about the nature of the jinn, their powers, or their relations with human beings today. **What the text reports is a capacity given to Solomon, and it is tied to him.**

## 27/18 — حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَتَوْا۟ عَلَىٰ وَادِ ٱلنَّمْلِ قَالَتْ نَمْلَةٌ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّمْلُ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ مَسَٰكِنَكُمْ لَا يَحْطِمَنَّكُمْ سُلَيْمَٰنُ وَجُنُودُهُۥ وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

*ḥattā idhā ataw ʿalā wādi'n-namli qālat namlatun yā ayyuha'n-namlu'dkhulū masākinakum lā yaḥṭimannakum Sulaymānu wa junūduhū wa hum lā yashʿurūn*

"**Until, when they came upon the valley of the ants, an ant said: 'O ants! Enter your dwellings, lest Solomon and his armies crush you without noticing.'**"

**This is the verse that gives the sūra its name.**

### First, a limit

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write this out plainly, and the limit is particularly needed at this verse:**

- **I do not load this verse with a scientific confirmation drawn from what is known today about the communication of ants.** Such a reading is the hunting for scientific miracles that `STYLE.md` forbids.
- **The text says nothing about how the ant spoke, what kind of sound it was, or by what means Solomon understood it.** I do not speculate about these gaps.
- **I do not enter into the storytellers' details that circulate about this scene — the ant's name, the location of the valley, the length of the speech. The Qurʾān does not supply them.**

**What the text reports is this, and I keep within it:** an ant spoke, what it said was reported, Solomon heard it and smiled.

### نَمْلَة / ٱلنَّمْل — a point of grammar

The root **ن-م-ل**. **In Arabic *naml* is a collective noun (*ism al-jins*): it names the species "ant" as a whole. *Namla* is its noun of unity (*ism al-waḥda*): a single ant.**

**Both are in the verse, and they do different work:**

| Word | Pattern | Its work |
|---|---|---|
| ***namlatun*** | **Noun of unity**, indefinite | **The speaker** — a single ant, unnamed |
| ***al-naml*** | **Collective noun**, definite | **The one addressed** — the whole community |
| ***wādi'n-naml*** | A construct | **The place** — the name of the valley |

**And the agreement of the verbs must be recorded: *qālat* — feminine singular; *udkhulū* — masculine plural imperative.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence builds an address from a single individual to a whole community out of the resources of grammar itself. **The speaker is one, those addressed are many — and the two words come from the same root.**

**Moreover *namlatun* comes indefinite: a single ant whose name, kind and standing are not stated.** I give this as a qualification: **the text itself leaves the speaker indefinite.**

### لَا يَحْطِمَنَّكُمْ — root ح-ط-م

**The root was worked out in detail in `104-humeze.md` and occurred again in `056-vakia.md` at 56/65.** The core there was this: **to break, to shatter, to crumble — to break something hard and whole into fragments.** *Ḥuṭām* — crumbs, crumbled remains; *al-Ḥuṭama* — one of the names of the Fire. **I do not repeat the analysis of the root; I rely on that treatment.**

**And this verse was already cited in `104-humeze.md`:** as the concrete use of the root, **being crushed underfoot and ground to powder.**

**What belongs here is the aptness of the word in this context, and I give it as my own reading:** the root describes **a whole thing turning into fragments.** **For an ant caught under the foot of an army there is no verb more exact** — because what happens is not killing but **the complete dissolution of a form.** And the object of the verb is the community itself: *yaḥṭimanna-kum.*

**Word order: the verb is reinforced with the heavy *nūn* of emphasis** — *lā yaḥṭiman-na*. **The possibility is thus named with the strongest form of emphasis.**

**The grammatical type of the clause has been debated in the classical commentaries, and the disagreement is not concealed:**

| Reading | What *lā yaḥṭimannakum* is | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A prohibition** — in wording, directed at Solomon and his army | The sense still comes back to the ants: "enter, so that they do not crush you" |
| 2 | **The answer to the imperative / a purpose clause** | "Enter, so that he does not crush you" |
| 3 | **An emphatic negation** — a strongly worded warning | "Otherwise they will crush you underfoot" |

**All three readings are transmitted and are close to one another in meaning; I do not impose a preference.**

### وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ — the knot of the verse

**The last part of the sentence is the most striking place in this verse, and it must be recorded as a datum of the text: this qualification stands inside the ant's own words.**

**The one who gives the warning, in the same sentence, also excuses the party it warns against.**

| Part of the sentence | What it does |
|---|---|
| *udkhulū masākinakum* | **Precaution** — a command to its own community |
| *lā yaḥṭimannakum Sulaymānu wa junūduh* | **The name of the danger** — where it comes from |
| ***wa hum lā yashʿurūn*** | **The excuse** — the note that there is no intent |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sentence's own order:** the ant's words are **not an accusation.** They report the danger, they command taking shelter from it, **and they do not charge the source of the danger with ill intent.** *Wa hum lā yashʿurūn* — "they do not notice."

**And this is what makes the reaction in the next verse intelligible:** Solomon smiles at these words. **Had the words been an accusation, the smile would be out of place.** I give this as a reading; its ground is the sequence of the two verses.

The root **ش-ع-ر**: **to sense the finest of differences.** The lexicographers connect the root with *shaʿr* (hair): **to notice something as fine as a hair.** *Shāʿir* — one who notices what the ordinary eye does not see. **The word differs from "to know": *ʿilm* conveys knowledge, *shuʿūr* conveys noticing.**

**The distinction works here:** the ant does not say "they do not know" — **it says "they do not notice."** **That an army on the march does not notice what lies under its feet is not a lack of knowledge but a matter of attention.**

**And the sūra will use this verb twice more:**

| Verse | Who does not notice | What |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | **Solomon and his armies** | What is beneath their feet |
| 50 | **The nine men of Thamūd** | That their own plot has turned back on them |
| 65 | **Everyone** | When they will be raised |

**Three occurrences, three scales: a footstep, a plan, a future.** I record this as my own reading; its ground is the three verses themselves.

### مَسَٰكِنَكُمْ

The root **س-ك-ن**: **to come to rest, the stilling of motion.** *Sakīna*, *maskan* and *sukūn* are from the same root. **The root was treated in `048-fetih.md` at 48/4.**

**And the choice of word is worth recording: the ant's nest is called a *maskan* — the word used for a human dwelling.** **A different word will be used of the houses of Thamūd at verse 52 of this sūra: *fa-tilka buyūtuhum khāwiyatan*.** **There the word is *bayt*; but the two scenes take up the same thing: the place where a community shelters.**

**I record this as an observation:** the ants are saved by entering their dwellings; **the houses of Thamūd are left empty.** The two scenes stand in two different blocks of the sūra, and I do not claim that a contrast is being drawn — **what I record is that the idea of "shelter" occurs in two places in the sūra with two different outcomes.**

### What this says to the present

**What the verse says concretely is this, and it needs no generalising: a power can do harm without noticing.**

**The ant's words do two things at once, and both are practical:**

1. **They name the danger** — they do not conceal where it comes from.
2. **They ascribe no intent** — *wa hum lā yashʿurūn.*

**Holding these two together is a hard thing in everyday life:** the party that suffers harm usually either shrinks from naming the danger or, in naming it, ascribes intent. **The verse records a third posture.** I give this as my own reading; its ground is that both parts of the sentence stand within the same utterance.

## 27/19 — فَتَبَسَّمَ ضَاحِكًا مِّن قَوْلِهَا وَقَالَ رَبِّ أَوْزِعْنِىٓ أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَىَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَىَّ وَأَنْ أَعْمَلَ صَٰلِحًا تَرْضَىٰهُ وَأَدْخِلْنِى بِرَحْمَتِكَ فِى عِبَادِكَ ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ

*fa-tabassama ḍāḥikan min qawlihā wa qāla rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka'llatī anʿamta ʿalayya wa ʿalā wālidayya wa an aʿmala ṣāliḥan tarḍāhu wa adkhilnī bi-raḥmatika fī ʿibādika'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*

"**He smiled at its words and said: 'My Lord! Dispose me to give thanks for the favour You have bestowed on me and on my parents, and to do good work that pleases You; and admit me, by Your mercy, among Your righteous servants.'**"

### فَتَبَسَّمَ ضَاحِكًا

The root **ب-س-م**, in Form V: ***tabassum* — the slight parting of the lips; a silent smile.** The root **ض-ح-ك**: ***ḍaḥk* — to laugh**, a laughter that may be audible.

**The two words stand in the same sentence, and the lexicographers dwell on the combination:**

| Reading | What *tabassama ḍāḥikan* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A smile that reaches laughter** — *ḍāḥikan*, as a circumstantial qualifier, states the degree of the smile |
| 2 | **Emphasis** — the two words reinforce the same act |
| 3 | **A statement of limit** — holding the laughter within a smile; showing that it did not become a burst of laughter |

**All three explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record an observation about the word order:** the main verb of the sentence is *tabassama*; *ḍāḥikan* is a **circumstantial qualifier**, a statement of state attached to the main verb. **Grammatically, the smile is what is primary.**

**And the cause of the smile is stated plainly in the verse: *min qawlihā* — "at its words."** **The cause is therefore not the ant itself, nor the sight of it, but what it said.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the phrase *min qawlihā*:** what makes Solomon smile is **an utterance that was understood.** The capacity given in verse 16 (*ʿullimnā manṭiqa'ṭ-ṭayr*) produces a result here — and the result it produces is not a conquest or a superiority but **a smile.**

### The content of the prayer — and its exact overlap with al-Aḥqāf

**The first sentence of this prayer is exactly the same as 46/15 in `046-ahkaf.md`:** *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka'llatī anʿamta ʿalayya wa ʿalā wālidayya wa an aʿmala ṣāliḥan tarḍāh.*

**The two verses can be set side by side, and the difference is worth recording:**

| | al-Aḥqāf 46/15 | **al-Naml 27/19** |
|---|---|---|
| Who speaks | **A man reaching forty** — a general portrait | **Solomon** — a prophet and a king |
| The occasion | **Coming to the age of full maturity** | **Hearing what an ant said** |
| The shared sentence | *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura… wa an aʿmala ṣāliḥan tarḍāh* | **The same** |
| What follows | *wa **aṣliḥ lī fī dhurriyyatī*** — for his offspring | *wa **adkhilnī bi-raḥmatika fī ʿibādika'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*** — for himself |

**The shared sentence overlaps word for word; the difference lies at the end.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**The finding recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` holds here too, and I do not repeat it:** the root of *awziʿnī* is **و-ز-ع** — to take hold and steer, to make one incline towards something and turn him to it; **and the content of the prayer was worth recording: the man asks neither for wealth nor for long life nor for success — he asks to be able to give thanks.**

**Two things belong to al-Naml, and both are recorded as my own reading.**

**First:** in al-Aḥqāf the prayer comes after **the fortieth year**, that is, after a threshold in a lifetime. **In al-Naml it comes after what an ant said.** **The same prayer is thus spoken once at the greatest turning point of a life and once at the smallest of incidents.** The ground is the context of the two verses.

**Second, and more decisive: the verb *awziʿnī* is from the same root as the *yūzaʿūn* of verse 17 of this sūra.**

| Verse | Word | Whose order |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | *yūzaʿūn* | **The army** — the order outside |
| 19 | *awziʿnī* | **His own self** — the order within |

**Between the two verses stands a single verse (18), and that verse is the ant's speech.** **The sūra thus places an ant's sentence between the outer order and the inner one.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root of the three verses:** the same root is used first for the power that holds an army in its ranks, then for what a man asks for within himself. **And the request is this: "turn me towards thankfulness."** The man who can hold his army in order acknowledges that he cannot himself order the inclination within him.

### The four items of the prayer

| No. | Expression | What is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *an ashkura niʿmatak* | **To be able to give thanks** — a posture towards what is given |
| 2 | *allatī anʿamta ʿalayya **wa ʿalā wālidayya*** | **The extent of the favour** — himself and his parents |
| 3 | *wa an aʿmala ṣāliḥan **tarḍāh*** | **The measure of the deed** — not his own approval but God's good pleasure |
| 4 | *wa adkhilnī **bi-raḥmatika** fī ʿibādika'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn* | **The outcome** — and its means: mercy, not the deed |

**The *bi-raḥmatika* of the fourth item is worth recording, and I give this as an observation about the word order:** in the third item a good deed is asked for; in the fourth, admission is asked for not as the return for that deed but **by mercy**. **The prayer thus does not count its own third item as sufficient.**

**And the last word is *al-ṣāliḥīn* — the same root as the *ṣāliḥan* of the third item (ص-ل-ح).** **The deed is *ṣāliḥ*; the company to be entered is *ṣāliḥīn*.** This is a repetition of a root that can be verified within the verse.

**And the choice of the word *ʿibād* (servants) must be recorded:** a king asks to be admitted **not among his own subjects but among God's servants.** **I give this as my own reading; its ground is the construct of the word** (*ʿibādika* — "Your servants").

## 27/20-21 — وَتَفَقَّدَ ٱلطَّيْرَ فَقَالَ مَا لِىَ لَآ أَرَى ٱلْهُدْهُدَ أَمْ كَانَ مِنَ ٱلْغَآئِبِينَ · لَأُعَذِّبَنَّهُۥ عَذَابًا شَدِيدًا أَوْ لَأَا۟ذْبَحَنَّهُۥٓ أَوْ لَيَأْتِيَنِّى بِسُلْطَٰنٍ مُّبِينٍ

*wa tafaqqada'ṭ-ṭayra fa-qāla mā liya lā ara'l-hudhuda am kāna mina'l-ghāʾibīn · la-uʿadhdhibannahū ʿadhāban shadīdan aw la-adhbaḥannahū aw la-yaʾtiyannī bi-sulṭānin mubīn*

"**He inspected the birds and said: 'How is it with me that I do not see the hoopoe? Or is he among the absent? I shall punish him severely, or I shall slaughter him — or he shall bring me a clear warrant.'**"

### تَفَقَّدَ — root ف-ق-د

**The concrete sense of the root: *faqd* — to lose something, to be deprived of it.** **The Form V *tafaqqud* is this, and the lexicographers record it explicitly: to check whether anything is missing, to go over one by one.**

**The verb thus conveys not the noticing of a loss but the searching for one.**

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the pattern:** Solomon does not see by chance that a bird is missing — **he holds an inspection.** **And this is the practical counterpart of the note in the preceding block, *yūzaʿūn* (they were held in order):** the order is maintained by inspection.

### مَا لِىَ لَآ أَرَى ٱلْهُدْهُدَ — a point about word order

**The construction of the sentence is striking, and it is a datum of the text: Solomon does not say "Where is the hoopoe?"**

***Mā liya lā ara'l-hudhud*** — word for word: **"How is it with me that I do not see the hoopoe?"**

**The question thus interrogates his own sight first.** *Mā liya* — "how is it with me." **And only after that does the second possibility come:** *am kāna mina'l-ghāʾibīn* — "or is he really absent?"

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two clauses:** the one who notices the absence **doubts his own eye first**, and only then names the other party's absence as a possibility. **In an inspection this order is a step that comes before a verdict.**

### A sentence with three alternatives

**Verse 21 offers three alternatives, and all three are reinforced with the *nūn* of emphasis:**

| Alternative | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *la-uʿadhdhibannahū ʿadhāban shadīdā* | **A heavy punishment** |
| 2 | *aw la-adhbaḥannahū* | **Slaughter** |
| 3 | ***aw la-yaʾtiyannī bi-sulṭānin mubīn*** | **That he bring a clear warrant** |

**The third alternative is the one that will annul the first two, and this is what must be recorded: a ruler leaving the door of evidence open before he punishes.**

**سُلْطَان** — root **س-ل-ط**: **to prevail, to have one's judgement carry.** In the Qurʾān *sulṭān* occurs both in the sense of "authority, dominion" and in the sense of **"an argument that silences the other side"**. **The second sense governs here, and it is reinforced by the adjective *mubīn* (clear).**

### A comparison with al-Aḥqāf 46/4 — two places where evidence is demanded

**The demand for evidence was treated in detail in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/4**, and what was recorded there was this: **the demand asks for two kinds of evidence — observation and transmission — and it is set at the lowest possible threshold** (*aw athāratin min ʿilm* — even a remnant of knowledge).

**The two demands can be set side by side, and the difference lies in who makes the demand:**

| | al-Aḥqāf 46/4 | **al-Naml 27/21** |
|---|---|---|
| Who demands | **A messenger** — of those he addresses | **A king** — of one under his command |
| The wording | *iʾtūnī bi-kitābin… aw athāratin min ʿilm* | *la-yaʾtiyannī bi-sulṭānin mubīn* |
| The matter | **A claim about belief** | **A breach of duty** |
| The direction of the demand | **From below** — from the party that holds no power | **From above** — from the party that holds the authority to punish |
| In common | **That no verdict be given without evidence** | **The same** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verb the two verses share (*iʾtūnī* / *yaʾtiyannī*):** the same demand issues once from a messenger who holds no power and once from a king who holds the authority to punish. **In the second case the demand weighs more** — because the party who might have dispensed with evidence is already in a position to carry out the punishment.

**And in `028-kasas.md` at 28/75 all the links of this demand across the series were set out in a table** (*hātū burhānakum*, *arūnī*, *bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum*). **Verse 64 of this sūra will enter the same chain: *qul hātū burhānakum in kuntum ṣādiqīn*.** **The sūra thus directs the demand for evidence first to a bird and then to all whom it addresses.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

### هُدْهُد

**The *hudhud* is the bird called the hoopoe in English.** The lexicographers record that its name comes from its call — that it is a name formed in imitation of a sound.

**The Qurʾān says nothing else about this bird**, and I **do not enter** into the details transmitted in the classical commentaries — an ability to find water, its sex, its lifespan. What the text gives is only what it did: it brought news from a place, it carried a letter.

## 27/22-23 — فَمَكَثَ غَيْرَ بَعِيدٍ فَقَالَ أَحَطتُ بِمَا لَمْ تُحِطْ بِهِۦ وَجِئْتُكَ مِن سَبَإٍۭ بِنَبَإٍ يَقِينٍ · إِنِّى وَجَدتُّ ٱمْرَأَةً تَمْلِكُهُمْ وَأُوتِيَتْ مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ وَلَهَا عَرْشٌ عَظِيمٌ

*fa-makatha ghayra baʿīdin fa-qāla aḥaṭtu bimā lam tuḥiṭ bihī wa jiʾtuka min Sabaʾin bi-nabaʾin yaqīn · innī wajadtu'mraʾatan tamlikuhum wa ūtiyat min kulli shayʾin wa lahā ʿarshun ʿaẓīm*

"**He did not stay away long, and said: 'I have encompassed what you have not encompassed, and I bring you from Sabaʾ a report that is certain. I found a woman ruling over them; she has been given of everything, and she has a mighty throne.'**"

### فَمَكَثَ غَيْرَ بَعِيدٍ

The root **م-ك-ث**: **to stay in a place, to wait.** **The root was treated in `043-zuhruf.md` at 43/77**, and its occurrences in the Qurʾān were recorded there — this verse was cited there as well. **I do not repeat it here.**

***Ghayra baʿīdin* — "for no long [while]."** **An observation about the word order:** the duration is given not with a number but **with a negation**. **What is reported is thus not how long it took but that it did not take long.**

### أَحَطتُ بِمَا لَمْ تُحِطْ بِهِۦ — the sharpest sentence in the sūra

The root **ح-و-ط** was worked out in `002-bakara.md` at 2/255, and the finding there was this: **to go round a thing, to encompass it;** *ḥāʾiṭ* — a wall. **And the distinction recorded there is decisive here: what is denied is not *knowing* but *encompassing*.** *Wa lā yuḥīṭūna bi-shayʾin min ʿilmihī* — a person can know; he cannot go round a thing and take in the whole of it. **I do not repeat the analysis of the root; I rely on that treatment.**

**And in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/54 the same root occurred from the other side:** *innahū bi-kulli shayʾin muḥīṭ* — He is the one who encompasses all things.

**I now set the uses in this sūra alongside them:**

| Place | Expression | Who encompasses |
|---|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/255 | *wa lā **yuḥīṭūna** bi-shayʾin min ʿilmih* | **Man cannot encompass** — God's knowledge |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/54 | *bi-kulli shayʾin **muḥīṭ*** | **God** — all things |
| **al-Naml 27/22** | ***aḥaṭtu** bimā lam **tuḥiṭ** bih* | **The bird** — what the king could not encompass |
| **al-Naml 27/84** | *wa lam **tuḥīṭū** bihā ʿilmā* | **Those who deny did not encompass** — the signs |

**Four occurrences; and al-Naml's two stand at the two ends of the sūra.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

**The point in verse 22 must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:**

**The one who frames the sentence is a bird, speaking to a king who inspects the birds of his army one by one. And what it says is that the king's knowledge has a limit.**

**Three details reinforce this, and all three are data of the text:**

1. **The verb is the verb of encompassing, not of knowing.** The bird does not say "I have known what you do not know" — **it says "I have encompassed what you could not encompass."** The limit drawn in al-Baqara is applied here to a difference of scale between created beings.
2. **The sentence comes immediately after verse 16.** There Solomon had said *ūtīnā min kulli shayʾ* ("we have been given of everything"). **Verse 22 shows the limit of that sentence.**
3. **And the *knowledge* given in verse 15 meets a deficiency here.** The narrative that the sūra opened with knowledge goes on with the limit of knowledge.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra does not distribute knowledge along a scale of rank. **The smallest, the one reckoned lowest, brings news from a place the greatest could not reach.** And this prepares the ruling at verse 65 of the sūra: **only God knows the unseen** — the knowledge of everyone else is completed by someone else's.

### مِن سَبَإٍۭ بِنَبَإٍ يَقِينٍ

**The weave of sound is worth recording, and it is a datum of the text: *Sabaʾ* and *nabaʾ* — two words on the same pattern and rhyming.**

**سَبَإ** — the name of a people who lived in southern Arabia (in the region of present-day Yemen). **It was treated in `034-sebe.md` at 34/15**, and it was recorded there: **it occurs in two places in the Qurʾān — there and here;** it is reported that the word is transmitted both as the name of a person and as the name of a tribe, and **no definite ruling was issued.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**نَبَأ** — root **ن-ب-أ**: **news that matters, news with a consequence.** The lexicographers distinguish the word from *khabar*: every *nabaʾ* is news, but not every piece of news is a *nabaʾ* — **because a *nabaʾ* is news that removes ignorance and produces a great result.**

**And its qualifier must be recorded: *yaqīn*.** **The root ي-ق-ن — the same as the *yūqinūn* of the sūra's third verse and the *istayqanat* of verse 14.** **The news the bird brings is thus qualified with the very word named at the sūra's opening as an attribute of the believers.**

**And Moses's sentence at verse 7 should be recalled: *sa-ātīkum minhā bi-khabarin*.** **The bringing of news is spoken of twice in the sūra:**

| Verse | Who | What he will bring |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Moses** | *bi-**khabarin*** — indefinite |
| 22 | **The hoopoe** | *bi-**nabaʾin yaqīn*** — great news, and certain |

**This is an overlap that can be verified within the sūra.** I record it as an observation.

### ٱمْرَأَةً تَمْلِكُهُمْ

**The report is summed up in three items, and their order is worth recording:**

| No. | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *imraʾatan **tamlikuhum*** | **The ruler** — a woman |
| 2 | *wa **ūtiyat min kulli shayʾ*** | **Her means** |
| 3 | *wa lahā **ʿarshun ʿaẓīm*** | **The throne** — the emblem of sovereignty |

**The second item is exactly the sentence of verse 16: *ūtīnā min kulli shayʾ*.** **The same construction, once for Solomon and once for the queen.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as an observation:** the report is given in language that does not belittle the other side. **The bird uses for the other party the very expression used of its own king.**

**The Qurʾān does not give the name of the woman who ruled.** A name is transmitted in the classical sources; **since it does not occur in the Qurʾān it is not used in this commentary.** **In the same way, the reports about the dates, the extent and the end of her reign are not entered into.**

**عَرْش** — root **ع-ر-ش**: **to raise up, to set up an arbour.** In Arabic it covers the throne, the arbour, the raised and roofed structure, and it is used as the emblem of sovereignty. **The root was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/4**, and this verse was already cited there. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the word will occur five more times in this sūra: 23, 26, 38, 41, 42.** **One of these (26) is for God; four are for the queen's throne.** It will be set out in a table in the next section.

## 27/24-26 — وَجَدتُّهَا وَقَوْمَهَا يَسْجُدُونَ لِلشَّمْسِ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ وَزَيَّنَ لَهُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ فَصَدَّهُمْ عَنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ فَهُمْ لَا يَهْتَدُونَ · أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى يُخْرِجُ ٱلْخَبْءَ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا تُخْفُونَ وَمَا تُعْلِنُونَ · ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ رَبُّ ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ

*wajadtuhā wa qawmahā yasjudūna li'sh-shamsi min dūni'llāhi wa zayyana lahumu'sh-shayṭānu aʿmālahum fa-ṣaddahum ʿani's-sabīli fa-hum lā yahtadūn · allā yasjudū li'llāhi'lladhī yukhriju'l-khabʾa fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa yaʿlamu mā tukhfūna wa mā tuʿlinūn · Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa rabbu'l-ʿarshi'l-ʿaẓīm*

"**I found her and her people prostrating to the sun instead of to God. Satan had made their deeds seem fair to them and had turned them from the way, so that they are not guided. That they should not prostrate to God, who brings out what is hidden in the heavens and the earth, and who knows what you conceal and what you make known… God — there is no god but He, the Lord of the mighty throne.**"

### A variant reading — and it changes the meaning

**There is a variant reading on the first word of verse 25, and since it bears directly on the meaning it must be recorded, per the method (`STYLE.md`):**

| Reading | How it is written | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| ***allā yasjudū*** | أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا | "…**so that they should not** prostrate" — it attaches to the preceding verse: Satan turned them away so that they should not prostrate |
| ***alā yā'sjudū*** | أَلَا يَا اسْجُدُوا | *alā* (behold!) + the vocative particle + the imperative: "**Behold! Prostrate**" — a direct imperative sentence |

**Both readings are transmitted by the imams of the variant readings; I do not impose a preference.**

**The difference changes whose sentence it is:** on the first reading the sentence is still a continuation of the hoopoe's report; **on the second it is a direct call breaking in.** **Both have been defended in the classical commentaries.**

**I record an observation, not a preference:** on the second reading the sentence is an imperative, and this agrees with the prostration of recitation at the end of verse 26. **This is an indication, not a proof.**

### A note on the prostration of recitation

**There is agreement that this sūra contains a prostration of recitation. As to its exact place, differing views are transmitted in the classical works of law:** on one view it falls at the end of verse 25, on another at the end of verse 26 (*rabbu'l-ʿarshi'l-ʿaẓīm*).

**I confine myself to transmitting the disagreement; I issue no legal ruling.** **Per the method (`STYLE.md`), the views of the schools of law are transmitted and no ruling is issued.**

### ٱلْخَبْء — root خ-ب-أ

**The concrete sense of the root: *khabʾ* — a thing hidden, a thing stored away.** *Khabaʾa* — he hid it. From the same root, *khabīʾa* — the thing that is hidden.

**And the verb of the sentence must be recorded: *yukhriju'l-khabʾ* — "who brings out what is hidden."** **The description is thus not of one who *knows* what is hidden but of one who *brings it out*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** to know is a state, to bring out is an act. **The sentence frames the relation to what is hidden not as a passivity but as an action.** Indeed the verb of knowing follows immediately, separately: *wa yaʿlamu mā tukhfūna wa mā tuʿlinūn.*

**The two clauses stand side by side, and each does a different work:**

| Clause | Verb | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| *yukhriju'l-khabʾa fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* | **Bringing out** | **The universe** — what lies hidden in the heavens and the earth |
| *wa yaʿlamu mā tukhfūna wa mā tuʿlinūn* | **Knowing** | **The human being** — what he conceals and what he declares |

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the two clauses name separately what is hidden outside and what is hidden within. **And both are spoken by a bird** — that is, the sūra puts the two most comprehensive statements into the mouth of the smallest character in the narrative.

**And the place of the word *khabʾ* within the sūra is worth recording:** the hoopoe had brought news from a place Solomon **had not seen** (22). **Now it names God as the one who brings out what is hidden.** This is a fitness between verses that can be verified; it is not a claim about intent.

### وَزَيَّنَ لَهُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ

**At verse 4 the same verb occurred as *zayyannā* ("We made fair"); here the agent is Satan.** **That the two usages do not contradict each other, and that this was treated in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/14, was recorded above.**

**The chain in this verse must be recorded, and it has three links:**

| Link | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *zayyana lahum… aʿmālahum* | **The adorning** — what they do comes to look fair |
| 2 | *fa-ṣaddahum ʿani's-sabīl* | **The turning away** — being diverted from the way |
| 3 | *fa-hum lā yahtadūn* | **The result** — not finding the way |

**The two *fāʾ* particles (fa-ṣaddahum, fa-hum) convey consequence.** **The chain is thus built as cause and effect, and the first link is a corruption of sight, not a lack of knowledge.**

**And the same verb (ص-د-د) will occur once more in this narrative, at verse 43: *wa ṣaddahā mā kānat taʿbudu min dūni'llāh*.** **The same root, once for the people and once for the queen.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the narrative.

### يَسْجُدُونَ لِلشَّمْسِ

**The verse names the people's act: prostration to the sun.** **And the qualification matters: *min dūni'llāh* — "instead of God."**

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I draw a limit:** this verse describes a people who worshipped the sun in history. **I issue no blanket verdict about an ethnic or geographical group;** what the verse describes is **a conduct**, and whoever does that conduct falls under it.

The root **س-ج-د** was treated in `053-necm.md` and in `084-insikak.md`: **to bow, to fall to the ground; the plainest bodily form of submission.** **I do not repeat it here.**

### رَبُّ ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ — the structural knot of the narrative

**The last construct of verse 26 meets the last construct of verse 23 word for word. This is a repetition that can be verified from the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Whose |
|---|---|---|
| **23** | *wa lahā **ʿarshun ʿaẓīm*** | **The queen** — she has a mighty throne |
| **26** | *rabbu'l-**ʿarshi'l-ʿaẓīm*** | **God** — the Lord of the mighty throne |

**The same two words, three verses apart, of two different owners.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the exact overlap of the two constructs:** the bird that brings the report first describes the queen's throne as *ʿarshun ʿaẓīm*; **three verses later it uses the same description of God**, and between them stands the sentence *lā ilāha illā huwa*. **The comparison is thus made not by calling the throne small, but by returning the same description to its true owner.**

**And at the end of the narrative the throne will come on stage once more** (38-42): **it will be brought, it will be made unrecognisable, and before it the queen will declare her submission.** **The sūra's line about the throne thus begins here and ends at verse 42.**

| Verse | Where the throne is |
|---|---|
| 23 | **In Sabaʾ** — named in a report |
| 26 | **God's throne** — the true owner |
| 38 | **Its bringing is asked for** |
| 41 | **It is made unrecognisable** |
| 42 | **It is recognised, and submission is declared** |

**This sequence is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.**

## 27/27-28 — قَالَ سَنَنظُرُ أَصَدَقْتَ أَمْ كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ · ٱذْهَب بِّكِتَٰبِى هَٰذَا فَأَلْقِهْ إِلَيْهِمْ ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ فَٱنظُرْ مَاذَا يَرْجِعُونَ

*qāla sa-nanẓuru aṣadaqta am kunta mina'l-kādhibīn · idhhab bi-kitābī hādhā fa-alqih ilayhim thumma tawalla ʿanhum fa'nẓur mādhā yarjiʿūn*

"**He said: 'We shall look: have you told the truth, or are you one of the liars? Take this letter of mine, deliver it to them, then withdraw from them and see what answer they return.'**"

### سَنَنظُرُ — the deferral of judgement

**Solomon's answer is not a verdict but a decision to look into the matter: *sa-nanẓuru* — "we shall look", the future formed with *sīn*.**

**And the two alternatives are given asymmetrically:** *aṣadaqta* ("have you told the truth") — **a verb**; *am kunta mina'l-kādhibīn* ("or are you one of the liars") — **a nominal sentence.**

**This asymmetry is recorded in the classical commentaries as well, and I give it as an observation about the word order:** the first alternative asks about **an act**, the second about **an attribute**. **It does not say "did you lie" but "are you one of the liars".** **Telling the truth is a deed, whereas lying is a class** — and the sentence does not hold the two at the same weight.

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the grammatical difference between the two alternatives.**

### And the verb enters the spine of the sūra: ن-ظ-ر

**The sūra uses this root again and again, and each time places it before a decision:**

| Verse | Expression | Who looks |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | *fa'**nẓur** kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-mufsidīn* | **The reader** — at history |
| 27 | *sa-**nanẓuru** aṣadaqta* | **Solomon** — at the report |
| 28 | *fa'**nẓur** mādhā yarjiʿūn* | **The hoopoe** — at the answer |
| 33 | *fa'**nẓurī** mādhā taʾmurīn* | **The queen** — at her own decision |
| 35 | *fa-**nāẓiratun** bima yarjiʿu'l-mursalūn* | **The queen** — at what the envoys bring back |
| 41 | *na**nẓur** a-tahtadī* | **Solomon** — at the queen's response |
| 51 | *fa'**nẓur** kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu makrihim* | **The reader** — at history |
| 69 | *fa'**nẓurū** kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-mujrimīn* | **Those addressed** — at history |

**Eight occurrences.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra makes looking before judging into a method — **and it repeats this both inside the narrative (to its characters) and outside it (to the reader).** **And what is striking is this: in verses 14, 51 and 69 what is looked at is the same — the *ʿāqiba*, the outcome.**

### فَأَلْقِهْ إِلَيْهِمْ ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ

**The command has four steps, and their order is worth recording:**

| No. | Command | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *idhhab bi-kitābī hādhā* | **Carry it** |
| 2 | *fa-alqih ilayhim* | **Deliver it** — hand it over |
| 3 | *thumma tawalla ʿanhum* | **Withdraw** — remove yourself from the scene |
| 4 | *fa'nẓur mādhā yarjiʿūn* | **Look** — watch for the answer |

**The third step is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading: the envoy delivers the letter and *withdraws*.**

**The letter is thus not read in the presence of the one who carried it.** **The other side decides in a setting that is not under pressure** — and indeed at verse 32 the queen will assemble her own council and take counsel.

**The ground is the order of the two commands (*alqih*, then *tawalla*), and the fact that its consequence appears at verse 32.**

**ثُمَّ** — this particle must be recorded: not *fāʾ* (immediately after) but ***thumma* (after an interval).** **The withdrawal is thus not joined to the delivery in one and the same moment.**

## 27/29-31 — قَالَتْ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْمَلَؤُا۟ إِنِّىٓ أُلْقِىَ إِلَىَّ كِتَٰبٌ كَرِيمٌ · إِنَّهُۥ مِن سُلَيْمَٰنَ وَإِنَّهُۥ بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ · أَلَّا تَعْلُوا۟ عَلَىَّ وَأْتُونِى مُسْلِمِينَ

*qālat yā ayyuha'l-malaʾu innī ulqiya ilayya kitābun karīm · innahū min Sulaymāna wa innahū bismi'llāhi'r-Raḥmāni'r-Raḥīm · allā taʿlū ʿalayya wa'tūnī muslimīn*

"**She said: 'O chiefs! A noble letter has been delivered to me. It is from Solomon, and it runs: In the name of God, al-Raḥmān, al-Raḥīm. Do not exalt yourselves against me, and come to me in submission.'**"

### كِتَٰبٌ كَرِيمٌ

The root **ك-ر-م** was worked out in `096-alak.md` at 96/3: **to give abundantly, expecting no return and keeping no count;** the one who is *karīm* does not throw his giving in your face. **And the second branch of the word: to be precious, noble, held in honour.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**Different explanations of what the word means here are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | What *karīm* qualifies |
|---|---|
| 1 | **That it was sealed and closed** — that it had not been read before being opened |
| 2 | **Its content** — that what it says is of worth |
| 3 | **Its sender** — that it comes from a person of standing |
| 4 | **Its opening** — that it begins with the basmala |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**I record a datum of the text:** the queen calls the letter *karīm* before she has conveyed its content at all. **The judgement is thus given before the content is read out** — the order is in the verse itself (*innī ulqiya ilayya kitābun karīm*, then *innahū min Sulaymān*).

### بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ — as a sentence inside the Qurʾān

**It was recorded in `001-fatiha.md`, and I rely on that treatment:** apart from its place at the heads of sūras, the basmala **also occurs as a sentence inside the Qurʾān**, and this is one of the two places (the other is Noah's setting the ark afloat at Hūd 11/41). **The formula appears here as the opening of a piece of state correspondence.**

**And the length of the letter is worth recording: a basmala and two sentences.**

| Part | Expression | Its function |
|---|---|---|
| The heading | *bismi'llāhi'r-Raḥmāni'r-Raḥīm* | **The opening** |
| The first demand | *allā taʿlū ʿalayya* | **A prohibition** — do not exalt yourselves |
| The second demand | *wa'tūnī muslimīn* | **A command** — come in submission |

**I record this as an observation:** the letter contains neither a threat, nor a promise, nor a justification. **A single prohibition and a single command.**

### أَلَّا تَعْلُوا۟ عَلَىَّ — and the sūra's ring of *ʿuluww* closes

***Taʿlū* is from the root ع-ل-و.** **This root occurred at verse 14 as the cause of the denial of Pharaoh's people:** *ẓulman wa ʿuluwwā.*

**The two occurrences within the sūra can be set side by side:**

| Verse | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | *ẓulman wa **ʿuluwwā*** | **A diagnosis** — as the cause of the denial |
| **31** | *allā **taʿlū** ʿalayya* | **A prohibition** — as the first item of a letter |

**And as was recorded in `028-kasas.md`, the same root framed that sūra** (28/4 and 28/83). **Al-Naml uses the same root twice, once as a diagnosis and once as a prohibition.** This is a weave between the two sūras that can be verified.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the function of the two verses:** al-Qaṣaṣ set *ʿuluww* down as the cause of an outcome (destruction); **al-Naml sets the same word down as the first condition of an invitation.** **In one place a diagnosis, in the other a precaution.**

### وَأْتُونِى مُسْلِمِينَ

The root **س-ل-م**: **to be sound, to be without flaw; and to hand over.** ***Muslim*** — one who submits.

**That the word occurs four times in this narrative must be recorded, and it is a datum of the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | *wa'tūnī **muslimīn*** | **The letter's demand** |
| 38 | *qabla an yaʾtūnī **muslimīn*** | **Solomon's qualification** — before they come in submission |
| 42 | *wa kunnā **muslimīn*** | **The queen** — beforehand |
| 44 | *wa **aslamtu** maʿa Sulaymāna li'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn* | **The queen** — the last sentence of the narrative |

**Four occurrences, and the narrative ends with this root.** This is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.

**And the qualification in the last occurrence is worth recording, and will be treated separately at verse 44: the queen says that she has submitted not *to Solomon* but *with Solomon, to God*.** **The demand of the letter at verse 31 and the answer at verse 44 thus do not coincide exactly — the answer corrects the addressee of the demand.**

## 27/32-33 — قَالَتْ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْمَلَؤُا۟ أَفْتُونِى فِىٓ أَمْرِى مَا كُنتُ قَاطِعَةً أَمْرًا حَتَّىٰ تَشْهَدُونِ · قَالُوا۟ نَحْنُ أُو۟لُوا۟ قُوَّةٍ وَأُو۟لُوا۟ بَأْسٍ شَدِيدٍ وَٱلْأَمْرُ إِلَيْكِ فَٱنظُرِى مَاذَا تَأْمُرِينَ

*qālat yā ayyuha'l-malaʾu aftūnī fī amrī mā kuntu qāṭiʿatan amran ḥattā tashhadūn · qālū naḥnu ulū quwwatin wa ulū baʾsin shadīdin wa'l-amru ilayki fa'nẓurī mādhā taʾmurīn*

"**She said: 'O chiefs! Give me your opinion in this matter of mine. I have never settled a matter unless you were present.' They said: 'We are possessors of strength, and possessors of great might in war. But the decision is yours; consider what you will command.'**"

### A comparison with al-Shūrā

***Shūrā* was worked out in detail in `042-sura.md` at 42/38, and the root analysis given there applies here. I do not repeat it; I summarise:** the concrete sense of the root **ش-و-ر** is ***shāra'l-ʿasal* — to draw honey out of the hive**; and all the branches of the root (*ashāra* — to point, *shawār* — goods set out for display) carry the same core: **to bring something out from where it lies and set it in view.** **An opinion is inside people; until it is drawn out, it is not there.**

**And the measure recorded there was this:** *wa amruhum shūrā baynahum* is **a nominal sentence**; the verse does not describe a form of government, it states **an attribute of a community** — and the measure lies in the word *amruhum*: **whose affair is the affair reckoned to be?**

**I now set the two texts side by side. The comparison can be verified from the text:**

| | **al-Shūrā 42/38** | **al-Naml 27/32** |
|---|---|---|
| Kind | **An attribute** — the state of a community | **A scene** — a single event |
| Form | **A nominal sentence** — *amruhum shūrā baynahum* | **A verbal sentence** — *aftūnī… mā kuntu qāṭiʿatan* |
| Who | **The believers** — plural, unnamed | **A ruler** — singular, taking counsel |
| Subject | **General** — "their affairs" | **A single matter** — *fī amrī* |
| Whether it binds | Not stated | **Stated** — *mā kuntu qāṭiʿatan amran ḥattā tashhadūn* |

**And the difference here is recorded as my own reading, its ground being the last row of the table above:** in al-Shūrā consultation is reported as **a state**; **in al-Naml a ruler sets out, in her own words, the procedure of her own decision.** **One is a description, the other an instance of practice.**

**And what is striking is this: the instance comes not from a community of believers but from the ruler of a people who are reported, at verse 24 of the sūra, to prostrate to the sun.** **I record this as a datum of the text and derive no value judgement from it** — what I record is the scene's place in the text.

### أَفْتُونِى — root ف-ت-و

**The concrete sense of the root: *fatā* — a youth, a young man; one in the freshness of his strength.** **From this, *iftāʾ*: to give an opinion in a matter, to open up what is closed and make it fresh.** The word *fatwā* is from this root.

**The lexicographers make the connection thus, and I transmit it as a report:** an opinion given on a matter is named with this root **because it brings to life what was closed and stagnant.**

**And the pattern of the verb must be recorded: *aftūnī* — an imperative, Form IV, and those addressed are plural.** **The request is thus made not of a single counsellor but of a council.**

### مَا كُنتُ قَاطِعَةً أَمْرًا حَتَّىٰ تَشْهَدُونِ

**Three details of the sentence are worth recording.**

**First — the tense of the verb. *Mā kuntu qāṭiʿatan* — a nominal predicate built on the past tense.** **It does not say "I shall not decide in this matter" but "I have never cut a matter short".** **This is a statement of habit, not of a single event.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the tense of the verb:** the sentence presents consultation not as what that day's matter required but as **her own manner of governing**. **And the distinction recorded in `042-sura.md` shows itself exactly here:** consultation is reported not as a **procedure** but as a **habit**.

**Second — the choice of verb: *qāṭiʿa* (ق-ط-ع) — to cut.** **That the verb "to cut" is used for deciding also conveys the character of the decision: to finish a matter, to close off the possibilities.** The English "to cut a matter short" carries the same picture.

**Third — *ḥattā tashhadūn*.** The root **ش-ه-د**: **to be present;** and from this, **to bear witness.** **The word carries both senses at once, and both work here:** "unless you are present" and "unless you state your opinion."

**I record this as an observation about the word:** the sentence describes consultation in terms of **being present**. **To give an opinion, one must first be there.**

### The council's answer — and the structure of the answer

**Verse 33 gives the council's answer in three parts:**

| Part | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *naḥnu ulū quwwatin wa ulū baʾsin shadīd* | **A statement of capacity** — we can fight |
| 2 | *wa'l-amru ilayk* | **The return of authority** — the decision is yours |
| 3 | *fa'nẓurī mādhā taʾmurīn* | **Waiting** — we await your command |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the three parts:** the council **does not state an opinion — it states a capacity.** They do not say "let us fight"; **they say "we have the strength to fight"** and hand the decision back.

**And the difference matters:** the queen had said *aftūnī* ("give me your opinion"); **the council delivers a report of capacity and returns the authority.** **What was asked for and what was given thus do not coincide.** This can be verified from the text's own dialogue.

**بَأْس** — root **ب-أ-س**: **violence in war, severity, hardship.** From the same root, *baʾs* (distress, punishment) and *baʾīs* (severe). **The word occurred in `057-hadid.md` at 57/25 as a property of iron** (*fīhi baʾsun shadīd*) **and was treated there.** **Here the same construction is used as a people's description of themselves: *baʾsin shadīd*.** This is an overlap of construction between the two sūras that can be verified.

### What this says to the present

**What the scene shows concretely is this: to consult is not to hand over the decision.**

**The council returns the decision (*wa'l-amru ilayk*), and the queen makes the decision herself (35).** **But the decision is not made without the council being heard.**

**And the converse is also true, and it is visible in the text:** the council stated a capacity (we can fight), **and the queen did not use it.** **To consult, then, does not create an obligation to act on the opinion received either.**

**I give this as my own reading; its ground is the sequence of verses 32-35.** **The measure recorded in `042-sura.md` holds here too: what is decisive is not the form but whose affair the affair is reckoned to be.**

## 27/34-35 — قَالَتْ إِنَّ ٱلْمُلُوكَ إِذَا دَخَلُوا۟ قَرْيَةً أَفْسَدُوهَا وَجَعَلُوٓا۟ أَعِزَّةَ أَهْلِهَآ أَذِلَّةً وَكَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ · وَإِنِّى مُرْسِلَةٌ إِلَيْهِم بِهَدِيَّةٍ فَنَاظِرَةٌۢ بِمَ يَرْجِعُ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ

*qālat inna'l-mulūka idhā dakhalū qaryatan afsadūhā wa jaʿalū aʿizzata ahlihā adhillatan wa kadhālika yafʿalūn · wa innī mursilatun ilayhim bi-hadiyyatin fa-nāẓiratun bima yarjiʿu'l-mursalūn*

"**She said: 'Kings, when they enter a town, ruin it and make the honoured among its people abased. That is what they do. I shall send them a gift, and then I shall see with what the envoys return.'**"

### An observation about politics

**Verse 34 transmits, from the mouth of a ruler, a generalisation about rulers. And the construction of the sentence must be recorded.**

| Element | Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | *al-mulūk* — **definite plural** | **Kings** — as a class |
| Condition | *idhā dakhalū qaryatan* | **Entry** — the moment of conquest |
| First outcome | *afsadūhā* | **Ruin** — the breaking of the order |
| Second outcome | *wa jaʿalū **aʿizzata** ahlihā **adhillatan*** | **Inversion** — the honoured brought low |
| The seal | *wa kadhālika yafʿalūn* | **Repetition** — a statement that there are no exceptions |

**عِزَّة / ذِلَّة** — the two words are opposites. The root **ع-ز-ز**: **to be hard and unwearing;** *ʿazīz* — one who is not overcome, whose worth does not fall. The root **ذ-ل-ل**: **to grow soft, to submit, to be crushed.** **The two roots have been treated in many places in this series** (`067-mulk.md`, `002-bakara.md`, `042-sura.md`). **I do not repeat them here.**

**A point about word order must be recorded: *aʿizza* and *adhilla* are on the same pattern (أَفْعِلَّة).** **Two opposed words stand side by side on the same pattern, and the sentence describes an exchange of places:** the same people, in the same pattern, with the opposite attribute.

**وَكَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ** — there is a disagreement in the classical commentaries about the last part of the sentence, and it is not concealed:

| Reading | Whose words *wa kadhālika yafʿalūn* are |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A continuation of the queen's speech** — reinforcing her own observation |
| 2 | **The text's own confirmation** — an endorsement added to what the queen said |

**I do not impose a preference.** **Both readings are transmitted, and on both the clause does the same work: it makes the finding admit of no exception.**

### A limit, per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**I do not enter into present-day politics on the basis of this verse, and I issue no verdict about any form of government.**

**What I shall record is the mechanism the verse itself describes, and that mechanism has two steps:**

1. **Ruin** (*afsadūhā*) — the interruption of order, of production, of settled life.
2. **The inversion of the order of standing** (*aʿizza → adhilla*) — the displacement of the group that had been honoured.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verbs:** the verse separates the second step from the first. **What is described is thus not only material destruction; it is the breaking, from outside, of a community's own internal order of worth.** **And the second is a more lasting outcome than the first** — because a building can be rebuilt, whereas an order of standing is not easily rebuilt.

**And in `028-kasas.md` at 28/4 a similar pair had occurred together:** *wa jaʿala ahlahā **shiyaʿan** yastaḍʿifu **ṭāʾifatan** minhum.* **The finding recorded there applies directly here: wrongdoing first breaks a society apart and then weakens one section of it.** **This verse of al-Naml describes the same process for a power coming from outside; al-Qaṣaṣ had described it for a power coming from within.**

| | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/4 | **al-Naml 27/34** |
|---|---|---|
| The power | **From within** — its own ruler | **From outside** — the kings who enter |
| The method | *shiyaʿan* — **division** | *afsadūhā* — **ruin** |
| The target | *yastaḍʿifu ṭāʾifatan* — weakening one section | *aʿizza → adhilla* — inverting the order of standing |

**This is a weave between the two sūras that can be verified.**

### وَإِنِّى مُرْسِلَةٌ إِلَيْهِم بِهَدِيَّةٍ

**The decision itself must be recorded: the queen neither fights nor surrenders. She does a third thing — she makes a test.**

**And the form of the wording shows it: *mursilatun* and *nāẓiratun* — both active participles.** **A nominal sentence conveys a resolve that is under way and continuing.**

**فَنَاظِرَةٌۢ بِمَ يَرْجِعُ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ** — "I shall see **with what** the envoys return."

**An observation about the word order:** the clause is built not with *man* ("who") but with ***bima* ("with what").** **What is awaited is thus not the envoys' return but the kind of answer they will bring.**

**And since the Qurʾān does not say what the gift was, as was noted in the discussion in `034-sebe.md`, the details transmitted about the content of the gift are not entered into in this commentary.** **The Qurʾān says only *hadiyya*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is verse 35 itself:** the gift is not a present but **an instrument of testing.** The queen means to learn who the other party is from his response to the gift — **because a king who takes a gift and a prophet who refuses one want different things.** **And the answer at verse 36 will bring out exactly this distinction.**

## 27/36-37 — فَلَمَّا جَآءَ سُلَيْمَٰنَ قَالَ أَتُمِدُّونَنِ بِمَالٍ فَمَآ ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ ٱللَّهُ خَيْرٌ مِّمَّآ ءَاتَىٰكُم بَلْ أَنتُم بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ تَفْرَحُونَ · ٱرْجِعْ إِلَيْهِمْ فَلَنَأْتِيَنَّهُم بِجُنُودٍ لَّا قِبَلَ لَهُم بِهَا وَلَنُخْرِجَنَّهُم مِّنْهَآ أَذِلَّةً وَهُمْ صَٰغِرُونَ

*fa-lammā jāʾa Sulaymāna qāla a-tumiddūnani bi-mālin fa-mā ātāniya'llāhu khayrun mimmā ātākum, bal antum bi-hadiyyatikum tafraḥūn · irjiʿ ilayhim fa-la-naʾtiyannahum bi-junūdin lā qibala lahum bihā wa la-nukhrijannahum minhā adhillatan wa hum ṣāghirūn*

"**When the envoy came to Solomon, he said: 'Do you reinforce me with wealth? What God has given me is better than what He has given you. It is you who take joy in your own gift. Go back to them! We shall come upon them with armies they cannot withstand, and we shall drive them out from there abased and made small.'**"

### أَتُمِدُّونَنِ بِمَالٍ

The root **م-د-د**: **to extend, to add to, to prolong.** **From Form IV, *imdād*: to help by adding to something, to send reinforcement.** **In a military context the word is also used for "sending up supporting troops".**

**The choice of verb is worth recording, and I give this as an observation about the word:** the gift is met not with the verb "to give" but with **"to reinforce"**. **The question is thus: are you making up something that is lacking in me?**

**And the ground of the answer follows immediately: *fa-mā ātāniya'llāhu khayrun mimmā ātākum*.** **The comparison is drawn between two things given, and on both sides the verb is the same: *ātā*.**

### بَلْ أَنتُم بِهَدِيَّتِكُمْ تَفْرَحُونَ

**The particle *bal* ("on the contrary") conveys a change of direction.** And the sentence says something beyond a refusal of the gift.

**A point about word order must be recorded: *bi-hadiyyatikum* has been brought forward** (it comes before the verb) **and in Arabic such fronting conveys emphasis and restriction.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the fronting:** the sentence does not say "you rejoice" — **it says "it is you who rejoice *in your own gift*".** **The joy thus comes not from what the other party will receive but from what the giver has given.** **This is the answer of someone who knows that the gift is a test.**

**And a measure recorded in `034-sebe.md` applies here:** that the means given are a test will be said by Solomon in his own words at verse 40 (*li-yabluwanī a-ashkuru am akfur*). **The ground of the answer at verse 36 is thus given in his own words four verses later.**

### لَّا قِبَلَ لَهُم بِهَا

The root **ق-ب-ل**: **the front, that which faces.** ***Qibal*** here: **the strength to withstand, the capacity.** The idiom means "they have no power to stand before it".

**وَهُمْ صَٰغِرُونَ** — root **ص-غ-ر**: **to be small.** *Ṣāghir* — one made small not by his own wish but by compulsion.

**And its link with verse 34 must be recorded, and it is a datum of the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| **34** | *wa jaʿalū aʿizzata ahlihā **adhillatan*** | **The queen** — what kings do |
| **37** | *wa la-nukhrijannahum minhā **adhillatan*** | **Solomon** — what he will do |

**The same word (*adhilla*), three verses apart, in two different mouths.**

**This overlap can be verified from the text and must be recorded.** **I propose a reading, and I state that it is my own:** the queen had described what kings do; **Solomon meets her words with the same word.** **And the distinction lies not in the outcome itself but in the condition under which it would come about:** the letter at verse 31 had left a door open (*wa'tūnī muslimīn*), and when that door was used at verses 42-44 the threat did not come to pass.

**I give this as a reading; the verse does not make this connection explicitly.** **What can be verified from the text is the repetition of the word and the outcome of the narrative.**

## 27/38-40 — قَالَ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْمَلَؤُا۟ أَيُّكُمْ يَأْتِينِى بِعَرْشِهَا قَبْلَ أَن يَأْتُونِى مُسْلِمِينَ · قَالَ عِفْرِيتٌ مِّنَ ٱلْجِنِّ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ قَبْلَ أَن تَقُومَ مِن مَّقَامِكَ وَإِنِّى عَلَيْهِ لَقَوِىٌّ أَمِينٌ · قَالَ ٱلَّذِى عِندَهُۥ عِلْمٌ مِّنَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ قَبْلَ أَن يَرْتَدَّ إِلَيْكَ طَرْفُكَ

*qāla yā ayyuha'l-malaʾu ayyukum yaʾtīnī bi-ʿarshihā qabla an yaʾtūnī muslimīn · qāla ʿifrītun mina'l-jinni ana ātīka bihī qabla an taqūma min maqāmika wa innī ʿalayhi la-qawiyyun amīn · qāla'lladhī ʿindahū ʿilmun mina'l-kitābi ana ātīka bihī qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk · fa-lammā raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahū qāla hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī a-ashkuru am akfur, wa man shakara fa-innamā yashkuru li-nafsih, wa man kafara fa-inna rabbī ghaniyyun karīm*

"**He said: 'O chiefs! Which of you will bring me her throne before they come to me in submission?' An ʿifrīt of the jinn said: 'I will bring it to you before you rise from your place; for this I am strong, and I am trustworthy.' The one who had a knowledge from the Book said: 'I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you.' And when Solomon saw it settled before him he said: 'This is of my Lord's bounty, to test me — whether I give thanks or am ungrateful. Whoever gives thanks gives thanks only for his own sake; and whoever is ungrateful — my Lord is beyond all need, and generous.'**"

### قَبْلَ أَن يَأْتُونِى مُسْلِمِينَ — a note about time

**The condition of the request must be recorded: the throne is asked for before they come in submission.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence asks for the task to be done within a limit of time. **And the limit is itself the demand made in the letter at verse 31** (*wa'tūnī muslimīn*) — **the sūra thus uses the same phrase twice, once as a demand and once as a measure of time.**

**Why the throne is asked for is not said in the verse.** **Different explanations of this are transmitted in the classical commentaries; there is no certain knowledge, and I do not impose a preference.** **The text does, however, give a ground of its own — at verse 41:** *nanẓur a-tahtadī am takūnu mina'lladhīna lā yahtadūn.* **The verse thus explains its own purpose three verses later.**

### عِفْرِيت

**As the lexical sense of the word the lexicographers record this: *ʿifrīt* — strong, agile, formidable; one who does his work soundly.** In connection with the root, the sense **to be covered in dust, to be rubbed into the ground** (*ʿafara*) is also transmitted; **it is reported that the word is used of one who throws his opponent to the ground.**

**The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.** **I derive from it no classification of the kinds, ranks or nature of the jinn;** the text says only *ʿifrītun mina'l-jinn*.

### Two offers — and the difference between them

**The two offers can be compared exactly, because both begin with the same sentence: *ana ātīka bihī qabla an…***

| | **The ʿifrīt (39)** | **The one with a knowledge from the Book (40)** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | *ʿifrītun mina'l-jinn* — **of the jinn** | *alladhī ʿindahū ʿilmun mina'l-kitāb* — **not named** |
| The shared sentence | *ana ātīka bihī qabla an…* | **The same** |
| The measure of time | *qabla an **taqūma min maqāmik*** — **before you rise from the assembly** | *qabla an **yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk*** — **before your glance returns** |
| The kind of measure | **An act** — the ending of the session | **A reflex** — the return of the eyelid |
| Self-description | ***la-qawiyyun amīn*** — strong and trustworthy | **None** — he does not describe himself |
| The ground | **His own strength** | ***ʿilmun mina'l-kitāb*** — a knowledge given |

**All four differences can be verified from the text.**

**And two of the differences must be recorded in particular.**

**First — the measures of time.** The ʿifrīt's measure **depends on a man's will** (Solomon rises when he wills to rise); **the second man's measure does not depend on the will** — the blink of an eye is a movement not in a person's own hands. **The second span is thus not merely shorter than the first; it is given by a measure of another kind.** I record this as my own reading; its ground is the character of the two measures.

**Second — the self-description.** The ʿifrīt introduces himself **with two attributes**: *qawiyyun amīn.*

**And these two attributes had occurred earlier in this series: they were treated in `028-kasas.md` at 28/26** — the measure a young woman in Midian named for Moses: *inna khayra mani'staʾjarta'l-qawiyyu'l-amīn.* **The finding recorded there was this: one conveys *competence*, the other *reliability*, and neither on its own is enough.**

| Place | Expression | Who says it | About whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/26 | *al-qawiyyu'l-amīn* | **A young woman** — to her father | **About Moses** — about someone else |
| **al-Naml 27/39** | *la-qawiyyun amīn* | **The ʿifrīt** — to Solomon | **About himself** |

**The same two attributes, in two sūras, once about another and once about oneself.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the last column of the table above:** in al-Qaṣaṣ the measure **rested on observation** — it was spoken by someone who had watched the scene. **In al-Naml the same measure is a declaration about oneself.** **And the task is not given to the one who declares it of himself.**

### ٱلَّذِى عِندَهُۥ عِلْمٌ مِّنَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ

**The Qurʾān does not give this man's name.** **More than one name is transmitted in the classical commentaries; none of them occurs in the Qurʾān, and there is no certain knowledge. No name is used in this commentary.**

**In the same way, what the phrase "a knowledge from the Book" refers to is not stated either:**

| Reading | What *ʿilmun mina'l-kitāb* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A knowledge from the revealed Book** — resting on revelation |
| 2 | **A share from the Preserved Tablet** — the manner of which is not known |
| 3 | **The knowledge of a particular prayer or name** — it is transmitted, but its basis is weak |

**I do not impose a preference, and I record that the basis of the third reading is weak.**

**There are two things that can be verified from the text, and both lie in the word order:**

1. **The word is indefinite: *ʿilmun* — "a knowledge", not *al-ʿilm*.** **What the man holds is thus not the whole of the Book but a share of it.**
2. **And the particle *min* reinforces this: *mina'l-kitāb* — [a part] from the Book.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the verse identifies the man not by the **magnitude** of his knowledge but by its **source**. **The ʿifrīt had stated his strength as an attribute of his own; this man refers his knowledge to a source.**

**And the sūra's spine of knowledge gains one more link here:** at verse 15 knowledge was given to David and Solomon; at verse 22 the bird encompassed what the king could not encompass; **here a man who is not even named does what the ʿifrīt could not do.** **The sūra thus goes on distributing knowledge independently of rank.** I record this as my own reading; its ground is the sequence of the three verses.

### هَٰذَا مِن فَضْلِ رَبِّى لِيَبْلُوَنِىٓ

**Solomon's response must be recorded, and it has two parts:**

| Part | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *hādhā **min faḍli rabbī*** | **The source** — referred not to himself but to his Lord |
| 2 | *li-**yabluwanī** a-ashkuru am akfur* | **The purpose** — a test |

The root **ب-ل-و**: **to try, to test a thing until it wears.** The lexicographers record the sense "to wear out a garment": *balā* — to wear something down by using it. **A test is thus not an idle question; it is a process of wearing.**

**And in `028-kasas.md` at 28/78 the exact opposite sentence was treated:**

| Place | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/78 | *innamā ūtītuhū ʿalā ʿilmin **ʿindī*** | **Qārūn** — by virtue of a knowledge I possess |
| **al-Naml 27/40** | *hādhā min **faḍli rabbī*** | **Solomon** — of my Lord's bounty |

**The two sentences are two different readings of the same situation (an unlooked-for capacity coming to pass).** This is a contrast between the two sūras that can be verified.

**And what is striking is this, and I record it as my own reading:** the key word in Qārūn's sentence was **knowledge**; **and the word that identifies the man in the throne scene is likewise knowledge.** **The difference lies in where the knowledge is referred:** one says *ʿindī* ("with me"), the other says *mina'l-kitāb* ("from the Book"). **The sūra thus draws its line not at knowledge but at the claiming of knowledge as one's own.**

### وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِۦ

The root **ش-ك-ر** was treated in detail in `034-sebe.md` as the spine of that sūra, and it occurred in `067-mulk.md`, `076-insan.md` and `054-kamer.md`. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the construction of the sentence, and I give it as an observation about the word order: *li-nafsih* — "for himself."** **The direction of thanksgiving is thus reversed: the one who gives thanks has done something not for the giver but for himself.**

**And the second half of the sentence completes this: *wa man kafara fa-inna rabbī ghaniyyun karīm*.**

**غَنِىّ** — root **غ-ن-ي**: **to have no need, to be sufficient in oneself.** **The root was treated in `057-hadid.md` at 57/24 and in `047-muhammed.md` at 47/38:** *al-Ghanī* is more exact than "rich" — **the one who has need of nothing.**

**كَرِيم** — root **ك-ر-م**, treated in `096-alak.md` at 96/3: **to give abundantly, expecting no return and keeping no count.** **And this verse was already cited there.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**That the two names stand side by side is worth recording, and I give it as an observation about the word order:**

| Name | What it conveys | Its function in the face of ingratitude |
|---|---|---|
| *Ghaniyy* | **He has no need** | He has no need of the thanks |
| *Karīm* | **He gives** | Ingratitude does not cut off the giving |

**The first name thus blocks one false conclusion (that thanksgiving is the paying of a debt); the second blocks another (that ingratitude stops the giving).**

## 27/41-42 — قَالَ نَكِّرُوا۟ لَهَا عَرْشَهَا نَنظُرْ أَتَهْتَدِىٓ أَمْ تَكُونُ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَهْتَدُونَ · فَلَمَّا جَآءَتْ قِيلَ أَهَٰكَذَا عَرْشُكِ قَالَتْ كَأَنَّهُۥ هُوَ وَأُوتِينَا ٱلْعِلْمَ مِن قَبْلِهَا وَكُنَّا مُسْلِمِينَ

*qāla nakkirū lahā ʿarshahā nanẓur a-tahtadī am takūnu mina'lladhīna lā yahtadūn · fa-lammā jāʾat qīla a-hākadhā ʿarshuk, qālat ka-annahū huw, wa ūtīna'l-ʿilma min qablihā wa kunnā muslimīn*

"**He said: 'Make her throne unrecognisable to her; let us see whether she is guided to it, or is one of those who are not guided.' When she came it was said: 'Was your throne like this?' She said: 'It is as though it were the very one.' 'And we were given the knowledge before this, and we had submitted.'**"

### نَكِّرُوا۟ — root ن-ك-ر

**The core of the root: to be unrecognised, to be strange.** *Nakira* — he did not recognise it; *munkar* — what is not accepted, what is found strange; *nakira* — an indefinite noun. **The root was treated in `031-lokman.md` at 31/17-19** (*al-munkar* and *ankara'l-aṣwāt*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**Here in Form II: *nakkirū* — "make it unrecognisable."**

**And the verb that follows in the sentence is from a different root: *a-tahtadī* (ه-د-ي) — "will she find her way to it."** **The question is thus framed not as "will she recognise it" but as "will she find her way".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** throughout the sūra the root **ه-د-ي** is used for guidance (2, 24, 63, 81, 92). **Here the same root is used for a test of recognition, and the two domains are bound together:** being able to recognise the familiar in its altered form, and finding what is right, are named by the same verb.

**And I do not repeat the analysis of *hidāya* recorded in `048-fetih.md` at 48/25 and in `047-muhammed.md`.**

### كَأَنَّهُۥ هُوَ — a guarded answer

**The queen's answer is neither "yes" nor "no": *ka-annahū huw* — "as though it were the very one."**

**An observation about the word order must be recorded: the sentence is built with a particle of comparison (*ka-anna*)**, and **both sides of the comparison are the same pronoun: *huwa* and *huwa*.** That is: "it, as though it."

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the construction of the sentence:** the answer conveys recognition and hesitation at once. **She gives the right answer without speaking with certainty.**

**And this agrees with her sentence at verse 32:** *mā kuntu qāṭiʿatan amran ḥattā tashhadūn* — "I never cut a matter short." **Both sentences are a deferral of certainty.** This is a consistency that can be verified within the narrative.

### وَأُوتِينَا ٱلْعِلْمَ مِن قَبْلِهَا

**Whose sentence this is has been debated in the classical commentaries, and the disagreement is not concealed:**

| Reading | Who speaks the sentence | What *min qablihā* points to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The queen** | We had been given knowledge even before this wonder |
| 2 | **Solomon and those with him** | We had been given knowledge before her submission |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record one indication, not a preference:** there is a shift between the plural pronouns of the sentence (*ūtīnā*, *kunnā*) and the singular of the preceding clause (*qālat*); **this by itself does not settle which of the two readings is right.**

**What can be verified from the text is this, and I record it:** the two words in the sentence — ***al-ʿilm*** and ***muslimīn*** — are two of the sūra's spines. **At this point in the narrative the two occur together.**

### ٱلْعِلْم — a difference of definiteness

**A point about word order is worth recording, and it is a countable datum:**

| Verse | Expression | Definiteness |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | *ātaynā Dāwūda wa Sulaymāna **ʿilmā*** | **Indefinite** — "a knowledge" |
| 40 | *alladhī ʿindahū **ʿilmun** mina'l-kitāb* | **Indefinite** — "a knowledge" |
| **42** | *wa ūtīna'l-**ʿilma** min qablihā* | **Definite** — "the knowledge" |

**I record this as an observation;** I issue no definite verdict about what the definiteness does here. **What I record is that the word occurs twice indefinite and once definite within the sūra.**

## 27/43-44 — وَصَدَّهَا مَا كَانَت تَّعْبُدُ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ إِنَّهَا كَانَتْ مِن قَوْمٍ كَٰفِرِينَ · قِيلَ لَهَا ٱدْخُلِى ٱلصَّرْحَ فَلَمَّا رَأَتْهُ حَسِبَتْهُ لُجَّةً وَكَشَفَتْ عَن سَاقَيْهَا قَالَ إِنَّهُۥ صَرْحٌ مُّمَرَّدٌ مِّن قَوَارِيرَ قَالَتْ رَبِّ إِنِّى ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِى وَأَسْلَمْتُ مَعَ سُلَيْمَٰنَ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

*wa ṣaddahā mā kānat taʿbudu min dūni'llāh, innahā kānat min qawmin kāfirīn · qīla laha'dkhuli'ṣ-ṣarḥ, fa-lammā raʾat'hu ḥasibat'hu lujjatan wa kashafat ʿan sāqayhā, qāla innahū ṣarḥun mumarradun min qawārīr, qālat rabbi innī ẓalamtu nafsī wa aslamtu maʿa Sulaymāna li'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn*

"**What she used to worship instead of God had turned her away; she was of a people who denied. It was said to her: 'Enter the hall.' When she saw it she took it for deep water and drew up her skirt. Solomon said: 'It is a hall paved smooth with glass.' Then the woman said: 'My Lord! I have wronged myself; and I have submitted, with Solomon, to God, the Lord of all the worlds.'**"

### وَصَدَّهَا مَا كَانَت تَّعْبُدُ

The root **ص-د-د**: **to turn away, to hold back, to block.** **The same verb had occurred in this narrative at verse 24:** *fa-ṣaddahum ʿani's-sabīl* — there the subject was Satan.

| Verse | Expression | Subject — what turns away |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | *fa-**ṣaddahum** ʿani's-sabīl* | **Satan** |
| 43 | *wa **ṣaddahā** mā kānat taʿbudu min dūni'llāh* | **What she worshipped** |

**The same verb, two different subjects.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the narrative.

**And the choice of subject here is worth recording, and I give it as an observation about the word order:** what turns her away is not she herself but **the thing she worshipped.** *Mā kānat taʿbudu.* **The verse thus looks for the obstacle not in the person's character but in the object of her habit.**

**And the ground follows immediately: *innahā kānat min qawmin kāfirīn* — "she was of a people who denied."** **The qualification matters: the sentence describes not her but *the community she came from*.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse issues no blanket verdict — **it reports where a person came from**, and in the next verse that person arrives somewhere else. **The work of the sentence is to make the change that follows intelligible.**

### ٱلصَّرْح and مُمَرَّد

**صَرْح** — root **ص-ر-ح**: **to be clear, unmixed, pure.** *Ṣarīḥ* — plain speech. **From this, *ṣarḥ*: a tall structure standing in the open; a tower, a hall.**

**The word occurred in `040-gafir.md` at 40/36-37 and in `028-kasas.md` at 28/38 for the tower Pharaoh wished to have built, and it was treated there.** **In this sūra the same word is used for a ruler's hall.**

| Place | Whose *ṣarḥ* | For what |
|---|---|---|
| Ghāfir 40/36 | **Pharaoh** | *laʿallī ablughu'l-asbāb* — to reach the means |
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/38 | **Pharaoh** | *laʿallī aṭṭaliʿu ilā ilāhi Mūsā* — to look upon the god of Moses |
| **al-Naml 27/44** | **Solomon** | **No reason is given** — only "enter" is said |

**This is a verbal overlap between three sūras that can be verified.** **I record it as an observation and build no interpretive link on it:** the Qurʾān uses the same word for a structure in one place as the instrument of a claim and in another as part of a palace.

**مُمَرَّد** — root **م-ر-د**. **This root was worked out in detail in `037-saffat.md` at 37/7 (*shayṭānin mārid*)**, and the concrete sense given there was this: **that no hair, leaf or bark is left on a thing; to be stripped bare, to be made smooth.** *Amrad* — a youth whose beard has not yet come; *shajaratun mardāʾ* — a leafless tree. **And this verse was already cited there: *ṣarḥun mumarradun min qawārīr* — a hall surfaced smooth.** **I do not repeat it; I rely on that treatment.**

**قَوَارِير** — the plural of *qārūra*: **glass, crystal.** **The word was treated in `076-insan.md` at 76/15-16** (*qawārīra min fiḍḍa*) **and the property recorded there works here: the distinguishing quality of glass is that it shows what lies within it.**

**And the mechanics of the scene rest exactly on this, and I record it as an observation:** because the surface is smooth (*mumarrad*) and transparent (*min qawārīr*), **it looks like water.** **The illusion thus arises from two physical properties coming together** — and the text states each of them separately.

### حَسِبَتْهُ لُجَّةً — and its link with verse 88

**لُجَّة** — root **ل-ج-ج**: **depth; a confused roaring together.** *Lujja* — the deep part of the sea; *lajaj* — to persist in a thing, to pile up one upon another.

**And the verb must be recorded: *ḥasibat* — root ح-س-ب: to suppose, to reckon.**

**The same verb will occur once more at verse 88 of this sūra, and there too it will describe an illusion of sight:**

| Verse | Expression | Who is deceived | What is supposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **44** | *ḥasibat'hu lujjatan* | **The queen** | A glass floor → **water** |
| **88** | *taḥsabuhā jāmidatan* | **Everyone who looks** | A moving mountain → **standing still** |

**The same verb, in two different blocks of the sūra, for two illusions of sight.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the repetition of the verb:** the sūra records twice, on two different scales, that what the eye sees and what is the case are not the same — **once on the floor of a hall, once in the mountains.** And I shall return to it at verse 88.

### وَكَشَفَتْ عَن سَاقَيْهَا

**سَاق** — leg, shin. **The word was mentioned in the discussion of `048-fetih.md` at 48/29 and elsewhere in this series; it was recorded there that the Qurʾān uses this word for the human leg.**

**What the text reports is a matter of necessity: she draws up her skirt because she supposes she is about to step into water.**

**I do not enter into the storytellers' details that circulate about this scene.** **The Qurʾān records a single movement, and its ground is in the sentence itself: *fa-lammā raʾat'hu ḥasibat'hu lujjatan* — she did it because she supposed.**

### رَبِّ إِنِّى ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِى — the last sentence of the narrative

**And the narrative ends with an admission.**

***Innī ẓalamtu nafsī*** — this sentence occurs in other places in the Qurʾān as well and has been treated in this series: **in `028-kasas.md` at 28/16, from the mouth of Moses** (*rabbi innī ẓalamtu nafsī fa'ghfir lī*).

| Place | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/16 | *rabbi innī ẓalamtu nafsī fa'ghfir lī* | **Moses** — after a death |
| **al-Naml 27/44** | *rabbi innī ẓalamtu nafsī wa **aslamtu maʿa Sulaymān*** | **The queen** — a submission |

**The same sentence, in two sūras, in two different scenes.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**And the continuation that belongs to al-Naml is the most striking point of word order in the narrative, and it must be recorded:**

***Wa aslamtu maʿa Sulaymāna li'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn.***

**The sentence is built on two particles, and each does a different work:**

| Particle | Word | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| *maʿa* (accompaniment) | *maʿa Sulaymāna* | **Together with Solomon** — he too is among those who submit |
| The *lām* (belonging) | *li'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿālamīn* | **To God** — the one to whom the submission is made |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of the two particles:** in answering the letter's demand *wa'tūnī muslimīn* ("come to me in submission"), the queen **changes the addressee.** **She submits not to Solomon but, together with Solomon, to God.**

**And this is a correction that can be verified from the sūra's own text:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | *wa'tūnī **muslimīn*** — "come to me" | **The letter** |
| 44 | *aslamtu **maʿa Sulaymāna li'llāh*** | **The queen** |

**A prophet's call does not end in attachment to himself.** I give this as my own reading; **its ground is the comparison of the two verses.**

### A note on the narrative as a whole

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I draw a limit, and it holds for the whole of this section:**

- **The reports about the queen's name, the history of her people, and her later relations with Solomon have not been entered into.** The Qurʾān does not supply them.
- **No attempt has been made to explain how the extraordinary events in the narrative (the bringing of the throne) came about.** The text gives a span of time; it does not give a mechanism.
- **No conclusion bearing on the present has been drawn from such items as the jinn being set to work, or the existence of the ʿifrīt.**

**The spine that emerges from the narrative's own text, and that can be verified, is this, and I give it as a table:**

| Step | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20-22 | **An absence is noticed, evidence is demanded, news arrives** |
| 2 | 27-28 | **Judgement is deferred and an inquiry is made** |
| 3 | 29-31 | **A short letter: do not exalt yourselves, submit** |
| 4 | 32-33 | **The other side takes counsel** |
| 5 | 34-35 | **A test instead of war: a gift** |
| 6 | 36-37 | **The gift is refused, and the reason is stated** |
| 7 | 38-42 | **The throne is brought, made unrecognisable, recognised** |
| 8 | 44 | **The hall, the illusion, and the submission** |

**In none of the eight steps is there war.** This is a fact that can be verified from the text, and it is the narrative's own course.

## 27/45-47 — وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَآ إِلَىٰ ثَمُودَ أَخَاهُمْ صَٰلِحًا أَنِ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ فَإِذَا هُمْ فَرِيقَانِ يَخْتَصِمُونَ · قَالَ يَٰقَوْمِ لِمَ تَسْتَعْجِلُونَ بِٱلسَّيِّئَةِ قَبْلَ ٱلْحَسَنَةِ لَوْلَا تَسْتَغْفِرُونَ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُونَ · قَالُوا۟ ٱطَّيَّرْنَا بِكَ وَبِمَن مَّعَكَ قَالَ طَٰٓئِرُكُمْ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ بَلْ أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ تُفْتَنُونَ

*wa laqad arsalnā ilā Thamūda akhāhum Ṣāliḥan ani'budu'llāha fa-idhā hum farīqāni yakhtaṣimūn · qāla yā qawmi lima tastaʿjilūna bi's-sayyiʾati qabla'l-ḥasanati lawlā tastaghfirūna'llāha laʿallakum turḥamūn · qālu'ṭṭayyarnā bika wa bi-man maʿaka, qāla ṭāʾirukum ʿinda'llāh, bal antum qawmun tuftanūn*

"**We sent to Thamūd their brother Ṣāliḥ, saying: 'Serve God.' And at once they were two factions, contending with one another. He said: 'O my people! Why do you seek to hasten the evil before the good? Why do you not ask God's forgiveness — that you might be shown mercy?' They said: 'We take you and those with you for an ill omen.' He said: 'Your omen is with God. Rather, you are a people under trial.'**"

### فَإِذَا هُمْ فَرِيقَانِ يَخْتَصِمُونَ

***Idhā fujāʾiyya*** — the particle that conveys "and all at once". **This particle was treated in detail in `036-yasin.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the outcome must be recorded: a single command is given (*iʿbudū'llāh*), and the community splits in two at once.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse gives the outcome of the call not as an acceptance or a refusal but as **a division**. **And the verb is one of contention:** *yakhtaṣimūn* — Form VIII, which conveys reciprocity.

The root **خ-ص-م**: **to go to law against one another, to be mutual adversaries.** The word also covers a dispute at law.

### لِمَ تَسْتَعْجِلُونَ بِٱلسَّيِّئَةِ قَبْلَ ٱلْحَسَنَةِ

**The sentence is built as a question, and two words are set against one another: *sayyiʾa* and *ḥasana*.**

**Different explanations of what these words mean here are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | What *sayyiʾa* / *ḥasana* are |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Punishment / mercy** — what is sought is the hastening of the punishment |
| 2 | **Penalty / pardon** — asking for the penalty while forgiveness stands open |
| 3 | **A bad outcome / a good outcome** — in general |

**The three explanations are close to one another; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the pattern of the verb must be recorded: *tastaʿjilūn* — Form X, from the root ع-ج-ل: to ask that a thing come quickly.** **The pattern puts the weight on the asking itself.**

**لَوْلَا تَسْتَغْفِرُونَ** — ***lawlā*** here, at the head of an imperfect verb, conveys **urging**: "why do you not…". **The lexicographers call this usage *taḥḍīḍ* (a call that mixes urging with reproach).**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence is built not in the imperative but in the form of an urging. **And the return is given not as a certainty but as a hope: *laʿallakum turḥamūn* — "that you might be shown mercy."**

### ٱطَّيَّرْنَا بِكَ — and the ring formed with Yā-Sīn

The root **ط-ي-ر**: **to fly.** ***Taṭayyur***: a word that comes from the custom of divining by releasing a bird; **and from this, "to take something for an ill omen."**

**This charge was treated in detail in `036-yasin.md` at 36/18-19, and three places were set out in a table there** — and this verse was already among them:

| Place | Who speaks | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aʿrāf 7/131 | **The people of Pharaoh** — about Moses | *yaṭṭayyarū bi-Mūsā wa man maʿah* |
| **al-Naml 27/47** | **Thamūd** — about Ṣāliḥ | *iṭṭayyarnā bika wa bi-man maʿak* |
| Yā-Sīn 36/18 | **The people of the city** — about the messengers | *innā taṭayyarnā bikum* |

**And the reading recorded in `036-yasin.md` holds here too; I do not repeat it:** the same move recurs in the three narratives — **there is a hardship, and the newcomer is pointed to as its cause.**

**What belongs to al-Naml is the answer given, and it must be recorded:**

| Place | The answer | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Yā-Sīn 36/19 | *ṭāʾirukum **maʿakum*** — your omen is with you | **It turns the responsibility inward** |
| **al-Naml 27/47** | *ṭāʾirukum **ʿinda'llāh*** — your omen is with God | **It ties the responsibility to a single centre** |

**Both answers use the same word (*ṭāʾir*) but attach it with different particles: *maʿa* (accompaniment) and *ʿinda* (in the presence of).** **This is a difference between the two sūras that can be verified.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the answer in Yā-Sīn returned **the cause to those addressed themselves**; **the answer in al-Naml says that an omen is not an independent power.** **The two answers do not contradict each other — one points to the responsibility, the other to the source.**

**بَلْ أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ تُفْتَنُونَ** — *tuftanūn*, from the root **ف-ت-ن** and in the **passive**: "you are being tested."

**The root was treated in `029-ankebut.md` at 29/2-3 and in `085-buruc.md`:** the concrete sense of the root is **to put metal into the fire and separate what is pure from the dross.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And that the verb comes in the passive is worth recording, and I give it as an observation about the word order:** the sentence says "you are being tested" and does not name the one who tests. **The answer thus puts another explanation in the place of the claim about ill omens: what befalls you is not a sign but a test.**

## 27/48-51 — وَكَانَ فِى ٱلْمَدِينَةِ تِسْعَةُ رَهْطٍ يُفْسِدُونَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا يُصْلِحُونَ · قَالُوا۟ تَقَاسَمُوا۟ بِٱللَّهِ لَنُبَيِّتَنَّهُۥ وَأَهْلَهُۥ ثُمَّ لَنَقُولَنَّ لِوَلِيِّهِۦ مَا شَهِدْنَا مَهْلِكَ أَهْلِهِۦ وَإِنَّا لَصَٰدِقُونَ · وَمَكَرُوا۟ مَكْرًا وَمَكَرْنَا مَكْرًا وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ · فَٱنظُرْ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ مَكْرِهِمْ أَنَّا دَمَّرْنَٰهُمْ وَقَوْمَهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ

*wa kāna fi'l-madīnati tisʿatu rahṭin yufsidūna fi'l-arḍi wa lā yuṣliḥūn · qālū taqāsamū bi'llāhi la-nubayyitannahū wa ahlahū thumma la-naqūlanna li-waliyyihī mā shahidnā mahlika ahlihī wa innā la-ṣādiqūn · wa makarū makran wa makarnā makran wa hum lā yashʿurūn · fa'nẓur kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu makrihim annā dammarnāhum wa qawmahum ajmaʿīn*

"**In that city there were nine men who spread corruption in the land and set nothing right. They said: 'Swear to one another by God: we shall fall upon him and his household by night, and then we shall say to his next of kin: we did not witness the killing of his household, and we are telling the truth.' They plotted a plot, and We plotted a plot — and they did not notice. Look, then, how the end of their plot came about: We destroyed them and their whole people.**"

### تِسْعَةُ رَهْطٍ

The root **ر-ه-ط**: **the close kin around a person, a small group.** **The lexicographers describe a *rahṭ* as a group of between three and ten men**, and record that the word is **a collective noun** with no singular of its own.

**How the number is stated is worth recording: *tisʿatu rahṭin* — nine *rahṭ*.**

**Two readings of the phrase are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | What *tisʿatu rahṭin* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Nine men** — *rahṭ* here as a specifier, in the sense of "person" |
| 2 | **Nine small groups** — each at the head of a body of people |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**And it must be recorded that the number occurs here for the second time in this sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | *fī **tisʿi** āyāt* | **Nine signs** — given to Moses |
| 48 | ***tisʿatu** rahṭin* | **Nine men** — spreading corruption among Thamūd |

**The same number, in two different blocks of the sūra.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text. **I build no link of meaning on it, and per the method (`STYLE.md`) I do not enter into numerical reckoning;** what I record is only the repetition itself.

### يُفْسِدُونَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا يُصْلِحُونَ

**The sentence is built with two verbs, and the second is not a completion of the first but a separate qualification.**

The root **ف-س-د**: **a thing's going out of its own working order, its going bad.** The root **ص-ل-ح**: **to be fit, to be in working order.**

**An observation about the word order must be recorded:** the sentence does not stop at "they spread corruption" — **it adds *wa lā yuṣliḥūn*.** **Two separate things are thus said: they break, and they do not mend.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the second clause has been added:** a community could be one that "does not mend" without the first clause holding of it as well. **The second leaves the first no door of excuse:** the one who breaks has no side to him that repairs what he has broken.

**And the frame recorded in `028-kasas.md` should be recalled:** that sūra was built on the pair *ʿuluww* and *fasād*, and **verse 14 of al-Naml carried the same pair** (*ʿuluwwā* … *al-mufsidīn*). **Here the same root occurs for the third time.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | *ʿāqibatu'l-**mufsidīn*** | **The people of Pharaoh** |
| 34 | ***afsadūhā*** | **Kings** — the queen's observation |
| 48 | ***yufsidūna** fi'l-arḍi wa lā yuṣliḥūn* | **The nine men of Thamūd** |

**Three occurrences, in three different blocks.** This is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.

### تَقَاسَمُوا۟ بِٱللَّهِ

The root **ق-س-م**: **to divide, to apportion;** and from this, **to swear an oath** (*qasam*). **The lexicographers make the connection thus:** the one who swears binds his word to a surety that cannot be divided.

**From Form VI, *taqāsum*: to swear oaths to one another.** **The oath is thus not one-sided; it is given by each to the others.**

**And what the oath is sworn by must be recorded: *bi'llāh* — by God.**

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the verse itself:** the plan for a killing is bound **with an oath in God's name.** **The text does not go on to name this as a contradiction — it reports the sentence as it stands.** And its force comes from that.

### The three stages of the plan

| Stage | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *la-nubayyitannahū wa ahlah* | **A raid by night** — the act |
| 2 | *thumma la-naqūlanna li-waliyyih* | **Denial** — a lie to his next of kin |
| 3 | *wa innā la-ṣādiqūn* | **The oath repeated** — "we are telling the truth" |

The root **ب-ي-ت**: **to pass the night, to do something by night.** *Bayt* (house) is from the same root — **the place where the night is passed.** **And *tabyīt*: to make a raid by night.**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verb names not the killing but **its hour**. **The plan thus rests less on the act itself than on its being unseen.**

**The third stage must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** the plan contains not only the killing but **the account to be given afterwards**. *Mā shahidnā mahlika ahlih* — "we did not witness the killing of his household." **And to this a claim of truthfulness is added: *wa innā la-ṣādiqūn*.**

**Two of the three stages thus belong to speech.** **This is a structure that can be verified from the text**, and it shows what the verse's corruption (*yufsidūn*) is described by: **the deed and the account built on top of it are planned together.**

### وَمَكَرُوا۟ مَكْرًا وَمَكَرْنَا مَكْرًا

The root **م-ك-ر**: the core sense the lexicographers give is **to do a thing secretly and by a roundabout way, to wind about.** **The word is not pejorative in itself; its character comes from the qualification it carries in the sentence.**

**The sharpest expression of this root was treated in `035-fatir.md` at 35/43:** *wa lā yaḥīqu'l-makru's-sayyiʾu illā bi-ahlih* — **"an evil plot closes in on none but its author."** **And the finding about word order recorded there was this: the sentence is built with a negation plus an exception, so that the possibility of the plot going anywhere else is shut off.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And there was one further qualification there, and it works here: in the sentence of Fāṭir the plot was qualified by the adjective *al-sayyiʾ* (evil).** **The verdict was carried not by the word itself but by its adjective.**

**The verse of al-Naml settles the same matter by another route, and this is one of the most striking points of word order in the sūra:**

| | What the men do | The response |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | *makarū* | *makarnā* |
| Cognate accusative | *makrā* | *makrā* |
| Difference | **None** — the words are exactly the same | **None** |

**The two sentences are word for word the same; only their subjects differ.**

**The classical commentaries dwell on how this usage is to be understood, and the disagreement is not concealed:**

| Reading | What the second *makr* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Mushākala** — in Arabic, the repetition of a word in the same form to match the one set against it; the sense is "We answered in kind" |
| 2 | **The proper sense of the root** — an ordering that works secretly, unnoticed; the word itself conveys no wrongdoing |
| 3 | **Naming the penalty with the name of the offence** — a widespread pattern in the Qurʾān |

**All three explanations are transmitted, and they do not contradict one another; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the finding recorded for this pattern in `104-humeze.md` works here too, and I rely on it:** there the name of the Fire (*Ḥuṭama*) was given **on the same pattern** as the name of the offender (*humaza / lumaza*), and it was recorded thus: **the penalty is described in the grammar of the offence.** **This verse of al-Naml does the same thing not by the pattern but by the very same word.**

**وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ** — and the sentence closes with the sūra's third spine.

**This phrase had occurred at verse 18 in the ant's speech, and there it conveyed an excuse.** **Here the same phrase conveys a heedlessness.**

| Verse | *wa hum lā yashʿurūn* | Who | Its function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18** | Solomon and his armies | **Those who might crush** | **An excuse** — there is no intent |
| **50** | The nine men | **Those who plot** | **Heedlessness** — they do not notice that their own plot has turned back on them |

**The same three words, in two different blocks, in two opposite functions.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two verses themselves:** in the first, not noticing **lightens the fault**; in the second it **does not save.** **The difference lies in what it is that goes unnoticed:** in one, a living thing beneath one's foot; in the other, the outcome of a plot one laid oneself.

### فَٱنظُرْ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ مَكْرِهِمْ

**And the sentence is the same as the sentence at verse 14** — only its last word has changed:

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| 14 | *fa'nẓur kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-**mufsidīn*** |
| 51 | *fa'nẓur kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu **makrihim*** |
| 69 | *fa'nẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-**mujrimīn*** |

**The same pattern three times, with three different endings.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**دَمَّرْنَٰهُمْ** — root **د-م-ر**: **to destroy, to root out.** **The root was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/25 (*tudammiru kulla shayʾin bi-amri rabbihā*) and in `037-saffat.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And its extent must be recorded: *wa qawmahum ajmaʿīn* — "and their whole people."** **The plan of nine men thus does not bind those nine alone.**

## 27/52-53 — فَتِلْكَ بُيُوتُهُمْ خَاوِيَةًۢ بِمَا ظَلَمُوٓا۟ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَةً لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ · وَأَنجَيْنَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَكَانُوا۟ يَتَّقُونَ

*fa-tilka buyūtuhum khāwiyatan bimā ẓalamū, inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-qawmin yaʿlamūn · wa anjaynā'lladhīna āmanū wa kānū yattaqūn*

"**And there are their houses, fallen empty, because of the wrong they did. In that is a sign for a people who know. And We delivered those who believed and were mindful.**"

### خَاوِيَة

The root **خ-و-ي** was worked out in `069-hakka.md` at 69/7, and the finding there was this: ***khawa'l-manzilu* — the house was emptied, no one was left in it; *khawa'l-baṭnu* — the belly was emptied.** **The centre of the root is the emptying of what is inside.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And this verse was already cited in `069-hakka.md` and in `053-necm.md` at 53/51:** of Thamūd, **stone** is left, but **no one** is left.

**And the demonstrative *tilka* ("there they are") must be recorded: a pronoun that points to what is far off.** **The verse thus shows the ruins as something that can be seen.**

**This connects with the command at verse 69 of the sūra: *qul sīrū fi'l-arḍi fa'nẓurū*.** **The evidence is thus in a place one can travel to.** **This method was treated in `035-fatir.md` at 35/44 and in `040-gafir.md` at 40/21 and 40/82;** I rely on that treatment.

### لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ

**And the addressee of the sign must be recorded: *li-qawmin yaʿlamūn* — "for a people who know."**

**The sūra uses this pattern twice, and the two words differ:**

| Verse | Pattern | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| **52** | *li-qawmin **yaʿlamūn*** | **The ruins** — history |
| **86** | *li-qawmin **yuʾminūn*** | **Night and day** — nature |

**This is a difference that can be verified within the sūra.**

**I record this as an observation and make no claim about the cause:** a ruin out of history requires **knowledge** (one has to know whose houses they were); **night and day, by contrast, are what everyone sees.** **The two verse-endings name two different kinds of reader.**

**The same triple pattern (*li'l-muʾminīn*, *li-qawmin yūqinūn*, *li-qawmin yaʿqilūn*) was treated in `045-casiye.md` at 45/3-6.** **I rely on that treatment.**

### وَأَنجَيْنَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَكَانُوا۟ يَتَّقُونَ

**The block closes with a sentence of deliverance, and two attributes are named.**

**An observation about the word order must be recorded: the first attribute is in the perfect (*āmanū*), the second is *kāna* + imperfect (*kānū yattaqūn*).**

**In Arabic the pattern *kāna* + imperfect conveys continuity.** **Faith is thus given as an event, and mindfulness as a state that continues.** I record this as an observation about the grammar.

## 27/54-55 — وَلُوطًا إِذْ قَالَ لِقَوْمِهِۦٓ أَتَأْتُونَ ٱلْفَٰحِشَةَ وَأَنتُمْ تُبْصِرُونَ · أَئِنَّكُمْ لَتَأْتُونَ ٱلرِّجَالَ شَهْوَةً مِّن دُونِ ٱلنِّسَآءِ بَلْ أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ تَجْهَلُونَ

*wa Lūṭan idh qāla li-qawmihī a-taʾtūna'l-fāḥishata wa antum tubṣirūn · a-innakum la-taʾtūna'r-rijāla shahwatan min dūni'n-nisāʾ, bal antum qawmun tajhalūn*

"**And Lot too We sent. He had said to his people: 'Do you commit that indecency with your eyes open? Do you approach men in desire instead of women? Truly you are a people who act in ignorance.'**"

### وَأَنتُمْ تُبْصِرُونَ — a clause of qualification

**The clause is a circumstantial one: "while you are seeing."**

**And this connects directly with the sūra's spine of knowledge. The expression at verse 14 should be recalled:**

| Verse | Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| 13-14 | *āyātunā **mubṣiratan*** … *wa'sta**yqan**athā anfusuhum* | **The people of Pharaoh** — denial though they saw what makes one see |
| **54** | *wa antum **tubṣirūn*** | **The people of Lot** — doing it though they see |

**The same root (ب-ص-ر), in two different blocks, doing the same work: the recording of acting knowingly.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure the two verses share:** the sūra ties neither the denial nor the deed **to ignorance.** **And this is decisive for reading the word *tajhalūn* in the next sentence.**

**Two readings of what the clause means are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | What *wa antum tubṣirūn* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Knowingly** — while you see how foul it is |
| 2 | **Openly** — before one another's eyes, without concealment |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **I record one indication:** in the telling of the same narrative in al-ʿAnkabūt the phrase *wa taʾtūna fī nādīkumu'l-munkar* occurs (29/29 — you commit that wrong in your assemblies); **this is an indication in support of the second reading.**

### ٱلْفَٰحِشَة

The root **ف-ح-ش** was treated in `053-necm.md` at 53/32: ***fuḥsh* — an ugliness that passes all bounds, excess.** *Fāḥisha*, plural *fawāḥish* — **an act whose foulness is plain.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And that the word comes definite must be recorded: *al-fāḥisha*.** **Not "an indecency" but "that indecency" — an act the ones addressed know and can name.**

**And indeed it is described in the next verse.** **That the Qurʾān names this people's act in several sūras was recorded in the discussion of `054-kamer.md` at 54/33-39**, and a distinction was drawn there as well: **Sūrat al-Qamar named the offence by way of *the right of the guest*.** **Al-Naml names the act directly.**

### بَلْ أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ تَجْهَلُونَ

The root **ج-ه-ل** was treated in `049-hucurat.md`, and the distinction recorded there is decisive here: **the common sense of the root is "not to know"; but in Arabic *jahl* is used as the opposite not only of *ʿilm* but also of *ḥilm*.** ***Ḥilm*** — mastery over anger and impulse, gravity of bearing. **On this, *jahl* means surrender to impulse as much as it means ignorance.**

**And the distinction is necessary here, because the verse itself had said in the preceding sentence *wa antum tubṣirūn* ("you see").**

| Word | Its opposite | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| *Jahl* | *ʿIlm* | **Not knowing** — this conflicts with the verse's own qualification |
| *Jahl* | *Ḥilm* | **Surrender to impulse** — this fits the context |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses stand together:** *tubṣirūn* ("you see") and *tajhalūn* ("you act in ignorance") **are two sentences of the same speech.** **For the two not to contradict each other, the second word must be read in its second sense rather than as "ignorance".**

**And the word *shahwatan* supports this: the root ش-ه-و — intense desire.** **The sentence locates the cause of the act in desire, not in ignorance.**

**A qualification, per the method (`STYLE.md`):** this verse contains **an act that the verse itself describes**, and in this commentary **no legal ruling is issued and the views of the schools of law are not debated.** **I speak not about persons but about the act the verse names.**

## 27/56-58 — فَمَا كَانَ جَوَابَ قَوْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّآ أَن قَالُوٓا۟ أَخْرِجُوٓا۟ ءَالَ لُوطٍ مِّن قَرْيَتِكُمْ إِنَّهُمْ أُنَاسٌ يَتَطَهَّرُونَ · فَأَنجَيْنَٰهُ وَأَهْلَهُۥٓ إِلَّا ٱمْرَأَتَهُۥ قَدَّرْنَٰهَا مِنَ ٱلْغَٰبِرِينَ · وَأَمْطَرْنَا عَلَيْهِم مَّطَرًا فَسَآءَ مَطَرُ ٱلْمُنذَرِينَ

*fa-mā kāna jawāba qawmihī illā an qālū akhrijū āla Lūṭin min qaryatikum innahum unāsun yataṭahharūn · fa-anjaynāhu wa ahlahū illa'mraʾatahū qaddarnāhā mina'l-ghābirīn · wa amṭarnā ʿalayhim maṭarā, fa-sāʾa maṭaru'l-mundharīn*

"**His people's only answer was to say: 'Drive the family of Lot out of your town; they are people who would keep themselves pure!' So We delivered him and his household — except his wife; her We decreed to be among those who stayed behind. And We rained down upon them a rain. Evil is the rain of those who have been warned!**"

### فَمَا كَانَ جَوَابَ قَوْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّآ أَن قَالُوٓا۟

**The pattern must be recorded: *mā kāna… illā* — negation plus exception.** **This pattern conveys that the answer given was the only one, and that there was no other.**

**And the character of the answer: not an argument but an order of eviction.**

**I record this as an observation:** before it reports the people's answer, the verse marks **by the pattern of the sentence** whether it counts as an answer at all. **The response to a question (54-55) is an order of banishment.**

### إِنَّهُمْ أُنَاسٌ يَتَطَهَّرُونَ

The root **ط-ه-ر** was treated in `076-insan.md` at 76/21 and in the discussion of `098-beyyine.md`: **purity, being free of filth.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the pattern must be recorded: *yataṭahharūn* — Form V, *tafaʿʿul*.** **This pattern conveys doing something to oneself and with effort: "they are trying to keep themselves pure."**

**The most striking thing about the sentence is the ground it gives, and I record it as a datum of the text: what is named as the reason for banishment is not an offence — it is a claim to purity.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the construction of the sentence:** the sentence opens with *inna*, that is, it states a ground. **And the ground is an attribute that is positive by the other side's own measure.** **The attribute thus turns into an accusation in a place where those who do not carry it are the majority.**

**This lies on the same line as a pattern recorded elsewhere in this series** — the mockery of the believers by the offenders and their calling them "the astray" was treated in `083-mutaffifin.md` at 83/29-32. **The two scenes carry the same structure: the measure being set by the majority.** I record this as an overlap.

### إِلَّا ٱمْرَأَتَهُۥ قَدَّرْنَٰهَا مِنَ ٱلْغَٰبِرِينَ

**ٱلْغَٰبِرِين** — root **غ-ب-ر**. **This root was worked out in `037-saffat.md` at 37/135, and the property recorded there works here:** the root means **both "to pass away" and "to remain behind"** — two senses that are the opposite of each other. **In Arabic this is called *aḍdād* (words that carry contrary senses).** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And it was recorded there: the Qurʾān uses the same word consistently in this narrative** — al-Ḥijr 15/60, al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/171, **al-Naml 27/57**, al-ʿAnkabūt 29/32-33. **This is a countable fact.**

**قَدَّرْنَٰهَا** — root **ق-د-ر**, Form II: **to determine by measure, to decree.** **The choice of verb is worth recording: it does not say that the woman "stayed behind" — it says "We decreed her to be among those who stayed behind".**

**In this commentary the details the Qurʾān does not supply about Lot's wife are not entered into.** **The Qurʾān records her conduct elsewhere (*khānatāhumā* — al-Taḥrīm 66/10), and it was treated in `066-tahrim.md`; I rely on that treatment.**

### وَأَمْطَرْنَا عَلَيْهِم مَّطَرًا

**The sentence is built with a cognate accusative: *amṭarnā… maṭarā.***

**And then: *fa-sāʾa maṭaru'l-mundharīn* — "evil is the rain of those who have been warned."**

**The choice of word must be recorded, and I give it as an observation about the word order:** the word **rain** is used for the punishment. **And rain will be named as a favour at verse 60 of the sūra:** *wa anzala lakum mina's-samāʾi māʾan fa-anbatnā bihī ḥadāʾiqa dhāta bahja.*

| Verse | What comes from the sky | What |
|---|---|---|
| **58** | *maṭar* | **Punishment** — upon those who had been warned |
| **60** | *māʾ* | **Orchards** — delightful to the eye |

**Two things coming from the same quarter, two opposite outcomes.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and **only one verse stands between the two (59).**

**I record this as my own reading:** in passing from the block of punishment to the block of proof the sūra **uses the same channel.** **And the difference lies in the word** — indeed at verse 58 the word is *maṭar*, and at verse 60 it is *māʾ*.

**ٱلْمُنذَرِين** — root **ن-ذ-ر**: to warn. **A passive participle: those who have been warned.**

**This must be recorded: the character of the punishment is tied to their having been warned.** **And this principle was treated in `035-fatir.md` at 35/24 and in `028-kasas.md` at 28/59:** *wa mā kāna rabbuka muhlika'l-qurā ḥattā yabʿatha fī ummihā rasūlā.* **I rely on that treatment.**

## 27/59 — قُلِ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَىٰ عِبَادِهِ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱصْطَفَىٰٓ ءَآللَّهُ خَيْرٌ أَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

*quli'l-ḥamdu li'llāhi wa salāmun ʿalā ʿibādihi'lladhīna'ṣṭafā, āllāhu khayrun ammā yushrikūn*

"**Say: Praise be to God, and peace be upon His servants whom He has chosen. Is God better, or what they associate with Him?**"

### The opening of the block — and its ring with the close of the sūra

**This verse shares a phrase exactly with the last verse of the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Place |
|---|---|---|
| **59** | *quli'**l-ḥamdu li'llāh*** | **The opening of the block of proof** |
| **93** | *wa quli'**l-ḥamdu li'llāh*** | **The last verse of the sūra** |

**The same command, the same sentence, in two different places in the sūra.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the position of the two verses:** once the narratives are done the sūra enters the block of proof with a command to praise, and **closes with the same command.** **Praise is thus both the door of the block and its seal.**

### وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَىٰ عِبَادِهِ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱصْطَفَىٰ

The root **ص-ف-و**: **purity, freedom from admixture.** From Form VIII, *iṣṭifāʾ*: **to pick out and take the purest part of a thing.** **The root was treated in `038-sad.md` and in `039-zumer.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The picture the lexicographers give is worth recording:** *ṣafwa* is the part of a thing that has been separated from its sediment and strained clear. **The act of choosing is thus described as a sifting.**

**And to whom the peace is given is left indefinite: *ʿibādihi'lladhīna'ṣṭafā* — "His servants whom He has chosen."** **No name is given.**

**I record this as an observation:** five prophets were named in the preceding block (Moses, David, Solomon, Ṣāliḥ, Lot). **The sentence gathers them all into a single construct and names none of them.**

### ءَآللَّهُ خَيْرٌ أَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

**And the block opens with a question. The form of the question must be recorded: it is a question of preference.**

***Khayrun*** — an elative: "better." **The question is thus framed as a comparison that presumes both sides to have some worth.**

**The classical commentaries dwell on how this pattern is to be understood:**

| Reading | What the question does |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A silencing comparison (*ilzām*)** — it is put in terms of what the other side itself grants |
| 2 | **Irony** — a question framed in order to show that no such comparison can be made |
| 3 | **A plain comparison** — calling those addressed to think by their own measure |

**The three explanations are close to one another; I do not impose a preference.**

**And I record an observation about the word order:** the question is **not answered** in the five verses that follow — **its answer is given by the enumeration of five separate facts.** **The answer is thus not a sentence but a list.**

## 27/60 — أَمَّنْ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَأَنزَلَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءً فَأَنۢبَتْنَا بِهِۦ حَدَآئِقَ ذَاتَ بَهْجَةٍ مَّا كَانَ لَكُمْ أَن تُنۢبِتُوا۟ شَجَرَهَآ أَءِلَٰهٌ مَّعَ ٱللَّهِ بَلْ هُمْ قَوْمٌ يَعْدِلُونَ

*am man khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa anzala lakum mina's-samāʾi māʾan fa-anbatnā bihī ḥadāʾiqa dhāta bahja, mā kāna lakum an tunbitū shajarahā, a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh, bal hum qawmun yaʿdilūn*

"**Or He who created the heavens and the earth and sent down water for you from the sky? With it We caused to grow orchards full of delight; it was not for you to make their trees grow. Is there a god beside God? No — they are a people who swerve.**"

### The sequence of five questions

**From verse 60 to verse 64 the same pattern is repeated five times, and the repetition can be verified word for word.**

| Verse | *am man…* — the subject of the question | The shared sentence | The close |
|---|---|---|---|
| **60** | *khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* + **water and orchards** | *a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh* | *bal hum qawmun **yaʿdilūn*** |
| **61** | *jaʿala'l-arḍa qarārā* + **rivers, mountains, a barrier** | *a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh* | *bal aktharuhum **lā yaʿlamūn*** |
| **62** | *yujību'l-**muḍṭarra*** + **the removal of harm, succession** | *a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh* | *qalīlan mā **tadhakkarūn*** |
| **63** | *yahdīkum fī ẓulumāti'l-barri wa'l-baḥr* + **the winds** | *a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh* | *taʿāla'llāhu **ʿammā yushrikūn*** |
| **64** | *yabdaʾu'l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduh* + **provision** | *a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh* | *qul **hātū burhānakum** in kuntum ṣādiqīn* |

**Five questions, five different closes — and the sentence in the middle never changes.**

**The five closes are worth examining one by one, and I give this as my own reading: the closes form a sequence.**

| No. | The close | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *yaʿdilūn* | **Swerving** — setting up an equal |
| 2 | *lā yaʿlamūn* | **Ignorance** |
| 3 | *qalīlan mā tadhakkarūn* | **Failure to remember** — the knowledge is there, it is not recalled |
| 4 | *taʿāla'llāhu ʿammā yushrikūn* | **Transcendence** — the subject is turned to God |
| 5 | *hātū burhānakum* | **A demand for evidence** — the close of the argument |

**The direction of the sequence is worth recording:** the first three describe **the state of those addressed**, the fourth **turns the subject to God**, the fifth **directs a demand at those addressed.** **The sequence thus begins in description and arrives at a demand.**

**And it was recorded above that the fifth (*hātū burhānakum*) belongs to the chain of demands for evidence set out in the table in `028-kasas.md` at 28/75.**

### حَدَآئِقَ ذَاتَ بَهْجَةٍ

The root **ب-ه-ج** was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/7, and **this verse was already cited there.** **I do not repeat it here.** The core of the root: **a beauty that opens the heart of the one who sees it, a gladdening appearance.**

**حَدِيقَة** — plural *ḥadāʾiq*: **a walled garden.** **The lexicographers attach the word to the sense "to encircle, to enclose" in the root ح-د-ق** — *ḥadaqa*, the dark ring of the eye, is from this root as well.

**I record this as an observation about the word:** the verse names not open country but **an enclosed garden** — a place a person has laboured over and whose boundary he has drawn.

**And at exactly this point the sentence sets its limit: *mā kāna lakum an tunbitū shajarahā* — "it was not for you to make its trees grow."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two clauses:** the verse does not deny human labour — **it names the garden as an enclosed garden.** **The place where it sets the limit is the verb of making grow.** **The distinction thus lies not in the question "who worked" but in "who made it grow".**

**And the word order must be recorded: the person changes in the middle of the verbs.** *Am man khalaqa… wa anzala…* — **third person**; then *fa-**anbatnā** bihī* — **first person plural.**

**In Arabic this is called *iltifāt* (a shift in the direction of address).** **And it occurs precisely at the verb of making grow** — that is, at the verb where the sentence sets its limit. I record this as an observation about the word order.

### يَعْدِلُونَ

The root **ع-د-ل**: **to be equal, to make equal.** **The word carries both the sense of "justice" and the sense of "treating as equal, setting up as a partner", and here it is the second.**

**The lexicographers record the distinction thus: *ʿadala bihī* — he made it equal to something else; *ʿadala fīhi* — he acted justly in it.** **The particle settles the meaning.**

**I record this as an observation about the word:** the same root can name both justice and the setting up of partners, **because both are acts of making equal.** **The difference lies in what is being made equal to what.**

## 27/61 — أَمَّن جَعَلَ ٱلْأَرْضَ قَرَارًا وَجَعَلَ خِلَٰلَهَآ أَنْهَٰرًا وَجَعَلَ لَهَا رَوَٰسِىَ وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَ ٱلْبَحْرَيْنِ حَاجِزًا أَءِلَٰهٌ مَّعَ ٱللَّهِ بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*am man jaʿala'l-arḍa qarāran wa jaʿala khilālahā anhāran wa jaʿala lahā rawāsiya wa jaʿala bayna'l-baḥrayni ḥājizā, a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh, bal aktharuhum lā yaʿlamūn*

"**Or He who made the earth a place of settlement, and made rivers run through it, and set firm mountains upon it, and set a barrier between the two seas? Is there a god beside God? No — most of them do not know.**"

### Four times: جَعَلَ

**The verse consists of four clauses, and all four begin with the same verb: *jaʿala*.**

| No. | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *jaʿala'l-arḍa **qarārā*** | **A place to settle** |
| 2 | *jaʿala khilālahā **anhārā*** | **Rivers** — running through it |
| 3 | *jaʿala lahā **rawāsiya*** | **Firm mountains** |
| 4 | *jaʿala bayna'l-baḥrayni **ḥājizā*** | **A barrier** — between the two seas |

**The fourfold repetition of the verb is a datum of the word order**, and in each of the four the object changes. **I record this as an observation:** the verse gathers four separate facts under a single verb.

**قَرَار** — root **ق-ر-ر**: **to stay in a place, to settle, to become fixed.** **The word was treated in `054-kamer.md`, `071-nuh.md` and `036-yasin.md`, and this verse was cited there.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**رَوَاسِى** — root **ر-س-و**: **to cast anchor, to stand fixed.** *Rāsiya* — that which is driven into its place. **The word was mentioned in the discussion of `081-tekvir.md`, where the descriptions of mountains were set out in a table.** **And that table will serve again at verse 88 of this sūra; I shall return to it there.**

**حَاجِز** — root **ح-ج-ز**: **to separate, to set a barrier between, to hold back.** **The root was treated in `069-hakka.md` at 69/47, and this verse was already cited there.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the same fact occurred with the word *barzakh* in `055-rahman.md` at 55/19-20, and it was treated there.** **The two sūras name the same thing with two different words: *ḥājiz* and *barzakh*.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I draw a limit:** I build **no claim of a miracle out of the findings of modern ocean science** regarding the barrier between the two seas. **The language of the verse points to something that can be seen with the eye** — places where fresh water and salt water do not mix, the boundaries of currents — **and this is an observation that a listener living in the desert could verify as well.** **I record it as a point of view, not as a proof.**

### بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

**And the close must be recorded: *aktharuhum* — "most of them", not all.**

**The sūra's ع-ل-م spine passes here to the negative side.** **It was recorded in the spine table above: at verses 15, 16, 22, 40 and 42 knowledge was being given; at 61 and 84 its absence is reported.**

## 27/62 — أَمَّن يُجِيبُ ٱلْمُضْطَرَّ إِذَا دَعَاهُ وَيَكْشِفُ ٱلسُّوٓءَ وَيَجْعَلُكُمْ خُلَفَآءَ ٱلْأَرْضِ أَءِلَٰهٌ مَّعَ ٱللَّهِ قَلِيلًا مَّا تَذَكَّرُونَ

*am man yujību'l-muḍṭarra idhā daʿāhu wa yakshifu's-sūʾa wa yajʿalukum khulafāʾa'l-arḍ, a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh, qalīlan mā tadhakkarūn*

"**Or He who answers the one in dire need when he calls upon Him, and removes the harm, and makes you successors in the earth? Is there a god beside God? How little you reflect!**"

### ٱلْمُضْطَرّ — root ض-ر-ر

**This word stands at the centre of the sequence of five questions and deserves to be treated in detail.**

**The concrete sense of the root: *ḍarr* — harm, distress, a state that breaks the working of a thing.** **The root occurred in the discussion of `031-lokman.md` at 31/6 and in `002-bakara.md`.**

**The pattern of the word is decisive: *muḍṭarr* — a passive participle from Form VIII (*iḍṭirār*).**

| Pattern | Word | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal noun | *ḍarr* | **Harm** — the fact |
| **Form VIII verbal noun** | *iḍṭirār* | **Being forced** — being driven into helplessness |
| **Form VIII passive participle** | ***muḍṭarr*** | **One who has been forced** — left without recourse |

**The most important property of the pattern is that it faces the passive, and I record this as my own reading:** *muḍṭarr* is **not one who falls into distress of himself but one who has been pressed into it.** **The word is thus not a state but the end of a being driven.**

**The lexicographers describe the word thus, and I transmit it as a report: one who has no other door left to him.** **Indeed the same word is used in the language of law for "a person in a state of necessity".**

**And how the word comes in this verse must be recorded: *al-muḍṭarr* — definite, that is, as a class.** **The one who is answered is not a particular person but anyone in that condition.**

### Its link with sincerity at sea

**The same fact was treated twice, and set out in a table, in `029-ankebut.md` at 29/65 and in `031-lokman.md` at 31/32.** **The summary was this:** a person who boards a ship, or who is covered over by the waves, calls upon Him **making his religion sincerely His alone**; **once ashore, in Luqmān some of them take a middle course, and in al-ʿAnkabūt they associate partners with Him.**

**The three verses can now be set side by side, and the comparison can be verified from the text:**

| | Luqmān 31/32 | al-ʿAnkabūt 29/65 | **al-Naml 27/62** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | **Those at sea** | **Those on the ship** | ***al-muḍṭarr*** — in general |
| The situation | *ghashiyahum mawj* | *rakibū fi'l-fulk* | *idhā daʿāh* — when he calls |
| Direction of the telling | **The human act** — he calls | **The human act** — he calls | **The divine act** — He answers |
| The outcome | *fa-minhum muqtaṣid* | *idhā hum yushrikūn* | *wa yakshifu's-sūʾ* |

**And the difference here is recorded as my own reading, its ground being the third row of the table:** Luqmān and al-ʿAnkabūt told the same fact as **a human behaviour** — **and both ended in criticism.** **Al-Naml tells the same fact from the other side, from *the side that answers*,** and builds not a criticism but **a proof.**

**The three sūras thus record the same truth: when a person is left without recourse he turns to Him alone.** **The difference lies in what this truth is used for:**

| Sūra | What the same fact is used for |
|---|---|
| Luqmān 31/32 | **A diagnosis of inconsistency** — changing once delivered |
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/65 | **The same diagnosis, sharper** |
| **al-Naml 27/62** | **A proof** — that there is one who answers |

**This is a weave among three sūras that can be verified.**

**And a distinction on this matter was recorded in `044-duhan.md` at 44/12-15, and it applies directly here; I rely on that treatment:** **turning to God in a moment of distress is not what the verses criticise** — **what is criticised is the break between the reason for the turning and its outcome.** **And this very verse was cited there as evidence.**

### وَيَجْعَلُكُمْ خُلَفَآءَ ٱلْأَرْضِ

The root **خ-ل-ف**: **to come after someone, to take his place.** **The root was treated in `038-sad.md` at 38/26 and in `035-fatir.md` at 35/39.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the clause's place in the verse is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:**

| No. | Expression | The human position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *yujību'l-**muḍṭarr*** | **At the weakest** — without recourse |
| 2 | *wa yakshifu's-sūʾ* | **The lifting of the distress** |
| 3 | *wa yajʿalukum **khulafāʾa'l-arḍ*** | **At the most authoritative** — holding sway in the earth |

**Three clauses carry the human being from the lowest point to the highest position — and the subject of all three is the same.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the three clauses stand in a single chain of verbs:** the verse ties helplessness and authority **to the same source.** **Succession is thus named not as the opposite of helplessness but as a second condition coming from the same hand.**

### قَلِيلًا مَّا تَذَكَّرُونَ

**And the close must be recorded: *tadhakkarūn* — the root ذ-ك-ر, to remember.**

**The close of the preceding verse was *lā yaʿlamūn* ("they do not know"); the close of this one is *qalīlan mā tadhakkarūn* ("you remember but little").**

**I record this as an observation about the word order, and the difference is meaningful:** the condition of helplessness is **not something unknown** — everyone has lived through it. **For this reason the verse speaks not of ignorance but of failing to remember.**

**And the change of person must be recorded: the preceding sentences used the third person (*aktharuhum*); here it passes to the second person (*tadhakkarūn*).** **This is *iltifāt*, and it occurs at precisely the most personal item.** I record this as an observation about the word order.

### What this says to the present

**What the verse concretely records is this: helplessness is a source of knowledge.**

**When a person becomes *muḍṭarr*, he sees which of the supports that carry him in everyday life are not really supports at all.** **And the verse does not make this moment a matter of criticism — it records it as evidence.**

**What is to be recorded is the closing sentence: *qalīlan mā tadhakkarūn*.** **The matter is thus not the living through of that moment but the remembering of it once it has passed.** I give this as my own reading; its ground is the verse's own close.

## 27/63 — أَمَّن يَهْدِيكُمْ فِى ظُلُمَٰتِ ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ وَمَن يُرْسِلُ ٱلرِّيَٰحَ بُشْرًۢا بَيْنَ يَدَىْ رَحْمَتِهِۦٓ أَءِلَٰهٌ مَّعَ ٱللَّهِ تَعَٰلَى ٱللَّهُ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

*am man yahdīkum fī ẓulumāti'l-barri wa'l-baḥri wa man yursilu'r-riyāḥa bushran bayna yaday raḥmatih, a-ilāhun maʿa'llāh, taʿāla'llāhu ʿammā yushrikūn*

"**Or He who guides you through the darknesses of land and sea, and who sends the winds as bearers of glad tidings before His mercy? Is there a god beside God? Exalted is God above what they associate with Him.**"

### ظُلُمَٰتِ ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْر

**The construction must be recorded: *ẓulumāt* is a plural — "darknesses."**

**The lexicographers record that the plural here covers three things at once:** the darkness of the night, the darkness of cloud, the depth of the sea — **that is, not a single darkness but darknesses laid one upon another.**

**And that the two domains are named together is a datum of the word order: *al-barr* and *al-baḥr*.** **Guidance is thus named for land as well as for sea.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse looks not at stars or at signs but directly at **the act of guiding**. **No instrument is named.**

**And the proofs drawn from travel by sea were treated in `036-yasin.md` at 36/40 and in `045-casiye.md` at 45/12.** **I rely on that treatment.**

### ٱلرِّيَٰحَ بُشْرًۢا بَيْنَ يَدَىْ رَحْمَتِهِ

**بُشْرًا** — root **ب-ش-ر**, the same root as the *bushrā* of the sūra's second verse.

| Verse | Word | Bearer of glad tidings of what |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | *bushrā* | **The Book** — for the believers |
| **63** | *bushrā / bushran* | **The winds** — before the rain |

**The same root, at the two ends of the sūra, once for revelation and once for the natural world.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**بَيْنَ يَدَىْ** — word for word "between his two hands"; as an idiom, ***before, ahead of.*** **The pattern was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/42.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And what the word *raḥmatihī* stands for must be recorded: in this context, the rain.** **The verse thus does not name the rain directly — it says *raḥma*.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence names the cause (the wind) by its name and the outcome (the rain) by its quality. **And at verse 58 the same thing coming from the sky was called *maṭar* (the rain of punishment).** **The sūra names the same phenomenon with two different words in two different places.**

### تَعَٰلَى ٱللَّهُ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

**The fourth close differs from the others, and this is a datum of the word order: this close looks not at those addressed but at the subject.**

***Taʿālā*** — root **ع-ل-و.** **And the sūra's line of *ʿuluww* occurs here for the third time:**

| Verse | Expression | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | *ẓulman wa **ʿuluwwā*** | **Man** — condemned |
| 31 | *allā **taʿlū** ʿalayya* | **Man** — forbidden |
| **63** | ***taʿāla'llāh*** | **God** — where it belongs |

**The same root, in three different places in the sūra, twice condemned and once ascribed.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the three occurrences themselves:** the root by itself conveys no verdict. **What is decisive is to whom it is ascribed.** **And this has the same structure as the finding recorded for the word *imāma* in `028-kasas.md` at 28/41:** there too the word by itself conveyed no value.

## 27/64 — أَمَّن يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ ثُمَّ يُعِيدُهُۥ وَمَن يَرْزُقُكُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ أَءِلَٰهٌ مَّعَ ٱللَّهِ قُلْ هَاتُوا۟ بُرْهَٰنَكُمْ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ

### يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ ثُمَّ يُعِيدُهُ

## 27/65-66 — قُل لَّا يَعْلَمُ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْغَيْبَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ أَيَّانَ يُبْعَثُونَ · بَلِ ٱدَّٰرَكَ عِلْمُهُمْ فِى ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ بَلْ هُمْ فِى شَكٍّ مِّنْهَا بَلْ هُم مِّنْهَا عَمُونَ

*qul lā yaʿlamu man fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi'l-ghayba illā'llāh, wa mā yashʿurūna ayyāna yubʿathūn · bali'ddāraka ʿilmuhum fi'l-ākhirati, bal hum fī shakkin minhā, bal hum minhā ʿamūn*

"**Say: No one in the heavens or the earth knows the unseen, save God. Nor do they know when they will be raised. No — their knowledge concerning the hereafter has run itself out. No — they are in doubt of it. No — they are blind to it.**"

### The sūra's spine of knowledge is tied off here

**This verse is the ruling that the sūra's whole line of knowledge has been moving towards, and it was set out in the spine table above.**

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded, and the point of word order lies here:**

***Lā yaʿlamu man fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi'l-ghayba illā'llāh.***

**The negation thus takes in all beings first, and the exception comes after.** **And the scope of the negation is striking: *man fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* — everyone in the heavens and the earth.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence does not say that part of knowledge is closed to human beings — **it says that it is open to no created thing.** **The angels fall within this scope, and the jinn as well** — and indeed an ʿifrīt of the jinn had spoken in this sūra (39).

**And this ruling was treated in `072-cin.md` at 72/26, and this verse was already cited there.** **I do not repeat it; I rely on that treatment.**

### The line drawn with Luqmān 31/34

**Five items were set out in a table in `031-lokman.md` at 31/34, and a limit was drawn there. That limit holds here too, and I repeat it, because this verse is even more open to the same misreading:**

> **To read this verse as a claim of scientific impossibility runs against the text's own word order.**

**The two verses can be set side by side:**

| | Luqmān 31/34 | **al-Naml 27/65** |
|---|---|---|
| Form | **A list** — five items | **A single ruling** — all-embracing |
| Scope | *ʿindahū ʿilmu's-sāʿa* + four items | *al-ghayb* — the whole of it |
| Who is excluded | **The human being** — *mā tadrī nafsun* | **Everyone in the heavens and the earth** |
| In common | **The knowledge of the Hour** | **And *ayyāna yubʿathūn*** |

**The two verses give the same ruling at two different breadths.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**And the distinction recorded in `031-lokman.md` works here, and I rely on that treatment:** the verse speaks **not of measurement but of *knowledge*.** **The word "unseen" is thus not a list of things that are unknown today.**

**I record the sense of the word once more:** the root **غ-ي-ب** — **to pass out of sight, to remain covered.** *Ghayba* — a thing's leaving the field of vision. ***Ghayb* is therefore not "what cannot be known" but "what is closed to the senses"**, and what falls within that scope is settled by the verses' own qualifications.

**I draw one further limit, and per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write it out plainly:** on the basis of this verse I **issue no verdict about any particular science or field of research.** **The verse reports to whom the unseen belongs; it does not regulate what a human being may investigate.**

### وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ أَيَّانَ يُبْعَثُونَ

**And the sūra's third spine (ش-ع-ر) occurs here for the third and last time.**

**This pattern was mentioned in the discussion of `079-naziat.md`** (*ayyāna yubʿathūn*). **And the particle *ayyāna* must be recorded: "when" — but in Arabic this particle is used of a time that is distant and indefinite.**

**The order of the three occurrences must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:**

| Verse | Who | What goes unnoticed | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | Solomon and his armies | **A living thing beneath their feet** | **A single step** |
| 50 | The nine men | **That their own plot has turned back on them** | **A plan** |
| **65** | **Everyone** | **The time when they will be raised** | **The whole of time** |

**The scale grows each time, and at the last occurrence no one is left outside it.** **The ground is the subjects of the three verses.**

### بَلِ ٱدَّٰرَكَ — three times: بَلْ

**Verse 66 is built with three *bal* particles, and each annuls the one before it:**

| No. | Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *bali'ddāraka ʿilmuhum fi'l-ākhira* | **A matter of knowledge** |
| 2 | *bal hum fī **shakkin** minhā* | **Doubt** |
| 3 | *bal hum minhā **ʿamūn*** | **Blindness** |

**The particle *bal* in Arabic conveys *iḍrāb*: leaving the preceding statement and passing to a new one.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the three steps **descend one after another** — knowledge, doubt, blindness. **And the last step has to do not with knowing but with seeing.**

There is a disagreement in the classical commentaries about what **ٱدَّٰرَكَ** means, and it is not concealed:

| Reading | What *iddāraka ʿilmuhum* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Their knowledge has run out, it did not reach so far** — from the sense of the verb *tadāraka*, "to follow one upon another and come to an end" |
| 2 | **Their knowledge will be completed in the hereafter** — there they will see and know |
| 3 | **As a question** — "has their knowledge reached as far as the hereafter?" (in some variant readings) |

**All three readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record an observation, not a preference:** what follows in the verse brings two further negative steps (*shakk* and *ʿamā*). **This seems to suit the first reading better; but the second has been defended as well.**

**عَمُون** — root **ع-م-ي**: blindness. **And it is not the same as the *yaʿmahūn* of the sūra's fourth verse (ع-م-ه); it is a separate root** — **this was recorded above, and no derivational link was claimed.**

| Verse | Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | *yaʿmahūn* | **ع-م-ه** | **Seeing but finding no direction** |
| 66 | *ʿamūn* | **ع-م-ي** | **Not seeing** |

**In its opening the sūra records the first state, and at verse 66 the second.** I give this as an observation; I draw no link of derivation between the two roots.

## 27/67-70 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ أَءِذَا كُنَّا تُرَٰبًا وَءَابَآؤُنَآ أَئِنَّا لَمُخْرَجُونَ · لَقَدْ وُعِدْنَا هَٰذَا نَحْنُ وَءَابَآؤُنَا مِن قَبْلُ إِنْ هَٰذَآ إِلَّآ أَسَٰطِيرُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ · قُلْ سِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَٱنظُرُوا۟ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ · وَلَا تَحْزَنْ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا تَكُن فِى ضَيْقٍ مِّمَّا يَمْكُرُونَ

*wa qāla'lladhīna kafarū a-idhā kunnā turāban wa ābāʾunā a-innā la-mukhrajūn · laqad wuʿidnā hādhā naḥnu wa ābāʾunā min qablu in hādhā illā asāṭīru'l-awwalīn · qul sīrū fi'l-arḍi fa'nẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'l-mujrimīn · wa lā taḥzan ʿalayhim wa lā takun fī ḍayqin mimmā yamkurūn*

"**Those who deny said: 'When we and our fathers have become dust, are we really to be brought forth? This was promised to us and to our fathers before; it is nothing but the tales of the ancients.' Say: 'Travel through the earth and see how the end of the guilty came about.' Do not grieve over them, and do not be pressed in by what they plot.**"

### The structure of the objection

**The objection has two steps, and the two are of different kinds:**

| Step | Expression | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *a-idhā kunnā turāban… a-innā la-mukhrajūn* | **A claim of impossibility** — how could that be |
| 2 | *laqad wuʿidnā hādhā naḥnu wa ābāʾunā min qabl* | **A historical claim** — it is said, but it does not happen |

**The second step is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** what is offered as evidence is **the age of the promise**. **That is: "this has been said for a long time and has not come about."**

**And the reverse form of this structure was treated in `046-ahkaf.md` at 46/17** — there the man who objects to his parents said *qad khalati'l-qurūnu min qablī* ("many generations have passed before me"). **And it was recorded there: what is offered as evidence is *the fact that it has not happened*.** **The same structure is repeated here.**

**أَسَٰطِير** — root **س-ط-ر**: **to write in lines, in rows.** *Asṭār* — lines; *asāṭīr* — old accounts written down and handed on. **The root was treated in `068-kalem.md` at 68/1 and in `052-tur.md` at 52/2-3.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The choice of word must be recorded:** the objection reckons the message not false but **old**. **The rejection is thus built not on truth but on provenance.**

### قُلْ سِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَٱنظُرُوا۟

**And the answer meets a claim about history with evidence from history.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses:** the objection said "this has been said for ages and did not happen"; **the answer says "travel and look at what did happen."** **The answer is thus given on the same plane — in the past.**

**And this method was treated in `035-fatir.md` at 35/44 and in `040-gafir.md` at 40/21 and 40/82:** the evidence is brought not from a remote history but **from the ruins along the road.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And there is a link within the sūra itself, and it must be recorded:** verse 52 had said *fa-tilka buyūtuhum khāwiyatan* — **that the ruins can be seen.** **Now verse 69 commands travel.**

| Verse | Expression | Its function |
|---|---|---|
| **52** | *fa-**tilka** buyūtuhum khāwiyatan* | **Showing** — a demonstrative |
| **69** | *qul **sīrū** fi'l-arḍi fa'nẓurū* | **A command** — go and look |

**Two verses use the same evidence, one by pointing to it and one by commanding it.** This is a link that can be verified within the sūra.

**And the verb *fa'nẓurū* is the last link in the sūra's sequence of looking** — it was set out in a table above, at verses 27-28.

### وَلَا تَحْزَنْ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا تَكُن فِى ضَيْقٍ

**These two prohibitions have been treated many times in this series, and were set out in a table in the discussion of `028-kasas.md` at 28/56.** **Six different words from six sūras were set side by side there** (*balāgh*, *wakīl*, *jabbār*, *ḥasarāt*, *fa-lā yaḥzunka*, *man aḥbabta*).

**This verse of al-Naml belongs to the same chain and adds a row to the table:**

| Place | Expression | What it limits |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/35 | *balāgh* | **The task** |
| al-Zumar 39/41 | *wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl* | **Responsibility** |
| Qāf 50/45 | *wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-jabbār* | **Authority to compel** |
| Fāṭir 35/8 | *fa-lā tadhhab nafsuka ʿalayhim ḥasarāt* | **Grief** |
| Luqmān 31/23 | *fa-lā yaḥzunka kufruh* | **Grief** |
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/56 | *lā tahdī man aḥbabta* | **Love** |
| **al-Naml 27/70** | *wa lā taḥzan… wa lā takun fī **ḍayqin** mimmā yamkurūn* | **Grief + constriction** |

**What belongs to al-Naml is the second word: *ḍayq*.**

The root **ض-ي-ق**: **narrowness, being pressed in.** **The concrete sense of the word is the narrowness of a place**, and from this, the tightening of the chest, distress.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two prohibitions are attached to different things:**

| Prohibition | What it attaches to | Its source |
|---|---|---|
| *lā taḥzan* | ***ʿalayhim*** — over them | **People** — do not grieve over their state |
| *lā takun fī ḍayq* | ***mimmā yamkurūn*** — at the plot they lay | **Their acts** — do not be pressed in by their plans |

**Two different sources, two different prohibitions.** **The verse thus releases the messenger from two separate burdens separately: the grief that comes from people's state, and the constriction that comes from their plans.**

**And the verb *yamkurūn* must be recorded: the root م-ك-ر had occurred at verses 50-51 of the sūra.** **There those who laid the plot were destroyed; here the Prophet is told not to be pressed in by that plot.** **The link between the two verses is the repetition of the root.**

## 27/71-75 — وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ · قُلْ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُونَ رَدِفَ لَكُم بَعْضُ ٱلَّذِى تَسْتَعْجِلُونَ · وَإِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَذُو فَضْلٍ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَهُمْ لَا يَشْكُرُونَ · وَإِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَيَعْلَمُ مَا تُكِنُّ صُدُورُهُمْ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ · وَمَا مِنْ غَآئِبَةٍ فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ إِلَّا فِى كِتَٰبٍ مُّبِينٍ

*wa yaqūlūna matā hādha'l-waʿdu in kuntum ṣādiqīn · qul ʿasā an yakūna radifa lakum baʿḍu'lladhī tastaʿjilūn · wa inna rabbaka la-dhū faḍlin ʿala'n-nāsi wa lākinna aktharahum lā yashkurūn · wa inna rabbaka la-yaʿlamu mā tukinnu ṣudūruhum wa mā yuʿlinūn · wa mā min ghāʾibatin fi's-samāʾi wa'l-arḍi illā fī kitābin mubīn*

"**They say: 'When is this promise, if you are telling the truth?' Say: 'It may be that some of what you seek to hasten is close behind you.' Your Lord is indeed bountiful towards people, but most of them are not thankful. And your Lord knows what their breasts conceal and what they declare. There is nothing hidden in the sky or the earth but it is in a clear Book.**"

### رَدِفَ لَكُم

The root **ر-د-ف** was worked out in `079-naziat.md` at 79/7: **to come behind someone; and in particular to ride behind him.** *Radīf* — the one who rides behind on a mount. **And this verse was already cited in that file.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The concrete picture of the root works here, and I record it as an observation:** the *radīf* is the one whom the rider in front **cannot see** but who is **immediately behind him.** **The answer reports not the distance of what is asked for but the fact that it is not seen.**

**And the construction of the sentence must be recorded: three hedges are laid one on top of another.**

| Hedge | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *ʿasā* | **"It may be"** — no certainty |
| 2 | *an yakūna* | **The possibility of its being so** |
| 3 | *baʿḍu'lladhī* | **"Some of"** — not all |

**The question asks "when"; the answer neither gives a date nor refuses one.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the three hedges:** the answer **changes the kind of the question.** **The time of the promise is not given; its nearness is.** **And this agrees with the ruling at verse 65:** *wa mā yashʿurūna ayyāna yubʿathūn* — the knowledge of the hour had been given to no one.

### وَإِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَذُو فَضْلٍ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ

**And that the sentence is placed here is worth recording.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** so that the delay of the punishment should not be read as a weakness or an indifference, **a statement of bounty follows immediately.** **The same structure was treated in `035-fatir.md` at 35/44-45:** there too a statement of power and a decision to defer were set side by side. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the close must be recorded: *wa lākinna aktharahum lā yashkurūn*.** **The delay is thus a bounty, and the return due to a bounty is thanks — but it is not given.**

### مَا تُكِنُّ صُدُورُهُمْ

The root **ك-ن-ن**: **to cover, to keep hidden inside a vessel.** *Kinān* — a cover; *maknūn* — kept hidden.

**And the subject of the verb must be recorded: *ṣudūruhum* — their breasts.** **What conceals is thus not the person but the breast itself.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence describes concealment not as an act of the will but as a weight the breast carries. **And *mā yuʿlinūn* (what they declare) is set opposite it.**

**The same pair had occurred at verse 25 of the sūra — and there it was the hoopoe who spoke:**

| Verse | Expression | Who speaks |
|---|---|---|
| **25** | *wa yaʿlamu mā **tukhfūna** wa mā **tuʿlinūn*** | **The hoopoe** — about the people of Sabaʾ |
| **74** | *la-yaʿlamu mā **tukinnu** ṣudūruhum wa mā **yuʿlinūn*** | **The sūra itself** — about those it addresses |

**Both verses have the same pair, and in the second element the word is exactly the same: *yuʿlinūn*.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the overlap of the two verses:** the measure spoken by a bird inside the narrative is applied, once the narrative is over, **directly to those addressed.** **The sūra carries a sentence from inside its narrative out of it.**

### وَمَا مِنْ غَآئِبَةٍ … إِلَّا فِى كِتَٰبٍ مُّبِينٍ

**غَآئِبَة** — root **غ-ي-ب**, the same root as the *al-ghayb* of verse 65. **And the lexicographers dwell on its coming in the feminine form:** either it conveys intensity (**what is deeply hidden**) or it is the adjective of an elided noun (**everything that is hidden**).

**And the construction must be recorded: *fī kitābin mubīn* — "in a clear Book."**

**The same construction had occurred in the sūra's first verse:**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *āyātu'l-qurʾāni wa **kitābin mubīn*** | **The Book sent down** |
| **75** | *illā fī **kitābin mubīn*** | **A record** — where what is hidden is kept |

**The same construction, at the two ends of the sūra, with two different referents.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as an observation, and I make no claim that the two books are the same.** **Different views on what the *kitāb* of verse 75 is are transmitted in the classical commentaries** (the Preserved Tablet, the divine knowledge, the register of deeds) **and I do not impose a preference.** **What I record is that the same construction occurs at the two ends of the sūra.**

**And its relation to verse 65 must be recorded, and I write plainly that there is no contradiction:** verse 65 says **who knows** the unseen; **verse 75 says that the unseen is recorded.** **The two are two faces of the same ruling:** there is a record, but access to that record is His alone.

## 27/76-77 — إِنَّ هَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ يَقُصُّ عَلَىٰ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ أَكْثَرَ ٱلَّذِى هُمْ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ · وَإِنَّهُۥ لَهُدًى وَرَحْمَةٌ لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

*inna hādha'l-qurʾāna yaquṣṣu ʿalā banī isrāʾīla akthara'lladhī hum fīhi yakhtalifūn · wa innahū la-hudan wa raḥmatun li'l-muʾminīn*

"**This Qurʾān relates to the Children of Israel most of what they differ about. And it is a guidance and a mercy for those who believe.**"

### يَقُصُّ — root ق-ص-ص

**The concrete sense of the root: to follow a track.** *Qaṣṣa'l-athar* — he followed the trail. **From this, *qiṣṣa*: the telling of an event by following its trail.**

**The root is the name of `028-kasas.md` and was treated at the head of that sūra.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the tense of the verb must be recorded: *yaquṣṣu* — imperfect, ongoing.** **Not "it related" but "it relates".**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence reports this work of the Book not as a completed event but as **a work under way** — **just like the *tulaqqā* of verse 6.** **Both verses use the imperfect.**

### أَكْثَرَ ٱلَّذِى هُمْ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ

**The qualification must be recorded: *akthara* — "most of", not all.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence limits the scope. **And the limitation does not weaken the claim — it makes it precise:** the Book does not undertake to settle all of those disagreements.

**اخْتِلاف** — root **خ-ل-ف**, Form VIII: **to fall into disagreement with one another.** **The root was treated in `042-sura.md` at 42/10, and the difference in ruling between *ikhtilāf* and *tafarruq* was worked out there.** **I do not repeat it; I rely on that treatment.**

**And the choice of addressee must be recorded: *ʿalā banī isrāʾīl*.**

**I record this as an observation and issue no blanket verdict:** the verse names a community by way of **its own internal disagreements.** **Per the method (`STYLE.md`): no blanket verdict is issued about an ethnic or religious group;** what the verse describes is what a text does to another community's disputes.

**And the sūra's own context must be recorded:** in its narratives al-Naml told of Moses, of David, of Solomon, of Lot. **This verse also reports whom those narratives address.**

### وَإِنَّهُۥ لَهُدًى وَرَحْمَةٌ لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

**And the sentence meets the sūra's second verse:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **2** | *hudan wa **bushrā** li'l-muʾminīn* |
| **77** | *la-hudan wa **raḥmatun** li'l-muʾminīn* |

**In both verses *hudan* and *li'l-muʾminīn* are the same; the word in the middle differs.** This is a repetition and a difference that can be verified within the sūra.

**And there was a third combination in `031-lokman.md` at 31/3: *hudan wa raḥmatan li'l-muḥsinīn*.**

| Place | The construction |
|---|---|
| al-Naml 27/2 | *hudan wa **bushrā** li'l-**muʾminīn*** |
| **al-Naml 27/77** | *hudan wa **raḥmatun** li'l-**muʾminīn*** |
| Luqmān 31/3 | *hudan wa **raḥmatan** li'l-**muḥsinīn*** |

**Three constructions, two sūras.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is al-Naml's own two verses:** in its opening the sūra names **glad tidings**, and once the narratives are done it names **mercy**. **The promise given at the beginning is thus repeated at the end as an attribute.**

## 27/78-79 — إِنَّ رَبَّكَ يَقْضِى بَيْنَهُم بِحُكْمِهِۦ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ إِنَّكَ عَلَى ٱلْحَقِّ ٱلْمُبِينِ

*inna rabbaka yaqḍī baynahum bi-ḥukmih, wa huwa'l-ʿazīzu'l-ʿalīm · fa-tawakkal ʿala'llāh, innaka ʿala'l-ḥaqqi'l-mubīn*

"**Your Lord will decide between them by His own judgement. He is the Mighty, the Knowing. So rely upon God; you stand upon the manifest truth.**"

### ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْعَلِيم — and its link with verse 9

**These two names had occurred earlier in the sūra as well, and the comparison is a datum of the text:**

| Verse | The names | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *ḥakīmin **ʿalīm*** | **The opening** — the source of the Book |
| **9** | *al-**ʿAzīz**u'l-Ḥakīm* | **The call to Moses** |
| **78** | *al-**ʿAzīz**u'l-**ʿAlīm*** | **The statement of judgement** |

**Three places, three pairs, and all three built from the same three names: ʿAzīz, Ḥakīm, ʿAlīm.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sūra uses these three names in three different contexts, two at a time. **And the last pair (*ʿAzīz–ʿAlīm*) comes after the sentence about judgement: the one who judges both has the power and knows.**

**بِحُكْمِهِ** — the word order must be recorded: the judgement is given by His own judgement. **The measure does not come from outside.**

### فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ

The root **و-ك-ل**: **to refer a matter to another, to appoint an agent.** **The root was treated in the discussion of `042-sura.md` at 42/36, and there was a finding there: reliance and consultation stood in the same list.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the place of the command must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** the command *fa-tawakkal* comes **immediately after it has been reported that the judgement belongs to God.** **The particle *fāʾ* conveys consequence.** **Reliance is thus commanded not as a consolation but as what a ruling requires.**

**إِنَّكَ عَلَى ٱلْحَقِّ ٱلْمُبِينِ** — and a ground is given.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence does not say "you are right" — **it says *ʿala'l-ḥaqq***, that is, as **a ground one stands upon.** **The preposition (*ʿalā*) conveys standing on top of something.**

**And the adjective *al-mubīn* is from the same root as the *kitābin mubīn* of the sūra's first verse (ب-ي-ن).** **The root occurs six times in the sūra: 1 (*kitābin mubīn*), 13 (*siḥrun mubīn*), 16 (*al-faḍlu'l-mubīn*), 21 (*sulṭānin mubīn*), 75 (*kitābin mubīn*), 79 (*al-ḥaqqi'l-mubīn*).**

**The distribution of the six occurrences is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the same adjective is used for **a book**, **an accusation of sorcery**, **a favour**, **a proof**, **a record** and **the truth**. **The adjective thus takes no side — it conveys plainness.** **Indeed at verse 13 those who deny use the same adjective: *hādhā siḥrun mubīn*.**

## 27/80-81 — إِنَّكَ لَا تُسْمِعُ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ وَلَا تُسْمِعُ ٱلصُّمَّ ٱلدُّعَآءَ إِذَا وَلَّوْا۟ مُدْبِرِينَ · وَمَآ أَنتَ بِهَٰدِى ٱلْعُمْىِ عَن ضَلَٰلَتِهِمْ إِن تُسْمِعُ إِلَّا مَن يُؤْمِنُ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا فَهُم مُّسْلِمُونَ

*innaka lā tusmiʿu'l-mawtā wa lā tusmiʿu'ṣ-ṣumma'd-duʿāʾa idhā wallaw mudbirīn · wa mā anta bi-hādi'l-ʿumyi ʿan ḍalālatihim, in tusmiʿu illā man yuʾminu bi-āyātinā fa-hum muslimūn*

"**You cannot make the dead hear; nor can you make the deaf hear the call when they turn their backs and go. Nor can you lead the blind out of their straying. You can make none hear but those who believe in Our signs; and they are the ones who have submitted.**"

### Three obstacles

**The verse enumerates three types, and all three are named by way of a sense:**

| Type | Expression | The obstacle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *al-mawtā* | **The dead** — they do not hear at all |
| 2 | *al-ṣumm* | **The deaf** — no hearing |
| 3 | *al-ʿumy* | **The blind** — no sight |

**And a qualification is added to the second type: *idhā wallaw mudbirīn* — "when they turn their backs and go."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the qualification is added only to the second:** a deaf person standing before you **can still be reached** — by the lips, by a sign, by touch. **Once he has turned his back and gone, even that is gone.** **The qualification thus completes the impossibility.**

**And almost these same verses occur in `030-rum.md` at 30/52-53, and they were treated there.** **The two sūras enumerate the same three obstacles in the same order.** **I do not repeat it; I rely on that treatment.**

### A limit — how these verses are not to be read

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write it out plainly:**

**These verses are not a sentence of judgement sorting people into "the dead, the deaf and the blind".** **The subject of the sentence is the Prophet, and what is said is *the limit of his power*** — indeed the verbs are his: *lā tusmiʿu*, *mā anta bi-hādī*.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the subject of the verbs:** the verse reports less the state of the other side than **what the messenger cannot do.** **And this too is a link in the chain of limits set out in a table in `028-kasas.md` at 28/56.**

### إِن تُسْمِعُ إِلَّا مَن يُؤْمِنُ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا

**And the positive side must be recorded: *in tusmiʿu illā…* — negation plus exception.**

**A point of word order must be recorded, and it is striking:** the sentence does not say "you make those who believe hear" — **it says *man yuʾminu***, **an imperfect verb.** **That is: "one who is believing / who will believe."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the pattern gives hearing not as the consequence of faith but as **something that goes together with faith.** **And the sentence closes with *fa-hum muslimūn*** — the sūra's line of س-ل-م occurs here for the last time, outside the narrative.

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | *wa'tūnī **muslimīn*** | **The letter** |
| 38 | *qabla an yaʾtūnī **muslimīn*** | **Solomon** |
| 42 | *wa kunnā **muslimīn*** | **The queen** |
| 44 | *wa **aslamtu** maʿa Sulaymān* | **The queen** |
| **81** | *fa-hum **muslimūn*** | **Those who believe** — outside the narrative |
| **91** | *wa umirtu an akūna mina'l-**muslimīn*** | **The Prophet** |

**Six occurrences; four inside the narrative, two outside it.** This is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above:** the word the sūra established inside a narrative is applied, once the narrative is over, **to those the sūra addresses**, and finally **to the Prophet himself.** **The last sentence of the narrative (44) and the last block of the sūra (91) are bound together by the same word.**

## 27/82 — وَإِذَا وَقَعَ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَيْهِمْ أَخْرَجْنَا لَهُمْ دَآبَّةً مِّنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ تُكَلِّمُهُمْ أَنَّ ٱلنَّاسَ كَانُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا لَا يُوقِنُونَ

*wa idhā waqaʿa'l-qawlu ʿalayhim akhrajnā lahum dābbatan mina'l-arḍi tukallimuhum anna'n-nāsa kānū bi-āyātinā lā yūqinūn*

"**When the word falls upon them, We shall bring forth for them a creature out of the earth, which will speak to them: that the people had no certainty about Our signs.**"

### First a qualification — and at this verse it is indispensable

**A great many reports and interpretations circulate about this verse. Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write this out plainly, and throughout this section I do not step outside this limit:**

- **There is no certain knowledge about what this *dābba* is, where or when it will come forth, or how it will appear.**
- **Some of the reports transmitted on this subject are disputed within the tradition of hadith criticism. I ascribe no saying I am not sure of to any source, and I do not pile up reports.**
- **I do not enter into the interpretations loaded onto this verse in the modern period — technology, an epidemic, a particular invention or event. These are claims with no ground in the text, and they are readings of the kind `STYLE.md` forbids.**
- **I fix no date, no place and no identity.**

**The only thing I can do at this verse is to work out what the wording says. That I do.**

### دَآبَّة — the root sense of the word

The root **د-ب-ب**. **The word was worked out in `045-casiye.md` at 45/4, and the finding there was this: *dābba* — that which moves, that which stirs upon the earth.** **And it was recorded there plainly: the word is not the name of a species; it means *a living thing that moves*.** **The Qurʾān uses this word to name living things by their movement. I do not repeat it; I rely on that treatment.**

**The consequence of that finding here is direct and must be recorded: the word, as it stands in the verse, specifies no species.**

**And that the word comes indefinite reinforces this: *dābbatan* — "a creature."** **The text gives neither its kind nor any known instance of it.**

**And a caution was recorded in `034-sebe.md` at 34/14 which applies directly here; I rely on that treatment:** **the construction *dābbatu'l-arḍ* in that verse must not be confused with *dābbatan mina'l-arḍ* in this one.** **The two constructions differ, their contexts differ, and neither can be read through the other.**

### What the wording of the verse says

**The verse reports four things, and all four are the text's own words:**

| No. | Expression | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *idhā **waqaʿa'l-qawlu** ʿalayhim* | **The time** — when the word falls |
| 2 | *akhrajnā lahum dābbatan **mina'l-arḍ*** | **The place** — out of the earth |
| 3 | *tukallimuhum* | **The act** — it speaks to them |
| 4 | *anna'n-nāsa kānū bi-āyātinā **lā yūqinūn*** | **What it will say** |

**Beyond these four the verse says nothing. I record what it does not say as well: the creature's kind, its size, its number, its duration, the place of its coming forth, and what event its time will fall together with.**

### وَقَعَ ٱلْقَوْل — twice within the sūra

**The construction occurs twice in this sūra, and this is a datum of the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **82** | *wa idhā **waqaʿa'l-qawlu** ʿalayhim* | **The coming forth of the creature** |
| **85** | *wa **waqaʿa'l-qawlu** ʿalayhim bimā ẓalamū* | **Their falling silent** |

**The same construction, three verses apart.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

*Al-qawl*, from the root **ق-و-ل**, comes here **definite**: "that word."

**Different explanations of what the construction means are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | What *waqaʿa'l-qawl* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The fulfilment of the word of punishment** — the sentence taking effect |
| 2 | **The coming of the appointed time** — the approach of the Last Day |
| 3 | **The completion of the proof** — the word becoming due upon them |

**I do not impose a preference.**

### تُكَلِّمُهُمْ — a variant reading

**A variant reading is transmitted on this word, and since it bears on the meaning it must be recorded:**

| Reading | Root | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| ***tukallimuhum*** | **ك-ل-م** (Form II) | **It speaks to them** — it utters words |
| ***taklimuhum*** | **ك-ل-م** (Form I) | **It wounds them, it marks them** — *kalm*: a wound |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record an observation, not a preference:** what follows in the verse gives **the content of an utterance** (*anna'n-nāsa kānū…*). **This appears to agree better with the first reading.** **But the second has been defended as well, and the two readings do not exclude each other.**

### What it will say — and its ring with the sūra's opening

**And the most noteworthy place in the verse is what the creature will say:**

***Anna'n-nāsa kānū bi-āyātinā lā yūqinūn.***

The root **ي-ق-ن** — **the same word as the *yūqinūn* of the sūra's third verse.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *wa hum bi'l-ākhirati hum **yūqinūn*** | **The believers** — at the sūra's opening |
| **14** | *wa'sta**yqan**athā anfusuhum* | **Those who deny** — their inner selves know |
| **82** | *kānū bi-āyātinā **lā yūqinūn*** | **The people** — the creature's utterance |

**Three occurrences; the sūra opens with this root and gives its ruling once more with the same root.** This is a weave that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above:** what the verse reports is not a creature's **coming forth** but a creature's **speaking**, and what it says. **And what it will say is the absence of the very word set at the sūra's third verse as the believers' attribute.**

**And this is the last link in the sūra's line of speech, and it is worth giving as a table:**

| Verse | Who speaks | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | **An ant** | It speaks, it warns — **and it is heard** |
| **22-26** | **A bird** | It brings news — **and it is listened to** |
| **82** | **A creature** | It speaks, it declares a verdict — **and the matter is over** |
| **85** | **Human beings** | ***lā yanṭiqūn*** — they cannot speak |

**In the sūra, speech passes not from the creatures to the human being but from the human being to the creatures.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence of the four verses:** the sūra takes its name from the speech of an ant, **and it closes that line with human beings unable to speak.** **This is a weave in the text; it is not a claim about the nature of the *dābba*.**

## 27/83-85 — وَيَوْمَ نَحْشُرُ مِن كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ فَوْجًا مِّمَّن يُكَذِّبُ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا فَهُمْ يُوزَعُونَ · حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا جَآءُو قَالَ أَكَذَّبْتُم بِـَٔايَٰتِى وَلَمْ تُحِيطُوا۟ بِهَا عِلْمًا أَمَّاذَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ · وَوَقَعَ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَيْهِم بِمَا ظَلَمُوا۟ فَهُمْ لَا يَنطِقُونَ

*wa yawma naḥshuru min kulli ummatin fawjan mimman yukadhdhibu bi-āyātinā fa-hum yūzaʿūn · ḥattā idhā jāʾū qāla a-kadhdhabtum bi-āyātī wa lam tuḥīṭū bihā ʿilman ammādhā kuntum taʿmalūn · wa waqaʿa'l-qawlu ʿalayhim bimā ẓalamū fa-hum lā yanṭiqūn*

"**On the day We gather from every community a troop of those who deny Our signs, and they are held in order. Until, when they have come, He will say: 'Did you deny My signs when you had not encompassed them in knowledge? Or what was it you were doing?' And the word falls upon them for the wrong they did, and they cannot speak.**"

### فَهُمْ يُوزَعُونَ — the spine closes

**This clause is word for word the same as verse 17, and the sūra's و-ز-ع spine closes here.** **It was set out in a table above; I do not repeat it.**

**And the two scenes can be set side by side:**

| | 27/17 | **27/83** |
|---|---|---|
| Who is gathered | *junūduhū mina'l-jinni wa'l-insi wa'ṭ-ṭayr* | *min kulli ummatin **fawjan** mimman yukadhdhib* |
| Who gathers | **For Solomon** — *ḥushira li-Sulaymān* | **God** — *naḥshuru* |
| The shared clause | *fa-hum yūzaʿūn* | **The same** |

**فَوْج** — root **ف-و-ج**: **a company that passes swiftly through a place, a troop.** **The word was mentioned in the discussion of `110-nasr.md`** (*yadkhulūna fī dīni'llāhi afwājā*) **and this verse was already recorded there.**

**And the construction *min kulli ummatin fawjan* must be recorded: a troop from every community — not all of them.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the verse restricts those gathered by an attribute — *mimman yukadhdhibu bi-āyātinā*. **The group is thus determined not by belonging but by conduct.**

### أَكَذَّبْتُم بِـَٔايَٰتِى وَلَمْ تُحِيطُوا۟ بِهَا عِلْمًا

**And the framing of the question gathers the sūra's whole line of knowledge into a single sentence.**

The root **ح-و-ط** — **the same root as the *aḥaṭtu bimā lam tuḥiṭ bih* of verse 22.**

| Verse | Expression | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **22** | *aḥaṭtu bimā lam **tuḥiṭ** bih* | **The bird → Solomon** | **The limit of knowledge is acknowledged** |
| **84** | *wa lam **tuḥīṭū** bihā ʿilmā* | **Those who deny** | **A verdict is given without encompassing** |

**The same root, at the two ends of the sūra, for two opposite attitudes.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the two verses themselves, and it is the sūra's most decisive contrast:**

**At verse 22 a bird says to its king, "I have encompassed what you could not encompass" — and the king makes an inquiry before he gives a verdict (27).** **At verse 84 human beings are asked: "did you deny when you had not encompassed?"**

**At the beginning of the sūra, then, a bird ties judgement to knowledge; at its end, human beings are asked why they judged without knowledge.**

**And the distinction recorded in `002-bakara.md` at 2/255 works exactly here, and I rely on that treatment:** what is denied is not **knowing** but **encompassing**. **The verse sets encompassing as the condition of denial — that is, it does not say "had you known you would have believed"; it says "you rejected without encompassing".**

**أَمَّاذَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ** — "or what was it you were doing?"

**The sentence offers a second alternative, and the classical commentaries explain it thus:** either you rejected the signs having encompassed them — which did not happen — **or you took no interest at all.** **The second alternative is not an excuse but another charge.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the question opens two doors, and both turn out to be shut.

### فَهُمْ لَا يَنطِقُونَ

**And the sūra's line of ن-ط-ق closes here — it had opened at verse 16.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **16** | *ʿullimnā **manṭiqa**'ṭ-ṭayr* | **Solomon** — the speech of the speechless is understood |
| **85** | *fa-hum **lā yanṭiqūn*** | **Those who deny** — the one who speaks falls silent |

**It was set out in the table at verse 16 above.**

**And in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/21 the same root ran the other way:** *anṭaqanā'llāhu'lladhī anṭaqa kulla shayʾ* — there the limbs were made to speak.

**Three verses can be set side by side:**

| Place | Expression | Who speaks / falls silent |
|---|---|---|
| al-Naml 27/16 | *manṭiqa'ṭ-ṭayr* | **The birds speak** — and are understood |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/21 | *anṭaqa kulla shayʾ* | **The limbs are made to speak** |
| **al-Naml 27/85** | *lā yanṭiqūn* | **Human beings fall silent** |

**This is a weave that can be verified from the text.**

## 27/86-87 — أَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ أَنَّا جَعَلْنَا ٱلَّيْلَ لِيَسْكُنُوا۟ فِيهِ وَٱلنَّهَارَ مُبْصِرًا إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَٰتٍ لِّقَوْمٍ يُؤْمِنُونَ · وَيَوْمَ يُنفَخُ فِى ٱلصُّورِ فَفَزِعَ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ إِلَّا مَن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ وَكُلٌّ أَتَوْهُ دَٰخِرِينَ

*a-lam yaraw annā jaʿalna'l-layla li-yaskunū fīhi wa'n-nahāra mubṣirā, inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-qawmin yuʾminūn · wa yawma yunfakhu fi'ṣ-ṣūri fa-faziʿa man fi's-samāwāti wa man fi'l-arḍi illā man shāʾa'llāh, wa kullun atawhu dākhirīn*

"**Have they not seen that We made the night for them to rest in, and the day giving sight? In that are signs for a people who believe. And on the day the trumpet is blown, all who are in the heavens and the earth will be struck with terror — save whom God wills. And all will come to Him, bowed low.**"

### لِيَسْكُنُوا۟ فِيهِ — its link with al-Qaṣaṣ 28/72-73

**This pair was treated in detail in `028-kasas.md` at 28/71-73** — the two symmetrical questions built with *sarmadan* — **and the finding recorded there was this: what was asked for as the counterpart of night was *ḍiyāʾ* (light), and as the counterpart of day, *sukūn* (rest).** **I rely on that treatment.**

**This verse of al-Naml gives the same pair in a single sentence:**

| | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/71-73 | **al-Naml 27/86** |
|---|---|---|
| Form | **Two questions** — *law jaʿala… sarmadā* | **A single sentence** — *a-lam yaraw* |
| Night | *laylin **taskunūna** fīh* | *al-layla li-**yaskunū** fīh* |
| Day | *yaʾtīkum bi-**ḍiyāʾ*** | *wa'n-nahāra **mubṣirā*** |
| Form of statement | **A supposition** — were it not so | **A fact** — thus We made it |

**The two sūras give the same pair in two different forms: one by supposing its absence, the other by showing its presence.** This is an overlap that can be verified from the text.

**مُبْصِرًا** — and the same pattern as the word in the sūra's thirteenth verse:

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **13** | *āyātunā **mubṣiratan*** | **The signs** — making one see |
| **86** | *wa'n-nahāra **mubṣirā*** | **The day** — making things visible |

**The same active participle, with two different subjects.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and it was recorded at verse 13.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sameness of the pattern:** the sūra names the signs and the day **with the same adjective** — **neither is what is seen; both are what make other things seen.**

**And the verse-ending must be recorded: *li-qawmin yuʾminūn*.** **The ending of verse 52 was *li-qawmin yaʿlamūn*; the two were compared above.**

### يُنفَخُ فِى ٱلصُّورِ

The root **ن-ف-خ**: **to blow.** **The root was treated in `078-nebe.md` at 78/18.** **The word ص-و-ر (the trumpet) was treated in `069-hakka.md` at 69/13 and in the discussion of `082-infitar.md`.** **I do not repeat them here.**

**And the word order must be recorded: *yunfakhu* is imperfect (and passive), and then *fa-faziʿa* is perfect.**

**In Arabic the shift from imperfect to perfect is used to convey the certainty of something that is to happen.** **That is: "it will be blown" and "they were struck with terror" — the second told as though it had already happened.** I record this as an observation about the grammar.

### فَفَزِعَ — root ف-ز-ع

**The sense of the root: to start with a sudden fear, to be startled.** **The lexicographers distinguish the word from *khawf*: *khawf* is a fear that continues, while *fazaʿ* is a sudden shudder.**

**The root was treated in `034-sebe.md` at 34/23 and in `038-sad.md` at 38/22.** **And in al-Naml it occurs twice:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **87** | *fa-**faziʿa** man fi's-samāwāti wa man fi'l-arḍ* | **Everyone** — but for the exception |
| **89** | *wa hum min **fazaʿin** yawmaʾidhin **āminūn*** | **Those who bring good** — they are secure |

**One verse stands between the two, and the two occurrences of the root complete one another.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**إِلَّا مَن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ** — the exception must be recorded, and its content is not stated.

**Different views on whom this exception covers are transmitted in the classical commentaries; there is no certain knowledge, and I do not impose a preference.** **Verse 89, however, gives an indication: *wa hum min fazaʿin yawmaʾidhin āminūn*.** **I record this as an indication, not as a preference.**

**دَٰخِرِين** — root **د-خ-ر**: **to be made small, to bow the neck, to be brought low.** **The root was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/60 and in `037-saffat.md` at 37/18, and it was recorded there: the word is the exact opposite of pride.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And its place within the sūra is worth recording:** the root **ع-ل-و** (self-exaltation) had been condemned twice in the sūra (14, 31). **Verse 87 reports that everyone will come in the state that is the exact opposite of that root.** I record this as an observation.

## 27/88 — وَتَرَى ٱلْجِبَالَ تَحْسَبُهَا جَامِدَةً وَهِىَ تَمُرُّ مَرَّ ٱلسَّحَابِ صُنْعَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ إِنَّهُۥ خَبِيرٌۢ بِمَا تَفْعَلُونَ

*wa tara'l-jibāla taḥsabuhā jāmidatan wa hiya tamurru marra's-saḥāb, ṣunʿa'llāhi'lladhī atqana kulla shayʾ, innahū khabīrun bimā tafʿalūn*

"**You see the mountains and suppose them frozen, while they pass by as the clouds pass. This is the work of God, who has made every thing sound and exactly in its place. He is aware of what you do.**"

### First, a limit

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write it out plainly:**

**From this verse I draw no reference to the rotation of the earth, to plate tectonics, to the movement of the continents, or to any other geological or astronomical fact.** **That is a reading of the scientific-miracle kind that `STYLE.md` forbids.**

**And this limit had already been drawn in the discussion of `081-tekvir.md` at 81/3, and this verse was set out in a table there. I preserve the note made there as it stands:**

> **Al-Naml 27/88 is striking because it says that the mountain's seeming fixed is an illusion. But to read this verse as "the Qurʾān reports the movements of the earth's crust" is a forcing of the kind `STYLE.md` forbids: the context of the verse is the Day of Judgement, and the description belongs to that day. I record it as a possible reading, not as a claim.**

**I add nothing to this and do not extend it.**

### The readings in the classical commentaries

**There is disagreement in the classical commentaries about what the verse describes, and the disagreement must be given as a table. I do not impose a preference:**

| Reading | What *wa hiya tamurru marra's-saḥāb* reports | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **A scene of the Last Day** — the mountains set moving on that day | **The context**: the preceding verse (87) describes the blowing of the trumpet, and the following verses (89-90) the reckoning |
| **2** | **A statement of movement in general** — that the mountains are in motion even now, though they appear to stand still | That the verb *taḥsabuhā* looks at the present |
| **3** | **A likeness** — a comparison conveying the transience of what looks solid | That the simile *marra's-saḥāb* conveys transience |

**All three readings have been defended in the classical commentaries.**

**I record one indication, not a preference, and its ground is the sequence:** the verse stands immediately after the trumpet scene of verse 87 and immediately before the table of reckoning in verses 89-90. **The context appears to support the first reading.** **But that the verb *taḥsabuhā* comes in the imperfect also leaves room for the second** — and this is a ground recorded in the classical commentaries as well. **I do not impose a preference.**

### تَحْسَبُهَا — its second occurrence within the sūra

**The verb occurs for the second time in this sūra; the first was the queen of Sabaʾ seeing the hall at verse 44. It was set out in a table at verse 44 above, and I give it again:**

| Verse | Expression | Who is deceived | What is the case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **44** | *ḥasibat'hu **lujjatan*** | **The queen** | *ṣarḥun mumarradun min qawārīr* — a floor of glass |
| **88** | ***taḥsabuhā** jāmidatan* | **Everyone who looks** | *wa hiya tamurru marra's-saḥāb* |

**The structure is the same in both verses, and this is a weave that can be verified from the text:**

1. **A sight** (*raʾat'hu* / *tarā*)
2. **A supposition** (*ḥasibat* / *taḥsabu*)
3. **And the reporting of what is the case** (*innahū ṣarḥun…* / *wa hiya tamurru…*)

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure the two verses share:** the sūra records twice that what the eye sees and what is the case are not the same — **once on the floor of a hall, once in the mountains.** **And in the first the illusion is one person's private experience; in the second it is the common sight of everyone.**

**And this is part of the sūra's spine of knowledge:** at verse 22 the limit of knowledge was recorded, at verse 65 the limit of the unseen. **This verse records the limit of the senses.**

### جَامِدَة

The root **ج-م-د**: **to freeze, to harden, to become something that does not flow.** *Jāmid* — frozen, motionless. **In Turkish the borrowed word *camit* ("inert, lifeless") comes from this root.**

**And the choice of word is worth recording:** it does not say "fixed" or "standing still" but ***frozen***. **What is supposed is thus not stillness alone — it is the absence of flow.**

**And the picture set opposite it completes this: *marra's-saḥāb* — the passing of the clouds.** **A cloud both moves and changes shape; and its movement most often goes unnoticed by the one who looks.**

**I record this as an observation about the word:** the two words (**frozen** and **cloud**) name two exactly opposite states. **And the choice of simile also carries the imperceptibility of the movement.**

**And the descriptions of mountains were set out in a table in `081-tekvir.md`** (*suyyirat*, *ka'l-ʿihni'l-manfūsh*, *kathīban mahīlā*, *bussat bassā*). **Al-Naml's description takes its own place in that table, and its difference was recorded there: this description takes as its subject not the mountain's movement but its *being supposed motionless*.**

### أَتْقَنَ — root أ-ت-ق-ن

**This root occurs only here in the Qurʾān, and it is worked out for the first time in this series here.**

**The root ت-ق-ن (in the classification of some lexicographers أ-ت-ق-ن), in Form IV: *itqān*.**

**The concrete sense the lexicographers give must be recorded, and it comes from an unexpected quarter:**

> ***Tiqn*** — **the fine silt a flood carries, which feeds the field.**

**And the connection is made thus:** when that silt settles onto the soil it forms **a tight, even layer.** **From this, *itqān*: to do a work tightly, evenly and exactly in its place.**

**I transmit this as a relation the lexicographers make; it is not an inference of my own.**

**The word's derivatives carry the same core:**

| Derivative | Sense | The common thread |
|---|---|---|
| *tiqn* | **Flood silt** — fine mud pressed close | **Tightness, absence of gaps** |
| *atqana* | **To make sound** | **To leave no slackness** |
| *itqān* | **Mastery, doing a work exactly** | The same |
| *mutqin* | **One who does his work soundly** | The same |

**And the work the word does here must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:**

**The word does not mean "He made it beautiful" (*aḥsana*) or "He created" (*khalaqa*) — it means *He made it sound and without a gap*.**

**And this agrees with where the sentence stands:** the verse uses this word immediately after saying **that what appears is not as it appears.** **The mountains' being supposed frozen is thus not a flaw — the work itself is sound; what is wanting is the sight of the one who looks.** The ground is the sequence of the two clauses.

**صُنْعَ ٱللَّهِ** — root **ص-ن-ع**: **to work a thing with skill, to fashion it.** *Ṣanʿa*, *ṣināʿa* and *maṣnūʿ* are from the same root. **Unlike the word *khalq* (creation), the word brings the dimension of *working* to the fore.**

**And the word order must be recorded: *ṣunʿa* is in the accusative — as a cognate accusative.** **In saying "this is the work of God" the sentence thus uses a pattern of emphasis.**

### إِنَّهُۥ خَبِيرٌۢ بِمَا تَفْعَلُونَ

The root **خ-ب-ر**: **to know the inner face of a thing.** **The lexicographers record the sense "to plough the ground deeply" in this root** (*khabara'l-arḍ*): **that is, to know not the surface but what lies beneath it.**

**And that the sentence is placed here is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the verse had been speaking of mountains; **the close turns to human acts.** **And there is a link between the two clauses: one takes as its subject what a person *cannot see*, the other what a person *does*.**

**The verse thus sets these side by side: you cannot see the mountain move — He sees what you do.** **The ground is that the two clauses stand in the same verse.**

**And the change of person must be recorded: *tafʿalūn* — second person plural.** **The verse began in the third person and ends in the second. This is *iltifāt*.**

## 27/89-90 — مَن جَآءَ بِٱلْحَسَنَةِ فَلَهُۥ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهَا وَهُم مِّن فَزَعٍ يَوْمَئِذٍ ءَامِنُونَ · وَمَن جَآءَ بِٱلسَّيِّئَةِ فَكُبَّتْ وُجُوهُهُمْ فِى ٱلنَّارِ هَلْ تُجْزَوْنَ إِلَّا مَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

*man jāʾa bi'l-ḥasanati fa-lahū khayrun minhā wa hum min fazaʿin yawmaʾidhin āminūn · wa man jāʾa bi's-sayyiʾati fa-kubbat wujūhuhum fi'n-nār, hal tujzawna illā mā kuntum taʿmalūn*

"**Whoever brings a good deed shall have better than it, and they will be secure from the terror of that day. And whoever brings an evil deed — their faces will be flung down into the Fire. Are you recompensed for anything but what you used to do?**"

### An exact overlap with al-Qaṣaṣ 28/84

**This pair was treated in `028-kasas.md` at 28/84, and the asymmetry was set out in a table there.** **The verses of the two sūras can be set side by side:**

| | al-Qaṣaṣ 28/84 | **al-Naml 27/89-90** |
|---|---|---|
| The good deed | *man jāʾa bi'l-ḥasanati fa-lahū **khayrun minhā*** | **The same** |
| The addition | **None** | *wa hum min **fazaʿin** yawmaʾidhin **āminūn*** |
| The evil deed | *fa-lā yujzā… illā mā kānū yaʿmalūn* | *fa-**kubbat wujūhuhum** fi'n-nār* + *hal tujzawna illā mā kuntum taʿmalūn* |

**The first sentence is word for word the same.** This is an overlap between the two sūras that can be verified.

**And the asymmetry recorded in `028-kasas.md` holds here too, and I do not repeat it:** to the one who brings good, **something better**; to the one who brings evil, **as much as he did.** **On one side an increase, on the other exact equivalence.**

**Two additions belong to al-Naml, and both must be recorded.**

**First — *wa hum min fazaʿin yawmaʾidhin āminūn*.** **The root ف-ز-ع had occurred at verse 87**, where it was reported that everyone would be struck with terror. **This verse fills in the exception to that sentence.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **87** | *fa-**faziʿa** man fi's-samāwāti wa man fi'l-arḍ **illā man shāʾa'llāh*** | **Everyone** — with one exception |
| **89** | *wa hum min **fazaʿin** yawmaʾidhin **āminūn*** | **Those who bring good** |

**I record this as an indication, not as a settled interpretation:** the exception at verse 87 (*illā man shāʾa'llāh*) was left open; **verse 89 names a group with the same root.** **The link between the two verses can be verified from the text; but I do not claim that verse 89 defines exactly the exception of verse 87.**

**Second — *fa-kubbat wujūhuhum fi'n-nār*.**

The root **ك-ب-ب** was worked out in `067-mulk.md` at 67/22: **to overturn a thing onto its face, to tip it over and pour it out.** *Kabba* — he overturned it, he threw it face down; *akabba* — he fell on his face. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the subject of the verb must be recorded: *wujūhuhum* — their faces.** **The sentence does not say "they are flung"; its subject is the face.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the face is where a person is recognised and where his standing shows. **And at verse 34 of the sūra the queen had said *aʿizzata ahlihā adhillatan* — the inverting of standing.** **This verse gives the same idea in a bodily image.** I record this as an overlap, not as a claim about intent.

**And this verse was mentioned in the discussion of `101-karia.md`** (where the readings of *hāwiya* were discussed). **I rely on that treatment.**

### هَلْ تُجْزَوْنَ إِلَّا مَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

**The sentence is an *istifhām inkārī*: a negation in the form of a question.** **"Are you recompensed for anything but what you did?" — that is, for nothing else.**

**And the change of person must be recorded: the sentence began in the third person (*man jāʾa*, *wujūhuhum*) and ends in the second (*tujzawna*, *kuntum*).** **This too is an *iltifāt*, and it is the same shift as at verse 88.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** in the sūra's last block *iltifāt* occurs twice in succession (88 and 90), **and in both the shift comes at the point where the reckoning is tied to the one addressed.**

## 27/91-93 — إِنَّمَآ أُمِرْتُ أَنْ أَعْبُدَ رَبَّ هَٰذِهِ ٱلْبَلْدَةِ ٱلَّذِى حَرَّمَهَا وَلَهُۥ كُلُّ شَىْءٍ وَأُمِرْتُ أَنْ أَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ · وَأَنْ أَتْلُوَا۟ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ فَمَنِ ٱهْتَدَىٰ فَإِنَّمَا يَهْتَدِى لِنَفْسِهِۦ وَمَن ضَلَّ فَقُلْ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ مِنَ ٱلْمُنذِرِينَ · وَقُلِ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ سَيُرِيكُمْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ فَتَعْرِفُونَهَا وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِغَٰفِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ

*innamā umirtu an aʿbuda rabba hādhihi'l-baldati'lladhī ḥarramahā wa lahū kullu shayʾ, wa umirtu an akūna mina'l-muslimīn · wa an atluwa'l-qurʾān, fa-mani'htadā fa-innamā yahtadī li-nafsih, wa man ḍalla fa-qul innamā ana mina'l-mundhirīn · wa quli'l-ḥamdu li'llāh, sa-yurīkum āyātihī fa-taʿrifūnahā, wa mā rabbuka bi-ghāfilin ʿammā taʿmalūn*

"**I have only been commanded to serve the Lord of this town — He who made it inviolable — and to Him belongs everything. And I have been commanded to be of those who submit; and to recite the Qurʾān. Whoever is guided is guided only for his own sake; and whoever goes astray — say: I am only one of those who warn. And say: Praise be to God. He will show you His signs and you will recognise them. And your Lord is not unaware of what you do.**"

### رَبَّ هَٰذِهِ ٱلْبَلْدَةِ ٱلَّذِى حَرَّمَهَا

**The construction must be read carefully, and it carries a point of word order.**

**The sentence does not say "I have been commanded to serve this town."** *Rabba hādhihi'l-balda* — **"the Lord of this town."**

**And immediately after it comes a widening: *wa lahū kullu shayʾ* — "and to Him belongs everything."**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence of the two clauses:** the verse establishes a relation to a place and **in the same sentence makes that relation unbounded.** **The naming of the town is thus not a restriction but a starting point** — the beginning is made from what the one addressed knows best, and the scope is opened at once.

**بَلْدَة** — root **ب-ل-د**: **to stay in a place, to settle.** *Balad* — a place of settlement. **The root was treated in `095-tin.md` at 95/3 (*hādha'l-baladi'l-amīn*) and in `050-kaf.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the name of `090-beled.md` is from this root.**

### حَرَّمَهَا — and the ring of sanctuary and safety

The root **ح-ر-م**: **to make a thing inviolable, to make it a forbidden precinct.** **The root was treated in `056-vakia.md`, `068-kalem.md` and `070-mearic.md`.**

**And this construction had occurred twice more in this series, and both were treated:**

| Place | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/67 | *a-wa-lam yaraw annā jaʿalnā **ḥaraman āminan** wa yutakhaṭṭafu'n-nāsu min ḥawlihim* | **A proof** — they are safe while all around them are snatched away |
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/57 | *a-wa-lam numakkin lahum **ḥaraman āminan** yujbā ilayhi thamarātu kulli shayʾ* | **An answer** — to an objection raised out of concern for safety |
| **al-Naml 27/91** | *rabba hādhihi'l-baldati'lladhī **ḥarramahā*** | **The one to whom service is due** — a relation |

**In `028-kasas.md` at 28/57 these two verses were compared, and it was recorded there:** the same verb (خ-ط-ف) occurred once for a fact that had come about and once for a possibility that was feared; **and the objection was built not on belief but on concern for safety.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference that belongs to al-Naml must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** al-ʿAnkabūt and al-Qaṣaṣ used the same fact as **a proof or an answer**. **Al-Naml uses the same fact as a *relation*** — that is, in identifying the one to whom service is due.

| Sūra | What the fact of the sanctuary does |
|---|---|
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/67 | **A proof** — to show the ingratitude |
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/57 | **An answer to an objection** — to show the source of the fear |
| **al-Naml 27/91** | **An identification** — the naming of the one who is served |

**Three sūras, one fact, three different tasks.** This is a weave that can be verified from the text.

**And the pattern of the verb must be recorded: *ḥarrama* — Form II, that is, the verb that *lays down* the inviolability.** **The town's inviolability is not its own; it has been given.**

### وَأَنْ أَتْلُوَا۟ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ

**Three commands are enumerated, and their order is worth recording:**

| No. | Command | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *an aʿbuda rabba hādhihi'l-balda* | **Service** |
| 2 | *an akūna mina'l-muslimīn* | **To be of those who submit** |
| 3 | *wa an atluwa'l-qurʾān* | **To recite** |

The root **ت-ل-و**: **to go after, to follow behind.** **From this, *tilāwa*: to read a text by following its letters.** **The root has been treated in many places in this series.**

**I record this as an observation:** all three commands are works that lie **upon the person himself.** **There is no command concerning the other side** — and indeed the limit is drawn plainly in the next sentence.

**And the second command must be recorded: *mina'l-muslimīn*.** **The sūra's line of س-ل-م closes here, bound to the sentence at verse 44 of the narrative. It was set out in a table at verse 81 above.**

### فَمَنِ ٱهْتَدَىٰ فَإِنَّمَا يَهْتَدِى لِنَفْسِهِ

**And the limit is set.**

**An observation about the word order must be recorded: the two sides of the sentence are not symmetrical.**

| Side | Expression | What is given |
|---|---|---|
| The one who is guided | *fa-innamā **yahtadī li-nafsih*** | **For his own sake** |
| The one who goes astray | *fa-qul innamā **ana mina'l-mundhirīn*** | **The outcome of the straying is not stated** — the messenger's position is |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the asymmetry of the two clauses:** on the first side the outcome is tied to the person; **on the second side the sentence does not enter into the outcome at all — it says what the messenger is.** **What is reported is thus not the end of the one who strays but the limit of the messenger's responsibility.**

**And this has the same structure as the sentence at verse 40:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **40** | *wa man **shakara** fa-innamā **yashkuru li-nafsih*** |
| **92** | *fa-mani'h**tadā** fa-innamā **yahtadī li-nafsih*** |

**The same pattern, with two different verbs, in two different blocks of the sūra.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** one is spoken by Solomon of thanksgiving, the other by the Prophet of guidance. **And both report the same thing: the return of an act comes back to the one who does it.**

**مِنَ ٱلْمُنذِرِين** — and the word by which the messenger describes himself must be recorded: *mundhir* (one who warns), **as one member of a plural.** *Mina'l-mundhirīn* — "of those who warn."

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the description is given not as a singular attribute but as **membership of a company.** **And all the messengers told of throughout the sūra (Moses, Ṣāliḥ, Lot, Solomon) belong to that company.**

### وَقُلِ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ — the close of the sūra

**And the sūra closes with the command of verse 59. It was set out in a table above.**

| Verse | Expression | Place |
|---|---|---|
| **59** | *quli'l-ḥamdu li'llāh* | **The opening of the block of proof** |
| **93** | *wa quli'l-ḥamdu li'llāh* | **The last verse of the sūra** |

**And it was recorded in the discussion of `034-sebe.md`: there are five sūras in the Qurʾān that open with *al-ḥamdu li'llāh*** (al-Fātiḥa, al-Anʿām, al-Kahf, Sabaʾ, Fāṭir). **Al-Naml does not open with this construction — it uses it twice, in the middle and at the end.** I record this as an observation.

### سَيُرِيكُمْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ فَتَعْرِفُونَهَا

**This sentence is on the same pattern as `041-fussilet.md` 41/53, and a limit was drawn there. I preserve the same limit here and do not extend it.**

**The limit drawn in Fuṣṣilat was this, and it holds as it stands:**

> **I draw from the verse no reference to any particular scientific discovery, invention or historical event. There is no ground in the text for such a finding; the verse names neither a field nor a time.**

**And three readings were set out in a table there** (developments in history / the signs of the Last Day / a state that works continuously) **and no preference was imposed. I apply the same table here, and I do not impose a preference.**

**The two verses can be set side by side, and the difference is a datum of the text:**

| | Fuṣṣilat 41/53 | **al-Naml 27/93** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb | *sa-nurīhim* — **third person** | *sa-yurīkum* — **second person** |
| Who shows | *nurī* — **We** | *yurī* — **He** |
| Where | *fi'l-āfāqi wa fī anfusihim* | **Not stated** |
| The outcome | *ḥattā **yatabayyana** lahum annahu'l-ḥaqq* | *fa-**taʿrifūnahā*** |

**Two differences are worth recording.**

**First — al-Naml names no place.** Fuṣṣilat enumerated two domains (*āfāq* and *anfus*); **al-Naml says only "His signs".** **This means that drawing an inference about a domain from this verse is even more groundless in al-Naml.**

**Second — the verb of outcome differs.**

The root **ع-ر-ف**: **to recognise.** **The lexicographers record the difference between *maʿrifa* and *ʿilm*: *maʿrifa* is recognising again something already known;** *ʿilm* is knowledge in general.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of verb:** the sentence does not say "you will learn" — **it says *you will recognise*.** **What will be shown is therefore something that will not seem strange when it is met.**

**And this sets up an overlap with verses 41-42 of the sūra:** there a throne was **made unrecognisable** (*nakkirū*) and the queen recognised it all the same (*ka-annahū huw*).

| Verse | Root | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **41** | **ن-ك-ر** — *nakkirū* | **Making unrecognisable** |
| **42** | — | **Recognition** — *ka-annahū huw* |
| **93** | **ع-ر-ف** — *taʿrifūnahā* | **Recognition** — the signs |

**ن-ك-ر and ع-ر-ف are two roots used in Arabic as opposites of each other** — indeed the grammatical terms *nakira* (indefinite) and *maʿrifa* (definite) come from these two roots.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the opposition of the two roots together with their distribution within the sūra:** in its middle the sūra tells of a test of recognition **and in its last verse it promises the same act to those it addresses.** **I make no claim about intent; what I record is that the two roots stand in two places in the sūra.**

### وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِغَٰفِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ

The root **غ-ف-ل**: **to be unaware of something, to have it fall outside one's attention.** *Ghafla* — inattention, not noticing.

**And the sūra's third spine passes here one last time, in reverse.**

**The root ش-ع-ر had reported three times in the sūra that human beings *do not notice* (18, 50, 65).** **The sūra's last sentence reports that not noticing does not apply to God** — this time with the root **غ-ف-ل**.

| Verse | Root | Who | The ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | **ش-ع-ر** | Solomon and his armies | **They do not notice** |
| 50 | **ش-ع-ر** | The nine men | **They do not notice** |
| 65 | **ش-ع-ر** | Everyone | **They do not notice** |
| **93** | **غ-ف-ل** | **Your Lord** | **He is not unaware** |

**Two different roots are used, and I claim no derivational link between them.** **What I record is that the sūra ties its line of "not noticing" to human beings three times and denies it in its last sentence.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the table above.**

**And the sūra's last word must be recorded: *taʿmalūn* — "what you do."** **The sūra takes its name from the speech of an ant and closes with a statement about deeds.**

### A reading of the sūra's narrative order

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the tables above.**

**The sūra tells four narratives, and all four turn on a question of knowledge:**

| Narrative | Verses | Its bearing on knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| **Moses** | 7-14 | **Denial against knowledge** — *wa'stayqanathā anfusuhum* |
| **Solomon** | 15-44 | **Knowledge given, taught, exceeded, bounded** |
| **Thamūd** | 45-53 | **A plot laid in secret, and an outcome not noticed** |
| **Lot** | 54-58 | **Acting with open eyes** — *wa antum tubṣirūn* |

**And the blocks that come after the narratives treat the same question directly:** the five questions enumerate **what can be known** (60-64), verse 65 sets apart **what cannot be known**, and verse 84 asks about **giving a verdict without encompassing.**

**The order of the sūra is thus this: first the circulation of knowledge is shown, then its limit is drawn, and at the end the one addressed is asked about the breach of that limit.**

**And the name of the sūra stands at a meaningful place within this order:** the sūra takes its name from the speech of a creature **no one would count as a source of knowledge.** **And that speech was heard, was understood, and became the occasion of a prophet's prayer.**

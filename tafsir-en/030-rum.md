# 30. Sūrat al-Rūm

**Sixty verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the announcement in **the second verse**, **غُلِبَتِ ٱلرُّومُ** — the announcement, given across the second to the fourth verses, of **a political outcome that was still to come.**

It is one of the sūras that open with *alif-lām-mīm*.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-7 | The news of a defeat, the promise given, and surface knowledge | *hum ʿani'l-ākhirati hum ghāfilūn* (7) |
| **II** | 8-16 | The failure to think, the earlier peoples, origination and restoration | *fi'l-ʿadhābi muḥḍarūn* (16) |
| **III** | 17-27 | The times of glorification and the ***wa min āyātihi*** series | *wa huwa'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm* (27) |
| **IV** | 28-45 | The parable, the fiṭra, division, ribā and zakāt | *innahu lā yuḥibbu'l-kāfirīn* (45) |
| **V** | 46-60 | The winds, dead earth, the three stages of a lifetime, and the closing | *lā yastakhiffannaka…* (60) |

**The backbone of the sūra gathers into a single word: *āya* — sign.** Between the second and the fifth verses a sign is given **from within history**: an outcome is announced in advance. Between the twentieth and the twenty-fifth verses signs are enumerated **from within nature**, and each of them opens with the same formula: ***wa min āyātihi***.

**The link between the two fields is established by the sūra's own sentence:** in the seventh verse the failing of the other side is named **not as ignorance but as superficiality** — *yaʿlamūna **ẓāhiran** mina'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* — "they know the **outside** of the life of this world". **Upon this the sūra builds a series that teaches one to stand inside that same world and look at its unseen side.**

## 30/1-5 — الٓمٓ · غُلِبَتِ ٱلرُّومُ · فِىٓ أَدْنَى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَهُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ غَلَبِهِمْ سَيَغْلِبُونَ · فِى بِضْعِ سِنِينَ لِلَّهِ ٱلْأَمْرُ مِن قَبْلُ وَمِنۢ بَعْدُ وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ · بِنَصْرِ ٱللَّهِ يَنصُرُ مَن يَشَآءُ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

*Alif-lām-mīm · Ghulibati'r-Rūm · Fī adna'l-arḍi wa hum min baʿdi ghalabihim sa-yaghlibūn · Fī biḍʿi sinīn, li'llāhi'l-amru min qablu wa min baʿd, wa yawmaʾidhin yafraḥu'l-muʾminūn · Bi-naṣri'llāh, yanṣuru man yashāʾ, wa huwa'l-ʿazīzu'r-raḥīm*

"Alif-lām-mīm. **The Rūm have been defeated — in the nearest land.** But they, **after their defeat, will be victorious: within a few years.** **The affair belongs to God, before and after.** On that day the believers will rejoice **at God's help.** He helps whom He wills; He is the Mighty, the Merciful."

**For the note on the disconnected letters I rely on `002-bakara.md` 2/1 and `050-kaf.md`; I do not repeat it here.**

### The most striking place in the sūra

**Places in the Qurʾān where a political outcome still to come is announced within a period bounded by a number are few. This is one of them.**

**And the construction of the announcement must be recorded:** the sentence comes not as a wish or a prayer but as a **notification**; **and the period too is not left indefinite:** *fī biḍʿi sinīn* — **within a few years.**

### A difference in the variant readings — a reading that reverses the sense

**Two readings are transmitted for these verses, and the difference turns the sense of the sentence into its exact opposite. For that reason it must be given:**

| Reading | Verse 2 | The end of verse 3 | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **غُلِبَتْ** — *ghulibat* (**passive**): "was defeated" | **سَيَغْلِبُونَ** — *sa-yaghlibūn* (**active**): "they will be victorious" | **The Rūm were defeated, then will be victorious** |
| **B** | **غَلَبَتْ** — *ghalabat* (**active**): "was victorious" | **سَيُغْلَبُونَ** — *sa-yughlabūn* (**passive**): "they will be defeated" | **The Rūm were victorious, then will be defeated** |

**The two readings are exact mirrors of one another:** in the one, the voice of the two verbs changes places and the event turns the other way about.

**Reading A is widely reported in the classical commentaries and in the sources on the variant readings; and the text of the muṣḥaf is written according to that reading.** **Reading B has also been recorded; but the reading that is widely reported is A.**

**I do not impose a preference, and I record both readings.**

**I record an observation about the word order, and it is independent of the two readings:** the two verbs in the sentence are **necessarily of opposite voice.** That is, whichever reading is taken, the verse says that **a situation will be reversed.** **What changes is which side turns in which direction — not the turning itself.**

**And the verse's own sentence says this already:** *li'llāhi'l-amru **min qablu wa min baʿd*** — **"the affair belongs to God, both before it and after it."** **This sentence is a note set between the two verbs: the owner of the turning is named.**

### Historical background — in the language of "it is reported"

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write this section without claims, and I make no claim about a definite date, place or number.**

**The following is widely reported in the classical commentaries:** in the period when the Qurʾān was being revealed there was a long-running war between the two great powers of that region. **At one stage of that war one of the sides (the Rūm) suffered a defeat; it is reported that the verses, after this defeat, announced that the outcome would be reversed.** That this reversal later came about is recorded in the sources as well.

**Reports are also transmitted in the classical sources that, upon the revelation of the verse, a wager or a contest of claims took place in Mecca.** **I do not give the wording or the source of these reports here**, because I cannot issue a definite ruling about their variants and their soundness. **I confine myself to recording that the idea is found in the sources.**

**I make no claim about a definite date, the name of a battle, the name of a commander, or a place.** **And I do not enter into present-day politics at all:** the verse speaks in the name of no state, no people and no side of today. **The principle recorded in `049-hucurat.md` holds here too: what the verse describes are attributes; no blanket ruling is issued about an ethnic or a political group.**

### فِىٓ أَدْنَى ٱلْأَرْضِ — root: د-ن-و

**The root means *to be near*; and *adnā*, in the elative pattern, carries two senses:**

| Sense | How it arises | What it answers to in the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Nearest** | From the root's original sense: nearness | The region **nearest** to the one addressed / to the Arabian peninsula |
| **Lowest / most low-lying** | From nearness being associated with "lying below" | A region **lying low** |

**Both senses are recorded by the lexicographers. And the word *al-dunyā* is from this root as well** — "the (life) that is nearest" — **this has come up in many places in the series** (*aw adnā* at `053-necm.md` 53/9, and in a great many sections by way of *al-dunyā*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**Which region the construct intends is disputed in the classical commentaries, and more than one place-name is transmitted. I make no claim to a particular place.**

**And per the method (`STYLE.md`) I draw a clear boundary:** on the strength of this construct **I make no claim of a scientific miracle concerning the lowest point on the earth's surface by elevation.** Such a reading takes one of the word's two linguistic senses and dresses it in a modern measurement. **The boundary set on the same ground in `070-mearic.md` and in `086-tarik.md` holds here too:** the truth of the text is not made to depend on a datum of measurement.

**The work the word does in the sentence is plain enough, and I record that much:** the event is mentioned not as **a distant legend** for the one addressed but as **an event that happened in a nearby place.** **The verse chooses its evidence from a distance the hearer can reach** — the sūra will do the same for the sky in the twenty-sixth verse and for the earth in the forty-second.

### فِى بِضْعِ سِنِينَ — root: ب-ض-ع

**The root is analysed here for the first time in the series.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to cut, to break a piece off a whole.** *Badaʿa'l-laḥma* — he cut up the meat. From the same root:

| Derivative | Sense |
|---|---|
| **بَضَعَ** | To cut, to separate off a piece |
| **بِضَاعَة** | The **share set apart** from capital; goods for trade |
| **مِبْضَع** | The scalpel — the instrument that cuts |
| **بِضْع** | **A piece separated off from a number**: a bounded interval |

**And there are small differences over the range the lexicographers give for *biḍʿ*:**

| View | Range |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Between three and nine** |
| 2 | **Between three and ten** |
| 3 | **From one to ten** — a wider usage |

**The one widely reported is the first: from three to nine.** **I do not impose a preference.**

**The choice of the word is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the verse neither gives a definite year nor leaves the period entirely open. ***Biḍʿ*, with the picture in its root of "a piece separated off from a whole", announces a bounded but not fully determined interval.** **That is: the announcement is kept narrow enough to be verifiable, and wide enough not to be reduced to a reckoning of dates.**

### وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ بِنَصْرِ ٱللَّهِ

**The division of the sentence must be recorded:** the construct *bi-naṣri'llāh* falls, by verse number, **in the following verse** (5). **That is, there is a verse boundary between "they will rejoice" and "at God's help".**

**The root ف-ر-ح occurs three times in the sūra: here (4), in the thirty-second verse (*fariḥūn*) and in the thirty-sixth verse (*fariḥū*).** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it will be tabulated at the end.** **And what is to be recorded here is this: the word is used in this sūra in both a positive and a negative context.** Here the rejoicing is attached **to God's help**; in the thirty-second verse it will be attached **to what they hold in their own hands.**

**وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ** — and the block closes with a pair of names that joins power to mercy. **The same pair had also occurred at `032-secde.md` 32/6, and was recorded there together with the table of how the names of the One who sends down change from sūra to sūra.**

**And the placing of the pair here is worth recording:** it comes immediately after the news of a defeat and a victory. **I record this as an observation:** at the end of an event in which both sides are defeated and both are victorious, a pair is set that names power and mercy together. **The sentence avoids reducing the outcome to power.**

## 30/6-7 — وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ لَا يُخْلِفُ ٱللَّهُ وَعْدَهُۥ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ · يَعْلَمُونَ ظَٰهِرًا مِّنَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَهُمْ عَنِ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ هُمْ غَٰفِلُونَ

*Waʿda'llāh, lā yukhlifu'llāhu waʿdahu wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn · Yaʿlamūna ẓāhiran mina'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā wa hum ʿani'l-ākhirati hum ghāfilūn*

"**It is the promise of God.** God does not go back on His promise; but **most people do not know.** **They know the outside of the life of this world; of the hereafter they are heedless.**"

### How the two verses are nailed together

**The sixth verse ends with *lā yaʿlamūn* (they do not know).** **The seventh verse begins with *yaʿlamūna* (they know).**

**The same verb, negative and positive, one after the other.** **And this is not a contradiction — it is a description.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the overlap between the end of the one verse and the beginning of the other:** after saying "they do not know", the verse immediately says **what they do know.** **That is: what is lacking is not knowledge but the *depth* of knowledge.** The sentence describes not ignorance but **a knowledge that has stopped where it stood.**

### ظَٰهِرًا مِّنَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا

**ظَاهِر** — root **ظ-ه-ر**: **to appear, to come out into the open, to be on top.** *Ẓahr* — **the back**, that is, the outer face and the upper side of a thing. **The root will occur twice more in this sūra: *tuẓhirūn* (to enter upon the noon hour) in verse 18, and *ẓahara'l-fasād* (corruption has appeared) in verse 41.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it will be tabulated at the end.**

**That the word comes indefinite must be recorded:** *ẓāhiran* — "**an** outside". Not definite. **And the particle *min* conveys the partitive: *mina'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* — *a part* of the life of this world.**

**That is, the sentence narrows twice:** what is known is not **the whole** of the life of this world, and that part is in turn **its outside**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these two narrowings:** the verse does not count the other side's knowledge as false — **it counts it as incomplete and as having stayed outside.** **What they know is true; it is not complete.**

### The link with the axis of knowledge in al-Jāthiya

**The whole backbone of `045-casiye.md` is a matter of knowledge, and the words for knowledge scattered across that sūra were tabulated there.** **This verse adds one more link to that table, and the difference must be recorded:**

| Place | The name of the failing | The expression |
|---|---|---|
| al-Jāthiya 45/24 | ***Ẓann*** — judgement without knowledge | *Mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm, in hum illā yaẓunnūn* |
| al-Jāthiya 45/32 | ***Confession*** — from their own mouths | *In naẓunnu illā ẓannan wa mā naḥnu bi-mustayqinīn* |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/23 | ***Ẓann as the cause of ruin*** | *Ẓannukum … ardākum* |
| **al-Rūm 30/7** | ***Superficiality*** — **there is knowledge, there is no depth to it** | *Yaʿlamūna **ẓāhiran** mina'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* |

**The difference can be verified and is worth recording:** in al-Jāthiya the failing was **ẓann** — that is, a guess put in the place of knowledge. **In al-Rūm the failing is not ẓann: the verb is plainly *yaʿlamūn* (they know).** **That is, al-Rūm describes a step above the failing described in al-Jāthiya:** to judge not by guesswork but **by a knowledge that is real yet has stayed on the surface.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of the verb.** **And this accords with the structure of the sūra:** in the second verse a historical event is announced — an event anyone can see. **The sūra sets out from the start the difference between the visible face of that same event and the side of it that is announced.**

**غَٰفِلُون** — root **غ-ف-ل**: **to be unaware of something; and the attention's going elsewhere.** The lexicographers record the concrete branch of the root as well: *ghufl*, a thing **that has no mark on it** — an unbranded animal, ground with no track across it. **That is, heedlessness is the state in which the sign goes unread.**

**The choice of the word is worth recording, and I give this as an observation about the language:** the backbone of the sūra is the word *āya* (sign). **And the word chosen for the other side is a word that carries "the absence of a mark" in its root.**

**وَهُمْ عَنِ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ هُمْ غَٰفِلُونَ** — in the word order the pronoun *hum* occurs **twice**. **In Arabic this repetition conveys emphasis (*taʾkīd*)** and sets up a stress close to a restriction of the subject of the nominal sentence.

## 30/8-10 — أَوَلَمْ يَتَفَكَّرُوا۟ فِىٓ أَنفُسِهِم مَّا خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَآ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَأَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى وَإِنَّ كَثِيرًا مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِلِقَآئِ رَبِّهِمْ لَكَٰفِرُونَ · أَوَلَمْ يَسِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَيَنظُرُوا۟ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ كَانُوٓا۟ أَشَدَّ مِنْهُمْ قُوَّةً وَأَثَارُوا۟ ٱلْأَرْضَ وَعَمَرُوهَآ أَكْثَرَ مِمَّا عَمَرُوهَا · ثُمَّ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسَٰٓـُٔوا۟ ٱلسُّوٓأَىٰٓ أَن كَذَّبُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ

*A-wa-lam yatafakkarū fī anfusihim, mā khalaqa'llāhu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā illā bi'l-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā, wa inna kathīran mina'n-nāsi bi-liqāʾi rabbihim la-kāfirūn · A-wa-lam yasīrū fi'l-arḍi fa-yanẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'lladhīna min qablihim, kānū ashadda minhum quwwatan wa athāru'l-arḍa wa ʿamarūhā akthara mimmā ʿamarūhā · Thumma kāna ʿāqibata'lladhīna asāʾu's-sūʾā an kadhdhabū bi-āyāti'llāh*

"**Have they never thought within themselves?** God created the heavens and the earth and what is between the two only **with truth and for a set term.** Yet most people deny the meeting with their Lord. **Have they not travelled in the earth and looked at how the end of those before them was?** They were stronger than these; **they turned the soil over and built the land up more than these have built it up.** Then **the end of those who did evil was the worst of ends** — because they had cried lies to the signs of God."

### أَوَلَمْ يَتَفَكَّرُوا۟ فِىٓ أَنفُسِهِم

**The place of the act of thinking is specified: *fī anfusihim* — "within themselves".**

**Two readings are transmitted for the construct:**

| Reading | What *fī anfusihim* attaches to |
|---|---|
| 1 | To the verb — "did they not think **within their own selves**" (the place itself: the inner world) |
| 2 | To the **subject** of the thinking — "did they not think **about their own selves**" |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The second reading overlaps with the *āfāq / anfus* pair at `041-fussilet.md` 41/53** — there the two fields of the evidence (the outer world and the person himself) were tabulated. **I rely on that treatment.**

**وَأَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى** — "a term whose name has been set". **The construct was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/3, at `039-zumer.md` 39/42 and at `035-fatir.md` 35/45.** **I do not repeat it here.** What was recorded there: **the term is fixed and its name has been set; but the one who set the name is not man.**

**And the place of the construct in this sūra is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** four verses earlier a political outcome had been bounded by **a few years** (*fī biḍʿi sinīn*). **Here the universe itself is bounded by a term.** **The two sentences say the same thing on two scales: the small event and the great order alike have a term falling due.**

### وَأَثَارُوا۟ ٱلْأَرْضَ وَعَمَرُوهَا

**This piece of evidence has been treated in several places in the series** — `035-fatir.md` 35/44 and `040-gafir.md` 40/21, 40/82. **I rely on those treatments and do not repeat them.**

**What belongs here are the two verbs this sūra adds, and the addition is worth recording:**

| Place | What is said of those before |
|---|---|
| Ghāfir 40/21 | *Kānū hum ashadda minhum **quwwatan wa āthāran fi'l-arḍ*** — power and **remains** |
| Fāṭir 35/44 | *Kānū ashadda minhum **quwwatan*** — power **only** |
| **al-Rūm 30/9** | *Ashadda minhum quwwatan **wa athāru'l-arḍa wa ʿamarūhā akthara mimmā ʿamarūhā*** — power, **the turning over of the soil**, and **building up more** |

**al-Rūm is the most detailed of the three, and it sets up a comparison: *akthara mimmā ʿamarūhā* — "more than these have built it up".** **That is, the superiority is granted not in power alone but in *what they made and did* as well.**

**أَثَارُوا۟** — **the root أ-ث-ر / ث-و-ر was analysed at `035-fatir.md` 35/9 (*fa-tuthīru saḥāban*):** to raise, to send up into the air, to lift from the ground and scatter; and the connection the lexicographers record with **ploughing and lifting the soil** (*thawr* — ox). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The usage here is worth recording:** in Fāṭir the verb is attributed **to a cloud**, here **to the soil**. **The same root, one in the sky and one on the ground.**

**عَمَرُوهَا** — root **ع-م-ر**: **to live long; and to make a place flourish, to build it up.** **The root was treated by way of *nuʿammirkum* (to give a lifespan) at `040-gafir.md` 40/67 and *a-wa-lam nuʿammirkum* at `035-fatir.md` 35/37.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**That the two branches of the root come together here is worth recording, and I give this as an observation about the language:** the same root means both **lifespan** and **building up.** **And the verse is recounting the end of a people that gave its lifespan to building up.** **The word itself gathers what is spent and what is made into a single root.**

### أَسَٰٓـُٔوا۟ ٱلسُّوٓأَىٰ

**The grammarians dwell on the parsing of the sentence, and two readings are transmitted:**

| Reading | What *al-sūʾā* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The **predicate** of *kāna* (*ʿāqibata* in the accusative) | "The end of those who did evil was **the worst of ends**" |
| 2 | The **object** of the verb *asāʾū* | "The end of those who did **the worst** was this…" |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**ٱلسُّوٓأَىٰ** — like *aswad/sawdāʾ*, **the feminine of the elative pattern** (*fuʿlā*): "the worst there is". **And its counterpart *al-ḥusnā* occurs elsewhere in the Qurʾān.** **That the word comes in this pattern must be recorded: the recompense is named not as a degree but as the end of the scale.**

**And the sentence of cause is worth recording:** *an kadhdhabū bi-āyāti'llāh* — **"because they cried lies to the signs of God".** **That is, the outcome is not attached to weakness or to a want of building up.** After granting their superiority in the ninth verse, the verse puts the cause somewhere else.

## 30/11-16 — ٱللَّهُ يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ ثُمَّ يُعِيدُهُۥ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ ٱلسَّاعَةُ يُبْلِسُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُم مِّن شُرَكَآئِهِمْ شُفَعَٰٓؤُا۟ وَكَانُوا۟ بِشُرَكَآئِهِمْ كَٰفِرِينَ · وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ ٱلسَّاعَةُ يَوْمَئِذٍ يَتَفَرَّقُونَ · فَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ فَهُمْ فِى رَوْضَةٍ يُحْبَرُونَ · وَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ وَكَذَّبُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا وَلِقَآئِ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ مُحْضَرُونَ

*Allāhu yabdaʾu'l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduhu thumma ilayhi turjaʿūn · Wa yawma taqūmu's-sāʿatu yublisu'l-mujrimūn · Wa lam yakun lahum min shurakāʾihim shufaʿāʾu wa kānū bi-shurakāʾihim kāfirīn · Wa yawma taqūmu's-sāʿatu yawmaʾidhin yatafarraqūn · Fa-amma'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti fa-hum fī rawḍatin yuḥbarūn · Wa amma'lladhīna kafarū wa kadhdhabū bi-āyātinā wa liqāʾi'l-ākhirati fa-ulāʾika fi'l-ʿadhābi muḥḍarūn*

"**God originates the creation, then repeats it; then to Him you are returned.** On the day the Hour stands, the guilty **lose all hope.** Of those they associated with Him no **intercessor** comes forward for them; and they in turn **deny** their associates. On the day the Hour stands, on that day **they part from one another.** Those who believed and did righteous deeds **are made glad in a garden.** But those who denied and cried lies to Our signs and to the meeting of the hereafter **are held in the punishment.**"

### يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ ثُمَّ يُعِيدُهُ

**This sentence occurs twice in the sūra: here (11) and in the twenty-seventh verse. This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and the difference must be recorded:**

| Verse | Sentence | What is added |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | *Allāhu yabdaʾu'l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduh* | *Thumma ilayhi turjaʿūn* — **the return** |
| **27** | *Wa huwa'lladhī yabdaʾu'l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduh* | *Wa huwa **ahwanu** ʿalayh* — **ease** |

**The same sentence, two different continuations.** It will be treated again at the twenty-seventh verse.

### يُبْلِسُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ — root: ب-ل-س

**The root was treated in `043-zuhruf.md`** (*mublisūn*) **and the core recorded there was this: the silent bewilderment that comes with the cutting off of hope; the state of finding nothing to say.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**Two things belong here:**

**One.** **The word occurs twice in this sūra** — here for the guilty (12), and in the forty-ninth verse for **the people before the rain** (*la-mublisīn*). **The same root, one in a scene of the resurrection and one in a drought.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it will be tabulated at the end.**

**Two.** **It was recorded in `043-zuhruf.md` that the name *Iblīs* has been connected with this root** — "the one who has despaired of mercy" — **and the note entered there holds here too:** this derivation is transmitted as it has been transmitted; the lexicographers disagree about the origin of the name, and in that disagreement no ruling is issued.

### يَتَفَرَّقُونَ — and the sūra's line of separation

**The root ف-ر-ق: to separate, to divide.** **And the word occurs twice in this sūra, in two different directions:**

| Verse | Expression | Who is separated | Its value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **14** | *Yawmaʾidhin **yatafarraqūn*** | **The two groups from one another** | **Verdict** — the separation comes to pass |
| **32** | *Alladhīna **farraqū** dīnahum* | **The religion is broken apart** | **Failing** — the separation is done |

**The same root, the one in Form V (reflexive), the other in Form II (transitive).** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of form:** in the fourteenth verse separation is something that **befalls**; in the thirty-second it is something **done**. **The sūra will recount division first as a failing and will then have shown it as an outcome — the ordering is reversed: the outcome first (14), the failing after (32).**

### يُحْبَرُونَ and مُحْضَرُونَ — two passive verbs

**يُحْبَرُونَ** — root **ح-ب-ر**. **The root was treated at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/70 (*antum wa azwājukum tuḥbarūn*).** **I do not repeat it here.** What was recorded there: **gladness and adornment; and *ḥibr*, ink, and *ḥabr/ḥibr*, a learned man — the connection the lexicographers draw is "a beauty that leaves a trace".**

**What belongs here is the setting of the two verbs face to face:**

| Group | Verb | Pattern | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|---|
| Those who believe | *Yuḥbarūn* | **Passive** | **They are made glad** — the agent is not named |
| Those who deny | *Muḥḍarūn* | **Passive participle** | **Brought / made to be present** |

**Both are passive.** **I record this as an observation about the word order:** the verb of neither side belongs to them. **And the same symmetry had been set up with different words at `032-secde.md` 32/19-20** (*jannātu'l-maʾwā* / *maʾwāhumu'n-nār*); **the two sūras build the same structure with two different pairs of words.**

**رَوْضَة** — root **ر-و-ض**: **low-lying ground that holds water and stays green; a garden.** From the same root comes *riyāḍa* (softening a thing and breaking it in); the lexicographers draw the connection through "the softness of the soil". **The word comes indefinite: *fī rawḍatin* — "in a garden".**

**وَكَانُوا۟ بِشُرَكَآئِهِمْ كَٰفِرِينَ** — "they deny their associates". **The same three-step sequence was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/13-14** (they do not hear → they cannot answer → in the end they refuse) **and the same structure was recorded at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/5-6 as well.** **I rely on those treatments.** **Here the third step is mentioned on its own: the relationship is refused from the other side.**

## 30/17-19 — فَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ حِينَ تُمْسُونَ وَحِينَ تُصْبِحُونَ · وَلَهُ ٱلْحَمْدُ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَعَشِيًّا وَحِينَ تُظْهِرُونَ · يُخْرِجُ ٱلْحَىَّ مِنَ ٱلْمَيِّتِ وَيُخْرِجُ ٱلْمَيِّتَ مِنَ ٱلْحَىِّ وَيُحْىِ ٱلْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا وَكَذَٰلِكَ تُخْرَجُونَ

*Fa-subḥāna'llāhi ḥīna tumsūna wa ḥīna tuṣbiḥūn · Wa lahu'l-ḥamdu fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa ʿashiyyan wa ḥīna tuẓhirūn · Yukhriju'l-ḥayya mina'l-mayyiti wa yukhriju'l-mayyita mina'l-ḥayyi wa yuḥyi'l-arḍa baʿda mawtihā, wa kadhālika tukhrajūn*

"**So glorify God when you enter the evening and when you come into the morning.** In the heavens and the earth, **in the late afternoon and when you come into the noon hour**, praise is His. **He brings the living out of the dead and the dead out of the living, and He gives life to the earth after its death. So shall you be brought out.**"

### The enumeration of the times

**Two verses enumerate four times, and the manner of the enumeration must be recorded — because two of them are verbs and two are nouns:**

| Time | Expression | Pattern | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ḥīna **tumsūn*** | **Verb** (Form IV) | **Entering the evening** |
| 2 | *Ḥīna **tuṣbiḥūn*** | **Verb** (Form IV) | **Coming into the morning** |
| 3 | ***ʿAshiyyan*** | **Noun** (adverbial) | **The late afternoon / the last part of the day** |
| 4 | *Ḥīna **tuẓhirūn*** | **Verb** (Form IV) | **Coming into the noon hour** |

**All three verbs are in Form IV (*ifʿāl*), and one use of this form in Arabic is "to enter upon a time":** *aṣbaḥa* — he entered the morning, *amsā* — he entered the evening, *aẓhara* — he entered the noon hour. **That is, the time is mentioned not as the name of a period but as the person's entering upon that time.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pattern:** the sentence does not say "glorify morning and evening"; it says **"when you come into the morning, when you enter the evening".** The subject is the person himself: **the time is set up not as something passed over but as something entered into.**

### A comparison with Ghāfir

**At `040-gafir.md` 40/55 the same command had been given with two times:** *wa sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika **bi'l-ʿashiyyi wa'l-ibkār*** — **in the late afternoon and early in the morning.** **What was recorded there was this: by naming the two extreme times, what lies between them is covered.**

**The two places can be set side by side, and the difference can be verified:**

| Place | Number of times | How they are named | The one addressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ghāfir 40/55 | **Two** — *al-ʿashiyy* and *al-ibkār* | **Noun** — the two extremes | **Singular** (*wa sabbiḥ*) |
| **al-Rūm 30/17-18** | **Four** — evening, morning, late afternoon, noon | **Three verbs, one noun** | **Plural** (*tumsūn…*) |

**Two differences are worth recording, and both can be verified from the text:**

**One — number.** Ghāfir names two extremes; **al-Rūm names four times and fills in the space between the two extremes as well.** **That is, the interval covered in Ghāfir is separately marked out in al-Rūm.**

**Two — the one addressed.** In Ghāfir the command is to the Prophet; **in al-Rūm the verbs are plural: to everyone.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that these two differences occur together:** al-Rūm's block accords with the seventh verse of the sūra. **There it had been said that the other side "knows the outside of the life of this world" — that is, living inside the day and seeing nothing from within the day.** **The seventeenth and eighteenth verses divide that same day into its times and set a turning in each of them.** The same day, the same hours; **the difference lies in what is done in those hours.**

**A note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** it is reported that in the classical commentaries these times are connected **with the times of the prayer**; some try to match the four times with the five. **I transmit these explanations as they have been transmitted, I draw no legal conclusion, and I do not impose an equating of numbers.**

### يُخْرِجُ ٱلْحَىَّ مِنَ ٱلْمَيِّتِ

**The sentence is built symmetrically: living↔dead, in both directions.** **And a third instance is added: *wa yuḥyi'l-arḍa baʿda mawtihā* — the coming to life of the soil.**

**This piece of evidence has been treated in many places in the series** — `035-fatir.md` 35/9 (*kadhālika'n-nushūr*), `050-kaf.md` 50/11 (*kadhālika'l-khurūj*), `041-fussilet.md` 41/39. **I rely on those treatments.**

**What belongs here is the closing word: *wa kadhālika tukhrajūn* — "so shall you be brought out".**

**And the verb is from the same root as the verse's own verb:** *yukhriju* (He brings out) — *tukhrajūn* (you will be brought out). **One active, one passive, the same root (خ-ر-ج).**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence builds the comparison not with a particle but **with the verb itself.** **The evidence and the conclusion share the same word.**

## 30/20 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦٓ أَنْ خَلَقَكُم مِّن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ إِذَآ أَنتُم بَشَرٌ تَنتَشِرُونَ

*Wa min āyātihi an khalaqakum min turābin thumma idhā antum basharun tantashirūn*

"**And of His signs is that:** He created you from dust; then **there you are, human beings spreading abroad.**"

### The backbone of the sūra: the ***wa min āyātihi*** series

**This formula occurs seven times in the sūra. Six of them come one after another (20-25), one in the forty-sixth verse.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it is the clearest structural marker of the sūra:**

| # | Verse | The sign | The closing — to whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **20** | **Creation from dust, and spreading abroad** | *(no closing — the series begins)* |
| 2 | **21** | **Spouses, rest, mawadda and mercy** | *Li-qawmin **yatafakkarūn*** — who think |
| 3 | **22** | **The heavens, the earth, the difference of tongues and colours** | *Li'l-**ʿālimīn*** — those who know |
| 4 | **23** | **Sleep by night, earning by day** | *Li-qawmin **yasmaʿūn*** — who hear |
| 5 | **24** | **Lightning: fear and hope; and water** | *Li-qawmin **yaʿqilūn*** — who reason |
| 6 | **25** | **The standing of heaven and earth by His command** | *(no closing — the series ends)* |
| 7 | **46** | **The sending of the winds as bearers of good news** | *Wa laʿallakum **tashkurūn*** — who give thanks |

**Three things are worth recording, and all three can be verified from the text:**

**One — the framing of the series.** **The first link (20) and the last (25) have no *li-qawmin…* closing.** The four in the middle (21-24) have one. **That is, the series stands enclosed between two links without a closing.**

**Two — the changing of the closings.** The four closings use four different verbs: **to think, to know, to hear, to reason.** **The same formula addresses a different capacity each time.**

**Three — the range.** The series begins **with the person himself** (20-21), opens out **to the universe and to peoples** (22), returns **to daily life** (23), rises **to the sky** (24), and ends **with the standing of the order** (25). **Inward → outward → inward → outward.** **The alternation of *āfāq / anfus* tabulated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/53 works here in the same way; I rely on that treatment.**

### أَنْ خَلَقَكُم مِّن تُرَابٍ

**The same beginning (*min turāb*) was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/11 and at `040-gafir.md` 40/67.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the second half of the sentence, and a detail of word order must be recorded:**

> *thumma **idhā** antum basharun tantashirūn*

**The lexicographers call this use of *idhā* in Arabic *idhā fujāʾiyya* (the *idhā* that conveys suddenness):** "and there you are". **That is, the sentence skips the process in between and shows the result directly.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** all the stages between the beginning (dust) and the result (a humankind spread abroad) are packed **into a single particle.** **The sentence itself skips the distance in order to convey the distance.**

**تَنتَشِرُونَ** — root **ن-ش-ر**: **to spread, to open out, to lay out.** *Nashara'th-thawba* — he opened out the cloth. **And from the same root comes *nushūr* (the resurrection — the spreading abroad of the dead); the word had occurred at `035-fatir.md` 35/9 (*kadhālika'n-nushūr*).**

**That the two uses of the root come together in this sūra is worth recording, and I give this as an observation about the language:** *tantashirūn* here conveys **a spreading abroad by birth**, *nushūr* in the other place **a spreading abroad by resurrection.** **The same picture, for two different beginnings.** **And the preceding verse (19) had already established this link by saying *tukhrajūn*.**

## 30/21 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦٓ أَنْ خَلَقَ لَكُم مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَٰجًا لِّتَسْكُنُوٓا۟ إِلَيْهَا وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَكُم مَّوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَٰتٍ لِّقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ

*Wa min āyātihi an khalaqa lakum min anfusikum azwājan li-taskunū ilayhā wa jaʿala baynakum mawaddatan wa raḥmah, inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-qawmin yatafakkarūn*

"**And of His signs is that:** He created for you spouses **from among yourselves** — **that you may warm to them and come to rest;** and **He placed love and mercy between you.** In that there are surely signs for a people who think."

### مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ

**Two readings are transmitted for the construct in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | What *min anfusikum* means |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Of the same kind** — the spouse was created of the human kind; not of another kind |
| 2 | **From within your own species** — from your stock, from among you |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **Both come out at the same place, and this is what the sentence is doing: the spouse is mentioned not as a being brought in from outside but as being of the same kind.**

### لِّتَسْكُنُوٓا۟ إِلَيْهَا — root: س-ك-ن

**The root was analysed in detail by way of *sakīna* at `048-fetih.md` 48/4.** **I do not repeat it here.** The core recorded there: **to stop, to become motionless, to settle in a place.** *Sukūn* — the ceasing of movement; *maskan* — **not "house" but the place where movement ends.**

**What belongs here is the particle, and it must be recorded.**

**The verb *sakana* is normally used with the particle *fī* or *bi*: *sakana fi'd-dār* — he dwelt in the house.** **Here, however, *ilā* comes:** *li-taskunū ***ilayhā*** — **"that you may come to rest towards them".**

***Ilā* is a particle of direction.** **That is, the sentence says not "come to rest in them" but "come to rest towards them".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of particle:** had it been built with *fī*, the spouse would have been named as a **place** — somewhere to settle into. Built with *ilā*, **a turning** is being described: coming to rest is the subsiding of movement in a direction. **And the lexicographers record this difference of particle; I draw no ruling beyond this point.**

**And the link with *sakīna* in `048-fetih.md` is worth recording:** there the same root was used for a coming to rest **sent down into the heart** — under the conditions of war. **Here the same root is used for the relationship between two people.**

| Place | Word | Where the rest is |
|---|---|---|
| al-Fatḥ 48/4 | *Sakīna* | **In the heart** — sent down |
| **al-Rūm 30/21** | *Li-taskunū ilayhā* | **In the relationship** — turned towards |

**The same root does the same work in two different places: the subsiding of movement.** This is an overlap between sūras that can be verified.

### مَّوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً — why the two words come together

**Both words are words of relationship, and both come indefinite. But they do not say the same thing.**

**مَوَدَّة — root: و-د-د.** **The root has been treated in many places in the series** — at `060-mumtehine.md` 60/7 (*an yajʿala baynakum wa bayna'lladhīna ʿādaytum minhum **mawadda***), at `042-sura.md` 42/23 (*al-mawaddata fi'l-qurbā*), in `085-buruc.md` (*al-Wadūd*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The core of the root: to love — and the joining of love with *wanting*.** The lexicographers generally record the difference between *wudd* and *ḥubb* thus: *ḥubb* is the feeling itself, *wudd* **the feeling in its outwardly expressed, wanted state.** The verb *wadda* means "he wanted, he desired" as much as "he loved". **I transmit this distinction as the lexicographers record it.**

**رَحْمَة — root: ر-ح-م.** **The root was analysed in `001-fatiha.md` and in many places in the series;** the core: **رَحِم** — **the womb, the shelter in the mother's belly.** **That is, mercy carries in its root a *place where a thing is protected and made to grow*.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference between the two, and why they are mentioned together:**

| | *Mawadda* | *Raḥma* |
|---|---|---|
| The picture in the root | **Turning towards, wanting** | **Sheltering, protecting** |
| Its direction | **A mutual attraction** | **Can also run from the strong towards the weak** |
| What it depends on | **The attractiveness of the other** | **The need of the other** |
| When it operates | **While closeness is there** | **Also when closeness has weakened** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pictures the two roots themselves carry:** *mawadda* is a mutual attraction and rests on there being a certain **desirability** in the other. *Raḥma*, however, answers not to desirability but **to need** — the womb in its root shelters a being from which nothing is expected.

**That is, the mentioning of the two words together is not a repetition but a completion:** the first builds the **attraction** side of the relationship, the second its **durability** side. **What holds the relationship when the attraction weakens is the second.**

**And the verb must be recorded: *wa jaʿala baynakum* — "He placed between you". Neither word is attributed to one of the two parties but to *what is between them*. I record this as an observation about the word order: the sentence does not say "He created you full of love"; it speaks of something placed into the relationship itself.**

**And the closing must be recorded:** *li-qawmin **yatafakkarūn*** — **for a people who think.** **The first closing of the series is given to the nearest relationship.**

## 30/22 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ خَلْقُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱخْتِلَٰفُ أَلْسِنَتِكُمْ وَأَلْوَٰنِكُمْ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَٰتٍ لِّلْعَٰلِمِينَ

*Wa min āyātihi khalqu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa'khtilāfu alsinatikum wa alwānikum, inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li'l-ʿālimīn*

"**And of His signs: the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the difference of your tongues and your colours.** In that there are surely signs for those who know."

### The equating in the word order

**The sentence places two things in the same list, and this can be verified from the word order:**

> *khalqu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* **and** *ikhtilāfu alsinatikum wa alwānikum*

**Both elements hang on the same *min āyātihi* predicate, in the same case.** **That is, the creation of the heavens and the fact that people speak differently and look different from one another are enumerated side by side in a single sentence.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this equality in the word order:** diversity is mentioned not as a **by-product** of creation but as a **sign** ranked with the heavens and the earth.

### A comparison with the list of colours in Fāṭir

**At `035-fatir.md` 35/27-28 the diversity of colours was treated in detail, and it was tabulated there that the construct *mukhtalifun alwānuhā* is repeated three times:** plant, stone, living creature. **And the sentence that came immediately after that list was this: *innamā yakhsha'llāha min ʿibādihi'l-**ʿulamāʾ***.** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

**The two places can be set side by side, and the overlap must be recorded:**

| | Fāṭir 35/27-28 | **al-Rūm 30/22** |
|---|---|---|
| What the diversity is in | **Colour** — in three domains (plant, stone, living creature) | **Colour *and tongue*** — in man alone |
| How many times | *Mukhtalifun alwānuhā* — **three times** | *Ikhtilāf* — **once, with two objects** |
| What comes after | *Innamā yakhsha'llāha … al-**ʿulamāʾ*** | *La-āyātin li'l-**ʿālimīn*** |
| The word the link runs through | **عُلَمَاء** — those who know | **عَالِمِين** — those who know |

**Both sūras end, after recounting the diversity of colours, with the root of *ʿilm* (ع-ل-م).** **This is an overlap that can be verified from the text, and it comes in the same order in the two sūras: first the list of diversity, then the reference to those who know.**

**And the finding recorded at `035-fatir.md` 35/28 holds here too:** the sentence is set down not as an abstract commendation but **as the conclusion of a list of observations.** **A link is made between the person who notices and knowledge.**

**Two differences are worth recording:**

**One — the pattern of the word.** In Fāṭir *ʿulamāʾ* (the learned, a plural), in al-Rūm *al-ʿālimīn* (those who know, the plural of the active participle). **al-Rūm's pattern is the wider one: not the name of a class, but everyone who performs the act.**

**Two — the field of the diversity.** Fāṭir spreads the diversity **across three domains.** **al-Rūm stays with man alone — but adds two dimensions: colour *and tongue*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this second difference:** when *alsina* (tongues) is set beside colour, diversity opens out from a difference that is **seen** to one that is also **heard.** **Colour is noticed by looking, language by speaking.** **That is, the second element carries diversity into a field that requires an encounter.**

### Reading it together with al-Ḥujurāt 49/13

**At `049-hucurat.md` 49/13 the purpose of diversity was treated: *wa jaʿalnākum shuʿūban wa qabāʾila li-taʿārafū* — "We made you peoples and tribes, that you might come to know one another**".

**The two findings recorded there work directly here as well:**

**One.** **"The verse does not abolish groups. It establishes them."** The verb is *wa jaʿalnākum*; the distinction is recorded not as a failing or as an obstacle to be got past but **as an act of creation.**

**Two.** **That the root of *shaʿb* (ش-ع-ب) is counted by the lexicographers among the *aḍdād*** — meaning both *to divide* and *to join* — and the shared idea beneath it: **"branches separating from one trunk are both separated and attached to the same trunk."**

**The two verses can be set side by side, and that they complete one another must be recorded:**

| | al-Ḥujurāt 49/13 | **al-Rūm 30/22** |
|---|---|---|
| Where the diversity is | **In the structure of a people** — nation, tribe | **In the person** — tongue and colour |
| What the sentence gives | ***Purpose*** — *li-taʿārafū* (that you might come to know one another) | ***Attribute*** — *āya* (sign) |
| The closing | *Inna akramakum ʿinda'llāhi **atqākum*** — **a measure** | *La-āyātin li'l-**ʿālimīn*** — **an understanding** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses give different elements:** al-Ḥujurāt says **what diversity is for** (coming to know one another); **al-Rūm says *what it is* (a sign).** **Read together, the two yield a complete sentence: diversity is a sign, and the work the sign points to is coming to know one another.**

**And the note entered in `049-hucurat.md` holds here too, and I repeat it:** no blanket ruling about an ethnic or a linguistic group is to be drawn from this. **What the verse enumerates is the difference itself; that the difference is not a measure of superiority al-Ḥujurāt says plainly.**

**أَلْسِنَة** — the plural of *lisān*; **root ل-س-ن: tongue (the organ) and tongue (speech).** **The same word was treated at `044-duhan.md` 44/58 (*yassarnāhu bi-lisānik*)**, and it was recorded there that the expression "in your tongue" bounds **the instrument of the making-easy.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The relation between the two places is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** in al-Dukhān the text is made easy in **one** tongue; **in al-Rūm the *multiplicity* of tongues is counted a sign.** **The two do not contradict: the one describes the address, the other describes humankind.**

## 30/23-25 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ مَنَامُكُم بِٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ وَٱبْتِغَآؤُكُم مِّن فَضْلِهِۦٓ · وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ يُرِيكُمُ ٱلْبَرْقَ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا وَيُنَزِّلُ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءً فَيُحْىِۦ بِهِ ٱلْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَآ · وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦٓ أَن تَقُومَ ٱلسَّمَآءُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ بِأَمْرِهِۦ ثُمَّ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ دَعْوَةً مِّنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ إِذَآ أَنتُمْ تَخْرُجُونَ

*Wa min āyātihi manāmukum bi'l-layli wa'n-nahāri wa'btighāʾukum min faḍlih · Wa min āyātihi yurīkumu'l-barqa khawfan wa ṭamaʿan wa yunazzilu mina's-samāʾi māʾan fa-yuḥyī bihi'l-arḍa baʿda mawtihā · Wa min āyātihi an taqūma's-samāʾu wa'l-arḍu bi-amrih, thumma idhā daʿākum daʿwatan mina'l-arḍi idhā antum takhrujūn*

"**And of His signs is:** your **sleeping** by night and by day, and your **seeking of gain** out of His bounty. **And of His signs is:** His showing you the lightning **as fear and as hope**, and His sending down water from the sky and giving life with it to the earth after its death. **And of His signs is:** the standing of the heaven and the earth **by His command.** Then, when He calls you with a call from the earth, **there you are, coming forth.**"

### مَنَامُكُم بِٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ

**The grammarians dwell on the parsing of the sentence, and two readings are transmitted:**

| Reading | What *bi'l-layli wa'n-nahāri* attaches to |
|---|---|
| 1 | **To both** — to sleep and to gain alike: one sleeps and works by night as by day |
| 2 | **Distributively**: sleep to **the night**, gain to **the day** — the word order we call *laff wa nashr* |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**I record a ground from the text for the first reading, as an observation:** the sentence places the expression *bi'l-layli wa'n-nahār* immediately after the word for **sleep**, before the word for gain. **The word order is favourable to the first reading; but the second is also within the limits Arabic allows.**

**The same pair was treated at `040-gafir.md` 40/61:** *alladhī jaʿala lakumu'l-layla **li-taskunū** fīhi wa'n-nahāra **mubṣirā*** — there the night was attached **to coming to rest**, the day **to seeing.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the difference must be recorded:** in Ghāfir the night's verb is *sukūn* (س-ك-ن) — **the same root that was used two verses earlier for spouses** (30/21: *li-taskunū ilayhā*). **In al-Rūm, by contrast, the night's counterpart is *manām* (sleep).**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this recurrence of the root:** in the twenty-first verse *sukūn* had been attached to a **relationship**, in Ghāfir to a **time of day.** **al-Rūm here names sleep with a separate word** — that is, within the sūra the root *sukūn* is left to the spouses.

**ٱبْتِغَآؤُكُم مِّن فَضْلِهِ** — root **ب-غ-ي**, Form VIII: **to seek, to want, to go after.** **And *min faḍlihi* — "out of His bounty".**

**The construct is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the object of the verb of working is not "provision" or "wealth" but ***faḍl*** — **bounty.** **That is, gain is attached to man as something sought, and, as to its source, to something given.** **The sentence holds both sides in a single construct: the seeking is man's, what is sought is His.** **The same distinction was tabulated at `045-casiye.md` 45/12 (in the discussion of *taskhīr*); I rely on that treatment.**

**And the closing must be recorded:** *li-qawmin **yasmaʿūn*** — **for a people who hear.** **That the closing of *hearing* should come for sleep and for gain is worth recording** — among the four closings of the series this is the only name of a sense.

### يُرِيكُمُ ٱلْبَرْقَ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا

**There is an irregularity in the word order, and it must be recorded:** in the other links, after *min āyātihi* comes **a noun** or ***an* + a verb.** **Here an imperfect verb comes directly: *yurīkum*.**

**The grammarians generally explain this by "an *an* or a verbal noun being implied".** **I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

**But the verb's coming directly does something, and I record this as an observation about the word order: the other links enumerated a state; this link comes with a verb of *showing* — *yurīkum*, "He shows you". That is, here the sign is mentioned not as an object but as an act.**

**خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا** — "as fear and as hope". **Both verbal nouns stand in the circumstantial accusative.**

**The same pair had occurred at `032-secde.md` 32/16:** *yadʿūna rabbahum **khawfan wa ṭamaʿan*** — there it was the state of the person who prays. **Here it is the state that the lightning stirs in the one who looks.**

| Place | Whose state *khawfan wa ṭamaʿan* is | Towards what |
|---|---|---|
| al-Sajda 32/16 | **The one who prays** | **His Lord** |
| **al-Rūm 30/24** | **The one who looks** | **The lightning** |

**The same pair, in two different places.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses share the same construct:** in neither is there a feeling running in one direction only. **Lightning carries both the prospect of harm and the prospect of rain; and the verse names the two together.** **That is, the sign is not given in a form to be read as having a single meaning.**

**بَرْق** — root **ب-ر-ق**: **to shine, to flash.** *Bariqa baṣaruhu* — his sight was dazzled, he froze in bewilderment; and this branch of the root was treated at `075-kiyame.md` 75/7 (*fa-idhā bariqa'l-baṣar*). **I rely on that treatment.** From the same root come *burāq* (glittering) and *ibrīq* (a bright vessel).

**A scene in which the word occurs had been treated in detail at `002-bakara.md` 2/19-20:** the man caught in a downpour, who took a few steps whenever the lightning flashed and froze when the darkness came down (*yakādu'l-barqu yakhṭafu abṣārahum*). **The finding recorded there was this: the advance is intermittent and dependent on outward conditions.**

**The two places can be compared, and the difference is worth recording:** in al-Baqara the lightning is **inside a comparison** and is part of a negative portrait; **in al-Rūm the same event is enumerated directly as an *āya*.** **The same image, the one used to describe a failing, the other as a sign.**

### أَن تَقُومَ ٱلسَّمَآءُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ بِأَمْرِهِ

**The last link of the series enumerates something different from those before it: not an event but a continuance.**

**At `035-fatir.md` 35/41 the same idea had been conveyed with the verb *imsāk* (to hold):** *inna'llāha yumsiku's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa **an tazūlā*** — "He holds them lest they pass away." **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two places can be set side by side, and the difference must be recorded:**

| Place | Verb | Who does it | The picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/41 | *Yumsiku* — **He holds** | **God** | **Holding from outside** |
| **al-Rūm 30/25** | *Taqūma … bi-amrih* — **it stands** | **The heaven and the earth** (by the command) | **Standing of themselves** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the subject of the verb:** in Fāṭir the subject is the one who holds; **in al-Rūm the subjects are the ones that stand — but with the qualification *bi-amrihi*.** **The two verses recount the same fact from two different sides: the one the holding, the other the standing.** **The finding recorded at `035-fatir.md` 35/41 holds for both: the continuance is not of itself; it is sustained.**

### ثُمَّ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ دَعْوَةً مِّنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ إِذَآ أَنتُمْ تَخْرُجُونَ

**And the series closes by returning to where it began.**

**In the twentieth verse there was *thumma idhā antum basharun tantashirūn* — the *idhā* that conveys suddenness. In the twenty-fifth verse the same structure comes back: *idhā antum takhrujūn*.**

| Verse | Expression | The beginning of what |
|---|---|---|
| **20** | *Thumma **idhā antum** basharun **tantashirūn*** | **This life** — spreading abroad out of the soil |
| **25** | *Thumma **idhā antum** takhrujūn* | **The next** — coming out of the ground |

**The same formula, at the two ends of the series.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this overlap of formula:** the series opens with a coming out of the earth and closes with a coming out of the earth. **The four links in between (21-24) are the life that passes between the two.** **That is, the *wa min āyātihi* series is framed so as to cover the two ends of a human lifetime and what lies between them.**

**دَعْوَةً — the absolute object (an emphasis by the verbal noun): "when He calls you with a call". In Arabic this pattern reinforces the verb. And the choice of the word is worth recording: the resurrection is conveyed not by a compulsion but by a call.**

## 30/26-27 — وَلَهُۥ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ كُلٌّ لَّهُۥ قَٰنِتُونَ · وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى يَبْدَؤُا۟ ٱلْخَلْقَ ثُمَّ يُعِيدُهُۥ وَهُوَ أَهْوَنُ عَلَيْهِ وَلَهُ ٱلْمَثَلُ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ

*Wa lahu man fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ, kullun lahu qānitūn · Wa huwa'lladhī yabdaʾu'l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduhu wa huwa ahwanu ʿalayh, wa lahu'l-mathalu'l-aʿlā fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ, wa huwa'l-ʿazīzu'l-ḥakīm*

"**Whoever is in the heavens and the earth is His; all are submissive to Him.** **It is He who originates the creation, then repeats it — and that is easier for Him.** In the heavens and the earth **the highest attribute** is His. He is the Mighty, the Wise."

**قَٰنِتُونَ** — root **ق-ن-ت**. **The root was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/9 (*amman huwa qānitun ānāʾa'l-layl*).** **I do not repeat it here.** The core: **to stand continuously in one state, to persist in obedience.** The lexicographers particularly record the **continuance** side of the word — *qunūt* is not a single act of obedience but **a standing fast.**

**The range of the word here must be recorded:** *kullun lahu qānitūn* — **all.** **That is, the continuance is mentioned not as an attitude left to choice but as the state of all that exists.**

### وَهُوَ أَهْوَنُ عَلَيْهِ — a disagreement

**There is a debate over this sentence in the classical commentaries, and it must not be concealed.**

**The problem is this:** *ahwan* is an elative — "easier". **But since degrees of difficulty and ease cannot be set up concerning God, what is the word doing here?**

| Reading | How *ahwanu ʿalayh* is understood |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The pattern is not comparative**: *ahwan* here means "easy" and conveys no comparison. Arabic has this use of the elative |
| 2 | **By the measure of man**: the sentence speaks to the hearer's own logic — by your measure, doing a thing a second time is easier |
| 3 | **The pronoun belongs to the created thing**: the pronoun in *ʿalayh* refers back to what is created — the restoration is easier for the created thing |

**All three readings are transmitted in the classical commentaries. I do not impose a preference, and I generate no account of the "how" concerning the Essence.**

**The continuation of the sentence enters a note of its own, and I transmit that note:** *wa lahu'l-**mathalu'l-aʿlā*** — **"the highest attribute is His".** **That this comes immediately after that sentence is worth recording:** after a word of comparison has been used, a boundary is set as to how comparison is to be framed concerning God.

**ٱلْمَثَلُ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ** — *mathal* means "attribute, quality" as much as "likeness". **The root of *mathal* (م-ث-ل) was treated in `043-zuhruf.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.**

## 30/28-29 — ضَرَبَ لَكُم مَّثَلًا مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ هَل لَّكُم مِّن مَّا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَٰنُكُم مِّن شُرَكَآءَ فِى مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ فَأَنتُمْ فِيهِ سَوَآءٌ تَخَافُونَهُمْ كَخِيفَتِكُمْ أَنفُسَكُمْ كَذَٰلِكَ نُفَصِّلُ ٱلْـَٔايَٰتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَعْقِلُونَ · بَلِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوٓا۟ أَهْوَآءَهُم بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ

*Ḍaraba lakum mathalan min anfusikum, hal lakum min mā malakat aymānukum min shurakāʾa fī mā razaqnākum fa-antum fīhi sawāʾun takhāfūnahum ka-khīfatikum anfusakum, kadhālika nufaṣṣilu'l-āyāti li-qawmin yaʿqilūn · Bali'ttabaʿa'lladhīna ẓalamū ahwāʾahum bi-ghayri ʿilm*

"**He has struck for you a parable from among yourselves:** among those your right hands possess, have you **partners** in what We have provided for you — such that you would be **equal** in it, and **would stand in awe of them as you stand in awe of one another?** Thus do We **set out the signs in detail** for a people who reason. **No; those who did wrong followed their own desires without knowledge.**"

### مَّثَلًا مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ

**The source of the parable is announced: *min anfusikum* — "from among yourselves".**

**And the construct had occurred in the twenty-first verse as well:** *khalaqa lakum **min anfusikum** azwājā.* **The same construct, twice in the sūra: one for creation, the other for a parable.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse brings its evidence not from a field the hearer knows nothing of but **from within his own life** — as, in the third verse of the sūra, the event took place at *adna'l-arḍ* (in the nearest land). **The sūra keeps on holding the distance of its evidence short.**

### How the parable works

**The question is this:** would you make those under your own hand **partners equal in your property**? **And would you stand in awe of them as you do of your own kind?**

**The answer is not stated.** **In Arabic this is *istifhām inkārī*: a negation in the form of a question.**

**And the logic of the sentence must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** the parable reminds the hearer of his own measure. **A person does not accept equality within his own property; yet he counts this acceptable concerning God.** **The verse offers no proof — it shows the hearer his own inconsistency.**

**A note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** the construct *mā malakat aymānukum* here recalls a reality belonging to the social order of that period, and the verse recalls it **not in order to endorse that order but in order to set up a parable.** **The Qurʾān's rulings in this field are a separate matter; I draw no legal conclusion here.** **The work the verse does is to show the hearer an equality he does not himself accept.**

**كَذَٰلِكَ نُفَصِّلُ ٱلْـَٔايَٰتِ** — **the root ف-ص-ل and the *tafṣīl* pattern were analysed in detail at `041-fussilet.md` 41/3:** to divide a whole into its parts, to set out in detail. **I do not repeat it here.**

**بَلِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوٓا۟ أَهْوَآءَهُم بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ** — and the cause is stated: **following one's desire.**

**The same line was treated at `045-casiye.md` 45/23** (*mani'ttakhadha ilāhahu hawāh*), and two parsings were tabulated there. **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference here is worth recording:** in al-Jāthiya the desire was what is **taken as a god**; **in al-Rūm the desire is what is *followed* (*ittabaʿa*).** **And a note is added: *bi-ghayri ʿilm* — without knowledge.**

**This note must be read together with the seventh verse of the sūra, and I give this as my own reading:** in the seventh verse it had been said that the other side **has a certain knowledge** (*yaʿlamūna ẓāhiran*). **Here it is said that what they follow is without knowledge.** **The two do not contradict: there is knowledge about the outside of the life of this world, and none about the direction being followed.** **The sūra draws a line between the presence of knowledge and its power to give direction.**

## 30/30 — فَأَقِمْ وَجْهَكَ لِلدِّينِ حَنِيفًا فِطْرَتَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلَّتِى فَطَرَ ٱلنَّاسَ عَلَيْهَا لَا تَبْدِيلَ لِخَلْقِ ٱللَّهِ ذَٰلِكَ ٱلدِّينُ ٱلْقَيِّمُ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*Fa-aqim wajhaka li'd-dīni ḥanīfā, fiṭrata'llāhi'llatī faṭara'n-nāsa ʿalayhā, lā tabdīla li-khalqi'llāh, dhālika'd-dīnu'l-qayyim wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn*

"**Set your face towards the religion, as a ḥanīf:** towards the fiṭra of God, **upon which He created mankind.** **There is no changing in God's creating.** This is **the upright religion**; but most people do not know."

### فِطْرَة — root: ف-ط-ر

**The root has been analysed in two places in the series:** at `035-fatir.md` 35/1 (*fāṭira's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*) and at `039-zumer.md` 39/46; also in `082-infitar.md` (*idha's-samāʾu'nfaṭarat*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The core recorded there was this: the concrete sense of the root is *to split, to crack open*.** *Faṭara'sh-shayʾa* — he split it. **And the lexicographers record that the root's sense of "bringing a thing out for the first time" comes from this picture:** the bud splits and the leaf comes out. **From the same root come *ifṭār* (breaking the fast) and *infiṭār* (being split open).**

**What belongs here is the pattern — and this is what marks this sūra out:**

| Place | Word | Pattern | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/1 · al-Zumar 39/46 | **فَاطِر** | **Active participle** | **The doer** — the creator |
| al-Infiṭār 82/1 | **ٱنفَطَرَتْ** | **Form VII verb** | **The event** — being split open |
| **al-Rūm 30/30** | **فِطْرَة** | **The فِعْلَة pattern** | **The manner of the doing** |

**The فِعْلَة pattern in Arabic conveys *hayʾa* (form, manner):** *jilsa* — the manner of sitting; *mīta* — the manner of dying; *rijla* — the manner of walking. **It shows not a single performance of the verb but *how* it is performed.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pattern: *fiṭra* is not "creation" but "the manner of creation". The word names not that man has been brought into being but *upon what manner* he has been brought into being. And the verse explains this itself: *allatī faṭara'n-nāsa ʿalayhā* — "upon which He created mankind". The particle *ʿalā* — upon: it conveys a ground to stand on.**

**And the grammarians dwell on the parsing of the word:** *fiṭrata'llāh* comes in **the accusative**. **The widespread explanation is that it is the object of an implied verb:** *ilzamū fiṭrata'llāh* — "hold fast to the fiṭra of God". **I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted.**

### حَنِيفًا — root: ح-ن-ف

**The root was analysed in detail at `098-beyyine.md` 98/5, and this verse was already cited there.** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.** A summary of what was recorded there:

- **The root: to incline in one direction, to bend, to turn.**
- **The concrete branch:** *al-aḥnaf*, according to the lexicographers, the person **whose feet turn inward**. **That is, the root first names a deviation.**
- **And *ḥanīf* conveys the turning back from that deviation:** to turn from a crooked stance — and since it is a turning, it is again an inclining, but towards what is straight.
- **The sister root ج-ن-ف:** the same movement, the opposite direction — **to incline towards injustice.**
- **The function of the word: it is not the name of a camp but the name of a direction.**

**What belongs here is the word's position in the sentence:** *fa-aqim wajhaka li'd-dīni **ḥanīfā*** — *ḥanīfan* stands in the **circumstantial accusative**. **That is, it says not "turn to the ḥanīf religion" but "turn to the religion as a ḥanīf".** **What is qualified is not the religion but the person who turns.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the parsing** (the grammarians generally record that *ḥanīfan* is a circumstantial accusative): **the verse describes not an attribute of the religion but the manner of turning towards it.** **And this accords with the picture in the root of the word — the root conveys a *stance*.**

**فَأَقِمْ وَجْهَكَ** — "set your face straight". **The root ق-و-م and the concept of *iqāma* were analysed at `002-bakara.md` 2/3;** **I do not repeat it here.** **And the object of the verb is *wajh* (face) — a bodily picture.** **As was recorded in `098-beyyine.md`, at al-Anʿām 6/79 too being a ḥanīf is mentioned with a verb of *turning the face*** (*wajjahtu wajhī*). **The sense of inclining in the root stands here as a bodily movement.**

### لَا تَبْدِيلَ لِخَلْقِ ٱللَّهِ — a statement or a prohibition

**There is a disagreement over this sentence in the classical commentaries, and I give it in a table:**

| Reading | The mood of the sentence | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Statement** — a report of a fact | "In God's creating **there is no changing**" — the fiṭra cannot be altered; whoever tries to alter it cannot abolish it |
| 2 | **A statement carrying the force of a prohibition** — a declarative sentence conveying a ban | "Let God's creating **not be changed**" — in Arabic it is common to set up a prohibition in declarative form |

**Both readings are transmitted in the classical commentaries and are within the limits Arabic allows. I do not impose a preference.**

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write it plainly: I frame no legal ruling from this sentence.** **This verse has, throughout history, been used as evidence for a great many concrete questions; I take no side in any of those debates, and I issue no ruling that narrows or widens the scope of *khalqu'llāh*.** **The boundary set for the word *ʿulamāʾ* at `035-fatir.md` 35/28 holds here too: I frame no ruling that fixes the scope of the word.**

**I record the one thing that can be verified from the context of the sentence:** the sentence comes immediately after the word **fiṭra**, and it is tied back to it by the construct *khalqu'llāh*. **The context is the manner of man's creation.**

### ذَٰلِكَ ٱلدِّينُ ٱلْقَيِّمُ and the overlap with al-Bayyina

**At `098-beyyine.md` 98/5 this verse and what follows it (30-32) were compared in detail with al-Bayyina, and it was tabulated there that six elements overlap.** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat the table.** Its summary: the sequence **being a ḥanīf → the upright religion → the prayer → not dividing** is built in the two sūras in almost the same words.

**And the difference recorded there belongs here:** **al-Rūm adds two things al-Bayyina does not say — the *fiṭra* and *the psychology of division*.** **The second will be treated at the thirty-second verse.**

**ذَٰلِكَ** — the far demonstrative; it conveys *taʿẓīm*. **The same use had been recorded in `098-beyyine.md`.**

## 30/31-32 — مُنِيبِينَ إِلَيْهِ وَٱتَّقُوهُ وَأَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَلَا تَكُونُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ فَرَّقُوا۟ دِينَهُمْ وَكَانُوا۟ شِيَعًا كُلُّ حِزْبٍۭ بِمَا لَدَيْهِمْ فَرِحُونَ

*Munībīna ilayhi wa'ttaqūhu wa aqīmu'ṣ-ṣalāta wa lā takūnū mina'l-mushrikīn · Mina'lladhīna farraqū dīnahum wa kānū shiyaʿan, kullu ḥizbin bimā ladayhim fariḥūn*

"**Turning to Him;** and be mindful of Him, and hold the prayer upright, and **be not of the mushrikūn** — **of those who broke their religion apart and became sects.** **Each party rejoices in what it holds.**"

### مُنِيبِينَ — root: ن-و-ب

**The concrete sense of the root: to return to a place again and again, to come round by turns. *Nāba* — he returned; *nawba* — a turn of watch, a task that comes round in rotation. Turkish has borrowed this root: *nöbet* (a turn of duty, a watch) and *münâvebe* (alternation) come from it.**

**The pattern is Form IV (*ināba*): to turn back and face towards, to repent and come back.**

**The idea of repetition in the root is worth recording, and I give this as an observation about the language:** *ināba* is not a single act of turning but **a turning back over and over.** **And the word occurs twice in this sūra:**

| Verse | Who | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **31** | **The one addressed** — in the context of a command | *Munībīna ilayh* — **as a continuous state** |
| **33** | **People in general** | *Daʿaw rabbahum **munībīna** ilayh* — **at a moment of distress** |

**The same word, two verses apart, in two different conditions.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it will be treated at the thirty-third verse — because there the *taking back* of a turning is recounted after the word.**

### فَرَّقُوا۟ دِينَهُمْ وَكَانُوا۟ شِيَعًا

**شِيَع** — the plural of *shīʿa*; **root ش-ي-ع: to follow someone, to go behind him; and to spread.** *Shāʿa'l-khabar* — the news spread. *Shīʿa* is **the group that goes behind a person or an opinion.**

**حِزْب** — root **ح-ز-ب**: a group gathered together; and the lexicographers record the root's branch of **"hardship, distress"** as well (*ḥazabahu'l-amr* — the matter pressed hard on him). **They explain the connection by the picture of "pressing together and gathering around a single question".**

**The three words build a series, and the word order must be recorded:**

| Stage | Word | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ***Farraqū dīnahum*** | **The religion was broken apart** — the verb is Form II (transitive, intensive) |
| 2 | ***Kānū shiyaʿan*** | **Groups formed** |
| 3 | ***Kullu ḥizbin … fariḥūn*** | **Each group is content with its own share** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this three-stage sequence:** the division is not recounted as an event and then left; **what happens afterwards is stated too.** **And what is stated is not a pain but a contentment.**

### بِمَا لَدَيْهِمْ فَرِحُونَ — why the division persists

**In `098-beyyine.md` this sentence was compared with al-Bayyina 98/4, and this was recorded:** *"Why does division persist? Because each piece is content with its own piece. The pain of the division is not felt by those who are divided; on the contrary, each side counts what it holds sufficient and correct."* **I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is the word's threefold occurrence within the sūra, and I give it in a table:**

| Verse | Expression | The source of the rejoicing | Its value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | *Yawmaʾidhin **yafraḥu**'l-muʾminūna bi-naṣri'llāh* | **God's help** | **Positive** |
| **32** | *Kullu ḥizbin bimā ladayhim **fariḥūn*** | **What is in its own hand** | **Negative** |
| **36** | *Idhā adhaqna'n-nāsa raḥmatan **fariḥū** bihā* | **The mercy that comes** | **Negative** — verse 37 will give the ground |

**The same root three times, once positive and twice negative.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this set of three:** what distinguishes them is not the rejoicing but **where the rejoicing is attached.** In the fourth verse the rejoicing is attached to *bi-naṣri'llāh* — to something not in their own hands. **In the thirty-second and thirty-sixth verses it is attached to *what is in hand*: *bimā ladayhim*, *bihā*.**

**And the word *ladayhim* is worth recording:** *ladā* means "beside one, right at hand" — **it conveys a nearer presence than *ʿinda*.** **That is, each group is satisfied with what lies right at its hand.**

**لَا تَكُونُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ فَرَّقُوا۟ — and two *min*s come one after the other. The grammarians count the second an appositive substitute for the first: that is, associating partners with God is being described by way of division. I transmit this parsing as it has been transmitted.**

**The work the sentence does is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** in the thirtieth verse the word *ḥanīf* was treated — **not the name of a camp but the name of a direction.** **The thirty-second verse describes its exact opposite: a religion that has become a camp and lost its direction.** **The two verses, two verses apart, show two contrary states of a single word.**

## 30/33-37 — وَإِذَا مَسَّ ٱلنَّاسَ ضُرٌّ دَعَوْا۟ رَبَّهُم مُّنِيبِينَ إِلَيْهِ ثُمَّ إِذَآ أَذَاقَهُم مِّنْهُ رَحْمَةً إِذَا فَرِيقٌ مِّنْهُم بِرَبِّهِمْ يُشْرِكُونَ · لِيَكْفُرُوا۟ بِمَآ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُمْ فَتَمَتَّعُوا۟ فَسَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ · أَمْ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَٰنًا فَهُوَ يَتَكَلَّمُ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يُشْرِكُونَ · وَإِذَآ أَذَقْنَا ٱلنَّاسَ رَحْمَةً فَرِحُوا۟ بِهَا وَإِن تُصِبْهُمْ سَيِّئَةٌۢ بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ إِذَا هُمْ يَقْنَطُونَ · أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَبْسُطُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ وَيَقْدِرُ

*Wa idhā massa'n-nāsa ḍurrun daʿaw rabbahum munībīna ilayhi thumma idhā adhāqahum minhu raḥmatan idhā farīqun minhum bi-rabbihim yushrikūn · Li-yakfurū bimā ātaynāhum fa-tamattaʿū fa-sawfa taʿlamūn · Am anzalnā ʿalayhim sulṭānan fa-huwa yatakallamu bimā kānū bihi yushrikūn · Wa idhā adhaqna'n-nāsa raḥmatan fariḥū bihā wa in tuṣibhum sayyiʾatun bimā qaddamat aydīhim idhā hum yaqnaṭūn · A-wa-lam yaraw anna'llāha yabsuṭu'r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu wa yaqdir*

"**When some distress touches people, they call upon their Lord, turning to Him.** Then, when He gives them a taste of mercy from Himself, **there you are — a group of them associate partners with their Lord** — **so as to deny** what We have given them. Enjoy yourselves, then; **you will soon know.** **Or have We sent down upon them an authority, so that it is this that speaks of what they associate with Him?** **When We give people a taste of mercy they rejoice in it; and when an evil befalls them for what their own hands have sent ahead, they fall into despair.** **Have they not seen that God spreads out provision for whom He wills, and straitens it?**"

### The taking back of the thirty-first verse

**In the thirty-first verse *munībīna ilayh* stood in the context of a command: stand turned towards Him.**

**In the thirty-third verse the same word comes back as a description** — and a turning away is recounted after it:

| Stage | Expression | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Idhā massa'n-nāsa ḍurrun* | **Distress touches them** |
| 2 | *Daʿaw rabbahum **munībīna ilayh*** | **They turn** — the word of verse 31 |
| 3 | *Thumma idhā **adhāqahum** minhu raḥma* | **Mercy is tasted** |
| 4 | *Idhā farīqun minhum … **yushrikūn*** | **A part of them turns back** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the recurrence of the word two verses apart:** the sūra says that the state it commanded in the thirty-first verse **is already present** in man — but conditionally. **That is, what is lacking is not the turning but the *continuance* of the turning.** **And the idea of "turning back over and over" in the root *ināba* conveyed exactly this.**

**فَرِيقٌ مِّنْهُم** — it must be recorded: **"a group of them".** **The sentence does not cover them all.** **It runs in the same direction as the finding recorded at `035-fatir.md` 35/32:** the verse does not characterise a community as a single piece.

**ذُوق / أَذَاقَ — root ذ-و-ق: to taste. And the verb occurs twice in this block: *adhāqahum … raḥmatan* (33) and *adhaqna'n-nāsa raḥmatan* (36). The choice of the word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation: *to taste* is a slight and direct contact — not to own. The verse mentions mercy not as a possession but as a tasting.**

### يَقْنَطُونَ and the link with Fuṣṣilat

**The root ق-ن-ط was treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/49** (*fa-yaʾūsun qanūṭ*) **and the distinction between *yaʾs* and *qunūṭ* was transmitted there.** **I do not repeat it here.** The core: **the complete cutting off of hope, the settling in of despondency.**

**And the portrait described at `041-fussilet.md` 41/49-51 is almost the same as this verse. The two places must be set side by side:**

| | Fuṣṣilat 41/49-51 | **al-Rūm 30/36** |
|---|---|---|
| When the favour comes | *Aʿraḍa wa naʾā bi-jānibih* — **he turns aside** | *Fariḥū bihā* — **they rejoice** |
| When evil touches | *Fa-yaʾūsun **qanūṭ*** — despairing and despondent | *Idhā hum **yaqnaṭūn*** — they fall into despair |
| Additional note | *Fa-dhū duʿāʾin ʿarīḍ* — **prayer at length and breadth** | *Bimā qaddamat aydīhim* — **by their own hands** |

**Two differences are worth recording:**

**One.** In Fuṣṣilat the reaction to the favour is **turning aside**; **in al-Rūm it is *rejoicing*.** **And al-Rūm's word is the finer one:** rejoicing is not a failing — **the failing lies in where the rejoicing is attached.** **The sūra had already established this in the thirty-second verse.**

**Two. al-Rūm adds a note: *bimā qaddamat aydīhim* — for what their own hands have sent ahead. In Fuṣṣilat the source of the evil was not stated; al-Rūm states it. And this construct will come back in almost the same words in the forty-first verse: *bimā kasabat aydi'n-nās*.**

**أَمْ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَٰنًا فَهُوَ يَتَكَلَّمُ** — "or have We sent down an authority, so that it is this that speaks?"

**سُلْطَان** — root **س-ل-ط**: **power, dominion; and the binding force of an argument.** **The word frequently occurs in the Qurʾān in the sense of "a warrant, a decisive proof".**

**The sentence has a mocking structure, and it must be recorded:** *fa-huwa **yatakallamu*** — "is it this that **speaks**?" **The proof is mentioned as though it were a being that talks.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse asks the other side for a **ground**, and it characterises the ground as being **able to speak.** **It was treated at `045-casiye.md` 45/29 that the book is mentioned with the verb *yanṭiqu* (it speaks)** — **there it was the record that spoke.** **Here it is said, by way of a question, that no such record exists.**

**يَبْسُطُ ٱلرِّزْقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ وَيَقْدِرُ** — **ب-س-ط (to spread out, to open) and ق-د-ر (to measure, to straiten) face one another.** **The "straitening" branch of the root ق-د-ر was treated at `089-fecr.md` 89/16** (*fa-qadara ʿalayhi rizqah*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the placing of this verse at the end of the block is worth recording:** the block had been recounting the reactions to distress and to relief. **The closing says that both come from the same hand.**

## 30/38 — فَـَٔاتِ ذَا ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ حَقَّهُۥ وَٱلْمِسْكِينَ وَٱبْنَ ٱلسَّبِيلِ ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ لِّلَّذِينَ يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَ ٱللَّهِ وَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ

*Fa-āti dha'l-qurbā ḥaqqahu wa'l-miskīna wa'bna's-sabīl, dhālika khayrun li'lladhīna yurīdūna wajha'llāh, wa ulāʾika humu'l-mufliḥūn*

"**So give the near relative his due; and the needy and the wayfarer too.** This is better for **those who seek the face of God.** **Those are the ones who attain.**"

### حَقَّهُۥ — "his due"

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded, and this is the most striking side of the verse:**

**The verse does not say "give to him".** *Āti dha'l-qurbā **ḥaqqahu*** — **"give the near relative his due".**

**That is, what is given is named not as a donation out of the giver's property but as the receiver's due.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word itself:** *ḥaqq* — root **ح-ق-ق**: to be fixed, to fall into its place. **The word conveys not a favour but a settling into place.** **And the pronoun must be recorded:** *ḥaqqa**hu*** — **"his" due, not the giver's.**

**مِسْكِين** — root **س-ك-ن**. **The word was analysed in `107-maun.md` and in `059-hasr.md`**, and what was recorded there was this: **the idea of "stopping, motionlessness" in the root describes the *miskīn* as the person who has been brought to a state of not being able to move.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**This is the root's second occurrence in this sūra, and the two occurrences can be set side by side:**

| Verse | Word | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | *Li-taskunū ilayhā* | **Coming to rest** — positive |
| **38** | *al-**Miskīn*** | **Being brought to a standstill** — negative |

**The same root, the one repose and the other helplessness.** **I record this as an observation about the language:** the ceasing of movement runs to two contrary values according to its context.

**ٱبْنَ ٱلسَّبِيلِ** — "the son of the road": the person stranded on the way. **The construct was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/177 and at `059-hasr.md` 59/7.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**وَجْهَ ٱللَّهِ — "the face of God", that is, His good pleasure and His direction. And the word had occurred in the thirtieth verse of this sūra as well: *fa-aqim wajhaka* — "set your face straight".**

| Verse | Whose *wajh* | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | **Man's** | **It is set straight** — a direction is given |
| **38** | **God's** | **It is sought** — that which is turned towards |

**The same word, eight verses apart, on two different sides.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.** **I record this as my own reading:** in the thirtieth verse a turning was commanded; in the thirty-eighth the **form that turning takes when it is done with property** is recounted. **The two verses hold the two ends of one line with the same word.**

## 30/39 — وَمَآ ءَاتَيْتُم مِّن رِّبًا لِّيَرْبُوَا۟ فِىٓ أَمْوَٰلِ ٱلنَّاسِ فَلَا يَرْبُوا۟ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ وَمَآ ءَاتَيْتُم مِّن زَكَوٰةٍ تُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَ ٱللَّهِ فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ

*Wa mā ātaytum min riban li-yarbuwa fī amwāli'n-nāsi fa-lā yarbū ʿinda'llāh, wa mā ātaytum min zakātin turīdūna wajha'llāhi fa-ulāʾika humu'l-muḍʿifūn*

"**The ribā you give so that it may grow within people's wealth does not grow with God.** **As for the zakāt you give seeking the face of God: it is those who multiply.**"

### ر-ب-و — the same root, the same reversal

**The root was analysed at `002-bakara.md` 2/275:** **to increase, to rise, to swell.** From the same root come *rabwa* (high ground) and *tarbiya* (nurture). **And the finding recorded there was this: the word itself means "increase"; what is forbidden is not the increase itself but the transaction it arises from.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And at `002-bakara.md` 2/276 a word order was recorded that does the same work as this verse:**

> **يَمْحَقُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلرِّبَوٰا۟ وَيُرْبِى ٱلصَّدَقَٰتِ** — "God **wipes out** ribā and **makes the alms grow.**"

**What was recorded there: *"The word order reverses things, and it does so with the words themselves: the verb *yurbī* is from the same root as *ribā*. That is, for the thing that bears the name 'increase' the verb of diminishing is used, and for giving without return the verb of increasing. The verse exchanges the names of the two transactions."*** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two verses must be set side by side, because they do the same work by two different routes:**

| | al-Baqara 2/276 | **al-Rūm 30/39** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb used for ribā | ***Yamḥaqu*** — **a different root** (م-ح-ق): to erase, to diminish | ***Lā yarbū*** — **the same root**, negated |
| For alms / zakāt | ***Yurbī*** — **the root of ribā**, to make grow | ***Al-Muḍʿifūn*** — **a different root** (ض-ع-ف): those who multiply |
| The method | **The root of ribā is given to the other side** | **The root of ribā is taken away from ribā** |

**The difference is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** **al-Baqara works the reversal by *addition*** — it gives the word of increase to alms. **al-Rūm works it by *subtraction*** — it takes the word of increase out of ribā's hand: *fa-lā **yarbū** ʿinda'llāh.*

**That is, the two verses apply two different operations to the same root.** **And the result is the same: the transaction that bears the name "increase" does not count as increase.**

**And one more detail of word order must be recorded:** *li-yarbuwa **fī amwāli'n-nās*** — "so that it may grow **within people's wealth**". **That is, the place where the increase occurs is stated too: the growth of one property takes place inside another's property.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this adverbial: the sentence does not say that the increase does not exist. Within people's wealth it really does grow — the sentence grants this. What it says is that this increase has no counterpart in another account: *fa-lā yarbū ʿinda'llāh*. The two accounts are plainly separated.**

**ٱلْمُضْعِفُونَ** — root **ض-ع-ف**: **a fold, a multiple; and weakness.** **Both branches of the root are alive in Arabic**, and in this sūra **both** are used:

| Verse | Word | Which branch of the root |
|---|---|---|
| **39** | *Al-**Muḍʿifūn*** | **Multiplying many times over** |
| **54** | *Khalaqakum min **ḍaʿf*** | **Weakness** |

**The same root, in two contrary values, in the same sūra.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it will be mentioned again at the fifty-fourth verse.**

**And the pattern must be recorded: *muḍʿif* is the active participle of Form IV — "one who multiplies".** **That is, the sentence does not say "their wealth increases"; it says "it is they who multiply". The subject is the one who gives.**

**A note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** whether the word *zakāt* here conveys the financial obligation that was set out in detail later, or, by the root's original sense (**ز-ك-و**: to become pure, to grow), a general act of purification and giving — **two readings were tabulated on this at `041-fussilet.md` 41/7.** **The same duality holds here; I do not impose a preference and I draw no legal conclusion.**

## 30/40 — ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُمْ ثُمَّ رَزَقَكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ هَلْ مِن شُرَكَآئِكُم مَّن يَفْعَلُ مِن ذَٰلِكُم مِّن شَىْءٍ سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ وَتَعَٰلَىٰ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

*Allāhu'lladhī khalaqakum thumma razaqakum thumma yumītukum thumma yuḥyīkum, hal min shurakāʾikum man yafʿalu min dhālikum min shayʾ, subḥānahu wa taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn*

"**It is God who created you, then provided for you, then causes you to die, then gives you life.** Among those you associate with Him is there any **who does anything of that**? He is **far above and exalted beyond** what they associate with Him."

### Four verbs and a change of tense

**Four verbs are enumerated, and two are in the perfect and two in the imperfect:**

| Order | Verb | Tense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Khalaqa**kum*** | **Perfect** — He created |
| 2 | *Razaqa**kum*** | **Perfect** — He provided |
| 3 | *Yumītu**kum*** | **Imperfect** — He causes to die |
| 4 | *Yuḥyī**kum*** | **Imperfect** — He gives life |

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the break falls exactly in the middle. **The first two have happened, the last two are to come** — and the hearer stands exactly in between. **The sentence marks the point where the listener stands by means of the tenses of the verbs.**

**The same line had been treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/3:** *hal min khāliqin ghayru'llāhi **yarzuqukum*** — **creating was there asked about together with the act of providing.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference is worth recording:** Fāṭir enumerates two verbs (creating + provision); **al-Rūm enumerates four and adds death and the giving of life.** **That is, Fāṭir's question looks at an ongoing work, al-Rūm's at a whole arc.**

**هَلْ مِن شُرَكَآئِكُم مَّن يَفْعَلُ مِن ذَٰلِكُم مِّن شَىْءٍ** — **three *min*s one after another.** **In Arabic, in a context of negation or of question, *min* reduces the scope to the least: "any one at all", "anything at all".** **That is, the sentence is asking after even the smallest share.**

## 30/41 — ظَهَرَ ٱلْفَسَادُ فِى ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِى ٱلنَّاسِ لِيُذِيقَهُم بَعْضَ ٱلَّذِى عَمِلُوا۟ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ

*Ẓahara'l-fasādu fi'l-barri wa'l-baḥri bimā kasabat aydi'n-nāsi li-yudhīqahum baʿḍa'lladhī ʿamilū laʿallahum yarjiʿūn*

"**Corruption has appeared on land and at sea — because of what people's hands have earned.** **So that He may give them a taste of some of what they did; perhaps they will return.**"

### The meanings of the words

**ظَهَرَ** — root **ظ-ه-ر**: **to appear, to come out into the open, to strike the surface.** *Ẓahr* — the back, the outer face.

**And this is a repetition that can be verified from the text — the root occurs for the third time:**

| Verse | Word | What "appears" |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *Yaʿlamūna **ẓāhiran** mina'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* | **The outside of the life of this world** — what is known |
| **18** | *Ḥīna **tuẓhirūn*** | **The noon hour** — the middle of the day |
| **41** | ***Ẓahara**'l-fasād* | **Corruption** — what has come out into the open |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this set of three:** in the seventh verse the other side was content **with a knowledge that stayed outside.** **In the forty-first verse what breaks out to the surface is a corruption.** **The same root: in the one, being stuck at the surface that is seen; in the other, reading what has come out to the surface.** **The sūra uses "what appears" twice in contrary values.**

**فَسَاد — root ف-س-د: a thing's going out of its own order, its spoiling. The opposite of *ṣalāḥ* (being in its right place). The word conveys not a destruction coming from outside but a spoiling from within.**

**بِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِى ٱلنَّاسِ** — "because of what people's hands have earned".

**The construct must be recorded, and it carries two details:**

**One.** **The cause is plainly attached to man** — and the word *aydī* (hands) gives a concrete picture: **the work that is done.**

**Two.** **The same construct had occurred in the thirty-sixth verse as well:** *bimā **qaddamat aydīhim***. **The difference between the two verses: there there was a pronoun (*aydīhim* — their hands), here there is a noun (*aydi'n-nās* — people's hands).** **That is, the scope opens out from a particular group to all people.**

**بَعْضَ ٱلَّذِى عَمِلُوا۟** — "**some** of what they did". **It must be recorded: not all of it.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not give the full requital, and it says so itself. **And the clause of purpose completes this:** *laʿallahum **yarjiʿūn*** — **perhaps they will return.** **That is, the outcome is set down not as a verdict but as a warning.**

**The same structure was treated at `032-secde.md` 32/21** (*wa la-nudhīqannahum mina'l-ʿadhābi'l-adnā dūna'l-ʿadhābi'l-akbari **laʿallahum yarjiʿūn***) **and it was recorded there that "the lesser one is not an *end* but a *call*".** **The two verses share the same verb (*adhāqa* — to give a taste), the same note of partiality and the same clause of purpose.** **This is an overlap between the two sūras that can be verified.**

### What this says to the present

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write the boundary first: I draw from this verse no contemporary environmental policy, no scientific thesis and no concrete agenda.** **The verse makes a diagnosis; it does not give a programme. And the word *fasād* is not used in the Qurʾān only for physical spoiling — it is used for moral and social spoiling as well.** **To narrow the word to a single field would be to narrow the scope of the text.**

**More than one explanation of the scope of *fasād* is transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | What *fasād* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Moral and social spoiling** — oppression, injustice, the loss of trust |
| 2 | **Material consequences** — famine, the withdrawal of increase, the falling off of the crop |
| 3 | **Both together** — the one gives rise to the other |

**All three explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And I frame what this says to the present with the note that *it is one point of view*, and without forcing:**

**There are three elements in the sentence structure of the verse that can be verified:** *(a)* the corruption is **on land and at sea** — that is, in two fields at once; *(b)* the cause is **what is earned by human hands**; *(c)* the outcome is the return to man of **some** of what he did.

**These three elements give a frame that can be read in every situation where the work a person does comes back to him.** **I record this as a point of view, not as a finding.** **The verse names no policy, no measurement and no side of today; and neither do I.**

**The one thing worth recording is where the sentence places the responsibility:** *bimā kasabat **aydi'n-nās***. **And the principle of the burden treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/18 becomes visible here on the scale of a society: the outcome is entered in the account of the one who does it.**

## 30/42 — قُلْ سِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَٱنظُرُوا۟ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلُ كَانَ أَكْثَرُهُم مُّشْرِكِينَ

*Qul sīrū fi'l-arḍi fa'nẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu'lladhīna min qabl, kāna aktharuhum mushrikīn*

"Say: **Travel in the earth and look at how the end of those before you was.** Most of them were **mushrikūn.**"

### The same evidence a second time — and a change of mood

**This evidence had already occurred in the ninth verse of the sūra.** **The two occurrences must be set side by side, because the mood changes:**

| Verse | Expression | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | ***A-wa-lam yasīrū** fi'l-arḍi fa-yanẓurū…* | **A question of reproach** — third person |
| **42** | *Qul ***sīrū*** fi'l-arḍi fa'nẓurū…* | **An imperative** — second person, and with *qul* |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this difference of mood:** in the ninth verse something not done **was being called into question**; in the forty-second the same thing **is commanded.** **Thirty-three verses apart, the same verb turns from a reproach into a call.**

**And the difference between the two verses is seen in the diagnosis given as well:**

| Verse | What is said of those before |
|---|---|
| **9** | *Kānū ashadda minhum **quwwatan**… wa **ʿamarūhā** akthara* — **power and building up** |
| **42** | *Kāna aktharuhum **mushrikīn*** — **the state of their belief** |

**The ninth verse enumerates the side on which they were superior; the forty-second names their failing.** **And when the two verses are read together, it is seen that the superiority granted in the ninth verse did not change the outcome.**

**And the note *aktharuhum* (most of them) must be recorded: the sentence does not characterise a community in its entirety.** **The same limitation is seen at `035-fatir.md` 35/32 and in the thirty-third verse of this sūra (*farīqun minhum*).** **Per the method (`STYLE.md`) this note matters: the verse describes an attribute; it does not pass a blanket judgement on a community.**

## 30/43-45 — فَأَقِمْ وَجْهَكَ لِلدِّينِ ٱلْقَيِّمِ مِن قَبْلِ أَن يَأْتِىَ يَوْمٌ لَّا مَرَدَّ لَهُۥ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ يَوْمَئِذٍ يَصَّدَّعُونَ · مَن كَفَرَ فَعَلَيْهِ كُفْرُهُۥ وَمَنْ عَمِلَ صَٰلِحًا فَلِأَنفُسِهِمْ يَمْهَدُونَ · لِيَجْزِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ مِن فَضْلِهِۦٓ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ

*Fa-aqim wajhaka li'd-dīni'l-qayyimi min qabli an yaʾtiya yawmun lā maradda lahu mina'llāh, yawmaʾidhin yaṣṣaddaʿūn · Man kafara fa-ʿalayhi kufruh, wa man ʿamila ṣāliḥan fa-li-anfusihim yamhadūn · Li-yajziya'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti min faḍlih, innahu lā yuḥibbu'l-kāfirīn*

"**So set your face towards the upright religion before there comes a day that cannot be turned back from God.** On that day **they split apart.** Whoever denies, his denial is against himself. And whoever does a righteous deed, **they are preparing a place for themselves** — that He may **reward** from His bounty those who believed and did righteous deeds. He does not love those who deny."

### The repetition of the thirtieth verse

**The forty-third verse repeats the first sentence of the thirtieth almost word for word. The two verses must be set side by side:**

| Verse | Expression | The difference |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | *Fa-aqim wajhaka **li'd-dīni ḥanīfā*** | The manner of the turning is qualified: ***ḥanīf*** |
| **43** | *Fa-aqim wajhaka **li'd-dīni'l-qayyim*** | The religion is qualified: ***al-qayyim*** |

**The same command, with two different qualifiers.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**And *al-dīnu'l-qayyim* had already occurred in the thirtieth verse — but there as a statement: *dhālika'd-dīnu'l-qayyim* — "this is the upright religion". In the forty-third verse the same construct enters into the command.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these two occurrences:** in the thirtieth verse the religion is **defined**, in the forty-third the **turning** towards that definition is commanded. **And the thirteen verses in between had recounted the opposite of the definition: division (32), turning in distress and parting in ease (33), corruption (41).**

**And a note of time is added to the command: *min qabli an yaʾtiya yawmun lā maradda lahu*.**

**مَرَدّ** — root **ر-د-د**: to turn back, to send back. **The mīmī verbal noun pattern: "a being turned back".** **And the place of the root in this sūra is worth recording:** in the forty-first verse it was said *laʿallahum **yarjiʿūn*** (perhaps they will return) — **the return is open.** **Here a day is announced on which the return will be closed.** **Between the two verses two different roots (ر-ج-ع and ر-د-د) carry the same idea in two different directions.**

### يَصَّدَّعُونَ — root: ص-د-ع

**The root was treated at `086-tarik.md` 86/12 (*wa'l-arḍi dhāti'ṣ-ṣadʿ*), at `056-vakia.md` 56/19 (*lā yuṣaddaʿūna ʿanhā*) and in `059-hasr.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.** The core: **the cracking, the splitting open of something hard; and the crack itself.** *Ṣadʿ* — a fissure, a crack.

**What belongs here is the attributing of the word to people.** **The verb is of Form V (*taṣadduʿ*, here with assimilation *yaṣṣaddaʿūn*): to crack of itself, to break into pieces.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the form of the verb:** the verse does not use a verb of dividing for "they part" — **it chooses a root that describes the cracking of stone.** **And this runs in the same direction as *farraqū dīnahum* (they broke their religion apart) in the thirty-second verse.**

| Verse | Verb | Who does it | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **32** | *Farraqū* — **Form II** | **People** — with their own hands | **Now** |
| **14** | *Yatafarraqūn* — **Form V** | **Of itself** | **On that day** |
| **43** | *Yaṣṣaddaʿūn* — **Form V** | **By cracking apart** | **On that day** |

**Three verbs, three patterns of one idea.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra says twice (14, 43) that the division made by hand in this world will on that day come about of itself. **And the word it uses the second time is a word that carries the impossibility of repair — a crack.**

**يَمْهَدُونَ** — root **م-ه-د**: **to level out and spread, to prepare a bed.** **The root was treated at `078-nebe.md` 78/6 (*alam najʿali'l-arḍa mihādā*) and in `043-zuhruf.md` (*mahdā*).** **I do not repeat it here.** *Mahd* — a cradle; *mihād* — a bedding.

**The usage here is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** in the other places the one who spreads the bedding is God. **Here the subject of the verb is man:** *fa-li-anfusihim **yamhadūn*** — **"they are preparing a place for themselves".** **The same root, the one a ground that is given, the other a ground that is prepared.**

**And the note *li-anfusihim* (for themselves) overlaps with the line at `035-fatir.md` 35/39** (*fa-man kafara fa-ʿalayhi kufruh* — the same sentence is in the forty-fourth verse of this sūra as well). **That is, the requital is attached in both directions to the person himself.**

## 30/46-51 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦٓ أَن يُرْسِلَ ٱلرِّيَاحَ مُبَشِّرَٰتٍ … ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِى يُرْسِلُ ٱلرِّيَٰحَ فَتُثِيرُ سَحَابًا فَيَبْسُطُهُۥ فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ كَيْفَ يَشَآءُ … فَٱنظُرْ إِلَىٰٓ ءَاثَٰرِ رَحْمَتِ ٱللَّهِ كَيْفَ يُحْىِ ٱلْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَآ … وَلَئِنْ أَرْسَلْنَا رِيحًا فَرَأَوْهُ مُصْفَرًّا لَّظَلُّوا۟ مِنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ يَكْفُرُونَ

*Wa min āyātihi an yursila'r-riyāḥa mubashshirātin wa li-yudhīqakum min raḥmatihi wa li-tajriya'l-fulku bi-amrihi wa li-tabtaghū min faḍlihi wa laʿallakum tashkurūn · Wa laqad arsalnā min qablika rusulan ilā qawmihim fa-jāʾūhum bi'l-bayyināti fa'ntaqamnā mina'lladhīna ajramū, wa kāna ḥaqqan ʿalaynā naṣru'l-muʾminīn · Allāhu'lladhī yursilu'r-riyāḥa fa-tuthīru saḥāban fa-yabsuṭuhu fi's-samāʾi kayfa yashāʾu wa yajʿaluhu kisafan fa-tara'l-wadqa yakhruju min khilālih, fa-idhā aṣāba bihi man yashāʾu min ʿibādihi idhā hum yastabshirūn · Wa in kānū min qabli an yunazzala ʿalayhim min qablihi la-mublisīn · Fa'nẓur ilā āthāri raḥmati'llāhi kayfa yuḥyi'l-arḍa baʿda mawtihā, inna dhālika la-muḥyi'l-mawtā wa huwa ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr · Wa la-in arsalnā rīḥan fa-raʾawhu muṣfarran la-ẓallū min baʿdihi yakfurūn*

"**And of His signs is that:** He sends the winds as **bearers of good news** — that He may give you a taste of His mercy, that the ships may run by His command, that you may seek of His bounty, and that you may give thanks. And We surely sent messengers before you to their own peoples; they brought them clear proofs. **We took retribution from those who committed crimes; and helping the believers became a duty upon Us.** **It is God who sends the winds, so that they raise a cloud, and He spreads it in the sky as He wills, and makes it into pieces, and then you see the rain coming out from between them.** When He brings it to whom He wills of His servants, **at once they rejoice** — though **before that, before it was sent down upon them, they had despaired.** **Look, then, at the traces of God's mercy: how He gives life to the earth after its death!** He it is who **gives life to the dead as well**; and He has power over all things. **But were We to send a wind and they saw the crop turned yellow, they would straight after that turn back to ingratitude.**"

### The seventh link of the ***wa min āyātihi*** series

**The forty-sixth verse brings back twenty-one verses later a formula belonging to the series that ran between the twentieth and the twenty-fifth verses.**

**And its closing must be recorded: *wa laʿallakum tashkurūn*. The other four closings of the series were verbs of cognition (to think, to know, to hear, to reason). This last one is different: *thanksgiving*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this difference together with the delay of the formula:** the series is built with verbs of understanding, **and the link set apart from it ends with a verb of response.** **That is, the sūra begins with the understanding of the signs and ties it to giving something in return.**

### The two occurrences of the wind and the contrary outcome

**The root ر-و-ح / ر-ي-ح occurs three times in this block, and the three do not say the same thing:**

| Verse | Expression | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **46** | *Yursila'r-riyāḥa **mubashshirāt*** — **plural**, bearers of good news | **Mercy, ships, gain, thanksgiving** |
| **48** | *Yursilu'r-riyāḥa fa-tuthīru saḥābā* — **plural** | **Rain** — *idhā hum yastabshirūn* |
| **51** | *Arsalnā **rīḥan*** — **singular**, unqualified | *La-ẓallū … **yakfurūn*** — **ingratitude** |

**The difference of number is worth recording, and the lexicographers generally record it:** in the Qurʾān *riyāḥ* (plural) occurs mostly in a context of mercy, *rīḥ* (singular) mostly in a context of punishment or of a negative outcome. **I transmit this observation as a tendency the lexicographers record, not as an absolute rule.**

**And the work the fifty-first verse does must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** in the forty-ninth verse man was **without hope** before the rain (*la-mublisīn*), and with the rain he **rejoiced** (*yastabshirūn*). **The fifty-first verse takes the same man back to the beginning when the crop turns yellow.** **That is, three verses set up a cycle: despair → rejoicing → ingratitude.** **And this is the very same cycle as in the thirty-third verse** (distress → turning → mercy → association of partners). **The sūra draws the same circle twice, with two different events.**

**And *min baʿdihi* in the sentence *la-ẓallū min baʿdihi yakfurūn* is worth recording: "straight after it". There is no interval in between.**

**فَتُثِيرُ سَحَابًا** — **the same expression was treated in detail at `035-fatir.md` 35/9** (*arsala'r-riyāḥa fa-tuthīru saḥābā*) **and the change of verb tenses was recorded there as an observation about the word order.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference here must be recorded:** in Fāṭir the cloud **was being driven to a dead land** (*fa-suqnāhu ilā baladin mayyit*). **In al-Rūm, by contrast, the cloud's state in the sky is set out in detail:** *fa-yabsuṭuhu* (He spreads it) → *wa yajʿaluhu **kisafan*** (He makes it into pieces) → *fa-tara'l-wadqa yakhruju min **khilālih*** (you see the rain coming out from between them).

**كِسَف** — the plural of *kisfa*, root **ك-س-ف**: **a piece, a cut-off portion.** **خِلَال** — the plural of *khalal*, root **خ-ل-ل**: **the space, the gap between two things.** **وَدْق** — root **و-د-ق**: **the rain itself, the falling drop.**

**The picture the three words give together is worth recording:** spreading out → breaking into pieces → coming out from between. **And the tense of the verb changes once more: *fa-**tarā*** — "you see", the address turns to the hearer.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** while the sentence moves in the third person it shifts in the middle to the second. **And in the fifty-first verse it will turn to the second person again: *fa'nẓur*.** **That is, the narration calls the reader onto the stage twice.**

**فَٱنظُرْ إِلَىٰٓ ءَاثَٰرِ رَحْمَتِ ٱللَّهِ** — "look at the **traces** of God's mercy".

**آثَار — root أ-ث-ر: a trace, a mark left behind. And the same root had occurred in the ninth verse as a verb: *wa athāru'l-arḍa* — they turned the soil over. This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this recurrence of the root: in the ninth verse what was recounted was the trace man left in the soil — and the end of those who left that trace was destruction. In the fiftieth verse what one is asked to look at is the trace mercy has left in the soil. The same root, the same ground (*al-arḍ*), two different traces.**

**إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ لَمُحْيِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ** — and the evidence is tied off. **The same structure was treated at `035-fatir.md` 35/9 (*kadhālika'n-nushūr*), at `050-kaf.md` 50/11 (*kadhālika'l-khurūj*) and at `041-fussilet.md` 41/39 (*inna'lladhī aḥyāhā la-muḥyi'l-mawtā*).** **The sentence in Fuṣṣilat is almost identical with this verse.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**وَكَانَ حَقًّا عَلَيْنَا نَصْرُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ** — "helping the believers **became a duty upon Us**".

**The sentence is worth recording, and it forms a ring with the opening of the sūra: in the fifth verse it had been said *yawmaʾidhin yafraḥu'l-muʾminūna bi-naṣri'llāh* — the joy of the help. In the forty-seventh verse the same word (نصر) comes back as a pledge.**

| Verse | Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **4-5** | *Yafraḥu'l-muʾminūna bi-**naṣri'llāh*** | **Joy** — the outcome as it is lived |
| **47** | *Wa kāna ḥaqqan ʿalaynā **naṣru**'l-muʾminīn* | **A pledge** — the ground of the outcome |

**The same root, at the two ends of the sūra.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this repetition:** the historical announcement of the second verse is tied in the forty-seventh to a principle. **The sūra does not recount a single event and leave it; it names the order the event rests on as well.**

**A note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** the promise of *naṣr* here is attached not to a particular state, army or side but to the **attribute** the verse enumerates. **The principle recorded in `049-hucurat.md` holds here too.**

## 30/52-53 — فَإِنَّكَ لَا تُسْمِعُ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ وَلَا تُسْمِعُ ٱلصُّمَّ ٱلدُّعَآءَ إِذَا وَلَّوْا۟ مُدْبِرِينَ · وَمَآ أَنتَ بِهَٰدِ ٱلْعُمْىِ عَن ضَلَٰلَتِهِمْ إِن تُسْمِعُ إِلَّا مَن يُؤْمِنُ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا فَهُم مُّسْلِمُونَ

*Fa-innaka lā tusmiʿu'l-mawtā wa lā tusmiʿu'ṣ-ṣumma'd-duʿāʾa idhā wallaw mudbirīn · Wa mā anta bi-hādi'l-ʿumyi ʿan ḍalālatihim, in tusmiʿu illā man yuʾminu bi-āyātinā fa-hum muslimūn*

"**You cannot make the dead hear; nor can you make the deaf hear the call when they turn their backs and go.** **Nor are you one to turn the blind from their straying and set them on the way.** You can make none hear but those who believe in Our signs; **they are the ones who have submitted.**"

### The limit of responsibility — a line treated in the series

**These two verses are part of a line treated in several places in the series, and I rely on those treatments:**

| Place | Expression |
|---|---|
| Fāṭir 35/22 | *Wa mā anta bi-musmiʿin man fi'l-qubūr* — **you cannot make those in the graves hear** |
| al-Zumar 39/19 | *A-fa-anta tunqidhu man fi'n-nār* |
| al-Zumar 39/41 | *Wa mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīl* |
| al-Aḥqāf 46/35 | *Balāgh* — the limit of conveying |
| Fāṭir 35/8 | *Fa-lā tadhhab nafsuka ʿalayhim ḥasarāt* |

**It was recorded in `035-fatir.md` that this limit is repeated several times across that sūra.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the enumeration of the three impediments, and the word order must be recorded:**

| Order | Who | What he cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ***Al-Mawtā*** — the dead | **Hearing** — not at all |
| 2 | ***Al-Ṣumm*** — the deaf | **Hearing** — and an added condition: *idhā wallaw mudbirīn* |
| 3 | ***Al-ʿUmy*** — the blind | **Finding the way** |

**And the added condition in the second item is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** no condition is set for the dead or for the blind. **One is set for the deaf: *idhā wallaw mudbirīn* — "when they turn their backs and go".**

**That is, deafness by itself is not counted sufficient; what is mentioned is being deaf and moving away. The lexicographers generally explain this note thus: a deaf person face to face can understand by signs; if he has turned his back, that channel too is closed. I transmit this explanation as it has been transmitted — and the word order supports it.**

**I record this as my own reading:** what the verse shuts off is not a sense but **a turning towards.** **And this overlaps with the thirtieth verse of the sūra:** what was commanded there was *aqim wajhaka* — **to set the face straight.** **What is recounted here is the one who has turned his face away.**

**Another stage of this line was treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/4-5:** there the verb *aʿraḍa* (he turned away) came **before** the outcome *lā yasmaʿūn* (they do not hear) — **that is, the not-hearing was the consequence of the turning away.** **al-Rūm 30/52 keeps the same order: first *wallaw mudbirīn*, then the failure to make them hear.** **The two sūras build the same sequence.**

**إِن تُسْمِعُ إِلَّا مَن يُؤْمِنُ** — **negation + exception.** **In Arabic this structure conveys restriction (*ḥaṣr*).** **And the same limitation had occurred with different words at `035-fatir.md` 35/18** (*innamā tundhiru'lladhīna yakhshawna rabbahum bi'l-ghayb*): **the effect of the warning is attached to a condition, and the condition is on the side of the one addressed.**

## 30/54 — ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُم مِّن ضَعْفٍ ثُمَّ جَعَلَ مِنۢ بَعْدِ ضَعْفٍ قُوَّةً ثُمَّ جَعَلَ مِنۢ بَعْدِ قُوَّةٍ ضَعْفًا وَشَيْبَةً يَخْلُقُ مَا يَشَآءُ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَلِيمُ ٱلْقَدِيرُ

*Allāhu'lladhī khalaqakum min ḍaʿfin thumma jaʿala min baʿdi ḍaʿfin quwwatan thumma jaʿala min baʿdi quwwatin ḍaʿfan wa shaybah, yakhluqu mā yashāʾu wa huwa'l-ʿalīmu'l-qadīr*

"**It is God who created you from weakness, then after weakness gave strength, then after strength gave weakness and grey hair.** He creates what He wills; He is the Knowing, the Powerful."

### A symmetry in three stages

**The construction of the sentence must be recorded, and it is symmetrical:**

| Stage | Word | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ***Ḍaʿf*** — weakness | *Min ḍaʿfin* — **indefinite** |
| 2 | ***Quwwa*** — strength | *Min baʿdi ḍaʿfin **quwwatan*** |
| 3 | ***Ḍaʿf and shayba*** — weakness and greying | *Min baʿdi quwwatin **ḍaʿfan wa shaybah*** |

**The same word (*ḍaʿf*) at the beginning and at the end; its opposite in the middle.** **And all the words come indefinite: no particular age or span is given.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence draws not a circle but **an arc.** **The beginning and the end are named with the same word** — but a word is **added** at the end: *wa shaybah.*

**شَيْبَة** — root **ش-ي-ب**: **the whitening of the hair.** **The root was treated at `073-muzzemmil.md` 73/17** (*yawman yajʿalu'l-wildāna shībā* — the day that turns children white-haired). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The addition of the word is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading: the third stage is not distinguished from the first by the second *ḍaʿf* — what distinguishes it is *shayba*. That is, the weakness at the end is told apart from the one at the beginning only by a visible mark. And as was recorded in `073-muzzemmil.md`, in Arabic greying is the name of the trace time leaves on the body.**

### A comparison with the six steps of Ghāfir

**At `040-gafir.md` 40/67 the stages of a lifetime were treated in detail and six steps were tabulated:** *turāb → nuṭfa → ʿalaqa → ṭifl → ashudd → shuyūkh.* **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two verses must be set side by side:**

| | Ghāfir 40/67 | **al-Rūm 30/54** |
|---|---|---|
| Number of steps | **Six** | **Three** |
| What they are named by | **Names of matter and of age** — dust, drop, infant, old man | **Names of states** — weakness, strength, weakness |
| Structure | **A linear series** — from the beginning to the end | **A symmetry** — the same word at both ends |
| The note added | *Wa minkum man yutawaffā min qabl* — **not everyone goes to the end** | *Yakhluqu mā yashāʾ* — **the Creator does what He wills** |

**Three differences are worth recording, and all three can be verified from the text:**

**One — the measure.** Ghāfir enumerates **matter and age**; al-Rūm enumerates **strength.** **That is, Ghāfir's measure is what you are, al-Rūm's measure is what you can do.**

**Two — structure.** Ghāfir's series runs in one direction: it begins somewhere and ends somewhere. **al-Rūm's series returns to the word it began with.** **As was recorded in `040-gafir.md`, Ghāfir joins all the steps with *thumma* — the particle that conveys an interval. al-Rūm uses the same particle; but its second *thumma* takes the second back to the first.**

**Three — the note added.** Ghāfir says the series **may be cut short** (not everyone grows old). **al-Rūm, by contrast, completes the series and says *yakhluqu mā yashāʾ*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this third difference:** al-Rūm's sentence names at its close **not the exception but the owner of the order.** **And this does the same work as the sentence in the fourth verse of the sūra:** *li'llāhi'l-amru min qablu wa min baʿd* — **before it and after it alike are His.** **The two verses enter the same note, the one for history and the other for a lifetime.**

### The root's second face within the sūra

**The other occurrence of the root ض-ع-ف in this sūra was in the thirty-ninth verse:** *fa-ulāʾika humu'l-**muḍʿifūn*** — **those who multiply.**

| Verse | Word | The branch of the root | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| **39** | *Al-Muḍʿifūn* | **Multiplying many times over** | **The one who gives** |
| **54** | *Ḍaʿf* (three times) | **Weakness** | **Every human being** |

**The same root, in two contrary values. I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this shared root: the fifty-fourth verse says that man stands on an arc that opens with weakness and closes with weakness. The thirty-ninth verse says, with the other branch of the same root, that within that arc there can be something that multiplies many times over. The link between the two verses lies in the word and can be verified; I claim nothing beyond that.**

## 30/55-57 — وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ ٱلسَّاعَةُ يُقْسِمُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ مَا لَبِثُوا۟ غَيْرَ سَاعَةٍ كَذَٰلِكَ كَانُوا۟ يُؤْفَكُونَ · وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ وَٱلْإِيمَٰنَ لَقَدْ لَبِثْتُمْ فِى كِتَٰبِ ٱللَّهِ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْبَعْثِ فَهَٰذَا يَوْمُ ٱلْبَعْثِ وَلَٰكِنَّكُمْ كُنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ · فَيَوْمَئِذٍ لَّا يَنفَعُ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟ مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ وَلَا هُمْ يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ

*Wa yawma taqūmu's-sāʿatu yuqsimu'l-mujrimūna mā labithū ghayra sāʿah, kadhālika kānū yuʾfakūn · Wa qāla'lladhīna ūtu'l-ʿilma wa'l-īmāna laqad labithtum fī kitābi'llāhi ilā yawmi'l-baʿth, fa-hādhā yawmu'l-baʿthi wa lākinnakum kuntum lā taʿlamūn · Fa-yawmaʾidhin lā yanfaʿu'lladhīna ẓalamū maʿziratuhum wa lā hum yustaʿtabūn*

"**On the day the Hour stands, the guilty swear that they stayed no longer than an hour.** Thus were they **turned about.** **But those who were given knowledge and faith say:** 'You surely stayed, as is written in the Book of God, **until the day of the resurrection.** This is the day of the resurrection — **but you did not know.**' **On that day their excuses will not profit those who did wrong; nor will they be asked to make amends.**"

### Two *sāʿa*s, one sentence

**The same word occurs twice in the same verse and in two different senses:**

| Occurrence | Word | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Yawma taqūmu's-**sāʿah*** | **The resurrection** — definite (*al-*) |
| 2 | *Mā labithū ghayra **sāʿah*** | **An hour** — indefinite, a measure of time |

**This is a play on words (*jinās*) that can be verified within the verse**, and the word order must be recorded: **the name of the resurrection and the name of the span they claim are the same.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this shared word:** the other side sets out to measure a span they could not measure, and the unit they give is the same as the name of the event confronting them. **The sentence says, with the word itself, that the measure is not in their hands.**

**كَذَٰلِكَ كَانُوا۟ يُؤْفَكُونَ** — **the root أ-ف-ك: to turn a thing from its direction, to turn it inside out.** *Ifk* — **to invert the truth, a slander.** **The word was treated at `051-zariyat.md` 51/9 (*yuʾfaku ʿanhu man ufik*).** **I do not repeat it here.**

**The verb is passive: *yuʾfakūn* — "they were being turned about".** **The one who turns them is not named.** **And the tense pattern must be recorded: *kānū yuʾfakūn* — a past conveying continuance.** **That is, the sentence attaches that day's mistaken estimate to a continuous state in this world.**

### وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ وَٱلْإِيمَٰنَ

**Those who give the answer must be recorded, and they are named by two words: *al-ʿilm* and *al-īmān*.**

**And the verb is passive: *ūtū* — "those who were given". That is, both knowledge and faith are mentioned not as acquired but as given.**

**I record this as an observation and set it opposite the seventh verse of the sūra: there it was said *yaʿlamūna ẓāhiran* — there was knowledge, and it had stayed outside. Here *ūtu'l-ʿilm* — knowledge itself is mentioned as a giving. And the end of the sentence closes with the same root: *wa lākinnakum kuntum lā taʿlamūn*.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *Wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi **lā yaʿlamūn*** | **Most people** |
| **7** | ***Yaʿlamūna** ẓāhiran mina'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* | **The same people** — superficial knowledge |
| **30** | *Wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi **lā yaʿlamūn*** | **In the discussion of the fiṭra** |
| **56** | *Wa lākinnakum kuntum **lā taʿlamūn*** | **On that day, to their faces** |
| **59** | *Kadhālika yaṭbaʿu'llāhu ʿalā qulūbi'lladhīna **lā yaʿlamūn*** | **In the discussion of the seal** |

**The same expression five times in the sūra, and the last of them comes turned to the one addressed: *lākinnakum* — from the third person to the second. This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as my own reading:** what is said throughout the sūra as "they do not know" is, in the fifty-sixth verse, **said to them.** **The verdict is the same; the one addressed has changed.**

**وَلَا هُمْ يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ** — **the root ع-ت-ب was analysed in detail at `041-fussilet.md` 41/24.** **I do not repeat it here.** What was recorded there: **to apologise in order to remove another's anger, to try to bring him round;** and the concrete branch of the root: ***ʿataba*, a doorstep and the step of a stair** — the lexicographers explain the connection by the picture of "the removal of an obstacle set in the way".

**The difference of pattern here must be recorded:**

| Place | Word | Pattern | Who does it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/24 | *Yastaʿtibū* | **Form X, active** | **They themselves ask** |
| **al-Rūm 30/57** | *Yustaʿtabūn* | **Form X, passive** | **It is not asked of them** |

**The same root, the one an asking and the other a not-being-asked.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of voice:** in Fuṣṣilat the door was closing **on their own initiative**; **in al-Rūm the door is not opened at all from the other side: no opportunity to set things right is *offered* them.**

**And the sentence at `032-secde.md` 32/29 holds the same place:** *lā yanfaʿu'lladhīna kafarū īmānuhum wa lā hum yunẓarūn* — **no profit + no respite.** **In al-Rūm it is: no profit + no opportunity.** **The two sūras build the same structure with two different second elements.**

## 30/58-59 — وَلَقَدْ ضَرَبْنَا لِلنَّاسِ فِى هَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ مِن كُلِّ مَثَلٍ وَلَئِن جِئْتَهُم بِـَٔايَةٍ لَّيَقُولَنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ إِنْ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا مُبْطِلُونَ · كَذَٰلِكَ يَطْبَعُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*Wa laqad ḍarabnā li'n-nāsi fī hādha'l-Qurʾāni min kulli mathal, wa la-in jiʾtahum bi-āyatin la-yaqūlanna'lladhīna kafarū in antum illā mubṭilūn · Kadhālika yaṭbaʿu'llāhu ʿalā qulūbi'lladhīna lā yaʿlamūn*

"**We have surely struck for people in this Qurʾān every kind of parable.** But were you to bring them a sign, those who deny would certainly say: '**You are nothing but purveyors of falsehood**'. **Thus does God set a seal upon the hearts of those who do not know.**"

### إِنْ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا مُبْطِلُونَ

**مُبْطِل** — root **ب-ط-ل**: **to be empty, groundless, void.** *Bāṭil* — the opposite of the true; the lexicographers record the core of the root as **"to come to nothing, to be left void".** **The active participle of Form IV: "one who puts forward what has no ground".**

**The form of the objection is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the other side does not discuss the *āya* that is brought — **it characterises those who bring it.** *In **antum** illā mubṭilūn* — the subject of the sentence is not the evidence but **the ones addressed.**

**The same mechanism had been treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/44:** there a counterfactual situation was set up, and it was shown that **whichever option obtained, an objection fitted to it would be found.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two verses say the same thing by two different routes:**

| Place | How it is shown |
|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/44 | **By setting up a hypothesis** — "what would they have said had it been otherwise?" |
| **al-Rūm 30/58** | **By setting up a condition** — *wa la-in jiʾtahum bi-āyatin la-yaqūlanna* |

**Both show that the objection arises not from the content but from the attitude.** **And al-Rūm's sentence gives this with the emphasis of *la-yaqūlanna* (they will certainly say): the outcome is known in advance.**

**مِن كُلِّ مَثَلٍ** — "every kind of parable". **And this forms a ring with the twenty-eighth verse of the sūra:** there it was said *ḍaraba lakum **mathalan** min anfusikum* — **a single parable.** **Here the whole body of the parables is mentioned.**

### يَطْبَعُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِ

**The root ط-ب-ع was treated in `047-muhammed.md` and in `063-munafikun.md`.** **I do not repeat it here.** The core: **to set a seal, to stamp; and to give a thing a lasting form.** *Ṭabʿ* — both a seal and a nature or character.

**And the joining of the sentence must be recorded: *kadhālika* — "thus". That is, the seal is mentioned after the attitude described in the preceding verse.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order, and per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write the boundary as well:** the verse's sequence gives the attitude first and the seal after. **From this I do not enter into the debate on destiny or on "whose responsibility the failure to believe is"; I confine myself to recording the literal order of the sentence.**

**And the name of those who are sealed must be recorded:** *alladhīna **lā yaʿlamūn*** — **those who do not know.** **The expression that occurred in the sixth and thirtieth verses of the sūra here names the subject of the seal.** **That is, the sentence repeated throughout the sūra is tied at the end to a verdict.**

## 30/60 — فَٱصْبِرْ إِنَّ وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ حَقٌّ وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُوقِنُونَ

*Fa'ṣbir inna waʿda'llāhi ḥaqqun wa lā yastakhiffannaka'lladhīna lā yūqinūn*

"**So be patient; the promise of God is true.** **And let not those who have no certainty unsettle you.**"

### A return to the sūra's first sentence

**The sūra had opened with a promise** — *fī biḍʿi sinīn* (3-4) — **and in the sixth verse that promise was named: *waʿda'llāh, lā yukhlifu'llāhu waʿdah*.**

**The last verse brings the same word back: *inna waʿda'llāhi ḥaqq*.**

| Verse | Expression | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *Waʿda'llāh, lā yukhlifu'llāhu waʿdah* | **A notification** — the promise is not broken |
| **60** | *Inna waʿda'llāhi **ḥaqq*** | **A ground** — the reason for patience |

**The same word, at the two ends of the sūra.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this ring: in the sixth verse the promise was the ground of the historical announcement of the second. In the sixtieth verse the same promise becomes the ground of a command about conduct. The sūra opens with an announcement and closes by saying what that announcement requires a person to do.**

### وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ — root: خ-ف-ف

**The root was analysed at `101-karia.md` 101/8 and treated in detail at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/54 (*fa'stakhaffa qawmahu*); it was mentioned in `079-naziat.md` as well.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**A summary of what was recorded there:** **the root is *to be light*.** *Istakhaffa* — **to count light, to belittle, to take lightly.** **And as was recorded in the discussion of al-Qāriʿa, in Arabic the same root carries both lightness on the scales and the failure to take a thing seriously; English has the same relation: to make *light* of something.**

**And it was tabulated at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/54 that the lexicographers record a second sense for *istakhaffa* as well, and that the exegetes differ between the two.** **The verse here is the clearest instance of that second sense:**

| Sense | What *istakhaffa* means | In al-Rūm 30/60 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To count light, to belittle** | "Let them not belittle you / take you lightly" |
| 2 | **To shift a thing from its place, to sweep it away** — to lift and carry off something light with ease | "**Let them not shift you from your place**, let them not sweep you away" |

**Both senses are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**But I record that the second sense has a strong ground in the word order here, and I give this as an observation:** the subject of the sentence is **they** (*alladhīna lā yūqinūn*), its object is **you** (*-ka*). **That is, the verb conveys something the other side would do upon the one addressed.** **In the first sense the verb is an *assessment* (they count you light); in the second it is an *effect* (they shift you from your place).** **And the sentence comes immediately after a command (*fa'ṣbir* — be patient): the opposite of patience is not staying in place.**

**At `043-zuhruf.md` 43/54 the same verb was applied to a people** (*fa'stakhaffa qawmahu fa-aṭāʿūh* — he made light of his people, and they obeyed him). **The two places can be set side by side:**

| Place | Who, and whom | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zukhruf 43/54 | **A ruler, his people** | *Fa-aṭāʿūh* — **they obeyed** |
| **al-Rūm 30/60** | **A crowd, a single person** | *Lā yastakhiffannak* — **a prohibition** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of direction:** in al-Zukhruf the making light runs **from above downward** and its counterpart is obedience. **In al-Rūm it runs from below upward and its counterpart is forbidden.** **The same verb, in two directions: the one an event that happened, the other something commanded not to happen.**

**ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُوقِنُونَ — the root ي-ق-ن: the absence of any doubt. And the choice of the word must be recorded: the sentence does not say "those who do not believe", it says "those who do not know with certainty".**

**This overlaps with the seventh verse of the sūra and closes the ring:** there the other side had been described as *yaʿlamūna ẓāhiran* — **they had knowledge, and it was on the surface.** **In the last verse the same side is named *lā yūqinūn* — not having reached certainty.** **The two descriptions say the same thing: there is knowledge, and it is not firm enough to stand on.**

**And the command itself must be recorded: *fa'ṣbir*.** **As was recorded in `070-mearic.md`, the name of the distance between the waiting and the outcome is patience.** **al-Rūm arrives at the same place: in the second verse an outcome was announced, and in the sixtieth the awaiting of that outcome is commanded.**

### And where the sūra leaves us

**The sūra opened with an announcement that could be verified:** an outcome was declared and its span was bounded (2-4). **And immediately afterwards a diagnosis was made:** *yaʿlamūna ẓāhiran mina'l-ḥayāti'd-dunyā* (7) — **they know the outside of the life of this world.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that these two verses come one after the other:** after giving a sign from within history, the sūra says why that sign goes unread. **The problem is not a failure to look — it is staying on the surface of what is looked at.**

**And the remaining fifty-three verses build a series that shows what lies beneath that same surface:** the bond between spouses (21), the difference of tongues and colours (22), sleep and gain (23), lightning (24), the standing of the heaven (25), wind and rain (48-50), the three stages of a lifetime (54). **All of them are things that are seen; and each of them is named an *āya*.**

**The sūra closes with a word, and that word has to do with knowledge:** *alladhīna **lā yūqinūn*** — those who do not reach certainty. **The diagnosis made in the seventh verse comes back in the sixtieth with the same family of words.** **Everything given in between is there to close that distance.**

# 21. Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ

**112 verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the **series of prophets** that runs from the forty-eighth verse to the ninety-first: *al-Anbiyāʾ* — "the prophets". The word does not occur in the sūra itself; the name is given from the content.

The sūra opens with a sentence of report: **ٱقْتَرَبَ لِلنَّاسِ حِسَابُهُمْ** — "the reckoning of mankind has drawn near". No introduction, no address, no oath. **The same verb (*iqtarabat*) opened a sūra with the same function at `054-kamer.md` on 54/1**, and it was recorded there, relying on `102-tekasur.md`: **sūras that open with a report outright are few, and the reader is set down in the middle of the scene.** al-Anbiyāʾ is another member of that list.

And the spine of the sūra is this: **the same verb comes once more, ninety-six verses later.** The ninety-seventh verse: *wa'qtaraba'l-waʿdu'l-ḥaqq* — "the true promise has drawn near". **Those who are in *ghafla* in the first verse say in their own words in the ninety-seventh that they were in *ghafla*:** *qad kunnā fī ghaflatin min hādhā.* **The sūra puts its opening sentence into the mouth of the one addressed at a point near the close.** This is a frame that can be verified from the text and will be tabulated at the end.

And the sūra's business is the long series in the middle. **Sixteen names are named one after another**, and the series has a technique of its own: **the stories are not told, they are called up.** Noah (Nūḥ), Job (Ayyūb), Jonah (Yūnus), Zechariah (Zakariyyā) — all four enter with the same verb (*nādā*), and to all four the same answer is given (*fa'stajabnā lah*). **The series records not what the prophets did but what they asked for and what they received.**

And when the series ends the sūra sets down its verdict in a single sentence: *inna hādhihi ummatukum ummatan wāḥidatan wa anā rabbukum fa'budūn* (92). **That is, the sixteen names have been ranged as the evidence of a single sentence.**

---

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What ends the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-15 | The nearing of the reckoning, listening in play, the humanity of the messengers, the cities broken | *ḥaṣīdan khāmidīn* (15) |
| **II** | 16-33 | Creation is not play; the rejection of a plurality of gods; the proofs of sky, earth and water | *kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn* (33) |
| **III** | 34-47 | There is no deathlessness; testing with evil and with good; haste; mockery; the balance | *wa kafā binā ḥāsibīn* (47) |
| **IV** | 48-91 | **The series of prophets** — from Moses and Aaron to Mary | *āyatan li'l-ʿālamīn* (91) |
| **V** | 92-93 | The declaration of a single community, and the breaking apart | *kullun ilaynā rājiʿūn* (93) |
| **VI** | 94-112 | The cities whose return is closed, Gog and Magog, the two ends, the rolling up of the sky, the close | *ʿalā mā taṣifūn* (112) |

**The boundaries of the blocks are drawn by the text's own words, and three of them are countable:**

- **Block I and block VI are joined by the same verb:** *iqtaraba* (1) — *wa'qtaraba* (97).
- **Block II opens with *lāʿibīn*** (16) and the same word occurs once more **in block IV, in the question put to Abraham**: *am anta mina'l-lāʿibīn* (55).
- **The root *waṣf* occurs three times in block II and once in the last verse of block VI:** *mimmā taṣifūn* (18) — *ʿammā yaṣifūn* (22) — *ʿalā mā taṣifūn* (112).

**And the sūra's densest word is the root ذ-ك-ر:** 2, 7, 10, 24, 36, 42, 48, 50, 105 — in nine verses. In this sūra the Qurʾān names itself *dhikr* in five separate places. **This is a countable fact; it will be tabulated at the end.**

## 21/1 — ٱقْتَرَبَ لِلنَّاسِ حِسَابُهُمْ وَهُمْ فِى غَفْلَةٍ مُّعْرِضُونَ

*Iqtaraba li'n-nāsi ḥisābuhum wa hum fī ghaflatin muʿriḍūn*

"**The reckoning of mankind has drawn near, while they turn away in heedlessness.**"

### ٱقْتَرَبَ — and a comparison with al-Qamar

**The root ق-ر-ب** and **the Form VIII (*iftiʿāl*) pattern were analysed in detail at `054-kamer.md` on 54/1.** The note there holds here as well, and its summary is this:

> *"Qaruba* conveys being near, *iqtaraba* conveys **the movement of nearing** — it is approaching, the distance is closing."*

**I do not repeat it. Here I add what is peculiar to al-Anbiyāʾ.**

**The Qurʾān uses this verb of a thing drawing near in three places, and all three are either treated or to be treated in this commentary:**

| Place | What draws near | The sentence beside it |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Qamar 54/1** | *al-sāʿa* — **the Hour** | *wa'nshaqqa'l-qamar* — together with an event |
| **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/1** | *ḥisābuhum* — **their reckoning** | *wa hum fī ghaflatin muʿriḍūn* — together with a state |
| **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/97** | *al-waʿdu'l-ḥaqq* — **the true promise** | *qad kunnā fī ghaflatin min hādhā* — with the admission of that same state |

**And the difference must be recorded: in al-Qamar what draws near is the Hour — a time. Here what draws near is *the reckoning* — a proceeding.** Time is the same for everyone; the reckoning has been **ascribed to its owner:** *ḥisāb**uhum*** — "their reckoning".

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pronoun:** the sentence does not say "the resurrection has drawn near". **It names what draws near as the addressee's own file.**

### لِلنَّاسِ — fronting

**And there is a fronting in the word order:** *iqtaraba **li'n-nāsi** ḥisābuhum.* The phrase *li'n-nās* has come in between the verb and its subject (*ḥisābuhum*).

**In Arabic, fronting conveys *ihtimām* (giving prominence).** And what is given prominence is **the one addressed** — not the reckoning.

**I record as my own reading:** the sentence first says to whom the thing has drawn near, then what has drawn near. **The order leaves the weight of the news on the human being.**

### وَهُمْ فِى غَفْلَةٍ مُّعْرِضُونَ

**The *wāw* here is the *wāw* of circumstance** ("while…"). **And two predicates come one upon the other:** *fī ghaflatin* (a prepositional phrase) and *muʿriḍūn* (an active participle).

**The root غ-ف-ل:** not to be aware of a thing, for the mind to be empty of it. *Ghufl* — that which bears no mark upon it, unbranded; **ground in the desert that carries no track.** So in the picture of the root there is **an absence of any sign.**

**And the difference between the two words is worth recording, because the verse says both at once:**

| Word | What it conveys | The share of the will |
|---|---|---|
| **غَفْلَة** | Not being aware | **None** — he does not know |
| **مُّعْرِض** | Turning away (ع-ر-ض: to turn one's side, one's breadth) | **Present** — he knows and turns |

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the two states do not normally stand together. One who is unaware cannot turn away; one who turns away is not counted unaware. **The verse ascribes both at once.**

**And the word will occur four times in the sūra:** *muʿriḍūn* (1), *muʿriḍūn* (24), *muʿriḍūn* (32), *muʿriḍūn* (42). **All four in the same plural form, and all four following a proof.** This is a countable fact and will be tabulated at the end.

## 21/2-3 — مَا يَأْتِيهِم مِّن ذِكْرٍ مِّن رَّبِّهِم مُّحْدَثٍ إِلَّا ٱسْتَمَعُوهُ وَهُمْ يَلْعَبُونَ · لَاهِيَةً قُلُوبُهُمْ

*Mā yaʾtīhim min dhikrin min rabbihim muḥdathin illa'stamaʿūhu wa hum yalʿabūn · Lāhiyatan qulūbuhum wa asarru'n-najwa'lladhīna ẓalamū hal hādhā illā basharun mithlukum a-fa-taʾtūna's-siḥra wa antum tubṣirūn*

"**No new remembrance comes to them from their Lord but they listen to it in play** · **their hearts distracted.** Those who do wrong spread the whisper in secret: '**Is this anything but a human being like yourselves? Will you go to sorcery with your eyes open?**'"

### مُّحْدَث — and an exact overlap with al-Shuʿarāʾ

**The word *muḥdath* was analysed at `026-suara.md` on 26/5.** What was recorded there holds here as well:

> **"The root ح-د-ث**: to come about afterwards, to occur. *Ḥadīth* — what is new, an event, a saying. **مُحْدَث** — a passive participle: **brought into being afterwards, newly sent down.** […] The word is an attribute of *dhikr* and looks to the moment of revelation: each newly descending portion."

**And a boundary was drawn there; I repeat it here because it is necessary:** it is known that the word has been connected with a debate in the history of theology. **This commentary does not enter into that debate; the context of the verse is the newness of the revelation.**

**What emerges when the two verses are set side by side is a plain instance of the Qurʾān's interpretation by the Qurʾān:**

| | **al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/5** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/2** |
|---|---|---|
| The shared pattern | *mā yaʾtīhim min dhikrin … muḥdathin illā* | *mā yaʾtīhim min dhikrin … muḥdathin illā* |
| From whom | *mina'**r-Raḥmān*** | *min **rabbihim*** |
| The response | *kānū ʿanhu **muʿriḍīn*** — they turned away | *istamaʿūhu wa hum **yalʿabūn*** — they listened, in play |

**And the difference must be recorded — these are two distinct responses:**

**In al-Shuʿarāʾ there is a turning away: the text is not heard. In al-Anbiyāʾ it is listened to — but in play.** The second is finer than the first, because **the act of listening has taken place.**

***Istamaʿū* — Form VIII.** And the burden of the form works here: *samiʿa* is to hear, *istamaʿa* is **to lend an ear** — deliberate listening, listening that costs effort. **That is, the verse describes not an inattentive hearing but an attentive listening.**

**I record as my own reading, and its ground is the form:** the person described in the verse is not missing the text. **He is listening — and playing while he listens.**

### وَهُمْ يَلْعَبُونَ · لَاهِيَةً قُلُوبُهُمْ — two words, two layers

**The root ل-ع-ب:** work without aim, without outcome; play. **The word is one of the key words of this sūra** and in the sixteenth verse it will be negated **of creation itself**: *wa mā khalaqna's-samāʾa wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā **lāʿibīn***.

**The root ل-ه-و:** what keeps a person from his real business, what distracts him. **The root was treated at `031-lokman.md` on 31/6 (*lahwa'l-ḥadīth*), at `062-cuma.md` on 62/11 and at `057-hadid.md` on 57/20.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the division of labour between the two words must be recorded:**

| Word | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ***yalʿabūn*** | **Outward** — a verbal sentence, the state of the limbs | They play while listening |
| ***lāhiyatan qulūbuhum*** | **Inward** — the *heart* is named openly | The heart is elsewhere |

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the verse states first the conduct and then the heart. **And *lāhiyatan* is in the accusative — it is a circumstantial qualifier:** that is, the second clause is not the cause of the first but **its state at the same moment.**

### وَأَسَرُّوا۟ ٱلنَّجْوَى

***Asarrū* — the root أ-س-ر-ر / س-ر-ر: to conceal.** ***Al-najwā* — the root ن-ج-و: to speak head to head, in lowered voices.**

**And the expression is striking: "they concealed the concealed".** *Najwā* is secret speech already; *asarrū* is to conceal it once more.

**The lexicographers transmit two readings here:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Intensification:** they hid the whisper thoroughly |
| 2 | ***Asarra* here is of opposite sense** (*aḍdād*): "they brought it out, they spread it" |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** That certain verbs in Arabic bear two opposite senses (*aḍdād*) is recorded by the lexicographers; *asarra* has been counted among them.

**وَٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا۟** — the subject comes after the verb. **The grammarians' explanation:** the sentence gives the act first and names its agent afterwards. **And the naming is not a name but an attribute:** *alladhīna ẓalamū* — "those who do wrong". **I record this per the method (`STYLE.md`): what is named here is not a community but an attribute.**

**هَلْ هَٰذَآ إِلَّا بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُكُمْ** — and the content of the whisper is the objection that will be answered in the seventh verse of the sūra.

## 21/4 — قَالَ رَبِّى يَعْلَمُ ٱلْقَوْلَ فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَهُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

*Qāla rabbī yaʿlamu'l-qawla fi's-samāʾi wa'l-arḍ, wa huwa's-samīʿu'l-ʿalīm*

"**He said: 'My Lord knows what is spoken in the heaven and the earth. He is the Hearing, the Knowing.'**"

### The target of the answer

**In the third verse there was a *whisper*. The fourth verse answers it with *knowledge* — not with content.**

**I record as my own reading, and its ground is the words of the two verses:** the objection was spoken in secret (*asarrū'n-najwā*); the answer takes aim at **the secrecy**, not at the substance of the objection. *Rabbī yaʿlamu'l-qawl* — "my Lord knows what is spoken."

**And the scope is given in two words: *fi's-samāʾi wa'l-arḍ*.** The whisper was spoken on earth; the verse names the heaven too. **That is, the scale is kept larger than the scale of the whisper.**

**A variant reading is transmitted:** *qāla* (perfect, "he said") and *qul* (imperative, "say"). **The direction of the sense changes — on the first the answer given by the Prophet is transmitted, on the second he is commanded to give the answer.** Both readings are transmitted; **I name no reciter and impose no preference.** **The same disagreement will appear once more in the sūra's last verse (112).**

**ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ** — two attributes: **hearing and knowing.** And the order is worth recording: **hearing first.** The subject of the verse is what is spoken; speech is heard first.

## 21/5-6 — بَلْ قَالُوٓا۟ أَضْغَٰثُ أَحْلَٰمٍۭ بَلِ ٱفْتَرَىٰهُ بَلْ هُوَ شَاعِرٌ فَلْيَأْتِنَا بِـَٔايَةٍ كَمَآ أُرْسِلَ ٱلْأَوَّلُونَ · مَآ ءَامَنَتْ قَبْلَهُم مِّن قَرْيَةٍ أَهْلَكْنَٰهَآ أَفَهُمْ يُؤْمِنُونَ

*Bal qālū aḍghāthu aḥlāmin bali'ftarāhu bal huwa shāʿir, fa'l-yaʾtinā bi-āyatin kamā ursila'l-awwalūn · Mā āmanat qablahum min qaryatin ahlaknāhā a-fa-hum yuʾminūn*

"**No — they said: 'A jumble of dreams!' 'No, he has invented it!' 'No, he is a poet!' Then let him bring us a sign, as the ancients were sent with one!** · **No town We destroyed before them believed; will these, then, believe?**"

### Three *bal*s and one demand

**The verse turns three times with *bal*. And *bal* in Arabic is the particle that abandons the preceding statement and puts a new one in its place.**

| Order | Claim | What it grants |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Aḍghāthu aḥlām* — **a jumble of dreams** | He sees something, but it is meaningless |
| 2 | *Iftarāhu* — **he invented it** | Deliberate production |
| 3 | *Huwa shāʿir* — **he is a poet** | A craft whose source is known |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence of the three *bal*s:** the three claims **do not hold together.** One does not say of a man who dreams that "he invented it"; one who invents is not counted a "poet". **The text ranges the objections one after another without correcting them** — and this arrangement shows that the objections cancel one another.

**The same technique was tabulated at `025-furkan.md` on 25/4-8:** there four objections were ranged and it was recorded that "not one of them looks at what the text says". **I rely on that treatment.** **What al-Anbiyāʾ adds is that the objections contradict one another.**

**أَضْغَٰث** — root **ض-غ-ث**: **a mixed bundle of stalks gathered into one handful.** *Ḍighth* — a tangled sheaf held in the hand. **And the same word occurs at `038-sad.md` on 38/44, in the command given to Job:** *wa khudh bi-yadika **ḍighthan***. **The same root, in two different offices: one the confusedness of a dream, the other a sheaf held in the hand.** I give this as a note about the root.

### فَلْيَأْتِنَا بِـَٔايَةٍ كَمَآ أُرْسِلَ ٱلْأَوَّلُونَ

**The demand is the demand that will determine the whole sūra: *āya* — a visible sign.**

**And the answer of the sixth verse rests on a datum of history:** *mā āmanat qablahum min qaryatin ahlaknāhā* — "no town We destroyed believed."

**The logic of the sentence must be recorded:** the sign demanded had been given to those before; **and when it was given, they did not believe.** So the answer does not reject the demand — **it shows from history that the demand does not work.**

***A-fa-hum yuʾminūn*** — a question that carries its answer within it (a rhetorical question implying denial).

## 21/7 — وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَا قَبْلَكَ إِلَّا رِجَالًا نُّوحِىٓ إِلَيْهِمْ فَسْـَٔلُوٓا۟ أَهْلَ ٱلذِّكْرِ إِن كُنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

*Wa mā arsalnā qablaka illā rijālan nūḥī ilayhim, fa'sʾalū ahla'dh-dhikri in kuntum lā taʿlamūn*

"**Before you too We sent none but men to whom We gave revelation.** If you do not know, ask the people of the remembrance."

### The same answer as in al-Furqān, with another word

**The objection in the third verse was: *hal hādhā illā basharun mithlukum* — "is this anything but a human being like yourselves?"**

**This objection and its answer were treated in detail at `025-furkan.md` on 25/7 and 25/20.** The note there was this:

> **"The answer does not reject the objection's description — it confirms it and generalises it. That is, not 'it is not so' but 'it was always so'. What was objected to is set down not as an exception but as the rule."**

**I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it. Here I add the difference of wording between the two sūras:**

| | **al-Furqān 25/20** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/7-8** |
|---|---|---|
| What is said | *la-**yaʾkulūna'ṭ-ṭaʿāma** wa yamshūna fi'l-aswāq* | *illā **rijālan** nūḥī ilayhim* |
| Which aspect | **Conduct** — they eat, they walk in the markets | **Kind** — they are human, they are men |
| The next verse | *wa jaʿalnā baʿḍakum li-baʿḍin fitna* | *wa mā jaʿalnāhum jasadan lā yaʾkulūna'ṭ-ṭaʿām* (8) |

**And what is worth recording is this: al-Anbiyāʾ itself builds al-Furqān's sentence one verse later** — *lā yaʾkulūna'ṭ-ṭaʿām* (8). **That is, the two sūras give the same answer; one says it directly, the other inside a negation.** This is a verifiable sharing of wording.

### رِجَالًا — the limit of the word

***Rijāl* — the root ر-ج-ل, the plural of *rajul*.** **The concrete sense of the root is *rijl* — the foot;** *rajul* means one who walks, one on foot. **At `025-furkan.md` the objection *yamshī fi'l-aswāq* carried the same picture as this root.**

**The word conveys two things at once here, and the lexicographers record both:**

| What it conveys | What it excludes |
|---|---|
| Being **human** | Being an angel |
| Being **male** | (This aspect too is recorded in the classical commentaries) |

**The context of the verse is the first:** the objection had said "he cannot be human", the answer says "it was always a human". **I transmit the second aspect because it is recorded in the classical commentaries; the proof the verse builds in this context is the first. I do not impose a preference.**

**نُّوحِىٓ إِلَيْهِمْ** — a relative clause, and it is **imperfect**. Not *awḥaynā* (perfect) but *nūḥī* (imperfect). **The imperfect conveys continuity and renewal.** **That is, the distinction is not "not being human" but "receiving revelation" — and this is given as a continuing state.**

### فَسْـَٔلُوٓا۟ أَهْلَ ٱلذِّكْرِ

**There is disagreement over who is meant:**

| View | Who *ahlu'dh-dhikr* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | The people of the earlier scripture — because the matter is what the past messengers were |
| 2 | Those who have knowledge in general — the wording of the word admits this |
| 3 | *Dhikr* here is the Qurʾān; its people are those who know it |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And what is to be recorded in respect of word order is this:** the sentence lays down a **method** — *in kuntum lā taʿlamūn.* **Not knowing is set down as the condition of asking.** And the word *dhikr* is the second link of the root that occurs nine times in this sūra; **three verses later the Qurʾān itself will be called *dhikrukum*.**

## 21/8-9 — وَمَا جَعَلْنَٰهُمْ جَسَدًا لَّا يَأْكُلُونَ ٱلطَّعَامَ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ خَٰلِدِينَ · ثُمَّ صَدَقْنَٰهُمُ ٱلْوَعْدَ فَأَنجَيْنَٰهُمْ وَمَن نَّشَآءُ وَأَهْلَكْنَا ٱلْمُسْرِفِينَ

*Wa mā jaʿalnāhum jasadan lā yaʾkulūna'ṭ-ṭaʿāma wa mā kānū khālidīn · Thumma ṣadaqnāhumu'l-waʿda fa-anjaynāhum wa man nashāʾu wa ahlakna'l-musrifīn*

"**We did not make them bodies that eat no food, nor were they deathless.** · **Then We made the promise true to them: We delivered them and whom We willed, and destroyed those who went to excess.**"

### جَسَدًا — its coming in the singular

**The word is singular (*jasadan*), though the pronoun is plural (*jaʿalnāhum*).** The grammarians explain this as a **generic noun**: "of the kind of body" — it conveys no number.

***Jasad* — the root ج-س-د.** The lexicographers record a distinction between *jasad* and *jism*: ***jasad* is used of a body showing no sign of life** — as the Qurʾān has *ʿijlan jasadan lahu khuwār* of the calf (al-Aʿrāf 7/148; Ṭāhā 20/88). **That is, the choice of the word hints that what the objection wanted was *a lifeless appearance*.**

**I record this as an observation about the word; I do not claim that the verse builds a ruling upon the hint.**

**وَمَا كَانُوا۟ خَٰلِدِينَ** — and the second negation. **This sentence looks at exactly the same matter as the thirty-fourth verse of the sūra:** *wa mā jaʿalnā li-basharin min qablika'l-khuld.* **There are twenty-six verses between the two, and both say the same thing. It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

### صَدَقْنَٰهُمُ ٱلْوَعْدَ

***Ṣadaqnā* — Form I, not Form II, taking two objects:** "we made the promise true to them". **The root ص-د-ق:** a word's conforming to fact.

**And a point of word order must be recorded:** the sentence does not say "we kept our word". ***Ṣadaqnāhumu'l-waʿd*** — "we made the promise true to them". **That is, what is made true is the word, and who is made true is the messenger.**

**وَمَن نَّشَآءُ** — a further group is added to those delivered, and it is not named. **The verse does not name it; nor do I.**

**ٱلْمُسْرِفِينَ** — root **س-ر-ف**: **to overstep the bound, to go past the measure.** **And the choice of the word is worth recording:** those destroyed are named not as *kāfirūn* or *mushrikūn* but as **those who overstep the measure.** **The scene of the balance that will be set up in the forty-seventh verse of the sūra is the other face of the same notion.** I record this as my own reading.

## 21/10 — لَقَدْ أَنزَلْنَآ إِلَيْكُمْ كِتَٰبًا فِيهِ ذِكْرُكُمْ أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ

*Laqad anzalnā ilaykum kitāban fīhi dhikrukum, a-fa-lā taʿqilūn*

"**Indeed We have sent down to you a Book in which is your remembrance. Will you not use your reason?**"

### ذِكْرُكُمْ — the two directions of the annexation

**The expression is *dhikrukum*, and there is disagreement over which way the construct is to be read:**

| Reading | What *dhikr* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Honour, being spoken of** | "A Book in which is your honour, the mention of your name" |
| 2 | **Admonition, reminder** | "A Book containing an admonition addressed to you" |
| 3 | **What you are to remember by** | "A Book by which you are to remember your Lord" |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **The two basic aspects of the root ذ-ك-ر** — to remember, and to mention or be spoken of — **are the source of this disagreement, and the root was treated at `036-yasin.md` on 36/69 (*wa mā huwa illā dhikrun wa qurʾānun mubīn*) and at `038-sad.md` on 38/1 (*dhi'dh-dhikr*).** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And a note within the sūra: in the seventh verse it was commanded to ask *ahla'dh-dhikr*; in the tenth it is said that the *dhikr* is in the addressee's own hands.** **This completes a movement of three verses:** ask (7) → if you do not know (7) → you already have it (10).

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the sequence of the three verses.**

**أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ** — root **ع-ق-ل**: **to bind, to tether a camel.** *ʿIqāl* — the cord tied to a camel's leg. **So intelligence is, by its root, a capacity for binding:** holding together what would scatter. **This question occurs twice in the sūra: 10 and 67 (in Abraham's mouth).** This is a countable fact.

## 21/11-12 — وَكَمْ قَصَمْنَا مِن قَرْيَةٍۭ كَانَتْ ظَالِمَةً وَأَنشَأْنَا بَعْدَهَا قَوْمًا ءَاخَرِينَ · فَلَمَّآ أَحَسُّوا۟ بَأْسَنَآ إِذَا هُم مِّنْهَا يَرْكُضُونَ

*Wa kam qaṣamnā min qaryatin kānat ẓālimatan wa anshaʾnā baʿdahā qawman ākharīn · Fa-lammā aḥassū baʾsanā idhā hum minhā yarkuḍūn*

"**How many a town that had gone to wrong have We broken, and raised up another people after it.** · **When they felt Our might, at once they were running from it.**"

### قَصَمْنَا — the harshness of the word

***Qaṣamnā* — the root ق-ص-م.** **And the word is analysed here for the first time in this commentary.**

**The sense the lexicographers give: *qaṣm* is to break a thing and separate it into two parts** — especially **to break a bone.** *Qaṣama'ẓ-ẓahr* — he broke the back.

**And the lexicographers define it by comparing it with a sister word:**

| Word | What it does |
|---|---|
| **قَصْم** | **Breaks and the parts come apart** — it snaps off |
| **فَصْم** | **Cracks but the parts do not come apart** — the fracture stays in place |

**So the word conveys a break that cannot be mended.** And its object is a town: *qaṣamnā min qaryatin.* **The town has been conceived as a body that can be broken.**

**The root occurs only here in the Qurʾān.** **I give this as a note about the word.**

**كَانَتْ ظَالِمَةً** — and the town is named not by its name but **by its attribute.** **I record this per the method (`STYLE.md`): the ground of the destruction is not an identity but a quality.**

**وَأَنشَأْنَا بَعْدَهَا قَوْمًا ءَاخَرِينَ** — the root ن-ش-أ: to bring a thing into being afresh, to raise it up. **The sentence leaves no gap: in the place of what was broken a new one is set.**

### يَرْكُضُونَ — and a root shared with Ṣād

***Yarkuḍūn* — the root ر-ك-ض.** **The concrete sense of the root: *to strike the ground with the foot.*** *Rakaḍa'd-dābba* — he spurred his mount on. **From this arises the sense "to run, to take to one's heels".**

**And the root occurs in two places in the Qurʾān; both are in this commentary:**

| Place | Expression | Who | For what |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ṣād 38/42** | *ur**kuḍ** bi-rijlik* | **Job** | Healing — strike the ground, water will come |
| **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/12** | *hum minhā **yarkuḍūn*** | **Those destroyed** | Flight — running away |

**That verse was treated at `038-sad.md` on 38/41-44. I rely on that treatment.**

**I record this as an observation about the root, and I do not claim that the two verses refer to one another:** the same verb is attached in one place to **deliverance by a command** and in the other to **flight in panic.** **The striking of the foot on the ground does two different works in the two places.**

***Idhā* here is the *idhā* of surprise** ("and at once") — it conveys suddenness. **And *aḥassū* — the root ح-س-س: to make out with the senses.** **That is, the punishment has not yet arrived, it has been felt.** The flight is not from what has come but **from what has been sensed.**

## 21/13-15 — لَا تَرْكُضُوا۟ وَٱرْجِعُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰ مَآ أُتْرِفْتُمْ فِيهِ وَمَسَٰكِنِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُسْـَٔلُونَ · قَالُوا۟ يَٰوَيْلَنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ · فَمَا زَالَت تِّلْكَ دَعْوَٰهُمْ حَتَّىٰ جَعَلْنَٰهُمْ حَصِيدًا خَٰمِدِينَ

*Lā tarkuḍū wa'rjiʿū ilā mā utriftum fīhi wa masākinikum laʿallakum tusʾalūn · Qālū yā waylanā innā kunnā ẓālimīn · Fa-mā zālat tilka daʿwāhum ḥattā jaʿalnāhum ḥaṣīdan khāmidīn*

"'**Do not run! Return to the luxury you were given and to your dwellings — perhaps you will be questioned.**' · **They said: 'Woe to us! We were indeed doing wrong.'** · **That did not cease to be their cry until We made them a harvested field, an extinguished fire.**"

### أُتْرِفْتُمْ — being given to luxury

***Utriftum* — root ت-ر-ف, Form IV passive.** ***Taraf* — to grow soft in abundance, to be indulged.** *Mutraf* — one made insolent by ease.

**The root was treated at `043-zuhruf.md` on 43/23 (*qāla mutrafūhā*) and at `034-sebe.md` on 34/34-35.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What I add here is the form: the verb is passive — *utriftum*, "you were given to luxury".** **That is, the being made insolent is given not as something they did of themselves but as something poured over them.** I record this as an observation about the pattern.

### لَعَلَّكُمْ تُسْـَٔلُونَ — the tone of the sentence

**The order of the imperatives must be recorded: *lā tarkuḍū* (do not run) → *irjiʿū* (return) → *laʿallakum tusʾalūn* (perhaps you will be questioned).**

**In the classical commentaries it is widely said that this sentence is by way of *tahzīl*/*istihzāʾ* (mockery, rebuke):** their return is not really wanted; an impossibility is set out in the imperative. **I transmit this as a transmitted explanation.**

**And in respect of word order I add this — as my own reading:** the verb *laʿallakum tusʾalūn* is from the root **س-أ-ل**, and this root will build the sūra's densest sentence in the twenty-third verse: *lā yusʾalu ʿammā yafʿalu wa hum yusʾalūn.* **The same verb, the same passive pattern. What is said by way of mockery in this group of verses is set down as a ruling in the twenty-third.** This is a sharing of wording that can be verified within the sūra.

### يَٰوَيْلَنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ — three times in the sūra

**This sentence occurs in three places in the sūra, and all three come from the same mouth: the mouth of those destroyed.**

| Verse | Who | When |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | The people of the town that was broken | **As the punishment comes** |
| **46** | Those warned (inside a conditional sentence) | **Were a breath of it to touch them** |
| **97** | Those who deny, when the promise draws near | **When the time is up** |

**Two of the three occurrences have taken place; one (46) is bound to a condition.** **This is a countable fact and is the sūra's most distinct refrain.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra repeats the same admission in three different spans of time — **and in all three the admission comes at a time when it will be of no use.**

### حَصِيدًا خَٰمِدِينَ — two pictures

**Two words, taken from two different fields:**

| Word | Root | The concrete picture |
|---|---|---|
| **حَصِيد** | ح-ص-د | **Harvested grain** — cut with the sickle and lying on the ground |
| **خَٰمِدِين** | خ-م-د | **A fire gone out** — its flame finished, its ash left |

***Khamada'n-nār* — the fire went out;** *khāmid* — that which has gone out. **The lexicographers record a difference of degree between *khumūd* and *humūd*:** *khumūd* is the ending of the flame, **embers may still remain**; the complete reduction to ash is called *humūd*.

**And a point of word order is worth recording:** *ḥaṣīdan* is singular, while *khāmidīn* is **the plural used of rational beings** (with *wāw-nūn*). **That is, the first word names them like a *thing*, the second like a *people*.** The two words carry two different treatments in the same sentence.

**I record this as an observation about the structure.** And the block closes here: the sūra's first block ends with **a fire gone out.**

## 21/16-18 — وَمَا خَلَقْنَا ٱلسَّمَآءَ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا لَٰعِبِينَ · لَوْ أَرَدْنَآ أَن نَّتَّخِذَ لَهْوًا لَّٱتَّخَذْنَٰهُ مِن لَّدُنَّآ إِن كُنَّا فَٰعِلِينَ · بَلْ نَقْذِفُ بِٱلْحَقِّ عَلَى ٱلْبَٰطِلِ فَيَدْمَغُهُۥ فَإِذَا هُوَ زَاهِقٌ وَلَكُمُ ٱلْوَيْلُ مِمَّا تَصِفُونَ

*Wa mā khalaqna's-samāʾa wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā lāʿibīn · Law aradnā an nattakhidha lahwan la'ttakhadhnāhu min ladunnā in kunnā fāʿilīn · Bal naqdhifu bi'l-ḥaqqi ʿala'l-bāṭili fa-yadmaghuhu fa-idhā huwa zāhiq, wa lakumu'l-waylu mimmā taṣifūn*

"**We did not create the heaven and the earth and what is between them in play.** · **Had We wanted a diversion, We would have taken it from Our own presence — if We were to do such a thing.** · **No! We hurl the truth against falsehood and it crushes its skull; and at once falsehood is gone. Woe to you for what you ascribe!**"

### لَٰعِبِينَ — and a sentence identical with al-Dukhān

**This sentence was treated in detail at `044-duhan.md` on 44/38, and this verse was expressly named there.** The note there:

> **"The word is in the accusative and is a circumstantial qualifier: 'as players'. […] The sentence does not say 'we did not create'. *Mā khalaqnā … lāʿibīn* — what is negated is not the act but the state of the act."**

**And the note about *bi'l-ḥaqq* was treated at `045-casiye.md` on 45/22 and at `046-ahkaf.md` on 46/3.** The two notes tabulated at al-Aḥqāf were: ***bi'l-ḥaqq*** — not in vain; ***ajalin musammā*** — not without end. **I rely on those treatments.**

**Here I add what is peculiar to al-Anbiyāʾ, and it is seen in a table of the three sūras:**

| | **al-Aḥqāf 46/3** | **al-Dukhān 44/38-39** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/16-18** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is negated | **None** | *lāʿibīn* | *lāʿibīn* |
| What is affirmed | *bi'l-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā* | *illā bi'l-ḥaqq* | ***naqdhifu bi'l-ḥaqqi ʿala'l-bāṭil*** |
| How *ḥaqq* appears | As **a qualification of the creating** | As **a qualification of the creating** | As **the object of a verb** — it is thrown |
| How many verses | One | Two | **Three** |

**And the most important difference is in the last row — I give this as my own reading:** in al-Aḥqāf and al-Dukhān *ḥaqq* is an **attribute** of the creating. **In al-Anbiyāʾ *ḥaqq* is used like a weapon: it is hurled, it strikes, it shatters.** The same word: in two sūras a quality, here an act.

**A difference of wording must also be recorded:** al-Dukhān says *al-samāwāt* (plural), **al-Anbiyāʾ *al-samāʾ* (singular).** This is a verifiable difference, and I build no ruling upon it.

### لَهْوًا — and the note "from Our own presence"

**The seventeenth verse strengthens the negation of the sixteenth with a conditional sentence.**

***Law* — the particle that conveys a condition unfulfilled.** And the sentence is in three parts: **the condition (*law aradnā*) → the answer (*la'ttakhadhnāhu min ladunnā*) → a second note (*in kunnā fāʿilīn*).**

**مِن لَّدُنَّا** — "from Our own presence". **And the work of this note must be recorded:** the sentence does not say "we would not have taken a diversion"; **it says "had we taken one, we would not have taken it from here".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the preposition:** *min ladunnā* conveys a **source.** **That is, the verse says that the heaven and the earth are not used as material for a diversion** — what has been created is not put in the class of playthings.

***In kunnā fāʿilīn*** — there is disagreement about it:

| Reading | What *in* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The particle of condition** | "If We were to do it" — a second condition, increasing the hypothetical force |
| 2 | **Negative** (*in* = *mā*) | "**We are not going to do this**" — a direct negation |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **On the second reading the sentence, having set up the condition, tears it up by the root.**

### نَقْذِفُ بِٱلْحَقِّ عَلَى ٱلْبَٰطِلِ — the harshness of three verbs

**And this is the harshest sequence of words in the sūra. Three verbs come one after another, and all three describe a physical collision.**

**نَقْذِفُ — root ق-ذ-ف.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to throw a thing far and fast, to hurl it.** *Qadhafa bi'l-ḥajar* — he hurled the stone. **The word occurs in the Qurʾān in places conveying a throwing**, and in Sūrat al-Nūr it is used of slander (whence the term *yarmūna*/*qadhf*): **a word too counts as a hurling.**

**And the prepositions must be recorded: *naqdhifu bi'l-ḥaqqi ʿala'l-bāṭil*.** *Bi* conveys the instrument, *ʿalā* the target. **That is, the truth is framed like a body hurled *upon* falsehood.**

**يَدْمَغُ — root د-م-غ.** **And the word is analysed here for the first time in this commentary.**

***Al-dimāgh* — the brain.** *Damaghahu* — **he struck him on the brain, broke the skull and reached the brain.** **The definition the lexicographers give is extremely narrow:** *damgh* is the blow's **passing through the bone and reaching the brain**; among the degrees of wounding that passed into Arab legal language, *al-dāmigha* is **the gravest head wound, the one that tears the membrane of the brain.**

**So the verb does not say "it defeats" or "it silences".** ***Yadmaghuhu* — "it shatters its brain".**

| Verb | Its concrete sense | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **نَقْذِف** | To hurl | A blow coming **from a distance and fast** |
| **يَدْمَغ** | A blow reaching the brain | **Lethal** — requiring no repetition |
| **زَاهِق** | The going out of the life | **The outcome** — nothing left standing |

**I do not soften the harshness of the word, because the verse does not soften it.** **And I record this as my own reading:** the three words together build **a scene of hunting or of battle.** Falsehood here is not an opinion under discussion but **a body that has been struck.**

**زَاهِق — root ز-ه-ق.** **This root too is analysed here for the first time in this commentary.**

***Zahaqa* — to perish, to go out and away.** And the primary use the lexicographers record: ***zahaqati'n-nafs* — the life went out.** The Qurʾān contains this use as well: *wa tazhaqa anfusuhum wa hum kāfirūn* (al-Tawba 9/55, 85).

**That is, *zāhiq* does not mean "false". *Zāhiq* is that whose life has gone out.**

***Fa-idhā huwa zāhiq*** — and here there is **the *idhā* of surprise**: "and at once". **The outcome comes without the process being told.**

**And an observation about the structure:** two of the three verbs are **imperfect** (*naqdhifu*, *yadmaghu*) — that is, they convey continuity; while the outcome is given **with a nominal sentence** (*huwa zāhiq*) — that is, fixity. **The throw is repeated; the outcome does not change.**

**وَلَكُمُ ٱلْوَيْلُ مِمَّا تَصِفُونَ** — the root و-ص-ف: to describe a thing, to frame an attribute of it.

**And this word is one of the frame-words of the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who describes |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | *mimmā **taṣifūn*** | The addressees — what they ascribe |
| **22** | *ʿammā **yaṣifūn*** | The same people, in the third person |
| **112** | *ʿalā mā **taṣifūn*** | **The last sentence of the sūra** |

**Three occurrences, at the beginning, the middle and the end of the sūra.** **This is a countable fact and will be tabulated at the end.**

## 21/19-20 — وَلَهُۥ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَنْ عِندَهُۥ لَا يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِهِۦ وَلَا يَسْتَحْسِرُونَ · يُسَبِّحُونَ ٱلَّيْلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ لَا يَفْتُرُونَ

*Wa lahu man fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ, wa man ʿindahu lā yastakbirūna ʿan ʿibādatihi wa lā yastaḥsirūn · Yusabbiḥūna'l-layla wa'n-nahāra lā yafturūn*

"**To Him belongs whoever is in the heavens and the earth. Those who are with Him are not too proud to serve Him, nor do they grow weary.** · **They glorify Him night and day, never pausing.**"

### Two negations, two different obstacles

**The verse removes separately the two obstacles that stand before service, and the two are of different kinds:**

| Verb | Root | The kind of obstacle |
|---|---|---|
| *lā **yastakbirūn*** | ك-ب-ر | **An obstacle of will** — pride, unwillingness |
| *lā **yastaḥsirūn*** | ح-س-ر | **An obstacle of strength** — tiring and giving up |

***Yastaḥsirūn* — Form X (*istifʿāl*), root ح-س-ر.** ***Ḥasara* — to uncover, to strip;** *ḥāsir* — bare-headed, without armour. **And from this arises the sense "one whose strength is laid bare, spent":** *ḥasīr* — exhausted.

**The root was treated at `067-mulk.md` on 67/4:** *yanqalib ilayka'l-baṣaru khāsiʾan wa huwa **ḥasīr*** — "the sight returns to you exhausted." **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the contrast between the two verses is worth recording:** in al-Mulk the **eye** that looks grows weary; in al-Anbiyāʾ those who are with Him **do not grow weary.** The same root, two opposite rulings. **I record this as an observation about the root.**

**لَا يَفْتُرُونَ — root ف-ت-ر:** **for the intensity of a work to slacken, to grow slack.** *Futūr* — a pause, a slackening of pace. **And the word differs from *lā yastaḥsirūn*:** one is **to be finished**, the other **to slow down.**

**So the verse makes three negations: no unwillingness, no exhaustion, no slackening.**

**يُسَبِّحُونَ ٱلَّيْلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ** — **the root س-ب-ح was analysed in detail at `037-saffat.md` on 37/143**, and it was recorded there that the root's original sense is **"to swim or glide, in water or in air, to flow along"**, while *tasbīḥ* is **to hold apart, to declare transcendent.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And there is a connection that must be recorded:** the same root will be used thirteen verses later, in the thirty-third verse, **of the heavenly bodies**: *kullun fī falakin **yasbaḥūn***. **In the same sūra the same root, once in Form II (*yusabbiḥūna*), once in Form I (*yasbaḥūna*).** This is a repetition of the root that can be verified within the sūra, and it will be taken up separately at the thirty-third verse.

## 21/21-22 — أَمِ ٱتَّخَذُوٓا۟ ءَالِهَةً مِّنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ هُمْ يُنشِرُونَ · لَوْ كَانَ فِيهِمَآ ءَالِهَةٌ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ لَفَسَدَتَا فَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَرْشِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ

*Ami'ttakhadhū ālihatan mina'l-arḍi hum yunshirūn · Law kāna fīhimā ālihatun illa'llāhu la-fasadatā, fa-subḥāna'llāhi rabbi'l-ʿarshi ʿammā yaṣifūn*

"**Or have they taken gods from the earth who raise the dead?** · **Were there in the two of them gods other than God, the two would have fallen into ruin. Glory be to God, Lord of the Throne, above what they ascribe.**"

### Where the proof is built: verse 21

**The proof of the twenty-second verse cannot be read on its own, because the question before it lays its ground.**

**The twenty-first verse asks about a function: *hum yunshirūn?* — "do they raise the dead?"** ***Yunshirūn* — root ن-ش-ر, Form IV: to bring the dead to life, to spread out, to unfold.** **And the note *mina'l-arḍ* is important:** the **source** of the gods taken up is stated — from the earth. **That is, the verse asks its question by recalling where the things worshipped came from.**

**The twenty-second verse then passes from function to *order*.** The division of labour between the two verses is this, and I give it as my own reading:

| Verse | What it asks / says | On what ground |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | Can they give life? | **Power** — being able to do a work |
| **22** | Were there two gods the order would break down | **Order** — the fitting together of things |

### The logic of the proof — the inference the verse itself builds

**And now I work out the verse's own word order step by step. I do not enter into the debate built upon this verse in the science of theology; what I shall do is follow the elements of the sentence itself.**

**The sentence consists of three parts:**

**1. The condition: *law kāna fīhimā ālihatun illa'llāh*.**

***Law*** in Arabic conveys a condition **not fulfilled** — "if it had been, but it was not." **That is, with its very first word the sentence marks the supposition it sets up as untrue.**

***Fīhimā* — "in the two of them": the pronoun refers to the heaven and the earth.** **And the heaven and the earth were named openly in the preceding verse** (*wa lahu man fi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*, 19). **So the referent of the pronoun is in the context.**

**2. The work of the particle of exception: *illa'llāh*.**

**And here there is a subtlety on which the grammarians dwell, because the sense of the sentence depends on it:**

***Illā* here is not an exception but an attribute** — it has the sense of *ghayr* (other than). **The evidence is this:** *ālihatun* is **indefinite**; in Arabic an *illā* coming after an indefinite mostly functions as an attribute. **That is, the sentence does not mean "gods, God excepted", but "gods other than God".**

**The difference is not trivial and must be recorded:**

| How *illā* is read | What the sentence says | The consequence |
|---|---|---|
| As **an exception** | "Had there been gods apart from God" | It is implied that God too is a member of that plurality |
| As **an attribute** (*ghayr*) | "Had there been gods other than God" | The plurality is rejected **entirely** |

**The grammarians hold the second reading necessary; the first ruins the proof the sentence builds.** **I transmit this as a datum of language.**

**3. The answer: *la-fasadatā*.**

***Fasadatā* — root ف-س-د, in the dual:** the heaven and the earth, the two together.

**And the sense of the word is decisive. *Fasād*, by the lexicographers' definition: a thing's departing from the state that is proper to it, the breaking down of its order.** Its opposite is *ṣalāḥ*. **The word does not say "to cease to exist" — it says *to break down*.**

**This determines what the proof is built upon, and I record it as my own reading:** the verse does not say "had there been two gods the universe would not exist". **It says "it would have broken down".** **That is, the ground of the proof is not existence but working.**

### The unspoken leg of the inference

**And now the most striking side of the verse: the sentence does not complete its inference.**

**What is set up is this:** *if A were so, B would follow.* **What is not said is this:** *B is not so.* **And the conclusion that would follow from the unsaid:** *neither is A.*

**The verse does not give the second premise.** After saying *la-fasadatā* it passes straight to glorification.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the incompleteness of the sentence:** the second premise **has been left to the eye of the one addressed.** It is not the verse that says the heaven and the earth have not broken down, but **the one who looks.** The proof is, for exactly this reason, not a sentence of debate but **a call to look** — and the sūra will do this openly three verses later: *awalam yara'lladhīna kafarū* (30).

**And a second observation:** the verse does not say **why** it would break down either. No chain of causes is set up. **The only thing the sentence gives is that plurality and order cannot stand together.**

### فَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَرْشِ

**And the attribute chosen at the close corresponds exactly to the subject of the proof — I record this as my own reading.**

***Rabbu'l-ʿarsh*** — and **the Throne** is the word that appears in the Qurʾān where **rule and governance** are named. **The verse names God not as *al-Khāliq* (the Creator) or *al-Qādir* (the Powerful) but as the Lord of the Throne.**

**The proof is a proof from *order*; and the closing attribute conveys *governance*.** **That is, the sentence seals its own subject with its own attribute.**

**عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ** — and the word of the eighteenth verse returns. **The second link.**

**One further note:** in the classical works of theology there is a construction of proof developed from this verse and known by a technical name. **This commentary does not enter into that construction** — because the inference the verse itself builds is the three steps worked out above, and those steps require no apparatus of theology.

## 21/23 — لَا يُسْـَٔلُ عَمَّا يَفْعَلُ وَهُمْ يُسْـَٔلُونَ

*Lā yusʾalu ʿammā yafʿalu wa hum yusʾalūn*

"**He is not questioned about what He does; they are questioned.**"

### The pattern of the two verbs — what the word order says

**And this verse must be read by its word order alone. First I record the form of the verbs, because most of the debate begins and ends here.**

**Both verbs are from the root س-أ-ل and both are passive:**

| Clause | Verb | Aspect | Is its subject stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Lā **yusʾalu** ʿammā yafʿal* | Imperfect, **passive**, negative | Continuous | **No** |
| *Wa hum **yusʾalūn*** | Imperfect, **passive**, affirmative | Continuous | **No** |

**This is the most easily missed side of the verse and must be recorded:** the sentence does not say **"He questions them".** **On neither side is the questioning party put into words.** What is said is only **who is the one questioned.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being that both verbs come in the passive:** the verse does not set up a **scene of interrogation.** **It separates two positions from one another.**

### Where the sentence stands in the context

**And the verse does not stand alone. The preceding sentence had ended with *rabbu'l-ʿarsh*.**

**I record as my own reading, and its ground is this:** the twenty-second verse had established that there is **a single centre of governance.** The twenty-third says that **above that centre there is no further authority.** **That is, the sentence is not a declaration of privilege but a note about a *chain*:** the chain of reckoning stops somewhere, and where it stops is not the place of those questioned but of the one not questioned.

**And there is disagreement about to whom the pronoun *hum* refers:**

| View | Who *hum* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Those taken as gods in the twenty-first verse** — it is they who will be held answerable |
| 2 | **Human beings** — the community addressed |
| 3 | **All created things** — angels included |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **I enter only a note about word order: those named with a plural pronoun in the preceding verse were the ones who say *yaṣifūn*; that is the nearest referent.**

### What the verse does not say

**I draw a boundary here per the method (`STYLE.md`), because this verse has often been used torn from its context.**

**The verse does not say that no reason is given.** **The evidence for this is the sūra itself, and it is read wholly from the text:**

| Verse | What it does |
|---|---|
| **16-17** | Explains **why** the creating is not play |
| **31** | Says **why** the mountains were set: *an tamīda bihim* |
| **35** | Says **why** the testing is: *fitna* |
| **107** | Says **why** the messengership is: *raḥmatan li'l-ʿālamīn* |

**The same sūra gives a ground in four separate places.** **That is, the twenty-third verse does not say "there is no ground";** it says **that there is no authority from which a ground may be extracted on demand.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is those four verses themselves.**

**And the verse has one further link within the sūra:** in the thirteenth verse those destroyed were told, by way of mockery, *laʿallakum **tusʾalūn***. **The same root, the same passive pattern.** **The sūra uses the verb "to be questioned" first in a scene and then in a ruling.**

## 21/24-25 — أَمِ ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ ءَالِهَةً قُلْ هَاتُوا۟ بُرْهَٰنَكُمْ هَٰذَا ذِكْرُ مَن مَّعِىَ وَذِكْرُ مَن قَبْلِى · وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ مِن رَّسُولٍ إِلَّا نُوحِىٓ إِلَيْهِ أَنَّهُۥ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنَا۠ فَٱعْبُدُونِ

*Ami'ttakhadhū min dūnihi āliha, qul hātū burhānakum, hādhā dhikru man maʿiya wa dhikru man qablī, bal aktharuhum lā yaʿlamūna'l-ḥaqqa fa-hum muʿriḍūn · Wa mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin illā nūḥī ilayhi annahu lā ilāha illā ana fa'budūn*

"**Or have they taken gods besides Him? Say: 'Bring your proof! This is the remembrance of those with me and the remembrance of those before me.'** No — most of them do not know the truth, and so they turn away. · **We sent no messenger before you but that We revealed to him: there is no god but I, so serve Me.**"

### هَاتُوا۟ بُرْهَٰنَكُمْ — the kind of demand

***Hātū* — a verbal noun used as a verb, in the sense of "bring".** ***Burhān* — a decisive, indisputable proof.** The lexicographers relate the word to *bariha* (to shine, to be evident); **a *burhān* is a proof that does not leave one in the dark.**

**And what must be recorded is that the demand is turned back on the other side.** **In the fifth verse the addressees had said *fa'l-yaʾtinā bi-āya* — "let him bring us a sign".** **Here the same structure is reversed: *hātū burhānakum*.**

| Verse | Who demands | What is demanded |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | The addressees | *bi-āya* — **a visible sign** |
| **24** | The text | *burhānakum* — **a proof** |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the shared pattern of the two verses:** one asks for a thing to be **shown**, the other for a thing to be **argued.** **The two demands are not of the same kind.**

**هَٰذَا ذِكْرُ مَن مَّعِىَ وَذِكْرُ مَن قَبْلِى** — and what is offered as proof is **a text.** The fourth link of the root *dhikr* in the sūra is here.

**There is disagreement over how the expression is to be understood:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | "This Qurʾān is the book of those with me; and the earlier books, of those before me" — **two books** |
| 2 | "This is the remembrance in which both those with me and those before me are named" — **one book, two contents** |
| 3 | *Dhikr* here means **admonition**: "the admonition given both to my contemporaries and to those before" |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ ٱلْحَقَّ فَهُم مُّعْرِضُونَ** — **and *muʿriḍūn* for the second time in the sūra.** In the first verse it was tied to *ghafla*; here it is tied to **not knowing**: *lā yaʿlamūna'l-ḥaqqa **fa**-hum muʿriḍūn.* **The *fāʾ* conveys cause.**

### 21/25 — the summary of the series, before the series begins

**And the twenty-fifth verse gives in advance the verdict of the series of prophets that will begin in the forty-eighth verse of the sūra.**

***Wa mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin illā nūḥī ilayhi…*** — **it is exactly the pattern of the seventh verse:** *wa mā arsalnā qablaka illā rijālan nūḥī ilayhim.*

| Verse | The shared pattern | The emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *wa mā arsalnā … illā … nūḥī* | The **kind** of the messengers: they are human |
| **25** | *wa mā arsalnā … illā nūḥī* | The **content** of the revelation: one god |

**The same pattern fills two different gaps.** **This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

**And a point of word order: the sentence ends with *fa'budūn* — a plural imperative.** Yet the one revealed to is a single messenger (*ilayhi*). **That is, the address passes over to the one addressed inside the reported speech.** **The same shift will be made once more in the ninety-second verse:** *wa anā rabbukum **fa'budūn***. **The two verses end with the same word, and the whole series of prophets stands between them.**

## 21/26-29 — وَقَالُوا۟ ٱتَّخَذَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ وَلَدًا سُبْحَٰنَهُۥ بَلْ عِبَادٌ مُّكْرَمُونَ · لَا يَسْبِقُونَهُۥ بِٱلْقَوْلِ وَهُم بِأَمْرِهِۦ يَعْمَلُونَ · يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ وَلَا يَشْفَعُونَ إِلَّا لِمَنِ ٱرْتَضَىٰ وَهُم مِّنْ خَشْيَتِهِۦ مُشْفِقُونَ · وَمَن يَقُلْ مِنْهُمْ إِنِّىٓ إِلَٰهٌ مِّن دُونِهِۦ فَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِيهِ جَهَنَّمَ كَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِى ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ

*Wa qālu'ttakhadha'r-Raḥmānu waladan subḥānahu bal ʿibādun mukramūn · Lā yasbiqūnahu bi'l-qawli wa hum bi-amrihi yaʿmalūn · Yaʿlamu mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum wa lā yashfaʿūna illā li-mani'rtaḍā wa hum min khashyatihi mushfiqūn · Wa man yaqul minhum innī ilāhun min dūnihi fa-dhālika najzīhi jahannam, kadhālika najzi'ẓ-ẓālimīn*

"**They said: 'The Merciful has taken a child!' Glory be to Him. No — they are honoured servants.** · **They do not go before Him in speech; they act by His command.** · **He knows what is before them and what is behind them, and they do not intercede except for one with whom He is pleased, and they tremble in awe of Him.** · **Whoever of them says 'I am a god besides Him' — him We recompense with Hell. Thus do We recompense the wrongdoers.**"

### بَلْ عِبَادٌ مُّكْرَمُونَ — the changing of the word

**And the technique of the answer must be recorded: the claim says *walad* (child); the answer says *ʿibād* (servants).**

**The two words are not two names for the same relation — they are of different kinds:**

| What is claimed | What is put in the answer | The difference |
|---|---|---|
| **وَلَد** — a child | **عِبَاد** — servants | **Servitude** in place of **descent** |
| Requires being of the same kind | Requires a difference of kind | — |

**And the attribute *mukramūn* is added: "honoured".** **That is, the answer does not lower the value** — **it changes the source of the value.** Honour is bestowed; descent is shared.

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the kind of the two words.**

**This debate was treated in detail at `037-saffat.md` on 37/149-160** (the question of daughters and sons, and *ʿibādu'llāhi'l-mukhlaṣīn*). **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**ٱلرَّحْمَٰن** — and the choice of the name is worth recording. **The same technique was recorded at `026-suara.md` on 26/5:** *al-Raḥmān* is set over against the turning away. **So it is here too: the claim is directed at the widest name of mercy.**

**And the name occurs four times in the sūra: 26, 36, 42, 112.** **The last verse of the sūra also ends with this name: *wa rabbuna'r-Raḥmānu'l-mustaʿān*.** **This is a countable fact.**

### لَا يَسْبِقُونَهُۥ بِٱلْقَوْلِ — the order of speaking

***Yasbiqūnahu bi'l-qawl*** — "they do not go before Him in speech". **The root س-ب-ق: to get ahead in a race.**

**And a point of word order: the boundary is set not by way of *obedience* but by way of speech.** First not speaking, then acting by command. **That is, the description puts the mouth on record before conduct.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure.**

### وَلَا يَشْفَعُونَ إِلَّا لِمَنِ ٱرْتَضَىٰ

**Intercession is bound to a condition of leave. The same note was treated at `053-necm.md` on 53/26:** *wa kam min malakin fi's-samāwāti lā tughnī shafāʿatuhum shayʾan illā min baʿdi an yaʾdhana'llāhu li-man yashāʾu wa yarḍā.* **I rely on that treatment.**

**The element the two verses share is in the root ر-ض-و:** in al-Najm *yarḍā*, here *irtaḍā* (Form VIII). **That is, the condition is sought not in the intercessor but in the one interceded for.** This is a note about word order.

**وَهُم مِّنْ خَشْيَتِهِۦ مُشْفِقُونَ** — **and two words for fear stand together:**

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **خَشْيَة** | خ-ش-ي | **The fear of reverence** felt through knowing the greatness |
| **مُشْفِق** | ش-ف-ق | **A trembling wariness** — *shafaq*, the thinned redness between day and night |

***Shafaq*, as the lexicographers record, is the thin and transitional;** *ishfāq* takes from this the sense **"to fear that a thing will thin out and snap".** **That is, the second word, unlike the first, carries a picture of *thinness*.**

**And the word occurs twice in the sūra: 28 (of the angels) and 49 (*wa hum mina's-sāʿati mushfiqūn* — of those who guard themselves).** **The same attribute, two different companies.** It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

### 21/29 — the hypothetical force of the condition

**The verse is a conditional sentence: *wa man yaqul minhum…*** — "whoever of them says". **The particle of condition is *man* and the verb is in the jussive.**

**And what is to be recorded is this: a conditional sentence does not report that the event has occurred.** **That is, the verse does not say that any of the angels said this;** it says that the rule covers everyone.

**I record this as an observation about the structure.** And the scope of the ruling is widened by the last sentence: *kadhālika najzi'ẓ-ẓālimīn* — **the punishment is attached not to a name but to an attribute.**

## 21/30 — أَوَلَمْ يَرَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ أَنَّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ كَانَتَا رَتْقًا فَفَتَقْنَٰهُمَا وَجَعَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلْمَآءِ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ حَىٍّ أَفَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ

*Awalam yara'lladhīna kafarū anna's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa kānatā ratqan fa-fataqnāhumā, wa jaʿalnā mina'l-māʾi kulla shayʾin ḥayy, a-fa-lā yuʾminūn*

"**Have those who deny not seen that the heavens and the earth were sewn together, and We tore them apart? And We made every living thing from water. Will they still not believe?**"

### First the boundary — per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**This verse is among the Qurʾānic verses most forced in the modern period. So I draw the boundary first and then pass to the words.**

**The same boundary was drawn at `041-fussilet.md` on 41/9-12, and I rely on that treatment.** The note there:

> **"I do not dress the verses in modern cosmology; I neither derive a model of the universe from the word 'smoke' nor equate the days with geological ages. Such an equation would be making the text say what it does not say."**

**I do the same here:** from this verse I derive **no model of the universe, no cosmogony and no biology.** I give my reasons below under a separate heading; first the text.

### رَتْق and فَتْق — the lexical sense of the two words

**And the two words are exact opposites of one another. The lexicographers define them as a pair: *al-ratqu ḍiddu'l-fatq*.**

| Word | Root | Lexical sense | Concrete picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **رَتْق** | ر-ت-ق | **To be joined, to be closed, to have no opening between** | *Rataqa'th-thawb* — **to sew up a tear and close it** |
| **فَتْق** | ف-ت-ق | **To split apart, to unpick a seam, to open** | *Fataqa'th-thawb* — **to unpick the seam, to separate the cloth** |

**The field shared by the two words is stitching.** *Ratq* is to join two pieces and leave no opening between them; *fatq* is **to unpick and part** what has been joined. **The Turkish word *fıtık* ("hernia") comes from this root (*fatq*) as well: the opening of the abdominal wall.**

**And both words occur only in this verse in the Qurʾān.** I give this as a note about the words.

### رَتْقًا — why singular?

**A point of grammar must be recorded:** the subject is dual (*al-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa … kānatā*) but the predicate is singular: *ratqan*, not *ratqayni*.

**The grammarians' explanation:** *ratq* here is a **verbal noun**, and a verbal noun used as an attribute **is not made dual or plural.** A construction like "the two of them were a being-joined".

**And the verb that follows is dual:** *fa-fataqnāhumā* — "We tore the two apart". **That is, the sentence counts one while they are joined and two after they are parted.** This is an observation about the structure.

### Four roots that convey splitting — set together

**When the root *shaqq* was treated at `054-kamer.md` on 54/1, a threefold distinction was set up, and it was recorded there:**

> **"*Falq* opens what is closed; *faṭr* splits from nothing and establishes; *shaqq* divides what has been established."**

**I rely on that treatment and add this verse's word to that list. All four are roots that occur in the Qurʾān:**

| Root | What it does | Its opposite |
|---|---|---|
| **ف-ل-ق** | Opens what is closed (a seed, the daybreak) | — |
| **ف-ط-ر** | Splits from nothing and establishes | — |
| **ش-ق-ق** | Divides what has been established | — |
| **ف-ت-ق** | **Unpicks the seam** | **ر-ت-ق** — sews / closes |

**And this is what sets the fourth apart: only *fatq* has an opposite, and that opposite occurs in the same verse.** **That is, the verse names both states: the former state and the latter.**

**One further note:** in the fifty-sixth verse of this same sūra ***faṭarahunna*** will occur in Abraham's mouth. **Two roots in the same sūra on the same subject — one conveying the establishing, the other the parting.** This is a fact that can be verified within the sūra.

### The explanations in the classical commentaries

**The classical commentaries differ on what the verse tells, and the disagreement is real. I give the principal explanations in a table.**

| # | Explanation | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The sky did not send rain and the earth did not bring forth plants; then the sky was "split" with rain and the earth with vegetation.** | The rest of the verse names water (*wa jaʿalnā mina'l-māʾi kulla shayʾin ḥayy*); and the verb *awalam yara* points to something that can be seen |
| **2** | **The heaven and the earth were joined together, and then they were parted.** | That the pronouns *fīhimā*/*humā* are dual; the sense of "joinedness" that first comes to mind |
| **3** | **The heavens were a single whole and were divided into seven heavens; the earth too was a single whole and was divided.** | That the heavens are named as seven elsewhere in the Qurʾān |

**All three explanations are transmitted in the classical exegetical tradition. I do not impose a preference.**

**I enter only a note belonging to the context — and it is not a preference:** the second half of the verse speaks of water and the verb is *awalam yara* (have they not seen). **The first explanation connects directly with these two elements.** **I give this as a note about the context; I do not use it to eliminate the other two explanations.**

### Modern correspondences — and why I reject them

**In the modern period this verse has been matched with physical models of the beginning of the universe. I do not make that match in this commentary, and I write my reasons openly, because an unreasoned rejection is itself an imposition.**

**1. The verb asks the addressee for a *seeing*.** *Awalam yara* — "have they not seen?" **The addressee is the person listening to this verse in the seventh century.** What is asked of him can only be something **open to him.** A model of the universe is not open to that addressee; rain splitting the soil is.

**2. The words are words of stitching, not words of physics.** *Ratq* and *fatq* speak in Arabic with the language of **cloth and hide.** The concrete picture of a word shows what it is used for.

**3. The match makes the verse's truth depend on a model.** Models in physics change. **If you tie the verse to a model, the verse is shaken when the model changes.** This is no kindness to the verse.

**4. The verse's own aim is not a piece of knowledge but a stance.** The sentence does not end with *a-fa-lā yaʿlamūn* (do they not know) — it ends with ***a-fa-lā yuʾminūn*** (do they not believe). **What is asked is not the assent to a fact but a trust.**

**These are my reasons, and I record them as my own reading.** **I issue no ruling about what modern science says; the ruling I issue is that the verse does not speak in that field.**

### وَجَعَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلْمَآءِ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ حَىٍّ

**There are two readings of the particle *min* in the sentence:**

| Reading | What *min* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inceptive** (of beginning) | "We made every living thing **from** water" — it conveys a source |
| 2 | **Partitive** | "We made every living thing from a part of water" |

**And disagreement is transmitted about the scope of the term *kulla shayʾin ḥayy* as well:** whether the expression covers **all living things** or **a particular class** has been disputed. **I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And here too I repeat the boundary:** **from this sentence I derive no biology, no cell chemistry and no definition of life.** What the verse gives is a **relation**: the dependence of the living upon water. **For an addressee living in the desert this is the plainest observation there is, and that observation is what the verse asks for.**

**أَفَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ** — and the block continues with a call to faith.

## 21/31-32 — وَجَعَلْنَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ رَوَٰسِىَ أَن تَمِيدَ بِهِمْ وَجَعَلْنَا فِيهَا فِجَاجًا سُبُلًا لَّعَلَّهُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · وَجَعَلْنَا ٱلسَّمَآءَ سَقْفًا مَّحْفُوظًا وَهُمْ عَنْ ءَايَٰتِهَا مُعْرِضُونَ

*Wa jaʿalnā fi'l-arḍi rawāsiya an tamīda bihim wa jaʿalnā fīhā fijājan subulan laʿallahum yahtadūn · Wa jaʿalna's-samāʾa saqfan maḥfūẓā, wa hum ʿan āyātihā muʿriḍūn*

"**And We set firm mountains in the earth, lest it sway with them, and made in it broad passes for roads, that they might find their way.** · **And We made the sky a guarded roof. Yet they turn away from its signs.**"

### رَوَٰسِىَ — root ر-س-و

**The root was analysed in detail at `050-kaf.md` on 50/7, and the same expression (*rawāsiya an tamīda bikum*) was treated at `031-lokman.md` on 31/10. I rely on those treatments and do not repeat them.**

**A summary of what was recorded at Qāf:**

> **"The word is a plural active participle: *rawāsī* — 'those that have cast anchor', 'those that stand fast'. That is, the mountain is named not by its name but by its state. […] This is an image; it is not a geological claim."**

**And the root *mayd* (م-ي-د) is treated at `016-nahl.md`, 16/15; I rely on that.**

**That note holds here too and must be repeated: I do not match the verse with modern explanations of the role of mountains in the earth's crust.**

**Here I add two differences peculiar to al-Anbiyāʾ, and both are data of wording:**

| | **Qāf 50/7** | **Luqmān 31/10** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/31** |
|---|---|---|---|
| The verb | *al**qaynā** fīhā* — We cast | *al**qā** fi'l-arḍ* — He cast | *ja**ʿalnā** fi'l-arḍ* — We set |
| The ground | **Not given** | *an tamīda **bikum*** — lest it sway with you | *an tamīda **bihim*** — lest it sway with them |
| What follows | *wa anbatnā fīhā min kulli zawjin bahīj* | *wa baththa fīhā min kulli dābba* | *wa jaʿalnā fīhā fijājan subulā* |

**Two differences must be recorded.** **The first:** Luqmān says *bikum* (with you), al-Anbiyāʾ *bihim* (with them) — **a shift from direct address to the third person.** In al-Anbiyāʾ the addressees had been named a verse earlier in the third person as *alladhīna kafarū*; the pronoun follows that.

**The second, and more important:** in Qāf and Luqmān **plants and living creatures** are named after the mountains; **in al-Anbiyāʾ a *road* is named.** *Fijājan subulā.*

***Fijāj* — the root ف-ج-ج: a broad pass between two mountains.** *Fajj* — a cleft, a pass; its plural *fijāj*. **And *subul* (roads) is its attribute or its appositive.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the arrangement of the three sūras:** in al-Anbiyāʾ the mountain is named first as **what makes firm** and then as **what gives passage.** **That is, the same thing provides both the standing and the walking.** And the clause of purpose confirms it: *laʿallahum **yahtadūn*** — that they might find their way.

### سَقْفًا مَّحْفُوظًا — a roof

***Saqf* — a roof, a ceiling.** **And the choice of the word is worth recording: the sky is named as an element of a *building*.** In the preceding verse the earth was named as ground walked upon; here the sky becomes a roof. **The two verses together build the picture of a dwelling.**

***Maḥfūẓ* — root ح-ف-ظ, a passive participle: guarded.** **From what it is guarded is not said**, and several explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries (guarded from falling / closed to entry from outside / guarded from decay). **The verse does not specify; nor do I.**

**وَهُمْ عَنْ ءَايَٰتِهَا مُعْرِضُونَ** — **and *muʿriḍūn* for the third time in the sūra.** **And there is a fine point of word order here:** what is turned away from is not the sky itself but ***āyātihā*** — "its signs". **That is, the sky is seen; it is not read as a sign.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the pronoun.**

## 21/33 — وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلَّيْلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ وَٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ كُلٌّ فِى فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ

*Wa huwa'lladhī khalaqa'l-layla wa'n-nahāra wa'sh-shamsa wa'l-qamar, kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn*

"**It is He who created the night and the day, the sun and the moon. Each swims in an orbit.**"

### Again a boundary

**This verse too has been matched in the modern period with celestial mechanics. The boundary I drew at the thirtieth verse holds here as well, and I repeat it:** **I derive from the verse no shape of orbit, no axis of rotation and no law of motion.** **What I shall do is give the lexical sense of two words and keep within the classical explanations.**

### فَلَك — the root of the word

***Falak* — root ف-ل-ك.** **The core sense the lexicographers give: *roundness, turning*.**

**And the kindred words of the root confirm this picture:**

| Word | Its sense |
|---|---|
| **فَلْكَة** | **The round weight at the head of a spindle** — the piece that turns as one spins |
| **فَلَّكَ** | To become round |
| **فُلْك** | **A ship** — the same three letters |

**And the last row is worth recording:** *fulk* (ship) is made of the same letters as *falak*. **The lexicographers have drawn a connection between the two:** both are things that **run along in their own channel.** **I give this as a note about the root, not as a claim about meaning.**

**Disagreement is transmitted in the classical commentaries about what a *falak* is:**

| View | What *falak* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The channel or path within which the heavenly body turns** |
| 2 | **The layer in which the heavenly body is set** |
| 3 | The word is an attribute conveying turning; it is not the name of a particular body |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **And I do not equate the verse with the astronomical conceptions of the classical period, just as I do not equate it with modern models.**

### يَسْبَحُونَ — root س-ب-ح

**The root was analysed in detail at `037-saffat.md` on 37/143, and precisely this verse was cited there as evidence.** The note there:

> **"The root's original sense: *to swim or glide, in water or in air, to flow along.* […] Its connection with the sense of swimming: what swims moves off from where it is."**

**I rely on that treatment. And here the root's *original* sense is at work: *sabḥ* — to swim.**

**The description the lexicographers give is this:** *sabḥ* is **to move forward in water or in air, by one's own power, without meeting resistance.** **That is, the word conveys two things: motion and freedom.**

**I give this as a note about the word and build no claim of physics upon it.**

### يَسْبَحُونَ — the plural of rational beings

**And the most striking grammatical point of the verse is here.**

**The verb is *yasbaḥūna* — with *wāw-nūn*, that is, the plural used of rational beings (those possessed of intellect).** Yet the subjects are the sun, the moon, the night and the day.

**The grammarians' explanation:** when things without intellect are told as though doing something belonging to those with intellect, they receive **the treatment of rational beings.** There are several instances of this in the Qurʾān.

**And this builds a line with the nineteenth and twentieth verses of the sūra — I record this as my own reading:**

| Verse | Subject | Verb | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| **20** | *man ʿindahu* — those who are with Him | *yusabbiḥūna* | **Form II** — glorification |
| **33** | The sun, the moon, the night, the day | *yasbaḥūna* | **Form I** — to swim |

**The same root, thirteen verses apart, in two different forms and for two different subjects.** **And in both the plural of rational beings is used.** This is a fact that can be verified within the sūra.

**كُلٌّ** — it carries nunation, and the grammarians count this as having **its second term of annexation elided**: *kulluhum* — "each of them". **And there is disagreement over whom *each* covers:**

| View | Scope |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Only the sun and the moon** — the night and the day do not "swim" |
| 2 | **All four** — the verse says *kull* after naming all four |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **And the block closes here.**

## 21/34-35 — وَمَا جَعَلْنَا لِبَشَرٍ مِّن قَبْلِكَ ٱلْخُلْدَ أَفَإِي۟ن مِّتَّ فَهُمُ ٱلْخَٰلِدُونَ · كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَآئِقَةُ ٱلْمَوْتِ وَنَبْلُوكُم بِٱلشَّرِّ وَٱلْخَيْرِ فِتْنَةً وَإِلَيْنَا تُرْجَعُونَ

*Wa mā jaʿalnā li-basharin min qablika'l-khuld, a-fa-in mitta fa-humu'l-khālidūn · Kullu nafsin dhāʾiqatu'l-mawt, wa nablūkum bi'sh-sharri wa'l-khayri fitnatan wa ilaynā turjaʿūn*

"**We gave no human being before you deathlessness. If you die, then, will they live on?** · **Every soul will taste death. We try you with evil and with good, as a testing. And to Us you will be returned.**"

### The logic of the question

**The thirty-fourth verse sets up an analogy, and the analogy turns the addressee's own expectation back upon him.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the structure of the question:** the addressees were waiting for the Prophet's death. The verse does not reject that expectation — **it asks about its consequence:** *a-fa-in mitta fa-humu'l-khālidūn?* **"If you die, will they live on?"**

**That is, the sentence puts the holder of the expectation under the same ruling.** **Even if the thing expected were true, it gains the one who expects it nothing.**

**ٱلْخُلْد** — root **خ-ل-د**: to remain long, to be lasting. **And the word occurred in the eighth verse of the sūra:** *wa mā kānū **khālidīn***. **The two verses repeat the same ruling, one of the past messengers and one of all human beings.** It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

### كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَآئِقَةُ ٱلْمَوْتِ

**This sentence was treated at `029-ankebut.md` on 29/57, and its connection with the following verse was recorded there.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The note at al-ʿAnkabūt was this:**

> **"Read together, the two verses enter, immediately after the call to migrate, the note that one will die in any case. That is, changing one's place is not fleeing death — it is choosing in what state one dies."**

**And the difference between the two sūras is in what is set beside the sentence:**

| | **al-ʿAnkabūt 29/56-57** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/34-35** |
|---|---|---|
| What precedes | *inna arḍī wāsiʿa* — **a call to migrate** | *mā jaʿalnā li-basharin … al-khuld* — **no deathlessness** |
| The sentence | *kullu nafsin dhāʾiqatu'l-mawt* | *kullu nafsin dhāʾiqatu'l-mawt* |
| What follows | *thumma ilaynā turjaʿūn* | ***wa nablūkum bi'sh-sharri wa'l-khayri fitnatan*** and *ilaynā turjaʿūn* |

**What al-Anbiyāʾ adds is the middle sentence, and it is treated here for the first time in this commentary.**

**ذَآئِقَة** — an active participle, feminine. **Root ذ-و-ق: to taste.** **And the choice of the verb is worth recording:** death is given not as something **met** but as something **tasted.** *Dhawq* is the nearest and most personal of the senses. **That is, the sentence names death not as an event coming from outside but as an experience passed through from within.**

### وَنَبْلُوكُم بِٱلشَّرِّ وَٱلْخَيْرِ فِتْنَةً — a testing from two directions

**And this is the real thing al-Anbiyāʾ adds.**

**The root ب-ل-و:** to test a thing by wearing it down; *balā* — to wear out, to fray. **Testing is, by its root, a bringing out by abrasion.**

**The root ف-ت-ن was treated in detail at `029-ankebut.md` on 29/2-10** (melting gold in fire to test it), and at `025-furkan.md` on 25/20 it was recorded that the word comes **by the hand of people.** **I rely on those treatments.**

***Fitnatan* is in the accusative, and the grammarians count it either an object of purpose (*mafʿūl lah*) or an emphatic verbal noun:** "as a testing" or "by way of testing".

**And there is a point of word order in the sequence: *bi'sh-sharri wa'l-khayr* — evil first, then good.**

**In Arabic the expected order is generally that the good is put first.** **Here it is reversed.** **I record this as an observation about the structure and build no ruling upon it;** I add only this: **what precedes the sentence is death** — that is, the side of evil is already under discussion; the order fits the context.

**Now I come to the pair itself. And this is the place in the verse that most needs to be dwelt on.**

**The sentence counts two things at once as a testing:**

| By what | How the testing works |
|---|---|
| **بِٱلشَّرّ** | Want, sickness, loss — **endurance** is measured |
| **بِٱلْخَيْر** | Health, means, power — **conduct** is measured |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being that the same verb (*nablū*) is shared by both prepositional phrases:** the verse sets up no difference of degree between the two states. **Both are the object of the same verb.**

**And what this requires must be written out:** if evil alone were a testing, good would be a **reward** and would convey a ruling about the one who has it. **The verse blocks this.** What has been given is as **open-ended** as what has not.

**The sūra itself applies this in two places, and both are in the series of prophets:**

| Verse | Who | Tested with what |
|---|---|---|
| **83** | Job | *massaniya'ḍ-ḍurr* — **with evil** |
| **81-82** | Solomon | The wind and what was put at his command — **with good** |

**And in the series both are named in the same way: both end in deliverance.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the standing of the two stories side by side in the sūra.**

**And one further link within the sūra: the same word will occur once more in the hundred and eleventh verse** — *wa in adrī laʿallahu **fitnatun** lakum wa matāʿun ilā ḥīn.* **The sūra names the notion of testing both in its middle and at its end.**

## 21/36-37 — وَإِذَا رَءَاكَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ إِن يَتَّخِذُونَكَ إِلَّا هُزُوًا أَهَٰذَا ٱلَّذِى يَذْكُرُ ءَالِهَتَكُمْ وَهُم بِذِكْرِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ هُمْ كَٰفِرُونَ · خُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ مِنْ عَجَلٍ سَأُو۟رِيكُمْ ءَايَٰتِى فَلَا تَسْتَعْجِلُونِ

*Wa idhā raʾāka'lladhīna kafarū in yattakhidhūnaka illā huzuwā, a-hādha'lladhī yadhkuru ālihatakum, wa hum bi-dhikri'r-Raḥmāni hum kāfirūn · Khuliqa'l-insānu min ʿajal, sa-urīkum āyātī fa-lā tastaʿjilūn*

"**When those who deny see you, they take you only in mockery: 'Is this the one who talks about your gods?' — while it is they who deny the remembrance of the Merciful.** · **The human being was created of haste. I shall show you My signs, so do not ask Me to hasten.**"

### Two *dhikr*s in the same verse

**And the word order of the verse shows how a single word is used on the two sides:**

| Expression | Who does it | In what sense *dhikr* is |
|---|---|---|
| *yadhkuru ālihatakum* | **The Prophet** | **To mention, to talk about** — their complaint |
| *bi-dhikri'r-Raḥmān* | **They** (they deny it) | **Revelation / remembrance** |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being that the root occurs twice in the verse:** the verse meets those who complain **with their own complaint.** They are troubled that someone **mentions** their gods; they themselves reject the **mentioning** of the Merciful. **The same verb, in two directions.**

**وَهُم بِذِكْرِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ هُمْ كَٰفِرُونَ** — and the pronoun *hum* comes **twice**. The grammarians count the second pronoun either **emphasis** or **a pronoun of separation**; on either account it conveys restriction (they alone).

### خُلِقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ مِنْ عَجَلٍ

**There is disagreement about the sentence, and the disagreement is real:**

| View | What *min ʿajal* means | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hyperbole/figure:** the human being is as though created out of haste; haste is in his very make | Usages of the type "so-and-so is created out of generosity" are common in Arabic |
| 2 | *ʿAjal* here means **clay/*ṭīn*** | A lexical usage transmitted by some lexicographers |
| 3 | There is an inversion: "haste was created from the human being" | The explanation of some of the grammarians |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **And I give as a note that the context supports the first reading:** the rest of the verse says *fa-lā tastaʿjilūn* — "do not ask to hasten". **The same root, in the same verse, as an imperative.**

**And this sentence is on the same pattern as the sentence treated at `070-mearic.md` on 70/19:** *inna'l-insāna khuliqa **halūʿā*** — "the human being was created fretful". **I rely on that treatment.**

| | **al-Maʿārij 70/19** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/37** |
|---|---|---|
| The pattern | *khuliqa'l-insānu …* | *khuliqa'l-insānu …* |
| The quality | *halūʿan* — **a circumstantial qualifier** (accusative adjective) | *min ʿajalin* — **a prepositional phrase** (conveying a source) |
| What follows | Two states are enumerated (*jazūʿan*, *manūʿan*) | A command comes (*fa-lā tastaʿjilūn*) |

**Two verses describe a quality of the human being with the same pattern; one opens it out, the other sets a limit to it.** **I give this as a comparison of wording.**

**سَأُو۟رِيكُمْ ءَايَٰتِى** — **س** (*sīn*) conveys the near future. **This note was set down at `026-suara.md` on 26/6** (*sīn* the near, *sawfa* the distant future). **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the verse's answer connects with the fifth verse:** there it had said *fa'l-yaʾtinā bi-āya*. **Here the answer is given: the signs will come — but the one who asks does not set the time.**

## 21/38-41 — وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ · لَوْ يَعْلَمُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ حِينَ لَا يَكُفُّونَ عَن وُجُوهِهِمُ ٱلنَّارَ وَلَا عَن ظُهُورِهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · بَلْ تَأْتِيهِم بَغْتَةً فَتَبْهَتُهُمْ فَلَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ رَدَّهَا وَلَا هُمْ يُنظَرُونَ · وَلَقَدِ ٱسْتُهْزِئَ بِرُسُلٍ مِّن قَبْلِكَ فَحَاقَ بِٱلَّذِينَ سَخِرُوا۟ مِنْهُم مَّا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ

*Wa yaqūlūna matā hādha'l-waʿdu in kuntum ṣādiqīn · Law yaʿlamu'lladhīna kafarū ḥīna lā yakuffūna ʿan wujūhihimu'n-nāra wa lā ʿan ẓuhūrihim wa lā hum yunṣarūn · Bal taʾtīhim baghtatan fa-tabhatuhum fa-lā yastaṭīʿūna raddahā wa lā hum yunẓarūn · Wa laqadi'stuhziʾa bi-rusulin min qablika fa-ḥāqa bi'lladhīna sakhirū minhum mā kānū bihi yastahziʾūn*

"**They say: 'When is this promise, if you are truthful?'** · **If only those who deny knew the hour when they will not keep the fire from their faces or from their backs, and will not be helped!** · **Rather, it will come upon them suddenly and stun them; they will not be able to turn it back, nor will they be given respite.** · **Messengers before you were mocked as well; and what they made light of closed in upon those who scoffed at them.**"

### An answer left unanswered

**The thirty-ninth verse is a *law* sentence, and it has no apodosis.**

**The grammarians call this the elision of the *jawāb* of *law*:** "if only they knew…" — and the rest is not said. **The elision conveys that the threat cannot be described.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the sentence is not completed, and its not being completed is a choice of narration. **The same technique was treated at `102-tekasur.md` on 102/5 (*kallā law taʿlamūna ʿilma'l-yaqīn*).** **I rely on that treatment.**

**عَن وُجُوهِهِمْ … وَلَا عَن ظُهُورِهِمْ** — **two directions: the front and the back.** **And the choice is worth recording:** a person can protect his face; he cannot protect his back. **By naming both, the verse leaves no direction to be protected.**

**فَتَبْهَتُهُمْ** — root **ب-ه-ت**: **to leave someone without an argument, to stun.** *Buhtān* — slander; *mabhūt* — struck dumb. **And in another place in the Qurʾān the same root is used of Abraham's opponent:** *fa-buhita'lladhī kafar* (al-Baqara 2/258).

**I give this as a note about the root and add this:** the same root is the verbal counterpart of the scene described in the sixty-fifth verse of this sūra — Abraham's opponents falling silent and hanging their heads. **The word does not occur in the sūra; the resemblance belongs to the fact.**

**فَحَاقَ بِٱلَّذِينَ سَخِرُوا۟** — ***ḥāqa* — root ح-ي-ق: for a thing to come back upon its owner and close in on him.** **And a point of word order: what closes in is not the punishment but *the very thing they mocked*** — *mā kānū bihi yastahziʾūn.*

**This sentence was named at `026-suara.md` on 26/6 and it was recorded there:** *"when the thing mocked comes to pass, it comes no longer as news but as an event."* **I rely on that treatment.**

## 21/42-44 — قُلْ مَن يَكْلَؤُكُم بِٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ مِنَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ بَلْ هُمْ عَن ذِكْرِ رَبِّهِم مُّعْرِضُونَ · أَمْ لَهُمْ ءَالِهَةٌ تَمْنَعُهُم مِّن دُونِنَا لَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ نَصْرَ أَنفُسِهِمْ وَلَا هُم مِّنَّا يُصْحَبُونَ · بَلْ مَتَّعْنَا هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ وَءَابَآءَهُمْ حَتَّىٰ طَالَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْعُمُرُ أَفَلَا يَرَوْنَ أَنَّا نَأْتِى ٱلْأَرْضَ نَنقُصُهَا مِنْ أَطْرَافِهَآ أَفَهُمُ ٱلْغَٰلِبُونَ

*Qul man yaklaʾukum bi'l-layli wa'n-nahāri mina'r-Raḥmān, bal hum ʿan dhikri rabbihim muʿriḍūn · Am lahum ālihatun tamnaʿuhum min dūninā, lā yastaṭīʿūna naṣra anfusihim wa lā hum minnā yuṣḥabūn · Bal mattaʿnā hāʾulāʾi wa ābāʾahum ḥattā ṭāla ʿalayhimu'l-ʿumur, a-fa-lā yarawna annā naʾti'l-arḍa nanquṣuhā min aṭrāfihā, a-fa-humu'l-ghālibūn*

"**Say: 'Who could keep watch over you by night and by day against the Merciful?' No — they turn away from the remembrance of their Lord.** · **Or have they gods to protect them against Us? They cannot help themselves, nor are they befriended by Us.** · **We let these and their fathers enjoy life so long that their lifetimes grew long. Do they not see that We come to the land and reduce it at its edges? Are they, then, the victors?**"

### يَكْلَؤُكُم — once in the Qurʾān

***Yaklaʾu* — root ك-ل-أ.** **This root is analysed here for the first time in this commentary, and it occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**The sense the lexicographers give: *kalaʾa* is to watch over a thing and guard it** — **to guard it by keeping watch through the night.** *Kāliʾ* — a watchman; *kilāʾa* — protection. **And in the picture of the word there is sleeplessness:** waiting without taking one's eyes away.

**And the note *bi'l-layli wa'n-nahār* completes this picture: night and day.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the question is not a question about a contest of power but a question about **keeping watch.** "Who could stand watch over you?"

**مِنَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰن** — **and the choice of the name must again be recorded.** The one to be protected against is named not *al-Jabbār* or *al-Muntaqim* but **al-Raḥmān.** **This technique was recorded at `026-suara.md` on 26/5; it is applied here for the third time in the sūra** (26, 36, 42).

**وَلَا هُم مِّنَّا يُصْحَبُونَ** — ***yuṣḥabūn* — root ص-ح-ب, passive: "they are not taken as companions / they are not given protection".** **There is disagreement about its sense:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **They receive no protection or company from Us** |
| 2 | *Ṣuḥba* here means protection: they are not protected |
| 3 | The verb means "no one who has help from Us keeps them company" |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

### نَنقُصُهَا مِنْ أَطْرَافِهَا

**There is disagreement in the classical commentaries about what the sentence tells, and I give it in a table:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A reduction of land** — the going of a part of the earth through destructions and ruin |
| 2 | **A reduction of people** — the diminishing of the population, its being cut down |
| 3 | **The reduction of knowledge and of those who have it** — the "edges" of the land going with the death of those who know |
| 4 | **The narrowing of territory** — the shrinking, from the perimeter inward, of the land in the hands of those who deny |

**All four explanations are transmitted. I do not impose a preference.**

**And I enter a note about word order:** the sentence continues *a-fa-humu'l-ghālibūn* — "are they, then, the victors?" **That is, the sentence stands inside a discussion of *victory*.** This affords some support to the fourth explanation; **it is not eliminative.**

**حَتَّىٰ طَالَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْعُمُرُ** — "the lifetime grew long upon them". **And a point of word order: the subject is *al-ʿumur*, not the people.** What grows long is the lifetime; **they are in the passive position.** **I record this as an observation about the structure: a long life is told not as a gain but as something heaped upon them.**

## 21/45-46 — قُلْ إِنَّمَآ أُنذِرُكُم بِٱلْوَحْىِ وَلَا يَسْمَعُ ٱلصُّمُّ ٱلدُّعَآءَ إِذَا مَا يُنذَرُونَ · وَلَئِن مَّسَّتْهُمْ نَفْحَةٌ مِّنْ عَذَابِ رَبِّكَ لَيَقُولُنَّ يَٰوَيْلَنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ

*Qul innamā undhirukum bi'l-waḥy, wa lā yasmaʿu'ṣ-ṣummu'd-duʿāʾa idhā mā yundharūn · Wa la-in massathum nafḥatun min ʿadhābi rabbika la-yaqūlunna yā waylanā innā kunnā ẓālimīn*

"**Say: 'I warn you only by revelation.' But the deaf do not hear the call when they are warned.** · **And if a breath of your Lord's punishment were to touch them, they would surely say: 'Woe to us! We were indeed doing wrong.'**"

### إِنَّمَآ أُنذِرُكُم بِٱلْوَحْىِ — the setting of a limit

***Innamā* is the particle of restriction: "only, nothing but".** **And what is restricted is the instrument of the warning:** *bi'l-waḥy*.

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the fifth verse:** there the addressees had asked for an *āya* — a visible sign. **Here the answer is given: the only thing in my hands is revelation.** That is, the verse also says **what the instrument in the messenger's hand is not.**

**At `025-furkan.md` on 25/56 and at `034-sebe.md` on 34/28 the scope of the messengership was given with two different words; this verse gives its instrument.** **The three will be tabulated at the hundred and seventh verse.**

**وَلَا يَسْمَعُ ٱلصُّمُّ ٱلدُّعَآءَ** — the counterpart of this sentence was treated at `030-rum.md` on 30/52: *fa-innaka lā tusmiʿu'l-mawtā wa lā tusmiʿu'ṣ-ṣumma'd-duʿāʾ*. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the grammatical difference between the two verses must be recorded:**

| | **al-Rūm 30/52** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/45** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb | *lā **tusmiʿu*** — Form IV: **you cannot make them hear** | *lā **yasmaʿu*** — Form I: **they do not hear** |
| The subject | **The Prophet** | **The deaf** |
| What it says | The limit of the messenger's power | The consequence of the addressee's state |

**The same fact, from two different sides.** **I record this as an observation about the pattern.**

### نَفْحَةٌ — a threefold diminishing

**And the forty-sixth verse is one of the finest pieces of word order in the sūra. Three separate diminishings come one upon another in the sentence:**

| Element | Root | What it diminishes |
|---|---|---|
| ***massat*** | م-س-س | **To touch** — not to strike but to graze |
| ***nafḥatun*** | ن-ف-ح | **A breath** — a slight stirring of scent or of wind |
| ***min ʿadhābi*** | — | *Min* **partitive**: **a part** of the punishment |

***Nafḥa* — root ن-ف-ح: to blow lightly, for a scent to spread.** *Nafaḥa'ṭ-ṭīb* — the perfume spread. **And the word carries a *tāʾ* (*nafḥa*, not *nafḥ*): in Arabic the *tāʾ* of unity conveys a single instance** — "one single breath".

**So the sentence says this:** were **a single breath** **of a part** of the punishment to **graze** them.

**And what they will say in return is given: *yā waylanā innā kunnā ẓālimīn*** — **the very sentence spoken by the town destroyed in the fourteenth verse.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being that the two sentences are word for word the same:** the forty-sixth verse repeats **hypothetically and on the smallest scale** what in the fourteenth verse **actually happened.** **That is, the sūra shows this: the lightest touch produces the same sentence as the gravest destruction.**

**The lightness of the root م-س-س was recorded at `038-sad.md` on 38/41:** *"what distinguishes the word is its lightness: *mass* is to touch a thing — not to strike, not to hit."* **I rely on that treatment.** **And the same verb will be used of Job in the eighty-third verse of this sūra: *massaniya'ḍ-ḍurr*.**

## 21/47 — وَنَضَعُ ٱلْمَوَٰزِينَ ٱلْقِسْطَ لِيَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ فَلَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْـًٔا وَإِن كَانَ مِثْقَالَ حَبَّةٍ مِّنْ خَرْدَلٍ أَتَيْنَا بِهَا وَكَفَىٰ بِنَا حَٰسِبِينَ

*Wa naḍaʿu'l-mawāzīna'l-qisṭa li-yawmi'l-qiyāmati fa-lā tuẓlamu nafsun shayʾā, wa in kāna mithqāla ḥabbatin min khardalin ataynā bihā, wa kafā binā ḥāsibīn*

"**We shall set up the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, and no soul will be wronged in anything. Even were it the weight of a mustard seed, We shall bring it forth. And We suffice as reckoners.**"

### ٱلْمَوَٰزِين — the disagreement about the plural

**The root و-ز-ن and the word *mīzān* were treated at `057-hadid.md` on 57/25.** There the three things sent down together were tabulated: **the Book, the balance, and iron** — and *mīzān* was recorded as **the instrument of measure.** **I rely on that treatment.**

***Mīzān* is on the *mifʿāl* pattern, and in Arabic this pattern forms nouns of instrument** (*miftāḥ* — a key, *miṣbāḥ* — a lamp). **That is, the word is the tool that does the measuring.**

**And there is disagreement about the plural here:**

| View | Of what *mawāzīn* is the plural | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The plural of ***mīzān*** | **Scales** — more than one balance |
| 2 | The plural of ***mawzūn*** | **The things weighed** — deeds |
| 3 | The plural is for conveying **multitude and grandeur** | One balance, a plural wording |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And the division of labour between the two sūras must be recorded — this is a verifiable comparison:**

| | **al-Ḥadīd 57/25** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/47** |
|---|---|---|
| The word | *al-**mīzān*** — singular | *al-**mawāzīn*** — plural |
| The verb | *anzalnā* — **We sent down** | *naḍaʿu* — **We set up** |
| When | **In this world** — together with the messengers | **On the Day of Resurrection** |
| For what | *li-yaqūma'n-nāsu bi'l-qisṭ* — that people may uphold justice | *fa-lā tuẓlamu nafsun shayʾā* — that no soul be wronged |

**And the shared word is *qisṭ*: the same root in both.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the two verses' shared word and their opposite times:** the same measure is **once given to the human being and once set up over him.**

**ٱلْقِسْطَ** — it is in the accusative and is an attribute of *mawāzīn*. **And a point of grammar:** *qisṭ* is a **verbal noun**; qualifying with a verbal noun conveys **intensification** in Arabic. Not "just scales" but **"scales that are justice itself".**

**لِيَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ** — two readings of the particle *lām* are transmitted:

| Reading | What the *lām* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **In the sense of *fī*** | "We set them up **on** the Day of Resurrection" |
| 2 | **Of cause** (*taʿlīl*) | "We set them up **for** the Day of Resurrection" |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

### مِثْقَالَ حَبَّةٍ مِّنْ خَرْدَلٍ — comparison with Luqmān

**This measure was treated at `031-lokman.md` on 31/16, and it was recorded there:**

> **"The mustard seed is one of the smallest grains known in the kitchen of that day. That is, the measure is taken from something the listener could handle."**

**I rely on that treatment and now set the two verses side by side, because they are the two occurrences in the Qurʾān:**

| | **Luqmān 31/16** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/47** |
|---|---|---|
| Who says it | **Luqmān, to his son** — inside an admonition | **The text** — inside the scene of reckoning |
| The measure | *mithqāla ḥabbatin min khardal* | *mithqāla ḥabbatin min khardal* |
| Where it is hidden | *fī ṣakhratin aw fi's-samāwāti aw fi'l-arḍ* — **three hiding places** | **Not said** |
| The verb | *yaʾti bihā**'llāh*** — God brings it forth | *ataynā bihā* — **We bring it forth** |
| The closing attribute | *inna'llāha **laṭīfun khabīr*** | *wa kafā binā **ḥāsibīn*** |

**And two differences must be recorded.**

**The first is the person of the verb:** Luqmān speaks in the third person (*yaʾti bihā'llāh*), because **the speaker is a human being.** al-Anbiyāʾ uses the first person plural (*ataynā bihā*), because **the speaker is the text itself.** **The same verb (أ-ت-ي, Form IV), in two different mouths.**

**The second, and more important, is the closing attribute — I give this as my own reading:**

**Luqmān ends with *laṭīf* and *khabīr*: the one who reaches the finest place, and the one who is informed.** **The subject of the verse there was *finding*.**

**al-Anbiyāʾ ends with *ḥāsibīn*: the one who reckons.** **The subject of the verse here is *weighing*.**

**That is, the same unit of measure is used for two different operations:** in Luqmān it shows **the reach of knowledge**, in al-Anbiyāʾ **the sensitivity of the balance.**

**وَكَفَىٰ بِنَا حَٰسِبِينَ** — **and *ḥāsibīn* is from the same root as *ḥisābuhum* in the sūra's first verse: ح-س-ب.**

**This is one of the most closed frames of the sūra and is wholly a datum of the text:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *iqtaraba li'n-nāsi **ḥisābuhum*** | **Mankind** — whose reckoning has drawn near |
| **47** | *wa kafā binā **ḥāsibīn*** | **The one who will do the reckoning** |

**The sūra names here the owner of the thing it said forty-seven verses earlier had drawn near.** **And the third block closes with this sentence; immediately afterwards the series of prophets begins.**

## The series of prophets — an introduction to 21/48-91

**The section running from the forty-eighth verse to the ninety-first is the sūra's longest block and has a technique of its own. I record that technique before entering the series, because I shall not repeat it at the individual verses.**

**A series of prophets was treated at `037-saffat.md`** (37/75-148: Noah, Abraham, Moses and Aaron, Elias, Lot, Jonah) **and the technique of that series was recorded there:**

> **"What is striking about the series is not the stories told but the way the stories are closed. Each ends with the same three or four sentences as a seal."**

**I rely on that treatment. And al-Anbiyāʾ's series works differently from al-Ṣāffāt's. I give the difference in a table, because both are countable from the text:**

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/75-148** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/48-91** |
|---|---|---|
| How many names | **Seven** | **Sixteen** |
| The stories | **Told** — there are scenes | **Mostly not told** — only Abraham's is long |
| The closing pattern | *salāmun ʿalā …* / *innā kadhālika najzi'l-muḥsinīn* | *fa'stajabnā lah* / *fī raḥmatinā* / *mina'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn* |
| The opening verb | *wa inna … la-mina'l-mursalīn* | *wa …* + a name (ranged with a conjunction) |
| What ends the series | *fa-mattaʿnāhum ilā ḥīn* (148) | *āyatan li'l-ʿālamīn* (91) |

**And the real technique peculiar to al-Anbiyāʾ is this — it can be verified from the text:**

**Four prophets are named with the same verb: *nādā* (he called).**

| Verse | Who | The wording of the call | The answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **76** | **Noah** | (Its content is not given) | *fa'stajabnā lahu* |
| **83** | **Job** | *annī massaniya'ḍ-ḍurru wa anta arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn* | *fa'stajabnā lahu* |
| **87** | **Jonah** | *lā ilāha illā anta subḥānaka innī kuntu mina'ẓ-ẓālimīn* | *fa'stajabnā lahu* |
| **89** | **Zechariah** | *rabbi lā tadharnī fardan wa anta khayru'l-wārithīn* | *fa'stajabnā lahu* |

**Four calls, four times the same answer: *fa'stajabnā lah*.** **This is a countable fact within the sūra and is the spine of the series.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the table above:** the series records not the prophets' **achievements** but their **voices.** And in all four the answer is given **with the same word.** **That is, the sūra shows by its very arrangement that the response does not vary with the person.**

**And a second pattern: two of the three prayers close with an attribute introduced by *anta*.**

| Verse | Who | The close |
|---|---|---|
| **83** | Job | *wa **anta** arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn* |
| **87** | Jonah | *subḥānaka* — (no petition) |
| **89** | Zechariah | *wa **anta** khayru'l-wārithīn* |

**Two of them end with an elative (*arḥam*, *khayr*): "most merciful", "best".** **The prayers close not with a request but with an attribute of the one addressed.** This is an observation about the structure.

## 21/48-50 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَا مُوسَىٰ وَهَٰرُونَ ٱلْفُرْقَانَ وَضِيَآءً وَذِكْرًا لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · ٱلَّذِينَ يَخْشَوْنَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَيْبِ وَهُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّاعَةِ مُشْفِقُونَ · وَهَٰذَا ذِكْرٌ مُّبَارَكٌ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ أَفَأَنتُمْ لَهُۥ مُنكِرُونَ

*Wa laqad ātaynā Mūsā wa Hārūna'l-furqāna wa ḍiyāʾan wa dhikran li'l-muttaqīn · Alladhīna yakhshawna rabbahum bi'l-ghaybi wa hum mina's-sāʿati mushfiqūn · Wa hādhā dhikrun mubārakun anzalnāh, a-fa-antum lahu munkirūn*

"**Indeed We gave Moses and Aaron the Criterion, and a light and a remembrance for those who guard themselves** · **who fear their Lord unseen and tremble at the Hour.** · **And this too is a blessed remembrance that We have sent down. Will you then deny it?**"

### ٱلْفُرْقَان — the word being given to Moses

**The word was analysed in detail at `025-furkan.md` on 25/1.** The note there:

> **"The root ف-ر-ق: to separate, to open a space between two things. […] The pattern *furqān* (*fuʿlān*) is counted by the lexicographers a noun conveying an action: 'the act of separating' or 'that which distinguishes'. […] This name given to the Book conveys not its content but its function."**

**I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it. Here I add what is peculiar to al-Anbiyāʾ, because it is striking:**

**In Sūrat al-Furqān this name was given to the Qurʾān. Here the same name is given to what was given to Moses and Aaron.**

| | **al-Furqān 25/1** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/48** |
|---|---|---|
| To whom | *ʿalā **ʿabdihi*** — to His servant | *to **Moses and Aaron*** |
| What was given | *al-**Furqān*** | *al-**Furqān**a wa ḍiyāʾan wa dhikrā* |
| For what | *li-yakūna li'l-ʿālamīna **nadhīrā*** | *li'l-**muttaqīn*** |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the two verses' shared word:** *Furqān* is not the **proper name** of a book but the name of a **function.** Two books that do the same work can be named by the same name. **This was recorded in Sūrat al-Furqān — al-Anbiyāʾ shows it in practice.**

**And three words come side by side: *furqān*, *ḍiyāʾ*, *dhikr*.**

| Word | Root | Its function |
|---|---|---|
| **فُرْقَان** | ف-ر-ق | **It separates** — the true from the false |
| **ضِيَاء** | ض-و-أ | **It lights** — it makes visible |
| **ذِكْر** | ذ-ك-ر | **It reminds** — it brings back what was forgotten |

**Three, and three different works. And in Sūrat Yūnus *ḍiyāʾ* is used of the sun and *nūr* of the moon** (Yūnus 10/5). **On the basis of this distinction the lexicographers separate *ḍiyāʾ* as "light that is a thing's own" from *nūr* as "reflected light".** **I transmit this as a transmitted distinction.**

### وَهَٰذَا ذِكْرٌ مُّبَارَكٌ — the sūra naming itself

**And the fiftieth verse is the densest use of the root *dhikr* in the sūra: the same word is used two verses apart for two different books** (48: what was given to Moses; 50: this one).

***Mubārak* — root ب-ر-ك, treated at `067-mulk.md` on 67/1 and at `025-furkan.md` on 25/1.** The core sense: **a camel's kneeling down, settling in a place and staying fixed** — whence **blessing**: a good that lasts and increases. **I do not repeat it here.**

**And a note about word order:** Sūrat al-Furqān had opened with *tabāraka* (a verb); al-Anbiyāʾ uses *mubārak* (a passive participle). **The same root, one qualifying the source, the other what is given.**

## 21/51-54 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَآ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ رُشْدَهُۥ مِن قَبْلُ وَكُنَّا بِهِۦ عَٰلِمِينَ · إِذْ قَالَ لِأَبِيهِ وَقَوْمِهِۦ مَا هَٰذِهِ ٱلتَّمَاثِيلُ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنتُمْ لَهَا عَٰكِفُونَ · قَالُوا۟ وَجَدْنَآ ءَابَآءَنَا لَهَا عَٰبِدِينَ · قَالَ لَقَدْ كُنتُمْ أَنتُمْ وَءَابَآؤُكُمْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Wa laqad ātaynā Ibrāhīma rushdahu min qablu wa kunnā bihi ʿālimīn · Idh qāla li-abīhi wa qawmihi mā hādhihi't-tamāthīlu'llatī antum lahā ʿākifūn · Qālū wajadnā ābāʾanā lahā ʿābidīn · Qāla laqad kuntum antum wa ābāʾukum fī ḍalālin mubīn*

"**Indeed We gave Abraham his right judgement before, and We knew him well.** · **When he said to his father and his people: 'What are these images to which you keep yourselves attached?'** · **They said: 'We found our fathers worshipping them.'** · **He said: 'Indeed you and your fathers alike have been in plain error.'**"

### رُشْدَهُۥ — what is given with a pronoun

***Rushd* — root ر-ش-د: finding the right way, hitting the mark.** Its opposite is *ghayy*.

**And a point of word order must be recorded: the word is annexed to a pronoun — *rushda**hu***, "his right judgement".** Not *rushdan* (indefinite).

**The grammarians' explanation:** the annexation here gives the sense **that which befits him, that which is proper to him.** **That is, what is given is not a general capacity but *his own* share.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure.**

**مِن قَبْلُ** — "before". **There is disagreement about before what:**

| View | Before what |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Before Moses and Aaron** — they were named in the preceding verse |
| 2 | **Before his prophethood** — in his youth |
| 3 | **Before the events told in this sūra** |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

### ٱلتَّمَاثِيل — and the sūra's war of words

**And now I come to the most striking fact of this section. It is wholly a datum of the text and is countable.**

**The same objects are called by five different names across the scene — and the naming changes with the speaker:**

| Verse | Word | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| **52** | *al-**tamāthīl*** — images | **Abraham** |
| **57** | *aṣ**nām**akum* — your idols | **Abraham** |
| **58** | *judhādhan* — pieces | **The text** |
| **59** | *bi-**ālihati**nā* — our gods | **The people** |
| **62** | *bi-**ālihati**nā* — our gods | **The people** |
| **66** | *mā lā yanfaʿukum* — what does not benefit you | **Abraham** |

**And what is seen in the table is this — I record it as my own reading, its ground being the count above:**

**Abraham does not once call those objects *gods*.** **And the people do not once say *image* or *idol*.**

**That is, the dispute in the scene begins, in its very first sentence, over the naming.** Abraham's question is not a question for information: *mā hādhihi't-tamāthīl* — "what are these?" **In asking the question he has already given the answer in the name.**

***Tamāthīl* — root م-ث-ل, the plural of *timthāl*: the likeness of a thing, its image, its representation.** **The word names the object as *the copy of something*** — not as a thing in itself.

**عَٰكِفُونَ — root ع-ك-ف:** to stand over a thing **without leaving it**, to stay there. *Iʿtikāf* is from the same root. **And the picture of the verb is worth recording:** the verse tells not of worship but of **not leaving its side.** **That is, what is criticised is first described as a *habit*.**

**وَجَدْنَآ ءَابَآءَنَا لَهَا عَٰبِدِينَ** — and the answer is not a proof but a **finding**: *wajadnā* (we found).

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the people do not defend the worship — **they say they inherited it.** And Abraham's answer aims at precisely this point: *laqad kuntum **antum wa ābāʾukum*** — "you and your fathers alike". **That is, the inherited end of the chain is taken into the ruling as well.**

## 21/55-58 — قَالُوٓا۟ أَجِئْتَنَا بِٱلْحَقِّ أَمْ أَنتَ مِنَ ٱللَّٰعِبِينَ · قَالَ بَل رَّبُّكُمْ رَبُّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلَّذِى فَطَرَهُنَّ وَأَنَا۠ عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكُم مِّنَ ٱلشَّٰهِدِينَ · وَتَٱللَّهِ لَأَكِيدَنَّ أَصْنَٰمَكُم بَعْدَ أَن تُوَلُّوا۟ مُدْبِرِينَ · فَجَعَلَهُمْ جُذَٰذًا إِلَّا كَبِيرًا لَّهُمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ إِلَيْهِ يَرْجِعُونَ

*Qālū a-jiʾtanā bi'l-ḥaqqi am anta mina'l-lāʿibīn · Qāla bal rabbukum rabbu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi'lladhī faṭarahunna wa anā ʿalā dhālikum mina'sh-shāhidīn · Wa ta'llāhi la-akīdanna aṣnāmakum baʿda an tuwallū mudbirīn · Fa-jaʿalahum judhādhan illā kabīran lahum laʿallahum ilayhi yarjiʿūn*

"**They said: 'Have you brought us the truth, or are you one of those at play?'** · **He said: 'No! Your Lord is the Lord of the heavens and the earth, who split them into being. And I am one of those who bear witness to that.'** · **'By God, I shall scheme against your idols after you have turned your backs and gone.'** · **So he broke them into pieces — all but the greatest of them, that they might turn back to it.**"

### أَمْ أَنتَ مِنَ ٱللَّٰعِبِينَ — the sūra's word returns

**And this is one of the most striking internal connections in the sūra. It is wholly a datum of the text.**

**In the sixteenth verse the text had said:** *wa mā khalaqna's-samāʾa wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā **lāʿibīn*** — "we did not create the heaven and the earth in play."

**In the fifty-fifth verse the same word occurs in a question put to a prophet:** *am anta mina'**l-lāʿibīn***?

| Verse | Expression | Of whom | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **16** | *lāʿibīn* — a circumstantial qualifier, negated | **The act of creating** | It is not play |
| **55** | *mina'l-lāʿibīn* — inside a question | **Abraham** | Are you at play? |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the two verses' shared word:** having set down the ruling "creating is not play", the sūra reports **that the man who carries that ruling is asked "are you at play?"** **The accusation is built with the very word the sūra had earlier rejected.**

**And Abraham's answer begins with *bal* — that is, it rejects the question and puts another in its place.**

**فَطَرَهُنَّ** — ***faṭr*, root ف-ط-ر.** **In the distinction set up at `054-kamer.md` on 54/1 it was recorded: *faṭr* is to split from nothing and establish.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And I record its connection with the thirtieth verse:** there we had *fatq* — **to part what has been established.** Here we have *faṭr* — **to split from nothing and establish.** **The same sūra uses two roots of splitting for two different works.**

***Faṭarahunna* — the pronoun is feminine plural (*hunna*), referring to the heavens.**

### وَتَٱللَّهِ — a rare oath

**The particle of oath is *tāʾ*: *wa-ta'llāh*.** **And the lexicographers record the peculiarity of this particle:**

| Oath particle | Its use |
|---|---|
| **و** (*wāw*) | The most widespread; used with any noun |
| **ب** (*bāʾ*) | The original one; may also come with a verb |
| **ت** (*tāʾ*) | Used **only with the word *Allāh***; conveys **astonishment and emphasis** |

**So the particle marks the oath as not an ordinary one.** **And this particle occurs in a few places in the Qurʾān.**

**لَأَكِيدَنَّ — root ك-ي-د**

**The root was treated at `052-tur.md`, `068-kalem.md` and `037-saffat.md`.** **And at al-Ṣāffāt precisely the counterpart of this story occurred:** *fa-arādū bihi **kaydan** fa-jaʿalnāhumu'l-asfalīn* (37/98).

**The comparison of the two sūras can be verified from the text and is striking:**

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/98** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/57 and 21/70** |
|---|---|---|
| Who does the *kayd* | **The people** — *fa-arādū bihi kaydā* | **Abraham** (57) — *la-akīdanna aṣnāmakum* |
| Then | *fa-jaʿalnāhumu'l-**asfalīn*** | *fa-jaʿalnāhumu'l-**akhsarīn*** (70) |
| The same root again | — | **The people do *kayd* too** (70): *wa arādū bihi kaydā* |

**In al-Anbiyāʾ the root occurs twice and is used by both sides: first Abraham (57), then the people (70).** **In al-Ṣāffāt it is used only by the people.**

**I record this as an observation about wording.** And I add this: **in this sūra the word is not the monopoly of one side.** The same verb works in two directions, and what separates the outcomes is not the name of the act but its end.

**بَعْدَ أَن تُوَلُّوا۟ مُدْبِرِينَ** — **and this expression occurs word for word at `037-saffat.md` on 37/90:** *fa-**tawallaw** ʿanhu **mudbirīn***.

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/90** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/57** |
|---|---|---|
| The expression | *fa-tawallaw ʿanhu mudbirīn* | *baʿda an tuwallū mudbirīn* |
| The aspect | **Perfect** — it happened | **Imperfect, inside a condition** — it will happen |
| Who says it | **The text** — telling | **Abraham** — announcing in advance |

**The same two words, in two sūras, in two different tenses.** **al-Ṣāffāt tells the event; al-Anbiyāʾ shows that Abraham knew of it beforehand.** **This is a verifiable sharing of wording, and I add as my own reading: in al-Anbiyāʾ the intention is declared before the event.**

### جُذَاذًا — fragments

***Judhādh* — root ج-ذ-ذ.** **This root is analysed here for the first time in this commentary.**

**The sense the lexicographers give: *jadhdha* is to break a thing by cutting it, to crumble it.** *Judhādh* — **broken pieces, fragments.** **And the same root occurs once more in the Qurʾān:** *ʿaṭāʾan **ghayra majdhūdh*** (Hūd 11/108) — "a gift not cut off".

**So the root has two faces: *to cut* and *what is not cut*.**

**And a point of word order: *judhādhan* is singular while the objects are plural.** The grammarians count it a **verbal-noun attribute**: "in a state of fragments". **That is, the verse does not say into how many pieces — it names the outcome.**

**إِلَّا كَبِيرًا لَّهُمْ** — "except the greatest of them".

**And the pronoun *lahum* must be recorded — I give this as my own reading:** the verse does not say "the great one", it says **the one great *to them***. **That is, the greatness is ascribed not to the object itself but to the people's measure.** The text reports the people's estimate; it does not endorse it.

**لَعَلَّهُمْ إِلَيْهِ يَرْجِعُونَ** — and a ground is given. **There is disagreement about to whom or what the pronoun (*ilayhi*) refers:**

| View | Who or what *ilayhi* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The great idol** — that they might turn to it and ask |
| 2 | **Abraham** — that they might turn to him and ask |
| 3 | **The truth, or reason** — the turning is a turning to thought |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And the counterpart of this sentence will come in the sixty-fourth verse — it is the sūra's strongest internal link, and I shall treat it there.**

## 21/59-63 — قَالُوا۟ مَن فَعَلَ هَٰذَا بِـَٔالِهَتِنَآ إِنَّهُۥ لَمِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · قَالُوا۟ سَمِعْنَا فَتًى يَذْكُرُهُمْ يُقَالُ لَهُۥٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمُ · قَالُوا۟ فَأْتُوا۟ بِهِۦ عَلَىٰٓ أَعْيُنِ ٱلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَشْهَدُونَ · قَالُوٓا۟ ءَأَنتَ فَعَلْتَ هَٰذَا بِـَٔالِهَتِنَا يَٰٓإِبْرَٰهِيمُ · قَالَ بَلْ فَعَلَهُۥ كَبِيرُهُمْ هَٰذَا فَسْـَٔلُوهُمْ إِن كَانُوا۟ يَنطِقُونَ

*Qālū man faʿala hādhā bi-ālihatinā innahu la-mina'ẓ-ẓālimīn · Qālū samiʿnā fatan yadhkuruhum yuqālu lahu Ibrāhīm · Qālū fa'tū bihi ʿalā aʿyuni'n-nāsi laʿallahum yashhadūn · Qālū a-anta faʿalta hādhā bi-ālihatinā yā Ibrāhīm · Qāla bal faʿalahu kabīruhum hādhā fa'sʾalūhum in kānū yanṭiqūn*

"**They said: 'Who did this to our gods? He is surely a wrongdoer.'** · **They said: 'We heard a youth talking about them; he is called Abraham.'** · **They said: 'Then bring him before the people's eyes, that they may witness.'** · **They said: 'Was it you who did this to our gods, Abraham?'** · **He said: 'No — this, the greatest of them, did it. Ask them, if they can speak.'**"

### Five times *qālū* — the building of a scene

**Five verses in succession begin with *qālū*/*qāla*.** **This is a technique of scene-building in the Qurʾān: the narrator does not intervene, only the speeches are ranged.**

**And the order of the speeches gives an investigative process step by step:**

| Verse | Step |
|---|---|
| **59** | **The establishing of the crime** and the naming of the doer in advance (*la-mina'ẓ-ẓālimīn*) |
| **60** | **The identifying of a suspect** — on hearsay |
| **61** | **The decision to make it public** — before the people's eyes |
| **62** | **The interrogation** |
| **63** | **The answer** |

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the verdict (59) has been given **before** the interrogation (62). **And what is asked for in the sixty-first verse is not evidence but *witnessing*** — *laʿallahum yashhadūn.*

**فَتًى** — root **ف-ت-ي**: **a youth, a young man.** **And the choice of the word is worth recording:** Abraham is named not by his name but **by his age** — and the name is then given as **hearsay**: *yuqālu lahu Ibrāhīm* — "one who is called Abraham".

**I record this as my own reading:** the two expressions together show that the speakers **did not know him closely.**

**يَذْكُرُهُمْ** — and again the root ذ-ك-ر. **The same verb was used of the Prophet in the thirty-sixth verse:** *a-hādha'lladhī **yadhkuru** ālihatakum* — "is this the one who talks about your gods?"

**The sharing of wording between the two verses is exact and can be verified within the sūra:**

| Verse | Of whom | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **36** | **The Prophet** | *a-hādha'lladhī **yadhkuru ālihatakum*** |
| **60** | **Abraham** | *samiʿnā fatan **yadhkuruhum*** |

**The same verb, the same accusation, two different ages.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the two verses' shared word:** the sūra shows with the same verb that the complaint its own addressee brings against the Prophet **was brought long before as well.**

### بَلْ فَعَلَهُۥ كَبِيرُهُمْ هَٰذَا — a disputed sentence

**And the sixty-third verse is one of the most debated sentences in the classical commentaries. I give the disagreement in a table and impose no preference.**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The condition governs the sentence:** the clause *in kānū yanṭiqūn* qualifies the whole — "if they can speak, then the greatest of them did it." Since they cannot speak, the sentence carries no assertion either |
| 2 | ***Ilzām*/*taʿrīḍ*:** the speech is framed to confront the addressee with the consequence of his own thesis. It is spoken according to what the other side grants, and does not aim to convey information |
| 3 | **A difference of pause:** one says *bal faʿalahu* and stops, and then a new sentence begins: *kabīruhum hādhā fa'sʾalūhum* |

**All three explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries. I do not impose a preference.**

**And I enter a note that can be read from the text itself — this is not a preference but an observation about the structure:**

**In the fifty-eighth verse the ground of Abraham's act had already been given: *laʿallahum ilayhi yarjiʿūn*.** **That is, the breaking was done in order to produce a *question*.** **And the answer in the sixty-third verse produces exactly a question: *fa'sʾalūhum*** — "ask them."

**The work of the sentence is not to convey information but to bring the addressee to a place.** **And the next verse shows that they arrived there.**

## 21/64-67 — فَرَجَعُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ فَقَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّكُمْ أَنتُمُ ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ · ثُمَّ نُكِسُوا۟ عَلَىٰ رُءُوسِهِمْ لَقَدْ عَلِمْتَ مَا هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ يَنطِقُونَ · قَالَ أَفَتَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا يَنفَعُكُمْ شَيْـًٔا وَلَا يَضُرُّكُمْ · أُفٍّ لَّكُمْ وَلِمَا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ

*Fa-rajaʿū ilā anfusihim fa-qālū innakum antumu'ẓ-ẓālimūn · Thumma nukisū ʿalā ruʾūsihim laqad ʿalimta mā hāʾulāʾi yanṭiqūn · Qāla a-fa-taʿbudūna min dūni'llāhi mā lā yanfaʿukum shayʾan wa lā yaḍurrukum · Uffin lakum wa limā taʿbudūna min dūni'llāh, a-fa-lā taʿqilūn*

"**At this they returned to themselves and said: 'It is you who are the wrongdoers!'** · **Then they were turned back onto their heads: 'You know well that these do not speak!'** · **He said: 'Do you then worship, besides God, what can neither benefit you at all nor harm you?** · **Fie on you and on what you worship besides God! Will you not use your reason?'**"

### فَرَجَعُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ — the sūra's strongest internal link

**And this sentence is the most striking place in this section — perhaps in the whole sūra. The fact belongs entirely to the text.**

**In the fifty-eighth verse the ground of Abraham's act had been given:**

> *fa-jaʿalahum judhādhan illā kabīran lahum **laʿallahum ilayhi yarjiʿūn*** — "that perhaps **they might turn back to it**."

**In the sixty-fourth verse the turning takes place — but not to the place named:**

> *fa-**rajaʿū ilā anfusihim*** — "**they returned to themselves.**"

| Verse | Verb | Where the turning goes |
|---|---|---|
| **58** | *yarjiʿūn* — imperfect, **expected** | *ilayhi* — **to it** |
| **64** | *rajaʿū* — perfect, **actual** | *ilā anfusihim* — **to themselves** |

**The same root (ر-ج-ع), six verses apart, with two different destinations.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the two verses' shared verb:** the device set up expected a return to an object; the return that took place was **to the subject.** **And the text does not comment on this separately — it only attaches the same verb to two different places.**

**In Arabic the idiom *rajaʿa ilā nafsihi* is used in the sense of "to come to oneself, to begin to think".** **So the sentence carries both a physical turning and a mental awakening.**

**إِنَّكُمْ أَنتُمُ ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ** — and the verdict they pass is the same verdict that came from their own mouths in the fifty-ninth verse.

| Verse | Sentence | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| **59** | *innahu **la-mina'ẓ-ẓālimīn*** | Of **an unknown doer** |
| **64** | *innakum antumu'**ẓ-ẓālimūn*** | Of **themselves** |

**The same word, five verses apart, turned in the opposite direction.** **I record this as an observation about wording.**

### ثُمَّ نُكِسُوا۟ عَلَىٰ رُءُوسِهِمْ

***Nukisū* — root ن-ك-س, passive.** **And the concrete sense of the root: to turn a thing over, to set it upside down.** *Nakasa'sh-shayʾ* — he put its top under. *Mankūs* — turned upside down. **From the same root *naks*: the relapse of an illness, its coming back.**

**And the verb is passive: *nukisū* — "they were turned back".** **They did not turn back of themselves.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** in the sixty-fourth verse **they themselves** turned (*rajaʿū*, active). In the sixty-fifth **they were turned** (*nukisū*, passive). **The voices of the two verbs are opposite.**

**The particle *thumma* is worth recording too: it conveys an *interval*.** Not *fāʾ* but *thumma.* **That is, there is a span between the waking and the going back.**

**عَلَىٰ رُءُوسِهِمْ** — "upon their heads". **The idiom pictures a complete inversion: feet up, head down.**

**لَقَدْ عَلِمْتَ مَا هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ يَنطِقُونَ** — and what they say is the very thing Abraham had shown them: **"these do not speak."**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the last word of the sixty-third verse:** Abraham had said *in kānū **yanṭiqūn***. The other side answers *mā hāʾulāʾi **yanṭiqūn***. **The same verb.** **That is, the sentence spoken as an objection repeats the conclusion of the proof.**

### أُفٍّ لَّكُمْ — and a comparison with al-Aḥqāf

***Uff* — a noun standing for a verb.** The lexicographers define the word as **a sound conveying weariness and disgust**; for its original sense such explanations are transmitted as "the dirt under a fingernail" or "dust blown away". **The meaning of the word is in its sound.**

**And in the Qurʾān the same word occurs at `046-ahkaf.md` on 46/17 and was treated there:** *wa'lladhī qāla li-wālidayhi **uffin lakumā***.

**The comparison of the two occurrences is verifiable and striking:**

| | **al-Aḥqāf 46/17** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/67** |
|---|---|---|
| Who says it | **A son** | **A prophet** |
| To whom | *li-wālidayhi* — **to his parents** | *lakum* — **to his people and their idols** |
| The text's verdict | **Condemned** — *ulāʾika'lladhīna ḥaqqa ʿalayhimu'l-qawl* (46/18) | **Not condemned** |

**The same word, in two places, with two opposite judgements.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the two contexts themselves:** what makes the word blameworthy is not the word itself but **to whom it is said.** In al-Aḥqāf it is said to those who have a claim; in al-Anbiyāʾ to those who have none.

**And the addressee of *uffin* is twofold: *lakum* (to you) and *limā taʿbudūn* (to what you worship).** **That is, the expression covers both the worshipper and the worshipped.**

**أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ** — **and this question for the second time in the sūra.** In the tenth verse the text itself asked it; here Abraham asks it. **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

---

## 21/68-70 — قَالُوا۟ حَرِّقُوهُ وَٱنصُرُوٓا۟ ءَالِهَتَكُمْ إِن كُنتُمْ فَٰعِلِينَ · قُلْنَا يَٰنَارُ كُونِى بَرْدًا وَسَلَٰمًا عَلَىٰٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · وَأَرَادُوا۟ بِهِۦ كَيْدًا فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمُ ٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ

*Qālū ḥarriqūhu wa'nṣurū ālihatakum in kuntum fāʿilīn · Qulnā yā nāru kūnī bardan wa salāman ʿalā Ibrāhīm · Wa arādū bihi kaydan fa-jaʿalnāhumu'l-akhsarīn*

"**They said: 'Burn him and help your gods — if you are going to do anything!'** · **We said: 'Fire! Be coolness and safety for Abraham.'** · **They meant to scheme against him, so We made them the greatest losers.**"

### وَٱنصُرُوٓا۟ ءَالِهَتَكُمْ — the sentence undoing itself

**And there is a fine point of word order in the sixty-eighth verse.**

**The people want their gods to be helped: *wa'nṣurū ālihatakum*.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the forty-third verse:** twenty-five verses earlier the sūra had said — *lā yastaṭīʿūna **naṣra** anfusihim* — "they cannot help themselves." **The same root (ن-ص-ر).**

**That is, the people's sentence, without their noticing, confirms the ruling the sūra had already set down:** a thing granted to be in need of help is counted a god.

***In kuntum fāʿilīn*** — "if you are going to do it". **And the same pattern occurred in the seventeenth verse:** *in kunnā **fāʿilīn***. **The same two words, once in the mouth of the text and once in the mouth of the people.** It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

### قُلْنَا يَٰنَارُ كُونِى بَرْدًا وَسَلَٰمًا

**And this is the most striking address in the sūra: the speech is directed at a fire.**

**The same technique was treated at `041-fussilet.md` on 41/11:** *fa-qāla lahā wa li'l-arḍi'tiyā ṭawʿan aw karhā.* **Two explanations were tabulated there** — a figurative telling (*tamthīl*), or a real address whose modality is unknown — **and no preference was imposed. I rely on that treatment; the same two explanations are transmitted for this place as well.**

**And a point of word order must be recorded — I give this as my own reading:**

**The command is not "go out".** ***Kūnī*** — "be". **That is, the fire is not removed; an attribute is given to the fire.**

| What is not said | What is said |
|---|---|
| *iṭfaʾī* — go out | ***kūnī*** — be |
| The **absence** of the fire | The **quality** of the fire |

**The sentence does not tell how the event came about; it says what was commanded.** **I do not go into the detail, because the verse does not.**

**بَرْدًا وَسَلَٰمًا** — **two words.** **And in the classical commentaries a widespread explanation is transmitted of why the second word was added:** had it said only *bard* (coolness), extreme cold would also have harmed; the note *salām* removes the harm altogether. **I transmit this as a transmitted explanation.**

**The root س-ل-م:** completeness, soundness, freedom from harm. **And this is exactly the work of the word here: *salām* is not "peace" but *harmlessness*.**

**عَلَىٰٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ** — **and a note: *ʿalā Ibrāhīm* — "for Abraham".** **That is, the coolness and safety are defined not as a general state of the fire but with respect to a particular person.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure; the verse says no more than this.**

### فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمُ ٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ — a difference of one word from al-Ṣāffāt

**And this sentence is almost word for word the same as `037-saffat.md` 37/98. The difference is in a single word.**

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/98** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/70** |
|---|---|---|
| The sentence | *fa-arādū bihi kaydan fa-jaʿalnāhumu'l-**asfalīn*** | *wa arādū bihi kaydan fa-jaʿalnāhumu'l-**akhsarīn*** |
| The last word | ***Asfalīn*** — root س-ف-ل: **the lowest** | ***Akhsarīn*** — root خ-س-ر: **the greatest losers** |
| Which axis | **Position** — up/down | **Reckoning** — gain/loss |

**Two sūras close the same event with the same sentence and change the last word.** **This is a verifiable comparison of wording.**

**And I add this as my own reading, its ground being the subjects of the two sūras:** al-Ṣāffāt is a sūra of **ranks** — its opening is *al-ṣāffāt*, its close *naḥnu'ṣ-ṣāffūn*. **A word of position fits there.** al-Anbiyāʾ is a sūra of **reckoning** — its opening is *ḥisābuhum*, its forty-seventh verse *al-mawāzīn*, *ḥāsibīn*. **A word of loss fits here.**

**I give this as a reading; I do not claim that the two sūras' choice of word was deliberate — I record only the fit.**

## 21/71-73 — وَنَجَّيْنَٰهُ وَلُوطًا إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلَّتِى بَٰرَكْنَا فِيهَا لِلْعَٰلَمِينَ · وَوَهَبْنَا لَهُۥٓ إِسْحَٰقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ نَافِلَةً وَكُلًّا جَعَلْنَا صَٰلِحِينَ · وَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ أَئِمَّةً يَهْدُونَ بِأَمْرِنَا وَأَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْهِمْ فِعْلَ ٱلْخَيْرَٰتِ وَإِقَامَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ وَإِيتَآءَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةِ وَكَانُوا۟ لَنَا عَٰبِدِينَ

*Wa najjaynāhu wa Lūṭan ila'l-arḍi'llatī bāraknā fīhā li'l-ʿālamīn · Wa wahabnā lahu Isḥāqa wa Yaʿqūba nāfilatan wa kullan jaʿalnā ṣāliḥīn · Wa jaʿalnāhum aʾimmatan yahdūna bi-amrinā wa awḥaynā ilayhim fiʿla'l-khayrāti wa iqāma'ṣ-ṣalāti wa ītāʾa'z-zakāh, wa kānū lanā ʿābidīn*

"**We delivered him and Lot to the land We had blessed for all beings.** · **We granted him Isaac, and Jacob over and above; and each of them We made righteous.** · **We made them leaders guiding by Our command, and We revealed to them the doing of good works, the establishing of prayer and the giving of alms. And they were servants to Us.**"

### ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلَّتِى بَٰرَكْنَا فِيهَا — twice in the sūra

**This expression occurs in two places in the sūra, and both are in the series of prophets:**

| Verse | Who | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **71** | Abraham and Lot | *ila'l-arḍi'llatī **bāraknā fīhā** li'l-ʿālamīn* — **the place to which they were delivered** |
| **81** | Solomon | *tajrī bi-amrihi ila'l-arḍi'llatī **bāraknā fīhā*** — **the place to which the wind ran** |

**There are ten verses between the two occurrences, and the expression is word for word the same.** **This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

**And a difference must be recorded: 71 has the note *li'l-ʿālamīn*, 81 does not.** **The name of the place is not given; the verse does not give it, and neither do I.**

**ٱلْعَٰلَمِين** — **and this word is one of the frame-words of the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **71** | *bāraknā fīhā **li'l-ʿālamīn*** |
| **91** | *wa jaʿalnāhā wa'bnahā **āyatan li'l-ʿālamīn*** |
| **107** | *wa mā arsalnāka illā **raḥmatan li'l-ʿālamīn*** |

**Three times, for three different things: a place, a sign, a messengership.** **It is a countable fact and will be tabulated at the end.**

### نَافِلَةً — what comes over and above

***Nāfila* — root ن-ف-ل: an increase added on top of what is basic.** *Nafal* — spoils (added to the basic share); **and in prayer a *nāfila* is what is added on top of the obligatory.**

**There is disagreement about what the word qualifies:**

| View | What *nāfila* qualifies |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Jacob alone** — Isaac had been asked for, Jacob was given over and above |
| 2 | **Both of them** — both were given over and above, as a gift |
| 3 | The word is a verbal noun: "as an increase" |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And a note: the verse does not name Ishmael here; it will name him in the eighty-fifth verse, in a separate group.** **I do not go into an order of succession or a detail of lineage that the Qurʾān has not given.**

### أَئِمَّةً يَهْدُونَ بِأَمْرِنَا — comparison with al-Sajda

**The word *imām* was treated at `028-kasas.md`**, where it was tabulated that the same word is used in two opposite directions (the weak being made leaders / leaders calling to the fire). **This note was relied upon at `025-furkan.md` on 25/74 as well. I rely on those treatments.**

**What I add here is that the same expression occurs at `032-secde.md` on 32/24:**

| | **al-Sajda 32/24** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/73** |
|---|---|---|
| The expression | *wa jaʿalnā minhum **aʾimmatan yahdūna bi-amrinā*** | *wa jaʿalnāhum **aʾimmatan yahdūna bi-amrinā*** |
| Who | **Some of the Children of Israel** | **Abraham, Isaac, Jacob** |
| The condition | ***lammā ṣabarū*** — when they were patient | **No condition** |
| The added note | *wa kānū bi-āyātinā yūqinūn* | *wa kānū lanā ʿābidīn* |

**The expression is word for word the same, the notes are different.** **This is a verifiable sharing of wording.**

**And the difference is worth recording as my own reading:** al-Sajda binds the leadership to **patience**, al-Anbiyāʾ to **servitude.** Both bind it to a condition; the kind of condition differs.

**وَأَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْهِمْ فِعْلَ ٱلْخَيْرَٰتِ** — and the content of the revelation is given in three verbal nouns: **doing, establishing, giving.**

**And a point of grammar: in the construct *iqāma'ṣ-ṣalāh* the *tāʾ* at the end of the verbal noun has been dropped** (*iqām*, not *iqāma*). **The grammarians explain this as the elision of the *tāʾ* on account of the annexation.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** revelation is given here not as a **piece of knowledge** but as **three acts.**

## 21/74-75 — وَلُوطًا ءَاتَيْنَٰهُ حُكْمًا وَعِلْمًا وَنَجَّيْنَٰهُ مِنَ ٱلْقَرْيَةِ ٱلَّتِى كَانَت تَّعْمَلُ ٱلْخَبَٰٓئِثَ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ قَوْمَ سَوْءٍ فَٰسِقِينَ · وَأَدْخَلْنَٰهُ فِى رَحْمَتِنَآ إِنَّهُۥ مِنَ ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ

*Wa Lūṭan ātaynāhu ḥukman wa ʿilman wa najjaynāhu mina'l-qaryati'llatī kānat taʿmalu'l-khabāʾith, innahum kānū qawma sawʾin fāsiqīn · Wa adkhalnāhu fī raḥmatinā innahu mina'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*

"**And to Lot We gave judgement and knowledge, and We delivered him from the town that did foul deeds. They were indeed a bad people, given to transgression.** · **And We admitted him into Our mercy. He was among the righteous.**"

### حُكْمًا وَعِلْمًا — twice in the sūra

**This pair occurs in two places in the sūra:**

| Verse | Who | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **74** | **Lot** | *ātaynāhu **ḥukman wa ʿilmā*** |
| **79** | **David and Solomon** | *wa kullan ātaynā **ḥukman wa ʿilmā*** |

**The same two words, five verses apart.** **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra, and I shall treat it at the seventy-ninth verse, because there it does a decisive work.**

***Ḥukm* — the root ح-ك-م:** to separate, to decide; the concrete sense of the root is **to rein in** (*ḥakama* — the bit of a horse). ***ʿIlm*** — to know. **Their being given together conveys that judgement and knowledge are two distinct things.**

**ٱلْخَبَٰٓئِث** — root **خ-ب-ث**: **what is bad, foul, loathsome.** Its opposite is *ṭayyib*. **And the word is plural and definite: *al-khabāʾith*.** **The verse does not say what the act was** — the condition of this people is set out in detail elsewhere in the Qurʾān. **This sūra does not give it; nor do I.**

**And a point of word order: the town is qualified by a verbal clause: *kānat taʿmalu'l-khabāʾith*.** **That is, the town is named not by its name but by what it did — the same pattern as *qaryatin kānat ẓālima* in the sūra's eleventh verse.** **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

**قَوْمَ سَوْءٍ** — **and this expression occurs twice in the sūra: 74 (Lot's people) and 77 (Noah's people).** **The same two words, for two different peoples.** **It is a countable fact.**

**وَأَدْخَلْنَٰهُ فِى رَحْمَتِنَا** — **and this sentence too occurs twice in the sūra: 75 (Lot) and 86 (Ishmael, Idrīs and Dhū'l-Kifl).** **In exactly the same wording.**

**And the preposition *fī* is worth recording: *adkhalnāhu fī raḥmatinā* — "We admitted him into Our mercy".** **Mercy is conceived as a place: something one enters.**

## 21/76-77 — وَنُوحًا إِذْ نَادَىٰ مِن قَبْلُ فَٱسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُۥ فَنَجَّيْنَٰهُ وَأَهْلَهُۥ مِنَ ٱلْكَرْبِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ · وَنَصَرْنَٰهُ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَآ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ قَوْمَ سَوْءٍ فَأَغْرَقْنَٰهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ

*Wa Nūḥan idh nādā min qablu fa'stajabnā lahu fa-najjaynāhu wa ahlahu mina'l-karbi'l-ʿaẓīm · Wa naṣarnāhu mina'l-qawmi'lladhīna kadhdhabū bi-āyātinā innahum kānū qawma sawʾin fa-aghraqnāhum ajmaʿīn*

"**And Noah, when he called out before: We answered him and delivered him and his household from the great distress.** · **And We helped him against the people who denied Our signs. They were indeed a bad people, and We drowned them all.**"

### نَادَىٰ — the key verb of the series

**And the first of the series' four calls is here.** **The content of the call is not given** — only the verb and its answer are recorded: *nādā* → *fa'stajabnā lah.*

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the series' first call is **without content.** The pattern is set up first and will be filled in the three that follow.

**ٱلْكَرْبِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ** — ***karb* — root ك-ر-ب.** **The sense the lexicographers give: a grief that narrows the chest and stops the breath.** *Kurba* — distress; *makrūb* — overwhelmed with distress. **And another derivative of the root is *karaba'sh-shams* — the sun's drawing near to setting: a thing's *drawing near and pressing in*.**

**So the word conveys not a danger at a distance but a distress that has come upon one.**

### وَنَصَرْنَٰهُ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْمِ — the changing of the preposition

**And a point of grammar must be recorded.**

**The verb *naṣara* in Arabic normally comes with *ʿalā*:** *naṣarahu **ʿalā** ʿaduwwih* — "he supported him against his enemy."

**Here *min* comes: *naṣarnāhu mina'l-qawm*.**

**The grammarians' explanation: by way of *taḍmīn* the verb has taken on the sense of another verb** — *manaʿa* (he protected) or *najjā* (he delivered). **That is, the sentence carries the sense not of "we helped him against them" but of "we delivered him from that people".**

**I record this as a datum of language.** **And the context confirms it: the verb *najjaynāhu* (we delivered) had already occurred in the preceding verse.**

## 21/78-79 — وَدَاوُۥدَ وَسُلَيْمَٰنَ إِذْ يَحْكُمَانِ فِى ٱلْحَرْثِ إِذْ نَفَشَتْ فِيهِ غَنَمُ ٱلْقَوْمِ وَكُنَّا لِحُكْمِهِمْ شَٰهِدِينَ · فَفَهَّمْنَٰهَا سُلَيْمَٰنَ وَكُلًّا ءَاتَيْنَا حُكْمًا وَعِلْمًا وَسَخَّرْنَا مَعَ دَاوُۥدَ ٱلْجِبَالَ يُسَبِّحْنَ وَٱلطَّيْرَ وَكُنَّا فَٰعِلِينَ

*Wa Dāwūda wa Sulaymāna idh yaḥkumāni fi'l-ḥarthi idh nafashat fīhi ghanamu'l-qawm, wa kunnā li-ḥukmihim shāhidīn · Fa-fahhamnāhā Sulaymān, wa kullan ātaynā ḥukman wa ʿilmā, wa sakhkharnā maʿa Dāwūda'l-jibāla yusabbiḥna wa'ṭ-ṭayr, wa kunnā fāʿilīn*

"**And David and Solomon, when they were giving judgement about the tilled field into which a people's sheep had strayed by night; and We were witnesses to their judging.** · **We made Solomon understand it. To each We gave judgement and knowledge. And with David We made subservient the mountains, glorifying, and the birds. It was We who did this.**"

### نَفَشَتْ — straying by night

***Nafashat* — root ن-ف-ش.** **This root is analysed here for the first time in this commentary, and it occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**The sense most of the lexicographers record: *nafsh* is the grazing of livestock at night without a herdsman, the flock's scattering and spreading out.** **And the lexicographers distinguish it from a sister word:** grazing loose by day is called *haml*. **That is, the word conveys by itself that the event happened at night.**

**Another derivative of the same root completes the picture of the word: *manfūsh* — wool that has been teased out and fluffed up.** **It is used of the mountains at `101-karia.md` on 101/5:** *ka'l-ʿihni'l-**manfūsh***. **The root carries the picture of "scattering and swelling out".**

**I give this as a note about the word:** the verse does not say that the sheep were **let loose on purpose.** The word conveys a time when control had slackened.

**ٱلْحَرْث** — root **ح-ر-ث**: to cleave the soil and sow it; *ḥarth* — a crop, a tilled field.

### وَكُنَّا لِحُكْمِهِمْ شَٰهِدِينَ — a plural pronoun

**And a point of grammar must be recorded: those judging are two (*yaḥkumāni*, dual), but the pronoun is plural: *li-ḥukm**ihim***.**

**The grammarians' explanations:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The litigants are included as well** — the judging is named together with the parties |
| 2 | In Arabic **two** is sometimes rendered with a plural pronoun |
| 3 | The pronoun covers both those judging and those who hear the judgement |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

### فَفَهَّمْنَٰهَا سُلَيْمَٰنَ — and the note the verse sets down

**And now I come to the sentence in this section that must be read most carefully.**

***Fahhamnā* — Form II (*tafʿīl*), root ف-ه-م.** **The burden of the form: to make a thing understood, to bring about its being grasped.** *Fahima* is to understand; *fahhama* is to make understand.

**The pronoun *-hā* is feminine and refers back to *ḥukm* or to *qaḍiyya* (the case).**

**What the sentence says is this: in a particular matter the understanding was given to Solomon.**

**And immediately afterwards the verse sets down a note — and this note carries the whole weight of the sentence:**

> ***Wa kullan ātaynā ḥukman wa ʿilmā*** — "**To each** We gave judgement and knowledge."

***Kullan* is in the accusative and has been brought forward as the object.** **In Arabic fronting conveys prominence.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the fronting:** immediately after telling of an understanding given to one man in a single matter, the verse says **with a fronted word** that both had judgement and knowledge.

**That is, the sentence does not set up a ranking of superiority.** **Getting a single matter right is not counted a ruling about the whole of a person.**

**And this connects with the sūra's seventy-fourth verse:** the same two words (*ḥukman wa ʿilmā*) were used there of **Lot.** **That is, in this sūra the expression is an attribute of a prophet; it is not a privilege peculiar to the David-and-Solomon scene.**

### The content of the judgement — the detail not given

**The verse does not say what the two judgements were.** Neither David's judgement nor the solution Solomon grasped appears in the text.

**In the classical commentaries there are reports that fill this gap. The verse does not give it; nor do I.**

**And what can be read from the text is this — I give it as my own reading:** what concerns the verse is **not the content of the decision but its source.** *Fa-fahhamnāhā* — the understanding was given. *Wa kunnā li-ḥukmihim shāhidīn* — the process was seen. **The whole weight of the telling is in these two sentences.**

### وَسَخَّرْنَا مَعَ دَاوُۥدَ ٱلْجِبَالَ — comparison with Ṣād

**This detail was treated at `038-sad.md` on 38/18-19.** **I rely on that treatment and set the two sūras side by side:**

| | **Ṣād 38/18-19** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/79** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb | *innā **sakhkharna**'l-jibāla maʿahu* | *wa **sakhkharnā** maʿa Dāwūda'l-jibāla* |
| The time | ***bi'l-ʿashiyyi wa'l-ishrāq*** — at evening and at sunrise | **Not given** |
| The birds | *wa'ṭ-ṭayra **maḥshūra**, kullun lahu awwāb* | *wa'ṭ-**ṭayr*** — without an attribute |
| The close | *wa shadadnā mulkahu* | ***wa kunnā fāʿilīn*** |

**Ṣād is detailed, al-Anbiyāʾ a summary.** **And the only thing al-Anbiyāʾ adds is the last sentence: *wa kunnā fāʿilīn* — "it was We who did this."**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the difference between the two sūras:** al-Anbiyāʾ shortens the **description** of the event and stresses its **doer.** **And this fits the whole of the sūra's series: the series does not tell stories, it records the source.**

***Yusabbiḥna* — the feminine plural in *nūn* (of the mountains).** **The root س-ب-ح was treated at the twentieth and thirty-third verses; it occurs here for the third time.** **In the sūra the root is in three places: 20 (those who are with Him), 33 (the heavenly bodies), 79 (the mountains).** **It is a countable fact.**

## 21/80-82 — وَعَلَّمْنَٰهُ صَنْعَةَ لَبُوسٍ لَّكُمْ لِتُحْصِنَكُم مِّنۢ بَأْسِكُمْ فَهَلْ أَنتُمْ شَٰكِرُونَ · وَلِسُلَيْمَٰنَ ٱلرِّيحَ عَاصِفَةً تَجْرِى بِأَمْرِهِۦٓ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلَّتِى بَٰرَكْنَا فِيهَا وَكُنَّا بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَٰلِمِينَ · وَمِنَ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينِ مَن يَغُوصُونَ لَهُۥ وَيَعْمَلُونَ عَمَلًا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ وَكُنَّا لَهُمْ حَٰفِظِينَ

*Wa ʿallamnāhu ṣanʿata labūsin lakum li-tuḥṣinakum min baʾsikum, fa-hal antum shākirūn · Wa li-Sulaymāna'r-rīḥa ʿāṣifatan tajrī bi-amrihi ila'l-arḍi'llatī bāraknā fīhā, wa kunnā bi-kulli shayʾin ʿālimīn · Wa mina'sh-shayāṭīni man yaghūṣūna lahu wa yaʿmalūna ʿamalan dūna dhālik, wa kunnā lahum ḥāfiẓīn*

"**We taught him the craft of making garments of mail for you, to protect you from your own violence. Are you, then, grateful?** · **And to Solomon the raging wind, running by his command to the land We had blessed. And We know all things.** · **And of the satans there were those who dived for him and did other work besides. And it was We who kept watch over them.**"

### لِتُحْصِنَكُم مِّنۢ بَأْسِكُمْ — the finest point in the verse

***Labūs* — root ل-ب-س, on the *faʿūl* pattern: a thing that is worn.** **The pattern gives the sense "the thing with which something is done"** (*rakūb* — a beast that is ridden, *ḥalūb* — a beast that is milked). **In the classical commentaries it is widely recorded that the word here means coats of mail.**

**A variant reading is transmitted:** *li-tuḥṣinakum* (its subject *labūs* or the craft), *li-yuḥṣinakum* (its subject David or God), *li-nuḥṣinakum* ("that We may protect you"). **I name no reciter. The direction of the sense changes, but those protected are the same: the addressees.**

**And now I come to the most striking place in the verse, and I record this as my own reading, its ground being the pronoun:**

***Min baʾsikum*** — "from **your** violence".

**The mail is defined not against a danger coming from outside but against the addressees' own violence.** **The verse does not say "from your enemy's force".**

| Expression | To whom it belongs |
|---|---|
| *li-tuḥṣina**kum*** — that it may protect **you** | The addressees |
| *min baʾsi**kum*** — from **your** violence | **The addressees again** |

**Both pronouns refer to the same people.** **That is, the verse names an implement of war not as a defence but as a precaution against the danger the human being produces himself.**

***Baʾs* — root ب-أ-س:** violence, hardship, might in war. **The word is used in the Qurʾān both of the force of war and of distress** — and it occurred in the twelfth verse of this sūra of the punishment: *fa-lammā aḥassū **baʾsanā***. **The same word, once ascribed to God and once to the human being.** It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**فَهَلْ أَنتُمْ شَٰكِرُونَ** — and the question is framed as a nominal sentence: *hal **antum** shākirūn* — "are you grateful ones?" **Not a verbal sentence (*hal tashkurūn*).** **The grammarians record this difference: a nominal sentence conveys fixity.** **That is, what is asked about is not a conduct but a state.**

### عَاصِفَةً — and the kind of what is given

***ʿĀṣifa* — root ع-ص-ف: to blow violently.** *ʿĀṣif* — a gale, a storm.

**And a point of word order must be recorded: the wind is qualified as *ʿāṣifa* (raging) but its verb is *tajrī* (runs) — a gentle verb.** **And its direction is fixed: *ila'l-arḍi'llatī bāraknā fīhā.***

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse says that what is violent has been **directed.** **The attribute conveys violence, the verb order.**

**And I record its connection with the thirty-fifth verse:** there the sūra had spoken of testing **with evil and with good.** **What was given to Solomon represents the *good* side of the series, and Job, who comes three verses later, the *evil* side.** **I give this as my own reading, its ground being the standing of the two stories side by side in the sūra.**

**وَكُنَّا لَهُمْ حَٰفِظِينَ** — and there is disagreement about to whom the pronoun *lahum* refers:

| View | Who *lahum* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The satans** — We watched over their work and did not let them escape |
| 2 | **Solomon and those with him** — We guarded them from the satans' harm |
| 3 | **The works done** — We guarded them from being spoiled |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

***Yaghūṣūna* — root غ-و-ص: to dive into water.** *Ghawwāṣ* — a diver. **The verse does not say what was brought up; nor do I.**

## 21/83-84 — وَأَيُّوبَ إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُۥٓ أَنِّى مَسَّنِىَ ٱلضُّرُّ وَأَنتَ أَرْحَمُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ · فَٱسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُۥ فَكَشَفْنَا مَا بِهِۦ مِن ضُرٍّ وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُ أَهْلَهُۥ وَمِثْلَهُم مَّعَهُمْ رَحْمَةً مِّنْ عِندِنَا وَذِكْرَىٰ لِلْعَٰبِدِينَ

*Wa Ayyūba idh nādā rabbahu annī massaniya'ḍ-ḍurru wa anta arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn · Fa'stajabnā lahu fa-kashafnā mā bihi min ḍurrin wa ātaynāhu ahlahu wa mithlahum maʿahum raḥmatan min ʿindinā wa dhikrā li'l-ʿābidīn*

"**And Job, when he called to his Lord: 'Affliction has touched me, and You are the most merciful of the merciful.'** · **We answered him and lifted the affliction that was on him, and gave him his household and the like of them with them — a mercy from Us and a reminder for those who serve.**"

### Comparison with Ṣād

**Job was treated in detail at `038-sad.md` on 38/41-44.** A summary of what was recorded there:

> **"Job ascribes the affliction to Satan: *massaniya'sh-shayṭān*. But the one he calls to is his Lord: *nādā rabbah*. […] That is, there is no reproach in the sentence."**

**I rely on that treatment. And now I set the two sūras side by side; the comparison is wholly a datum of wording:**

| | **Ṣād 38/41-44** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/83-84** |
|---|---|---|
| The opening | *wa'dhkur **ʿabdanā** Ayyūb* — "Our servant" | *wa **Ayyūba*** — without an attribute, with a conjunction |
| The verb | *idh **nādā** rabbah* | *idh **nādā** rabbah* |
| To whom the affliction is ascribed | ***al-shayṭān*** — *massaniya'sh-shayṭānu bi-nuṣbin wa ʿadhāb* | **To no one** — *massaniya'**ḍ-ḍurr*** |
| How the prayer ends | (It does not end; the answer comes directly) | ***wa anta arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn*** |
| The answer | *urkuḍ bi-rijlik* — **a command** | *fa'stajabnā lahu fa-kashafnā* — **an act** |
| The household | *wa **wahabnā** lahu ahlahu wa mithlahum* | *wa **ātaynāhu** ahlahu wa mithlahum* |
| A reminder for whom | *dhikrā li-**uli'l-albāb*** — for those of understanding | *dhikrā li'l-**ʿābidīn*** — for those who serve |

**Three differences must be recorded in particular.**

**The first:** in Ṣād the affliction is ascribed to **Satan**; in al-Anbiyāʾ it is ascribed **to no one.** *Massaniya'ḍ-ḍurr* — "affliction has touched me". **The agent is not named.**

**The second:** in al-Anbiyāʾ the prayer ends with an **attribute**: *wa anta arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn.* **This is the pattern of the series and will be seen once more in the eighty-ninth verse.**

**The third and most striking:** the same reminder is directed in the two sūras **to two different companies** — *uli'l-albāb* and *al-ʿābidīn*. **One looks to the intellect, the other to service.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the wording of the two sūras; and it fits the general weave of the sūras as well: al-Anbiyāʾ uses the word *ʿābidīn* twice more in the series** (73: *wa kānū lanā ʿābidīn*; 106: *li-qawmin ʿābidīn*).

### The structure of the prayer — no petition

**And this is the most striking side of the eighty-third verse.**

**There is no request in the sentence. There are only two pieces of information:**

| Part | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| *annī massaniya'ḍ-ḍurr* | **A statement of the case** — what is with me |
| *wa anta arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn* | **An attribute of the one addressed** — what is with You |

**No imperative, no optative, no "give" or "lift".**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the elements of the sentence:** the prayer contents itself with setting two states side by side. **And the eighty-fourth verse says the answer came: *fa'stajabnā lahu*.**

**And this structure will be seen once more in the eighty-seventh verse (Jonah) — there too there is no petition.** **Both prayers are without a request, and both are answered with the same sentence.** **It is a fact that can be verified within the sūra.**

**ٱلضُّرّ** — root **ض-ر-ر**: harm, distress, an evil that strikes the body or the condition. **And the word is definite: *al-ḍurr*.** **The grammarians explain the definiteness as "the known affliction" or as generic; the detail is not given.**

***Kashafnā* — root ك-ش-ف: to uncover, to lift a covering.** **And the picture of the verb is worth recording:** the affliction is **not annihilated**; **its covering is opened and lifted away.** The affliction is conceived as something laid over him.

## 21/85-86 — وَإِسْمَٰعِيلَ وَإِدْرِيسَ وَذَا ٱلْكِفْلِ كُلٌّ مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰبِرِينَ · وَأَدْخَلْنَٰهُمْ فِى رَحْمَتِنَآ إِنَّهُم مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ

*Wa Ismāʿīla wa Idrīsa wa Dha'l-Kifl, kullun mina'ṣ-ṣābirīn · Wa adkhalnāhum fī raḥmatinā innahum mina'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*

"**And Ishmael, Idrīs and Dhū'l-Kifl: each of them was among the steadfast.** · **We admitted them into Our mercy. They were among the righteous.**"

### Three names without a story

**And this is the most striking place in the series: three names are named and no event is told about any of them.**

**The only thing given is an attribute: *kullun mina'ṣ-ṣābirīn*.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the section itself:** the series comes down here to its barest form. **No call, no scene, no account of deliverance — only a sentence of belonging.**

**And the pattern *kullun mina'ṣ-ṣābirīn* is a pattern seen elsewhere in the sūra as well:** *innahu **mina'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*** (75), *innahum **mina'ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*** (86). **The series names its people by joining them to groups.**

### ذَا ٱلْكِفْل

**The Qurʾān speaks of this name in two places: here and at `038-sad.md` on 38/48** (*wa'dhkur Ismāʿīla wa'l-Yasaʿa wa Dha'l-Kifl*). **In both it occurs inside a list and no event is told.**

***Kifl* — root ك-ف-ل.** **Two directions are transmitted for the sense of the word:**

| Sense of the root | What *Dhū'l-Kifl* would mean |
|---|---|
| ***Kifl* — a share, a portion** | "The one with a share" — one to whom a portion has been given |
| ***Kafāla* — undertaking, standing surety** | "The one who undertakes" — one who takes a matter upon himself |

**And in the classical commentaries it has even been disputed whether this person was a prophet.**

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **And I enter into no detail the Qurʾān has not given — no name, no place, no event.** **The same boundary holds in this commentary for Idrīs as well: the Qurʾān tells no event about him in this sūra.**

**وَأَدْخَلْنَٰهُمْ فِى رَحْمَتِنَا** — **and the sentence of the seventy-fifth verse is repeated word for word.** **There singular (*adkhalnāhu*), here plural (*adkhalnāhum*).** **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

## 21/87-88 — وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ إِذ ذَّهَبَ مُغَٰضِبًا فَظَنَّ أَن لَّن نَّقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ فَنَادَىٰ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ أَن لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبْحَٰنَكَ إِنِّى كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · فَٱسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُۥ وَنَجَّيْنَٰهُ مِنَ ٱلْغَمِّ وَكَذَٰلِكَ نُۨجِى ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

*Wa Dha'n-Nūni idh dhahaba mughāḍiban fa-ẓanna an lan naqdira ʿalayhi fa-nādā fi'ẓ-ẓulumāti an lā ilāha illā anta subḥānaka innī kuntu mina'ẓ-ẓālimīn · Fa'stajabnā lahu wa najjaynāhu mina'l-ghamm, wa kadhālika nunji'l-muʾminīn*

"**And Dhū'l-Nūn, when he went off angry and thought We would not straiten him. Then he called out in the darknesses: 'There is no god but You. Glory be to You. I have indeed been among the wrongdoers.'** · **We answered him and delivered him from the grief. Thus do We deliver the believers.**"

### ذَا ٱلنُّون — a third naming

**And this name is the third designation the Qurʾān uses for the same person. All three are in this commentary, and the comparison is wholly a datum of wording.**

| Sūra | The designation | Its sense | The context |
|---|---|---|---|
| **al-Qalam 68/48** | *ṣāḥibu'l-**ḥūt*** | "The man of the fish / its companion" | **A negative example:** *wa lā takun ka-…* |
| **al-Ṣāffāt 37/139** | ***Yūnus*** | **His own name** | *la-mina'l-mursalīn* — as a messenger |
| **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/87** | *Dhu'**n-Nūn*** | "The man of the fish" (*nūn* — fish) | Inside the series, with his call |

**The first two columns were tabulated in detail at `037-saffat.md` on 37/139-148. I rely on that treatment.**

**And what al-Anbiyāʾ adds must be recorded: al-Qalam says *ḥūt*, al-Anbiyāʾ *nūn* — both mean fish.** **That is, two different sūras name the same person by the same means (the fish) but with two different words.**

**And a fact within the sūra: two people in this series are not named by name.**

| Verse | How named | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **87** | *Dhu'n-Nūn* — **by an attribute** | Jonah |
| **91** | *wa'llatī aḥṣanat farjahā* — **by an act** | (Her name is not given in the verse) |

**There are sixteen names in the series; two of them are unnamed.** **This is a fact countable from the text, and I shall take it up separately at the ninety-first verse.**

### إِذ ذَّهَبَ مُغَٰضِبًا — a difference of word from al-Ṣāffāt

**At `037-saffat.md` on 37/140 the verb was *abaqa*, and it was analysed in detail there:**

> **"*Ibāq* is *a slave's running away from his master without leave*. […] The word is not used in Arabic of any other kind of flight."**

**In al-Anbiyāʾ the verb is *dhahaba* — "he went".** **And the difference must be recorded:**

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/140** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/87** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb | *abaqa* — **the flight of a runaway slave** | *dhahaba* — **he went** (a neutral verb) |
| The qualification | *ila'l-fulki'l-mashḥūn* — **a direction** is given | *mughāḍiban* — **a state** is given |
| The harshness | **High** — the word by itself carries an accusation | **Low** — the verb is neutral, the state explanatory |

**Two sūras tell the same departure with two different verbs.** **I record this as an observation about wording.**

***Mughāḍiban* — root غ-ض-ب, a Form III active participle, in the accusative as a circumstantial qualifier.** **Form III (*mufāʿala*) conveys reciprocity in Arabic.** **And there is disagreement about who was angry with whom:**

| View | With whom the anger is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **With his people** — over their denial |
| 2 | **With his own situation** — over some distress |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **And I enter into no detail the Qurʾān has not given.**

### فَظَنَّ أَن لَّن نَّقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ — the centre of the disagreement

**And this sentence is one of the places handled most carefully in the classical commentaries. The source of the disagreement is the two distinct senses of the root ق-د-ر.**

| View | What *naqdira ʿalayh* means | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"That We would not straiten him"** — *qadara ʿalayh* is used in Arabic in the sense of "he narrowed his provision, he pressed him" | The Qurʾān carries the same usage: *wa man **qudira** ʿalayhi rizquh* (al-Ṭalāq 65/7); *fa-**qadara** ʿalayhi rizqah* (al-Fajr 89/16) |
| 2 | **"That We would have no power over him"** — the root's sense of "power" | The sense of the word that first comes to mind |
| 3 | The sentence is read as **a question**: "did he think We would have no power?" | The explanation of some of the grammarians |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And I enter a note about the language, and it is not a preference:** **the core sense of the root ق-د-ر was treated at `097-kadr.md`, at `054-kamer.md` on 54/49 and at `039-zumer.md` on 39/67, and it was recorded there: the core of the root is *measure*.** **From the sense "to measure" arise both "to have power" and "to narrow"** — to set a measure to a thing is to limit it. **That is, both readings are two extensions of the same root.**

### فَنَادَىٰ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ — the prayer

***Ẓulumāt* — plural: darknesses.** **In the classical commentaries several explanations are transmitted of whose darknesses they are** (of the sea, of the night, of the fish's belly). **The verse does not enumerate them; nor do I.** **What is to be recorded is that the word comes in the plural: not a single darkness.**

**And now the prayer itself. It consists of three parts, and in none of them is there a request:**

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| *lā ilāha illā **anta*** | **Affirming oneness** — it defines the one addressed |
| ***subḥānaka*** | **Declaring transcendence** — it clears the one addressed |
| *innī kuntu mina'ẓ-**ẓālimīn*** | **Admission** — it names the speaker himself |

**And the order must be recorded: first He, then I.** **Two thirds of the prayer are about the one addressed.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the elements of the sentence:** like Job's prayer in the eighty-third verse, this prayer too is **without a petition.** **Both prayers state a condition, and both are answered with the same sentence: *fa'stajabnā lah*.**

### The prayer compared with al-Ṣāffāt — and a surprising overlap

**At `037-saffat.md` on 37/143 the deliverance was tied to this sentence:**

> *fa-lawlā annahu kāna **mina'l-musabbiḥīn*** — "had he not been one of those who glorify…"

**And it was recorded there:**

> **"The verse does not say 'because he glorified', it says 'because he was one of those who glorify'. That is, what saves is not an act at that moment but a belonging."**

**Now I look at al-Anbiyāʾ and find a verifiable overlap between the two sūras:**

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/143** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/87** |
|---|---|---|
| What is said | *kāna mina'l-**musabbiḥīn*** — he was one of those who glorify | ***subḥānaka*** — "glory be to You" |
| The root | **س-ب-ح** | **س-ب-ح** |
| The form | **An attribute** — belonging to a group | **The act itself** — the words spoken |

**That is, al-Ṣāffāt says he was *among those who glorify*; al-Anbiyāʾ transmits the wording of that glorification.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the root shared by the two verses:** the two sūras do not conflict, **they complete one another.** One gives the quality, the other the text. **I do not claim that the two sūras refer to one another; what I record is the sharing of the root.**

**And the difference in telling between the two sūras can be gathered in a table:**

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/139-148** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/87-88** |
|---|---|---|
| Length | **Ten verses** | **Two verses** |
| The ship, the lot, the fish | **Present** — in detail | **Absent** |
| The text of the prayer | **Absent** | **Present** |
| The ground of the deliverance | *kāna mina'l-musabbiḥīn* — **a past attribute** | *fa'stajabnā lahu* — **the answer at that moment** |
| The people's belief | **Present** — *fa-āmanū* | **Absent** |
| Generalisation | **Absent** | **Present** — *wa kadhālika nunji'l-muʾminīn* |

**The last row must be recorded:** al-Ṣāffāt ends the story as an event; **al-Anbiyāʾ binds it to a rule.**

**وَكَذَٰلِكَ نُۨجِى ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ** — "thus do We deliver the believers."

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the sentence itself:** the verse makes a prophet's deliverance an **example.** **And this is the only sentence of generalisation in the sūra's series.**

**A note on the variant readings: differences are transmitted in the spelling and the pronunciation of the verb** (*nunjī* / *nunajjī*). **The sense does not markedly change; I name no reciter.**

## 21/89-90 — وَزَكَرِيَّآ إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُۥ رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْنِى فَرْدًا وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ ٱلْوَٰرِثِينَ · فَٱسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُۥ وَوَهَبْنَا لَهُۥ يَحْيَىٰ وَأَصْلَحْنَا لَهُۥ زَوْجَهُۥٓ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ يُسَٰرِعُونَ فِى ٱلْخَيْرَٰتِ وَيَدْعُونَنَا رَغَبًا وَرَهَبًا وَكَانُوا۟ لَنَا خَٰشِعِينَ

*Wa Zakariyyā idh nādā rabbahu rabbi lā tadharnī fardan wa anta khayru'l-wārithīn · Fa'stajabnā lahu wa wahabnā lahu Yaḥyā wa aṣlaḥnā lahu zawjah, innahum kānū yusāriʿūna fi'l-khayrāti wa yadʿūnanā raghaban wa rahaban wa kānū lanā khāshiʿīn*

"**And Zechariah, when he called to his Lord: 'My Lord! Do not leave me alone, and You are the best of inheritors.'** · **We answered him and granted him John, and made his wife fit for him. They used to vie in good works and to call upon Us in longing and in awe. And they were humble before Us.**"

### لَا تَذَرْنِى فَرْدًا — the fourth call of the series

**And this is the last of the series' four calls. And it differs from the first three in one respect: here there is a petition.**

| Verse | The call | Is there a petition |
|---|---|---|
| **76** | Noah | (The content is not given) |
| **83** | Job | **None** — a statement of case + an attribute |
| **87** | Jonah | **None** — oneness + transcendence + admission |
| **89** | **Zechariah** | **Present** — *lā tadharnī fardan* |

**But even the petition is framed in the negative: not "give" but ***"do not leave"***.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the mood of the verb:** the sentence does not ask for something — it asks that a state **not continue.** **And the word *fard* says not what is wanted but what is feared.**

***Fard* — root ف-ر-د: single, without like or equal, alone.** **And the word differs from *waḥd*: *wāḥid* is one in number; *fard* is one who has no one beside him.**

**وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ ٱلْوَٰرِثِينَ** — **and the prayer again ends with an attribute.**

**This is exactly the pattern of the eighty-third verse:**

| Verse | The close | The pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **83** | *wa **anta** arḥamu'r-rāḥimīn* | *wa anta* + **an elative** + the plural of the same root |
| **89** | *wa **anta** khayru'l-wārithīn* | *wa anta* + **an elative** + a plural |

**The same pattern, two prayers, six verses apart.** **It is a fact that can be verified within the sūra.**

**And the choice of the attribute is worth recording — I give this as my own reading:** what is asked for is **a child**; the attribute chosen is ***wārith*** — inheritor. **That is, having voiced its request, the prayer says within itself that no lack would arise even if the request were not granted.** **The sentence sets a limit to its own petition.**

### وَأَصْلَحْنَا لَهُۥ زَوْجَهُۥ — a disagreement

**There is disagreement about what the sentence conveys:**

| View | In what sense *iṣlāḥ* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Bodily fitness** — she was made able to bear |
| 2 | **Temper and condition** — she was brought to a good state |
| 3 | The word conveys a general "making fit"; the detail is not given |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **And I enter into no detail the Qurʾān has not given.**

### رَغَبًا وَرَهَبًا — a calling in two directions

**The two words are in the accusative; the grammarians count them either circumstantial qualifiers or objects of purpose: "longing and fearing" or "out of longing and out of fear".**

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **رَغَب** | ر-غ-ب | **To turn towards with desire** — *raghiba fīhi*: he desired it |
| **رَهَب** | ر-ه-ب | **To shrink back with a shudder** — *rahba*: a fear that goes inward |

**And the roots convey movement in opposite directions: one *towards it*, the other *back from it*.** **In Arabic *raghiba fī* (he turned towards it) and *raghiba ʿan* (he turned away from it) are distinguished by the preposition; there is a direction in the root itself.**

**I give this as a note about the words:** the verse does not describe prayer as a movement **in one direction.** **Two opposite inclinations meet in the same act.**

**يُسَٰرِعُونَ فِى ٱلْخَيْرَٰتِ** — ***yusāriʿūn* — Form III: to run as though racing.** **And the word *al-khayrāt* occurred in the seventy-third verse:** *wa awḥaynā ilayhim **fiʿla'l-khayrāt***. **The same word, at the two ends of the series.** **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

**خَٰشِعِين** — root **خ-ش-ع**: **to be lowered, to bow, for the voice to drop.** **The concrete picture of the word: the eye's falling to the ground, the voice's dropping.** **And the subjects of the eighty-ninth and ninetieth verses are plural (*innahum*) — that is, the ruling covers Zechariah, John and his wife together.**

## 21/91 — وَٱلَّتِىٓ أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهَا مِن رُّوحِنَا وَجَعَلْنَٰهَا وَٱبْنَهَآ ءَايَةً لِّلْعَٰلَمِينَ

*Wa'llatī aḥṣanat farjahā fa-nafakhnā fīhā min rūḥinā wa jaʿalnāhā wa'bnahā āyatan li'l-ʿālamīn*

"**And her who guarded her chastity: We breathed into her of Our spirit and made her and her son a sign for all beings.**"

### The form of the naming

**And the last link of the series is the shortest: a single verse.**

**And the first thing to be recorded about the verse is this: no name is given. The person named comes with a relative pronoun: *wa'llatī* — "and she who".**

**This happens for the second time in the sūra's series:**

| Verse | How named | According to what |
|---|---|---|
| **87** | *Dhu'n-Nūn* | According to **what happened to him** |
| **91** | *wa'llatī aḥṣanat farjahā* | According to **what she did** |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the shared form of the two verses:** of the two people in the series left unnamed, one is named by an **event** and the other by an **act.** **And in the second case the naming rests on the person's own deed.**

**The same expression occurs in the Qurʾān at `066-tahrim.md` on 66/12 and was treated there.** **I rely on that treatment and set the two verses side by side:**

| | **al-Taḥrīm 66/12** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/91** |
|---|---|---|
| The naming | ***Maryama'bnata ʿImrān**a'llatī aḥṣanat farjahā* — **by name** | *wa'**llatī** aḥṣanat farjahā* — **unnamed** |
| The breathing | *fa-nafakhnā **fīhi*** — a masculine pronoun | *fa-nafakhnā **fīhā*** — a feminine pronoun |
| The added note | *wa ṣaddaqat bi-kalimāti rabbihā wa kutubihi wa kānat mina'l-qānitīn* | *wa jaʿalnāhā wa'bnahā āyatan li'l-ʿālamīn* |

**And the difference between the two pronouns is recorded in the classical commentaries and is disputed:**

| View | To what *fīhi* (al-Taḥrīm) refers |
|---|---|
| 1 | **To the opening of her garment** — hence masculine |
| 2 | To *farj* — the word has been treated as masculine |
| 3 | The two verses tell the same thing from two different sides; the difference of pronoun does not change the sense |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

### أَحْصَنَتْ — the subject of the verb

***Aḥṣanat* — root ح-ص-ن, Form IV, active.**

***Ḥiṣn* — a fortress;** *ḥaṣīn* — firm, well-defended. **The core sense of the root: to make a thing defensible, to make it firm.**

**And the voice of the verb must be recorded — I give this as my own reading:**

**The verb is not passive but active.** Not *uḥṣinat* (she was guarded) but ***aḥṣanat*** (she guarded). **That is, the verse records the being guarded as a person's own act.**

**And its place in the series is understood by this voice:** in the series' other links the prophets were named **by calling out**; here the person named is named **by an act.** **The common point is that both are the person's own deed.**

**I record this in measured language and go no further:** the verse conveys an attribute, it does not give a description. **No detail the Qurʾān has not given is entered into.**

**مِن رُّوحِنَا** — **and here I enter a necessary note.**

**The note of transcendence set down at `039-zumer.md` on 39/67 for a similar annexation holds here too:** these expressions cannot be understood **in a way that ascribes a part, a portion or an organ to God.**

**The explanation the lexicographers and the exegetes widely record: the annexation here is an annexation of honour** — that is, in saying "Our spirit" what is meant is a relation conveying the **honour** of that thing; the same structure is present in the Qurʾān in expressions such as *baytī* (My house) and *nāqatu'llāh* (the she-camel of God). **I transmit this as a transmitted explanation.**

**And in the classical commentaries what the *rūḥ* is has been disputed. This commentary does not enter into that debate; what the verse gives is a relation, not a definition.**

### ءَايَةً لِّلْعَٰلَمِينَ — a singular "sign"

**And a point of grammar must be recorded: two people are named (*jaʿalnāhā wa'bnahā*) but the sign is singular: *āyatan*, not *āyatayni*.**

**The grammarians' explanation: the two together constitute a single sign** — not two separate signs.

**I record this as an observation about the structure and do not go into its detail.**

**لِّلْعَٰلَمِين** — **and the word for the third time in the sūra.** (71, 91, 107.) **The series closes here, and in the next verse the sūra sets down its verdict.**

## 21/92-93 — إِنَّ هَٰذِهِۦٓ أُمَّتُكُمْ أُمَّةً وَٰحِدَةً وَأَنَا۠ رَبُّكُمْ فَٱعْبُدُونِ · وَتَقَطَّعُوٓا۟ أَمْرَهُم بَيْنَهُمْ كُلٌّ إِلَيْنَا رَٰجِعُونَ

*Inna hādhihi ummatukum ummatan wāḥidatan wa anā rabbukum fa'budūn · Wa taqaṭṭaʿū amrahum baynahum, kullun ilaynā rājiʿūn*

"**This community of yours is one single community, and I am your Lord; so serve Me.** · **Yet they cut their affair into pieces among themselves. All of them will return to Us.**"

### The verdict of the series

**And the ninety-second verse is the conclusion of a series that has run for forty-four verses. I record this as my own reading, its ground being the verse's place in the sūra:** sixteen names have been ranged one after another, and for none of them is a separate religion or a separate call mentioned. **And when the series ends, one single sentence comes.**

**The verse points back by saying *hādhihi* (this).** **There is disagreement about what the demonstrative refers to:**

| View | What *hādhihi* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The company of the prophets named** — "this company is your company" |
| 2 | **This religion or way** — *umma* here means "a path, a religion followed" |
| 3 | **The call to oneness** — the one call repeated across the series |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

***Umma* — root أ-م-م.** **The core sense of the root: to be in front, to be that towards which one turns.** *Imām* (a leader), *amām* (in front), *umm* (a mother) are from the same root. **And *umma*, by the lexicographers' definition, is a company gathered around something — or a way that is followed.**

**And a point of grammar:** *ummatan wāḥidatan* is in the **accusative**, and the grammarians count it a **circumstantial qualifier.** **That is, the sentence is not "this is your community and it is one" but "this is your community in the state of being one".** **Unity is given not as an attribute but as a *state*.**

**وَأَنَا۠ رَبُّكُمْ فَٱعْبُدُونِ** — **and this sentence repeats the close of the twenty-fifth verse:** *annahu lā ilāha illā ana **fa'budūn***.

| Verse | The sentence | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **25** | *lā ilāha illā ana **fa'budūn*** | **Before the series** — the revelation given to all the messengers |
| **92** | *wa anā rabbukum **fa'budūn*** | **After the series** — the verdict |

**The same command, at the two ends of the series.** **This is a frame that can be verified within the sūra, and I record as my own reading: the series has been set between these two sentences.**

### وَتَقَطَّعُوٓا۟ أَمْرَهُم بَيْنَهُمْ

***Taqaṭṭaʿū* — root ق-ط-ع, Form V (*tafaʿʿul*).** **And the burden of the form must be recorded:** Form V here conveys **multiplicity and gradualness** — *qaṭaʿa* is to cut, *qaṭṭaʿa* is to cut much, and *taqaṭṭaʿa* is **to fall or break into pieces.**

**And the verb is used transitively here:** *taqaṭṭaʿū amrahum* — "they cut their affair into pieces". It has an object.

**I record this as an observation about the structure:** the sentence does not say "they parted"; **it says "they cut their affair into pieces".** **That is, what is cut into pieces is not the community but the *affair*** — and the community splits after it.

### The order of the disagreement — with al-Shūrā and al-Baqara

**This subject was treated in detail at `042-sura.md` on 42/13-14 and at `002-bakara.md` on 2/213, and the two verses were compared point by point. I rely on those treatments.**

**The note at al-Shūrā was this:**

> **"What is shared: the note of time — *min baʿdi mā jāʾahum*, 'after it had come to them'; the cause — *baghyan baynahum*, 'the envy among them'."**

**The note at al-Baqara was this:**

> **"That is, the cause of the division is not presented as ignorance. The text says it plainly: *min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu'l-bayyināt* — after the clear proofs had come."**

**Now I set the three verses side by side:**

| | **al-Baqara 2/213** | **al-Shūrā 42/14** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/92-93** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What comes before | *kāna'n-nāsu ummatan wāḥidah* | *sharaʿa lakum mina'd-dīn…* — **a command** | *inna hādhihi ummatukum ummatan wāḥidah* |
| The verb | *ikhtalafa* — they fell into difference | *tafarraqū* — they broke apart | ***taqaṭṭaʿū*** — they cut into pieces |
| The note of time | *min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu'l-bayyināt* | *min baʿdi mā jāʾahumu'l-ʿilm* | **Absent** |
| The cause | *baghyan baynahum* | *baghyan baynahum* | **Absent** |
| What follows | God guided the believers to the truth | The judgement was deferred | ***kullun ilaynā rājiʿūn*** |

**And al-Anbiyāʾ's difference must be recorded: the note of time and the cause are not stated.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the structure of the sūra:** al-Baqara and al-Shūrā say within the sentence that the disagreement arose **after the proof.** **al-Anbiyāʾ does not say it in the sentence, because the sūra itself says it:** verses forty-eight to ninety-one **are the proof itself.** Sixteen names have been ranged, and in all of them the same call has been shown. **And the sentence about the breaking apart has been set immediately after that list.**

**That is, three sūras give the same fact in three different ways: two by wording, one by structure.** **I offer this as a reading; the wording of the three verses is verifiable, the interpretation of the structure is mine.**

**كُلٌّ إِلَيْنَا رَٰجِعُونَ** — ***kull* with nunation, followed by a predicate in the plural of rational beings.** **And the verb occurred in the sūra's thirty-fifth verse:** *wa ilaynā **turjaʿūn***. **The same root (ر-ج-ع), one a verb and one a noun.** **And the same root occurred in the sixty-fourth verse too (*fa-rajaʿū ilā anfusihim*).** **The root is in three places in the sūra: 35, 64, 93.** **It is a countable fact.**

## 21/94-95 — فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِنَ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ فَلَا كُفْرَانَ لِسَعْيِهِۦ وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ كَٰتِبُونَ · وَحَرَٰمٌ عَلَىٰ قَرْيَةٍ أَهْلَكْنَٰهَآ أَنَّهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ

*Fa-man yaʿmal mina'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti wa huwa muʾminun fa-lā kufrāna li-saʿyih, wa innā lahu kātibūn · Wa ḥarāmun ʿalā qaryatin ahlaknāhā annahum lā yarjiʿūn*

"**Whoever does anything of righteous works as a believer, his striving will not be denied; and We are writing it down for him.** · **And it is forbidden to a town We have destroyed that they should not return.**"

### كُفْرَان — the covering over of labour

***Kufrān* — root ك-ف-ر, on the *fuʿlān* pattern.** **And the core sense of the root is to cover:** *kāfir* is used even of the farmer who covers the seed. **The pattern turns the root's core sense into a process: *kufrān* — to cover a thing over, to treat it as nothing.**

**And the object of the verse must be recorded: what is refused to be covered over is not faith but *saʿy*** — striving.

***Saʿy* — root س-ع-ي: to walk fast, to hurry about, to spend effort on a task.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the choice of the word:** the verse does not say "his deed will not go for nothing". It says **his *striving*** will not be covered over. **That is, the recompense is tied not to the outcome but to the effort spent.**

**وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ كَٰتِبُونَ** — **and there is a fine point in the preposition *lahu*: "we are writers for him."** Not *ʿalayhi* but *lahu*. **That is, the record is kept not against the person but in his favour.**

**I record this as an observation about the structure.**

### 21/95 — the most disputed sentence in the sūra

**And this verse is one of the sentences most dwelt on in the classical commentaries. The difficulty lies in the standing together of the word *ḥarām* and the negation *lā*.**

**I give the disagreement in a table:**

| View | What *lā* is | The sense of the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ***Lā* is redundant** (it does not affect the sense) | "It is **impossible** for a town We have destroyed **to return**" — they cannot come back to this world |
| 2 | ***Lā* is original**, and *ḥarām* here conveys **impossibility** (*imtināʿ*) | "It is impossible that they **should not return**" — that is, **they will certainly return** (for the reckoning) |
| 3 | *Lā* is original, and the return is a return **to repentance or belief** | "It is settled that they will not turn back (from unbelief)" |

**All three explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries. I do not impose a preference.**

**A variant reading is transmitted as well: *wa ḥarāmun* and *wa ḥirmun*.** **Both readings carry the sense "forbidden, barred"; I name no reciter.**

**And I enter a note about the context, and it is not a preference:** the next verse begins with *ḥattā idhā…* — that is, the ninety-fifth verse is **syntactically bound to the ninety-sixth.** *Ḥattā* conveys the **final limit** of a process. **The completion of the sentence is in the next verse, and this affords some support to the second reading.** **It is not eliminative.**

## 21/96-97 — حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا فُتِحَتْ يَأْجُوجُ وَمَأْجُوجُ وَهُم مِّن كُلِّ حَدَبٍ يَنسِلُونَ · وَٱقْتَرَبَ ٱلْوَعْدُ ٱلْحَقُّ فَإِذَا هِىَ شَٰخِصَةٌ أَبْصَٰرُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ يَٰوَيْلَنَا قَدْ كُنَّا فِى غَفْلَةٍ مِّنْ هَٰذَا بَلْ كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ

*Ḥattā idhā futiḥat Yaʾjūju wa Maʾjūju wa hum min kulli ḥadabin yansilūn · Wa'qtaraba'l-waʿdu'l-ḥaqqu fa-idhā hiya shākhiṣatun abṣāru'lladhīna kafarū, yā waylanā qad kunnā fī ghaflatin min hādhā bal kunnā ẓālimīn*

"**Until, when Gog and Magog are let loose and they come streaming down from every height,** · **and the true promise draws near, then behold: the eyes of those who denied are fixed and staring: 'Woe to us! We were in heedlessness of this — no, we were wrongdoers!'**"

### يَأْجُوجُ وَمَأْجُوجُ — a boundary

**The Qurʾān speaks of these two names here and in Sūrat al-Kahf (18/94). They do not occur anywhere else.**

**And about their identity, their place or their number the Qurʾān says nothing.** **I record this per the method (`STYLE.md`): that gap will not be filled in this commentary.** Various explanations are found in the classical commentaries and in the history books; **not one of them is information the Qurʾān has given, and none is transmitted in this commentary.**

**And a point of grammar:** the verb *futiḥat* (was opened) has come in the feminine; the grammarians attach this to an elided governing noun such as *sadd* (the barrier) or *jiha* (the direction). **A variant reading is transmitted as well: *futiḥat* and *futtiḥat* (with doubling).** **The second conveys multiplicity and emphasis; I name no reciter.**

**حَدَب** — root **ح-د-ب**: **a height, a mound, a place swelling up like a back.** *Aḥdab* — hunchbacked. **In the picture of the word there is a *swelling*.**

**يَنسِلُون** — root **ن-س-ل**: **to advance fast and in succession.** **And from the same root *nasl* — offspring, progeny: what comes one after another.** **That is, the verb conveys coming in succession, not one by one.**

**I give this as a note about the word.**

### وَٱقْتَرَبَ ٱلْوَعْدُ ٱلْحَقُّ — the sūra's frame closes

**And now I come to the sūra's plainest structural fact. It is wholly a datum of the text.**

**The first verse was this:**

> *Iqtaraba li'n-nāsi ḥisābuhum wa hum **fī ghaflatin** muʿriḍūn*

**The ninety-seventh verse is this:**

> *Wa'**qtaraba**'l-waʿdu'l-ḥaqq … qad kunnā **fī ghaflatin** min hādhā*

| | **21/1** | **21/97** |
|---|---|---|
| The verb | *iqtaraba* | *wa'**qtaraba*** |
| What draws near | *ḥisābuhum* — their reckoning | *al-waʿdu'l-ḥaqq* — the true promise |
| The heedlessness | *wa hum **fī ghaflatin*** — **the text says it** | *qad kunnā **fī ghaflatin*** — **they say it themselves** |

**Both words are word for word the same: *iqtaraba* and *fī ghaflatin*.** **Ninety-six verses later, the sūra puts its opening sentence into the mouth of the one addressed.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the table above:** the sūra's first sentence was a **finding**; in the last section the same finding comes back as an **admission.** **And the only thing that has changed in between is who says it.**

**شَٰخِصَة** — root **ش-خ-ص**: **for a thing to rise up from its place, to be set up and stay fixed.** *Shakhṣ* — a standing shape seen from afar. **And when used of the eye: to be fixed on a point without blinking.**

**And a point of word order: *fa-idhā hiya shākhiṣatun abṣāru…*** — the grammarians count the *hiya* here a **pronoun of the case** (*ḍamīr al-shaʾn*): "and then, the case is that…". **And the *idhā* is again the *idhā* of surprise: "and behold."**

**بَلْ كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ** — **and the sūra's refrain for the third time.** **But here there is an addition, and it must be recorded: the admission is corrected.**

***Bal*** — the particle that abandons the preceding statement and puts a new one in its place. **The sentence first says "we were heedless", then takes it back with *bal* and says "no, we were wrongdoers".**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the particle:** the admission removes its own excuse. **Heedlessness could have been a defence; *bal* cancels it.**

**And the comparison with the fourteenth verse confirms this:**

| Verse | The admission |
|---|---|
| **14** | *yā waylanā **innā kunnā ẓālimīn*** — direct |
| **46** | *yā waylanā **innā kunnā ẓālimīn*** — direct |
| **97** | *yā waylanā qad kunnā fī ghaflatin min hādhā **bal kunnā ẓālimīn*** — **first an excuse, then a correction** |

**The third occurrence is set apart from the first two by a *bal*.** **It is a fact that can be verified within the sūra.**

## 21/98-100 — إِنَّكُمْ وَمَا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ حَصَبُ جَهَنَّمَ أَنتُمْ لَهَا وَٰرِدُونَ · لَوْ كَانَ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ ءَالِهَةً مَّا وَرَدُوهَا وَكُلٌّ فِيهَا خَٰلِدُونَ · لَهُمْ فِيهَا زَفِيرٌ وَهُمْ فِيهَا لَا يَسْمَعُونَ

*Innakum wa mā taʿbudūna min dūni'llāhi ḥaṣabu jahannam, antum lahā wāridūn · Law kāna hāʾulāʾi ālihatan mā waradūhā, wa kullun fīhā khālidūn · Lahum fīhā zafīrun wa hum fīhā lā yasmaʿūn*

"**You and what you worship besides God are the fuel of Hell; you will go down to it.** · **Had these been gods, they would not have gone down to it. And all of them will abide in it.** · **There they will have a deep groaning, and there they will hear nothing.**"

### حَصَب — the picture of the word

***Ḥaṣab* — root ح-ص-ب.** **The sense the lexicographers give: what is thrown into a fire — pebbles, wood, an object cast in.** *Ḥaṣaba* — he threw pebbles; *ḥaṣbāʾ* — small stones.

**And the same root was treated in the form *ḥāṣib* at `054-kamer.md` on 54/34, at `067-mulk.md` on 67/17 and at `029-ankebut.md` on 29/40:** **a violent wind that rains down stones.** **I rely on those treatments.**

**And the work of the word here must be recorded: the things worshipped are named as *fuel*** — that is, as what feeds a fire, what is thrown into it. **The word gives the end of objects once exalted in the language of raw material.**

### مَا تَعْبُدُونَ — the limit set by the particle *mā*

**And here there is an important point of language recorded in the classical commentaries.**

**The particle in the verse is ***mā*** — not *men*.** **And in Arabic *mā* is used of those without intellect; *men* is for those possessed of intellect.**

**This determines the scope of the verse, and it is mentioned in the classical commentaries as an answer to this objection:** "In the past there were also **beings possessed of intellect** who were worshipped — do they too fall under this ruling?" **The answer is one of language: the particle *mā* does not cover them.**

**I transmit this as a datum of language and add this:** the ruling of the verse is directed at **the objects worshipped** and at **those who worship.** **I record this per the method (`STYLE.md`): what is named here is not a community but an act and its object.**

### 21/99 — the form of the proof

**And the ninety-ninth verse is on the same pattern as the twenty-second. Both begin with *law*.**

| Verse | The condition | The answer | The axis of the proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| **22** | *law kāna fīhimā ālihatun illa'llāh* | *la-fasadatā* | **Order** — the breaking down of order |
| **99** | *law kāna hāʾulāʾi ālihatan* | *mā waradūhā* | **Outcome** — their not entering the fire |

**The same particle, the same subject, two different proofs.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the shared pattern of the two verses:** the sūra tests the claim of godhood from two ends — **at the beginning from order, at the end from outcome.**

### زَفِير — and a word shared with al-Furqān

***Zafīr* — root ز-ف-ر:** **a rasping breath drawn from deep within**, the sound made as the chest expands and contracts.

**The word was treated at `025-furkan.md` on 25/12** (*samiʿū lahā taghayyuẓan wa zafīrā*), **and it was recorded there:** *"both words have to do with sound, and both are sounds belonging to a living thing."* **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the difference between the two sūras must be recorded:**

| | **al-Furqān 25/12** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/100** |
|---|---|---|
| Who makes the sound | **The fire** — *samiʿū **lahā** … zafīrā* | **Those who enter** — ***lahum** fīhā zafīr* |
| Who hears | **Those who enter** hear it | ***lā yasmaʿūn*** — they do not hear |

**The same word, in two sūras, with the parties that make the sound and that hear it exchanged.** **I record this as an observation about wording.**

**وَهُمْ فِيهَا لَا يَسْمَعُونَ** — **there is disagreement about its sense:**

| View | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **They hear nothing at all** — they are made deaf |
| 2 | **They hear nothing** that would gladden them |
| 3 | They do not hear one another — because of the din |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And I record a link within the sūra:** in the forty-fifth verse it said *wa lā yasmaʿu'ṣ-ṣummu'd-duʿāʾa idhā mā yundharūn* — "the deaf do not hear the call when they are warned." **The same verb (س-م-ع), the same negation.** **The sūra matches the sentence about those who do not hear in this world with the sentence about those who do not hear in the hereafter.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the verb the two verses share.**

## 21/101-103 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ سَبَقَتْ لَهُم مِّنَّا ٱلْحُسْنَىٰٓ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ عَنْهَا مُبْعَدُونَ · لَا يَسْمَعُونَ حَسِيسَهَا وَهُمْ فِى مَا ٱشْتَهَتْ أَنفُسُهُمْ خَٰلِدُونَ · لَا يَحْزُنُهُمُ ٱلْفَزَعُ ٱلْأَكْبَرُ وَتَتَلَقَّىٰهُمُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ هَٰذَا يَوْمُكُمُ ٱلَّذِى كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ

*Inna'lladhīna sabaqat lahum minna'l-ḥusnā ulāʾika ʿanhā mubʿadūn · Lā yasmaʿūna ḥasīsahā wa hum fī ma'shtahat anfusuhum khālidūn · Lā yaḥzunuhumu'l-fazaʿu'l-akbaru wa tatalaqqāhumu'l-malāʾikah, hādhā yawmukumu'lladhī kuntum tūʿadūn*

"**As for those for whom the best has already gone forth from Us, they will be kept far from it.** · **They will not hear even its faintest sound, and they will abide in what their souls desire.** · **The greatest terror will not grieve them, and the angels will meet them: 'This is your day, the one you were promised.'**"

### Two groups, the same verb

**And these three verses are the exact counterpart of the three before them. The contrast is built in the words:**

| Element | **98-100 (the first group)** | **101-103 (the second group)** |
|---|---|---|
| Relation to the fire | *antum lahā **wāridūn*** — those who go down to it | *ulāʾika ʿanhā **mubʿadūn*** — those kept far from it |
| Hearing | *wa hum fīhā **lā yasmaʿūn*** | *lā yasmaʿūna **ḥasīsahā*** |
| Duration | *wa kullun fīhā **khālidūn*** | *wa hum … **khālidūn*** |

**And the second row must be recorded in particular — I give this as my own reading:**

**The same verb (*lā yasmaʿūn*) is used of both groups, but in two different senses:**

| Group | What *lā yasmaʿūn* means |
|---|---|
| **Those who go down (100)** | While inside it they **do not hear** — a deprivation |
| **Those kept far (102)** | They do not hear even its faintest sound — a **protection** |

**The same negation: one conveys a lack, the other a safety.**

**حَسِيس** — root **ح-س-س**: **the faintest trace perceived by the senses.** *Ḥiss* — sense; *ḥasīs* — **the slightest sound that reaches the ear, a whisper, a rustle.**

**And the word is from the same root as the verb in the sūra's twelfth verse:** *fa-lammā **aḥassū** baʾsanā* — "when they felt Our might". **The same root: one conveys a sensing, the other a not-sensing.** **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the shared root:** in the twelfth verse a people who **felt** the punishment fled. In the hundred and second a people is described who do not even **sense** the faintest sound of it. **Two states, the two ends of the same root.**

**سَبَقَتْ لَهُم مِّنَّا ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ** — ***sabaqat* — root س-ب-ق: to go ahead, to come first.** **And the subject of the verb is *al-ḥusnā*: the best, which has come first.**

**And there is again a fine point in the preposition *lahum*: "for them".** **And the note *minnā* conveys the source.** **That is, the sentence says three things at once: time (before), direction (for them), source (from Us).**

**ٱلْفَزَعُ ٱلْأَكْبَر** — root **ف-ز-ع**: sudden fear, a start of alarm. **And the attribute is an elative: *al-akbar* — the greatest.** **What is meant is not said; several explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries. The verse does not give it; nor do I.**

**تَتَلَقَّىٰهُمُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَة** — ***tatalaqqā* — root ل-ق-ي, Form V: to meet, to receive.** **And the form conveys that the meeting is done *by going towards*.**

## 21/104 — يَوْمَ نَطْوِى ٱلسَّمَآءَ كَطَىِّ ٱلسِّجِلِّ لِلْكُتُبِ كَمَا بَدَأْنَآ أَوَّلَ خَلْقٍ نُّعِيدُهُۥ وَعْدًا عَلَيْنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا فَٰعِلِينَ

*Yawma naṭwi's-samāʾa ka-ṭayyi's-sijilli li'l-kutub, kamā badaʾnā awwala khalqin nuʿīduh, waʿdan ʿalaynā, innā kunnā fāʿilīn*

"**The day We roll up the sky as a scroll is rolled up over its writings. As We began the first creation, so shall We bring it back — a promise binding on Us. We shall surely do it.**"

### طَىّ — rolling up

***Ṭayy* — root ط-و-ي.** **Its sense: to fold a thing and wind it up, to roll it.** *Ṭawā'ṣ-ṣaḥīfa* — he rolled up the sheet. **Its opposite is *nashr*: to open, to spread out.**

**And the same root was treated at `039-zumer.md` on 39/67:** *wa's-samāwātu **maṭwiyyātun** bi-yamīnih.* **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the note of transcendence set down there holds here too and must be repeated.** The note at al-Zumar was this:

> **"These expressions cannot be understood in a way that ascribes an organ, a body or a place to God. […] Two positions are transmitted in the classical commentaries: accepting the wording as it stands and leaving its modality to God (*tafwīḍ*), or interpreting it in the sense of power and complete disposal (*taʾwīl*). I transmit both and impose no preference."**

**And the difference between the two verses must be recorded:**

| | **al-Zumar 39/67** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/104** |
|---|---|---|
| The form | *maṭwiyyātun* — **a passive participle** | *naṭwī* — **a verb, first person plural** |
| The qualifying note | *bi-yamīnih* | *ka-ṭayyi's-sijilli li'l-kutub* — **a comparison** |
| What follows | *subḥānahu wa taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn* — **a declaration of transcendence** | *kamā badaʾnā awwala khalqin nuʿīduh* — **a rule** |

**That is, al-Zumar sets a declaration of transcendence after the description, while al-Anbiyāʾ sets a rule after the comparison.**

### ٱلسِّجِلّ — a disputed word

***Sijill* — root س-ج-ل.** **And there is a real disagreement in the classical commentaries about what the word is. I give it in a table:**

| View | What *sijill* is | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A scroll of writing / a sheet** | The paper or hide that is written on and rolled up |
| 2 | **The one who writes (a scribe)** | The scribe who rolls up the writings; on this reading *li'l-kutub* becomes "for what he has written" |
| 3 | **A register** | The ledger into which records are entered |

**All three explanations are transmitted. I do not impose a preference.**

**And there is disagreement about the *lām* in the phrase *li'l-kutub* as well:**

| View | What the *lām* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Of cause** — "for the writings", that is, in order to preserve them |
| 2 | **In the sense of *ʿalā*** — "over the writings" |
| 3 | **Redundant** — marking the object: "as it rolls up the writings" |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**There is a note about this root at `083-mutaffifin.md`:** it was recorded there that some connect it with the word *sijjīl*, but that this is held weak and that **the two words come from different roots.** **I rely on that treatment.**

### Again a boundary

**I draw the boundary here too, per the method (`STYLE.md`), because this verse as well has been matched with cosmological models.**

**I derive from the verse no physical model of the end of the universe.** **My reason is the same as the reasons I gave at the thirtieth verse, and one of them is particularly strong here:**

**The verse itself sets up a comparison: *ka-ṭayyi…* — "as … is rolled up".** ***Kāf*** is the particle of comparison. **That is, the sentence says not how the event will happen but what it is like.** **To derive physics from a simile is to fail to see what a simile is.**

**And the other side of the comparison is a piece of writing — not a heavenly body.** **The words are taken from the world of writing and paper.**

**I record this as my own reading.**

### كَمَا بَدَأْنَآ أَوَّلَ خَلْقٍ نُّعِيدُهُ

**There is disagreement about the parsing of the sentence:**

| View | The construction | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Kamā* is comparative, *awwala khalqin* the object | "As We began the first creation, so shall We bring it back" |
| 2 | *Awwala khalqin* is adverbial | "As it was at the first creation" |
| 3 | *Mā* is the *mā* of the verbal noun | "We shall bring it back as We began the creating" |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**And what the sentence builds must be recorded: an analogy.** The second creation is likened to the first. **That is, the proof is taken not from the future but from the past.**

**وَعْدًا عَلَيْنَا** — ***waʿdan* is in the accusative, an emphatic verbal noun.** **And there is a fine point in the preposition *ʿalaynā*: *ʿalā* in Arabic conveys obligation.** **That is, the word is not merely a promise — it is named as a burden taken on.**

**إِنَّا كُنَّا فَٰعِلِينَ** — **and this expression occurs for the fourth time in the sūra.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **17** | *in kunnā **fāʿilīn*** | **The text** — inside a condition |
| **68** | *in kuntum **fāʿilīn*** | **The people** — in the decision to burn Abraham |
| **79** | *wa kunnā **fāʿilīn*** | **The text** — what was given to David |
| **104** | *innā kunnā **fāʿilīn*** | **The text** — the promise of the resurrection |

**The same word in four places.** **It is a countable fact and will be tabulated at the end.**

## 21/105-106 — وَلَقَدْ كَتَبْنَا فِى ٱلزَّبُورِ مِنۢ بَعْدِ ٱلذِّكْرِ أَنَّ ٱلْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِىَ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · إِنَّ فِى هَٰذَا لَبَلَٰغًا لِّقَوْمٍ عَٰبِدِينَ

*Wa laqad katabnā fi'z-zabūri min baʿdi'dh-dhikri anna'l-arḍa yarithuhā ʿibādiya'ṣ-ṣāliḥūn · Inna fī hādhā la-balāghan li-qawmin ʿābidīn*

"**Indeed We wrote in the Psalter, after the Remembrance, that the earth would be inherited by My righteous servants.** · **In this there is a sufficient message for a people who serve.**"

### ٱلزَّبُور and ٱلذِّكْر — two words, two disagreements

***Zabūr* — root ز-ب-ر.** **The root was treated at `054-kamer.md` and at `035-fatir.md`.** **The sense of the root: to write, to incise in stone;** *zabara'l-kitāb* — he wrote the book. *Zubur* — written pages. **The word conveys writing that has been incised to last.**

**And there is disagreement about what the two words correspond to:**

| Word | View 1 | View 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلزَّبُور** | **The book given to David** | **A generic noun:** the books sent down |
| **ٱلذِّكْر** | **The Preserved Tablet** — the original record | **The Torah** or an earlier book |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.** **Differences are transmitted in the reading of the two words as well (*al-Zabūr* / *al-Zubur*); I name no reciter.**

### عِبَادِىَ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ — the counterpart of the series

**And this expression is directly connected with the sūra's series of prophets. The fact belongs entirely to the text.**

**Across the series the same word had been repeated:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **72** | *wa kullan jaʿalnā **ṣāliḥīn*** | Abraham, Isaac, Jacob |
| **75** | *innahu mina'**ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*** | Lot |
| **86** | *innahum mina'**ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*** | Ishmael, Idrīs, Dhū'l-Kifl |
| **105** | *ʿibādiya'**ṣ-ṣāliḥūn*** | **Those who will inherit the earth** |

**Four occurrences; three in the series, the fourth outside it.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the count above:** the attribute the sūra used three times in the series is made, at the end, **the subject of a promise.** **That is, the promise is tied not to a community but to the quality described in the series.**

**And I enter a note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** the verse does not set up a promise of property to an ethnic or a religious group. **The condition is in the verse's own wording: *ʿibādī* (My servants) and *al-ṣāliḥūn* (the righteous).** **Whoever carries that quality is included in it.**

**بَلَٰغ** — root **ب-ل-غ**: **to reach a place, to arrive.** **And the word is used in two senses:** *tablīgh* (conveying) and **"what is enough"** (*balāgh* — provision that suffices). **The second sense gives the sentence the tone of "this much is enough".**

**لِّقَوْمٍ عَٰبِدِينَ** — **and the word *ʿābidīn* for the third time in the sūra:** 73 (*wa kānū lanā ʿābidīn*), 84 (*dhikrā li'l-ʿābidīn*), 106 (*li-qawmin ʿābidīn*). **It is a countable fact.**

## 21/107 — وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَٰكَ إِلَّا رَحْمَةً لِّلْعَٰلَمِينَ

*Wa mā arsalnāka illā raḥmatan li'l-ʿālamīn*

"**We sent you only as a mercy for all beings.**"

### Three sūras, the scope of the messengership

**And this verse is the third link of the same pattern across three different sūras. All three have been treated in this commentary, and the comparison is wholly a datum of wording.**

| | **al-Furqān 25/56** | **Sabaʾ 34/28** | **al-Anbiyāʾ 21/107** |
|---|---|---|---|
| The pattern | *wa mā arsalnāka illā…* | *wa mā arsalnāka illā…* | *wa mā arsalnāka illā…* |
| The determining word | ***mubashshiran wa nadhīrā*** | ***kāffatan li'n-nās*** | ***raḥmatan li'l-ʿālamīn*** |
| Which axis it gives | **Work** — what he does | **Scope** — to whom | **Quality** — as what |
| Where it draws the limit | At two acts: glad tidings and warning | *al-nās* — human beings | *al-ʿālamīn* — all beings |
| Where the sūra was treated | `025-furkan.md` on 25/55-57 | `034-sebe.md` on 34/28 | Here |

**Three sūras use the same negation-plus-exception pattern (*mā … illā*) and fill it with three different words.**

**And at `034-sebe.md` this was recorded about the pattern:**

> **"*Wa mā arsalnāka illā* — the negation-plus-exception pattern does not narrow the scope; it removes the possibility of narrowing."**

**I rely on that treatment and add this, as my own reading:**

**The three words answer three different questions:**

| Question | The sūra that answers |
|---|---|
| **What does he do?** | al-Furqān: he brings glad tidings and warns |
| **To whom?** | Sabaʾ: to all human beings |
| **As what?** | **al-Anbiyāʾ: as a mercy** |

**And the third differs from the first two in one respect: *bashīr* and *nadhīr* are a commission; *kāffatan* is a scope; but *raḥma* is a quality.** **That is, al-Anbiyāʾ defines the messengership not by the work it does but by what it is.**

**And the word *al-ʿālamīn* is wider than *al-nās* in Sabaʾ.** **This is a difference of wording; I build no ruling about scope upon it, I only record it.**

### رَحْمَةً — its parsing

***Raḥmatan* is in the accusative, and the grammarians differ over it:**

| View | What it is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A circumstantial qualifier** | "We sent you as a mercy" — the state of the one sent |
| 2 | **An object of purpose** | "We sent you so that there might be mercy" — the ground of the sending |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

***Raḥma* — root ر-ح-م.** **The concrete root the lexicographers record: *raḥim* — the womb.** **And the connection is this: the womb shelters and feeds what is in it unconditionally.** **The root was analysed in detail at `055-rahman.md`. I do not repeat it here.**

**And a point of word order must be recorded — I give this as my own reading:**

**The verse does not say "We gave you mercy".** ***Raḥmatan* — the one sent is himself named as a mercy.** **That is, mercy is not a burden carried but the quality of the one sent.**

### Its place within the sūra

**And the verse's position in the sūra is worth recording.**

**The sūra had opened with the objections its addressees raised against the Prophet** (3: *hal hādhā illā basharun mithlukum*; 36: *a-hādha'lladhī yadhkuru ālihatakum*). **And in the forty-fifth verse it was said that the only instrument in the messenger's hand is revelation** (*innamā undhirukum bi'l-waḥy*).

**The hundred and seventh verse names the same man, this time by a quality.**

| Verse | How the Prophet is named | Who says it |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *basharun mithlukum* — a human being like you | **The addressees** |
| **36** | *alladhī yadhkuru ālihatakum* | **The addressees** |
| **45** | *innamā undhirukum bi'l-waḥy* — the instrument | **He himself** |
| **107** | ***raḥmatan li'l-ʿālamīn*** | **The text** |

**Four namings, from four different mouths.** **I record this as my own reading, its ground being the count above:** the sūra closes with a quality the file it opened with an objection.

**The same structure was recorded at `025-furkan.md` as well:** *"the sūra closes the debate about 'what a messenger should be like' with a description of 'how a servant lives'."* **al-Anbiyāʾ does the same work with a single word.**

## 21/108-109 — قُلْ إِنَّمَا يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ أَنَّمَآ إِلَٰهُكُمْ إِلَٰهٌ وَٰحِدٌ فَهَلْ أَنتُم مُّسْلِمُونَ · فَإِن تَوَلَّوْا۟ فَقُلْ ءَاذَنتُكُمْ عَلَىٰ سَوَآءٍ وَإِنْ أَدْرِىٓ أَقَرِيبٌ أَم بَعِيدٌ مَّا تُوعَدُونَ

*Qul innamā yūḥā ilayya annamā ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥid, fa-hal antum muslimūn · Fa-in tawallaw fa-qul ādhantukum ʿalā sawāʾ, wa in adrī a-qarībun am baʿīdun mā tūʿadūn*

"**Say: 'It is revealed to me only that your god is one God. Will you then submit?'** · **And if they turn away, say: 'I have announced it to you all alike. And I do not know whether what you are promised is near or far.'**"

### Two particles of restriction, in one sentence

**And the word order of the hundred and eighth verse must be recorded: there are two particles of restriction in the sentence.**

| Particle | What it restricts |
|---|---|
| ***innamā*** yūḥā ilayya | **The content of the revelation:** only this is revealed to me |
| *annamā* ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥid | **The number of the god:** your god is only one God |

**Two restrictions, one inside the other.** **I record this as an observation about the structure.**

**فَهَلْ أَنتُم مُّسْلِمُونَ** — **and the question is framed as a nominal sentence** — the same pattern as *fa-hal antum shākirūn* in the eightieth verse. **It is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.**

**And in the classical commentaries it is recorded that this form of question stands in place of a command:** instead of saying "submit", "will you submit?" **I transmit this as a transmitted explanation.**

### عَلَىٰ سَوَآءٍ

***Ādhantukum* — root أ-ذ-ن, Form IV: to announce, to make known.** *Udhun* — the ear; **the root conveys the bringing of a thing to the ear.**

***ʿAlā sawāʾ*** — "upon an equal footing". **And two readings of the phrase are transmitted:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **I have announced it to you all alike** — none privileged, none left uninformed |
| 2 | **I have announced it openly** — leaving no hidden side |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**A similar phrase occurs in the Qurʾān at al-Anfāl 8/58:** *fa'nbidh ilayhim **ʿalā sawāʾin*** — "cast (the treaty) back to them on equal terms". **This is a verifiable sharing of wording.**

### وَإِنْ أَدْرِى — the sūra's closing move

**And now I come to the sūra's most striking structural fact. It is wholly a datum of the text.**

***In adrī* — here *in* is negative (in the sense of *mā*): "I do not know."**

**And this expression occurs twice in the sūra: 109 and 111.**

| Verse | The sentence | What is not known |
|---|---|---|
| **109** | *wa **in adrī** a-qarībun am baʿīdun mā tūʿadūn* | **When the promise will come** |
| **111** | *wa **in adrī** laʿallahu fitnatun lakum* | **What the delay means** |

**And now I set this beside the sūra's first verse:**

| Verse | The sentence |
|---|---|
| **1** | *Iqtaraba li'n-nāsi ḥisābuhum* — "**their reckoning has drawn near**" |
| **109** | *wa in adrī **a-qarībun am baʿīd*** — "**I do not know whether it is near or far**" |

**The same root (ق-ر-ب) is in both verses: in the first as a verb (*iqtaraba*), in the second as an adjective (*qarīb*).**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the root the two verses share:**

**The sūra opens with "it has drawn near" and closes, from the messenger's mouth, with "I do not know whether it is near or far".** **The two sentences do not conflict:** the first says the reckoning is **inescapable**, the second that its **calendar** has not been given. **And the difference between them is who holds the knowledge.**

**And this connects with the sūra's thirty-seventh verse too:** *sa-urīkum āyātī **fa-lā tastaʿjilūn*** — "do not ask Me to hasten." **The sūra first says "do not hasten" to the one who asks for haste, and at the end says that the messenger himself does not know the time.**

## 21/110-111 — إِنَّهُۥ يَعْلَمُ ٱلْجَهْرَ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْلِ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا تَكْتُمُونَ · وَإِنْ أَدْرِى لَعَلَّهُۥ فِتْنَةٌ لَّكُمْ وَمَتَٰعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

*Innahu yaʿlamu'l-jahra mina'l-qawli wa yaʿlamu mā taktumūn · Wa in adrī laʿallahu fitnatun lakum wa matāʿun ilā ḥīn*

"**He knows what is spoken aloud and He knows what you conceal.** · **And I do not know: perhaps it is a testing for you, and an enjoyment for a while.**"

### A return to the fourth verse

**And the hundred and tenth verse says the same thing as the sūra's fourth. The comparison is wholly a datum of wording.**

| | **21/4** | **21/110** |
|---|---|---|
| The sentence | *rabbī **yaʿlamu'l-qawla** fi's-samāʾi wa'l-arḍ* | *innahu **yaʿlamu'l-jahra mina'l-qawli** wa yaʿlamu mā taktumūn* |
| Its axis | **Place** — in heaven and on earth | **Openness/concealment** — what is said and what is hidden |
| Its context | *wa asarru'n-najwā* (3) — **a hidden whisper** | *fa-in tawallaw* (109) — **a turning away** |

**Both verses use the same verb (*yaʿlamu*) and the same object (*al-qawl*).** **One gives the scope by place, the other by degree of openness.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the words the two verses share:** the sūra closes in the hundred and tenth verse the matter it opened in the third with a concealed whisper. **And the two verses stand at the two ends of the sūra.**

**ٱلْجَهْر** — root **ج-ه-ر**: to come out into the open, to raise the voice. *Jahrī* — aloud. **And *katm* (ك-ت-م) is its opposite: to hide, to keep within.**

**And the note *mina'l-qawl* carries a point of grammar: *al-jahr* has not been left absolute but has been qualified by *qawl*.** **That is, the subject is not the loudness of the voice but what is made open of the speech.**

### لَعَلَّهُۥ فِتْنَةٌ لَّكُمْ

**And the hundred and eleventh verse connects with the sūra's thirty-fifth.**

**The thirty-fifth verse had said: *wa nablūkum bi'sh-sharri wa'l-khayri fitnatan*.**

**Here the same word is used of the delay itself: *laʿallahu fitnatun lakum*.**

| Verse | What the *fitna* is | For whom |
|---|---|---|
| **35** | **Evil and good** — what is lived through | Everyone |
| **111** | **The delay** — the promise's not coming | **The addressees** |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the word the two verses share:** the sūra brings the delay too within the **scope** of the testing. **That is, the promise's not coming is named not as the absence of the promise but as a part of the testing.**

***And there is disagreement about what the pronoun (*hū*) refers to:***

| View | What *hū* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The delay itself** — the deferring of the punishment |
| 2 | **The respite and the favours given to them** |
| 3 | **What was said in the preceding verse** |

**I transmit the disagreement and do not impose a preference.**

**وَمَتَٰعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ** — ***matāʿ* — root م-ت-ع: a thing enjoyed for a time.** ***Ilā ḥīn* — until an indefinite term.**

**And this phrase occurred at `037-saffat.md` on 37/148, at the close of that series:** *fa-mattaʿnāhum **ilā ḥīn***. **The same two words, in the last sections of two sūras.** **I give this as a note about wording.**

**And the work of the word here must be recorded: *matāʿ* conveys a favour, but *ilā ḥīn* sets an end to it.** **That is, in giving what it gives, the sentence also states its limit.**

## 21/112 — قَٰلَ رَبِّ ٱحْكُم بِٱلْحَقِّ وَرَبُّنَا ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ

*Qāla rabbi'ḥkum bi'l-ḥaqq, wa rabbuna'r-Raḥmānu'l-mustaʿānu ʿalā mā taṣifūn*

"**He said: 'My Lord, judge with truth.' And our Lord is the Merciful, whose help is sought against what you ascribe.**"

### A variant reading

**A variant reading is transmitted in the first word of the verse:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| ***Qāla*** — perfect | "**He said:** My Lord, judge with truth" — the Prophet's prayer is transmitted |
| ***Qul*** — imperative | "**Say:** My Lord, judge with truth" — he is commanded to make the prayer |

**Both readings are transmitted; I name no reciter and impose no preference.**

**And the same disagreement was present in the sūra's fourth verse.** **That is, the sūra carries the same variant reading at both its ends.** **This is a fact; I build no ruling upon it.**

### ٱحْكُم بِٱلْحَقِّ — the sūra's words return

**And the last verse gathers three key words of the sūra together. All three can be verified from the text:**

| Word | Where it had occurred in the sūra |
|---|---|
| ***al-ḥaqq*** | **18** — *naqdifu **bi'l-ḥaqqi** ʿala'l-bāṭil*; **24** — *lā yaʿlamūna'**l-ḥaqq***; **97** — *al-waʿdu'**l-ḥaqq*** |
| ***al-Raḥmān*** | **26, 36, 42** |
| ***taṣifūn*** | **18** — *mimmā **taṣifūn***; **22** — *ʿammā **yaṣifūn*** |

**And the link with the eighteenth verse is especially tight — two words are shared at once:**

| | **21/18** | **21/112** |
|---|---|---|
| *Ḥaqq* | *naqdifu **bi'l-ḥaqqi** ʿala'l-bāṭil* | *rabbi'ḥkum **bi'l-ḥaqq*** |
| *Waṣf* | *wa lakumu'l-waylu **mimmā taṣifūn*** | *al-mustaʿānu **ʿalā mā taṣifūn*** |

**In the eighteenth verse the truth was a thing hurled; in the last verse it is a judgement asked for.** **And both verses have the same preposition: *bi'l-ḥaqq*.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the words the two verses share:** in its middle the sūra had said that falsehood is shattered by the truth. **At its end it shows the same truth being asked for as a judgement.** **And what has changed in between is whose the act is: there *naqdifu* (we hurl), here *iḥkum* (do You judge).**

### ٱلْمُسْتَعَان — the one whose help is sought

***Mustaʿān* — root ع-و-ن, a Form X (*istifʿāl*) passive participle.** ***ʿAwn* — help;** *istiʿāna* — **asking for help.** **And because it is a passive participle: "the one from whom help is sought".**

**The word is from the same root as *wa iyyāka nastaʿīn*, treated at `001-fatiha.md`.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the name set beside the attribute must be recorded: *al-Raḥmān*.**

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the sūra's use of the name:** in the twenty-sixth verse a child was imputed to this name; in the thirty-sixth the mention of this name was denied; in the forty-second protection from this name was asked about. **And the sūra names that same name in its closing sentence as *the one whose help is sought*.**

**That is, the sūra makes the name most objected to the name taken refuge in, in its last sentence.**

**عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ** — **and the sūra closes with the addressee's own language:** the help is asked **against what they ascribe.**

**And one last observation about the structure: the verse ends with *taṣifūn* — an imperfect verb.** **That is, at the moment it closes, the sūra is looking at an act still going on.**

### Close — the sūra's own line

**This section is my own reading, and its ground is the tables above.**

**al-Anbiyāʾ opens with a sentence of reckoning and closes with a prayer for judgement.** In between it does three things.

**First: it describes what the messengership is.** In the seventh verse it is said that the messengers were human, in the twenty-fifth that the same thing was revealed to all of them, in the forty-fifth that the only instrument in the messenger's hand is revelation, and in the hundred and seventh that the one sent is a **mercy**. **Four verses, the four steps of one definition.**

**Second: it tests oneness from two ends.** In the twenty-second verse from **order** (*la-fasadatā*), in the ninety-ninth from **outcome** (*mā waradūhā*). **The same particle (*law*), two different proofs.**

**Third, and the longest: it ranges sixteen names** — and gives none of them a separate religion, a separate call or a separate rule. **When the series ends, one single sentence comes: *inna hādhihi ummatukum ummatan wāḥida*.** **That is, the list is the proof of that sentence.**

**And the sūra's most striking move is at the end.**

The first verse says **"the reckoning has drawn near"**. The hundred and ninth verse says, from the messenger's mouth, **"I do not know whether it is near or far"**. **Between the two sentences there is not a contradiction but a division of labour:** one conveys the **reality** of the reckoning, the other that its **calendar** has not been given.

**And for exactly this reason the sūra is not a sūra of haste but a sūra of measure:** in its middle the scales are set up (47), a mustard seed is weighed (47), the testing is with evil and with good (35) — and at the end the knowledge of the time is left not to the one who asks for it but to its owner.

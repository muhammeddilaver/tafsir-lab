# 15. Sūrat al-Ḥijr

**Ninety-nine verses.** It is widely reported that it came down in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the construct **أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْحِجْرِ in the eightieth verse** (the people of al-Ḥijr).

**The origin of the name is worth recording:** *al-Ḥijr* occurs in the Qurʾān only in this verse, as the name of the homeland of the people of Thamūd. **And the sūra gives them only five verses** (80-84). **That is, the name of the sūra is taken from its short stories.**

**The root of the word (ح-ج-ر), however, suits the sūra as a whole** — and this will be taken up separately in the eightieth verse.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What ends the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-15 | The Book, a regret deferred, **the preservation of the reminder**, those who mock | *Bal naḥnu qawmun masḥūrūn* (15) |
| **II** | 16-25 | **The guarded sky**, the measured earth, the storehouses, the winds | *Innahu Ḥakīmun ʿAlīm* (25) |
| **III** | 26-48 | **Adam (Ādam) and Iblīs**; the end of the two groups | *Wa mā hum minhā bi-mukhrajīn* (48) |
| **IV** | 49-50 | **Two announcements:** forgiveness and punishment | *Huwa'l-ʿadhābu'l-alīm* (50) |
| **V** | 51-77 | **Abraham's (Ibrāhīm's) guests and Lot (Lūṭ)** | *La-āyatan li'l-muʾminīn* (77) |
| **VI** | 78-84 | The people of al-Ayka and **the people of al-Ḥijr** | *Fa-mā aghnā ʿanhum mā kānū yaksibūn* (84) |
| **VII** | 85-99 | The commands given to the Prophet | *Ḥattā yaʾtiyaka'l-yaqīn* (99) |

**The backbone of the sūra gathers into two words: *ḥifẓ* (preservation) and *maʿlūm* (known, determined).**

**The first:** the sky is preserved (17), the reminder is preserved (9), Lot's guests are protected (65), and the people of al-Ḥijr **supposed themselves protected** (82 — *buyūtan āminīn*). **The sūra compares real protection with false protection.**

**The second is a repetition of vocabulary that can be verified — the attribute *maʿlūm* occurs three times in the sūra, and all four set a *boundary*:**

| Verse | Expression | Boundary of what |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | *Kitābun **maʿlūm*** | **The time of the destruction** |
| **21** | *Bi-qadarin **maʿlūm*** | **The measure of what comes down** |
| **38** | *Yawmi'l-waqti'l-**maʿlūm*** | **The end of the term given to Iblīs** |
| **44** | *Juzʾun **maqsūm*** (on the same pattern) | **The share falling to each gate** |

**That is, throughout the sūra time, measure, respite and share — all are named as *determined*.** **This is the tightest weave of the sūra and can be verified from the text.**

## 15/1 — الٓر ۚ تِلْكَ ءَايَٰتُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَقُرْءَانٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Alif-lām-rā · Tilka āyātu'l-kitābi wa qurʾānin mubīn*

"Alif-lām-rā. **These are the verses of the Book and of a clear Qurʾān.**"

### الٓر

**The general note about the disconnected letters was entered in the index and repeated at `014-ibrahim.md` 14/1. I do not repeat it here.**

### ٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَقُرْءَانٍ — one definite, the other indefinite

**A point of word order must be recorded:** *al-kitāb* is **definite**, *qurʾānin* is **indefinite**.

**The lexicographers explain this difference in two ways:**

| # | Explanation | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two separate things are spoken of: the known Book and a recitation | The conjunction separates |
| 2 | The same thing is named twice; the indefiniteness conveys **magnification** (*taʿẓīm*) | The conjunction explains |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**The sense of the two names is worth recording:** *kitāb* means **what is written**, *qurʾān* **what is recited** (the roots ك-ت-ب and ق-ر-أ were treated in `096-alak.md` and at `075-kiyame.md` 75/17-18; I do not repeat it here).

**I record this as an observation:** the text names itself with two separate verbs — one belonging to writing, the other to sound. **And in the ninth verse it will be named with a third name: *al-dhikr* — what is mentioned, what is brought to mind.**

***The two-way readability of the attribute *mubīn* (clear in itself / making clear) was set out in a table at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/2. I rely on that treatment.***

## 15/2 — رُّبَمَا يَوَدُّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَوْ كَانُوا۟ مُسْلِمِينَ

*Rubamā yawaddu'lladhīna kafarū law kānū muslimīn*

"**Those who disbelieve will one day wish that they had been Muslims.**"

### رُّبَمَا — the subtlety of the particle

**The particle is built of two parts: *rubba* + *mā*.**

***Rubba* in Arabic is a particle of fewness.** The classical description of the lexicographers is this: *rubba* is used for what is few, *kam* for what is many. *Rubba rajulin laqītuhu* — "many a man have I met" (but few in number).

**And when *mā* is added, the particle becomes able to attach to a verb rather than a noun** — *rubba* normally enters only upon a noun; *mā* opens to it the door of the verbal sentence. The lexicographers call this *mā al-kāffa* (the restraining *mā*).

**Now the real matter:** the verse announces that those who disbelieve will feel this regret. **And it says so with a particle of *fewness*.** Why?

**The classical explanations run mainly along three lines:**

| # | Explanation | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Rubba* is used in Arabic for **muchness** as well; the particle works in both directions | Examples from poetry are transmitted |
| 2 | The particle here conveys **a threat**: even if it be little, such a moment will come — and that moment will be unbearable | The context |
| 3 | The fewness is real: the regret will be felt **not often**, but at a few particular moments (the moment of death, the moment of the reckoning, the moment of entering the fire) | Other scenes of regret in the Qurʾān |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**There is a datum of word order that can be recorded, and I give it as an observation:** in Arabic, when a particle of fewness is used with an unexpected piece of news, it produces **not diminishment but unignorability**. Had the sentence said "they will regret it much", it would stand like a prediction; **when it says "they will regret it, if only a little", the weight of that little is left to the reader.**

**And the verb must be recorded: *yawaddu* — root و-د-د: to love, to desire, to wish.** **The word was treated at `019-meryem.md` 19/96 and at `060-mumtehine.md` 60/7 (*mawadda*). I rely on that treatment.**

**So what is said is not that they acquire a piece of knowledge — but that a *desire* is born.** The verb of the sentence has to do not with the mind but with wanting.

## 15/3 — ذَرْهُمْ يَأْكُلُوا۟ وَيَتَمَتَّعُوا۟ وَيُلْهِهِمُ ٱلْأَمَلُ ۖ فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ

*Dharhum yaʾkulū wa yatamattaʿū wa yulhihimu'l-amal · Fa-sawfa yaʿlamūn*

"**Leave them; let them eat, let them take their enjoyment, and let hope divert them. Soon they will know.**"

### ذَرْهُمْ — "leave" as a command

**The verb is from *wadhara*, whose perfect is not used; only the imperfect and imperative forms are current. This is a fact of the language recorded in the index at `076-insan.md` 76/27, and it was stated there that this root must not be confused with ن-ذ-ر and ذ-ر-ر. I rely on that treatment.**

**The one addressed by the command is the Prophet, and what is commanded is an *inaction*.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the second verse of the sūra announced a future, the third says what is to be done in the present — **and what is said is: do not intervene.** In the ninety-fourth verse the sūra will command the opposite (*fa-ṣdaʿ bimā tuʾmar*). **The difference between the two commands will be taken up at the end of the sūra.**

### وَيُلْهِهِمُ ٱلْأَمَلُ

The **ل-ه-و** root: **a diversion that keeps one from a task.** *Lahw* — an occupation that draws one away from the real work. **The root was analysed in detail at `102-tekasur.md` 102/1 (*alhākumu'l-takāthur*). I do not repeat it here.**

The **أ-م-ل** root: **hope, expectation.** *Amal* — an expectation directed forward.

**The word order of the verb must be recorded: *yulhihimu'l-amal* — the doer is hope, not they.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of the doer:** the sentence does not say "they are being diverted". **A thing that diverts is named, and that thing is not an evil desire — it is *hope*.**

**And at `018-kehf.md` 18/46 the same word occurred in a positive context:** *wa'l-bāqiyātu'l-ṣāliḥātu khayrun ʿinda Rabbika thawāban wa khayrun **amalā***. **I rely on that treatment.**

| Place | *Amal* | Value |
|---|---|---|
| al-Kahf 18/46 | *Khayrun amalā* | **Positive** — hope attached to the right place |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/3** | *Yulhihimu'l-amal* | **Diverting** — attached to the wrong place |

**The word is the same; what changes is where it is attached.** This is a verifiable overlap between sūras.

## 15/4-5 — وَمَآ أَهْلَكْنَا مِن قَرْيَةٍ إِلَّا وَلَهَا كِتَابٌ مَّعْلُومٌ · مَّا تَسْبِقُ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلَهَا وَمَا يَسْتَـْٔخِرُونَ

*Wa mā ahlaknā min qaryatin illā wa lahā kitābun maʿlūm · Mā tasbiqu min ummatin ajalahā wa mā yastaʾkhirūn*

"**Every town We destroyed had a known writing.** · **No community can outrun its term, nor can they hold it back.**"

### كِتَابٌ مَّعْلُومٌ — the sūra's first *maʿlūm*

**The attribute will occur four times in the sūra (4, 21, 38, and in 44 on the same pattern as *maqsūm*); it was set out in a table in the section on the structure.**

**Its use here must be recorded: *kitāb* and *maʿlūm* side by side.** **The word comes not as the name of a document but as a *determinedness*.**

**And the pattern of the sentence is a *ḥaṣr*: *wa mā ahlaknā… illā…* — without exception.** **That is, destruction, however sudden it may appear, carries a time.**

### مَّا تَسْبِقُ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلَهَا

**The sentence is negated in two directions at once: it can neither go ahead nor fall behind.**

**And a point of word order must be recorded: the first verb is feminine singular (*tasbiqu* — for *umma*), the second masculine plural (*yastaʾkhirūn*).**

**I record this as an observation about the language:** the sentence begins with the word *umma* (a community — feminine), then passes to the persons who make up the community (plural). **That is, the term is attached first to the community, then to the individuals.**

**The same sentence occurs at al-Aʿrāf 7/34 and Yūnus 10/49 as well; these sūras have not yet been treated in the index, and I record only the places where they occur.**

## 15/6-8 — وَقَالُوا۟ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِى نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلذِّكْرُ إِنَّكَ لَمَجْنُونٌ · لَّوْ مَا تَأْتِينَا بِٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ إِن كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلصَّٰدِقِينَ · مَا نُنَزِّلُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةَ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَمَا كَانُوٓا۟ إِذًا مُّنظَرِينَ

*Wa qālū yā ayyuhā'lladhī nuzzila ʿalayhi'l-dhikru innaka la-majnūn · Law mā taʾtīnā bi'l-malāʾikati in kunta mina'l-ṣādiqīn · Mā nunazzilu'l-malāʾikata illā bi'l-ḥaqqi wa mā kānū idhan munẓarīn*

"They said: '**O you upon whom the reminder has been sent down! You are surely possessed.** · **If you are of the truthful, why do you not bring us the angels!**' · **We send the angels down only with the truth; and then they would be given no respite.**"

### The contradiction in the framing of the objection

**The form of address must be recorded: *yā ayyuhā'lladhī nuzzila ʿalayhi'l-dhikr* — "O you upon whom the reminder has been sent down."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the first half of the sentence carries an **acceptance** — they say "the reminder was sent down to you." **The second half rejects: "you are possessed."** The two halves do not hold together.

**The lexicographers explain this in two ways:**

| # | Explanation | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The address is **mockery**: they are jeering by repeating his own claim | *Istihzāʾ* |
| 2 | In their mouths the verb *nuzzila* means "which you say was sent down" | Reported speech |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **On either reading the work of the sentence is the same: the claim is repeated and immediately an attempt is made to demolish it.**

**ٱلذِّكْر** — and the third name of the Book comes here: *al-dhikr*. **The word will return in the ninth verse and will be treated there.**

### لَّوْ مَا تَأْتِينَا بِٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ

***Law mā*, in Arabic, like *hallā*, is a particle conveying incitement or reproach: "why do you not do it, do it then."**

**The demand was treated at `025-furkan.md` 25/7 and 25/21** (*law lā unzila ilayhi malakun fa-yakūna maʿahu nadhīrā*; *law lā unzila ʿalaynā'l-malāʾika*). **I rely on that treatment.**

### مَا نُنَزِّلُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةَ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ

**The answer does not reject the demand — it states its condition.**

***Bi'l-ḥaqq* is explained here by the lexicographers in two ways:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **With the decree** — the descent of the angels means the finishing of the matter |
| 2 | **With a real cause** — not for an arbitrary display |

**Both are transmitted. The second half of the verse lends itself to the first reading, and I record this as a supporting indication:** *wa mā kānū idhan munẓarīn* — **"and then they would have been given no respite."**

From the root **ن-ظ-ر**, *inẓār*: **to grant a term, to defer.** **And the word will return in the thirty-seventh verse of the sūra:** *fa-innaka mina'l-munẓarīn* — the respite given to Iblīs. **The same word, one a respite refused and one a respite granted.** This is a repetition within the sūra that can be verified.

**I record this as my own reading:** the thing demanded (the coming of the angels), had it been given, **would have ended the term of the one demanding it.** The answer does not refuse the request; **it states the price of the request.**

## 15/9 — إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا ٱلذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ لَحَٰفِظُونَ

*Innā naḥnu nazzalnā'l-dhikra wa innā lahu la-ḥāfiẓūn*

"**It is We who sent down the reminder, and it is We who will preserve it.**"

### The pile-up of emphasis in the sentence

**The verse is one of the most densely emphasised sentences in the Qurʾān. They can be counted one by one:**

| Element | Its function |
|---|---|
| *Innā* | **Inna** — the particle of emphasis |
| *Naḥnu* | **A separate pronoun** — appears superfluous, there for emphasis |
| *Nazzalnā* | **First person plural in the verb** — the same doer a third time |
| *Wa innā* | **A second inna** |
| *Lahu* | **A fronted prepositional phrase** — emphasis |
| *La-* | **The emphatic lām** |
| *Ḥāfiẓūn* | **The predicate of a nominal sentence** — conveys continuity |

**Seven elements, in one sentence.** **I record this as an observation about the word order:** the weight of the sentence is proportionate to the weight of what it says.

**And the symmetry of the two halves must be recorded:**

| First half | Second half |
|---|---|
| *Innā naḥnu **nazzalnā*** | *Wa innā lahu la-**ḥāfiẓūn*** |
| **A verb** — a work accomplished | **A noun** — a state continuing |

**Sending down is given as a completed act, preserving as a continuing attribute.** **In Arabic a nominal sentence conveys continuity, a verbal sentence an event; the verse uses this distinction.**

### What is preserved — the classical explanations

**The wording of the verse says this: what was sent down is *al-dhikr*, and what will be preserved is that (the pronoun *lahu* returns to *al-dhikr*).** **Beyond this, different explanations of what the preserving *covers* are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | What is preserved | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The wording of the Qurʾān** — the text itself | That *al-dhikr* was used for the Qurʾān in the preceding verse |
| 2 | **The meaning of the Qurʾān** — its preservation from alteration and misinterpretation | The wide sense of the verb *ḥifẓ* |
| 3 | **The Prophet himself** — the pronoun *lahu* returning to him | That the next group of verses is about him |
| 4 | **Both wording and meaning** | The joining of two readings |

**All four explanations are transmitted in the sources. I do not impose a preference, and I do not enter the debates conducted by way of this verse.**

**I keep within what the wording of the verse says, and I record two things:**

**One.** **The subject of the sentence is the same from beginning to end: the one who sent down and the one who preserves are the same party.** The work of preserving is not handed over to another.

**Two.** **The verb is *ḥifẓ*, and this root will come once more eight verses later in the sūra:** *wa **ḥafiẓnāhā** min kulli shayṭānin rajīm* (17). **The same verb, one for a text and one for the sky.**

| Verse | What is preserved | From what |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | ***Al-dhikr*** | **Not said** |
| **17** | **The sky** | *Min kulli shayṭānin rajīm* |

**I record this as a weave within the sūra, and it can be verified:** the sūra names preservation in two separate fields — and in both the preserving party is the same.

## 15/10-13 — وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ فِى شِيَعِ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ · وَمَا يَأْتِيهِم مِّن رَّسُولٍ إِلَّا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ · كَذَٰلِكَ نَسْلُكُهُۥ فِى قُلُوبِ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ · لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهِۦ ۖ وَقَدْ خَلَتْ سُنَّةُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ

*Wa laqad arsalnā min qablika fī shiyaʿi'l-awwalīn · Wa mā yaʾtīhim min rasūlin illā kānū bihi yastahziʾūn · Kadhālika naslukuhu fī qulūbi'l-mujrimīn · Lā yuʾminūna bihi wa qad khalat sunnatu'l-awwalīn*

"**We sent messengers before you among the parties of the ancients.** · **No messenger came to them but they mocked him.** · **Thus do We insert it into the hearts of the guilty.** · **They do not believe in it; yet the way of the ancients has passed.**"

### شِيَع

**The plural of *shīʿa*. The root ش-ي-ع: to follow someone, to go after him; and from this, a body of partisans. *Tashyīʿ* (seeing someone off) is from this root too: going after someone.**

**The word was treated at `030-rum.md` 30/32 (*wa lā takūnū mina'l-mushrikīn… wa kānū shiyaʿā*). I rely on that treatment.**

### كَذَٰلِكَ نَسْلُكُهُۥ فِى قُلُوبِ ٱلْمُجْرِمِينَ

The **س-ل-ك** root: **to pass a thing through a narrow place, to insert as one threads a needle.** *Salaka'l-khayṭa fī'l-ibra* — he threaded the thread through the needle. **That is, the verb recounts a thing's being *passed through* somewhere.**

**The lexicographers differ over what the pronoun *hu* returns to:**

| Reading | What the pronoun returns to | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The mocking** (*istihzāʾ*) | Mockery is inserted into their hearts thus |
| 2 | **The reminder / the Qurʾān** | The text enters their hearts but they do not believe |
| 3 | **The denial** | Denial settles thus |

**All three readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the continuation of the verse lends itself to the second reading:** *lā yuʾminūna bihi* — the same pronoun is repeated. **I record this as a supporting indication, not as a preference.**

**The voice of the verb must be recorded: *naslaku* — first person plural, active.** **This is a pattern treated in several places in the index** (the sealing at `002-bakara.md` 2/7, the turning aside of hearts at `061-saff.md` 61/5). **I rely on those treatments, and what was recorded there holds here too: the verb recounts not a beginning but the registering of the end of a process — the order of the verses shows this, because the mocking is named first.**

### وَقَدْ خَلَتْ سُنَّةُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ

The **س-ن-ن** root: **to make a thing flow smoothly, to open a way.** *Sunna* — an opened road, one that is walked upon. **The root was treated at `048-fetih.md` 48/23 (*sunnatallāh*), and a boundary was set there per the method (`STYLE.md`): the application of the concept to contemporary events is not entered into. I rely on that treatment.**

**The verb must be recorded: *khalat* — "it has passed, it has been left behind."** **That is, the way is named not as a rule but as something that has happened.**

## 15/14-15 — وَلَوْ فَتَحْنَا عَلَيْهِم بَابًا مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ فَظَلُّوا۟ فِيهِ يَعْرُجُونَ · لَقَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّمَا سُكِّرَتْ أَبْصَٰرُنَا بَلْ نَحْنُ قَوْمٌ مَّسْحُورُونَ

*Wa law fataḥnā ʿalayhim bāban mina'l-samāʾi fa-ẓallū fīhi yaʿrujūn · La-qālū innamā sukkirat abṣārunā bal naḥnu qawmun masḥūrūn*

"**Even if We opened a gate to them from the sky and they kept climbing up through it,** · **they would surely say: 'Our eyes have been dazzled; no, we are a people bewitched.'**"

### A counterfactual experiment

**The sentence is built with a *law* (a condition unrealised)**, and this is the same structure treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/44: **"what would they say had it been otherwise?"** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference here is this, and it must be recorded: in Fuṣṣilat the assumed change was *the language of the Book*. Here what is assumed is that the requested miracle is given *in excess*.**

**In the seventh verse the angels had been demanded. Here they are given the possibility of climbing into the sky — something beyond the demand.** **And the answer is again an objection.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses:** the sūra does not content itself with refusing the demand. **It also shows what would happen if the demand were met** — and what it shows is that the proof would this time be referred to **perception**: "our eyes have been dazzled."

### سُكِّرَتْ

**The lexicographers differ over the root of the word:**

| Root | Sense | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **س-ك-ر** (intoxication) | **The eyes were made drunk, they swam** | Sight was disordered |
| **س-ك-ر** (closing off; *sakara'l-nahr* — to dam a river) | **The eyes were closed off, veiled** | Sight was blocked |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **And it must be recorded that both senses are counted branches of the same root: intoxication is the "closing" of the mind.**

### بَلْ نَحْنُ قَوْمٌ مَّسْحُورُونَ — a judgement about oneself

The particle **bal** is used in Arabic **to turn from what has been said and state something more decisive** (*iḍrāb*).

**A point of word order must be recorded:** in the first sentence the verb was passive and the object was "our eyes" — **a partial disorder.** In the second sentence the subject becomes "we" and the attribute covers the whole: *qawmun masḥūrūn* — **a bewitched people.**

**I record this as an observation:** the objection magnifies itself. **And the point reached is this: in order not to accept what he sees, a person sacrifices his own perception.**

**And the word *masḥūr* carries an echo:** the same attribute is used in the Qurʾān in the accusations directed at the Prophet. **Here the other side uses it of *itself*.** **A similar usage was treated at `026-suara.md` 26/153 (*innamā anta mina'l-musaḥḥarīn*). I rely on that treatment.**

## 15/16-18 — وَلَقَدْ جَعَلْنَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ بُرُوجًا وَزَيَّنَّٰهَا لِلنَّٰظِرِينَ · وَحَفِظْنَٰهَا مِن كُلِّ شَيْطَٰنٍ رَّجِيمٍ · إِلَّا مَنِ ٱسْتَرَقَ ٱلسَّمْعَ فَأَتْبَعَهُۥ شِهَابٌ مُّبِينٌ

*Wa laqad jaʿalnā fī'l-samāʾi burūjan wa zayyannāhā li'l-nāẓirīn · Wa ḥafiẓnāhā min kulli shayṭānin rajīm · Illā mani'staraqa'l-samʿa fa-atbaʿahu shihābun mubīn*

"**We made constellations in the sky and adorned it for those who look.** · **And We guarded it from every accursed satan.** · **Except one who steals a hearing — then a clear flame pursues him.**"

### First, a boundary

**This subject was treated in detail twice in the index: in the section on `072-cin.md` 72/8-9 and at `037-saffat.md` 37/6-10.** **In both, these verses (15/16-18) were already named and quoted.** **I do not repeat the findings made there.**

**But the note of boundary made there must be repeated here as well:**

> The verse does not describe a heavenly body. It does not recount a physical event. It does not give a piece of astronomy.

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) this commentary does not go down that road.** No matching will be made with notions such as meteorites, shooting stars or cosmic radiation.

**The frame recorded in the section on al-Jinn holds here too, and I rely on it:** this group of verses is part not of a lesson in cosmology but of a **dispute about a source**. One of the accusations directed at the Prophet in Mecca was that what he said came from a jinn; **these passages are an answer to that accusation.**

**And it was recorded at `037-saffat.md` that what is peculiar to al-Ṣāffāt is the "ordering" — adornment first, guarding after.** **The same order is here too and can be verified:** *zayyannāhā* (16) → *ḥafiẓnāhā* (17).

### What is peculiar to al-Ḥijr: بُرُوج and لِلنَّٰظِرِينَ

**The difference of these three verses from the other two passages lies in two words.**

**بُرُوج** — the plural of *burj*. **The word was treated at `085-buruc.md` in connection with the name of that sūra, and I rely on that treatment.**

**The concrete sense of the root ب-ر-ج: to come out into the open, to be conspicuous, to rise.** From the same root comes *tabarruj* (the display of adornment). **And *burj* in the language means "a high and visible structure, a fortress tower".**

**There is disagreement over what the word *burūj* corresponds to in the Qurʾān, and it was recorded in the section on al-Burūj. The principal readings for the context here:**

| Reading | What *burūj* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Stars / large heavenly bodies** |
| 2 | **Determined positions or stations in the sky** |
| 3 | **Fortresses, protected structures** — in agreement with the "guarding" of the next verse |

**I do not impose a preference.** **And I record it per the method (`STYLE.md`): I do not derive from the word an astronomical classification or a star map.**

**The third reading agrees at the level of vocabulary with *ḥafiẓnāhā* in the seventeenth verse, and I record this as a supporting indication, not as a preference:** a *burj* is a defensive structure; **and immediately after it comes an announcement of guarding.**

**لِلنَّٰظِرِينَ** — **"for those who look."**

**This word is not in al-Ṣāffāt or in al-Mulk; it is peculiar to al-Ḥijr.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word's absence from the other two passages:** the adornment is named as having been made not for itself but **for someone who looks**. **That is, the verse takes into the sentence the existence of one who beholds the sky.** The one for whom the adornment is meant is stated.

**And this agrees with the general line of the sūra:** in the fourteenth verse the other side would deny what it saw even if it were taken up to a gate in the sky. **Two verses later the sky is named as adorned "for those who look".** **I record this encounter between two verses as an observation about the word order.**

### شِهَاب

**The word was analysed at `072-cin.md`, and it was recorded there: *shihāb* is not the name of a heavenly body; it means flame. In the Qurʾān it is used both for the live coal Moses (Mūsā) saw (al-Naml 27/7 — *bi-shihābin qabas*) and here.** **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

**The attribute here is *mubīn* (clear); in al-Ṣāffāt it was *thāqib* (piercing).** **The two sūras name the same thing with two different attributes:** one looks to **visibility**, the other to **penetration**.

## 15/19-20 — وَٱلْأَرْضَ مَدَدْنَٰهَا وَأَلْقَيْنَا فِيهَا رَوَٰسِىَ وَأَنۢبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ مَّوْزُونٍ · وَجَعَلْنَا لَكُمْ فِيهَا مَعَٰيِشَ وَمَن لَّسْتُمْ لَهُۥ بِرَٰزِقِينَ

*Wa'l-arḍa madadnāhā wa alqaynā fīhā rawāsiya wa anbatnā fīhā min kulli shayʾin mawzūn · Wa jaʿalnā lakum fīhā maʿāyisha wa man lastum lahu bi-rāziqīn*

"**And the earth We spread out; We set firm mountains in it and made grow in it of every measured thing.** · **And in it We made means of living for you, and for those whose provision is not from you.**"

### مَدَدْنَٰهَا

The **م-د-د** root: **to stretch, to spread, to extend.** **The root was treated with a near word at `067-mulk.md` and at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/10 (*jaʿala lakumu'l-arḍa mahdā*). I rely on those treatments.**

**I draw a boundary per the method (`STYLE.md`):** I derive from this verb no conclusion about the shape of the earth — neither to confirm a claim nor to refute one. **The lexical sense of the word is "to spread out"; what the verse says to the one it addresses is that a breadth to be walked upon has been prepared.** **The same boundary was set at `039-zumer.md` 39/5 for the verb *yukawwiru*; I rely on that treatment.**

**رَوَٰسِى** — the plural of *rāsiya*. **Root ر-س-و: to be fixed in a place, to cast anchor.** *Mursā* — the place where a ship anchors.

**The verb must be recorded: *alqaynā* — "we cast, we set down."** The lexicographers record that this verb is used here in the sense of "to place".

### مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ مَّوْزُونٍ

The **و-ز-ن** root: **to weigh.** *Mīzān* — the balance; *wazn* — weight. **The root was treated in detail at `055-rahman.md` 55/7-9 (*wa waḍaʿa'l-mīzān*). I rely on that treatment.**

***Mawzūn* is a passive participle: "weighed, its measure determined."**

**The lexicographers differ over what the word corresponds to here:**

| Reading | What *mawzūn* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Its quantity determined** — everything is made to grow in its measure |
| 2 | **Having value, coming to the scale** — precious metals included |
| 3 | **Matched to one another, harmonious** — ordered |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**What must be recorded is the word's place within the sūra:** two verses later *bi-qadarin maʿlūm* (with a determined measure) will come. **That is, *mawzūn* and *qadar* say the same idea with two different roots, two verses apart.**

### مَعَٰيِش

**The plural of *maʿīsha*. The root ع-ي-ش: to live. *Maʿīsha* — the means of living, livelihood.**

### وَمَن لَّسْتُمْ لَهُۥ بِرَٰزِقِينَ

**The framing of the sentence must be recorded, and the lexicographers differ over what *man* attaches to:**

| Reading | What *man* attaches to | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Jaʿalnā lakum… wa li-**man*** | We made a living also for **the creatures whose provision is not from you** |
| 2 | *Jaʿalnā lakum… **maʿāyisha wa man*** | We gave you both a livelihood and **beings you do not feed** (those placed at your service) |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**But the first reading says something, and I record this as my own reading, its ground being the expression *lastum lahu bi-rāziqīn* itself:** the verse says that man is not the only one addressed on the earth. **And it does this by naming creatures man does not feed.** That is, the scope spills beyond man's field of responsibility.

## 15/21 — وَإِن مِّن شَىْءٍ إِلَّا عِندَنَا خَزَآئِنُهُۥ وَمَا نُنَزِّلُهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِقَدَرٍ مَّعْلُومٍ

*Wa in min shayʾin illā ʿindanā khazāʾinuhu wa mā nunazziluhu illā bi-qadarin maʿlūm*

"**There is nothing whose storehouses are not with Us; and We send it down only in a determined measure.**"

### خَزَآئِن

**The plural of *khizāna*. The root خ-ز-ن: to keep a thing hidden, to take it into safekeeping, to store it up. *Khāzin* — a treasurer, one who keeps.**

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** a store is not what is **unused** but what is **kept**; it is held to be brought out at the moment of need.

**And the pattern of the sentence is twice a *ḥaṣr*:** *wa in min shayʾin **illā**…* and *wa mā nunazziluhu **illā**…* — **two restrictions in succession, leaving no exception.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the first restriction determines **the scope** (nothing is excluded), the second **the measure** (no sending down is without measure).

### بِقَدَرٍ مَّعْلُومٍ

**The ق-د-ر root was analysed in detail at `097-kadr.md` and taken up once more at `054-kamer.md` 54/49 (*innā kulla shayʾin khalaqnāhu bi-qadar*). I do not repeat it here.**

**What was recorded in the section on al-Qadr holds here too, and I rely on it:**

> *Qadara* — he measured, he shaped, he determined its quantity. Its derivatives: *qadar* (a determined measure), *miqdār*, *taqdīr*, *qudra*. **And the relation between determining and worth is built into the language itself: to know the *qadr* of a thing is to give it its right measure.**

**The difference belonging here is this, and it must be recorded:**

| Place | Expression | What is measured |
|---|---|---|
| al-Qamar 54/49 | *Kulla shayʾin khalaqnāhu **bi-qadar*** | **Creation** — it was created with measure |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/21** | *Mā nunazziluhu illā **bi-qadarin maʿlūm*** | **The sending down** — it was sent down with measure |

**In al-Qamar the measure is attached to creating, here to giving.** **And an attribute has been added to the measure here: *maʿlūm* — known.** In al-Qamar this attribute is absent.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the presence of the attribute:** the measure's being "known" says **that there is one who knows it**. **That is, the verse does not merely state a quantity; it says that the quantity is not random, that it is on record.** **And this is the same attribute as the *kitābun maʿlūm* of the fourth verse of the sūra.**

## 15/22 — وَأَرْسَلْنَا ٱلرِّيَٰحَ لَوَٰقِحَ فَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءً فَأَسْقَيْنَٰكُمُوهُ وَمَآ أَنتُمْ لَهُۥ بِخَٰزِنِينَ

*Wa arsalnā'l-riyāḥa lawāqiḥa fa-anzalnā mina'l-samāʾi māʾan fa-asqaynākumūh · Wa mā antum lahu bi-khāzinīn*

"**We sent the winds as fertilisers; We sent down water from the sky and gave it to you to drink. It is not you who store it up.**"

### لَوَٰقِح — the lexical situation of the word

The **ل-ق-ح** root. **The concrete sense of the root is given by the lexicographers thus: the she-camel's conceiving; and the fertilising of the date palm (*talqīḥu'l-nakhl*) — the transfer of the pollen of the male palm to the female.**

**Its derivatives:** *lāqiḥ* — a pregnant camel; *laqūḥ* — one that has conceived; *talqīḥ* — fertilising.

**The lexicographers differ over the grammatical status of the word *lawāqiḥ*, and this determines the sense:**

| Reading | What *lawāqiḥ* is | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The plural of *lāqiḥa*** — an active participle | The winds are **fertilising** |
| 2 | **Counted the plural of *mulqiḥa*** (a use outside the pattern) | The winds are **the fertilisers** |
| 3 | ***Lāqiḥ*** means one that is itself pregnant | The winds are **laden** — laden with water |

**All three explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

### A boundary per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**This verse is one of the most forced verses of the modern period, and an explicit note must be entered here.**

**In this commentary no meteorological or botanical information is derived from the verse.** No matching will be made with notions such as cloud physics, the formation of rainfall, the carrying of pollen, or condensation nuclei. **The same boundary was set at `039-zumer.md` 39/5 and at `037-saffat.md` 37/6-10; I rely on those treatments.**

**The reason is this, and I give it as a note of method:** the lexical sense of the word is **fertilisation**, and this sense existed in Arabic before the verse and after it. **The one the verse addressed was a society that fertilised date palms, and it understood the word.** To tear a word away from the sense its addressee understood and attach it to a piece of knowledge a thousand years later **is contrary to the principle the verse itself declares:** *wa mā arsalnā min rasūlin illā bi-lisāni qawmihi li-yubayyina lahum* (`014-ibrahim.md` 14/4). **The principle treated there works here as a boundary.**

**I keep within the classical explanations, and those too were given in the table: the wind is either a *fertiliser* (of the cloud or of the plant), or it is *laden*.** **Deciding which of these two readings is correct is not the business of this commentary.**

### وَمَآ أَنتُمْ لَهُۥ بِخَٰزِنِينَ

**And the sentence is joined to the preceding verse at the level of vocabulary:** *ʿindanā **khazāʾinuhu*** (21) → *wa mā antum lahu bi-**khāzinīn*** (22).

**The same root (خ-ز-ن), two verses apart, one positive and one negative.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | *ʿIndanā khazāʾinuhu* | **The storehouses are with Him** |
| **22** | *Mā antum lahu bi-khāzinīn* | **You are not the ones who store** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root in the two verses:** the sūra first says where the storehouse is, then who is not the storekeeper. **The two sentences together say a single thing: the holding of water is not man's work.**

**And there is a weave with the twentieth verse too:** *wa man **lastum lahu bi-rāziqīn*** (20) — *wa mā antum **lahu bi-khāzinīn*** (22). **Two negative sentences very close to one another in two verses, on the same pattern.** **Both name a work man does not do:** one feeding, the other storing. **This is a repetition of word order within the sūra that can be verified.**

## 15/23-25 — وَإِنَّا لَنَحْنُ نُحْىِۦ وَنُمِيتُ وَنَحْنُ ٱلْوَٰرِثُونَ · وَلَقَدْ عَلِمْنَا ٱلْمُسْتَقْدِمِينَ مِنكُمْ وَلَقَدْ عَلِمْنَا ٱلْمُسْتَـْٔخِرِينَ · وَإِنَّ رَبَّكَ هُوَ يَحْشُرُهُمْ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ حَكِيمٌ عَلِيمٌ

*Wa innā la-naḥnu nuḥyī wa numītu wa naḥnu'l-wārithūn · Wa laqad ʿalimnā'l-mustaqdimīna minkum wa laqad ʿalimnā'l-mustaʾkhirīn · Wa inna Rabbaka huwa yaḥshuruhum · Innahu Ḥakīmun ʿAlīm*

"**We give life and We give death; and it is We who remain.** · **We know those of you who go ahead, and We know those who stay behind.** · **Your Lord will gather them. He is wise, knowing.**"

### وَنَحْنُ ٱلْوَٰرِثُونَ

The **و-ر-ث** root: **to come into possession of what is left after someone.**

**The word's use here must be recorded:** inheritance comes about by the going of the owner. **The verse says who remains after everyone has gone.**

**And the pattern is a plural *active participle* (*al-wārithūn*), not a verb.** **That is, it conveys not an event but a *state*.**

### ٱلْمُسْتَقْدِمِينَ / ٱلْمُسْتَـْٔخِرِينَ

**The two words are active participles of the *istifʿāl* form: "those who go ahead" and "those who stay behind."**

**The lexicographers differ over their sense:**

| Reading | The distinction is by |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Time** — those who came first / those who will come later (the order of death or of creation) |
| 2 | **Place** — those who stand in front in the ranks of battle or of prayer / those who stand behind |
| 3 | **Deed** — those who go ahead in good / those who lag |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**What must be recorded is the repeated framing of the sentence:** *wa laqad ʿalimnā… wa laqad ʿalimnā…* — **the verb twice, with the same emphasis.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence could have gathered the two groups under one verb. **Its repeating the verb emphasises that the two groups are known separately.** The knowledge is given not collectively but as **directed at each of the two sides separately**.

**And its link with the *maʿlūm* weave must be recorded:** in four places the sūra speaks of a determined measure; **here what is known is not measures but persons.**

### إِنَّهُۥ حَكِيمٌ عَلِيمٌ

**The block closes with two names, and the second is from the same root as the verb of the block:** *ʿalimnā* (24) → *ʿAlīm* (25). **The verb belongs to the servant, the name to God.**

## 15/26-27 — وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِن صَلْصَٰلٍ مِّنْ حَمَإٍ مَّسْنُونٍ · وَٱلْجَآنَّ خَلَقْنَٰهُ مِن قَبْلُ مِن نَّارِ ٱلسَّمُومِ

*Wa laqad khalaqnā'l-insāna min ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn · Wa'l-jānna khalaqnāhu min qablu min nāri'l-samūm*

"**We created man from dry clay, from a moulded black mud.** · **And the jānn We created before, from the fire of the scorching wind.**"

### صَلْصَٰل — root: ص-ل-ص-ل

**The root was analysed at `055-rahman.md` 55/14, and what was recorded there holds here too; I rely on that treatment.**

**A summary of what was recorded in the section on al-Raḥmān:**

> **The root is a *muḍāʿaf rubāʿī*** — a four-letter root built by the doubling of a two-letter core. **This type of root was analysed at `099-zilzal.md`:** doubled roots recount **a repeated, back-and-forth movement and the sound that movement makes**. **And the first item of the list of examples given there was exactly this word:** *ṣalṣala* — to ring, the clinking of dry clay.

**The two branches the lexicographers record for the word were given there too, and I do not repeat them:** one is **ringing dry clay**, the other **clay whose smell has altered** (*ṣalla'l-laḥm* — the meat went off).

### The difference between the two sūras — a comparison

**The same word occurs in two sūras within two different constructions, and the difference must be recorded:**

| Sūra | Expression | The second element | What it is likened to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **al-Raḥmān 55/14** | *Ṣalṣālin **ka'l-fakhkhār*** | **A simile** — with the particle *ka* | **Fired pottery** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/26** | *Ṣalṣālin **min ḥamaʾin masnūn*** | **A statement of origin** — with the particle *min* | **Moulded black mud** |

**The difference lies at two points, and I record it as an observation about the word order:**

**One.** **In al-Raḥmān there is a *comparison* (*ka'l-fakhkhār* — like pottery); in al-Ḥijr there is a *statement of what it is from* (*min ḥamaʾin masnūn* — from this mud).** One says what it resembles, the other what it is made of.

**Two.** **The second element in al-Raḥmān looks to *being fired* (*fakhkhār* — pottery, passed through fire), the one in al-Ḥijr to *being shaped*.** **That is, the two sūras name two different stages of the same material.**

**I record this difference; I do not derive from the two verses an order of creation or a table of stages. The text does not recount a process.**

### حَمَإٍ مَّسْنُونٍ

The **ح-م-أ** root: **thick, blackish clay whose smell has altered.** *Ḥamaʾ* — the black mud gathered at the bottom of a pool of water. The lexicographers record the word's aspect of having **darkened and gone rank**.

**And this word supports the second branch of *ṣalṣāl*** — because *ḥamaʾ* is already clay whose smell has altered. **I record this as a supporting indication.**

**مَّسْنُون** — root **س-ن-ن**. **The lexicographers differ over what the word corresponds to:**

| Reading | Which branch of the root | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Sanna* — **to pour into a mould, to give shape** | **Shaped** mud |
| 2 | *Sanna* — **to make flow, to pour** (the root of the word *sunna*) | **Poured out**, in a flowing state |
| 3 | *Sanna'l-ḥadīda* — **to whet, to rub** | **Rubbed and kneaded** |
| 4 | The word's being connected with *āsin* (gone rank) | **Altered, gone rank** |

**All four explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**There is a shared root that must be recorded:** in the thirteenth verse *sunnatu'l-awwalīn* occurred — the same root. **And there it was in the sense of "an opened road".** **I set up no link of meaning between the two usages; I only record that the same root occurs in two different branches within the sūra.**

### مِن نَّارِ ٱلسَّمُومِ

The **س-م-م** root: **pore, opening; and poison.** ***Samūm* — the scorching hot wind that works into the pores.**

**The word was analysed at `052-tur.md` 52/27 (*wa waqānā ʿadhāba'l-samūm*), and its occurrence at `056-vakia.md` 56/42 was named there too. I rely on those treatments.**

**What was recorded in the section on al-Ṭūr:**

> The word names a phenomenon known in the desert: **a waterless, very hot wind that dries a person out.** **And note: the word is the name of a *wind*.**

**The difference belonging here is this, and it must be recorded:**

| Place | Construction | For what |
|---|---|---|
| al-Ṭūr 52/27 / al-Wāqiʿa 56/42 | *ʿAdhāba'l-samūm* / *samūmin wa ḥamīm* | **Punishment** — what is suffered |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/27** | *Nāri'l-samūm* | **The material of creation** — the origin of the jinn |

**The same word: in two sūras the name of a punishment, here the material of a being.** **I record this as an observation about the vocabulary.**

### The word order of the two sentences

**At `055-rahman.md` 55/14-15 it was recorded that the two sentences are in exactly the same mould and that this conveys equal treatment of the two kinds** (*khalaqa'l-insāna min…* / *wa khalaqa'l-jānna min…*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**Here the mould is different, and this must be recorded:**

| Sūra | Man | Jinn |
|---|---|---|
| al-Raḥmān 55/14-15 | *Khalaqa'l-insāna min…* | *Wa khalaqa'l-jānna min…* — **the same mould** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/26-27** | *Khalaqnā'l-insāna min…* | *Wa'l-**jānna** khalaqnāhu…* — **the object fronted** |

**In Arabic the bringing of the object before the verb conveys emphasis.** **And a note of time is added to it: *min qablu* — "before."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** in al-Raḥmān the two kinds were set side by side; **in al-Ḥijr the second is pulled forward in respect of time.** And this is necessary for the scene that is to come immediately after: **Iblīs's objection will rest on the priority of his own material.**

## 15/28-31 — وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى خَٰلِقٌۢ بَشَرًا مِّن صَلْصَٰلٍ مِّنْ حَمَإٍ مَّسْنُونٍ · فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ · فَسَجَدَ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ · إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ أَبَىٰٓ أَن يَكُونَ مَعَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ

*Wa idh qāla Rabbuka li'l-malāʾikati innī khāliqun basharan min ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn · Fa-idhā sawwaytuhu wa nafakhtu fīhi min rūḥī fa-qaʿū lahu sājidīn · Fa-sajada'l-malāʾikatu kulluhum ajmaʿūn · Illā Iblīsa abā an yakūna maʿa'l-sājidīn*

"Recall **when your Lord said to the angels: 'I am creating a human being from dry clay, from a moulded black mud.** · **When I have shaped him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down before him in prostration.**' · **All the angels prostrated together;** · **only Iblīs refused to be with those who prostrated.**"

### فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى

**These two stages were treated at `038-sad.md` 38/71-74, and the expression *nafakhtu fīhi min rūḥī* was analysed at `066-tahrim.md` 66/12. I rely on those treatments and do not repeat them.**

**The note of "two stages" recorded in the section on Ṣād holds here too:** *taswiya* (the completion of the shape) and *nafkh* (the breathing). **The command to prostrate is attached to the end of both.**

**The difference between Ṣād and al-Ḥijr must be recorded:** in Ṣād the material was *ṭīn* (clay), here *ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn.* **The same scene is recounted with the name of the material changed.**

### فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ

The **و-ق-ع** root: **to fall, to come down upon a place.** *Qaʿū* — "fall down, drop at once."

**The verb must be recorded: the command is not "prostrate" but "**fall down as those who prostrate**".** **That is, both the movement (*wuqūʿ*) and the state (*sājidīn*) are stated.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence pictures the prostration not as a ceremony but as **a falling that happens in an instant**. **And this will meet the word used for Iblīs in the thirty-first verse of the sūra: he *abā* — he held out.**

### كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ — two emphases

**In Arabic *kull* and *ajmaʿ* are both emphases of scope; the two used together leave no exception.**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence closes the scope twice, **and then an exception comes.** **That is, the exception comes not from within the scope but from outside it** — and the dispute over whether Iblīs was of the angels is born here.

**I do not enter this dispute.** **What the wording of the verse says is this: the command to prostrate was given to the angels, and Iblīs did not prostrate.** **In another place in the Qurʾān (al-Kahf 18/50 — `018-kehf.md`) it is announced that Iblīs was of the jinn; I rely on that treatment and do not join the two pieces of information to set up a new ruling.**

### أَبَىٰٓ

The **أ-ب-ي** root: **to refuse outright, to have nothing to do with.** The lexicographers describe *ibāʾ* as "the harshest form of not wanting": not the **choice** of not doing, but a **refusal to come near** doing.

**And in Ṣād different words were used in the same place: *istakbara wa kāna mina'l-kāfirīn* (38/74).**

| Sūra | The act of Iblīs |
|---|---|
| Ṣād 38/74 | *Istakbara* — **he grew proud** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/31** | *Abā* — **he refused** |

**The same event, with two different verbs.** **One names the cause (pride), the other the act (refusal).** This is a verifiable overlap between sūras.

## 15/32-35 — قَالَ يَٰٓإِبْلِيسُ مَا لَكَ أَلَّا تَكُونَ مَعَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · قَالَ لَمْ أَكُن لِّأَسْجُدَ لِبَشَرٍ خَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن صَلْصَٰلٍ مِّنْ حَمَإٍ مَّسْنُونٍ · قَالَ فَٱخْرُجْ مِنْهَا فَإِنَّكَ رَجِيمٌ · وَإِنَّ عَلَيْكَ ٱللَّعْنَةَ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ

*Qāla yā Iblīsu mā laka allā takūna maʿa'l-sājidīn · Qāla lam akun li-asjuda li-basharin khalaqtahu min ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn · Qāla fa-khruj minhā fa-innaka rajīm · Wa inna ʿalayka'l-laʿnata ilā yawmi'l-dīn*

"**'O Iblīs! What is the matter with you, that you are not with those who prostrate?'** · **He said: 'I am not one to prostrate before a human being You created from dry clay, from a moulded black mud.'** · **'Then get out of it; you are accursed.** · **And upon you is the curse until the day of judgement.'**"

### The framing of the question

**The question is not "why did you not prostrate": *mā laka allā takūna maʿa'l-sājidīn* — "what is the matter with you, that you are not with those who prostrate?"**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the question looks not at the act but at **the rank**. The matter is not a single piece of behaviour but **parting from the company.** **And Iblīs's answer accepts exactly this point:** *lam akun li-asjuda* — "I was not one to prostrate."

### لَمْ أَكُن لِّأَسْجُدَ — the weight of the pattern

**The sentence does not say "I did not prostrate". *Lam akun li-asjuda* — word for word: "it would not have been in my way to prostrate."**

**In Arabic this pattern (*lām al-juḥūd*) conveys that an act is contrary to the condition of the person.** **That is, Iblīs presents his not prostrating not as a decision but as a requirement of his own position.**

### The ground of the objection

**And the ground must be recorded: *li-basharin khalaqtahu min ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn.***

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** Iblīs repeats the **material** of man — with the very words the verse itself used. **That is, the objection does not reject the information given; it uses it as evidence.**

**And in Ṣād this objection was given in the form of a comparison:** *anā khayrun minhu khalaqtanī min nārin wa khalaqtahu min ṭīn* (38/76). **Here only one half of the comparison is present: he does not speak of his own material.**

| Sūra | The objection |
|---|---|
| Ṣād 38/76 | **Two-sided** — "I am of fire, he of clay" |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/33** | **One-sided** — only man's material is named |

**I record this as my own reading, its ground being the comparison of the two texts:** in al-Ḥijr the claim of superiority is **not stated, it is assumed.** The sentence puts his own position outside discussion. **And the sūra had already prepared this in the twenty-seventh verse: by saying that the jinn was created *before*.**

### فَٱخْرُجْ مِنْهَا — from where?

**The referent of the pronoun is not stated in the verse. The lexicographers differ:**

| Reading | *Minhā*, from where |
|---|---|
| 1 | **From the garden** |
| 2 | **From the sky / from the station where the angels are** |
| 3 | **From the position or rank he was in** |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference, and I do not enter into a description of place.**

**رَجِيم** — root **ر-ج-م**: **to stone, to drive off.** *Rajm* — stoning; *rajīm* — stoned, driven off.

**And the word had occurred in the seventeenth verse of the sūra: *min kulli shayṭānin rajīm*.** **That is, the attribute had already been used in the sūra before it was given to Iblīs.** **This is a repetition within the sūra that can be verified, and I record it as an observation about the word order: the sūra recounts first the attribute, then the moment at which the attribute was laid on.**

## 15/36-38 — قَالَ رَبِّ فَأَنظِرْنِىٓ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ · قَالَ فَإِنَّكَ مِنَ ٱلْمُنظَرِينَ · إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْوَقْتِ ٱلْمَعْلُومِ

*Qāla Rabbi fa-anẓirnī ilā yawmi yubʿathūn · Qāla fa-innaka mina'l-munẓarīn · Ilā yawmi'l-waqti'l-maʿlūm*

"**He said: 'My Lord! Then give me respite until the day they are raised.'** · **'You are of those given respite** · **— until the day of the known time.'**"

### The granting of the request and its narrowing

**What must be recorded is the difference between what was asked and what was given:**

| | **Asked** | **Given** |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | *Ilā yawmi **yubʿathūn*** | *Ilā yawmi'l-**waqti'l-maʿlūm*** |
| Limit | **Until the raising** | **Until the known time** |

**The request is accepted, but the term is described with another word.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two expressions:** Iblīs asked for a day **that he himself knew** (the raising). **The answer bound the limit to a time that is *known* — but by whom it is known was not said.** **That is, the term is given; the knowledge that determines the term is not left to the other party.**

**And the attribute *maʿlūm* comes for the third time in the sūra** (4, 21, 38) — **it was set out in a table in the section on the structure.**

**م-ن-ظ-ر / ن-ظ-ر** — *inẓār*: to grant a term. **And the word had occurred in the eighth verse of the sūra:** *wa mā kānū idhan **munẓarīn*** — had the angels been sent down they would have been given no respite.

| Verse | *Munẓarīn* | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | **Would not have been given** | Those who disbelieve |
| **37** | **Was given** | Iblīs |

**I record this as a weave within the sūra, and it can be verified:** the same word, twenty-nine verses apart, one a respite refused and one a respite granted.

## 15/39-40 — قَالَ رَبِّ بِمَآ أَغْوَيْتَنِى لَأُزَيِّنَنَّ لَهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ · إِلَّا عِبَادَكَ مِنْهُمُ ٱلْمُخْلَصِينَ

*Qāla Rabbi bimā aghwaytanī la-uzayyinanna lahum fī'l-arḍi wa la-ughwiyannahum ajmaʿīn · Illā ʿibādaka minhumu'l-mukhlaṣīn*

"**He said: 'My Lord! For Your having led me astray, I shall surely make things fair-seeming to them on the earth and shall surely lead them all astray** · **— except those of Your servants who have been made pure.'**"

### لَأُزَيِّنَنَّ — the owner of the verb *tazyīn*

**This is one of the strongest textual data of the sūra and needs to be given in a table.**

**The ز-ي-ن root and the verb *tazyīn* were treated in several places in the index. And in every place where it was treated, what was recorded was that the verb comes in the *passive* — that is, that the one who adorns is not named:**

| Place | Expression | The voice of the verb | The doer |
|---|---|---|---|
| `035-fatir.md` 35/8 | *A-fa-man **zuyyina** lahu sūʾu ʿamalihi fa-raʾāhu ḥasanā* | **Passive** | **Not said** |
| `040-gafir.md` 40/37 | *Wa kadhālika **zuyyina** li-Firʿawna sūʾu ʿamalih* | **Passive** | **Not said** |
| `037-saffat.md` 37/6 | *Innā **zayyannā**'l-samāʾa'l-dunyā* | **Active — first person plural** | **God** (of the sky) |
| `015-hicr.md` 15/16 | *Wa **zayyannāhā** li'l-nāẓirīn* | **Active — first person plural** | **God** (of the sky) |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/39** | *La-**uzayyinanna** lahum fī'l-arḍ* | **Active — first person singular** | **Iblīs** |

**What the table shows is this, and I record it as my own reading, its ground being the differences of voice and person in the verbs:**

**One.** **The making of an evil deed fair-seeming is recounted in the Qurʾān with the passive pattern** — in Fāṭir and in Ghāfir alike. **In the section on Fāṭir this was recorded under the heading "the one who adorns is not named".**

**Two.** **The active pattern is used in two places, and in both the object is *the sky*** — in al-Ṣāffāt and in the sixteenth verse of this sūra. **That is, when the doer of the verb *tazyīn* is stated openly, it is attached to a good work.**

**Three.** **And the place in the Qurʾān where the verb is ascribed to Iblīs is here — and this is not a *report* but an *undertaking* out of Iblīs's own mouth.**

**Four.** **The verb comes with emphasis: *la-uzayyinanna* — the lām and the doubled nūn.** That is, the strongest pattern of undertaking.

**The sum of these four gives the following finding, and I record it as my own reading:** the Qurʾān generally does not say **by whom** the making fair-seeming is done. **And here the one who says it is not the text — it is the adorner himself.** **That is, the information is given not as an announcement but as a confession.**

**It is important to preserve this distinction:** when the text recounts a person's seeing evil as beautiful, it gives no doer. **The one who states the doer is the party declaring his own work.**

### بِمَآ أَغْوَيْتَنِى

The **غ-و-ي** root: **to stray from the road, to go astray.** *Ghayy* — the opposite of the right road, error.

**The lexicographers differ over the function of the particle *bimā*:**

| Reading | *Bimā* | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The causal *bāʾ*** | "Because You led me astray" |
| 2 | **The *bāʾ* of oath** | "By Your having led me astray" |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And it must be recorded that the verb will return within the sūra:** *wa la-**ughwiyannahum*** (39) → *illā mani'ttabaʿaka mina'l-**ghāwīn*** (42). **The same root, one an undertaking and one a limit.**

### إِلَّا عِبَادَكَ مِنْهُمُ ٱلْمُخْلَصِينَ

**The خ-ل-ص root was analysed in detail at `039-zumer.md` 39/1-3, and what was recorded there holds here too; I rely on that treatment:**

> **The root: a thing's being separated from what has been mixed into it and becoming pure.** The straining of oil or milk and its separation from its dregs is recounted with this root. **That is, *ikhlāṣ* is not the adding of something — but the sifting out.**

**And in the section on al-Zumar two patterns were set out in a table:**

| Verse | Word | Pattern | To whom it belongs |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/2 | *Mukhliṣan* | **Active participle** — one who makes pure | **The work of man** |
| al-Zumar 39/3 | *Al-khāliṣ* | **An attribute** — that which is pure | **The religion itself** |

**Now a third pattern is added here, and the difference must be worked out:**

| Pattern | Reading | Sense | Who is the doer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **مُخْلِص** *(mukhliṣ)* | Active participle | **One who makes pure** | **The servant** |
| **مُخْلَص** *(mukhlaṣ)* | **Passive participle** | **One who has been made pure** | **Another** |

**The word in the verse is *al-mukhlaṣīn* — a passive participle.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pattern itself:** the group Iblīs leaves outside is named not as **those who become pure by their own effort** but as **those who have been made pure**. **The doer of the verb is not in the sentence; but the pattern says that the work is done from outside.**

**And this agrees with the other half of the verse: *ʿibādaka* — "Your servants."** **The belonging is already stated.**

**A difference of variant reading is transmitted, and it bears directly on this distinction:** the word has been read both as *mukhlaṣīn* (passive participle) and as *mukhliṣīn* (active participle). **I give no imam's name.** **The two readings correspond to the two patterns above and change the sense markedly; I do not impose a preference.**

**The same word occurs at `038-sad.md` 38/83 as well** (*illā ʿibādaka minhumu'l-mukhlaṣīn*) — **the same sentence in two sūras, to the letter.** This is a repetition that can be verified.

## 15/41-42 — قَالَ هَٰذَا صِرَٰطٌ عَلَىَّ مُسْتَقِيمٌ · إِنَّ عِبَادِى لَيْسَ لَكَ عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَٰنٌ إِلَّا مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَكَ مِنَ ٱلْغَاوِينَ

*Qāla hādhā ṣirāṭun ʿalayya mustaqīm · Inna ʿibādī laysa laka ʿalayhim sulṭānun illā mani'ttabaʿaka mina'l-ghāwīn*

"**He said: 'This is a straight path leading to Me.** · **Over My servants you have no power — except those of the erring who follow you.'**"

### هَٰذَا صِرَٰطٌ عَلَىَّ مُسْتَقِيمٌ — the matter of the particle

**There is the particle *ʿalā* in the sentence, and the lexicographers differ over what it conveys:**

| Reading | *ʿAlayya* | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Leading to Me"** — the destination of the road | The straight road comes out at Me |
| 2 | **"Incumbent upon Me"** — an undertaking | Showing the straight road belongs to Me |
| 3 | **"Under My watch"** — oversight | The road is in My keeping |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**A difference of variant reading is also transmitted:** the word has also been read as *ʿaliyyun* (exalted); on that reading the sense becomes "this is an exalted and straight road". **I give no imam's name.**

**What must be recorded is *where* the sentence is said:** immediately after Iblīs's undertaking. **That is, what is given in answer to a threat is the existence of a road.** **I record this as an observation about the word order: the answer rests not on refuting the threat but on setting something over against it.**

### لَيْسَ لَكَ عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَٰنٌ

***Sulṭān* — proof, argument; and the power of decision. The root س-ل-ط: to have the power of disposal over something.**

**And this sentence is the same as what Satan says out of his own mouth at `014-ibrahim.md` 14/22:** *wa mā kāna liya ʿalaykum min sulṭānin illā an daʿawtukum fa'stajabtum lī.*

| Place | Who says it | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Ibrāhīm 14/22 | **Satan** | **A confession** — on the day of reckoning |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/42** | **God** | **A ruling** — a limit set from the outset |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the two verses use the same word:** the same limit is declared at the outset in one sūra and confessed at the end in the other. **The two texts confirm one another.**

**And the exception must be recorded: *illā mani'ttabaʿaka mina'l-ghāwīn* — "except those of the erring who follow you."**

**A point of word order:** the exception conveys not Satan's power but **the consequence of following.** **The verb is *ittabaʿa* — to follow; that is, the movement comes from the other side.** This is on the same line as the two-verb description at Ibrāhīm 14/22 (*daʿawtukum* / *fa'stajabtum lī*).

## 15/43-44 — وَإِنَّ جَهَنَّمَ لَمَوْعِدُهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ · لَهَا سَبْعَةُ أَبْوَٰبٍ لِّكُلِّ بَابٍ مِّنْهُمْ جُزْءٌ مَّقْسُومٌ

*Wa inna jahannama la-mawʿiduhum ajmaʿīn · Lahā sabʿatu abwābin li-kulli bābin minhum juzʾun maqsūm*

"**Hell is the appointed meeting place of them all.** · **It has seven gates; for each gate there is an allotted portion of them.**"

### مَوْعِد

A noun of place or time from the **و-ع-د** root: **a place or time of meeting, an appointed point.**

**The choice of the word is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** hell is named here not as a place of punishment but as **a place of appointment**. **The word carries a sense announcing that the parties come there *by agreement*** — and this agrees with the verb *ittabaʿaka* (they followed) of the preceding verse.

### سَبْعَةُ أَبْوَٰبٍ

**The number is in the wording of the verse and I record it as it stands.**

**Various explanations of what the gates are and by what they are divided are transmitted in the classical commentaries.** **The wording of the verse gives no classification; for this reason I set up no table and do not enter into description.**

**I record it further per the method (`STYLE.md`): no calculation is built upon this number.** **The same boundary was set at `097-kadr.md` for the thousand months; I rely on that treatment.**

### جُزْءٌ مَّقْسُومٌ

The **ق-س-م** root: **to divide, to apportion.** *Qisma* — the share set apart.

***Maqsūm* is a passive participle and its pattern must be recorded:** the same pattern as the *maʿlūm* weave of the sūra. **It was set out in a table in the section on the structure: *maʿlūm* at 4, 21, 38, and *maqsūm* here.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared pattern:** throughout the sūra time, measure and respite were named as determined; **here, with the same pattern, a *share* is determined.** **All four words say "not at random".**

## 15/45-48 — إِنَّ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ فِى جَنَّٰتٍ وَعُيُونٍ · ٱدْخُلُوهَا بِسَلَٰمٍ ءَامِنِينَ · وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِى صُدُورِهِم مِّنْ غِلٍّ إِخْوَٰنًا عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُّتَقَٰبِلِينَ · لَا يَمَسُّهُمْ فِيهَا نَصَبٌ وَمَا هُم مِّنْهَا بِمُخْرَجِينَ

*Inna'l-muttaqīna fī jannātin wa ʿuyūn · Udkhulūhā bi-salāmin āminīn · Wa nazaʿnā mā fī ṣudūrihim min ghillin ikhwānan ʿalā sururin mutaqābilīn · Lā yamassuhum fīhā naṣabun wa mā hum minhā bi-mukhrajīn*

"**Those who guard themselves are in gardens and springs.** · **'Enter it in peace, secure.'** · **We drew out whatever was in their breasts of rancour; they are brothers, on couches facing one another.** · **No weariness touches them there, nor are they driven out of it.**"

### ءَامِنِينَ — one of the key words of the sūra

The **أ-م-ن** root: **to be secure.** **The root was treated at `002-bakara.md` and at `106-kureys.md` 106/4. I rely on that treatment.**

**And the word will occur twice in the sūra — and the two occurrences are opposites of one another:**

| Verse | Expression | Who | How the security comes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **46** | *Udkhulūhā bi-salāmin **āminīn*** | **Those who guard themselves** | **Given** — it is said at the entrance |
| **82** | *Yanḥitūna mina'l-jibāli buyūtan **āminīn*** | **The people of al-Ḥijr** | **Their own making** — by carving the mountain |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two occurrences of the same word:** the sūra sets the two sources of security over against one another. **One is said to a person, the other he digs with his own hand.** **And in the eighty-third verse what the second comes to will be seen: *fa-akhadhathumu'l-ṣayḥatu muṣbiḥīn*.**

### وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِى صُدُورِهِم مِّنْ غِلٍّ

The **ن-ز-ع** root: **to pull a thing out of the place it is in.** **The root was treated at `079-naziat.md` in connection with the name of that sūra. I rely on that treatment.**

The **غ-ل-ل** root: **a thing's entering into something secretly.** *Ghill* — rancour hidden within, concealed enmity. **From the same root come *ghull* (a yoke) and *ghulūl* (secretly taking goods from the spoils).** **The common core: to get inside secretly.**

**I record this as an observation about the vocabulary:** the root of the word says that rancour settles **unnoticed**. **And the verb (*nazaʿnā* — we drew out) sets up the picture of something settled being extracted.** **The two words together give a consistent picture: what got in secretly is taken out by force.**

**إِخْوَٰنًا عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُّتَقَٰبِلِينَ** — **"facing one another."**

**The word *mutaqābilīn* was treated at `037-saffat.md` 37/44 and at `056-vakia.md` 56/15-16. I rely on that treatment.**

**The order must be recorded:** first the rancour is taken out, **then** they sit facing one another. **I record this as an observation about the word order: sitting face to face is named after what stood between has been removed.**

### لَا يَمَسُّهُمْ فِيهَا نَصَبٌ

The **ن-ص-ب** root: **to set up; and toil, weariness.** The lexicographers join the two branches by way of "the effort spent in setting a thing up".

**And the last sentence must be recorded: *wa mā hum minhā bi-**mukhrajīn*** — "they are not driven out of it."**

**The verb is from the same root (خ-ر-ج) as the thirty-fourth verse of the sūra:** *qāla fa-**khruj** minhā fa-innaka rajīm* — what was said to Iblīs.

| Verse | Verb | Who | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **34** | *Fa-khruj minhā* — **a command** | **Iblīs** | **Driven out** |
| **48** | *Mā hum minhā bi-mukhrajīn* | **Those who guard themselves** | **Not driven out** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two occurrences of the same root:** the sūra uses the act of driving out first as the name of a punishment, then gives its negation as a reward. **There are fourteen verses between the two, and both are in the same block.**

## 15/49-50 — نَبِّئْ عِبَادِىٓ أَنِّىٓ أَنَا ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ · وَأَنَّ عَذَابِى هُوَ ٱلْعَذَابُ ٱلْأَلِيمُ

*Nabbiʾ ʿibādī annī ana'l-Ghafūru'l-Raḥīm · Wa anna ʿadhābī huwa'l-ʿadhābu'l-alīm*

"**Inform My servants that I am the much-forgiving, the much-merciful** · **and that My punishment is the painful punishment.**"

### The balance of the two sentences

**The two verses are the two halves of a single sentence and are built symmetrically. But the symmetry is not complete — and the difference must be recorded:**

| | **Verse 49** | **Verse 50** |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | **The Essence** — "I" | **An attribute** — "My punishment" |
| Structure | *Annī **ana** al-Ghafūru'l-Raḥīm* | *Wa anna **ʿadhābī huwa** al-ʿadhābu'l-alīm* |
| Number of elements | **Two names** (Ghafūr, Raḥīm) | **One attribute** (alīm) |
| The separating pronoun | *Ana* | *Huwa* |
| To what it is attached | **Directly to God** | **To the punishment** — a word comes in between |

**The difference is this, and I record it as my own reading, its ground being the table:** in the first sentence the predicate is attached **directly to the Essence** — "I am the forgiving." **In the second sentence a word comes in between: the punishment.** That is, it does not say "I am the one who punishes"; **it says "My punishment is painful."**

**And while there are two names on the side of forgiveness, there is one attribute on the side of punishment.**

### A comparison with `014-ibrahim.md` 14/7

**The same balance was set up in the seventh verse of Sūrat Ibrāhīm within a conditional sentence, and four differences were set out in a table there. I rely on that treatment.**

**A comparison of the two places:**

| | **Ibrāhīm 14/7** | **al-Ḥijr 15/49-50** |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | **Two conditional sentences** | **Two declarative sentences** |
| The positive side | *La-**azīdannakum*** — **a verb**, with the pronoun of the one addressed | *Ana'l-**Ghafūru'l-Raḥīm*** — **two names** |
| The negative side | *Inna **ʿadhābī** la-shadīd* | *Wa anna **ʿadhābī** huwa'l-ʿadhābu'l-alīm* |
| The shared element | ***ʿAdhābī*** — "My punishment" | ***ʿAdhābī*** — "My punishment" |
| The shared structure | **On the positive side the doer is stated; on the negative side the quality of the punishment is named** | **The same** |

**In two sūras the same balance is set up in two different modes, and the shared element is the same word: *ʿadhābī*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared word order of the two texts:** when the Qurʾān sets the two sides over against one another, it attaches **forgiveness to the doer, and punishment not to an act but to an *object***. **That is, "I forgive" and "My punishment is severe" do not stand on the same grammatical level.** **The same asymmetry is in both sūras; this is the repetition in two places of something that in a single verse could be counted a coincidence.**

### نَبِّئْ

The **ن-ب-أ** root: **important, new and profitable news.** **The root was analysed at `078-nebe.md` in connection with the name of that sūra, and the difference between *nabaʾ* and *khabar* was recorded there. I do not repeat it here.**

**And the verb will come once more two verses later in the sūra: *wa nabbiʾhum ʿan ḍayfi Ibrāhīm* (51).** **That is, the same command, one for two names and one for a story.** **I record this as an observation about the word order: immediately after giving the news of forgiveness and of punishment, the sūra begins to recount an event in which the two appear together** — glad tidings for Abraham, destruction for the people of Lot.

## 15/51-53 — وَنَبِّئْهُمْ عَن ضَيْفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · إِذْ دَخَلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِ فَقَالُوا۟ سَلَٰمًا قَالَ إِنَّا مِنكُمْ وَجِلُونَ · قَالُوا۟ لَا تَوْجَلْ إِنَّا نُبَشِّرُكَ بِغُلَٰمٍ عَلِيمٍ

*Wa nabbiʾhum ʿan ḍayfi Ibrāhīm · Idh dakhalū ʿalayhi fa-qālū salāman qāla innā minkum wajilūn · Qālū lā tawjal innā nubashshiruka bi-ghulāmin ʿalīm*

"**Inform them too of the guests of Abraham.** · **When they entered upon him and said 'peace', he said: 'We are afraid of you.'** · **They said: 'Do not be afraid! We bring you glad tidings of a knowing boy.'**"

### ضَيْف — a singular word, a plural verb

The **ض-ي-ف** root: **guest.** **And because in Arabic the word comes from a verbal-noun origin, it is used for both singular and plural** — this is why *ḍayf* is singular while the verb that follows (*dakhalū*) is plural.

**The same story was treated at `051-zariyat.md` 51/24-30. I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

**I record the differences between al-Dhāriyāt and al-Ḥijr, because this is a comparison that can be verified:**

| Element | **al-Dhāriyāt 51/24-30** | **al-Ḥijr 15/51-56** |
|---|---|---|
| The attribute of the guests | *Al-mukramīn* — **honoured** | No attribute |
| The scene of hospitality | **Present** — *bi-ʿijlin samīn* (a fatted calf) | **Absent** |
| The expression of the fear | *Fa-awjasa minhum **khīfatan*** — fear fell within him | *Innā minkum **wajilūn*** — he says it openly |
| The calming | *Lā **takhaf*** | *Lā **tawjal*** |
| Who receives the tidings | **Abraham and his wife** — the woman's reaction is recounted | **Abraham alone** |
| Abraham's reaction | **Not recounted** | *A-bashshartumūnī ʿalā an massaniya'l-kibar* |

**The two sūras show the same event from two different places.** **I record it per the method (`STYLE.md`): I do not enter into detail the Qurʾān does not give** — who the guests were, how many they were, how they appeared, is not in the text.

### وَجِلُون — root: و-ج-ل

**The root was analysed at `022-hac.md` 22/35, and what was recorded there holds here too; I rely on that treatment:**

> **The root: shuddering with fear, an inward trembling.** The lexicographers describe *wajal* as "the sudden stirring of the heart". **And the distinguishing side of the word: *khawf* is generally felt at a harm to come; *wajal* is a momentary, bodily shudder.**

**What belongs here is this, and it must be recorded:** the word used in al-Dhāriyāt was *khīfa* (from the خ-و-ف root); **here it is *wajal*.**

| Sūra | Word | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| al-Dhāriyāt 51/28 | *Khīfa* — **fear falling within** | The verb: *awjasa* (he concealed it, felt it within) |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/52** | *Wajilūn* — **a shudder** | **Said openly** |

**I record this as an observation about the vocabulary:** in al-Dhāriyāt the fear was **concealed** (*awjasa* — he held it within), in al-Ḥijr it is **put into words.** The two sūras give two different faces of the same moment.

**And the response comes with the same root: *lā tawjal* — "do not be afraid."** **That is, the answer negates the very word that was used.** This is a weave that can be verified by way of the word order.

### بِغُلَٰمٍ عَلِيمٍ

**The attribute must be recorded: *ʿalīm* — knowing.** **The same attribute was used in al-Dhāriyāt too** (*bi-ghulāmin ʿalīm*). **At al-Ṣāffāt 37/101, however, *bi-ghulāmin ḥalīm* (forbearing) occurs.**

**Whether the two attributes belong to two different children or to the same one is debated in the classical commentaries.** **I do not enter this debate; under the rule of not entering into detail the Qurʾān does not give, I make no preference as to which verse recounts which child.**

## 15/54-56 — قَالَ أَبَشَّرْتُمُونِى عَلَىٰٓ أَن مَّسَّنِىَ ٱلْكِبَرُ فَبِمَ تُبَشِّرُونَ · قَالُوا۟ بَشَّرْنَٰكَ بِٱلْحَقِّ فَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ ٱلْقَٰنِطِينَ · قَالَ وَمَن يَقْنَطُ مِن رَّحْمَةِ رَبِّهِۦٓ إِلَّا ٱلضَّآلُّونَ

*Qāla a-bashshartumūnī ʿalā an massaniya'l-kibaru fa-bima tubashshirūn · Qālū bashsharnāka bi'l-ḥaqqi fa-lā takun mina'l-qāniṭīn · Qāla wa man yaqnaṭu min raḥmati Rabbihi illā'l-ḍāllūn*

"**He said: 'Do you bring me glad tidings when old age has come upon me? Of what, then, do you bring me tidings?'** · **They said: 'We have brought you glad tidings with the truth; do not be of those who despair.'** · **He said: 'And who despairs of the mercy of his Lord but those who have gone astray?'**"

### فَبِمَ تُبَشِّرُونَ — the framing of the question

**The question is in two stages: first astonishment (*a-bashshartumūnī*), then a question (*fa-bima tubashshirūn*).**

**The lexicographers differ over the sense of the second question:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | **"On what ground do you bring tidings?"** — the ground is asked |
| 2 | **"Of what do you bring tidings?"** — the content is asked, in astonishment |
| 3 | The question is not a **denial** but a **request for confirmation** |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**What must be recorded is what the answer is: *bashsharnāka bi'l-ḥaqq* — "we have brought you tidings with the truth."** **That is, whether or not the question was directed at the ground, the answer gives the ground.**

**And a difference of variant reading is transmitted:** differences are conveyed as to whether the *nūn* at the end of the word *tubashshirūn* is read doubled or undoubled. **It does not change the sense; I give no imam's name.**

### عَلَىٰٓ أَن مَّسَّنِىَ ٱلْكِبَرُ

***ʿAlā* here conveys "in spite of". The same particle occurred at `014-ibrahim.md` 14/39 in Abraham's praise:** *wahaba lī **ʿalā'l-kibari** Ismāʿīla wa Isḥāq.*

| Place | Expression | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Ḥijr 15/54** | *ʿAlā an massaniya'l-kibar* | **A question** — the moment of the tidings |
| Ibrāhīm 14/39 | *ʿAlā'l-kibar* | **Praise** — afterwards |

**The two sūras give the same situation in two different modes.** **This was recorded in the section on Ibrāhīm too, and it is an overlap that can be verified.**

**The verb is worth recording: *massaniya* — "it touched me."** **The م-س-س root: to touch.** **The word pictures old age not as a state but as something that comes from outside and makes contact.**

### فَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ ٱلْقَٰنِطِينَ — root: ق-ن-ط

**The root was analysed at `039-zumer.md` 39/53, and it was compared there with its occurrence at `041-fussilet.md` 41/49. I rely on those treatments.**

**The table recorded in the section on al-Zumar:**

| Place | *Qunūṭ* |
|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/49 | **Description** — man is like this |
| al-Zumar 39/53 | **Prohibition** — do not be like this |

**Now a third usage is added here, and the table widens:**

| Place | Expression | Mode | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/49 | *Yaʾūsun **qanūṭ*** | **Description** | **The text** — about man |
| al-Zumar 39/53 | *Lā **taqnaṭū** min raḥmatillāh* | **Prohibition** | **A command** — to the servants |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/55** | *Fa-lā takun mina'l-**qāniṭīn*** | **A warning** | **The angels** — to a prophet |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/56** | *Wa man **yaqnaṭu** min raḥmati Rabbihi illā'l-ḍāllūn* | **A question** | **Abraham** — as his answer |

**Four occurrences must be recorded, and they draw this sequence:** man's tendency is described → it is forbidden → it is recalled to a prophet → and he repeats it in the form of a question.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of the four verses:** Abraham's answer is not a defence. **He does not reject the warning; he gives it back, widened.** The angels said "do not be of those who despair"; **he says, "and who despairs?"**

### إِلَّا ٱلضَّآلُّونَ

The **ض-ل-ل** root: **to lose the way.** **The root was treated at `001-fatiha.md` and at `093-duha.md` 93/7. I rely on that treatment.**

**The pattern of the sentence must be recorded: a question + an exception.** **In Arabic this structure (*man… illā…*) is one of the strongest forms of negation:** it says that despairing is equated with having lost one's way.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence does not say "despairing is wrong". **It says who the one who despairs is** — and the definition attaches a behaviour to a position.

## 15/57-60 — قَالَ فَمَا خَطْبُكُمْ أَيُّهَا ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّآ أُرْسِلْنَآ إِلَىٰ قَوْمٍ مُّجْرِمِينَ · إِلَّآ ءَالَ لُوطٍ إِنَّا لَمُنَجُّوهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ · إِلَّا ٱمْرَأَتَهُۥ قَدَّرْنَآ إِنَّهَا لَمِنَ ٱلْغَٰبِرِينَ

*Qāla fa-mā khaṭbukum ayyuhā'l-mursalūn · Qālū innā ursilnā ilā qawmin mujrimīn · Illā āla Lūṭin innā la-munajjūhum ajmaʿīn · Illā'mraʾatahu qaddarnā innahā la-mina'l-ghābirīn*

"**He said: 'What, then, is your real business, O messengers?'** · **'We have been sent to a guilty people** · **— save the family of Lot; all of them we shall deliver.** · **Save his wife; we have determined that she is of those who stay behind.'**"

### خَطْب

The **خ-ط-ب** root: **to speak, to address; and from this, "a matter important enough to be spoken about".** *Khuṭba*, *khiṭāb*, *khāṭib* are from the same root.

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** a *khaṭb* is not an ordinary matter — it is a matter that **requires talking about**.

**The same question occurs at `051-zariyat.md` 51/31 in exactly the same words:** *qāla fa-mā khaṭbukum ayyuhā'l-mursalūn.* **The same sentence in two sūras** — a repetition that can be verified.

### The structure with two exceptions

**The word order must be recorded: in three verses two exceptions come one upon the other.**

| Step | Expression | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ilā qawmin mujrimīn* | **A people** |
| 2 | *Illā āla Lūṭ* | **A family is taken out** |
| 3 | *Illā'mraʾatahu* | **One person from the family is put back** |

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence narrows twice, the second from within the first. **That is, deliverance is not given in the name of a family; even after the family is named, an exception remains.**

**And at `066-tahrim.md` 66/10 the same line was treated for Lot's wife** — two prophets' wives were given there as an example and it was recorded that the marriage tie does not deliver. **I rely on that treatment.**

### قَدَّرْنَآ

**The ق-د-ر root occurred in the twenty-first verse of the sūra (*bi-qadarin maʿlūm*). Here it returns as a verb.**

| Verse | Word | What is determined |
|---|---|---|
| **21** | *Bi-qadarin maʿlūm* | **The measure of what comes down** |
| **60** | *Qaddarnā* | **The end of one person** |

**The same root, one a measure and one a judgement.** This is a repetition within the sūra that can be verified.

**And a difference of variant reading is transmitted:** the readings *qaddarnā* (doubled) and *qadarnā* (undoubled) are conveyed; the difference of sense is not marked. **I give no imam's name.**

**ٱلْغَٰبِرِينَ** — root **غ-ب-ر**: **to stay behind; and dust** (*ghabara* — it passed away / it stayed behind). The lexicographers record that the root is used in both directions. **The sense here is "those who stay behind"** — remaining together with those destroyed.

## 15/61-64 — فَلَمَّا جَآءَ ءَالَ لُوطٍ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · قَالَ إِنَّكُمْ قَوْمٌ مُّنكَرُونَ · قَالُوا۟ بَلْ جِئْنَٰكَ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ فِيهِ يَمْتَرُونَ · وَأَتَيْنَٰكَ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَإِنَّا لَصَٰدِقُونَ

*Fa-lammā jāʾa āla Lūṭini'l-mursalūn · Qāla innakum qawmun munkarūn · Qālū bal jiʾnāka bimā kānū fīhi yamtarūn · Wa ataynāka bi'l-ḥaqqi wa innā la-ṣādiqūn*

"**When the messengers came to the family of Lot,** · **Lot said: 'You are people unknown.'** · **They said: 'No, we have brought you the very thing they kept doubting.** · **We have brought you the truth; we are truthful.'**"

### قَوْمٌ مُّنكَرُونَ

The **ن-ك-ر** root: **not to recognise, to find strange.** *Munkar* — unrecognised; and from this, "not welcomed, rejected".

**The same expression occurred at `051-zariyat.md` 51/25 out of Abraham's mouth:** *qāla salāmun **qawmun munkarūn***. **The same sentence in two scenes, in the mouths of two different prophets.** This is a repetition that can be verified.

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sūra opens two encounters with the same word. **In both the first reaction is not to recognise** — and in both the guests introduce themselves with a piece of news.

### يَمْتَرُونَ

The **م-ر-ي** root: **to doubt, to dispute.** *Mirya* — doubt. The lexicographers record the concrete sense of the root as "to tug at the udder in order to milk it", and explain doubt by the picture of "tugging a matter back and forth".

**And the object of the word must be recorded: *bimā kānū fīhi yamtarūn* — "the thing they kept doubting."** **That is, what is brought is the very thing that was being disputed.**

**I record this as an observation:** the answer does not give the name of the punishment. **It names it by the other side's own doubt.** What they were disputing is coming.

## 15/65-66 — فَأَسْرِ بِأَهْلِكَ بِقِطْعٍ مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱتَّبِعْ أَدْبَٰرَهُمْ وَلَا يَلْتَفِتْ مِنكُمْ أَحَدٌ وَٱمْضُوا۟ حَيْثُ تُؤْمَرُونَ · وَقَضَيْنَآ إِلَيْهِ ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْأَمْرَ أَنَّ دَابِرَ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ مَقْطُوعٌ مُّصْبِحِينَ

*Fa-asri bi-ahlika bi-qiṭʿin mina'l-layli wa'ttabiʿ adbārahum wa lā yaltafit minkum aḥadun wa'mḍū ḥaythu tuʾmarūn · Wa qaḍaynā ilayhi dhālika'l-amra anna dābira hāʾulāʾi maqṭūʿun muṣbiḥīn*

"**Set out with your family in a part of the night; and follow behind them. Let none of you turn to look back. Go on to where you are commanded.** · **And We made known to him this decree: that by morning the last of these would be cut off.**"

### Four commands, in one sentence

**The verse strings four commands, and their order sets up a scene:**

| Order | Command | What it regulates |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Asri bi-ahlika bi-qiṭʿin mina'l-layl* | **Time** — a part of the night |
| 2 | *Wa'ttabiʿ adbārahum* | **Order** — Lot at the rear |
| 3 | *Wa lā yaltafit minkum aḥad* | **Gaze** — no turning back |
| 4 | *Wa'mḍū ḥaythu tuʾmarūn* | **Direction** — to where you are told |

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the commands determine time, order, gaze and direction separately. **Nothing is left to decision.**

The **س-ر-ي** root: **travelling by night.** *Sarā / asrā* — he walked by night. **The root was analysed at `017-isra.md` in connection with the name of that sūra. I do not repeat it here.**

**ٱتَّبِعْ أَدْبَٰرَهُمْ** — "follow behind them." **That is, Lot is to walk last.** **I record this as an observation: the command asks the leader to be not in front but behind** — the reason is not stated, but read together with the next command it lends itself to meaning that the company can be **seen**. **I give this as an inference, not as the wording of the verse.**

The **ل-ف-ت** root: **to turn a thing, to change its direction.** *Iltifāt* — to turn and look. **(The same word is used in the index as a term of rhetoric in the sense of "a shift in the direction of address"; the picture in the root is the same.)**

### دَابِر

The **د-ب-ر** root: **the back, the end of a thing.** *Dābir* — **the last remaining** of a company, its root, its remnant.

**The construction must be recorded: *dābira hāʾulāʾi maqṭūʿun* — "the back or root of these is cut."** **That is, the expression recounts that no one is left behind.**

**And *ittabiʿ adbārahum* (65) and *dābira hāʾulāʾi* (66) are from the same root.** **Two verses apart, one the back of those delivered and one the back of those destroyed.** **I record this as a weave within the sūra, and it can be verified.**

**مُّصْبِحِين** — root **ص-ب-ح**: to enter upon the morning. **The word will come once more in the eighty-third verse for the people of al-Ḥijr:** *fa-akhadhathumu'l-ṣayḥatu **muṣbiḥīn***. **The same word, at the hour of destruction of two peoples.**

## 15/67-71 — وَجَآءَ أَهْلُ ٱلْمَدِينَةِ يَسْتَبْشِرُونَ · قَالَ إِنَّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ ضَيْفِى فَلَا تَفْضَحُونِ · وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَلَا تُخْزُونِ · قَالُوٓا۟ أَوَلَمْ نَنْهَكَ عَنِ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · قَالَ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ بَنَاتِىٓ إِن كُنتُمْ فَٰعِلِينَ

*Wa jāʾa ahlu'l-madīnati yastabshirūn · Qāla inna hāʾulāʾi ḍayfī fa-lā tafḍaḥūn · Wa'ttaqullāha wa lā tukhzūn · Qālū a-wa-lam nanhaka ʿani'l-ʿālamīn · Qāla hāʾulāʾi banātī in kuntum fāʿilīn*

"**The people of the city came rejoicing.** · **Lot said: 'These are my guests; do not disgrace me.** · **Guard yourselves against God and do not shame me.'** · **They said: 'Did we not forbid you the whole world?'** · **He said: 'Here are my daughters, if you are going to act.'**"

### يَسْتَبْشِرُونَ

The **ب-ش-ر** root and *istibshār*: **to receive glad news, to rejoice.** **The root was treated at `053-necm.md` and at `075-kiyame.md`; its link with *bashar* (skin) — the news showing in the face — was recorded there. I do not repeat it here.**

**Its use here must be recorded, and this is a point of word order:** the same root was used thirteen verses earlier for the tidings the angels gave Abraham (*nubashshiruka*, *bashsharnāka*, *tubashshirūn* — 53, 54, 55).

| Verse | Word | Who is gladdened |
|---|---|---|
| **53-55** | *Bashshar-* (three times) | **Abraham** — the tidings of a child |
| **67** | *Yastabshirūn* | **The people of the city** — the arrival of the guests |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two occurrences of the root:** the sūra uses the same root thirteen verses apart in two opposite scenes. **One the glad tidings of a birth, the other a rejoicing that will become the cause of a destruction.**

### فَلَا تَفْضَحُونِ / وَلَا تُخْزُونِ

**Two verbs, two separate roots:**

- **ف-ض-ح** — *faḍīḥa*: **the coming to light of a hidden fault**, to be disgraced.
- **خ-ز-ي** — *khizy*: **humiliation, shame.**

**A point of word order must be recorded: both of Lot's sentences are about *himself*** — "do not disgrace me", "do not shame me." **He speaks of the guests but frames his demand by way of himself.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence rests on a social order in which a guest is counted as belonging to his host. **And the sentence in the middle attaches this to a measure: *wa'ttaqullāh* — "guard yourselves against God."** It is placed between the two sentences of shame.

### أَوَلَمْ نَنْهَكَ عَنِ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

**The framing of the answer must be recorded: a question, and inside it a past prohibition.**

**The lexicographers differ over what the expression *ʿani'l-ʿālamīn* — "from the worlds" — corresponds to:**

| Reading | Sense |
|---|---|
| 1 | We forbade you **to take people as guests** |
| 2 | We forbade you **to speak about people or to defend them** |
| 3 | We forbade you **to protect strangers** |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**What must be recorded is *by whom* the sentence is framed:** the party that laid down the prohibition is recalling the prohibition it laid down itself. **I record this as an observation: when a community makes its own order into a norm, it names the one who does not conform to it "a breaker of the prohibition".**

### هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ بَنَاتِى

**Different explanations of how the sentence is to be understood are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | Who *banātī* are |
|---|---|
| 1 | **His own daughters** — as an offer of marriage |
| 2 | **The women of the people** — because a prophet is counted the father of his community, he said "my daughters" |
| 3 | The expression is a **sentence of helplessness**; not an offer but the last point of speech |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference, and I derive no legal ruling from here.**

**I record it further per the method (`STYLE.md`): I do not enter into detail the Qurʾān does not give.** The text gives no information about the number of the daughters, who they were, or how the offer was met.

**What the wording of the verse says is this, and I keep within it:** Lot proposed an alternative and qualified it with a conditional clause: *in kuntum fāʿilīn* — "if you are going to act."

## 15/72-74 — لَعَمْرُكَ إِنَّهُمْ لَفِى سَكْرَتِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ · فَأَخَذَتْهُمُ ٱلصَّيْحَةُ مُشْرِقِينَ · فَجَعَلْنَا عَٰلِيَهَا سَافِلَهَا وَأَمْطَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ حِجَارَةً مِّن سِجِّيلٍ

*La-ʿamruka innahum la-fī sakratihim yaʿmahūn · Fa-akhadhathumu'l-ṣayḥatu mushriqīn · Fa-jaʿalnā ʿāliyahā sāfilahā wa amṭarnā ʿalayhim ḥijāratan min sijjīl*

"**By your life, they were floundering in their drunkenness.** · **The blast seized them at sunrise.** · **We turned the place upside down and rained upon them stones of baked clay.**"

### لَعَمْرُكَ — an oath

The **ع-م-ر** root: **lifetime, life; and to make a place flourish** (*ʿimāra*, *maʿmūr* are from the same root). **The root was treated at `052-tur.md` 52/4 (*al-bayti'l-maʿmūr*). I rely on that treatment.**

**The grammatical structure of the sentence: *la-ʿamruka* is the subject and its predicate is elided** (*qasamī* — "is my oath"). **In Arabic this ellipsis in oaths is frequently spoken of.**

**What must be recorded is the object of the oath.** **The lexicographers differ over who is addressed by this expression:**

| Reading | Who is addressed |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The Prophet** — his life is sworn by |
| 2 | **Lot** — said to him within the story |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And a note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** various assessments are made in the classical commentaries about a human being's life being sworn by in the Qurʾān. **I do not enter these assessments and record the wording of the verse as it stands.**

**سَكْرَة** — root **س-ك-ر**: drunkenness. **And the word is from the same root as the fifteenth verse of the sūra:** *innamā **sukkirat** abṣārunā.*

| Verse | Word | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **15** | *Sukkirat abṣārunā* | **Those who disbelieve** — a claim about themselves |
| **72** | *Fī sakratihim yaʿmahūn* | **The people of Lot** — the judgement of the text |

**I record this as a weave within the sūra, and it can be verified:** the same root, one as an excuse and one as a diagnosis.

The **ع-م-ه** root: **to wander about in bewilderment, to be unable to find one's direction.** The lexicographers record a difference between *ʿamiha* and *ʿamiya* (to be blind): the first is the loss of **insight**, the second of **the eye**.

### ٱلصَّيْحَة

The **ص-ي-ح** root: **a loud sound, a cry.** **The word was treated at `036-yasin.md` 36/29 and 36/49, and at `054-kamer.md` 54/31. I rely on those treatments.**

**مُشْرِقِين** — root **ش-ر-ق**: the rising of the sun. *Mushriq* (here) — being at sunrise.

**The note of time must be recorded:** in the sixty-sixth verse *muṣbiḥīn* (as they entered upon the morning) had been said; **here *mushriqīn* (as the sun rose).** **The two words point to two different points of the same stretch of time** and both recount the same event. **I record this as an observation about the word order: the hour announced and the hour that came to pass agree with one another.**

### فَجَعَلْنَا عَٰلِيَهَا سَافِلَهَا

**The expression word for word: "we made its high its low."**

**A point of word order must be recorded: two words, two opposite attributes, active participles on the same pattern.** **The sentence gives not a description but an *exchange of place*.**

**سِجِّيل** — **the lexicographers differ over the sense and root of the word, and this was recorded in the index:** at `083-mutaffifin.md` it was stated that there are those who set up a kinship between *sijjīn* and *sijjīl*, but that this is counted weak and that **the two words come from different roots**; `021-enbiya.md` relied on that record. **I rely on those treatments.**

**At `051-zariyat.md` 51/33-34 it was recorded that *ḥijāratan min ṭīn* (stones of clay) is used for the same event, and that *sijjīl* is *widely said* to mean "baked clay, brick". I rely on that treatment and do not speak with certainty.**

## 15/75-77 — إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتٍ لِّلْمُتَوَسِّمِينَ · وَإِنَّهَا لَبِسَبِيلٍ مُّقِيمٍ · إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَةً لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

*Inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li'l-mutawassimīn · Wa innahā la-bi-sabīlin muqīm · Inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li'l-muʾminīn*

"**In this there are signs for those who know how to read traces.** · **That city still stands on a road in use.** · **In this there is a sign for those who believe.**"

### ٱلْمُتَوَسِّمِين — root: و-س-م

**The root was analysed at `068-kalem.md` 68/16, and what was recorded there holds here too; I rely on that treatment:**

> **وَسَمَ — the concrete sense of the root is to brand, to mark:** *wasama'l-baʿīra* — he branded the camel with a hot iron. **Its derivatives:** *sima* (a brand), *sīmā* (a mark on the face — the word Turkish has borrowed as "sima"), *mawsim* (a time known by particular marks — the word Turkish has borrowed as "mevsim", season).

**That the lexicographers differ over the root of the word *sīmā* (و-س-م / س-و-م) was recorded at `055-rahman.md` and at `048-fetih.md` 48/29; I rely on that treatment and do not speak with certainty.**

**Now what belongs here: the *mutawassim* pattern.**

**An active participle of the *tafaʿʿul* form**, and this form in Arabic conveys **effort, repetition and gradual acquisition**. **That is, a *mutawassim* is not "one who sees the brand" — but one who tries to read from the brand, who looks at the mark and draws out what lies behind it.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pattern itself:** the verse says that the lesson is not visible to everyone. **But it attaches the distinction not to a privilege but to a *work* — the work of reading traces.** The trace is in plain view; the reading of it requires an effort.

**And as was recorded at `053-necm.md`, the Qurʾān repeats the same phenomenon by attaching it to different qualities. Here this happens two verses apart:**

| Verse | Quality | What is given |
|---|---|---|
| **75** | *Li'l-mutawassimīn* — **those who read traces** | *Āyāt* — **plural** |
| **77** | *Li'l-muʾminīn* — **those who believe** | *Āyatan* — **singular** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of number:** to the one who reads traces, **many** signs; to the one who believes, **one** sign. **The two do not conflict; the two do different work** — one draws much out of the event, the other takes the event as a whole.

### وَإِنَّهَا لَبِسَبِيلٍ مُّقِيمٍ

***Muqīm* — "standing, still upright."** **That is, the road is still in use.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse says that the ruins are not in a far and unreachable place. **The lesson is not somewhere reached by turning aside — it is on a road that is travelled over.**

**The same line was treated at `037-saffat.md` 37/137-138** (*wa innakum la-tamurrūna ʿalayhim muṣbiḥīn*). **I rely on that treatment.**

## 15/78-79 — وَإِن كَانَ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْأَيْكَةِ لَظَٰلِمِينَ · فَٱنتَقَمْنَا مِنْهُمْ وَإِنَّهُمَا لَبِإِمَامٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Wa in kāna aṣḥābu'l-Aykati la-ẓālimīn · Fa'ntaqamnā minhum wa innahumā la-bi-imāmin mubīn*

"**The people of al-Ayka too were wrongdoers.** · **So We took retribution from them. Both are on a plain road.**"

### أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْأَيْكَة

***Ayka*: root أ-ي-ك: a place thick with trees, a grove, a thicket.**

**The word occurred at `050-kaf.md` 50/13-14 within a list of destructions and was treated there. I rely on that treatment.**

**Whether this community is the same as the people of Midian is debated in the classical commentaries.** **I do not enter this debate and fix no geographical location;** the verse gives no place name, it gives an **attribute**: *la-ẓālimīn.*

### وَإِنَّهُمَا لَبِإِمَامٍ مُّبِينٍ

**The pronoun is dual: *innahumā* — "the two of them."** **That is, the homeland of the people of Lot and al-Ayka.**

**إِمَام** — root **أ-م-م**: **to go in front, the direction intended.** *Imām* — the one in front, the one followed; and **an open, travelled road.**

**The word was treated at `028-kasas.md` 28/5 and 28/41, and also at `025-furkan.md` 25/74, where it was set out in a table that the same word is used in two opposite directions. I rely on those treatments.**

**Its use here must be recorded: the word is used not for a person but for a road.**

**And two verses apart two synonymous expressions come:**

| Verse | Expression | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **76** | *La-bi-sabīlin **muqīm*** | **A standing road** |
| **79** | *La-bi-**imāmin** mubīn* | **An open road** |

**The same idea, with two different words.** **I record this as an observation about the word order: the sūra emphasises the visibility of the remains twice, with two different words.**

## 15/80-84 — وَلَقَدْ كَذَّبَ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْحِجْرِ ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ · وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُمْ ءَايَٰتِنَا فَكَانُوا۟ عَنْهَا مُعْرِضِينَ · وَكَانُوا۟ يَنْحِتُونَ مِنَ ٱلْجِبَالِ بُيُوتًا ءَامِنِينَ · فَأَخَذَتْهُمُ ٱلصَّيْحَةُ مُصْبِحِينَ · فَمَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُم مَّا كَانُوا۟ يَكْسِبُونَ

*Wa laqad kadhdhaba aṣḥābu'l-Ḥijri'l-mursalīn · Wa ātaynāhum āyātinā fa-kānū ʿanhā muʿriḍīn · Wa kānū yanḥitūna mina'l-jibāli buyūtan āminīn · Fa-akhadhathumu'l-ṣayḥatu muṣbiḥīn · Fa-mā aghnā ʿanhum mā kānū yaksibūn*

"**The people of al-Ḥijr too called the messengers liars.** · **We gave them Our signs; they turned away from them.** · **They used to carve secure houses out of the mountains.** · **The blast seized them as they entered upon the morning.** · **What they had earned availed them nothing.**"

### ٱلْحِجْر — the name of the sūra

**The ح-ج-ر root was analysed in detail at `049-hucurat.md` (relying there on `089-fecr.md`), and what was recorded there is decisive here; I rely on that treatment:**

> **The root's basic idea is not stone but *to hinder, to hold back, to enclose, to draw a boundary*.** *Ḥajar* (stone) is a member of this family because a stone is what sets a boundary and blocks passage. **Ḥijr** — what is forbidden, made inviolable. **Ḥajr** — the restriction of a person's power of disposal. **Ḥijr** — the lap: the area the arms enclose. **Ḥujra** — a room: a place separated and enclosed by a wall.

**And it was further recorded in the section on al-Ḥujurāt: ح-ج-ر and ه-ج-ر are separate roots; *muhājir* is from the second.** **I preserve this note here too.**

**Now what belongs here:**

**One.** ***Al-Ḥijr* occurs in the Qurʾān only in this verse** and is the name of the homeland of Thamūd.

**Two.** **And there is a fittingness between the sense of the root and the sūra as a whole. I record this as my own reading, its ground being the core of the root analysed in al-Ḥujurāt:** the root means **to enclose, to draw a boundary, to take into protection**. **And the sūra is from beginning to end about boundary and protection:** the sky was guarded (17), the reminder was guarded (9), a limit was set to Iblīs's power (42), a measure was set to the sending down of everything (21), an end was set to Iblīs's term (38), a share was allotted to the gates of hell (44).

**Three.** **And the people who carry the name of the sūra are named precisely with a claim of "protection":** *yanḥitūna mina'l-jibāli buyūtan **āminīn***. **They are making themselves a shelter out of stone.**

**It is hard to count this link a coincidence; but it must also not be forgotten that the name of the sūra was given later, from the word in the eightieth verse.** **That is, the name is not a claim of the text but a choice of the tradition. I can say that the choice was apt; I record this as my own reading.** **The same note was entered at `049-hucurat.md` as well.**

### وَكَانُوا۟ يَنْحِتُونَ مِنَ ٱلْجِبَالِ بُيُوتًا ءَامِنِينَ

The **ن-ح-ت** root: **to carve, to shape a hard body by cutting.**

**The same verb was treated at `026-suara.md` 26/149:** *wa **tanḥitūna** mina'l-jibāli buyūtan **fārihīn***. **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference between the two sūras lies in a single word, and this is a comparison that can be verified:**

| Sūra | Sentence | The last word | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/149 | *Tanḥitūna mina'l-jibāli buyūtan **fārihīn*** | *Fārihīn* — **with skill / in exultation** | **The manner of the making** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/82** | *Yanḥitūna mina'l-jibāli buyūtan **āminīn*** | *Āminīn* — **being secure** | **The expected result** |

**And the mode of the verb differs too:** in al-Shuʿarāʾ *tanḥitūna* (second person plural — Ṣāliḥ is speaking to them), in al-Ḥijr *kānū yanḥitūna* (*kāna* + imperfect — a habit continuing in the past).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two words:** al-Shuʿarāʾ recounts their **state** while doing the work; **al-Ḥijr recounts the result they expected from the work.** And the next verse cancels that result.

### The counterpart of the claim of security within the sūra

**The word occurs for the second time in the sūra, and its opposite is in the forty-sixth verse. It was set out in a table above; here it closes:**

| Verse | *Āminīn* | Source | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **46** | *Udkhulūhā bi-salāmin āminīn* | **Given** | *Wa mā hum minhā bi-mukhrajīn* (48) |
| **82** | *Buyūtan āminīn* | **Their own carving** | *Fa-akhadhathumu'l-ṣayḥatu muṣbiḥīn* (83) |

**Two securities, two results.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two occurrences of the same word together with the sentences that follow each:** the sūra compares by two examples where security comes from. **And it says that what was made of stone was brought down by a sound.**

### فَمَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُم مَّا كَانُوا۟ يَكْسِبُونَ

The **غ-ن-ي** root: **needlessness; and from this, "to avail, to be of use".** **The root was treated at `014-ibrahim.md` 14/8 (*la-ghaniyyun ḥamīd*) and at `111-tebbet.md` 111/2 (*mā aghnā ʿanhu māluhu*). I rely on those treatments.**

**And the object of the verb must be recorded: *mā kānū yaksibūn* — "what they were earning."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the verse does not say "their houses", it says "what they earned". **The scope is widened** — not only houses of stone but everything acquired. **And this joins with the third verse of the sūra:** *dharhum yaʾkulū wa yatamattaʿū* — leave them, let them take their enjoyment. **What the sūra left at the start it weighs here.**

## 15/85-86 — وَمَا خَلَقْنَا ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَآ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ ۗ وَإِنَّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ لَءَاتِيَةٌ ۖ فَٱصْفَحِ ٱلصَّفْحَ ٱلْجَمِيلَ · إِنَّ رَبَّكَ هُوَ ٱلْخَلَّٰقُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

*Wa mā khalaqnā'l-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā illā bi'l-ḥaqq · Wa inna'l-sāʿata la-ātiyatun fa'ṣfaḥi'l-ṣafḥa'l-jamīl · Inna Rabbaka huwa'l-Khallāqu'l-ʿAlīm*

"**We created the heavens and the earth and what is between them only with the truth. The hour is surely coming. So forgive with a beautiful forgiveness.** · **Your Lord is the great creator, the knowing.**"

### The logic of the sentence: from creation to conduct

**The word order must be recorded: the verse is built of three sentences and the third is joined to what precedes with *fāʾ*.**

| Order | Sentence | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Mā khalaqnā… illā bi'l-ḥaqq* | **That creation has a purpose** |
| 2 | *Wa inna'l-sāʿata la-ātiya* | **That there is an end** |
| 3 | *Fa'ṣfaḥi'l-ṣafḥa'l-jamīl* | **A command about conduct** |

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the command is given not as a piece of advice about managing anger but **as the consequence of two announcements**. **If a reckoning is to be held, there is no need to hold the reckoning now.**

**The same structure was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/39-40:** there too, after the chain of proof was complete, the command was **patience and glorification**. **I rely on that treatment.**

### ٱلصَّفْحَ ٱلْجَمِيلَ — root: ص-ف-ح

**The root was first analysed at `043-zuhruf.md` 43/5, and what was recorded there holds here too; I rely on that treatment:**

> **The concrete sense of the root is the name of a surface: *ṣafḥa* — the side of a thing, its flat face.** *Ṣafḥa* — the cheek, the flat of a sword. *Ṣafaḥa ʿanhu* — he turned his face from him; and from this, **he forgave, he passed over.** *Muṣāfaḥa* — a handshake: **the touching of two palms, that is, of two flat surfaces, to one another.** **The picture beneath the root: the turning of the flat face of a thing.**

**And the most important thing recorded in al-Zukhruf was that the same root is used in that sūra in two opposite rulings:**

| Place | Expression | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zukhruf 43/5 | *A-fa-naḍribu ʿankumu'l-dhikra **ṣafḥan*** | **Rejected** — the reminder is not pushed aside |
| al-Zukhruf 43/89 | *Fa'**ṣfaḥ** ʿanhum wa qul salām* | **Commanded** — turn away from the people |

**In the section on al-Zukhruf this was set out in a table as "the clearest and most verifiable link of the sūra".** **I rely on that treatment.**

**Now what belongs here: the usage in al-Ḥijr is in the same direction as the second ruling of al-Zukhruf — but it has taken an attribute.**

**ٱلْجَمِيل** — root **ج-م-ل**: **beauty; and gathering, bringing together** (*jumla*, *ijmāl* are from the same root). The lexicographers explain *jamāl* by "the parts being in harmony with one another".

**And in the Qurʾān this attribute is used of a bearing in three places:** *ṣabran jamīlā* (a beautiful patience), *hajran jamīlā* (a beautiful parting), and here *ṣafḥan jamīlā*.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the idea of "harmony" in the root of the attribute:** the note *jamīl* attaches the act not to a degree but to a **form**. **That is, what is commanded is not "forgive much" — but "make your forgiving without reproach mixed into it, consistent in itself."**

**And the note *wa qul salām* of `043-zuhruf.md` 43/89 is not here; the command of al-Ḥijr is completed with an attribute, the command of al-Zukhruf with a word.** **The two sūras give the same command with two different additions.**

**ٱلْخَلَّٰق** — the *faʿʿāl* pattern, intensive. **It conveys that creating is not a one-time but a continuing work.** **The word occurs at `036-yasin.md` 36/81 as well (*wa huwa'l-Khallāqu'l-ʿAlīm*) — the same two names side by side in two sūras.**

## 15/87 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَٰكَ سَبْعًا مِّنَ ٱلْمَثَانِى وَٱلْقُرْءَانَ ٱلْعَظِيمَ

*Wa laqad ātaynāka sabʿan mina'l-mathānī wa'l-Qurʾāna'l-ʿaẓīm*

"**We have given you seven of the paired ones and the mighty Qurʾān.**"

### مَثَانِى — root: ث-ن-ي

**The root was analysed at `039-zumer.md` 39/23, and what was recorded there holds here too; I rely on that treatment:**

> **ث-ن-ي: to fold, to double, to repeat.** And in al-Zumar three readings were set out in a table for what the word *mathānī* corresponds to:

| Reading | What *mathānī* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Repeated** — the recurrence of the stories, the rulings, the warnings |
| 2 | **In pairs** — its coming in couples such as garden and fire, glad tidings and warning |
| 3 | **Folded** — coming section by section, one upon another |

**In al-Zumar no preference was made among these three readings; I make none here either.**

**The difference between al-Zumar and al-Ḥijr must be recorded, and it is important:**

| Place | Construction | What *mathānī* qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/23 | *Kitāban mutashābihan **mathāniya*** | **The whole of the Book** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/87** | *Sabʿan **mina'l-mathānī*** | ***A part* of the mathānī** — seven |

**That is, in al-Zumar the word is an attribute of the whole, in al-Ḥijr the source of a part.** **The particle must be recorded: *min* here conveys partitiveness (*tabʿīḍ*).**

### What is "seven"? — the disagreement

**In the classical commentaries the following views are chiefly transmitted about this construction:**

| View | What *sabʿan mina'l-mathānī* is | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sūrat al-Fātiḥa** — seven verses | Al-Fātiḥa is seven verses and is repeated in the prayer; *mathānī* means "repeated" |
| 2 | **The seven long sūras** (*al-sabʿu'l-ṭiwāl*) | The longest sūras of the Qurʾān are counted as a group |
| 3 | **The whole of the Qurʾān** — *mathānī* is its name, and "seven" states its divisions | At al-Zumar 39/23 the whole Book is called *mathānī* |
| 4 | **Seven subjects or seven pairs** repeated in the Qurʾān | The "in pairs" reading of *mathānī* |

**All four views are transmitted in the sources.** **I do not impose a preference.**

**There are two linguistic data that can be recorded, and I give them as observations:**

**One.** ***Sabʿan* is indefinite and without a specifier** — the question "seven what?" is not answered in the sentence. **The verse gives the number and not the thing numbered.** This is the source of the disagreement.

**Two.** **There is a conjunction in the sentence: *wa'l-Qurʾāna'l-ʿaẓīm*.** **Disagreement arises over the function of the conjunction as well:**

| Reading | The work of the conjunction |
|---|---|
| a | **Separating** — the seven are one thing, the Qurʾān another |
| b | **Explanatory** (*ʿaṭf al-bayān*) — the seven are the Qurʾān itself |
| c | **A reference from part to whole** — after the part is named, the whole is named |

**All three are possible; I do not impose a preference.**

**Sūrat al-Fātiḥa was treated in the index at `001-fatiha.md`; the number of its verses and the question of counting the basmala were taken up there. I rely on that treatment.**

### The verse's place within the sūra

**I record an observation about the word order:** the verse prepares the command of the eighty-eighth verse. **First what has been given is named, then it is said "do not look at what is in another's hand".** **Had the sequence been reversed, the command would have stood bare.**

## 15/88-89 — لَا تَمُدَّنَّ عَيْنَيْكَ إِلَىٰ مَا مَتَّعْنَا بِهِۦٓ أَزْوَٰجًا مِّنْهُمْ وَلَا تَحْزَنْ عَلَيْهِمْ وَٱخْفِضْ جَنَاحَكَ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ · وَقُلْ إِنِّىٓ أَنَا ٱلنَّذِيرُ ٱلْمُبِينُ

*Lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka ilā mā mattaʿnā bihi azwājan minhum wa lā taḥzan ʿalayhim wa'khfiḍ janāḥaka li'l-muʾminīn · Wa qul innī ana'l-nadhīru'l-mubīn*

"**Do not stretch your eyes towards what We have given some sections of them to enjoy; do not grieve over them, and lower your wing to the believers.** · **And say: 'I am a clear warner.'**"

### لَا تَمُدَّنَّ عَيْنَيْكَ — the picture in the verb

The **م-د-د** root: **to stretch, to extend.** **The same root was used for the earth in the nineteenth verse of the sūra:** *wa'l-arḍa **madadnāhā***.

| Verse | Verb | What is stretched |
|---|---|---|
| **19** | *Madadnāhā* | **The earth** — it was spread out |
| **88** | *Lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka* | **The eye** — its stretching is forbidden |

**I record this as a weave within the sūra, and it can be verified.**

**And the picture in the verb must be recorded:** the verse does not say "do not look". **It says "do not stretch your eyes"** — a movement like the gaze breaking loose from its owner and going.

**And the verb is an emphatic prohibition: *lā tamuddanna* — with the doubled nūn.**

### Its two parallels within the Qurʾān

**The same expression occurs once more in the Qurʾān, almost word for word, at Ṭāhā 20/131:** *wa lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka ilā mā mattaʿnā bihi azwājan minhum zahrata'l-ḥayāti'l-dunyā li-naftinahum fīh.*

**In the index `020-taha.md` currently covers the range 20/1-44; that verse has not yet been analysed.** **A comparison of the two verses is work that can be done when its turn comes.** **Here I record only the difference: in Ṭāhā the sentence has two additions — *zahrata'l-ḥayāti'l-dunyā* (the bloom of the life of this world) and *li-naftinahum fīh* (to test them by it). In al-Ḥijr these two additions are absent.**

**And at `018-kehf.md` 18/27-31 the same matter was treated with another verb:** *wa lā **taʿdu** ʿaynāka ʿanhum turīdu zīnata'l-ḥayāti'l-dunyā.* **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference between al-Kahf and al-Ḥijr is a difference of direction and must be recorded:**

| Place | Verb | Direction | What is forbidden |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Kahf 18/28 | *Lā taʿdu ʿaynāka **ʿanhum*** | **Departure** | The eye's **sliding away from the poor believers** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/88** | *Lā tamuddanna ʿaynayka **ilā**…* | **Turning towards** | The eye's **reaching out to what has been given** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particles in the two verses (*ʿan* / *ilā*):** the two prohibitions are the two ends of the same gaze. **One forbids whom the eye may leave, the other to whom it may reach out.** **And in al-Ḥijr the third command names precisely the first group: *wa'khfiḍ janāḥaka li'l-muʾminīn*.**

**أَزْوَٰجًا مِّنْهُمْ** — *azwāj*: **pairs, kinds, classes.** **Its sense here is "some sections or groups of them", and the lexicographers attach this to the word's sense of "mate, like": sets that resemble one another.**

**I record a point of word order:** the verse does not say "what We have given them"; **it says "what We have given some sets of them to enjoy".** **That is, what is given is named as belonging not to the whole community but to a part of it.**

### وَٱخْفِضْ جَنَاحَكَ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

The **خ-ف-ض** root: **to lower, to bring down.** ***The idiom of the *janāḥ* (wing) was treated at `017-isra.md` 17/24 and was set out in a table there with its occurrence at `028-kasas.md` 28/32. I rely on those treatments.***

**What was recorded in the section on al-Isrāʾ:**

> **When the word is used of a human being it means "arm, side";** the lexicographers explain the idiom by a person's gathering himself in, and rest it on the picture of **a bird spreading its wings when startled and folding them when it grows calm.**

**And a table was given there:**

| Place | Command | Direction | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Qaṣaṣ 28/32 | *Wa'ḍmum ilayka janāḥak* | **Inward** — to gather | The passing of fear |
| al-Isrāʾ 17/24 | *Wa'khfiḍ lahumā janāḥ* | **Downward** — to lower | Mercy |

**Now a third usage is added here and the one addressed changes:**

| Place | Command | The one addressed |
|---|---|---|
| al-Isrāʾ 17/24 | *Wa'khfiḍ lahumā janāḥaka mina'l-raḥma* | **Mother and father** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/88** | *Wa'khfiḍ janāḥaka **li'l-muʾminīn*** | **Those who believe** |
| al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/215 | *Wa'khfiḍ janāḥaka **li-mani'ttabaʿaka mina'l-muʾminīn*** | **Those who follow** |

**At `017-isra.md` it was recorded that this comparison "will be taken up when its turn comes". In the index `026-suara.md` currently covers the range 26/1-159; 26/215 has not yet been analysed.** **For this reason I give the al-Shuʿarāʾ row only as a record of vocabulary, not as a reference to a place already analysed.**

**I record the difference between al-Ḥijr and al-Isrāʾ as my own reading, its ground being the difference of those addressed:** in al-Isrāʾ the command is given to a relation of **kinship**, in al-Ḥijr to a fellowship of **belief**. **And in al-Isrāʾ there was a qualification — *mina'l-raḥma* (out of mercy); in al-Ḥijr this qualification is absent, and in its place the one addressed is specified.**

**And the place of the command must be recorded:** the verse is built of three commands — **do not look, do not grieve, lower your wing.** **The first two are against one group, the third towards another group.** **I record this as an observation about the word order: the sentence says in a single breath where attention is to be drawn from and where it is to be given.**

## 15/90-93 — كَمَآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَى ٱلْمُقْتَسِمِينَ · ٱلَّذِينَ جَعَلُوا۟ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ عِضِينَ · فَوَرَبِّكَ لَنَسْـَٔلَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ · عَمَّا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ

*Kamā anzalnā ʿalā'l-muqtasimīn · Alladhīna jaʿalū'l-Qurʾāna ʿiḍīn · Fa-wa Rabbika la-nasʾalannahum ajmaʿīn · ʿAmmā kānū yaʿmalūn*

"**Just as We sent down upon those who divided it among themselves** · **— those who made the Qurʾān into pieces.** · **By your Lord, We shall surely question them all** · **— about what they were doing.**"

### كَمَآ أَنزَلْنَا — what does it attach to?

**The lexicographers differ over what the sentence attaches to, and this is the most disputed aspect of the verse:**

| Reading | The attachment | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To verse 87** | We gave you, **just as** We sent down upon those who divided |
| 2 | **To verse 89** | "I am a clear warner" — **just like** the warning We sent down upon those who divided |
| 3 | **On its own** | We sent down punishment upon them (*anzalnā* here for punishment) |

**All three readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

### ٱلْمُقْتَسِمِين

The **ق-س-م** root: **to divide, to apportion.** **Form VIII (*iqtisām*) conveys reciprocity and effort: "they divided it among themselves."**

**Different explanations of who is meant are transmitted in the classical commentaries** — a past community, or a group in Mecca who shared out the roads among themselves in order to meet those who came. **I ascribe it to no particular event or name, because I do not write a report of which I am not certain.**

**The word's echo within the sūra must be recorded:** in the forty-fourth verse *juzʾun **maqsūm*** had occurred — the share allotted to the gates of hell. **The same root, one the people's own dividing and one the share allotted to them.** **I record this as a weave within the sūra, and it can be verified.**

### عِضِين

**The lexicographers differ over the root of the word, and this determines the sense:**

| Root | Sense | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **ع-ض-و** | *ʿUḍw* — **a limb, a member**; *ʿiḍa* — a piece | They **broke the Qurʾān into pieces** |
| **ع-ض-ه** | *ʿIḍh* — **calumny, lie, sorcery** | They **slandered the Qurʾān** (saying "it is sorcery, it is poetry") |

**Both explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**There is a datum of word order that can be recorded, and I give it as an observation:** the word is in the same sentence as *muqtasimīn* (those who divided). **Read according to the first root, the two words repeat the same idea: the dividers divided.** **I give this as a supporting indication, not as a preference.**

### فَوَرَبِّكَ لَنَسْـَٔلَنَّهُمْ

**An oath and an emphatic verb together: *fa-wa Rabbika* + *la-nasʾalanna*.**

**And what is asked about is given in the next verse: *ʿammā kānū yaʿmalūn* — "what they were doing."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the verse puts the question in one sentence and its answer in a separate verse. **And what is asked about is not speech but act.** **Yet what is recounted in these verses was a speech** — what they said about the Qurʾān. **That is, the verse asks by counting saying as a *deed* too.**

## 15/94-96 — فَٱصْدَعْ بِمَا تُؤْمَرُ وَأَعْرِضْ عَنِ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · إِنَّا كَفَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْمُسْتَهْزِءِينَ · ٱلَّذِينَ يَجْعَلُونَ مَعَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَٰهًا ءَاخَرَ ۚ فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ

*Fa'ṣdaʿ bimā tuʾmaru wa aʿriḍ ʿani'l-mushrikīn · Innā kafaynāka'l-mustahziʾīn · Alladhīna yajʿalūna maʿallāhi ilāhan ākhar · Fa-sawfa yaʿlamūn*

"**Bring out openly what you are commanded, and turn away from the mushrikūn.** · **We suffice you against those who mock.** · **Those who set another god alongside God. Soon they will know.**"

### ٱصْدَعْ — root: ص-د-ع

**The word was named at `086-tarik.md` and this verse was pointed to there; I rely on that treatment.**

**The concrete sense of the root: to split a thing, to crack it.** *Ṣadʿ* — a crack, a fissure. **And the splitting of the earth for the plant is recounted in the Qurʾān with this root** (al-Ṭāriq 86/12 — *wa'l-arḍi dhāti'l-ṣadʿ*).

**What was recorded in the section on al-Ṭāriq:**

> ***Iṣdaʿ* means "split through and come out, appear openly, say it openly" — to split the silence.**

**I record this as an observation about the vocabulary:** the command is not "say" (*qul*) or "convey" (*balligh*). **The word chosen pictures the splitting of a covering.**

### A turn within the sūra

**And this command meets the third verse of the sūra. This is a link within the sūra that can be verified:**

| Verse | Command | What is asked |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *Dharhum yaʾkulū wa yatamattaʿū* | **Leave them** — do not intervene |
| **94** | *Fa'ṣdaʿ bimā tuʾmar* | **Split, bring out openly** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the opposition of the two commands:** the sūra opens with a command to "leave" and closes with a command to "declare". **The two do not conflict, because their objects differ:** in the first what is left is **their way of living**, in the second what is declared is **what has been commanded**.

**And the second command carries the same line: *wa aʿriḍ ʿani'l-mushrikīn* — "turn away from them."** **That is, the word will be said openly, but the dispute will not be kept up.** **The same balance was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/35 with the note of *balāgh*. I rely on that treatment.**

### إِنَّا كَفَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْمُسْتَهْزِءِينَ

The **ك-ف-ي** root: **to suffice, to take a matter upon oneself and leave no need of another.**

**The mode of the verb must be recorded: *kafaynā* — perfect (past).** **That is, not "we shall suffice you" but "we have sufficed you."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the work is announced as finished before it is yet visible. **And at `035-fatir.md` 35/4 the same line was treated: consolation is given not by the removal of the event but by its being set in order.** **Here consolation is given by the announcing of the outcome in advance.**

**ٱلْمُسْتَهْزِءِين** — and the word joins with the eleventh verse of the sūra: *illā kānū bihi **yastahziʾūn***. **The same root, one for past peoples and one for the present addressees.** **The sūra set up mockery as a fact of history, and at the end gives an undertaking against it.**

## 15/97-98 — وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّكَ يَضِيقُ صَدْرُكَ بِمَا يَقُولُونَ · فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَكُن مِّنَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ

*Wa laqad naʿlamu annaka yaḍīqu ṣadruka bimā yaqūlūn · Fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi Rabbika wa kun mina'l-sājidīn*

"**We know that your breast is straitened by what they say.** · **So glorify your Lord with praise and be of those who prostrate.**"

### وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ — the announcing of knowledge

**The mode of the verb must be recorded: *naʿlamu* — imperfect (continuing).** **That is, not "we knew" but "we know."**

**I record this as an observation about the word order:** the sentence makes a **finding** before it offers a solution. **And the finding does not treat the state of the one addressed as invalid — it names it.**

The **ض-ي-ق** root: **narrowness.** *Ḍayq* — to be narrow. **And the construction *yaḍīqu ṣadruka* — "your breast is straitened."**

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** in Arabic inward distress is recounted by way of **the width or narrowness of the breast**. **Its opposite was analysed at `094-insirah.md` in connection with the name of that sūra:** *a-lam nashraḥ laka ṣadrak* — the splitting open and widening of the breast. **I rely on that treatment.**

| Place | Expression | State |
|---|---|---|
| al-Inshirāḥ 94/1 | *A-lam nashraḥ laka ṣadrak* | **Widening** |
| **al-Ḥijr 15/97** | *Yaḍīqu ṣadruka* | **Narrowing** |

**The same organ, two opposite states.** This is a verifiable overlap between sūras.

**And the cause of the narrowing is stated: *bimā yaqūlūn* — "by what they say."** **Not what they do, but what they say.** **This joins with the sixth verse of the sūra:** *innaka la-majnūn.*

### فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَكُن مِّنَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ

**The response given must be recorded: two commands, and both are acts of worship.**

**The same structure was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/39-40, and it was recorded there:** *"After the chain of proof is complete, the command given to the Prophet is not to keep up the dispute; it is to be patient and to glorify."* **I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is this, and I record it as a weave within the sūra:** the expression *kun mina'l-**sājidīn*** uses the same word as the twenty-ninth and thirty-first verses of the sūra.

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **29** | *Fa-qaʿū lahu **sājidīn*** | **The angels** — a command |
| **31** | *Abā an yakūna maʿa'l-**sājidīn*** | **Iblīs** — he refused |
| **98** | *Wa kun mina'l-**sājidīn*** | **The Prophet** — a command |

**Three occurrences, the same word.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word shared by the three verses:** after recounting one who refused to be with those who prostrate, the sūra says to its own addressee, with the same word, "be of those who prostrate." **And the difference between the two is the whole subject of the sūra.**

## 15/99 — وَٱعْبُدْ رَبَّكَ حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَكَ ٱلْيَقِينُ

*Wa'ʿbud Rabbaka ḥattā yaʾtiyaka'l-yaqīn*

"**And serve your Lord until the certainty comes to you.**"

### يَقِين — root: ي-ق-ن

**The root was analysed in detail at `102-tekasur.md` 102/5, and what was recorded there holds here too; I rely on that treatment:**

> **The complete removal of doubt; knowledge settling, becoming fixed.** Some of the lexicographers record that under the root there is a sense of **settling, sedimenting, coming to rest** — like turbid water clearing and sinking to the bottom. **Yaqīn is opinion settling into knowledge.**

**And in the section on al-Takāthur this very verse was named:**

> *"Serve your Lord until the certainty comes to you" (al-Ḥijr 15/99). According to the majority of the exegetes the certainty here is death — because death is the moment about which no doubt remains.*

### The disagreement

**In the classical commentaries the following views are chiefly transmitted about what the word corresponds to here:**

| View | What *yaqīn* is | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Death** | At `074-muddessir.md` 74/47 the same word occurs in this sense: *ḥattā atānā'l-yaqīn* — the words of the people of the fire |
| 2 | **Certain knowledge, the reaching of certainty** | This is the basic sense of the root |
| 3 | **The resurrection and what follows it** | The moment at which doubt is wholly removed |
| 4 | **The promised help or victory** | The context of the sūra — distress and consolation |

**All four views are transmitted. I do not impose a preference.**

**The ground given for the first view is a datum that can be verified in the index, and I record it:** at `074-muddessir.md` 74/43-47 the same construction (*ḥattā atānā'l-yaqīn*) occurs at the end of a speech, and what is recounted there is **the ending** of a group's state in this world. **This is a textual indication supporting the first reading; but I do not count it by itself a ground for a preference.**

**And I record this as well:** the second and fourth readings require the command of the verse to be **for a term** — they can be pulled to mean that after a certain point service would end. **It is widely said that the classical commentaries reject this possibility and emphasise that the particle *ḥattā* here means "throughout life".** **I transmit this as the widespread tendency of the tradition; I ascribe it to no particular source.**

### The closing of the sūra

**And the last thing that must be recorded is the order of the last three verses of the sūra:**

| Verse | What is given |
|---|---|
| **97** | **A diagnosis** — we know your breast is straitened |
| **98** | **Two commands** — glorification and prostration |
| **99** | **A term** — service, until the certainty comes |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the arrangement of the three verses:** the sūra does not close by ending the dispute outside. **It closes with a command that turns back into the one addressed** — and it sets a term to that command. **Four times throughout the sūra a "determined" boundary was spoken of (4, 21, 38, 44); the last verse sets a boundary to the addressee's own work as well.**

### Boundaries set per the method (`STYLE.md`)

- **At 15/16-18** **no astronomical interpretation was made** of the guarding of the sky and of *shihāb*; the boundary set at `072-cin.md` and `037-saffat.md` was preserved.
- **At 15/19** no conclusion about the shape of the earth was drawn from the verb *madadnāhā*.
- **At 15/22** **no meteorological or botanical information was derived** from the word *lawāqiḥ*; the ground was rested on the principle of "the language of the one addressed" treated at `014-ibrahim.md` 14/4.
- **At 15/44** no calculation was built upon the number seven.
- **At 15/51-77** details of the story not found in the Qurʾān were not entered into: the identity of the guests, the number of the daughters, the location of the city.
- **At 15/80-84** no wholesale judgement was set up about any people or lineage; the attributes the verse gives (*kadhdhaba*, *muʿriḍīn*, *ẓālimīn*) were taken as the basis.

### The following readings are my own and were not presented as transmitted

**The naming at 15/3 of the thing that diverts as *hope*; the *inẓār* link between 15/8 and 15/37; the absence of the word *li'l-nāẓirīn* at 15/16 from the other two parallel passages; the positive and negative use of the *khazāʾin* root two verses apart at 15/21-22; the preparing of Iblīs's objection at 15/26-27 by al-Ḥijr's pulling the jinn forward in respect of time; the framing of the objection at 15/33 as one-sided, unlike the one in Ṣād; the not leaving of the knowledge to the other party at 15/38 by the binding of the term to a *known* time; the table of voice and person for the verb *tazyīn* at 15/39 and the verb's ascription to Iblīs being not an announcement but a confession; the *mukhlaṣ* pattern at 15/40 attaching the work to the outside; the comparison of the two securities between 15/46 and 15/82; the shared asymmetry between 15/49-50 and `014-ibrahim.md` 14/7; Abraham's giving the warning back widened at 15/55-56; the singular/plural *āya* difference between 15/75 and 15/77; the fittingness between the sense of the root and the sūra as a whole at 15/80; the difference of the last word between al-Shuʿarāʾ and al-Ḥijr at 15/82; the two prohibitions in al-Kahf and al-Ḥijr at 15/88 being the two ends of the same gaze; the meeting of the two commands between 15/94 and 15/3; the three occurrences of the word *sājidīn* at 15/98.**

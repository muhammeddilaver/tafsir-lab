# 41. Sūrat Fuṣṣilat

**Fifty-four verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the word **فُصِّلَتْ** in the third verse: **its verses have been made distinct, set apart from one another.** The sūra is also known as *Ḥā-mīm al-Sajda*; there is a verse of prostration at verse 37.

It is the second of the seven sūras that open with *Ḥā-mīm*.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-8 | The description of the Book, and the addressee who stays closed | *Lahum ajrun ghayru mamnūn* (8) |
| **II** | 9-12 | Earth and heaven — the founding | *Dhālika taqdīru'l-ʿazīzi'l-ʿalīm* (12) |
| **III** | 13-25 | ʿĀd, Thamūd, and the scene of testimony | *Ḥaqqa ʿalayhimu'l-qawl* (25) |
| **IV** | 26-36 | The tactic of drowning out the voice, and the counter-method | *Innahū huwa's-samīʿu'l-ʿalīm* (36) |
| **V** | 37-54 | The signs, the character of the Book, and the closing | *Innahū bi-kulli shayʾin muḥīṭ* (54) |

**The backbone of the sūra is a problem of communication:** the text arrives, but it does not get through. **In the fifth verse the addressee describes this himself** (our hearts are covered, there is a heaviness in our ears, there is a veil between us), **and in the twenty-sixth verse it turns into a tactic** (do not listen, make noise). **The sūra gives both the description of this blockage and its solution** — the solution will come in verse 34.

## 41/1-5 — حمٓ · تَنزِيلٌ مِّنَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ · كِتَٰبٌ فُصِّلَتْ ءَايَٰتُهُۥ قُرْءَانًا عَرَبِيًّا لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ · بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا فَأَعْرَضَ أَكْثَرُهُمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ

*Ḥā-mīm · Tanzīlun mina'r-Raḥmāni'r-Raḥīm · Kitābun fuṣṣilat āyātuhū qurʾānan ʿarabiyyan li-qawmin yaʿlamūn · Bashīran wa nadhīran fa-aʿraḍa aktharuhum fa-hum lā yasmaʿūn*

"Ḥā-mīm. A sending down from al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm. A Book **whose verses have been made distinct**: an Arabic Qurʾān for a people who know; as a bringer of good news and as a warner. But most of them turned away; they no longer hear."

### فُصِّلَتْ — root: ف-ص-ل

**The concrete sense of the root: to separate two things from one another, to put a distance between them.** The root was analysed at several points in this corpus — in `077-murselat.md` and `078-nebe.md`, in the context of *yawmu'l-faṣl* (the day of separation). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The pattern here is Form II, and it changes the sense:** *tafṣīl* — **to divide a whole into its parts, to set it out in detail.** For a text to be *mufaṣṣal* is for its subjects to have been separated from one another and their boundaries drawn.

**That this word became the name of the sūra is worth recording:** the name of the sūra is about the **form** of the Book, not its **content**. **And this name will return in the forty-fourth verse inside an objection** — *law lā fuṣṣilat āyātuh.*

### تَنزِيلٌ مِّنَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

**In the other *ḥā-mīm* sūras this sentence has the form *mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ḥakīm* (from God, the Mighty, the Wise)** — it occurred thus at `045-casiye.md` 45/2 and `046-ahkaf.md` 46/2, and was treated there.

**Here two names change:** in place of *al-ʿAzīz* and *al-Ḥakīm*, **al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm.**

| Sūra | The names it is sent down from |
|---|---|
| al-Jāthiya 45/2 · al-Aḥqāf 46/2 | *al-ʿAzīz* · *al-Ḥakīm* — **power and fittingness** |
| **Fuṣṣilat 41/2** | *al-Raḥmān* · *al-Raḥīm* — **mercy** |

**This is a difference that can be verified, and it fits the subject of the sūra.** I record this as my own reading: **the sūra is the sūra of a text that does not get through. In its first sentence the source of the text is named not by power but by mercy** — that is, the failure to get through does not arise from the disposition of the sender.

### فَأَعْرَضَ أَكْثَرُهُمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ

**Two *fāʾ*s one after the other, and the order must be recorded:** first *aʿraḍa* (he turned away), **then** *lā yasmaʿūn* (they do not hear).

**That is, the not-hearing is set down as the consequence of the turning away — not as its cause.** In Arabic the *fāʾ* conveys consequence and succession.

**The reverse of this scene was treated at `045-casiye.md` 45/8:** there the type **hears**, and then resists. **The two sūras describe two separate stages of the same matter: the one, hearing and then resisting; the other, resistance closing off hearing.**

## 41/5 — وَقَالُوا۟ قُلُوبُنَا فِىٓ أَكِنَّةٍ مِّمَّا تَدْعُونَآ إِلَيْهِ وَفِىٓ ءَاذَانِنَا وَقْرٌ وَمِنۢ بَيْنِنَا وَبَيْنِكَ حِجَابٌ فَٱعْمَلْ إِنَّنَا عَٰمِلُونَ

"They said: '**Our hearts are within coverings against that to which you call us; in our ears there is a heaviness, and between us and you there is a veil. So work; we too are working.**'"

### Three obstacles, three separate places

**The other side describes its own condition, and it uses three words:**

| Word | Root | Where | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **أَكِنَّة** | ك-ن-ن | **Heart** | Coverings, sheaths |
| **وَقْر** | و-ق-ر | **Ear** | Heaviness, deafness |
| **حِجَاب** | ح-ج-ب | **In between** | A veil |

**The root ك-ن-ن was analysed in `056-vakia.md`** (*al-luʾluʾi'l-maknūn* — the pearl kept hidden in its shell; *kinān* — a sheath). **I do not repeat it here.** **The use here is turned around: there it was protection, here it is closedness.**

**The root و-ق-ر conveys weight** (it occurred in `071-nuh.md` and `051-zariyat.md`); *waqār* (gravity of bearing) is from this root too. **The heaviness in the ear is the load that keeps the sound from entering.**

**The placement of the three obstacles is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** one is **inside** (the heart), one is **at the entrance** (the ear), one is **in between** (the veil). **That is, all three points of communication are described as closed.**

**And the most striking side of it is this: the one who says it is the other side itself.** It states its own closedness not as a defect but as **a declaration of position.**

**فَٱعْمَلْ إِنَّنَا عَٰمِلُونَ** — "so work; we too are working." **The argument is brought to an end, and it is proposed that the two sides go their separate ways.** This is the calmest form of refusal: **no quarrel, but a parting.**

## 41/6-8 — قُلْ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُكُمْ يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ أَنَّمَآ إِلَٰهُكُمْ إِلَٰهٌ وَٰحِدٌ فَٱسْتَقِيمُوٓا۟ إِلَيْهِ وَٱسْتَغْفِرُوهُ

"Say: **I am only a human being like you**; it is revealed to me that your god is one God. So set yourselves straight towards Him, and ask His forgiveness."

### Where the answer is given

**In the fifth verse the other side had set a veil in between:** *wa min bayninā wa baynika ḥijāb* — "between us and you there is a veil."

**The sixth verse answers this, and the word of the answer is *bashar*:** *innamā anā basharun mithlukum* — **"I am only a human being like you."**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is that the two verses come one after the other:** the other side had made the distance between them its reason; **the answer says that the distance is not between the parties.** The difference is not between one human being and another — **it is in the knowledge that comes.** The clause *yūḥā ilayya* (it is revealed to me) is in the passive: **the act is not his.**

**فَٱسْتَقِيمُوٓا۟ إِلَيْهِ** — the root of *istiqāma* is **ق-و-م**; the analysis of the word was made at `002-bakara.md` 2/255 and, in this corpus, at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/13.

**ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْتُونَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ** — "those who do not give the zakāt."

**Because this expression occurs in a sūra revealed in the Meccan period, two readings are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Reading | What *zakāt* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A financial obligation** — the zakāt that was set out in detail later |
| 2 | **Purification** — in the original sense of the root (**ز-ك-و**: to be cleansed, to increase) |

**Both readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference, and I draw no legal conclusion.**

**أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ** — "an uninterrupted reward". The root **م-ن-ن**: to cut off; and to reproach someone with a favour done. **The same construction was treated in `095-tin.md`**, and *mann* (reproaching with a favour) was analysed at `002-bakara.md` 2/262-264. **Both senses work here: a reward that is neither cut off nor thrown back in the face.**

## 41/9-12 — قُلْ أَئِنَّكُمْ لَتَكْفُرُونَ بِٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلْأَرْضَ فِى يَوْمَيْنِ … ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَهِىَ دُخَانٌ فَقَالَ لَهَا وَلِلْأَرْضِ ٱئْتِيَا طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًا قَالَتَآ أَتَيْنَا طَآئِعِينَ

"Say: Do you really disbelieve in the One who created the earth **in two days**?… Then He turned to the heaven, which was **smoke**, and said to it and to the earth: '**Come, willingly or unwillingly.**' They both said: '**We come willingly.**'"

### The question of the days — and my limit

**The relation between the durations named in these verses (two days, four days, two days) and the expression "six days" that occurs elsewhere in the Qurʾān has been debated in the classical commentaries.** The principal explanation is that the four days are counted so as to include the two preceding days as well (*sawāʾan li's-sāʾilīn* — "exactly, for those who ask").

**I transmit this as an explanation that is reported.**

**And per the method (`STYLE.md`) I draw the limit explicitly:** the text does not state how long a duration the word "day" corresponds to here. **I do not dress these verses in modern cosmology;** I derive no model of the universe from the word "smoke", nor do I equate the days with geological ages. **Such an equation would be making the text say what it does not say.**

**What the text says is this, and that much is what I record:** creation is described as **ordered** and **measured**; not at one stroke, but in stages. **And the last sentence lays down a judgement: *dhālika taqdīru'l-ʿazīzi'l-ʿalīm* — that is the measuring of the Mighty, the Knowing.**

### ٱئْتِيَا طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًا

**This sentence is a scene of speech, and the lexicographers generally record that it is figurative expression (*tamthīl*):** a picture set up to show that there is no resistance in the face of the command.

**A second reading is transmitted as well:** that the expression is literal, and that its manner cannot be known. **I transmit both explanations; I do not impose a preference.**

**But one detail of the word order works on either reading and is worth recording:** the offer is presented with two options (*ṭawʿan aw karhan*), **the answer comes with one:** *ataynā **ṭāʾiʿīn*** — "we come willingly."

**A one-directional answer to a two-option command.** I record this as my own reading: **the scene shows that the possibility of resistance was not in fact used.** And what will be described through the rest of the sūra is precisely the human counterpart of this: **the answer man gives to the same call was seen in the fifth verse.**

## 41/13-18 — فَأَمَّا عَادٌ فَٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ ٱلْحَقِّ وَقَالُوا۟ مَنْ أَشَدُّ مِنَّا قُوَّةً … وَأَمَّا ثَمُودُ فَهَدَيْنَٰهُمْ فَٱسْتَحَبُّوا۟ ٱلْعَمَىٰ عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ

"As for ʿĀd: they were arrogant in the land without right, and said '**Who is greater than us in power?**'… As for Thamūd: **We showed them the right way, but they preferred blindness to guidance.**"

### Two peoples, two separate defects

**The verse separates the two peoples with the *ammā … wa ammā* pattern, and makes a separate diagnosis for each:**

| People | Defect | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **ʿĀd** | **The measure of power** | *Man ashaddu minnā quwwatan* |
| **Thamūd** | **Preference** | *Fa'staḥabbū'l-ʿamā ʿala'l-hudā* |

**The first is an error of comparison:** they suppose themselves the strongest. **The answer comes with the same word** — *a-wa-lam yaraw anna'llāha'lladhī khalaqahum huwa **ashaddu** minhum quwwatan.* **The verse does not change the measure; it carries the same measure through to the end.**

**The second is a matter of preference, and the verb must be recorded: *istaḥabbū* — Form X, root ح-ب-ب: to love, to prefer.** **That is, of Thamūd it is not said "they did not know"; it is said *hadaynāhum* — "We showed them the way."** Then the preference comes.

**I record this as an observation:** in the fifth verse of the sūra the addressee had declared himself closed. **Here it is shown, through the example of a people, that closedness can be a preference.** A choice is made between *ʿamā* (blindness) and *hudā* (guidance) — **and what is chosen is not seeing.**

**فِىٓ أَيَّامٍ نَّحِسَاتٍ** — the word *naḥisāt* in this expression, used in the account of the destruction of ʿĀd, means "ill-omened, calamitous days". **The same event occurred with the expression *fī yawmi naḥsin mustamirr* at `054-kamer.md` 54/19, and was treated there.** **The two sūras use the same root; one says a single day, the other days.**

**A note per the method (`STYLE.md`):** no ground for a belief in lucky and unlucky days is to be derived from this. **The word qualifies particular days that befell that community; it lays down no general ruling about the calendar.**

## 41/19-23 — وَيَوْمَ يُحْشَرُ أَعْدَآءُ ٱللَّهِ إِلَى ٱلنَّارِ فَهُمْ يُوزَعُونَ · حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا مَا جَآءُوهَا شَهِدَ عَلَيْهِمْ سَمْعُهُمْ وَأَبْصَٰرُهُمْ وَجُلُودُهُم بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ · وَقَالُوا۟ لِجُلُودِهِمْ لِمَ شَهِدتُّمْ عَلَيْنَا قَالُوٓا۟ أَنطَقَنَا ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنطَقَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ

"On the day when the enemies of God are gathered to the Fire, **they are all held together.** When they reach it, **their hearing, their eyes and their skins** bear witness against them concerning what they used to do. They say to their skins: '**Why did you bear witness against us?**' And they say: '**God, who gives speech to everything, gave us speech.**'"

### The choice of witnesses

**Three witnesses are named: the ear, the eye, the skin.**

**The triad ear–eye–heart was treated at `045-casiye.md` 45/23 and at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/26.** **Here in third place there is not *qalb* (heart) but *jild* (skin).**

| Place | The triad |
|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/26 · al-Jāthiya 45/23 | Ear · eye · **heart** |
| **Fuṣṣilat 41/20** | Ear · eye · **skin** |

**This difference is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the heart is inside, and is identical with the person himself; **the skin, by contrast, is a boundary — the outer surface of the body, the place that touches the world.** In the scene of testimony the organ chosen is **the one that touches the outside.** And indeed the protest too is addressed to the skins: *lima shahidtum ʿalaynā.*

**أَنطَقَنَا ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنطَقَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ** — the answer takes the testimony out of the organs' own decision.

**At `045-casiye.md` 45/29 I treated the Book's being named with the verb *yanṭiqu* (it speaks)** — there it was the **record** that spoke, here it is the **body.** **The same root (ن-ط-ق), two separate witnesses in two sūras.**

### وَذَٰلِكُمْ ظَنُّكُمُ ٱلَّذِى ظَنَنتُم بِرَبِّكُمْ أَرْدَىٰكُمْ

*"This supposition of yours that you supposed about your Lord has destroyed you."*

**The cause is named plainly, and the word is *ẓann*.**

**I tabulated there that this word is the whole axis of `045-casiye.md`** (45/24 *in hum illā yaẓunnūn*, 45/32 *in naẓunnu illā ẓannā*). **Here the same word is taken a step further: supposition is named not merely as a lack of knowledge but as a *cause of destruction*.**

**أَرْدَىٰ** — root **ر-د-ي**: to fall from a height, to tumble and perish. *Mardā* — a precipice. **The concrete picture in the word carries where supposition leads.**

**And the content of the supposition is given in the preceding verse:** *wa lākin ẓanantum anna'llāha lā yaʿlamu kathīran mimmā taʿmalūn* — **"you supposed that God does not know much of what you do."** That is, the supposition is not about existence, but about **whether one is known or not.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra carries the source of the defect back to before denial — **to supposing oneself unseen.** And the next sentence says this plainly: *wa mā kuntum tastatirūna an yashhada ʿalaykum samʿukum* — **"you were not concealing yourselves"**; that is, they had not even felt the need to hide.

## 41/24 — فَإِن يَصْبِرُوا۟ فَٱلنَّارُ مَثْوًى لَّهُمْ وَإِن يَسْتَعْتِبُوا۟ فَمَا هُم مِّنَ ٱلْمُعْتَبِينَ

"Even if they endure, the Fire is their **dwelling**; and **even if they seek to win favour**, this chance is not granted them."

**يَسْتَعْتِبُوا۟** — root **ع-ت-ب**. The core the lexicographers give: **to apologise in order to remove anger, to try to bring the other side round.** *Iʿtāb* — to accept the apology and be satisfied; *istiʿtāb* — to **seek** that acceptance.

**The concrete branch of the word is recorded as well: *ʿataba*, the threshold of a door and the step of a stair.** The lexicographers explain the link with the picture of "the removal of an obstacle placed in between". **I transmit this relation in the form in which the lexicographers establish it.**

**مَثْوًى** — root **ث-و-ي**: to remain in a place, to reside. **The word means "dwelling, the place where one stays"** — not a temporary stopping-place.

**The construction of the verse closes off both possibilities:** enduring (*yaṣbirū*) and apologising (*yastaʿtibū*). **I record this as an observation:** this block of the sūra states, with a sentence that runs in two directions, that responsibility cannot be deferred — **neither by bearing it out nor by turning back.**

## 41/25-26 — وَقَيَّضْنَا لَهُمْ قُرَنَآءَ فَزَيَّنُوا۟ لَهُم مَّا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ · وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَا تَسْمَعُوا۟ لِهَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ وَٱلْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَغْلِبُونَ

"We set upon them certain **companions**, and they made what was before them and what was behind them seem fair… Those who disbelieved said: '**Do not listen to this Qurʾān; make noise while it is being recited — perhaps you will drown it out.**'"

### Noise as a tactic

**This verse is the most concrete scene in the sūra, and it must be read together with the fifth verse.**

| Verse | Who says it | What |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | The other side | **"Our hearts are covered, there is a heaviness in our ears"** — a declaration of closedness |
| 26 | The other side | **"Do not listen, make noise"** — the act of closing |

**What is described in the fifth verse as a condition turns, in the twenty-sixth verse, into something done.** This is a development that can be verified within the sūra.

**وَٱلْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ** — the verb is from the root **ل-غ-و**: *laghw* — **speech that is not counted, that goes to waste.** The root was analysed in detail at `056-vakia.md` 56/25 (including the use recorded by the lexicographers for the chirping of a bird, and its relation to *lugha*). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The use here is worth recording:** in al-Wāqiʿa *laghw* was the thing **not found** in the Garden. **Here it is used as an instrument: making meaningless noise in order to drown out meaningful speech.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the tactic is not to object — **it is to prevent the thing being heard.** That is, the argument is not entered into; **the argument is not allowed to be set up.** *Laʿallakum taghlibūn* — "perhaps you will drown it out": the word itself describes victory as **superiority in sound.**

**قُرَنَآء** — root **ق-ر-ن**: to bind two things side by side. *Qarīn* — **the one who is continually beside another, the one who keeps company.**

## 41/27-29 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ رَبَّنَآ أَرِنَا ٱلَّذَيْنِ أَضَلَّانَا مِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ وَٱلْإِنسِ نَجْعَلْهُمَا تَحْتَ أَقْدَامِنَا لِيَكُونَا مِنَ ٱلْأَسْفَلِينَ

"Those who disbelieve say: '**Our Lord! Show us those two, of the jinn and of mankind, who led us astray; let us put them under our feet, so that they may be among the lowest.**'"

### The transfer of guilt

**The scene is built on a demand, and the content of the demand is worth recording:** the person does not ask to change his own condition, but **to see the one responsible and to crush him.**

**I record this as my own reading; its ground is the verbs:** *arinā* (show), *najʿalhumā taḥta aqdāminā* (let us put them under our feet), *li-yakūnā mina'l-asfalīn* (so that they may be among the lowest). **None of the three verbs has to do with the person himself.**

**And this is directly connected with the twenty-fifth verse:** there *qarīns* (those continually beside them) had been set upon them and had made what was before them and what was behind them seem fair. **Now it is those qarīns whose showing is demanded.**

**مِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ وَٱلْإِنسِ** — the two kinds are named together. **The same pair had occurred at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/18** and was treated there.

**One further side of the scene must be recorded:** the demand is neither refused nor granted — **no answer is given.** The verse reports the request and leaves it. **The next verse passes to another subject.**

---

## 41/30-32 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟ تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا۟ وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا۟ وَأَبْشِرُوا۟ بِٱلْجَنَّةِ ٱلَّتِى كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ

"Upon **those who say 'Our Lord is God' and then stand straight**, the angels descend: 'Do not fear, do not grieve; rejoice in the Garden you were promised.'"

### The same sentence, two sūras

**The first half of the verse is word for word the same as `046-ahkaf.md` 46/13:** *inna'lladhīna qālū Rabbuna'llāhu thumma'staqāmū.*

**The particle *thumma* in this sentence was treated in the al-Aḥqāf section:** there is a **distance** between the saying and the standing straight; saying happens in a moment, standing straight lasts. **I rely on that treatment.**

**The continuations of the two sūras differ, and the difference is worth recording:**

| Sūra | Its continuation |
|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/13 | *Fa-lā khawfun ʿalayhim wa lā hum yaḥzanūn* — **a statement of judgement** |
| **Fuṣṣilat 41/30** | *Tatanazzalu ʿalayhimu'l-malāʾika allā takhāfū wa lā taḥzanū* — **direct address** |

**In al-Aḥqāf the absence of fear and grief is given as a *statement*; here the same two words are said as a *command*, from the mouths of the angels.**

**And this fits the communication axis of the sūra:** in the fifth verse there were those who set a veil in between; **here there is a coming down.** *Tatanazzalu* — Form V, conveying repetition and continuity: **not a single occurrence, but the state of being in descent.**

**نَحْنُ أَوْلِيَآؤُكُمْ فِى ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَفِى ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ** — "we are your friends **in the life of this world** and in the hereafter." **That is, what is promised does not belong to what comes after alone.**

---

## 41/33-36 — وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ قَوْلًا مِّمَّن دَعَآ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًا … وَلَا تَسْتَوِى ٱلْحَسَنَةُ وَلَا ٱلسَّيِّئَةُ ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا ٱلَّذِى بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُۥ عَدَٰوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُۥ وَلِىٌّ حَمِيمٌ

"Who is better in speech than one who calls to God, does righteous work, and says '**I am one of those who submit**'? **The good deed and the bad deed are not equal. Repel with what is better.** Then the one between whom and you there is enmity becomes **as though a warm friend.**"

### The solution of the sūra is here

**The problem was set out in the twenty-sixth verse: the other side drowns out the voice.** **The thirty-fourth verse answers it — and the answer is not to raise the voice.**

**ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ** — *dafʿ* — root **د-ف-ع**: to push, to drive back. **The same root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/251** (*wa lawlā dafʿu'llāhi'n-nāsa baʿḍahum bi-baʿḍ*): **the setting of a balance by forces limiting one another.**

**The difference here is worth recording:** there *dafʿ* was a balance of forces; **here "what is better" is shown as the instrument of the same verb.** That is, the act of pushing remains; **the instrument changes.**

**فَإِذَا ٱلَّذِى بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُۥ عَدَٰوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُۥ وَلِىٌّ حَمِيمٌ** — the result is given by way of a comparison: *ka-annahū* — **"as though".**

**This particle must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** the verse does not say that the enmity **will disappear**; it says that the relation will be **like friendship**. **The particle of comparison also draws the limit of the promise.**

**وَلِىٌّ حَمِيمٌ** — the word *ḥamīm* is from the root **ح-م-م**, and the two branches of the root (**boiling water** / **close friend**) were treated in detail in `069-hakka.md`; the boiling-water branch also occurred three times in `056-vakia.md`. **Here the friendship branch is used.**

**وَمَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ وَمَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ إِلَّا ذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍ** — "none are brought to this but those who have endured, and none are brought to it but the possessor of **a great portion.**"

**The same sentence twice, with two separate conditions.** And the verb is passive: *yulaqqāhā* — **"is brought to it".** That is, this bearing is named not as a temperament that comes of itself but as **something given.**

**وَإِمَّا يَنزَغَنَّكَ مِنَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ نَزْغٌ فَٱسْتَعِذْ بِٱللَّهِ** — **the root ن-ز-غ occurs for the first time in this corpus here.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to prod, to jab, to come between and incite.** *Nazagha baynahum* — he sowed discord between them.

**The choice of the word works together with the preceding verse:** to meet evil with good is hard work, and **the sudden prod that comes in the midst of that hardship** is named separately. **Having given the counsel, the verse also says where that counsel will most likely crack.**

---

## 41/37-39 — وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِ ٱلَّيْلُ وَٱلنَّهَارُ وَٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ لَا تَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَهُنَّ

"The night, the day, the sun and the moon are among His signs. **Do not prostrate to the sun or to the moon; prostrate to God, who created them.**"

### Not stopping at the sign itself

**The construction of the verse must be recorded:** first four things are named as **āyāt** (signs), then prostration to those signs is forbidden.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the sentence:** a thing's being a sign does not make it something **to be turned towards.** **A sign exists for the sake of what it points to.** The logic used of the Book in the third verse of the sūra works here for the sky.

**فَإِنِ ٱسْتَكْبَرُوا۟ فَٱلَّذِينَ عِندَ رَبِّكَ يُسَبِّحُونَ لَهُۥ بِٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ وَهُمْ لَا يَسْـَٔمُونَ** — "if they are arrogant: those who are with your Lord glorify Him night and day, and **they do not weary.**"

**لَا يَسْـَٔمُونَ** — root **س-أ-م**: to tire, to weary. **Keep this word in mind: it will return in the forty-ninth verse, of man.**

### وَمِنْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦٓ أَنَّكَ تَرَى ٱلْأَرْضَ خَٰشِعَةً فَإِذَآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَآءَ ٱهْتَزَّتْ وَرَبَتْ

"That you see the earth **with its neck bowed** is also among His signs. When We send down water upon it, **it stirs and swells.**"

**خَٰشِعَة** — root **خ-ش-ع**: to be lowered, to bow, the sinking of the voice. **In the Qurʾān the word is generally used of man** (humility in prayer, the falling of the eyes at the resurrection). **Here it is given to dried-out soil.**

**اِهْتَزَّتْ وَرَبَتْ** — *ihtizāz* (to tremble, to stir) and *rabwa* (to swell, to rise — root **ر-ب-و**; treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/275 in the discussion of *ribā*).

**The order of the three words sets up a process:** collapsed → stirred → swollen. **And the verse ties this directly to the proof:** *inna'lladhī aḥyāhā la-muḥyi'l-mawtā* — "the one who gave it life will surely give life to the dead."

---

## 41/40 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يُلْحِدُونَ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِنَا لَا يَخْفَوْنَ عَلَيْنَآ

"**Those who swerve aside concerning Our signs** are not hidden from Us."

**يُلْحِدُونَ** — root **ل-ح-د**. **The concrete sense: to hollow out a cavity towards the side of a pit.** The *laḥd* is the niche opened not at the bottom of the grave but in its **side wall** (the one dug straight down is called *shaqq* or *ḍarīḥ*).

**From this the sense of the word follows: not to go straight, but to swerve *to the side*.** *Ilḥād* — to depart from what is straight.

**The picture in the word is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the verse describes the defect in the face of the signs not as **rejecting** them but as **shifting them aside.** The text stays where it is; the direction is changed.

**This fits the forty-fourth verse of the sūra:** there it was shown that whichever option had been taken, an objection would be found. **Ilḥād is something that can be done without objecting at all.**

**أَفَمَن يُلْقَىٰ فِى ٱلنَّارِ خَيْرٌ أَم مَّن يَأْتِىٓ ءَامِنًا يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ** — and a question comes: the comparison is left open, the answer is not stated. **The verse leaves the judgement to the reader himself.**

---

## 41/41-44 — وَإِنَّهُۥ لَكِتَٰبٌ عَزِيزٌ · لَّا يَأْتِيهِ ٱلْبَٰطِلُ مِنۢ بَيْنِ يَدَيْهِ وَلَا مِنْ خَلْفِهِۦ تَنزِيلٌ مِّنْ حَكِيمٍ حَمِيدٍ

"It is a **mighty** Book. **Falsehood cannot come to it from before it or from behind it.** It is a sending down from One wise, worthy of praise."

**مِنۢ بَيْنِ يَدَيْهِ وَلَا مِنْ خَلْفِهِ** — "from before it and from behind it". **In Arabic this pair is used to cover all the directions of a thing.**

**The same pair had also occurred in verse 25 of this sūra** — there the *qarīns* made what was before them and what was behind them **seem fair.** **The same construction, in two different directions: the one the scope of the adorning, the other the scope of the protection.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

### وَلَوْ جَعَلْنَٰهُ قُرْءَانًا أَعْجَمِيًّا لَّقَالُوا۟ لَوْلَا فُصِّلَتْ ءَايَٰتُهُۥٓ ءَا۬عْجَمِىٌّ وَعَرَبِىٌّ

"Had We made it a Qurʾān in **a foreign tongue**, they would certainly have said: '**If only its verses had been made distinct! A foreign tongue, and an Arab?**'"

**The name of the sūra returns here:** in the third verse it was said *kitābun **fuṣṣilat** āyātuh*; in the forty-fourth verse the same verb occurs **inside an objection.**

| Verse | Who says it | How |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **The text** | *Fuṣṣilat āyātuh* — **an affirmative statement** |
| 44 | **The supposed objection** | *Law lā fuṣṣilat āyātuh* — **a claim of deficiency** |

**What the verse does is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** a counterfactual situation is set up — "what would they have said had it been otherwise?" **And the answer shows that the objection arises not from the content but from the disposition: whichever option had been taken, an objection to fit it would have been found.**

**وَهُوَ عَلَيْهِمْ عَمًى … فِىٓ ءَاذَانِهِمْ وَقْرٌ** — and at the end of the verse **the word of the fifth verse returns: *waqr*** (the heaviness in the ear).

| Verse | Who says it | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **The other side** — about itself | *Wa fī ādhāninā **waqr*** |
| 44 | **The text** — about them | *Fī ādhānihim **waqr*** |

**The same word forty verses apart, the one as a claim and the other as a judgement.** **In the end the sūra confirms what the other side said about itself** — but with one difference: in the fifth verse it was an **excuse**, here it is a **statement of fact.**

**And in the same sentence a counterpart is given for the other side as well:** *huwa li'lladhīna āmanū hudan wa shifāʾ* — **the same text: guidance and healing for the one, heaviness and blindness for the other.** The text does not change; **the place that receives it changes.** This is the closing of the frame set up in verses 3–5 of the sūra.

---

## 41/45-46 — وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَا مُوسَى ٱلْكِتَٰبَ فَٱخْتُلِفَ فِيهِ · مَّنْ عَمِلَ صَٰلِحًا فَلِنَفْسِهِۦ وَمَنْ أَسَآءَ فَعَلَيْهَا وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِظَلَّٰمٍ لِّلْعَبِيدِ

"We gave Moses (Mūsā) the Book, and **disagreement arose over it**… Whoever does righteous work, it is for himself; whoever does evil, it is against himself. **Your Lord is not one who wrongs His servants.**"

**فَٱخْتُلِفَ فِيهِ** — the verb is **passive**: who it was that fell into disagreement is not stated.

**This is a line treated at several points in this corpus:** `002-bakara.md` 2/213 and 2/253, `045-casiye.md` 45/17. **All of them say the same thing: the disagreement arises after the Book has come.** I rely on those treatments.

**The function of the verse here is consolation:** *wa lawlā kalimatun sabaqat min Rabbika la-quḍiya baynahum* — had a word not gone before, judgement would have been passed between them. **That is, the delay is not indifference.**

**بِظَلَّٰمٍ لِّلْعَبِيدِ** — *ẓallām* is an intensive form (*faʿʿāl*).

**The lexicographers dwell on what the form does here, and two explanations are transmitted:**

| Explanation | Ground |
|---|---|
| 1 | The intensive negates **multitude**: in proportion to the great number of servants |
| 2 | The form conveys no intensive force here; it means **no wrong at all** |

**I transmit both explanations; I do not impose a preference.** On either reading the ruling of the sentence comes out in the same place: **the recompense is tied to the person's own deed** — the preceding sentence had already said this in both directions.

---

## 41/47-48 — وَمَا تَخْرُجُ مِن ثَمَرَٰتٍ مِّنْ أَكْمَامِهَا وَمَا تَحْمِلُ مِنْ أُنثَىٰ وَلَا تَضَعُ إِلَّا بِعِلْمِهِۦ

"No fruit comes out of **its sheath**, and no female conceives or gives birth, save with His knowledge."

**أَكْمَام** — singular *kimm*: **the sheath in which a fruit or a flower sits, the envelope of the bud.** Root **ك-م-م**: to cover, to close over. **The cuff of a garment — the opening at the sleeve — is linked to this root as well, and Turkish, which borrowed the word, ties its *kem* to the same root. English has no such link: *husk* and *cuff* are two unconnected words.**

**The word occurred at `055-rahman.md` 55/11** (*wa'n-nakhlu dhātu'l-akmām*) and was treated there. **I do not repeat it here.**

**The choice of the two examples is worth recording:** the fruit's coming out of its sheath, and birth. **Both describe a coming out from an enclosed place** — and both **become visible at the end of a process that is not seen.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse describes the scope of the knowledge by way of **the most hidden processes.** And this stands exactly opposite the supposition in the twenty-second verse — there it had been supposed that "He does not know much of what we do".

---

## 41/49-51 — لَّا يَسْـَٔمُ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ مِن دُعَآءِ ٱلْخَيْرِ وَإِن مَّسَّهُ ٱلشَّرُّ فَيَـُٔوسٌ قَنُوطٌ

"**Man does not weary of asking for good.** But when evil touches him, he becomes **despairing and hopeless.**"

### لَا يَسْـَٔمُ — and the return to the thirty-eighth verse

**The word occurs for the second time in this sūra, and the two occurrences can be compared:**

| Verse | Who | What he does not weary of |
|---|---|---|
| 38 | *Alladhīna ʿinda Rabbik* | **Glorifying** — night and day |
| **49** | *al-Insān* | **Asking for good** |

**The same verb, two subjects.** I record this as my own reading: **not wearying is described in the verse not as a virtue but as a capacity.** Man has that capacity — but it is used **in asking.** **The difference lies not in the not-wearying but in the direction.**

**يَـُٔوس** and **قَنُوط** — both are in the intensive form. **ي-أ-س**: to cut off hope. **ق-ن-ط** — **the root occurs for the first time in this corpus here**: the sense the lexicographers give is **the complete severing of hope, the settling in of despondency.**

**The use of the two words together sets up a gradation**, and the lexicographers generally give the difference between them thus: the one is the cutting off of hope, the other the outward showing of that state. **I transmit this distinction in the form in which it is transmitted.**

**وَإِذَآ أَنْعَمْنَا عَلَى ٱلْإِنسَٰنِ أَعْرَضَ وَنَـَٔا بِجَانِبِهِۦ وَإِذَا مَسَّهُ ٱلشَّرُّ فَذُو دُعَآءٍ عَرِيضٍ**

"When We bestow favour on man he turns away and **draws his side aside**; but when evil touches him, **he prays at length and breadth.**"

**نَـَٔا بِجَانِبِهِ** — "he moved his side away": **a bodily picture.** Turning the shoulder, turning aside.

**دُعَآءٍ عَرِيضٍ** — *ʿarīḍ*: **broad, wide.** **The choice of the word is striking:** the abundance of the prayer is qualified not by "long" but by **"wide".**

**I record this as an observation:** *ṭūl* (length) would convey continuity; *ʿarḍ* (breadth) conveys **spreading out.** That is, the prayer does not deepen; **it scatters in every direction.** And this comes from the same body-language as *naʾā bi-jānibih* (drawing the side aside) at the beginning of the same verse.

---

## 41/52 — قُلْ أَرَءَيْتُمْ إِن كَانَ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ ثُمَّ كَفَرْتُم بِهِۦ مَنْ أَضَلُّ مِمَّنْ هُوَ فِى شِقَاقٍۭ بَعِيدٍ

"Say: **And what if it is from God, and you have denied it?** Who is further astray than one who is in a far-off breach?"

### The form of the question

**The verse does not set up a claim — it sets up a possibility.** *In kāna min ʿindi'llāh* — "and what if it is?"

**I record this as an observation about method:** the addressee is not asked to accept the other's position; he is asked **to take his own possibility into account.** In the fifth verse of the sūra the other side had closed the argument (*fa'ʿmal innanā ʿāmilūn* — you work, we are working). **This verse reopens the closed argument with a question.**

**شِقَاق** — root **ش-ق-ق**: to split, to cleave in two. **The root was treated at `054-kamer.md` 54/1 (*wa'nshaqqa'l-qamar*) and in `084-insikak.md`.** *Shiqāq* is the falling of two sides into **separate halves.**

**And the adjective *baʿīd* (far):** the breach not only exists, **its distance too is great.** **The same adjective was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/3 (*rajʿun baʿīd*)** — there those who denied counted the resurrection "far off". **Here what is far off is their own condition.**

---

## 41/53-54 — سَنُرِيهِمْ ءَايَٰتِنَا فِى ٱلْءَافَاقِ وَفِىٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَهُمْ أَنَّهُ ٱلْحَقُّ · أَوَلَمْ يَكْفِ بِرَبِّكَ أَنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ شَهِيدٌ

"We shall show them Our signs **in the horizons and in themselves**, until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. **Is it not enough that your Lord is witness over everything?**"

### ٱلْءَافَاق and ٱلْأَنفُس

**آفاق** — singular *ufuq*: **the furthest limit sight reaches, the surroundings, the outer world.** **أنفس** — the person himself, his inner world.

**The pair sums up the whole order of proof in the sūra:** in the third block the peoples that were destroyed (outer), in the twentieth verse the organs that bear witness (inner), in the thirty-seventh verse the sky (outer), in the forty-seventh verse birth (inner). **The two fields had been used alternately throughout the sūra; here the two are named in a single sentence.**

### My limit on this verse

**In the modern period this verse has been made the subject of a great many claims.** Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I write it out plainly:

**I derive from the verse no pointer to any particular scientific discovery, invention or historical event.** There is no ground in the text for such a determination; **the verse specifies neither a field nor a time.**

**More than one reading is transmitted in the classical commentaries as well:**

| Reading | What *sanurīhim* announces |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Historical developments** — the spread of the call, the confirmation of events |
| 2 | **The signs of the Hour** — what will be seen at the end |
| 3 | **A state continually in operation** — what goes on being seen in every age |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**And the verse's own closing is worth reading as a note:** *a-wa-lam yakfi bi-Rabbika annahū ʿalā kulli shayʾin shahīd* — **"Is it not enough that your Lord is witness over everything?"**

**I record this as my own reading:** after the signs that are to be shown, the sentence puts a question **to the search for proof itself.** The sūra opened in the fifth verse with those who drew a veil, reported in the twenty-sixth verse those who drowned it out with noise, and showed in the forty-fourth verse that whichever option had been taken an objection would be found. **The last question stands at the end of this chain: multiplying proofs changes nothing for one who does not want proof.**

**أَلَآ إِنَّهُم فِى مِرْيَةٍ مِّن لِّقَآءِ رَبِّهِمْ أَلَآ إِنَّهُۥ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ مُّحِيطٌ** — the sūra closes with two *alā* (take note).

**مِرْيَة** — root **م-ر-ي**: the concrete sense the lexicographers record is **squeezing and pulling at an animal's udder to milk it**; from this comes the sense of "to wrangle over a matter, to waver in doubt".

**مُّحِيط** — root **ح-و-ط**: to encompass, to surround. **The root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/255** (*wa lā yuḥīṭūna bi-shayʾin min ʿilmih* — man's not being able to encompass). **Here the same root from the other side: He is the one who encompasses everything.**

**The last two words of the sūra complete the picture set up in its first five verses:** there were those who set a veil in between. **At the close it is announced that there is no place left to stay outside of.**

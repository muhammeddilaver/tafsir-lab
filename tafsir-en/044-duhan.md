# 44. Sūrat al-Dukhān

Fifty-nine verses. It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It takes its name from a single word in the tenth verse: **دُخَان** — **smoke.**

The sūra stands at the middle of the seven sūras that open with *ḥā-mīm* (the **Ḥawāmīm**) and keeps up the shared habit of the group: immediately after the letters, **the Book** is named. This pattern was recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`; it holds in al-Dukhān as well — the second verse begins *wa'l-kitābi'l-mubīn*.

**The subject of the sūra is not a single thing; but its method is single.** What it does from beginning to end is to exchange the places of **what the addressee expects** and **what he ought to expect**.

We may see it thus. The sūra begins with a night: on one night every affair is **separated** (4). Then a day comes: on that day everything is **separated** (40). Everything that stands between — the smoke, Pharaoh, Tubbaʿ, the zaqqūm, the muttaqīn (those who guard themselves) — takes its place between these two separations.

**And the sūra lays a single verb upon its addressee: *irtaqib* — watch.** This command is given in the tenth verse, repeated in the fifty-ninth, and the sūra closes with it. **The forty-nine verses between are the content of that watching.**

The most disputed place in the sūra comes immediately after this command: *"watch for the day when the sky will bring a manifest smoke."* **What this smoke is is one of the oldest and most open disagreements in the history of exegesis**, and it will be given below in a table, with no preference imposed.

---

## The structure of the sūra

Eight blocks. Most of the block boundaries can be read off the nominal sentences at the ends of the verses.

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-8 | The letters, the oath, the blessed night, the affair that is separated, lordship | *rabbukum wa rabbu ābāʾikumu'l-awwalīn* (8) |
| **II** | 9-16 | Play inside doubt; **the smoke**; the lifting of the punishment and the return | *innā **muntaqimūn*** (16) |
| **III** | 17-33 | Pharaoh and Moses (Mūsā); the leaving of the sea; those who were left behind | *fīhi balāʾun **mubīn*** (33) |
| **IV** | 34-37 | Mecca's objection to the raising; the people of Tubbaʿ | *innahum kānū **mujrimīn*** (37) |
| **V** | 38-42 | Creation is not play; the day of separation | *innahu huwa'l-**ʿazīzu'r-raḥīm*** (42) |
| **VI** | 43-50 | The zaqqūm, the *muhl*, the dragging, the mocking address | *mā kuntum bihi **tamtarūn*** (50) |
| **VII** | 51-57 | The station of the muttaqīn; no death but the first death | *dhālika huwa'l-**fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm*** (57) |
| **VIII** | 58-59 | The making easy, and the command to watch | *innahum **murtaqibūn*** (59) |

**Two of the blocks look directly at one another, and they say so in their very wording.** Block III enumerates what the house of Pharaoh **left behind** (25-27); block VII enumerates what is **given** to the muttaqīn (51-55). As will be shown below, the two lists are built **out of the same words**, and the only difference between them is one particle and one word.

---

## Two large chains within the sūra

These are countable data of the text. What is interpretation is the claim that they form a pattern, and I present that as an observation.

### The first chain: the garden left / the garden given

| | The house of Pharaoh (25-27) | The muttaqīn (51-55) |
|---|---|---|
| Place | *wa **maqāmin karīm*** (26) | *fī **maqāmin amīn*** (51) |
| Garden and water | ***min** jannātin wa ʿuyūn* (25) | ***fī** jannātin wa ʿuyūn* (52) |
| The taste of the favour | *wa naʿmatin kānū fīhā **fākihīn*** (27) | *bi-kulli **fākihatin** **āminīn*** (55) |
| The verb | *kam **tarakū*** — they left | *yadʿūna fīhā* — they call for |

**The phrase that occurs in the twenty-fifth and the fifty-second verses is identical letter for letter: جَنَّاتٍ وَعُيُونٍ.** The only thing that changes is the particle before it: **مِن** (from — the place they departed) and **فِي** (in — the place they are in).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** the sūra builds its two tableaux out of the same material and reduces the difference to **the person's position with respect to that material**. And the two adjectives added to the two lists say what the difference is: *karīm* (splendid) and **amīn** (safe); *fākihīn* (taking their ease) and **āminīn** (in safety).

**What is missing in the two lists is not splendour but safety.**

### The second chain: *inna + the pronoun of separation + two definite predicates*

The same grammatical pattern occurs in four places in the sūra:

| Verse | Expression | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *inna**hu huwa**'s-Samīʿu'l-ʿAlīm* | God |
| **42** | *inna**hu huwa**'l-ʿAzīzu'r-Raḥīm* | God |
| **49** | *inna**ka anta**'l-ʿazīzu'l-karīm* | **Of a human being — in mockery** |
| **57** | *dhālika **huwa**'l-fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm* | The deliverance |

**The pattern is the same: emphasis + an intervening pronoun of separation (*huwa/anta*) + a predicate with the definite article.** In Arabic this triad is used to say that an attribute **belongs to that one alone** — *qaṣr* (restriction).

**And the sūra gives this pattern three times to its owner and once to someone who is not its owner.** In the forty-second verse *al-ʿAzīz* is God's; in the forty-ninth the same attribute, in the same pattern, is said of a man inside the punishment.

**This is the sharpest point of word order in the sūra, and it will be taken up in detail at the forty-ninth verse.**

---

## The verse-ending: the shared sound of fifty-nine verses

In `050-kaf.md` it was recorded that forty-five verses end in a single sound-pattern. There is a similar case in al-Dukhān, and it can be counted. The list below begins at the second verse; the first is *ḥā-mīm*, and it too closes on the same sound, on *mīm*:

*mubīn, mundhirīn, ḥakīm, mursilīn, ʿalīm, mūqinīn, awwalīn, yalʿabūn, mubīn, alīm, muʾminūn, mubīn, majnūn, ʿāʾidūn, muntaqimūn, karīm, amīn, mubīn, tarjumūn, faʿtazilūn, mujrimūn, muttabaʿūn, mughraqūn, ʿuyūn, karīm, fākihīn, ākharīn, munẓarīn, muhīn, musrifīn, ʿālamīn, mubīn, yaqūlūn, munsharīn, ṣādiqīn, mujrimīn, lāʿibīn, yaʿlamūn, ajmaʿīn, yunṣarūn, raḥīm, al-zaqqūm, al-athīm, al-buṭūn, al-ḥamīm, al-jaḥīm, al-ḥamīm, al-karīm, tamtarūn, amīn, ʿuyūn, mutaqābilīn, ʿīn, āminīn, al-jaḥīm, al-ʿaẓīm, yatadhakkarūn, murtaqibūn.*

**All fifty-nine of the fifty-nine verses end in this pattern: a long *ī* or *ū* sound, followed by a single nasal letter — *n* or *m*.** There is no exception.

**I record this as a countable fact.** The note entered in `050-kaf.md` holds here too: no reckoning of letters or numbers is entered into; what is recorded is only that the sūra is read **on a single breath and with a single closing sound**.

## 44/1-2 — حمٓ · وَٱلْكِتَٰبِ ٱلْمُبِينِ

*Ḥā-mīm · Wa'l-kitābi'l-mubīn*

"Ḥā-mīm. By the clear Book."

### حمٓ — the letters

**The general record on the disconnected letters was entered at length in the section `002-bakara.md` 2/1, and in summary at `050-kaf.md` 50/1. I do not repeat it here.**

The summary of the records made there is this:

> **The meaning of these letters is not known with certainty.** One group of scholars refers the meaning to God (*tafwīḍ*). Among the views that seek an explanation, the one that finds most support from within the text is the explanation that the letters make the challenge meaningful by pointing to the **raw material** of the Qurʾān. **And no numerical reckoning, no *abjad* and no counting of letters is done over these letters.**

In `050-kaf.md` this record was entered once again, in bold. **I enter it here for the third time, and I write the reason:** *ḥā-mīm* stands at the head of seven sūras, that is, it is the most frequently repeated of the groups of disconnected letters; and repetition is the place most open to the manufacture of claims. **This commentary does not go down that road.**

**What can be recorded is a verifiable fact of word order, and it was already recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`:**

> *"In almost all the sūras that open with *ḥā-mīm*, **the Book** is named immediately after the opening."*

**al-Dukhān is one of the shortest examples of this pattern:** the letters are said, and in the second verse *al-kitāb* comes with nothing at all between.

The table drawn up in `050-kaf.md` for the three sūras with a single letter gains one more row here:

| Sūra | The opening | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Ṣād 38/1 | صٓ | *wa'l-Qurʾāni dhi'dh-dhikr* — **oath + Qurʾān** |
| Qāf 50/1 | قٓ | *wa'l-Qurʾāni'l-majīd* — **oath + Qurʾān** |
| al-Qalam 68/1 | نٓ | *wa'l-qalami wa mā yasṭurūn* — **oath + instrument** |
| **al-Dukhān 44/1-2** | حمٓ | *wa'l-kitābi'l-mubīn* — **oath + Book** |
| al-Aḥqāf 46/1-2 | حمٓ | *tanzīlu'l-kitāb* — **no oath, a statement** |

**The difference is worth recording:** of two sūras opening with the same group of letters, one speaks of the Book **by swearing by it**, the other **by making a statement**. **I give this as an observation;** it agrees with the difference in how the two sūras work — al-Aḥqāf demands proof, al-Dukhān commands watching.

### وَٱلْكِتَٰبِ — the object of the oath

**The Qurʾān's swearing by itself was treated in `050-kaf.md`;** the record made there was this: the text begins its work by swearing by itself and ends it by commanding a reminding by means of itself.

**In al-Dukhān the same chain is built more plainly still:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **2** | *wa'l-**kitāb**i'l-mubīn* — **an oath** by the Book |
| **58** | *fa-innamā **yassarnāhu** bi-lisānik* — that Book was **made easy** |

**The sūra opens by swearing by the Book and closes by saying that the Book has been made easy.** The referent of the pronoun *-hu* in the fifty-eighth verse is disputed and will be taken up there; but the most widespread reading refers it to the Qurʾān.

### ٱلْمُبِين — the root ب-ي-ن and the most repeated word of the sūra

**Root: ب-ي-ن.** In `046-ahkaf.md` the double use of this root at al-Aḥqāf 46/7-8 was treated; the concrete sense of the root is **"the opening of a space between two things"**. *Bayn* — the space between. *Bāna* — it parted, it was distinguished. *Bayān* — making distinguishable. *Mubīn* — the active participle of Form IV.

**And the most instructive side of the root is that Form IV works in Arabic both transitively and intransitively:**

| Reading | Meaning | What *al-kitābu'l-mubīn* would mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Intransitive** | *Abāna* = it became clear | The Book that **is itself clear** |
| **Transitive** | *Abāna* = it made clear, it distinguished | The Book that **makes clear, that distinguishes** |

**The lexicographers transmit both readings, and no preference is imposed in this commentary.** The two do not exclude one another: a thing that is not itself clear is hardly to be expected to make anything else clear.

**Now we come to the count within the sūra, and this is the most conspicuous fact of wording in al-Dukhān.**

**The word *mubīn* occurs five times in this sūra**, and each time it qualifies **something else**:

| Verse | Construct | What is qualified |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | *al-kitābu'l-**mubīn*** | **The Book** |
| **10** | *dukhānin **mubīn*** | **The smoke** |
| **13** | *rasūlun **mubīn*** | **The messenger** |
| **19** | *sulṭānin **mubīn*** | **The proof** |
| **33** | *balāʾun **mubīn*** | **The trial** |

**Five separate things, one single adjective.** And the things qualified form a series: the Book (a word), the smoke (an image), the messenger (a person), the proof (evidence), the trial (a situation).

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the five verses:** the sūra names with the same adjective every kind of evidence that can be brought against the addressee. It leaves, that is, no claim of **unintelligibility** standing. And the consequence of this will be stated plainly in the thirteenth verse: *"how should the reminder come to them, when a manifest messenger has come to them?"* — that is, **the plea of obscurity has been closed off.**

In `046-ahkaf.md` it was recorded that the same root is used by the other party as well (*hādhā siḥrun mubīn* — "this is manifest sorcery"). **The finding recorded there works here too:** the dispute is over *what* the manifest thing is, not over whether it is manifest.

### The answer to the oath

In `050-kaf.md` the question of the answer to an oath was taken up. **In al-Dukhān the case is easier: the answer comes at once.**

The third verse begins *innā anzalnāhu*, and the pattern *inna* + past tense at its head is one of the known forms of the answer to an oath in Arabic. **Nothing, then, is left out here; the thing sworn by (the Book) and the thing said in the answer to the oath (the sending down of the Book) are the same thing.**

**And this is not a vicious circle but a deliberate one:** the Book is sworn by, concerning how the Book came.

## 44/3 — إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ فِى لَيْلَةٍ مُّبَٰرَكَةٍ ۚ إِنَّا كُنَّا مُنذِرِينَ

*Innā anzalnāhu fī laylatin mubārakatin, innā kunnā mundhirīn*

"We sent it down on a blessed night. We are ever warners."

### إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ — an opening shared with Sūrat al-Qadr

**These three words are identical letter for letter with the first three words of al-Qadr 97/1.** This was already recorded in `097-kadr.md`:

> *"This verse begins **word for word the same** as al-Qadr 97/1; only the attribute of the night changes."*

**Let us set the two verses side by side:**

| | al-Dukhān 44/3 | al-Qadr 97/1 |
|---|---|---|
| Shared | *innā anzalnāhu fī laylati…* | *innā anzalnāhu fī laylati…* |
| Difference | *…**mubārakatin*** — **indefinite** | *…**al-qadr*** — **definite** |

**The difference is not only in the word but in the definiteness.** al-Dukhān says "on a night" and names the night by **an attribute**; al-Qadr says "on that night" and names the night by **a name**.

**I record this as my own reading:** the context of al-Dukhān does not require the giving of a name, because the sūra is concerned not with the identity of the night but with **the work done** on that night — the next verse will pass straight to the work (*fīhā yufraqu*).

**أَنزَلْنَا — *anzala* or *nazzala*.** This distinction was treated in `097-kadr.md`; I do not repeat it here.

**ٱلْهَاء — the referent of the pronoun is not stated.** In `097-kadr.md` it was recorded that this should be considered together with al-ʿAlaq 96/1. **In al-Dukhān the case is different, and the difference matters: here the referent of the pronoun stands in the preceding verse** — *al-kitābu'l-mubīn.* The gap that exists in al-Qadr, then, does not exist in al-Dukhān.

### لَيْلَةٍ مُّبَٰرَكَةٍ — the disagreement

**Which night this phrase intends is one of the known disagreements of exegesis.** There are two main views, and I do not impose a preference.

| View | Which night | Its ground | The objection of the opposing view |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Night of Decree** | *Laylatu'l-qadr* within the month of Ramaḍān | The identity of wording with al-Qadr 97/1; **al-Baqara 2/185**: *"The month of Ramaḍān is the month in which the Qurʾān was sent down"* — that is, the month of the sending down is named in the Qurʾān | — |
| **The middle of Shaʿbān** | The fifteenth night of the month of Shaʿbān | The reports transmitted about that night; and the joining of the expression *"every wise affair is separated on that night"* with accounts of a yearly apportioning | al-Baqara 2/185 binds the sending down to **Ramaḍān**; there is no wording at all about Shaʿbān in the sūra |

**I enter two records, and neither is a preference but a determination of the data:**

**One.** *"That the two sūras speak of the same night is the widespread view"* — this is the stance of the majority in the exegetical tradition, and in `097-kadr.md` too the reading was made through this link.

**Two.** The material on which the second view rests comes **not from the wording of the verse** but from outside the verse. This does not of itself make the view wrong; but the **kind of ground** of the two views differs, and the reader has a right to be told: one is from within the text, the other from outside it.

**I declare no preference in this commentary.**

### مُّبَٰرَكَة — the root ب-ر-ك

**The concrete sense, so far as the lexicographers record it, runs in two directions, and both are vivid:**

- **بَرَكَ** — the **kneeling** of a camel, its settling in a place and staying there. From the same root *mabrak* — the place where a camel kneels.
- **بِرْكَة** — a pool; the place where water **gathers and stands.**
- **بَرَكَة** — blessing: the **remaining and increasing** of the good in a thing.

**The link between the two branches is made by the lexicographers thus: blessing is the *lastingness* of the good.** An abundance that comes and goes is not blessing; blessing is a small thing that **stays and suffices**. The camel's kneeling and the water's gathering carry the same idea: **to stay in place.**

**And *mubāraka* is a passive participle: "made blessed".** The blessing of the night, then, is not from itself; **it has been given.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the root's sense of "staying in place" makes its choice as an attribute of the night meaningful. **A night is a thing that passes; the attribute given to it describes a thing that stands.** That the work done on that night is described in the next verse as a **fixing** (*ḥakīm* — see below) agrees with this reading.

### إِنَّا كُنَّا مُنذِرِينَ — the second *inna*

**There are two *inna*s in the verse: *innā anzalnā* and *innā kunnā*.** And the pattern of the second is striking: *kāna* + a plural active participle.

**In Arabic *kāna* + an active participle conveys not a single act in the past but *an ongoing attribute*.** The sentence, that is, does not say "we warned"; it says **"we are ever warners"**.

**The root ن-ذ-ر was treated in many sections** (`067-mulk.md`, `071-nuh.md`, `074-muddessir.md`, `077-murselat.md`); I do not repeat it here. The summary: *indhār* is the announcing of an oncoming danger **in time**.

**And the point of word order is this:** the sending down is given as an event (a perfect verb), the warning as an attribute (*kāna* + active participle). **The coming down of this Book, that is, is new; the work of warning is not new.**

**The same pattern will be repeated two verses later:** *innā kunnā mursilīn* (5) — "we are ever senders". **The same structure twice in two verses, and two separate attributes:** to warn and to send.

| Verse | Expression | Attribute |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | *innā kunnā **mundhirīn*** | **To warn** — a relation to the addressee |
| **5** | *innā kunnā **mursilīn*** | **To send** — a relation to the messenger |

**Both are plural active participles, both with *kāna*.** I record this as a verifiable repetition.

## 44/4 — فِيهَا يُفْرَقُ كُلُّ أَمْرٍ حَكِيمٍ

*Fīhā yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm*

"On it every wise affair is separated out."

### فِيهَا — the fronted adverbial

**The sentence does not begin with *yufraqu*; it begins with *fīhā*.** In Arabic the normal order is verb-subject; fronting the adverbial (*taqdīm*) produces emphasis.

**The direction of the emphasis is this: the work is done there — not elsewhere.** The information the sentence carries, that is, is not "it is separated" but **"it is separated on that night"**.

**I record this as an observation about word order;** that the night is named in the third verse as *a* night (indefinite) and brought back in the fourth with *fīhā* completes the way the night is defined: **the night is defined by the work done in it.**

### يُفْرَقُ — the root ف-ر-ق

**The root was treated in `098-beyyine.md` and `077-murselat.md`.** The record in the al-Mursalāt section was this:

> *"The core of the root: **to separate two things from one another, to open a space between them.** Its derivatives: *farq*, *firāq* (parting), *farīq* (a party), *firqa* (a division), *iftirāq*, *tafriqa*. And most important, **فُرْقَان (furqān)** — that which separates."*

**I do not repeat it. I add what is peculiar to al-Dukhān.**

**One. The verb is passive: *yufraqu*.** Who separates is not said. **And the next verse fills that gap:** *amran min ʿindinā* — "as a command from Us". The agent, that is, is given not within the sentence but **in the next verse**.

**Two. The object is *kullu amrin* — "every affair".** The word is singular and indefinite, but has *kull* before it: an all-encompassing singular.

**Three — and here lies the real point within the sūra:** in `077-murselat.md` it was recorded that Sūrat al-Mursalāt opens with ف-ر-ق and gives a day its name with ف-ص-ل, and the two roots were compared in a table:

| | **ف-ر-ق** | **ف-ص-ل** |
|---|---|---|
| Core | **To open a space** between two things | To part a whole **at the joint** |
| What it conveys | **Parting** — the result is two sides | **Distinguishing** — the result is a verdict |

**al-Dukhān does the same thing, and this is a verifiable datum within the sūra:**

| Verse | Root | Expression | What is separated |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | **ف-ر-ق** | *yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm* | **Affairs** — on one night |
| **40** | **ف-ص-ل** | *inna yawma'l-faṣli mīqātuhum* | **Persons** — on one day |

**The sūra opens with a *separating* and is brought to its verdict with a *separating*.** The first is the distributing of affairs; the second is the parting of people. **And the difference between the two roots sits exactly upon this:** *farq* puts a distance between, *faṣl* gives a verdict.

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording of the two verses and the definitions of the two roots are verifiable, but it is I who count them a pattern.

**A note on variant readings.** The sources on the readings transmit a doubled reading of this verb as well (*yufarraqu* — Form II); the contribution of Form II is **takthīr**, that is, separating one by one and abundantly. **The direction of the meaning does not change, the intensity does. I issue no ruling about these readings.**

### حَكِيمٍ — what it means here

**Root: ح-ك-م.** As recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`, the concrete sense of the root is **to restrain and hold in place**; *ḥakama* — the bit put in a horse's mouth. All the derivatives of the root — *ḥakīm*, *ḥukm*, *iḥkām*, *muḥkam* — meet in this single idea: **to keep a thing from slipping away.**

**Now the real question: in which sense of the pattern is *ḥakīm* here?**

In Arabic **the pattern فَعِيل (*faʿīl*) works in two directions**, and this is a fact the lexicographers often record:

| Function of the pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **In place of the active participle** | *ʿalīm* = *ʿālim* | Knowing |
| **In place of the passive participle** | *qatīl* = *maqtūl*; *jarīḥ* = *majrūḥ* | Killed; wounded |

**And for the *ḥakīm* here the exegetes transmit both:**

| Reading | What *amrun ḥakīm* means | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **Active participle** | **A wise affair** — one that is in its right place, not in vain | This is the most widespread use of the word |
| **Passive participle** (*muḥkam*) | **A fixed, settled affair** — one that will not be altered | The second function of the *faʿīl* pattern; and the concrete sense of the root (*to restrain and hold*) gives this directly |

**The second reading works better with the context, and I write the reason — this is not a preference but an observation:** the verb of the sentence is *yufraqu*, that is, a **distribution** is being described. To call what is distributed "wise" states a value; **to call it "fixed" states the character of the distribution.** The second is more directly related to the work of the sentence.

**But this is no imposing of a preference, and the two readings do not exclude one another:** in the logic of the root, wisdom is already firmness. That an affair be in its right place and that it be bound so as not to slip are two faces of the same root.

**And I draw a limit:** how this verse has been used in the debate over decree is a separate subject in the history of exegesis and theology. **I do not enter that debate in this section.** What is given here is the **linguistic side** of the word: the two functions of the *faʿīl* pattern and the concrete sense of the root.

In `097-kadr.md` this verse was already cited, and the sharing of the word *amr* with al-Qadr 97/4 was recorded there; I rely on that treatment.

## 44/5-6 — أَمْرًا مِّنْ عِندِنَآ ۚ إِنَّا كُنَّا مُرْسِلِينَ · رَحْمَةً مِّن رَّبِّكَ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

*Amran min ʿindinā, innā kunnā mursilīn · Raḥmatan min rabbika, innahu huwa's-Samīʿu'l-ʿAlīm*

"As a command from Us. We are ever senders. As a mercy from your Lord. He is indeed the Hearing, the Knowing."

### أَمْرًا — a disagreement of case

**The word comes in the accusative, and the lexicographers differ over what it attaches to.** The disagreement does not change the meaning altogether, but the axis of the sentence changes:

| Reading | Of what is *amran*? | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **A circumstantial accusative** | Of *kullu amrin ḥakīm* | "Every wise affair — **as a command from Us** — is separated" |
| **An absolute object** | In place of the verbal noun of the verb *yufraqu* | "The separating takes place in the form of a **command**" |
| **A circumstantial accusative attached to *anzalnāhu*** | Of the sending down in the third verse | "We sent it down … **as a command**" |

**I do not impose a preference.** What is common to all three is this, and it is the thing to be recorded: **the separating is described not as a process that happens of itself but as a *command*.**

### مِّنْ عِندِنَا — the naming of the source

**عِنْد** — being with something, at its side. It does not report a place; it reports **belonging and authority**.

**And this expression fills the gap of the passive verb (*yufraqu*) in the fourth verse.** There "who separates" was not said; here it is said — yet again not by a name but by a **place**: *from Us*.

**I record this as my own reading:** read together, the two verses bring to the fore not **by whom** the work is done but **whence it issues**. The agent is not hidden; the source is stressed.

### رَحْمَةً مِّن رَّبِّكَ — and the chain with the fifty-seventh verse

**The second accusative word: *raḥmatan*.** The same dispute over case holds here too and arrives at the same result.

**But the thing really to be recorded is its counterpart within the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | What it qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *raḥmatan **min rabbika*** | **The sending down of the Book** |
| **57** | *faḍlan **min rabbika*** | **Being guarded from the Fire** |

**Both verses are in the same pattern: an indefinite noun in the accusative + *min rabbika*.** One before the second block of the sūra, the other at the end of the last block.

**And their shared work is this — I give it as my own reading:** the sūra names both the coming of the Book and the realising of the deliverance not as **an outcome earned** but as **a thing given**. Both are accusative and indefinite; both are rendered "as a…"; both are **a qualification**, not a declaration of right.

**مِّن رَّبِّكَ — "from your Lord".** The sūra shifts here abruptly to the second person. In the fifth verse *ʿindinā* (from Us — first person plural), in the sixth *rabbika* (from your Lord — second person singular).

**This shift is called *iltifāt* (a shift in the direction of address) in Arabic** and was treated in `056-vakia.md`. **Its function here is this:** while the source of the Book is told as "we", when it is told to whom the Book comes **the relation is turned to** — "your Lord".

The finding recorded in `085-buruc.md` for the address *rabbuka* works here too: this construct speaks to the addressee by way of **a relation that belongs to him**.

### إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ — the sūra's pattern for the first time

**This is the first link of the four-verse chain recorded in the section on the structure of the sūra.**

**The pattern: *inna* + a pronoun + the pronoun of separation (*huwa*) + two predicates with the definite article.**

**In Arabic this intervening pronoun is called *ḍamīru'l-faṣl* (the pronoun of separation)** and it does two things:

1. **It keeps the predicate from being taken for an attribute.** Had it been *innahu's-samīʿu*, it could have gone on "he, the hearing…"; with *huwa* in place, the sentence closes there.
2. **It produces restriction (*qaṣr*).** "The Hearing is He alone."

**And two names are chosen: *al-Samīʿ* and *al-ʿAlīm*.**

**Why these two?** Look at the context: the sūra has just said that a **word** was sent down (the Book), and it will shortly report **what the other party says** (13-14: *"a man taught, possessed"*; 34-36: *"bring back our fathers"*).

**I record this as my own reading:** *Samīʿ* looks to what is said, *ʿAlīm* to what is not said. The rest of the sūra requires both of them — because open words will be reported and hidden attitudes will be diagnosed.

## 44/7-8 — رَبِّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَآ ۖ إِن كُنتُم مُّوقِنِينَ · لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ يُحْىِۦ وَيُمِيتُ ۖ رَبُّكُمْ وَرَبُّ ءَابَآئِكُمُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ

*Rabbi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi wa mā baynahumā, in kuntum mūqinīn · Lā ilāha illā huwa yuḥyī wa yumīt, rabbukum wa rabbu ābāʾikumu'l-awwalīn*

"The Lord of the heavens and the earth and what is between them — if you are people of certainty. There is no god but He; He gives life and gives death. Your Lord, and the Lord of your forefathers."

### رَبِّ — an appositive

**The word is in the genitive and stands in apposition to *rabbika* in the preceding verse.** That is, after "from your Lord" is said, that Lord is defined.

**ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا — and this phrase will occur three times in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | *rabbi's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi **wa mā baynahumā*** | **Lordship** |
| **29** | *fa-mā bakat ʿalayhimu's-samāʾu wa'l-arḍ* | **Not being mourned** |
| **38** | *wa mā khalaqna's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa **wa mā baynahumā*** | **The purpose of creation** |

**Two of the occurrences have *wa mā baynahumā*, the middle one does not.** In the twenty-ninth verse the heaven and the earth stand as **a subject** (they did not weep), in the other two as **an object** (that which is lorded over, that which is created).

**I record this as a verifiable repetition.** The difference among the occurrences of the phrase is not interpretation but a datum of wording.

At `046-ahkaf.md` 46/3 the same phrase occurred inside *mā khalaqnā… illā bi'l-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā* and was treated there; at the thirty-eighth verse I shall rely on that treatment.

### إِن كُنتُم مُّوقِنِينَ — the place of the condition

**A condition is added at the end of the sentence: "if you are people of certainty."**

**And the answer to the condition is not stated.** In Arabic this is called *ḥadhf*, and a similar leaving-half-said was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/3.

**Two readings are transmitted as to what the conditional clause does:**

| Reading | What it says | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **The condition qualifies the acceptance of the statement** | "This knowledge is knowledge only for one who seeks certainty" | Its being placed at the end of the sentence; it sifts the addressee |
| **The condition rests on the other party's own claim** | They already accepted the Lord of the heavens and the earth; the verse recalls that acceptance | It is known that the Qurʾān in such places makes the addressee's own admission its proof |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**The root ي-ق-ن:** the leaving of doubt, the settling of knowledge in place as water settles to the bottom. **The root was treated in `102-tekasur.md` and `069-hakka.md` by way of the phrases *ʿilmu'l-yaqīn / ʿaynu'l-yaqīn / ḥaqqu'l-yaqīn*;** it was also set out in a table at `056-vakia.md` 56/95. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What is to be added here is the pattern: *mūqinīn* — the active participle of Form IV.** That is, not "one who possesses certainty" but **"one who has arrived at certainty"**. The pattern conveys an arrival.

### لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ — the structure of the sentence

**The best-known negating structure in Arabic: *lā* that negates the genus.** That is, not "there is no god" but **"there is no such thing as a god"** — the whole kind is lifted, and then a single exception is placed with *illā*.

**How the structure works is this, and it is worth recording:** the sentence first **erases them all**, then **puts one back.** Had the order been reversed (*huwa ilāhun* — "He is a god"), the same thing would not have been said.

### يُحْىِۦ وَيُمِيتُ — two verbs, one order

**Both verbs are Form IV: *aḥyā* (he gave life) and *amāta* (he gave death).** Both, that is, are in the **causative** pattern.

**The order is striking: first giving life, then giving death.**

**The expected order in English is the reverse** — when we say life first and then death we are describing one lifetime. **But the Qurʾān mostly gives this pair in this order, and the order itself says something.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the context of the sūra:** the sūra will shortly (verse 35) report this objection — *"there is only our first death, and we are not to be raised."* The other party, that is, builds the order **life → death → the end.**

**The eighth verse begins the order with *giving life*.** And the verb *yuḥyī* is imperfect: **it conveys continuity.** Not once, then, but repeatable.

**The sentence thus answers the objection of the thirty-fifth verse twenty-seven verses in advance.** It is I who make this link; the wording of the two verses is verifiable.

### رَبُّكُمْ وَرَبُّ ءَابَآئِكُمُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ — the bond made with the fathers

**This phrase makes one of the Qurʾān's most striking moves.**

At Mecca the ground the other party most often put forward is **the fathers**: *"we found our fathers upon a religion."* The fathers, that is, are the other party's **proof**.

**And the verse does not take that proof out of their hands; it changes its owner:** *rabbu ābāʾikumu'l-awwalīn* — **"and the Lord of your forefathers".**

**I record it as my own reading:** the sentence does not say "your fathers were in error". **It says "He is your fathers' Lord too."** The appeal to the fathers is not rejected; **it is carried back to its source.** The fathers too had a Lord; the chain does not end with them.

**ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ — the root أ-و-ل.** And this root will occur three times in the sūra:

| Verse | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *ābāʾikumu'l-**awwalīn*** | **The forefathers** |
| **35** | *mawtatuna'l-**ūlā*** | **The first death** — from the mouths of the deniers |
| **56** | *al-mawtata'l-**ūlā*** | **The first death** — about the muttaqīn |

**The sūra uses the word "first" three times, and in all three there is an implication of a *second*:** the first fathers (is there anything before them?), the first death (is there a second?), the first death (and there is no other).

**I record this as a pattern; I make no claim that the sūra sets up a deliberate play on the word.**

## 44/9 — بَلْ هُمْ فِى شَكٍّ يَلْعَبُونَ

*Bal hum fī shakkin yalʿabūn*

"No; they are playing inside a doubt."

### بَلْ — the particle that turns the discourse

***Bal* (the particle of *iḍrāb*) was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/2;** the record there: it either cancels what precedes, or leaves what precedes and jumps to the real matter.

**Here it is the second, and there is a structural likeness with Qāf.** Both sūras open with an oath and a statement, then pass with *bal* to the condition of the other party:

| Sūra | The transition | The diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Qāf 50/2 | *bal **ʿajibū*** | **They wondered** |
| **al-Dukhān 44/9** | *bal hum **fī shakkin yalʿabūn*** | **They are playing inside a doubt** |

**And the difference between the two diagnoses is worth recording.** In Qāf the objection was named as **a reaction** (wondering). In al-Dukhān it is named as **a condition** and **an act**: an element they are inside (doubt) and a thing they do there (play).

### فِى شَكٍّ — the root ش-ك-ك

**The concrete sense is given by the lexicographers thus: *shakka* — to pierce a thing through, to run through with a spear, to fasten two things together.** *Shikka* — the tie between the poles of a tent. And from this *shakk* — **two possibilities standing in the mind with equal weight.**

**The connection is this:** doubt is the mind's being pinned to two things at once. The man can neither break from the one nor pass to the other.

**And the particle *fī* matters: *fī shakkin* — "*inside* a doubt".** Doubt is given not as an opinion but as **a vessel**.

In `052-tur.md` exactly this structure was treated — there *fī khawḍin yalʿabūn* (playing inside a muddying) was analysed and this table was drawn up:

| | **The vessel** (in the genitive with *fī*) | **The verb** (imperfect) |
|---|---|---|
| What it conveys | The element one is inside | The work being done |

**al-Dukhān uses the same pattern and only changes the vessel:** in al-Ṭūr *khawḍ* (plunging into muddied water), in al-Dukhān *shakk* (doubt).

### يَلْعَبُونَ — the root ل-ع-ب and the thirty-eighth verse

**As recorded in `052-tur.md`:** *luʿb* — play; the Qurʾān qualifies the life of this world with this word (al-Anʿām 6/32; al-ʿAnkabūt 29/64). And the definition given there: **"Play is seriousness with no outcome."**

**Now we come to what is peculiar to al-Dukhān, and this is one of the cleanest internal chains of the sūra.**

***The same root occurs twice in the sūra, and the two occurrences stand exactly opposite one another:***

| Verse | Expression | Who is playing |
|---|---|---|
| **9** | *fī shakkin **yalʿabūn*** | **They** — inside a doubt |
| **38** | *wa mā khalaqnā… **lāʿibīn*** | **Not We** — creating is not play |

**The ninth verse gives play to them; the thirty-eighth lifts play away from God.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root and the opposed subjects of the two verses:** the sūra returns the charge of play to one side. The party that does not take creation seriously is itself described as at play.

**And the mood of the verb is imperfect: *yalʿabūn* — it continues.** Not a single act but an ongoing state.

## 44/10-11 — فَٱرْتَقِبْ يَوْمَ تَأْتِى ٱلسَّمَآءُ بِدُخَانٍ مُّبِينٍ · يَغْشَى ٱلنَّاسَ ۖ هَٰذَا عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

*Fa'rtaqib yawma taʾti's-samāʾu bi-dukhānin mubīn · Yaghsha'n-nāsa, hādhā ʿadhābun alīm*

"So watch for the day when the sky will bring a manifest smoke. It will envelop the people. This is a painful punishment."

### فَٱرْتَقِبْ — the first command of the sūra

**For nine verses the sūra gave no command.** In the tenth the first command comes, and the sūra will close with this command (59).

**Root: ر-ق-ب.** It was treated in `090-beled.md` and `058-mucadele.md`, and summarised at `050-kaf.md` 50/18. The summary of the record there:

> *"The original sense of the root is to watch, to keep an eye on, to wait. […] And from the same root **raqaba** — the neck. The connection is this: the neck is the axis on which the head turns and the gaze is directed. One who watches stretches his neck and turns it."*

**I do not repeat it. What is peculiar to al-Dukhān is the pattern.**

**The verb is in Form VIII: *irtaqaba*.** The contribution of Form VIII (*iftiʿāl*) in Arabic is **the taking of the work upon oneself, the making of an effort**. *Raqaba* is to watch; *irtaqaba* is **to make watching one's business**.

**And this pattern occurs in a countable number of places in the Qurʾān. One of them was treated in `054-kamer.md`:**

> *"**al-Qamar 54/27** — *fa'rtaqibhum wa'ṣṭabir*. […] The order of the two commands is worth recording: first watch, then be patient. Patience here, that is, is **not inactivity**; it is patience while watching. The man does not withdraw, he goes on looking."*

**The use in al-Dukhān parts from that in al-Qamar at one point, and the difference matters:**

| | al-Qamar 54/27 | al-Dukhān 44/10 |
|---|---|---|
| The object of the command | ***-hum*** — watch **them** | ***yawma*** — watch for **that day** |
| What is awaited | The conduct of a group | The coming of a time |
| The command beside it | *wa'ṣṭabir* — be patient | None — a single command |

**In al-Qamar what is watched is people, in al-Dukhān a day.** I record this as an observation about the wording.

**And the particle *fāʾ*: *fa'rtaqib*.** It attaches to the preceding verse. **The command, that is, is given as the *consequence* of the finding "they are playing inside a doubt".**

**I record this as my own reading:** what is proposed as the thing to do about a man at play is not **argument** but **waiting**. There is a moment when the play will end of itself; the command looks to that moment.

### يَوْمَ تَأْتِى ٱلسَّمَآءُ بِدُخَانٍ — the word order

**The verb is *taʾtī* — to come; and it has been made transitive with the particle *bi-*: *atā bi-* = to bring.**

**The sentence, that is, does not say "smoke comes from the sky".** It says **"the sky comes with a smoke"**. The sky is here the subject; the smoke is what the sky brings.

**I record this as an observation about word order, and it has a consequence:** the smoke is described not as a fault in the sky but as something the sky **brings**. When the subject of the verb is the sky, the event is built not as a breakdown but as **a delivery**.

### دُخَان — the word itself

**Root: د-خ-ن.** This root has not been treated anywhere else in this index; I give it here in full.

**The concrete sense: the thick vapour that rises from something burning — smoke.**

Its derivatives show the direction of the root:

- **دَخَّنَ** — it smoked, it gave off smoke.
- **دُخْنَة** — the colour of smoke: a dirty, sooty darkness. In Arabic it is the name of a colour.
- **أَدْخَن** — that which is of a smoky colour.
- **دَخَن** — **a figurative use:** a flaw within an affair, a hidden rancour in the heart, a murkiness behind a word. The lexicographers explain it by way of "something whose fire looks to have gone out but which still smoulders within".

**The two ideas the root carries must be recorded together:** **the closing off of sight** and **the presence of a fire behind it.** Smoke says both at once — it blocks seeing, and it is evidence of a burning.

**The word occurs once more in the Qurʾān:** at Fuṣṣilat 41/11 the first state of the heaven is called *dukhān* (*thumma'stawā ila's-samāʾi wa hiya dukhān*). **The contrast between the two occurrences is worth recording:** there the smoke is the **beginning** of the sky, here it is what the sky **brings**.

**I give this as an observation; I make no claim that a pattern is set up between the two verses.**

### يَغْشَى ٱلنَّاسَ — the root غ-ش-ي

**The root was treated in `092-leyl.md`, `071-nuh.md` and `053-necm.md`;** the summary: **to cover a thing, to overspread it, to envelop it.** *Ghishāwa* — a veil that comes down over the eye. *Ghāshiya* — that which covers (the name of a sūra).

**And the object of the verb must be recorded: *al-nās* — the people, with the definite article.**

**This word stands at the centre of the disagreement below.** *Al-nās* in Arabic can mean both "the people" (a particular group) and "mankind" (the whole); the definite article can convey both *ʿahd* (a known group) and *istighrāq* (comprehensiveness). **This is exactly where the two sides take their stand.**

### هَٰذَا عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ — who is speaking?

**The sentence begins with a demonstrative and its speaker is not specified.** Two readings are transmitted:

| Reading | Who speaks | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **The text's own verdict** | The verse names the event | "This is a painful punishment" — a finding |
| **Their words** | The people say it while inside the smoke | It comes from the same mouth as *rabbana'kshif* in the next verse |

**The second reading makes a smooth continuity with the twelfth verse:** *"This is a painful punishment! Our Lord, lift the punishment from us!"* — two sentences from one mouth.

**I do not impose a preference.** But one datum of word order can be recorded: in the twelfth verse there is no "they said" at all. **The speech begins directly.** This makes easier the reading that the eleventh verse is from the same mouth — but it does not make it necessary.

**عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ — *alīm* is on the *faʿīl* pattern and is here in the sense of an active participle: *muʾlim* — causing pain.** The discussion of *ḥakīm* in the fourth verse should be recalled: the same pattern, working here in the other direction.

**And a contrast is built within the sūra:**

| Verse | The attribute of the punishment | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | *ʿadhābun **alīm*** | **Painful** — it looks to the body |
| **30** | *al-ʿadhābi'l-**muhīn*** | **Abasing** — it looks to honour |

**In the same sūra the punishment is named with two separate attributes** — one promised to Mecca, the other the punishment from which the Children of Israel were delivered. **I record this as a verifiable repetition;** the difference between the two attributes will be taken up at the thirtieth verse.

## 44/12 — رَّبَّنَا ٱكْشِفْ عَنَّا ٱلْعَذَابَ إِنَّا مُؤْمِنُونَ

*Rabbana'kshif ʿanna'l-ʿadhāba innā muʾminūn*

"Our Lord! Lift this punishment from us; we do believe."

### The speech given with no introduction at all

**There is no "they said" in the verse.** The speech is conveyed directly, with no verb coming between.

**In Arabic this is a known ellipsis (*ḥadhfu'l-qawl*)** and it is often used in the Qurʾān. **Its effect is this: no narrator comes between, and the reader hears the words directly.**

**I record this as an observation about word order;** the whole tension of this section rests on how **sincere** the words look — and the fifteenth verse will pass judgement on that sincerity.

### رَّبَّنَا — and the call itself

**The vocative particle (*yā*) has been dropped: *rabbanā*, not *yā rabbanā*.**

**In Arabic the dropping of the vocative particle conveys that the one called is reckoned *near*.** The one calling, that is, is not shouting into the distance.

**And there is a tension here — I record it as my own reading:** in the twelfth verse God is called upon in the **nearest** manner, and in the fourteenth the messenger is called *"a man taught, possessed"*. **Two verses stand between the two sentences.** The same mouth, at two extremes.

**And note: the name called upon is *Rabb*, not *Allāh*.** *Rabb* — the one who nurtures, who rears, who owns. The name chosen in the moment of distress, that is, is the name that establishes **the relation of need**.

### ٱكْشِفْ — the root ك-ش-ف

**The concrete sense: to lift the covering off a thing, to uncover it.** *Kashf* — an uncovering. *Kāshifa* — that which brings to light.

**The root was treated in `050-kaf.md`** — there it was taken up by way of the verse *fa-kashafnā ʿanka ghiṭāʾak* (We removed your veil), and this finding was made: **what is removed is the covering that blocks sight.**

**Now let us look at the use here and record a point of language.**

**In the eleventh verse the punishment was described as something that *covers*: *yaghsha'n-nās*.** **And the verb whose performance is asked for in the twelfth is *kashf* — the exact opposite of covering.**

| Verse | Verb | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | *yaghshā* (غ-ش-ي) | **To cover over** |
| **12** | *ikshif* (ك-ش-ف) | **To lift the covering** |
| **15** | *kāshifū* (ك-ش-ف) | **We shall lift** |

**The two roots are opposites of one another and they come one upon another in three verses.** This is a verifiable datum; that I count it a pattern is my own reading.

**The choice of word says one thing more — my own reading:** for the removal of the punishment it is not "destroy it" that is said but **"uncover"**. The punishment is described, that is, not as something that **disappears** when it is removed but as something that is **lifted off from above**. And the fifteenth verse will confirm this: the lifting will be **temporary**.

### إِنَّا مُؤْمِنُونَ — and the sūra's chain of أ-م-ن

**The sentence is a nominal sentence: *innā muʾminūn*.** Not a verbal sentence (*āmannā* — "we have believed").

**In Arabic a nominal sentence conveys *thubūt* (fixedness), a verbal sentence *ḥudūth* (coming to be).** What is said, that is, is not "we have now believed" but **"we are people who believe"**.

**I give this as my own reading, and it produces a tension:** the words said in the most permanent form are the words the fifteenth verse declares **the most temporary**. *Innā muʾminūn* (a claim of fixedness) stands beside *innakum ʿāʾidūn* (a verdict of return).

**The root أ-م-ن occurs five times in the sūra**, and after *mubīn* this is the densest root in it:

| Verse | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **12** | *innā **muʾminūn*** | **Those under the punishment** — a claim |
| **18** | *rasūlun **amīn*** | **Moses** — a trustworthy messenger |
| **21** | *wa in lam **tuʾminū*** | **The house of Pharaoh** — a condition |
| **51** | *maqāmin **amīn*** | **The muttaqīn** — a safe place |
| **55** | ***āminīn*** | **The muttaqīn** — in safety |

**The two branches of the root work together here:** *īmān* (to trust, to hold true) and *amān/amn* (to be safe). **The same root gives both believing and being secure** — in Arabic the link is made thus: to believe is **to trust** someone; to be secure is **for fear to be lifted**.

**And the arrangement of the sūra puts these two branches at the two ends:** at the beginning faith is **claimed** (12), at the end safety is **given** (51, 55). **I record this as my own reading;** the wording of the five occurrences is verifiable.

## 44/13-14 — أَنَّىٰ لَهُمُ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ وَقَدْ جَآءَهُمْ رَسُولٌ مُّبِينٌ · ثُمَّ تَوَلَّوْا۟ عَنْهُ وَقَالُوا۟ مُعَلَّمٌ مَّجْنُونٌ

*Annā lahumu'dh-dhikrā wa qad jāʾahum rasūlun mubīn · Thumma tawallaw ʿanhu wa qālū muʿallamun majnūn*

"How should the reminder come to them, when a manifest messenger had already come to them? Then they turned away from him and said, 'A man taught, possessed.'"

### أَنَّىٰ — an interrogative particle

***Annā* carries three senses in Arabic: whence, how, when.** And there is something common to all three: **the question is asked not to seek information but to report an impossibility** (*istifhām inkārī*).

**The sentence, that is, does not ask "when will they take the reminder?"; it says "how should taking the reminder be possible for them?"**

**And the ground comes immediately after: *wa qad jāʾahum rasūlun mubīn*.**

### وَقَدْ — a circumstantial clause and a ground

**The *wāw* at the head is the *wāw* of circumstance: "while…".** And *qad* + the perfect strengthens the fact of its having happened.

**The logic of the sentence is this, and it is worth recording:** the impossibility of their taking the reminder is drawn not from **their incapacity** but from **the opportunity's having already been used.**

**The verse says, that is: the reminder has already come. There is no place for asking for something new.**

**I record this as my own reading:** when the offer of faith made under punishment is refused, the ground given is not "you are not sincere" — it is **"it has already been tried"**. The measure is not intention but **history**.

### ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ — the root ذ-ك-ر and the fifty-eighth verse

**The root was treated in many sections** (`050-kaf.md`, `054-kamer.md`, `087-ala.md`); I do not repeat it here.

**What is peculiar to al-Dukhān is the root's two occurrences in the sūra, and the difference between them is sharp:**

| Verse | Expression | Mood | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13** | *annā lahumu'dh-**dhikrā*** | **A question — denial** | Their taking the reminder is **not possible** |
| **58** | *laʿallahum **yatadhakkarūn*** | **A particle of hope (*laʿalla*)** | Their taking the reminder is **hoped for** |

**The same root, at the two ends of the sūra, in two opposite moods.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses.** And it is no contradiction: **the thirteenth verse speaks of the moment *inside* the punishment, the fifty-eighth of the time *before* it.** The matter, that is, is not whether the reminder is possible but **at which moment it is possible.**

**And this carries the whole logic of the sūra: the invalidity of the appeal made when the punishment arrives comes not from the appeal itself but from its *timing*.**

### رَسُولٌ مُّبِينٌ — the third *mubīn*

**The third of the five occurrences of *mubīn* in the sūra.** And what is qualified this time is a **person**.

Two readings are transmitted for *rasūlun mubīn*, and both rest on the two branches of the root:

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intransitive** | A messenger who **is himself manifest** — his identity, his speech, his life in the open |
| **Transitive** | A messenger who **makes clear** — who expounds what he has brought |

**I do not impose a preference.**

### ثُمَّ تَوَلَّوْا۟ عَنْهُ — the work of *thumma*

***Thumma* in Arabic conveys a delayed sequence: "then".** Its difference from *fāʾ* is that time passes in between.

**And here it says something: the turning away did not happen the moment the messenger came.** There is an interval.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse describes the rejection not as a sudden reaction but as **a stance taken after a while**. This strengthens the ground given in the thirteenth verse — the opportunity was not merely given, **there was also time to use it.**

**The root ت-و-ل:** *tawallā ʿan* — to turn away from a thing, to turn one's back. The original sense of the root is **nearness and adjacency** (*walī*, *mawlā*); *tawallā* + *ʿan* is **the reversal of that nearness**. The root was treated in sections such as `002-bakara.md` and `092-leyl.md`.

**And what is striking is this:** the same root will come back in the forty-first verse as *mawlā* — *"on that day no *mawlā* will avail a *mawlā* anything."* **What they turned away from and what will not avail on that day are from the same root.** I record this as an observation about the wording.

### مُعَلَّمٌ مَّجْنُونٌ — the two words standing together

**Two attributes are placed side by side, and this deserves careful reading, because the two together are strange.**

**مُعَلَّم — the root ع-ل-م, the passive participle of Form II: "taught".** That is, he has taken lessons from someone; he does not speak on his own.

**مَجْنُون — the root ج-ن-ن, a passive participle: "possessed by jinn".** The concrete sense of the root is **to cover, to conceal** (*janna'l-layl* — the night covered; *janīn* — the one covered in the womb; *janna* — a garden whose trees cover the ground). *Majnūn* — one whose mind is covered over.

**Now let us look at the strangeness:**

| Attribute | What it implies | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| ***Muʿallam*** | He takes his words **from somewhere** | A teacher, a source, **an orderly transmission** |
| ***Majnūn*** | His words **are not his own** | Lack of control, inconsistency, **randomness** |

**The two accusations spoil one another.** A man who has been taught cannot be mad; a madman cannot transmit an orderly teaching.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the definitions of the two words:** the other party cannot produce a single explanation, and lays two explanations one upon another. **And this confirms the diagnosis of the thirteenth verse: what is present is not a proof but a *decision to reject* — the ground is sought afterwards.**

**And a detail of word order:** there is no *wāw* between the two words. *Muʿallamun majnūn* — "a man taught, possessed". **The unconjoined sequence fuses the two attributes into a single verdict;** they are said, that is, not as two separate claims but as a single piece of abuse. **And this too turns their words into a hurling rather than an analysis.**

## 44/15 — إِنَّا كَاشِفُوا۟ ٱلْعَذَابِ قَلِيلًا ۚ إِنَّكُمْ عَآئِدُونَ

*Innā kāshifu'l-ʿadhābi qalīlan, innakum ʿāʾidūn*

"We shall lift the punishment a little; you will return."

### كَاشِفُوا۟ — a noun, not a verb

**The sentence does not say *sanakshifu* (we shall lift).** *Kāshifū* — the plural of the active participle, joined in a genitive construction: *kāshifu'l-ʿadhāb.*

**In Arabic the active participle also covers the future; but it differs from a verb, and the difference is at work here:**

| | **A verb** (*nakshifu*) | **An active participle** (*kāshifū*) |
|---|---|---|
| What it conveys | An **event** | A **position, an attribute** |
| Its relation to time | Bound to a particular moment | Not bound to a moment |

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence builds not a promise but **a description**. The lifting is told not as an act of favour but as **an operation that will bring the other party's condition to light**.

**And the nominal sentence has one more consequence:** *innā muʾminūn* in the twelfth verse was a nominal sentence too. **Two nominal sentences stand face to face:**

| Who | Expression | Its claim |
|---|---|---|
| **They (12)** | *innā **muʾminūn*** | "We are people who believe" |
| **The text (15)** | *innakum **ʿāʾidūn*** | "You are **people who return**" |

**The same pattern, the same emphasis (*inna*), the same claim of fixedness — two opposite verdicts.** **I record this as a verifiable datum of word order;** that the two sentences form an exchange is my own reading.

### قَلِيلًا — the littleness of what

**The word is in the accusative and two readings are transmitted as to what it qualifies:**

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **An adverb of time** (*zamānan qalīlan*) | "We shall lift it for **a short time**" |
| **A quantity** (*kashfan qalīlan*) | "We shall lift it **a little**" — not entirely |

**I do not impose a preference.** What is common to both is that the lifting is **incomplete**: either its duration is short, or it is itself partial.

### إِنَّكُمْ عَآئِدُونَ — the cycle is given its name

**Root: ع-و-د.** It was treated in `085-buruc.md` (in the discussion of *yubdiʾu wa yuʿīd*): **to return, to repeat.** *Iʿāda* — a bringing back; **ʿāda** — a custom, the thing repeated; *maʿād* — the place of return.

**And the most instructive side of the root is here: *ʿāda* (custom) and *ʿawda* (return) come from the same root.** To return to a thing is to have made that thing one's custom.

**The verse does not say "you will disbelieve again"; it says "you are people who return".** Disbelief, that is, is named not as a place gone to but as **the place returned to**.

**I record this as my own reading:** the word describes the claim of faith as a **deviation** and disbelief as the **real position**. On this reading, the *innā muʾminūn* said in the twelfth verse is not a return but **a way-station**.

### The returning of "we believe" once the punishment is lifted — the cycle itself

**Let us now look at what these three verses (12, 13-14, 15) build together, because the most widespread human situation in the sūra is described here.**

**The cycle has four steps, and the verses give them in order:**

| Step | Verse | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Distress** | 10-11 | The punishment comes, it envelops the people |
| **2. The appeal** | 12 | *"Our Lord! Lift it. We do believe."* |
| **3. The lifting** | 15a | *"We shall lift it a little."* |
| **4. The return** | 15b | *"You are people who return."* |

**And when the fourth step joins the first, the cycle closes.**

**The Qurʾān describes this cycle in many places, and its plainest example is that of those caught in a storm at sea:** *"When they are aboard the ship they call upon God, making the religion His alone. But when He brings them safe to land, at once they associate others with Him"* (al-ʿAnkabūt 29/65). The same structure is present at Yūnus 10/22-23 as well.

**The difference of al-Dukhān from those verses is this — I record it as my own reading:** those verses **describe** the cycle; al-Dukhān **announces it in advance.** The sentence *innakum ʿāʾidūn* is said before the return takes place.

**And this takes the verse out of being a sentence of observation and makes it a sentence of diagnosis.**

**Let us now look at the content of the diagnosis. The question to be asked is: what is wrong here?**

**A distinction must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading.** To turn to God in a moment of distress is not what the verse criticises — the Qurʾān says elsewhere that the call of one in straits is answered (al-Naml 27/62). **What is criticised is the relation between the *cause* of the turning and its *result*.**

It may be seen thus:

| | **The cause** | **What happens** |
|---|---|---|
| **If distress produces the faith** | Pain | When the pain lifts, the support lifts |
| **If a proof produces the faith** | A proof | Even if the pain lifts, the proof stands |

**What the verse points to is this second column.** The one who says *"we do believe"* may not be lying; **at that moment he really does believe.** But what carries his belief is the punishment itself — and when the punishment is lifted, the carrier is lifted too.

**And the thirteenth verse was saying exactly this:** *"How should the reminder come to them, when a manifest messenger had come?"* **The proof, that is, was already there; what was accepted was not the proof.**

**This reading is mine;** its ground is the order of the three verses: the appeal (12) → the reminder that the proof had already come (13) → the verdict of return (15).

## 44/16 — يَوْمَ نَبْطِشُ ٱلْبَطْشَةَ ٱلْكُبْرَىٰٓ إِنَّا مُنتَقِمُونَ

*Yawma nabṭishu'l-baṭshata'l-kubrā, innā muntaqimūn*

"On the day We seize with the greatest seizing. We are indeed exacting requital."

### بَطْش — the root ب-ط-ش

**The root was treated at length in `085-buruc.md` and this verse was already cited there. I do not repeat it.** The summary of that record:

> *"*Baṭsh*: **to grasp a thing violently and harshly, to spring upon it and seize it.** The word describes the movement of the hand — clawing, snatching, taking hold and not letting go. […] The three elements the word carries: **suddenness**, **harshness**, **not letting go**."*

**And one thing recorded in the al-Burūj discussion must be carried straight over here:** the same root occurs in the Qurʾān as **the act of tyrants** as well — *"And when you seize, you seize like tyrants"* (al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/130).

**The principle established in al-Burūj was this: *the verdict is announced in the language of the crime.***

**There are three things to add peculiar to al-Dukhān.**

**One — the absolute object.** *Nabṭishu'l-**baṭshata***: the verb is reinforced with its own verbal noun. In Arabic this structure (*mafʿūl muṭlaq*) **makes the verb certain**, and when it takes an adjective it **qualifies** it.

**Two — the adjective: *al-kubrā*.** The feminine of the elative: "the greatest". **There is a scale, then** — there are lesser seizings too, and this is the greatest of them.

**And this is the second centre of the disagreement below:** if the lesser seizings are those that happen in this world, then the "greatest" must be somewhere else.

**Three — *innā muntaqimūn*.** The root **ن-ق-م**: to find a thing foul and reject it, to feel displeasure. *Niqma* — the penalty given as the counterpart of that displeasure.

**The difference between the word and the English "vengeance" must be recorded, and this is a point the lexicographers dwell on too:** in English, vengeance is the satisfying of a personal anger. In the Arabic root, what stands at the centre is **finding a thing unacceptable**. *Naqima ʿalayhi* — he reproached him, he found him foul.

**The core of the word, that is, is not emotion but judgement:** a thing has been **rejected**, and its counterpart is being given.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the lexicographers' definition of the root.** And the pattern supports it too: *muntaqimūn* is a plural active participle — it reports an attribute, not a moment of anger.

## The word that gives the sūra its name: the disagreement over *dukhān*

**This is the most disputed matter in the sūra and one of the oldest disagreements in the history of exegesis. There are two main views, and no preference is imposed in this commentary.**

**First a record: the disagreement is not something the moderns have produced.** From the earliest exegetes onward the two views have been transmitted side by side. **I do not give the views by ascribing them to particular persons;** the ascriptions occur differently in different sources, and the condition of transmitting with a name in this commentary is to be certain of the ascription.

### The two views

| | **(a) A state already lived through** | **(b) A sign of the Hour** |
|---|---|---|
| **What the smoke is** | A period of severe famine and hunger; people whose eyes darken with hunger seeing a smoke-like murkiness between themselves and the sky | **A real smoke** — one that will appear before the Hour and really be seen |
| **When** | In a period near the revelation of the sūra, **over and done with** | **Not yet happened**, in the future |
| **Its strongest ground** | **44/15**: *innā kāshifu'l-ʿadhābi qalīlan innakum ʿāʾidūn* — it is said that the punishment is **lifted** and that afterwards there is a **return**. A punishment that is a sign of the Hour is not lifted and returned from | **44/16**: *yawma nabṭishu'l-baṭshata'l-**kubrā*** — the expression "the greatest seizing" befits not a famine but a final reckoning |
| **Its second ground** | The command *fa'rtaqib* suits a near event better; and the Qurʾān names famine elsewhere too as a warning | **44/11**: *yaghsha'**n-nās*** — "it envelops the people". If the definite article is read comprehensively, a local famine does not answer to it |
| **Its third ground** | *Rabbana'kshif ʿanna'l-ʿadhāba innā muʾminūn* — a plea and a promise; this is meaningful in this world, not at the Hour | The expression *"hādhā ʿadhābun alīm"* suggests a scene of punishment rather than a passing hardship |
| **The other side's objection** | The smoke is qualified as "manifest" (*mubīn*); the darkening of the eye from hunger is a state **particular to a person** and cannot be "manifest" | The **lifting** and the **return** in the fifteenth verse do not fall into place on the reading that it is a sign of the Hour |

### What each side does with verse 15

**The fifteenth verse is the key point of the disagreement, and the two sides read it in two separate ways:**

| View | How it reads verse 15 |
|---|---|
| **(a)** | Directly: the punishment was lifted, they returned. The verse describes something that happened |
| **(b)** | Two routes are transmitted: **(i)** the lifting belongs to some other hardship, before the smoke; **(ii)** the sentence has the sense of a **condition** — "even if We lift it, you will return" — that is, it reports an attribute, not an event |

**The second route has a linguistic ground:** in Arabic a nominal sentence (*innā kāshifū*) can report not an event but **a state**; and *innakum ʿāʾidūn* is in the same pattern. **But the objection that this is forced is also made, and it must be recorded.**

### A third stance

**A third stance is transmitted in the sources as well and must be recorded honestly:** it is said that the two views **can be combined** — that while the sūra names a nearby hardship, the language it uses may have a breadth that looks to the Hour as well.

**This stance tries to preserve both views; but since it loads two separate events onto one expression, it may be counted a separate explanation. I do not present it either as a preference in this commentary.**

### What we can say and what we cannot

**What we cannot say:** which of the two the smoke is. **The text does not determine it**, and I do not stand in for a text that does not determine.

**What we can say is the following three data drawn from the wording of the verses:**

1. **The smoke is qualified as *mubīn*** — with the adjective the sūra uses five times. The smoke, that is, is named like the Book and the messenger in the category of **clarity**.
2. **The verb takes the form "the sky brings"** — the event is built not as a breakdown but as a coming.
3. **What the verse commands is not a piece of knowledge but a stance:** *irtaqib* — watch. **The sūra does not describe what the smoke is; it asks the addressee to await it.**

**And one last record, as my own reading:** the reason this disagreement has lasted so long is that the sūra does not **introduce** the smoke. What was recorded for *zaqqūm* in `056-vakia.md` works here too: the word comes indefinite (*dukhānin* — "a smoke"), that is, **it does not introduce, it estranges.** The *al-kubrā* of the sixteenth verse likewise leaves a comparison but does not give the measure.

**To produce definiteness where the text has left indefiniteness is not the business of exegesis.**

## 44/17 — وَلَقَدْ فَتَنَّا قَبْلَهُمْ قَوْمَ فِرْعَوْنَ وَجَآءَهُمْ رَسُولٌ كَرِيمٌ

*Wa laqad fatannā qablahum qawma firʿawna wa jāʾahum rasūlun karīm*

"Before them We tried the people of Pharaoh too, and a noble messenger came to them."

### قَبْلَهُمْ — why the story is here

**The first work of the sentence is to establish a link: *qablahum* — "before them".**

**The story, that is, enters not as a narrative but as a *parallel*.** In the thirteenth verse Mecca was told *"a manifest messenger came to you"*; in the seventeenth the people of Pharaoh are told *"a messenger came to them"*. **The same verb, the same structure.**

| Verse | Who | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **13** | Mecca | *wa qad **jāʾahum rasūlun** mubīn* |
| **17** | The people of Pharaoh | *wa **jāʾahum rasūlun** karīm* |

**The shared part of the two sentences is identical letter for letter: جَآءَهُمْ رَسُولٌ.** The only thing that changes is the messenger's attribute: *mubīn* and *karīm.*

**I record this as a verifiable repetition.** And it has a consequence: **the sūra describes two communities with the same sentence, and then tells how each of them behaved.**

### فَتَنَّا — the root ف-ت-ن and the frame of the block

**The concrete sense: to put gold into the fire and separate out what is pure.** *Fattāna* — the goldsmith's crucible. From this *fitna* — both a **testing** and the **disorder** that the testing produces.

**The logic of the root is this:** fire does not destroy gold, it **separates** it. So too a testing: it does not make anything new, it makes what is there visible.

**And now let us look at the frame of the block — this is a verifiable datum within the sūra:**

| Verse | Word | Root | Its place in the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **17** | *fatannā* | **ف-ت-ن** | **The first word of the Pharaoh block** |
| **33** | *balāʾun mubīn* | **ب-ل-و** | **The last word of the Pharaoh block** |

**A block of seventeen verses stands between two words for *trial*.** One for the people of Pharaoh, the other for the Children of Israel.

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** the sūra does not frame the story as a tale of **victor and vanquished**. **It frames both sides with the same word: being tested.** For those delivered too there is a *balāʾ* — this will be taken up separately at the thirty-third verse.

### رَسُولٌ كَرِيمٌ — and the sūra's chain of ك-ر-م

**Root: ك-ر-م.** It was treated at `056-vakia.md` 56/44 and the sense the lexicographers give was recorded there:

> *"In Arabic *karīm* is also used in the sense of a thing's being **the good and useful of its own kind**. *Nakhlun karīm* — a fruitful palm."*

**The chain established for this word in the al-Wāqiʿa discussion was this, and it looks exactly to this place:**

| al-Wāqiʿa | Verse | What |
|---|---|---|
| Negation | 44 | *lā bāridin wa **lā karīm*** — **denied** of a shade |
| Affirmation | 77 | *la-qurʾānun **karīm*** — **given** to the Qurʾān |

**And the conclusion recorded in al-Wāqiʿa:** *"The same attribute, denied in one place and ascribed in another."*

**al-Dukhān does something more complex with the same word. *Karīm* occurs three times in the sūra:**

| Verse | Construct | Of whom / of what | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **17** | *rasūlun **karīm*** | **Moses** | **Real** — the attribute of the messenger |
| **26** | *maqāmin **karīm*** | **The place the house of Pharaoh left** | **Real but temporary** — abandoned |
| **49** | *anta'l-ʿazīzu'l-**karīm*** | **A man inside the punishment** | **Mockery** — the word returned to its claimant |

**In al-Wāqiʿa there were two poles: denial and affirmation. In al-Dukhān there are three positions: deserved, not held on to, and claimed without deserving.**

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording of the three verses is verifiable, but it is I who count them a series.

**And a detail: the attribute *karīm* is used here of Moses — one of the places in the Qurʾān where this attribute is used of a messenger.** The sūra qualifies the messenger as *mubīn* (13, to Mecca) and *karīm* (17, to Pharaoh). **The two attributes report two separate directions:** the one the messenger's **intelligibility**, the other his **worth**.

## 44/18 — أَنْ أَدُّوٓا۟ إِلَىَّ عِبَادَ ٱللَّهِ ۖ إِنِّى لَكُمْ رَسُولٌ أَمِينٌ

*An addū ilayya ʿibāda'llāh, innī lakum rasūlun amīn*

"Saying: 'Hand over to me the servants of God. I am to you a trustworthy messenger.'"

### أَنْ — the explicative particle

**The *an* at the head is the explicative *an*:** it opens out the content of a verb of speaking that precedes it. Here there is no explicit verb of speaking before it; **the sense of speaking is implicit** in the expression *jāʾahum rasūl*, and *an* brings it out.

**The sentence, that is, builds the structure "a messenger came and said" with a single letter.**

### أَدُّوٓا۟ — the root أ-د-ي and the point of the verb chosen

**This root has not been treated anywhere else in this index; I give it here.**

**The concrete sense: to convey a thing to its place complete and entire.**

- **أَدَّى** — he paid, he handed over, he discharged.
- **أَدَاء** — *adāʾ*: the paying of a debt, the full performance of a task.
- **تَأْدِيَة** — a conveying.

**At the centre of the root stands the idea of *a trust*, and the Qurʾān establishes this openly:** *"God commands you to **hand over** trusts to those they belong to (*an tuʾaddu'l-amānāti ilā ahlihā*)"* (al-Nisāʾ 4/58). **The same verb, the same pattern.**

**Now we come to the real point, and I give it as my own reading.**

**The Qurʾān tells the same demand elsewhere with another verb:** *"**Send** the Children of Israel with us"* — *fa-arsil maʿanā banī isrāʾīl* (Ṭāhā 20/47; al-Shuʿarāʾ 26/17). **There the verb is the root ر-س-ل: to send, to let go.**

**In al-Dukhān the verb changes: *addū* — hand over.**

| Verb | Root | What it implies |
|---|---|---|
| ***Arsil*** | ر-س-ل | **Let go** — release what is in your hand |
| ***Addū*** | أ-د-ي | **Hand over** — give the trust you hold to its owner |

**The difference is not small.** *Arsil* counts Pharaoh an **owner** and asks him to let go. *Addū* counts him **one who holds** and asks for **restitution**.

**And what strengthens this is the object: *ʿibāda'llāh* — "the servants of God".** The owner of what is to be handed over, that is, is the one named within the sentence.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence builds a demand for freedom as a **correction of ownership**. Not "set them free"; but **"they are not yours; give them to their owner."**

### عِبَادَ ٱللَّهِ — object or vocative

**The exegetes differ over the place of this phrase in the sentence, and the disagreement changes the meaning:**

| Reading | What the sentence becomes | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **Object** | "Hand over to me **the servants of God**" — that is, the Children of Israel | The verb (*addū*) requires an object; and this is the subject of the story |
| **Vocative** | "**O servants of God!** Give me [my due / a hearing]" — that is, an address to the house of Pharaoh | The dropping of *yā* is ordinary in Arabic; and the object of *addū* would then be elided |

**The first reading is the more widespread** and agrees better with the context (in the next verse *lā taʿlū ʿala'llāh*, then at 23 *fa-asri bi-ʿibādī*). **But I do not impose a preference;** the second reading too is transmitted and is linguistically possible.

**I record one datum, and it is not a preference:** in the twenty-third verse God will name the same community *ʿibādī* ("My servants"). **The name the messenger uses and the name God uses are the same.**

| Verse | Who speaks | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | **Moses** | *ʿibāda**'llāh*** — "the servants of God" |
| **23** | **God** | *bi-**ʿibādī*** — "with My servants" |

**This is a verifiable repetition within the sūra** and may be counted a datum supporting the first reading.

### إِنِّى لَكُمْ رَسُولٌ أَمِينٌ — the word order

**There are three elements and their order is meaningful: *innī* (emphasis) + *lakum* (fronted) + *rasūlun amīn* (the predicate).**

***Lakum* — "to you".** Its fronting produces emphasis: **a messenger sent to you, not to someone else.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** before introducing the messengership, the sentence introduces **its addressee**. The house of Pharaoh, that is, is here not the party excluded but the party **addressed**.

**أَمِين — the root أ-م-ن.** Above (at verse 12) the sūra's chain of five occurrences was given.

**And the work of the word *amīn* here must be recorded:** the *faʿīl* pattern carries both directions of the root — both **trusted** (one with whom a trust may be left) and **secure**. The context is the first.

**And it has a link with the preceding sentence — my own reading:** the messenger asked for **a trust** to be restored (*addū*); immediately after, he introduced himself as **trustworthy**. **Two ends of the same field of meaning:** the man who asks for a trust says first that he himself is fit for a trust.

## 44/19 — وَأَن لَّا تَعْلُوا۟ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۖ إِنِّىٓ ءَاتِيكُم بِسُلْطَٰنٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Wa an lā taʿlū ʿala'llāh, innī ātīkum bi-sulṭānin mubīn*

"'And do not exalt yourselves against God. I bring you a manifest proof.'"

### The order of the two demands

**The messenger's speech has two items, and both are joined with *an*:**

1. **Hand over** (18) — a demand for an **act**.
2. **Do not exalt yourselves** (19) — a demand for an **attitude**.

**The order is worth noting: first the work to be done, then the stance to be taken.** And the second is the cause of the first — what lies beneath the not handing over is self-exaltation.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two items.**

### تَعْلُوا۟ — the root ع-ل-و and the thirty-first verse

**The concrete sense: to rise, to be high.** *ʿUluww* — height. *Aʿlā* — higher. *Taʿālā* — He is exalted.

**And the root's use of a human being is negative in the Qurʾān:** to rise is to climb to a position that does not belong to one.

**Now let us look at the chain within the sūra — this is one of the cleanest internal links of al-Dukhān:**

| Verse | Who speaks | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **19** | **Moses — a warning** | *wa an lā **taʿlū** ʿala'llāh* — "do not rise" |
| **31** | **The text — a verdict** | *innahu kāna **ʿāliyan** mina'l-musrifīn* — "he was one of those who rise" |

**The warning and the verdict are given with the same root.** The warning is in the imperative and negative; the verdict comes with *kāna* + an active participle, that is, as **a settled attribute**.

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root of the two verses:** the sūra announces the outcome in the language of the offence. The principle recorded for *baṭsh* in `085-buruc.md` works here too.

**عَلَى ٱللَّهِ — the particle.** "**Against** God". The direction of the self-exaltation, that is, is specified: not against people, but against God.

**And this is a fine point — I give it as my own reading:** Pharaoh's exalting himself over people was already visible. **The verse gives that visible self-exaltation another name.** A superiority established over human beings is, in the messenger's eyes, a taking of position against God.

### سُلْطَٰن — the root س-ل-ط

**The root was treated in `069-hakka.md`, `059-hasr.md`, `053-necm.md` and `055-rahman.md`.** The summary: **to be set over, to prevail, to have one's judgement carry.** *Sallaṭa* — to loose a thing upon a thing.

**And the most striking side of the word in the Qurʾān is here: *sulṭān* is used in the sense both of *power* and of *proof*.**

**The link between the two senses is made by the lexicographers thus:** a proof too is a kind of sovereignty — **it rules the mind, it cannot be withstood.**

**I record this, and it has a consequence:** in the verse Moses speaks in Pharaoh's own language. Pharaoh has *sulṭān* (power) in his hand; Moses says he too has a *sulṭān* in his hand — **but of another kind.**

**This reading is mine;** its ground is the word's having two senses and the identity of the addressee.

**مُّبِين — the fourth occurrence.** This time it qualifies the proof. The table of the five occurrences was given at the second verse.

**And *ātīkum* is an active participle: "I bring / I am bringing to you".** Not a verb but a noun; the bringing, that is, is presented as a **position** — the messenger is already in that state.

## 44/20 — وَإِنِّى عُذْتُ بِرَبِّى وَرَبِّكُمْ أَن تَرْجُمُونِ

*Wa innī ʿudhtu bi-rabbī wa rabbikum an tarjumūn*

"'And I have taken refuge in my Lord and your Lord, lest you stone me.'"

### عُذْتُ — the root ع-و-ذ and the point of the tense

**The root was treated at length in `113-felak.md` and `114-nas.md`** (by way of the pattern *aʿūdhu*), and it also occurred in `072-cin.md` and `071-nuh.md`. **I do not repeat it here.** The summary: *ʿawdh* — **to draw close** to a firm place for protection from a danger, to take refuge.

**What is peculiar to al-Dukhān is the tense, and it matters.**

**In al-Falaq and al-Nās the verb is imperfect: *aʿūdhu* — "I take refuge".** Here it is perfect: ***ʿudhtu* — "I have taken refuge".**

| Pattern | Where | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| ***Aʿūdhu*** (imperfect) | al-Falaq 113/1, al-Nās 114/1 | A **continual, repeated** taking of refuge |
| ***ʿUdhtu*** (perfect) | **al-Dukhān 44/20** | A taking of refuge **already made, completed** |

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence is not a prayer but **a statement**. The messenger does not say "I take refuge"; he says **"I have taken refuge"** — the precaution, that is, was taken before the threat came.

**And this sets the tone of the speech: in the face of the possibility of being threatened there is no panic, but a completed preparation.**

### بِرَبِّى وَرَبِّكُمْ — the address taken refuge in

**The construct is repeated twice: "my Lord and your Lord."**

**And this does the same work as the eighth verse of the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who speaks |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | *rabbukum wa rabbu ābāʾikumu'l-awwalīn* | **The text** — to Mecca |
| **20** | *bi-rabbī **wa rabbikum*** | **Moses** — to the house of Pharaoh |

**Both sentences do the same thing: they do not leave the addressee outside the relation of lordship.**

**I record it as my own reading:** the messenger takes refuge, from those who threaten him, in **the one who is their Lord too**. The station taken refuge in, that is, is a station that belongs to the other party as well. **This forestalls from the outset any turning of the threat into a contest of "our god is stronger than yours".**

### أَن تَرْجُمُونِ — the root ر-ج-م

**The root occurred in `067-mulk.md` and `081-tekvir.md`** (in the discussion of *rujūm li'sh-shayāṭīn*). Its concrete sense: **to throw stones, to stone.**

**The branches of the root in Arabic must be recorded, because the disagreement arises from them:**

- **رَجْم** — to stone, to kill by stoning.
- **رَجْم بِالغَيْب** — to throw without knowledge, to speak as one throws a stone in the dark. The Qurʾān uses this: *"**guessing at** what they did not see"* (al-Kahf 18/22).
- **رَجِيم** — stoned, driven off.
- And the lexicographers record one further branch: **to revile, to speak harshly** — "to hurl words as one hurls a stone".

**Two readings are transmitted as to the meaning here:**

| Reading | Meaning | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **To kill by stoning** | A threat of actual killing | The original and most widespread sense of the root |
| **To revile, to insult** | An attack by speech | The figurative branch of the root; and the next verse (*fa'ʿtazilūni*) asks for a severing of relation rather than for the ending of a physical attack |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**And the dropping of the *yāʾ* at the end of the verb (*tarjumūni*, not *tarjumūnī*) must be recorded:** in Arabic this dropping is widespread at the ends of verses and preserves the verse-ending. The same phenomenon was recorded for the word *waʿīd* in `050-kaf.md`. **The next verse has the same dropping: *fa'ʿtazilūni*.**

## 44/21 — وَإِن لَّمْ تُؤْمِنُوا۟ لِى فَٱعْتَزِلُونِ

*Wa in lam tuʾminū lī fa'ʿtazilūn*

"'And if you do not believe me, then keep away from me.'"

### تُؤْمِنُوا۟ لِى — the point of the particle

**The verb has been made transitive not with *bi-* but with *li-*: *tuʾminū lī*.**

**This difference is recorded in Arabic:**

| Particle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***Āmana bi-*** | To believe in the **truth** of a thing |
| ***Āmana li-*** | To trust a **person**, to accept his word |

**Here *li-* comes.** The sentence, that is, does not say "if you do not believe what I have brought" but **"if you do not trust me"**.

**I record this as a datum of language, and it has a consequence:** in the preceding verse the messenger had introduced himself as *amīn* (trustworthy). **Here he speaks of the possibility that this introduction will be refused.**

### فَٱعْتَزِلُونِ — the root ع-ز-ل

**This root has not been treated anywhere else in this index.**

**The concrete sense: to draw a thing to one side, to set it apart, to put it aside.** *ʿAzala* — he removed from office; *maʿzil* — a corner, a place apart. In Form VIII, *iʿtazala* — to withdraw oneself, to keep away.

**And the pattern matters: the verb is in Form VIII, that is, the pattern of drawing *oneself* aside.** But the command is directed to the addressee: **"draw yourselves away from me."**

**Now let us look at the scale of the demand, and I record this as my own reading.**

**The messenger's speech descends in three steps:**

| Verse | The demand | Level |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | *addū ilayya* | **The highest:** hand over the servants |
| **19** | *lā taʿlū* | **The middle:** do not exalt yourselves |
| **21** | *fa'ʿtazilūni* | **The lowest:** at least **leave me alone** |

**The demand, that is, grows smaller as it goes unaccepted.** At the last step what is asked is not even faith but **neutrality.**

**This reading is mine;** its ground is the order of the three demands and the fact that the last sentence is made conditional (*wa in lam tuʾminū*).

**And one more thing must be recorded:** in the twentieth verse the messenger had taken refuge from being stoned. **In the twenty-first what he asks is exactly the counterpart of that: do not touch me.** The two verses together match the taking of refuge with the demand.

## 44/22 — فَدَعَا رَبَّهُۥٓ أَنَّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ قَوْمٌ مُّجْرِمُونَ

*Fa-daʿā rabbahu anna hāʾulāʾi qawmun mujrimūn*

"Then he called upon his Lord: 'These are a guilty people.'"

### فَ — nothing in between

***Fāʾ* conveys succession without interval** (*fāʾu't-taʿqīb*); it was treated in `056-vakia.md`.

**In the twenty-first verse the last demand is made, and in the twenty-second the text passes straight to the calling.** In between neither an answer nor a waiting is described.

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the text does not narrate the refusal — it **skips** the refusal. We understand it from its consequence.

### دَعَا — the root د-ع-و and its counterpart within the sūra

**The concrete sense: to call out, to summon.** *Duʿāʾ* — a call; *dāʿī* — one who calls; *daʿwa*.

**And the root has two branches in Arabic:** to **call to** someone and to **ask for** a thing. Both issue from the idea of "calling" — what is asked for is in a manner called for.

**The sūra uses this root twice, and both its branches:**

| Verse | Expression | Which branch | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| **22** | *fa-**daʿā** rabbah* | **To call out** | **Moses** |
| **55** | ***yadʿūna** fīhā bi-kulli fākihatin* | **To ask for** | **The muttaqīn** |

**I record this as a verifiable repetition within the sūra.**

### أَنَّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ قَوْمٌ مُّجْرِمُونَ — the content of the calling

**And here is the most striking place in the verse.**

**A prayer is made — but the content of the prayer is not a *request*, it is a *report*.**

*"These are a guilty people."* The sentence ends there. There is no demand: their destruction is not asked for, their deliverance is not asked for, nothing is asked for.

**Let us compare it with other examples of prayer in the Qurʾān:**

| Who | The form of the prayer |
|---|---|
| Noah | *"My Lord! **Leave not** upon the earth any of the disbelievers"* (Nūḥ 71/26) — an explicit demand |
| Moses (elsewhere) | *"Our Lord! **Blot out** their wealth"* (Yūnus 10/88) — an explicit demand |
| **Moses (here)** | *"These are a guilty people"* — **no demand** |

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the three verses:** here the prayer is in the form of a **report**. The messenger states the situation and leaves the verdict.

**And the form of the answer supports this reading:** the answer that comes in the next verse is not a command to destroy but a **command to march** — *fa-asri bi-ʿibādī.*

In `071-nuh.md` Noah's prayer was treated; the difference between the long demand there and the one-sentence report here agrees with the difference in how the two stories are told.

### مُّجْرِمُونَ — the root ج-ر-م and its counterpart within the sūra

**The concrete sense is given by the lexicographers thus: *jarama* — to cut a thing, to pluck it off;** particularly **to pluck fruit from the branch** (*jarama'n-nakhla*). From this *jurm* — **an evil thing acquired**, an offence committed.

**The logic of the root is worth recording:** an offence is named as **the plucking of a thing from its place**. One who plucks the fruit before its time has broken an order.

**And *mujrim* is an active participle — not a single act but an attribute.**

**The sūra uses this root twice, and both in the same pattern:**

| Verse | Expression | About whom | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **22** | *inna hāʾulāʾi **qawmun mujrimūn*** | **The people of Pharaoh** | **Moses** |
| **37** | *innahum kānū **mujrimīn*** | **The people of Tubbaʿ and those before** | **The text** |

**And twelve verses apart there are two more sentences built with the same demonstrative:**

| Verse | Expression | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **22** | ***inna hāʾulāʾi** qawmun mujrimūn* | **The people of Pharaoh** |
| **34** | ***inna hāʾulāʾi** la-yaqūlūn* | **The Meccans** |

**The first two words of the two sentences are the same: إِنَّ هَٰؤُلَاءِ.** One about a community of the past, the other about the community present at the sūra's revelation.

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** *hāʾulāʾi* is a demonstrative indicating **what is near**. The sūra points to a far-off people and to a nearby people with the same pronoun of nearness. **The distance between the story and the present, that is, is removed in the language.**

## 44/23-24 — فَأَسْرِ بِعِبَادِى لَيْلًا إِنَّكُم مُّتَّبَعُونَ · وَٱتْرُكِ ٱلْبَحْرَ رَهْوًا ۖ إِنَّهُمْ جُندٌ مُّغْرَقُونَ

*Fa-asri bi-ʿibādī laylan innakum muttabaʿūn · Wa'truki'l-baḥra rahwan, innahum jundun mughraqūn*

"'Then set out with My servants by night; you will be pursued. And leave the sea as it is; they are an army to be drowned.'"

### فَأَسْرِ — the form of the answer

**It comes with *fāʾ*: there is no gap between the prayer and the answer.**

**And the answer is not an announcement of destruction but a *command for a movement*.** This completes the reading recorded at the twenty-second verse.

**Root: س-ر-ي.** It was treated in `089-fecr.md` (*wa'l-layli idhā yasr* — "by the night as it passes"). The concrete sense: **to travel by night, to journey by night.** *Isrāʾ* — Form IV: to make travel by night.

**And the root already contains the night. Why, then, is *laylan* said?**

**The lexicographers explain this in several ways, and all are transmitted:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Emphasis** | To reinforce the sense already in the root |
| **Its being indefinite sets a limit** | *Laylan* is indefinite: during **part** of the night — not the whole night |
| **To specify the hour of departure** | It reports that the march must **begin** at night |

**I do not impose a preference.** The second explanation, since it rests on the word's being indefinite, is the closest to the wording; I record this as an observation.

### بِعِبَادِى — "My servants"

**It was recorded above (at verse 18): the name the messenger uses and the name God uses are the same.**

**What is to be added here is the particle: *asrā bi-* — "he made travel with…", that is, he had them taken along. The command is to Moses, but the object belongs to the community: the messenger does not merely go, he takes.**

### إِنَّكُم مُّتَّبَعُونَ — the pursuit announced in advance

**A passive participle: "you are people who will be pursued".** The root **ت-ب-ع** — to follow, to go after; it was treated in `050-kaf.md` in the discussion of *Tubbaʿ* and in `077-murselat.md`.

**And its point is this: the pursuit is announced before it has begun.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sentence is not a warning but a piece of **information**. The departure is built not as a flight but as a **movement whose outcome is known**. It is said that they will be pursued, and it is also said what will be done afterwards (24). **No frightening uncertainty, that is, is left.**

### وَٱتْرُكِ ٱلْبَحْرَ — and the two occurrences of the root ت-ر-ك

**Root: ت-ر-ك** — to leave, to abandon, to leave as it is.

**And now let us look at the two closest occurrences in the sūra — in two consecutive verses:**

| Verse | Expression | Who leaves | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **24** | *wa'**truki**'l-baḥra rahwan* | **Moses** | **The sea** — by command |
| **25** | *kam **tarakū** min jannātin wa ʿuyūn* | **The house of Pharaoh** | **Everything** — of necessity |

**Two verses in succession, the same root, two opposite forms.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root of the two verses:** one leaves **by command**, the other by having it **taken from his hand.** And the scale of what is left is inverted: the messenger leaves a sea, the others leave gardens, springs, crops, station and ease.

### رَهْوًا — the root ر-ه-و

**This root has not been treated anywhere else in this index, and it occurs only here in the Qurʾān. For this reason I give it in full.**

**The sense of the root recorded by the lexicographers divides into two branches, and the disagreement arises exactly from this:**

**The first branch — stillness, calm:**
- **رَهَا** — it grew calm, it grew still.
- **مَشَى رَهْوًا** — he walked slowly, quietly.
- The sea's being *rahw*: **without waves, still.**

**The second branch — openness, a gap:**
- **رَهْو** — the hollow between two hills, a gap.
- **الرَّهْوَاء** — a wide, open plain.
- The sea's being *rahw*: **in an opened, parted state, not closing.**

**And the lexicographers join the two branches thus:** a gap that stays open is a gap that stays motionless. **The shared idea: to stand untouched, as it is.**

**Now let us give the disagreement:**

| Reading | What *rahwan* means | What the command becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Still, calm** | The sea in a state of calm | "Leave the sea calm" — do not stir it up after crossing |
| **Open, parted** | The sea in an opened state | "Leave the road open, do not close it" — let those behind enter |
| **Walking slowly** | *Rahwan* is Moses' own state | "Leave the sea, crossing it slowly" — the state is of those crossing, not of the sea |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But an observation can be recorded, and its ground is the next clause:** the verse continues *innahum jundun mughraqūn* — "they are an army to be drowned". **For a drowning the sea must *close*.** The second reading (leave it open) builds a direct ground for this outcome: the road is left open so that they may enter.

**This is an observation, not a preference;** the first reading too arrives at the same outcome indirectly.

### إِنَّهُمْ جُندٌ مُّغْرَقُونَ — and the event that is not told

**A passive participle again: *mughraqūn* — "those who will be drowned".**

**And now we come to the most striking choice of narration in the sūra.**

**The sūra does not narrate the drowning.**

In the twenty-fourth verse *"they are an army to be drowned"* is said; the twenty-fifth begins straight off with *"how much they left behind"*. **There is no event in between.**

| Verse | What there is |
|---|---|
| 24 | **A verdict:** an army to be drowned |
| 25 | **The consequence:** how much they left behind |
| — | **The event: none** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses.** A similar observation was made in `054-kamer.md` for Sūrat al-Qamar (that the drowning is never mentioned). **In al-Dukhān the drowning is named as an *attribute*, not as an *event*.**

**And its effect is this:** the sūra does not build the destruction as a scene. What it builds is the gap between the **before** of the destruction (an army) and its **after** (gardens left empty). **And the reader crosses the gap himself.**

**جُنْد — the root ج-ن-د.** Its concrete sense is explained by the lexicographers by way of **"hard, stony ground"**; from this *jund* — a body locked together and hardened: an army.

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "Pharaoh and his people", it says **"an army"**. The other party, that is, is named here not as a group of belief but as a **mass of force**.

**I give this as an observation.**

## 44/25-27 — كَمْ تَرَكُوا۟ مِن جَنَّٰتٍ وَعُيُونٍ · وَزُرُوعٍ وَمَقَامٍ كَرِيمٍ · وَنَعْمَةٍ كَانُوا۟ فِيهَا فَٰكِهِينَ

*Kam tarakū min jannātin wa ʿuyūn · Wa zurūʿin wa maqāmin karīm · Wa naʿmatin kānū fīhā fākihīn*

"How many gardens and springs they left behind! And crops, and a noble station. And a comfort in which they took their ease."

### كَمْ — a "how many" that asks no number

***Kam* is of two kinds in Arabic: interrogative (*istifhāmiyya*) and declarative of multitude (*khabariyya*).** Here it is the second: **"how many", "how much".**

**And *kam khabariyya* has a feature: its specifier comes with *min*** — as indeed here: *min jannātin.*

**The sentence, that is, gives no number; it reports a multitude.** And the withholding of the number is made up for by the length of the list: four items are enumerated.

### The list itself — a civilisation drawn in three words

**The things enumerated form a series, and the order is worth noting:**

| Order | Word | What | Which field |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *jannāt* | **Gardens** | Land |
| 2 | *ʿuyūn* | **Springs** | Water |
| 3 | *zurūʿ* | **Crops** | Production |
| 4 | *maqām karīm* | **Noble stations** | Position, standing |
| 5 | *naʿma* | **Comfort** | A way of living |

**The first three are the three legs of an agricultural civilisation: land, water, produce.** The fourth is social, the fifth subjective.

**I record this as my own reading: the list runs from the outside inward — land is concrete, comfort is a feeling. And in the last item the construction changes: the first four are nouns, and with the fifth a clause comes (*kānū fīhā fākihīn*). The list, that is, ends by passing from the things possessed to the state of possessing.**

**جَنَّٰت — the root ج-ن-ن.** It was recorded at the fourteenth verse in the discussion of *majnūn*: the sense of the root is **to cover**; a *janna* is a garden whose trees cover the ground.

**And now we come to the plainest internal chain of the sūra.**

### مِن جَنَّٰتٍ وَعُيُونٍ and فِى جَنَّٰتٍ وَعُيُونٍ

**The fifty-second verse repeats the phrase of this verse letter for letter:**

| Verse | Expression | Who | Particle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **25** | ***min** jannātin wa ʿuyūn* | **The house of Pharaoh** | ***min*** — the place they departed |
| **52** | ***fī** jannātin wa ʿuyūn* | **The muttaqīn** | ***fī*** — the place they are in |

**The only thing that changes is the particle.**

**This is a verifiable datum of the text.** And what is interpretation is this, which I give as my own reading: **the sūra builds its two tableaux out of the same material.** The kind of favour is the same; the difference lies in the relation the person has with the favour — in the one the favour is the thing **left**, in the other the thing **one is inside**.

**And the difference does not stop at the particle.** The fifty-first verse names the place of the muttaqīn *maqāmin **amīn***; the twenty-sixth names the place of the others *maqāmin **karīm***. **Two *maqām*s, two attributes.**

### وَمَقَامٍ كَرِيمٍ — what is it?

**Root: ق-و-م.** *Maqām* — the place where one rises and stands; a noun of place. The root was treated in many sections.

**The exegetes differ over what the phrase intends:**

| View | What |
|---|---|
| **Assemblies, places of sitting** | The places where men of standing gathered |
| **Palaces, mansions** | The buildings themselves |
| **Rank, position** | Not a concrete place but a social position |

**I do not impose a preference.** What is common to all three is that *maqām* reports **a place in the eyes of others** — unlike a garden and a spring, the worth of this item depends on **being seen**.

**I give this as my own reading, and the comparison with the fifty-first verse opens it out:**

| Verse | Construct | What the attribute reports |
|---|---|---|
| **26** | *maqāmin **karīm*** | **Splendour** — it looks outward |
| **51** | *maqāmin **amīn*** | **Safety** — it looks inward |

**The difference between the two stations is not one of size but of kind.** The first was splendid and was left; the second is safe, and the fifty-sixth verse will say what that means: there is no death there.

### وَنَعْمَةٍ — *naʿma* or *niʿma*

**The word is read نَعْمَة (with fatḥa) and is not the same thing as نِعْمَة (with kasra). The difference must be recorded:**

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **نِعْمَة (*niʿma*)** | **A favour given** — a bestowal |
| **نَعْمَة (*naʿma*)** | **Living in comfort, a state of plenty** — a way of living |

**This distinction was treated in `073-muzzemmil.md`** — there, in the analysis of *ūli'n-naʿma* (those in comfort), this verse was already cited:

> *"The stress shifts from what is given to **the standard of life being lived**."*

**I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

### فَٰكِهِينَ — the root ف-ك-ه

**The root was treated in `056-vakia.md`** — by way of 56/20, 56/32 and 56/65. **The record there:**

> *"**Root: ف-ك-ه.** *Fākiha* — fruit. And the second branch of the root: **فُكَاهَة (*fukāha*)** — pleasant talk, jesting, banter. *Fākih* — jesting, in good spirits. […] In Arabic *ṭaʿām* (food) reports a necessity, *fākiha* a surplus."*

**I do not repeat it. I add what is peculiar to al-Dukhān.**

**In al-Wāqiʿa the root always occurred as an object: *fākiha* — fruit. Here it occurs as a state**: *fākihīn* — "as those taking their ease".

**And in al-Wāqiʿa at verse 65 the root reported a state once more (*tafakkahūn*), and there too what was described was the going of the favour.** In the closing table of `056-vakia.md` this was recorded thus: *"ف-ك-ه — *fākiha* (20) — *fākiha* (32) — *tafakkahūn* (65) — The favour, and the going of the favour."*

**al-Dukhān builds the same pair and completes it within its own sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **27** | *naʿmatin kānū fīhā **fākihīn*** | **The house of Pharaoh** | **They left it** |
| **55** | *bi-kulli **fākihatin** **āminīn*** | **The muttaqīn** | **In safety** |

**The difference between the two verses is a single word: *āminīn*.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** in the first tableau there is ease and no security; in the second, security is set beside the ease. **And the twenty-seventh verse says what ease without security is by means of the past tense: *kānū* — "they were".**

**A note on variant readings:** it is transmitted that this word is also read *fakihīn*; the difference of meaning is small (to be in good spirits / to have come to fruit). **I issue no ruling about the readings.**

## 44/28 — كَذَٰلِكَ ۖ وَأَوْرَثْنَٰهَا قَوْمًا ءَاخَرِينَ

*Kadhālika wa awrathnāhā qawman ākharīn*

"So it was. And We made another people inherit them."

### كَذَٰلِكَ — what it attaches to

**A one-word sentence, and the exegetes differ over what it attaches to:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| **It attaches to what precedes** | "So it **was**" — it closes the list in 25-27 |
| **It attaches to what follows** | "**So it was** — We made another people inherit" — it qualifies the manner of the inheriting |
| **The predicate of an elided subject** | "The matter **is thus**" |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**I record one datum, and it is a repetition within the sūra:** the same word occurs in the same position in the fifty-fourth verse as well — **at the head of the sentence, followed by *wāw* and a first person plural verb:**

| Verse | Expression | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| **28** | *kadhālika **wa awrathnāhā** qawman ākharīn* | **We made inherit** |
| **54** | *kadhālika **wa zawwajnāhum** bi-ḥūrin ʿīn* | **We paired** |

**The two sentences are in the same pattern: *kadhālika* + *wāw* + a verb of the *faʿalnā* pattern + a pronoun + an object.**

**I record this as a verifiable repetition within the sūra.** And what is interpretation is this, which I give as my own reading: **the two sentences stand at the same place in the two tableaux** — one closing the list of the house of Pharaoh, the other closing the list of the muttaqīn. **In the one there is a passing out of hand, in the other a joining together.**

### أَوْرَثْنَٰهَا — the root و-ر-ث

**The root occurred in `057-hadid.md` and `089-fecr.md`.** Its concrete sense: **the passing of a man's property to another after his death.** *Wārith* — the one to whom the property passes; *mīrāth* — the thing that passes.

**And Form IV (*awratha*) is transitive: "he gave as an inheritance".**

**The choice of word is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the verse does not say "We gave" (*aʿṭaynā*) or "it passed into the hands of others". **It says "We made them inherit".**

**The idea the word "inheritance" carries is this:** the property changes hands but **itself remains.** The owner goes, the possession stays. The garden is still there, the spring is still flowing — the only thing that changes is whose it is.

**And this prepares the logic of the next verse (29):** if the garden is still standing and only its owner has gone, it becomes possible to ask whether anything at all was lessened by his going. **The twenty-ninth verse will answer exactly this question.**

**قَوْمًا ءَاخَرِينَ — "another people".** Who they are is not said.

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the text does not name the heir. **The stress lies not on the heir but on the fact that the inheritance has changed hands.**

## 44/29 — فَمَا بَكَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلسَّمَآءُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُنظَرِينَ

*Fa-mā bakat ʿalayhimu's-samāʾu wa'l-arḍu wa mā kānū munẓarīn*

"Neither the heaven nor the earth wept for them; nor were they granted respite."

**This verse is one of the most discussed expressions in the sūra and deserves detailed treatment.**

### بَكَتْ — the root ب-ك-ي

**The root occurred in `053-necm.md`** (*wa annahu huwa aḍḥaka wa abkā* — "it is He who makes laugh and makes weep").

**The concrete sense: the coming of tears from the eye; and the lexicographers record two branches of the root** — weeping aloud (*bukāʾ*, when read with a long vowel) and silent tears (*bukan*). **In the Qurʾānic usages the distinction does not always operate.**

### The subjects of the verb: heaven and earth

**A human act is given to inanimate things. In Arabic rhetoric this is called *tashkhīṣ* (personification).**

**This concept was treated in `050-kaf.md` and this verse was already cited there:**

> *"**In Arabic rhetoric this is called *tashkhīṣ* (giving personality to what is inanimate)** and it is used in several places in the Qurʾān: the weeping of heaven and earth (al-Dukhān 44/29), the heavens' and the earth's saying 'we come willingly' (Fuṣṣilat 41/11), the speaking of the skins (Fuṣṣilat 41/21)."*

**I rely on that treatment and open out the particular case here.**

### The convention in Arab poetry

**The background most often appealed to in understanding this verse is a convention of hyperbole in Arab poetry.**

**It is transmitted that when a great man died the Arabs, in lamenting him, used expressions such as *"the sky wept for him", "the earth wept for him", "the stars darkened", "the wind stood still after him"*.** This was the poetic way of saying that the person's loss was felt **on a universal scale**.

**I transmit this convention without making a claim:** I transmit no particular poem or line, because to ascribe a text whose wording I am not sure of is contrary to the rules of this commentary. **What I record is that the classical works of exegesis and rhetoric transmit that this usage was known among the Arabs.**

**And if this background is correct, the work of the verse is this:** the Qurʾān takes a **formula of praise** its addressee knows, and **negates** it.

**The sentence, that is, does not give a piece of information — it leaves an *expectation* unanswered.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the form of the negation.** It may be seen thus: to say of a person "the sky did not weep for him" gains its sense only if there is a formula "the sky weeps for him". **An absence carries news only if it is the absence of something expected.**

### The disagreement over what is meant

**The exegetes differ over what the expression conveys, and I do not impose a preference:**

| View | What it says | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| **Figurative — they had no worth at all** | "No one mourned them; their loss diminished nothing" | The negating of the hyperbolic formula of Arab poetry; and the conveying of worthlessness by way of *kināya* |
| **Literal — heaven and earth really weep** | That heaven and earth weep when a believer dies, and do not weep for a disbeliever | This view is based on transmitted material; **I issue no ruling on the soundness of the reports** |
| **The place his deeds went up from and the place of his prostration** | The believer's deeds that ascend to heaven and his place of prayer on earth weep; and they had none of these | It is an explanation transmitted in the classical commentaries |

**All three views are transmitted. I declare no preference in this commentary.**

**But a datum of language can be recorded, and it is not a preference:** the verb of the verse is **negative.** The text, that is, does not say **for whom** heaven and earth weep. The only thing it says is that they did **not** weep for these. What the positive side is cannot be drawn from this verse — everything drawn comes from outside it.

### The work of the verse within the sūra — the change of measure

**Now let us look at the link of the verse with verses twenty-five to twenty-eight, because its real work shows there.**

**From the twenty-fifth to the twenty-eighth verse there was a list: gardens, springs, crops, stations, comfort — and all of it passed to someone else.**

**The twenty-ninth verse appears to add one more item to that list; but what it adds is not a possession, it is a *response*.**

| What | Verse | The loss |
|---|---|---|
| Gardens, springs, crops | 25-26 | **Property** |
| Station | 26 | **Standing** |
| Comfort | 27 | **A way of life** |
| **Mourning** | **29** | **The trace left behind** |

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the verses:** the list starts from what is possessed and moves towards **what is left behind.** And the last item measures all the preceding ones again.

**For this question may be asked:** if a man possessed so much, must he not have left a trace? **The verse answers it: there is no record.**

**And this touches man's most widespread consolation — I give it as my own reading:** a man hopes that what he possesses will carry a **meaning** after him as well; being remembered, being grieved for, leaving a gap. **After telling of the changing of hands of property, the verse looks at this hope too.**

### وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُنظَرِينَ — the root ن-ظ-ر

**The root was treated in many sections** (`002-bakara.md`, `050-kaf.md`, `080-abese.md`, `083-mutaffifin.md`); its concrete sense is **to look**.

**But here it is in Form IV and the sense changes: *anẓara* — to grant a respite, to give time.** *Munẓar* — one to whom a respite is granted.

**The link between the two senses is made by the lexicographers thus:** to grant a respite is to **wait** and to **watch** — to keep an eye on someone until he finishes his business.

**And now we come to the point within the sūra.**

**In the tenth verse the sūra had said *irtaqib* (watch) to its addressee — a command to wait.** In the twenty-ninth verse it is said of the house of Pharaoh *mā kānū munẓarīn* — **no waiting time was granted them.**

| Verse | Word | Root | To whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10, 59** | *irtaqib / murtaqibūn* | **ر-ق-ب** | **To the addressee: wait** |
| **29** | *munẓarīn* | **ن-ظ-ر** | **To them: no time** |

**Both roots lie in the field of waiting, and they work in two separate directions:** the one describes the man **who waits**, the other the man **who is not made to wait.**

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording and the meaning of the two roots are verifiable, but to establish a relation between them is my interpretation.

**And the pattern of the sentence: *mā kānū munẓarīn* — *kāna* + a passive participle, negated.** The same pattern as *innā kunnā mundhirīn* in the third verse. **The sūra gave the attribute of warning with *kāna*; it gives the withholding of respite with the same pattern.**

## 44/30-31 — وَلَقَدْ نَجَّيْنَا بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ مِنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْمُهِينِ · مِن فِرْعَوْنَ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ عَالِيًا مِّنَ ٱلْمُسْرِفِينَ

*Wa laqad najjaynā banī isrāʾīla mina'l-ʿadhābi'l-muhīn · Min firʿawna, innahu kāna ʿāliyan mina'l-musrifīn*

"And We delivered the Children of Israel from the abasing punishment — from Pharaoh. He had risen high, one of those who exceed all bounds."

### نَجَّيْنَا — the root ن-ج-و

**The root occurred in `054-kamer.md`, `061-saff.md` and `070-mearic.md`.** Its concrete sense is explained by the lexicographers by way of **"high ground, a hill"**: *najwa* — high ground the flood does not reach. From this *najāt* — **deliverance**: to climb where the water cannot come.

**And the verb is in Form II: *najjā*.** Its difference from Form I (*najā* — he was saved) is **transitivity** and **intensification**: to deliver a whole community, one by one.

### ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْمُهِينِ — the choice of the attribute

**Root: ه-و-ن.** It occurred in `089-fecr.md` and `058-mucadele.md`. Its concrete sense: **lightness, worthlessness.** *Hawn* — softness, ease; *huwān* — degradation. *Muhīn* — the active participle of Form IV: **that which lowers, that which abases.**

**And now let us look at the contrast within the sūra:**

| Verse | Construct | What it looks to |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | *ʿadhābun **alīm*** | **Pain** — the body |
| **30** | *al-ʿadhābi'l-**muhīn*** | **Abasement** — honour |

**The sūra names the punishment with two separate attributes, and the two show two separate kinds of wounding.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** what the Children of Israel were delivered from is named not as **pain** but as **humiliation**. And this is the truest description of the condition of slavery — pain comes and goes, humiliation is a **position**.

**And the principle recorded in `050-kaf.md` should be recalled here too:** what an attribute says depends on what the one who bears it does with that attribute.

### مِن فِرْعَوْنَ — and the finest point of word order

**These two words stand in apposition to *mina'l-ʿadhābi'l-muhīn* in the preceding verse.**

**The sentence, that is, is built thus: "We delivered them from the punishment — from Pharaoh."**

**And this is not a repetition but a *definition*.**

**In Arabic an appositive explains what precedes it.** What is explained here is **the punishment itself**, and what is given as the explanation is a **person**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the case-ending of the two verses:** the verse sets Pharaoh not as the **cause** of the punishment but as the punishment **itself**. The expected expression would be *"from Pharaoh's punishment"* (a genitive); the expression that comes is an appositive that puts Pharaoh in the place of the punishment.

**A human being is named as a punishment.**

### إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ عَالِيًا — the counterpart of the nineteenth verse

**It was recorded above (at verse 19):** *lā taʿlū ʿala'llāh* (the warning) and *kāna ʿāliyan* (the verdict) are from the same root.

**What is to be added here is the pattern: *kāna* + an active participle.** At the third verse it was recorded that this pattern reports **an ongoing attribute**. **Not "he exalted himself once", then, but "he was one who rises".**

**And note: *ʿāliyan* comes indefinite, and *mina'l-musrifīn* follows it.** The sentence, that is, places him within a class: **one of those who exceed all bounds.**

**I give this as my own reading:** the verse does not present Pharaoh as an example of a unique evil; **it presents him as an example of a kind.** And the principle recorded in `USLUP.md` works here: what the verse describes is an **attribute**, and whoever bears that attribute is included in it.

### مُّسْرِفِين — the root س-ر-ف

**The root occurred in `051-zariyat.md`.** Its concrete sense: **to exceed the bound, to lose the measure, to pass the limit in a thing.** *Isrāf* — exceeding the limit in spending is only one instance; the root itself covers **every kind of exceeding of a limit.**

**And the standing together of the two words builds a description:**

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| ***ʿĀliyan*** | ع-ل-و | Overflowing **upward** — position |
| ***Musrifīn*** | س-ر-ف | Overflowing **sideways** — measure |

**Both are words of overflowing, and they work on two separate axes.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the definitions of the two roots:** the verse describes a tyrant by two overflowings — rising above his own place and exceeding his own measure.

## 44/32-33 — وَلَقَدِ ٱخْتَرْنَٰهُمْ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ عَلَى ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · وَءَاتَيْنَٰهُم مِّنَ ٱلْءَايَٰتِ مَا فِيهِ بَلَٰٓؤٌا۟ مُّبِينٌ

*Wa laqadi'khtarnāhum ʿalā ʿilmin ʿala'l-ʿālamīn · Wa ātaynāhum mina'l-āyāti mā fīhi balāʾun mubīn*

"And We chose them, upon a knowledge, above all beings. And We gave them of the signs that in which there is a manifest trial."

### ٱخْتَرْنَا — the root خ-ي-ر

**The root occurred in `056-vakia.md`, `068-kalem.md`, `055-rahman.md` and `062-cuma.md`.** Its concrete sense: **good, benefit.** And Form VIII, *ikhtāra* — **to choose**: that is, "to take the better".

**The logic of the root is worth recording:** in Arabic to choose is not to make a preference but **to set apart what is good**. The word carries a judgement of value within itself.

**And it has a counterpart within the sūra — this is a verifiable datum:**

| Verse | Word | About whom |
|---|---|---|
| **32** | *i**khtar**nāhum* | **The Children of Israel** — they were chosen |
| **37** | *a-hum **khayr**un am qawmu tubbaʿ* | **The Meccans** — "are they better?" |

**The same root, five verses apart, for two communities.** The one reports a being chosen, the other asks a question.

**I present this as my own reading;** the shared root of the two verses is verifiable, but that they form a pattern is my interpretation.

### عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ — a knowledge left indefinite

**The phrase is indefinite: "upon a knowledge".**

**And whose knowledge it is is not said.** This is a pattern repeated across the sūras and has been treated twice before:

| Phrase | Where | `File` |
|---|---|---|
| *yawmin maʿlūm* | al-Wāqiʿa 56/50 | `056-vakia.md` |
| *ajalin musammā* | al-Aḥqāf 46/3 | `046-ahkaf.md` |
| ***ʿalā ʿilmin*** | **al-Dukhān 44/32** | — |

**The principle recorded in `046-ahkaf.md` works here too:**

> *"Not indefinite, but closed to man."*

**And the work of the phrase is this:** the choice is not arbitrary — it rests on a knowledge. **But what that knowledge is is not said.** The verse, that is, both says something about the ground of the choice (there is a ground) and does not say it (what it is is not reported).

### عَلَى ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ — the disagreement over the scope

**The exegetes differ over the scope of this phrase:**

| View | What it says |
|---|---|
| **The people of their own age** | The pre-eminence is limited to that period |
| **Pre-eminence in particular respects** | In particular matters, such as the number of prophets and the giving of a book |
| **A general pre-eminence** | The phrase is unqualified |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But I am bound to enter two records, and both are required by `USLUP.md`.**

**One. In this commentary no wholesale ruling is issued about a community.** What the verse describes is a **situation**: something was given to a community. What was given is said in the next verse, and that word is decisive: ***balāʾ*** — a trial.

**Two. The Qurʾān does not leave this expression standing alone.** The same pattern occurs at al-Baqara 2/47 and 2/122 (*wa annī faḍḍaltukum ʿala'l-ʿālamīn*), and there too an obligation follows it. **This context was treated in `002-bakara.md`; I rely on that treatment.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses:** the thirty-second verse states the pre-eminence, the thirty-third the trial. **There is nothing between them.** The pre-eminence, that is, is presented not as a privilege whose outcome is settled but as **an examination whose outcome is open.**

### مَا فِيهِ بَلَٰٓؤٌا۟ مُّبِينٌ — the fifth *mubīn*

**Root: ب-ل-و.** Its concrete sense: **to test, to try** — and the lexicographers record the root's second branch as well: **to wear out, to wear away** (*bilā* — wearing out). **The link is this:** to test a thing is to use it until it wears.

**And the most important feature of the word is this, and the Qurʾān says it openly:** a *balāʾ* comes both **with good** and **with evil**. *"We try you with evil and with good, as a testing"* (al-Anbiyāʾ 21/35).

**The usual English rendering, "affliction", is therefore not the same thing as the Arabic *balāʾ*:** the English word carries only misfortune, while the Arabic covers both sides.

**And this is decisive for understanding the verse:** what is given is **signs** — that is, proofs, marks, miracles. **And within those signs there is a *balāʾ*.**

**I record this as my own reading:** a proof is not by itself a favour. **A proof is at the same time a burden** — because the responsibility of one who has seen the proof is heavier than that of one who has not.

**And this reading completes the frame of the block.** In the seventeenth verse *fatannā* (We tried) was said of the people of Pharaoh; in the thirty-third *balāʾun mubīn* is said of the Children of Israel.

| Verse | Word | Who | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **17** | *fatannā* (ف-ت-ن) | **The people of Pharaoh** | The block opens |
| **33** | *balāʾun mubīn* (ب-ل-و) | **The Children of Israel** | The block closes |

**At the two ends of the block stand two words for trial, and the two belong to two separate sides.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** the sūra does not close the story as a tale of **taking sides**. **The party that is delivered is named with a trial too.**

**And *mubīn* occurs here for the fifth and last time.** The table drawn up at the second verse of the sūra is completed: the Book, the smoke, the messenger, the proof, the trial — **all five "manifest".**

## 44/34-36 — إِنَّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ لَيَقُولُونَ · إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّا مَوْتَتُنَا ٱلْأُولَىٰ وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمُنشَرِينَ · فَأْتُوا۟ بِـَٔابَآئِنَآ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ

*Inna hāʾulāʾi la-yaqūlūn · In hiya illā mawtatuna'l-ūlā wa mā naḥnu bi-munsharīn · Faʾtū bi-ābāʾinā in kuntum ṣādiqīn*

"These people do indeed say: 'There is nothing but our first death, and we are not to be raised up. Bring back our fathers, if you speak the truth.'"

### إِنَّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ — the return from the story to the present

**The opening shared with the twenty-second verse was recorded above.** What is to be added here is the abruptness of the transition.

**The thirty-third verse ends with the signs given to the Children of Israel; the thirty-fourth turns back to Mecca with no connective at all.** There is no "thus", no "but" — only *inna* and a demonstrative.

**I record this as an observation about word order:** a story of seventeen verses closes without a sentence of transition. **And the place of the closing is held by a demonstrative:** *hāʾulāʾi* — "these", that is, the ones standing before you now.

**لَيَقُولُونَ — reinforced with the *lām al-muzaḥlaqa*.** As recorded in `085-buruc.md`, the pairing of *inna* + *lām* is the highest degree of certainty in Arabic and is used **against a denying addressee**.

**And there is something strange here — I give it as my own reading:** what is reinforced is not a truth but **the fact that the other party says something.** The verse says, that is, "they really do say it". **This draws attention not to the words themselves but to their having been said** — because what will be seen shortly is that the words are inconsistent within themselves.

### إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّا — the pattern of restriction

***In* here is a particle of negation (*in nāfiya*), and together with *illā* it builds restriction (*ḥaṣr*): "there is nothing but…".**

**And the referent of the pronoun *hiya* is not stated.** The exegetes fill it differently:

| View | What *hiya* refers to |
|---|---|
| ***al-Mawta*** | Death: "Death is only our first death" |
| ***al-Ḥayāt*** | Life: "Life is only this life, and then comes the first death" |
| **An indefinite "matter"** | "The matter is only this…" |

**I do not impose a preference.** In all three the result is the same: **one death, and nothing after.**

### مَوْتَتُنَا ٱلْأُولَىٰ — and the fifty-sixth verse

**The word is in the form *mawta* — not *mawt*.**

**In Arabic the final *tāʾ* conveys *marra* (a single instance):** *ḍarb* to strike, *ḍarba* a single blow; *mawt* death, *mawta* **one dying.**

**The sentence, that is, does not say "there is nothing but our death"; it says "there is nothing but our dying once".** The number is buried in the pattern of the word.

**And now we come to the most striking repetition of the sūra.**

**The same phrase returns in the fifty-sixth verse — this time about the muttaqīn:**

| Verse | Expression | Who says it | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **35** | *in hiya illā **mawtatuna'l-ūlā*** | **Those who deny** | "There is one death, that is all" |
| **56** | *lā yadhūqūna fīha'l-mawta illā **al-mawtata'l-ūlā*** | **The text** | "They tasted one death, that is all" |

**Both verses have the same phrase and the same exceptive pattern (*illā*).**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** the sentence of those who deny turns out **true** for the muttaqīn — but somewhere else. They said "there is only one death, and nothing after"; for the muttaqīn too there will be only one death, **but there is something after.**

**The sūra, that is, neither takes up nor discards the wording of the objection; it changes its *place*.**

In `050-kaf.md` the same technique was recorded for the word *baʿīd*: *"The sūra takes the word the objector chooses and spreads it over the whole text. And each time it turns its direction."* **al-Dukhān does the same thing for a phrase.**

### وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمُنشَرِينَ — the root ن-ش-ر

**The root occurred in `077-murselat.md`, `081-tekvir.md`, `074-muddessir.md` and `067-mulk.md`.** Its concrete sense: **to open out something folded, to spread.** *Nashara'th-thawb* — he unfolded the cloth. *Nashr* — a spreading; *muntashir* — spread out.

**And the root's use for the raising of the dead follows from the logic of the root itself — this is worth recording:**

| State | The root's counterpart |
|---|---|
| **Death** | **Being folded** — closing, gathering in |
| **The raising** | ***Nashr*** — opening out, spreading |

**The raising, that is, is named not as a re-creating but as an *unfolding*.** The opening of a rolled-up scroll.

**And the form of the negation is decisive: *mā naḥnu bi-munsharīn*. The بِ at its head is a particle of emphasis; the same structure was recorded at `050-kaf.md` 50/45 (*mā anta… bi-jabbār*). Not "we shall not be raised", then, but "we are certainly not to be raised".**

### فَأْتُوا۟ بِـَٔابَآئِنَآ — the form of the demand

**And now we come to the most instructive place in the verse.**

**The other party asks for a proof. And the form of the proof they ask for must be recorded: a *specimen*.**

*"Bring back our fathers."* That is: show us a raising, and we will believe.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the logic of the demand:** the demand is logically inconsistent. **Because what is asked for removes what the raising itself is for.**

It may be seen thus: the raising, as described in the fourth and fortieth verses of the sūra, is a day of **separating** — the day on which the reckoning is held. **To raise one person by way of a specimen is not to bring that day forward but to turn it into a display.**

**And note: the verse does not *answer* this demand.** The thirty-seventh verse asks something else.

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the demand is left unanswered and a question is put in its place. **Instead of giving the proof asked for, the text calls the addressee to look somewhere else** — and the place to look is again the dead, but not those who have come, **those who have gone.**

**إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ — "if you speak the truth".** This pattern occurs often in the Qurʾān from the mouth of the other party, and its reverse was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/4: there the same pattern came **from the mouth of the text** (*iʾtūnī bi-kitābin… in kuntum ṣādiqīn*).

**Setting the two uses side by side is instructive:**

| Who asks | What is asked | `File` |
|---|---|---|
| **The text** (al-Aḥqāf 46/4) | **A book or a remnant of knowledge** — a transmission or an observation | `046-ahkaf.md` |
| **The other party** (al-Dukhān 44/36) | **The bringing back of the dead** — an extraordinary display | — |

**The threshold of the two demands differs.** As recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`, what was asked there was "not even a whole book — a remnant". **What is asked here is the reversing of the working of nature.**

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording of the two verses is verifiable, but the comparison is mine.

## 44/37 — أَهُمْ خَيْرٌ أَمْ قَوْمُ تُبَّعٍ وَٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ ۚ أَهْلَكْنَٰهُمْ ۖ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ مُجْرِمِينَ

*A-hum khayrun am qawmu tubbaʿin wa'lladhīna min qablihim, ahlaknāhum, innahum kānū mujrimīn*

"Are they better, or the people of Tubbaʿ and those before them? We destroyed them; they were guilty."

### أَهُمْ خَيْرٌ أَمْ — the form of the question

**The question is in the pattern *a … am*: which of two things.** And the word *khayr* is here an elative (the shortened form of *akhyar*): **better, superior.**

**Exactly the same pattern was treated in `054-kamer.md`:**

> *"The forty-third verse turns directly to the addressee: *a-kuffārukum khayrun min ulāʾikum?*"*

**The question in al-Qamar was built with *min* (the particle of comparison), the one in al-Dukhān with *am* (the particle of alternatives).** The difference is small; both do the same work.

**And the logic of the question must be recorded — this is a repeated method of the Qurʾān:**

The other party had advanced a claim of **impossibility** (35: we shall not be raised). **The verse answers it not with an argument about possibility but with a *parallel*:** there were those stronger than you; what became of them?

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses:** the demand was "bring back the dead"; the answer is "look at those who have gone". **The kind of proof asked for, that is, is not changed — what is asked for is a dead man too — but its direction is turned.**

**A note is also needed on the sense of the word *khayr* here:** the word can report moral superiority and can report superiority of strength and means. **The context is closer to the second** — because what is compared is a people that was destroyed. **But this is an observation, not a preference.**

**And its sharing of a root with the thirty-second verse was recorded above:** *ikhtarnāhum* (We chose) and *khayrun* (better) are from the same root.

### قَوْمُ تُبَّعٍ — the people of Tubbaʿ

**A record was entered about this people in `050-kaf.md` and I keep that record exactly. The finding there was this:**

> *"**تُبَّع** is not a proper name but a **title.** In the historical records it is known as the title borne by the Ḥimyarite rulers who held sway in South Arabia (Yemen). The word is from the root **ت-ب-ع**: to follow, to come after — that is, 'the one who is followed'."*
>
> *"**The Qurʾān names this community in two places** (Qāf 50/14 and here) and **gives no detail in either.**"*
>
> *"**Who Tubbaʿ was, which Tubbaʿ he was, and how his people were destroyed are not reported.** In the works of exegesis and history there are accounts of rulers bearing the title Tubbaʿ; some of these carry favourable judgements about Tubbaʿ himself and unfavourable ones about his people. **Since I cannot vouch for the soundness of these accounts I do not transmit them.**"*

**I keep the same care here: there is no certain knowledge, and I do not produce any.**

**I add here two data of wording not present in `050-kaf.md`, and both are read off the text.**

**One. The context of the two occurrences differs:**

| Sūra | Expression | How it is named |
|---|---|---|
| **Qāf 50/14** | *wa aṣḥābu'l-aykati wa qawmu **tubbaʿ*** | **Inside a list** — one of eight communities |
| **al-Dukhān 44/37** | *am qawmu **tubbaʿ** wa'lladhīna min qablihim* | **As a measure of comparison** — named on its own |

**In Qāf the people are one of a series of communities. In al-Dukhān they are used as a *measure*** — Mecca is compared with them.

**Two. al-Dukhān does not leave the people standing alone: *wa'lladhīna min qablihim* — "and those before them".**

**I record this as my own reading:** the comparison is made not with one people but with **an open-ended chain**. Not "are you better than that people", that is, but **"than that people and all those before them"**.

**And this makes the answer to the question unnecessary.** Since the chain is open backwards, the comparison cannot be closed.

### أَهْلَكْنَٰهُمْ — the answer not given

**The question was asked and not answered.** In its place came a verb: *ahlaknāhum* — "We destroyed them".

**In Arabic this may be called a move close to *uslūbu'l-ḥakīm*:** instead of answering the question, to give a piece of information that makes the answer unnecessary.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word order of the sentence:** it is not said "they were better" or "you are better". **What is said is this: even if they were better, they were destroyed.** Whatever the result of the comparison, that is, the outcome does not change.

**And the sentence is unconnected (*istiʾnāf*):** *ahlaknāhum* comes with no particle between. **This makes the sentence read not as an answer but as a verdict.**

### إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ مُجْرِمِينَ — the setting down of the ground

**And the last clause gives the ground of the destruction: because they were guilty.**

**The pattern is again *kāna* + an active participle: an ongoing attribute.**

**And the chain with the twenty-second verse closes:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **22** | *inna hāʾulāʾi qawmun **mujrimūn*** | **The people of Pharaoh** — from Moses' mouth |
| **37** | *innahum kānū **mujrimīn*** | **The people of Tubbaʿ and those before** — the text's verdict |

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root of the two verses:** nowhere does the sūra tie destruction to a difference of strength. **In both places the ground is given with the same word: guilt.**

**And this becomes an indirect answer to the question of the thirty-seventh verse:** a question of superiority was asked, and the answer is given **on another axis.** The measure is not *khayr* (superiority) but ***jurm*** (guilt).

## 44/38-39 — وَمَا خَلَقْنَا ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا لَٰعِبِينَ · مَا خَلَقْنَٰهُمَآ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*Wa mā khalaqna's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā lāʿibīn · Mā khalaqnāhumā illā bi'l-ḥaqqi wa lākinna aktharahum lā yaʿlamūn*

"We did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them in play. We created them only with the truth; but most of them do not know."

### The division of labour between the two verses: first the denial, then the affirmation

**The pair of verses builds the same sentence twice — once negatively, once with an exception.**

| Verse | Structure | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **38** | *mā khalaqnā … **lāʿibīn*** | **What it is not:** not play |
| **39** | *mā khalaqnāhumā **illā bi'l-ḥaqq*** | **What it is:** with the truth |

**And at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/3 these two clauses were given in *a single verse* and with another word:**

> *"We created the heavens and the earth and what is between them only **with the truth and for an appointed term**."*

**Let us set the two sūras side by side — this is a verifiable comparison of wording:**

| | **al-Aḥqāf 46/3** | **al-Dukhān 44/38-39** |
|---|---|---|
| The shared part | *mā khalaqna's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā* | *mā khalaqna's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā* |
| What is affirmed | *illā **bi'l-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā*** | *illā **bi'l-ḥaqq*** |
| What is denied | **None** | ***lāʿibīn*** |
| How many verses | **One** | **Two** |

**In al-Aḥqāf two qualifications were placed on the creating: *ḥaqq* and *term*. The table in `046-ahkaf.md` was this:**

| Qualification | What it conveys |
|---|---|
| **بِٱلْحَقّ** | **Not in vain** — upon a reality and a purpose |
| **أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى** | **Not endless** — with a named term |

**al-Dukhān does not say the second qualification in this verse — but it says it one verse later: *inna yawma'l-faṣli mīqātuhum* (40).** The idea of *ajal*, that is, has been moved one verse back in al-Dukhān and given **by the name of a day**.

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording of the two sūras is verifiable, but the interpretation of the division of labour is mine.

**The same sentence occurs at al-Anbiyāʾ 21/16 as well** (*wa mā khalaqna's-samāʾa wa'l-arḍa wa mā baynahumā lāʿibīn*) — almost identical with the thirty-eighth verse of al-Dukhān. **I give this as a record of wording.**

### لَٰعِبِينَ — a circumstantial accusative, and what is denied

**The word is in the accusative and is a *ḥāl*: "as ones playing".**

**And this is the finest point in the verse — I give it as my own reading.**

**The sentence does not say "We did not create".** *Mā khalaqnā … lāʿibīn* — what is negated is **not the verb but the state of the verb.**

| What is negated | What the sentence would say |
|---|---|
| **The verb** | "We did not create" |
| **The state (here)** | "We did not create **as play**" |

**The fact of creating, that is, is accepted; the character loaded onto it is refused.**

**And the name of the refused character is the very verb given to the other party in the ninth verse.**

### The two occurrences of the root ل-ع-ب in the sūra

**It was recorded above (at verse 9) and is completed here:**

| Verse | Expression | Subject | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| **9** | *fī shakkin **yalʿabūn*** | **They** | **Imperfect** — ongoing |
| **38** | *wa mā khalaqnā… **lāʿibīn*** | **We — negated** | **Active participle** — a state |

**In `052-tur.md` the definition of the root was given:** *"Play is seriousness with no outcome: the player spends real effort, but what he spends has no return outside the game."*

**And this definition explains both verses at once — I give it as my own reading:**

- **In the ninth verse** the other party is at play: they doubt, they argue, they object — **but they do not take the outcome of it seriously.**
- **In the thirty-eighth verse** the same character is lifted from the creating: **the universe is not an effort without an outcome.**

**And the two verses together build a thesis:** if the universe is not play, then what is done within the universe cannot be play either. **The charge of play changes sides.**

**This reading is mine;** the shared root and the opposed subjects of the two verses are verifiable data of the text.

### مَا خَلَقْنَٰهُمَآ — the point of the dual pronoun

**A detail of word order must be recorded.**

**Three things were enumerated in the thirty-eighth verse: the heavens (plural), the earth (singular), and what is between them.** In the thirty-ninth a dual pronoun comes: ***khalaqnāhumā*** — "the two of them".

**The plural *samāwāt* and the singular *arḍ*, that is, are gathered up as a single pair.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the second sentence reduces what the first enumerated **to two items**. This shortens the sentence and shifts the focus from number to relation: **what is above and what is below.**

### بِٱلْحَقّ — the root ح-ق-ق

**The root was treated in `050-kaf.md` as a chain within that sūra; and in `046-ahkaf.md` this phrase was analysed.** Its concrete sense: **that which is in its right place, fixed, not falling.**

**And the particle must be recorded: *bi'l-ḥaqq* — "*with* the truth".** The *bāʾ* here conveys **accompaniment** (*mulābasa*, being clothed in a thing) or **cause**.

**Both readings are transmitted:**

| The work of the *bāʾ* | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Accompaniment** | The creating **is interwoven with truth** — it cannot be parted from reality |
| **Cause / purpose** | The creating is **for a purpose** |

**I do not impose a preference.**

### وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ — the note about the majority

**The sentence ends with a corrective (*istidrāk*): *wa lākinna*.**

**And its object must be recorded: *aktharahum* — "most of them".** Not all.

**I record this as an observation about word order:** in sentences of this kind the Qurʾān mostly does not build an absolute ruling. **And *lā yaʿlamūn* — "they do not know" — is not an accusation but a report of a lack.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the verse names the source of the objection not as **ill will** but as **ignorance**. And this separates the thirty-ninth verse from the thirty-seventh — there the ground was *jurm* (guilt), here *jahl* (not knowing).

## 44/40-42 — إِنَّ يَوْمَ ٱلْفَصْلِ مِيقَٰتُهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ · يَوْمَ لَا يُغْنِى مَوْلًى عَن مَّوْلًى شَيْـًٔا وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · إِلَّا مَن رَّحِمَ ٱللَّهُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

*Inna yawma'l-faṣli mīqātuhum ajmaʿīn · Yawma lā yughnī mawlan ʿan mawlan shayʾan wa lā hum yunṣarūn · Illā man raḥima'llāh, innahu huwa'l-ʿAzīzu'r-Raḥīm*

"The day of separation is the appointed time of them all. A day when no protector will avail a protector anything, and they will not be helped. Save him on whom God has mercy. He is indeed the Mighty, the Merciful."

### يَوْمَ ٱلْفَصْلِ — and the chain with the fourth verse

**This link was recorded above (at verse 4) and is completed here.**

| Verse | Root | Expression | What is separated | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | **ف-ر-ق** | *yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm* | **Affairs** | **On one night** |
| **40** | **ف-ص-ل** | *yawmu'l-faṣl* | **Persons** | **On one day** |

**In `077-murselat.md` the difference between the two roots was given in a table, and I do not repeat it.** The summary: *farq* **opens a space between**, *faṣl* **gives a decisive verdict.**

**And al-Mursalāt and al-Dukhān make the same move: to build the oath or the opening with ف-ر-ق and to name the day with ف-ص-ل.**

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording of the two sūras is verifiable.

**And I record one further observation:** the separating in the fourth verse is a **night**; the separating in the fortieth is named a **day**. **The sūra places the two separations in two separate divisions of time.**

### مِيقَٰت — the root و-ق-ت

**The root occurred in `056-vakia.md`, `077-murselat.md` and `078-nebe.md`.** *Waqt* — an appointed time. *Mīqāt* is on the **mifʿāl** pattern: the pattern that forms nouns of instrument or of place and time.

**The word, that is, is not "a time" but "an appointed meeting point".**

**And another use of the word in the Qurʾān is worth recording:** the term set for Moses' meeting with his Lord is also called a *mīqāt* (al-Aʿrāf 7/142, 7/143).

**I give this as a record of wording:** the word reports **an appointment** — the meeting of two parties at a set time.

**أَجْمَعِينَ — emphasis.** "All together". The word reinforces the pronoun in *mīqātuhum*.

**And this reinforcement says something:** the meeting is not optional. **To those who say in the thirty-sixth verse "bring back our fathers", the fortieth speaks of a time of general assembly** — in place of the single display asked for, a time that includes everyone.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the two verses.**

### يَوْمَ لَا يُغْنِى مَوْلًى عَن مَّوْلًى — the word used twice

**Root: و-ل-ي.** It was treated in many sections (`002-bakara.md`, `060-mumtehine.md`, `057-hadid.md`); its concrete sense is **nearness, adjacency**. *Walī* — one who is near; *walā* — to come one after another.

**And the most striking side of the word is this: *mawlā* in Arabic covers opposite positions at once.**

**The usages the lexicographers enumerate:**

- **A master** — one who frees a slave.
- **A freed slave** — one who is freed.
- **A cousin, a close relative.**
- **A friend, a helper.**
- **A neighbour, an ally.**

**The word, that is, can name *both ends* of a relation.** The shared idea: **nearness**. Whoever is near to whom is that other's *mawlā*.

**And now we come to the point of the verse — I give it as my own reading.**

**The verse uses the same word twice, on both sides: *lā yughnī mawlan ʿan mawlan*.**

**The expected expression would have been two different words** — "a friend for a friend", "a master for a slave", "a kinsman for a kinsman". **But the verse names both sides with the same word.**

**Its effect is this:** the **direction** of the relation is left undefined. Who is above and who below, who protects and who is protected — it is not said. **And because it is not said, the sentence closes off every direction at once.**

**This reading is mine;** the word's multiplicity of sense and its occurring twice in the verse are verifiable data of the text.

**And its sharing of a root with the fourteenth verse must be recorded:** there *tawallaw ʿanhu* (they turned away from him) came from the same root. **The name of the relation they turned away from, that is, and the name of the relation that will not avail on that day are bound to the same root.** I give this as an observation about the wording.

### لَا يُغْنِى — the root غ-ن-ي

**The root was treated in many sections** (`092-leyl.md`, `069-hakka.md`, `093-duha.md`, `088-gasiye.md`). Its concrete sense: **to suffice, to leave no need.** *Ghinā* — wealth: the ceasing of need. *Mughnī* — that which suffices.

**And its negative use is settled in the Qurʾān: *lā yughnī* — "it is of no avail".**

***Shayʾan* — "anything".** Accusative and indefinite: an absolute negation. **Not partly, then, but not at all.**

### وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ — the second door

**The root ن-ص-ر: to help.** And the verb is **passive**: "they will not be helped" — by whom they will not be helped is not said.

**And the division of labour between the two clauses must be recorded — I give it as my own reading:**

| Clause | What is closed |
|---|---|
| *lā yughnī mawlan ʿan mawlan* | **Help from within** — the relations of nearness |
| *wa lā hum yunṣarūn* | **Help from outside** — from anywhere at all |

**The first clause closes the known relations, the second the unknown.** The result: all the channels of help.

**And at exactly this point an exception comes.**

### إِلَّا مَن رَّحِمَ ٱللَّهُ — the place of the exception

**The exegetes differ over the set from which the exception is drawn:**

| View | What the exception attaches to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| To ***mawlan*** | To the protectors who will be of no avail | "Except him on whom God has mercy — he can avail" (the context of intercession) |
| To ***hum*** | To those who will not be helped | "Except those on whom God has mercy — they will be helped" |
| **A disjunctive exception** | Not from the set at all | "But the mercy of God is another matter" — that is, mercy remains as the only channel |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**I record one observation, and it is not a preference:** in all three readings the result arrives at the same place — **after all the channels are closed, the one thing left open is not relations but mercy.**

### إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ — and the preparation of the forty-ninth verse

**The pattern repeated in four places in the sūra occurs here for the second time** (it occurred for the first time in the sixth verse).

**ٱلْعَزِيز — the root ع-ز-ز.** As recorded in `046-ahkaf.md`, the concrete core of the root is explained by the lexicographers by way of **"hard and impassable ground"**: *arḍ ʿazāz* — hard ground that cannot be dug. From this *ʿizza* — **imperviousness, invincibility.**

**And that the two names come together is worth recording:** one reports **unreachability**, the other **nearness**. A similar observation was made in `046-ahkaf.md` for *al-ʿAzīzu'l-Ḥakīm*: **power is not named on its own.**

**Now we come to the real record, and this is the most important preparation in the sūra.**

**In the forty-second verse the attribute *al-ʿAzīz* is given to God. Seven verses later, in the forty-ninth, the same attribute, in the same pattern, will be said of a human being — in mockery.**

| Verse | Expression | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| **42** | *inna**hu huwa**'l-**ʿAzīz**u'r-Raḥīm* | **God** |
| **49** | *inna**ka anta**'l-**ʿazīz**u'l-karīm* | **A human being** |

**The grammatical structure of the two sentences is exactly the same: *inna* + a pronoun + the pronoun of separation + *al-ʿazīz* + a second attribute with the definite article.**

**I record this as a verifiable datum within the sūra.** And what is interpretation is this, which I give as my own reading: **the mockery of the forty-ninth verse would not be so sharp without the forty-second.** The sūra first shows the real owner of the attribute, and then gives the same pattern to one who does not own it. **I shall take it up at the forty-ninth verse.**

## 44/43-44 — إِنَّ شَجَرَتَ ٱلزَّقُّومِ · طَعَامُ ٱلْأَثِيمِ

*Inna shajarata'z-zaqqūm · Ṭaʿāmu'l-athīm*

"The tree of zaqqūm is the food of the sinner."

### زَقُّوم — the word itself

**This word was treated in detail at `056-vakia.md` 56/52 — including the disagreement over its root. I do not repeat it.** The record there was this:

> **The lexicographers differ over its root, and the disagreement must not be concealed:**
>
> | View | Explanation |
> |---|---|
> | **An Arabic root** | *Tazaqqama* — to swallow a thing by force, with revulsion |
> | **The name of a known plant** | Some lexicographers transmit that a bitter and poisonous plant in Arabia was called by this name |
> | **Of foreign origin** | The view that the word entered Arabic from outside is also transmitted |
>
> **I issue no firm etymological ruling.**

**And in `056-vakia.md` the word's three occurrences in the Qurʾān were also recorded: al-Ṣāffāt 37/62, al-Dukhān 44/43 and al-Wāqiʿa 56/52.**

**Now I treat what is peculiar to this place: the difference between al-Wāqiʿa and al-Dukhān.**

### The difference between al-Wāqiʿa 56/52 and al-Dukhān 44/43-44

**The two sūras name the same tree and do two separate things. I give the difference in a table; the wording of the two texts is verifiable.**

| | **al-Wāqiʿa 56/51-56** | **al-Dukhān 44/43-46** |
|---|---|---|
| **Kind of sentence** | **A chain of verbal clauses:** *la-ākilūn → fa-māliʾūn → fa-shāribūn → fa-shāribūn* | **A nominal sentence:** *shajaratu'z-zaqqūmi **ṭaʿāmu'l-athīm*** |
| **What it gives** | **A scene** — an event flowing in four steps | **A definition** — what the tree is |
| **Definiteness** | *min shajarin min zaqqūm* — **indefinite**, "from a tree" | *shajarata'z-**zaqqūm*** — **definite**, in a genitive construction |
| **Who eats** | *aḍ-ḍāllūna'l-mukadhdhibūn* — **plural**, addressed directly (*innakum*) | *al-**athīm*** — **singular**, with the definite article |
| **What the tree does** | *fa-māliʾūna minha'l-**buṭūn*** — **it fills the bellies** | *ka'l-muhli **yaghlī** fi'l-buṭūn* — **it boils in the belly** |
| **The boiling water** | Something **drunk**: *fa-shāribūna ʿalayhi mina'l-ḥamīm* | **An element of comparison**: *ka-ghalyi'l-ḥamīm* |

**Three differences are worth recording, and I give all three as my own reading.**

**One — al-Wāqiʿa narrates an event, al-Dukhān gives a definition.** In al-Wāqiʿa the verbs were chained with *fāʾ*, and this was recorded in `056-vakia.md`: *"the scene flows in four steps, without a break."* **In al-Dukhān there is no verb at all — the tree is *defined* as someone's food.**

**Two — al-Wāqiʿa names the tree by its effect, not by its eater; al-Dukhān by its eater.** The sentence in al-Wāqiʿa is *"they will fill their bellies with it"*; **the sentence in al-Dukhān is *"it is the food of the sinner"*.** al-Dukhān, that is, gives the tree by way of a relation of **belonging**: **this food is that man's.**

**Three — and this is the most important difference: al-Dukhān speaks in the singular.** In al-Wāqiʿa the address was plural and direct (*innakum ayyuha'ḍ-ḍāllūn*). **In al-Dukhān there is a single person: *al-athīm*.** And this singularity will continue as far as the forty-ninth verse — there too a single person will be addressed: *dhuq innaka anta…*

**The scene of the Fire in al-Dukhān, that is, is not a scene of a crowd but a scene of a *person*.**

### شَجَرَتَ — a record on the spelling

**The word is written in the muṣḥaf with an open *tāʾ*: شجرت.** This is something seen in a few words in the orthography (*rasm*) of the muṣḥaf.

**I record this only as a datum of writing;** no meaning, reckoning or claim of a secret is drawn from it. The record entered in `002-bakara.md` holds.

### ٱلْأَثِيم — the root أ-ث-م

**The root occurred in `068-kalem.md`, `083-mutaffifin.md`, `076-insan.md` and `049-hucurat.md`.**

**Its concrete sense is given by the lexicographers thus: *ithm* — that which holds a man back from good, that which weighs him down. There is an idea of slowing, hindering in the root: *nāqatun āthima* — a camel that walks slowly. From this *ithm* — sin**: a burden that makes going to what is good heavy.

**And the pattern *athīm*: *faʿīl*, here conveying intensity — "deeply sunk in sin".**

**The word's other occurrences in the Qurʾān are worth recording, because it is always in the same company:**

- *"A hoarder of wealth, an aggressor, **a sinner**"* — at al-Qalam 68/12, *muʿallā mannāʿin li'l-khayri muʿtadin **athīm***.
- *"Every aggressor, **sinner**"* — at al-Muṭaffifīn 83/12, *kulli muʿtadin **athīm***.

**In both places the word occurs together with *muʿtadī* (an aggressor, one who exceeds the bound).**

**I record this as an observation about the wording:** in the Qurʾān the word occurs in a company that describes not merely "one who commits a sin" but one who has **made a habit of exceeding the bound**. **And the word *musrif* (one who exceeds the bound) used of Pharaoh in the thirty-first verse is in the same field.**

### The work of the definite article — a genus, or a particular person

***Al-athīm* has the definite article, and the article can be read in two ways:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| **The genus (*istighrāq*)** | "Whoever is a sinner" — a kind |
| **A particular person (*ʿahd*)** | Someone known in the context of the sūra's revelation |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**But I enter a record, and it is required by `USLUP.md`:** even on the second reading what the verse builds is an **attribute**. The principle recorded in `050-kaf.md` and throughout this commentary works here too: **what the verse describes is an attribute, and whoever bears that attribute is included in it.**

## 44/45-46 — كَٱلْمُهْلِ يَغْلِى فِى ٱلْبُطُونِ · كَغَلْىِ ٱلْحَمِيمِ

*Ka'l-muhli yaghlī fi'l-buṭūn · Ka-ghalyi'l-ḥamīm*

"Like molten metal it boils in the bellies — like the boiling of scalding water."

### The division of labour between the two comparisons

**Two *kāf*s (particles of comparison) come one after another, and each likens something different.** This deserves careful reading.

| Comparison | What it likens | How |
|---|---|---|
| ***ka'l-muhl*** | **The matter** — what the food itself resembles | *al-muhl* — molten metal |
| ***ka-ghalyi'l-ḥamīm*** | **The act** — what kind of boiling the boiling is | *ghalyu'l-ḥamīm* — the boiling of scalding water |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the two phrases:** the term of comparison in the first is a **noun** (*al-muhl*), in the second a **verbal noun** (*ghaly*). **The one says what it resembles, the other how it is.**

**And it has a consequence:** the matter and the movement are likened separately. The text does not give a single image; it builds a two-layered image.

### ٱلْمُهْل — the root م-ه-ل

**The root was treated at length in `070-mearic.md` and this verse was already cited there. I do not repeat it.** The summary of that record:

> *"**مَهْل** — to move slowly, not to hurry. *Muhla* (the granting of time), *imhāl*, *tamahhul* are of the same root. […] **The link between the two senses:** molten metal **flows slowly.** The flow of water is quick; the flow of molten metal is slow and heavy."*

**And in the closing record of `070-mearic.md` this was entered:**

> *"**What ٱلْمُهْل is** (molten metal / the dregs of oil) — the view of the majority of the lexicographers was transmitted; **no firm ruling was issued.**"*

**I keep the same record here.**

**What is peculiar to al-Dukhān is the word's *position*, and this is worth recording:**

| Sūra | What *muhl* is likened to | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Maʿārij 70/8** | **The sky** — *yawma takūnu's-samāʾu ka'l-muhl* | **Above** |
| **al-Kahf 18/29** | **The water to be drunk** — *bi-māʾin ka'l-muhl* | **Outside** |
| **al-Dukhān 44/45** | **The food** — *ka'l-muhli yaghlī fi'l-buṭūn* | **Inside** |

**The same substance, in three separate positions.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the three verses:** in al-Maʿārij *muhl* is the **spectacle** of a scene of the Hour; in al-Dukhān it is **inside** a person. And in `070-mearic.md` the root's sense of "flowing slowly" was recorded — **a thing's flowing slowly is, seen from outside, a sight; when it is inside, it is a duration.**

### يَغْلِى — the variant reading and the subject

**A difference of reading is transmitted concerning the subject of the verb:**

| Reading | Subject | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ***yaghlī*** (masculine) | *al-muhl* or *aṭ-ṭaʿām* | **The molten metal / the food** boils |
| ***taghlī*** (feminine) | *ash-shajara* | **The tree** boils |

**I issue no ruling about the readings.** The difference of meaning is small: what boils is either the tree itself or the food made from it.

### فِى ٱلْبُطُونِ — and a last comparison with al-Wāqiʿa

**It was recorded above and appears once more here: both sūras use the word *buṭūn* (bellies), but with two separate verbs.**

| Sūra | Verb | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Wāqiʿa 56/53** | *fa-māliʾūna minha'l-**buṭūn*** | **Inward** — being filled |
| **al-Dukhān 44/45** | *yaghlī fi'l-**buṭūn*** | **Inside** — boiling |

**The finding recorded in `056-vakia.md` should be recalled:** *"to be satisfied is a contentment; to fill is a matter of volume."*

**al-Dukhān takes one step further — I give it as my own reading:** in al-Wāqiʿa the food **goes in**; in al-Dukhān what happens **after it has gone in** is told. **The two sūras hold two moments of the same scene.**

### ٱلْحَمِيم — the root ح-م-م

**The root was treated in `069-hakka.md` and `070-mearic.md`; it was recorded in `056-vakia.md` as a chain within that sūra. I do not repeat it.** The summary: the word means both **"scalding water" and "a devoted friend"**; both issue from the idea of **heat** in the root.

**What is peculiar to al-Dukhān is the difference of work between its two occurrences:**

| Verse | Expression | The work of the word |
|---|---|---|
| **46** | *ka-**ghalyi'l-ḥamīm*** | **An element of comparison** — a measure |
| **48** | *min ʿadhābi'l-**ḥamīm*** | **The substance of the punishment** — what is poured |

**The same word, two verses apart, once a measure and once a substance.**

**I record this as a verifiable repetition within the sūra.**

## 44/47-48 — خُذُوهُ فَٱعْتِلُوهُ إِلَىٰ سَوَآءِ ٱلْجَحِيمِ · ثُمَّ صُبُّوا۟ فَوْقَ رَأْسِهِۦ مِنْ عَذَابِ ٱلْحَمِيمِ

*Khudhūhu fa'ʿtilūhu ilā sawāʾi'l-jaḥīm · Thumma ṣubbū fawqa raʾsihi min ʿadhābi'l-ḥamīm*

"'Seize him and drag him into the very middle of the blaze. Then pour over his head of the punishment of scalding water.'"

### The addressee of the commands is not stated

**There are three plural imperatives: *khudhū*, *iʿtilū*, *ṣubbū*.** And the addressee of none of them is specified.

**This verse was already cited in `069-hakka.md` and the finding there was this:**

> *"**And the addressee is not stated.** To whom is the command given? The verse does not say. This is a settled form for such scenes in the Qurʾān: *'Seize him and drag him into the middle of the blaze!'* (al-Dukhān 44/47). There too the addressee is nameless. The not naming of the addressee **depersonalises** the scene. The command is given not to a person but to a working."*

**I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

**What is to be added here is a contrast — I give it as my own reading:** the addressee of the commands is **nameless**, but the object of the commands is **singular and definite**: *khudhū**hu*** — "seize **him**". **There is a single person in the scene, and around him a nameless working.**

**And this prepares the forty-ninth verse: the nameless ones will not speak; what is spoken will be said to that single person.**

### فَٱعْتِلُوهُ — the root ع-ت-ل

**The root was treated in `068-kalem.md`** (in the discussion of the word *ʿutull*) **and this verse was already cited there:**

> *"**One who drags off.** The verb *ʿatala* means 'to seize a man by the collar and drag him'; the Qurʾān uses it: *'Seize him and **drag** him into the middle of the blaze'* (al-Dukhān 44/47 — *fa'ʿtilūhu*)."*

**And the conclusion recorded in al-Qalam looks directly to this place:**

> *"*ʿutull* is one who has brute strength enough to drag a man, and sees no objection to using it."*

**In al-Qalam, then, the word derived from this root was the attribute of a guilty man; in al-Dukhān the same root, as a verb, describes what is done to that man.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root of the two verses:** the principle recorded in `085-buruc.md` works once again — **the verdict is announced in the language of the offence.** The one who drags is dragged.

**And the particle *fāʾ*: *khudhūhu fa'ʿtilūhu*.** No interval. There is no gap between the seizing and the dragging.

### سَوَآءِ ٱلْجَحِيم — "the very middle"

**Root: س-و-ي.** Its concrete sense: **equality, equivalence.** *Sawāʾ* — the **middle** of a thing: the point equidistant from every side.

**The choice of word is worth recording.**

**The verse does not say "drag him into the blaze", it says "into the *sawāʾ* of the blaze".**

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the definition of the word:** the middle is the place that has **no edge**. A man at the edge of a thing is near the way out. For a man in the middle every direction is equal.

**The word, that is, reports not a distance but a *directionlessness*.**

**ٱلْجَحِيم — the root ج-ح-م.** Its concrete sense: **the flaring, the fierce heating of a fire.** *Jaḥīm* — a fiercely burning fire.

**And the word occurs twice in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **47** | *ilā sawāʾi'l-**jaḥīm*** | **The one dragged** — into it |
| **56** | *wa waqāhum ʿadhāba'l-**jaḥīm*** | **The muttaqīn** — those guarded |

**The same word: for one the place of arrival, for the other the thing guarded against.**

**I record this as a verifiable repetition within the sūra.**

### ثُمَّ صُبُّوا۟ — the root ص-ب-ب

**The root was treated in `089-fecr.md` and `080-abese.md`, and this verse was already cited in the al-Fajr discussion:**

> *"Another use in the Qurʾān shows the weight of the word: *'Then pour over his head of the punishment of scalding water'* (al-Dukhān 44/48). The same verb, in the same direction."*

**And the description recorded in al-Fajr should be carried over here:** *"A liquid is a thing that has no shape of its own and seeps everywhere. It is not stopped by a wall; it finds a gap."*

**And the particle *thumma*:** as recorded at `044/13-14`, it conveys a delayed sequence. **The pouring, then, does not come immediately after the dragging — there is an interval.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the first two verbs were joined with *fāʾ* (without interval), the third with *thumma* (with an interval). **The scene begins fast, then stops.**

### فَوْقَ رَأْسِهِ — the choosing of the head

**That the place of the pouring is specified as the *head* is worth recording.**

**The head is both the highest and the most protected place of the body.** The most protected organ of the human body is there; and a man's act of **setting himself high** is also described by way of the head — "to hold one's head up", "to be at the head".

**I give this as my own reading, and it is connected with the nineteenth verse:** the sūra had said *lā taʿlū* (do not rise). **In the forty-eighth what is poured is poured exactly upon the place that was set high.**

**It is I who make this link; the wording of the two verses is verifiable.**

### مِنْ عَذَابِ ٱلْحَمِيم — the word that comes between

**And a detail of word order is the finest point in the verse.**

**The expected expression would have been *"min ḥamīmin"* (of scalding water).** The expression that comes carries a genitive: *min **ʿadhābi'l-ḥamīm*** — **"of the punishment of scalding water".**

**A word, that is, comes between the substance and the head: *ʿadhāb*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the phrase:** what is poured is named not as water but as **the punishment of water**. **An abstract noun takes the place of a concrete substance.**

**And it has an effect:** the sentence takes the event out of being a physical description and makes it **the carrying out of a sentence**. What is poured is not a substance but a **requital**.

## 44/49 — ذُقْ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْكَرِيمُ

*Dhuq innaka anta'l-ʿazīzu'l-karīm*

"'Taste it! You were the mighty one, the noble one.'"

**This is the sharpest point of word order in the sūra and deserves detailed treatment.**

### The address turning suddenly to the singular

**In the forty-seventh and forty-eighth verses the commands were plural and their addressees nameless: *khudhū*, *iʿtilū*, *ṣubbū*.**

**In the forty-ninth the command turns singular and the addressee is definite: *dhuq* — "taste".**

| Verse | Command | Addressee | Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| 47 | *khudhū*, *iʿtilū* | **A nameless plural** | *-hu* — that man |
| 48 | *ṣubbū* | **A nameless plural** | *raʾsihi* — that man's head |
| **49** | ***dhuq*** | **The man himself** | — |

**The person spoken about throughout the scene, that is, is at the very end addressed directly.**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and the finding recorded for the same move in `056-vakia.md` works here too:**

> *"the narrative turns to the addressee at the very point where it grows most concrete. The classification is made in the third person, **the scene is made to be lived in the second.**"*

**In al-Dukhān this is done on the scale of a single person.**

### ذُقْ — the root ذ-و-ق

**The root was treated in `078-nebe.md` and this was recorded there:**

> *"Tasting is the nearest of the senses: seeing happens at a distance, hearing happens at a distance, touching requires contact, but tasting requires **taking in**. And the Qurʾān uses this verb very often for punishment."*

**I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.**

**What is peculiar to al-Dukhān is the root's second occurrence in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **49** | ***dhuq*** | **A man** | **Taste the punishment** — a command |
| **56** | *lā **yadhūqūna** fīha'l-mawt* | **The muttaqīn** | **They do not taste death** — a negation |

**The same root, one a command and one a denial.** And their objects differ: the one punishment, the other death.

**I record this as a verifiable repetition within the sūra.**

### إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْكَرِيمُ — the pattern itself

**Now we come to the real matter of the verse. And first the grammar of the sentence must be set out, because the point lies exactly there.**

**There are four separate elements of certainty in the sentence, and the four come one upon another:**

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1. إِنَّ** | A particle of emphasis — "indeed" |
| **2. أَنتَ** (the pronoun of separation) | Keeps the predicate from being taken for an attribute; produces **restriction** (*qaṣr*) |
| **3. ٱلْعَزِيز** (with the definite article) | Makes the attribute **definite** — "that known mighty one" |
| **4. ٱلْكَرِيم** (with the definite article) | The second attribute, again definite |

**In Arabic the coming together of these four elements builds the most certain sentence of identity the language can give. Normally this pattern is used to say that an attribute belongs solely and entirely** to someone.

**And the sūra had used this pattern for God seven verses earlier:**

| Verse | Sentence | Of whom |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *inna**hu huwa**'s-Samīʿu'l-ʿAlīm* | **God** |
| **42** | *inna**hu huwa**'l-**ʿAzīz**u'r-Raḥīm* | **God** |
| **49** | *inna**ka anta**'l-**ʿazīz**u'l-karīm* | **A human being** |
| **57** | *dhālika **huwa**'l-fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm* | **The deliverance** |

**Four sentences, one pattern.** And in the third the pattern is **taken out of its place**.

### How the mockery (*tahakkum*) works

**In Arabic rhetoric this is called *tahakkum*: to say a thing meaning exactly its opposite.**

**And the strongest form of *tahakkum* is the taking of a word from its claimant's own mouth and giving it back.**

**It is transmitted that there was someone who said these two attributes of himself — it is transmitted that he said he counted himself the mightiest and the noblest of his people. I give no name of a person or of a tribe;** in this commentary accounts of occasions of revelation are transmitted without names, and I issue no ruling about the details of this account.

**But even without the report, how the verse works can be read from the text, and it rests on these three data:**

**One.** *ʿAzīz* and *karīm* are the **two basic attributes** a man in Arab society could want for himself: the one **power** (invincibility, inviolability), the other **worth** (lineage, generosity, standing).

**Two.** The sentence gives those two attributes to that man **in the most certain language** — and precisely at the moment they are taken from his hand.

**Three.** The sūra had given one of the same attributes (*al-ʿAzīz*) to God seven verses earlier.

**And this is the mechanism of the mockery — I give it as my own reading:**

**The sentence is not lying; it is *changing the place*.** The man really did bear those attributes — within his own people, on his own measure. **The verse does not deny those attributes; it carries them somewhere else and measures them there.** And there the same attributes mean nothing.

**For a saying to be mockery in a language it need not be false.** It is enough that it be out of place.

### The choosing of the two attributes — and how it works

**What the two attributes do here must be seen separately, because each takes back a different thing.**

**ٱلْعَزِيز — the root ع-ز-ز.** It was recorded above (at verse 42): the concrete core of the root is **"hard and impassable ground"**. *ʿIzza* — **imperviousness, inviolability.**

**And now see what happens:** in the forty-seventh verse that man is **seized and dragged**. That is, the exact opposite of inviolability.

| Attribute | What it claims | What happens in the scene |
|---|---|---|
| ***ʿAzīz*** | **Untouchable** | *Khudhūhu* — **he was seized** |
| ***Karīm*** | **Honoured, one who is served** | *Fa'ʿtilūhu* — **he was dragged** |

**ٱلْكَرِيم — the root ك-ر-م.** As recorded in `056-vakia.md`, one side of the word is **"the useful of its own kind"**; another side is **one who is honoured, who is entertained as a guest.**

**And *karam* in Arabic is a word of hospitality: *ikrām* — the entertaining of a guest.**

**Now let us look at the forty-eighth verse — I give this as my own reading:** something is **served** to that man. It is poured over his head. **All the elements of a scene of hospitality are there: the arriving guest, the thing set before him, the manner of its presentation.**

**And in `056-vakia.md` this was named with exactly one word:** at al-Wāqiʿa 56/56 the food of the left hand is called *nuzul* — **"the fare set before a guest".** The sentence in al-Wāqiʿa was: *hādhā nuzuluhum yawma'd-dīn.*

**The two sūras, that is, make the same inversion:** al-Wāqiʿa uses the word **hospitality**, al-Dukhān the attribute **karīm**.

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording of the two sūras is verifiable, but it is I who establish the link between them.

### A comparison with the *karīm* chain in al-Wāqiʿa

**The chain established for this word in `056-vakia.md` was this, and it entered the closing table there too:**

| al-Wāqiʿa | Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Denial** | 44 | *lā bāridin wa **lā karīm*** | **Denied** of a shade |
| **Affirmation** | 77 | *la-qurʾānun **karīm*** | **Given** to the Qurʾān |

**And the conclusion recorded in al-Wāqiʿa:** *"The same attribute, denied in one place and ascribed in another. And the sense the lexicographers give for *karīm*, 'the useful of its own kind', works in both places."*

**al-Dukhān does something different, and the difference matters.**

| | **al-Wāqiʿa's move** | **al-Dukhān's move** |
|---|---|---|
| How many positions | **Two:** denial and affirmation | **Three:** real, temporary, false |
| How it works | The attribute is **denied** in one place and **given** in another | The attribute is **given** three times — one deserved (17), one not held on to (26), one **claimed** (49) |
| Is there mockery | **No** — both sentences are plain | **Yes** — the third is *tahakkum* |
| Of whom | **Objects**: a shade and a book | **Persons**: a messenger and a man |

**And what the two sūras share is this:** both use the same attribute **more than once** within the sūra and **draw the difference of meaning from position.**

**I present this as my own reading and rely on the closing table of `056-vakia.md`.**

### A link of method with `050-kaf.md`

**And one further link of method must be recorded.**

**In `054-kamer.md` a technique was named: *"giving the word back to its claimant"*.** In `085-buruc.md` the same thing was recorded with another word: *"the verdict is announced in the language of the offence."*

**al-Dukhān 44/49 is one of the plainest examples of this technique** — because what is given back here is not an act (*baṭsh*) or an accusation (*siḥr*) but **the two attributes the man applied to himself.**

**And the giving back is done by way of mockery: the attribute is returned, but in a place where its owner cannot use it.**

### A limit

**Finally I draw a limit.**

**There is disagreement over who the addressee of the verse is:**

| View | Who the addressee is |
|---|---|
| **A particular person** | Someone known in the context of the sūra's revelation |
| **The genus** | Everyone who bears the attribute *al-athīm* (44) |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**And one more record, required by `USLUP.md`: I issue no ruling about any particular person on the strength of this verse.** What the text builds is an **attribute** and the counterpart of that attribute.

## 44/50 — إِنَّ هَٰذَا مَا كُنتُم بِهِۦ تَمْتَرُونَ

*Inna hādhā mā kuntum bihi tamtarūn*

"This is the very thing about which you kept on doubting."

### The address returns to the plural

**In the forty-ninth verse the address was singular: *dhuq*, *innaka*, *anta*.** In the fiftieth it returns to the plural: ***kuntum***, ***tamtarūn***.

| Verse | Address | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **47-48** | **Plural** — commands | **Nameless executants** |
| **49** | **Singular** | **One man** |
| **50** | **Plural** | **Those who doubt** |

**The scene changes person three times.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the text brings a single person forward, and then makes that person the specimen of a community. **What is told in the forty-ninth verse, that is, is not left as the story of a single man.**

### تَمْتَرُونَ — the root م-ر-ي

**The root occurred in `002-bakara.md`, `054-kamer.md`, `055-rahman.md` and `053-necm.md`.**

**On its concrete sense the lexicographers record this: *marā* — to rub, to stroke an animal's udder in order to milk it.** *Mirya* — the milk drawn.

**And two branches issue from this:**

- **مِرَاء (*mirāʾ*)** — to argue, to contend. The link the lexicographers make: in an argument too **words are drawn** out of the other party, the subject is worried at.
- **Form VIII: امْتَرَى (*imtarā*)** — to doubt, to remain in two minds.

**I transmit this link as a relation established by the lexicographers; I issue no firm etymological ruling.**

**And the pattern matters: the verb is in Form VIII and comes with *kāna* + the imperfect: *kuntum … tamtarūn*.**

**The finding recorded for this pattern in `083-mutaffifin.md` works here too:** *kāna* + the imperfect conveys **continuity**. **Not "you doubted", then, but "you kept on doubting".**

**And the choice of root is worth recording — I give it as my own reading:** the verse does not say *shakk* (the word of the ninth verse), it says *imtirāʾ*. **The difference between the two words comes out of the definition of the root:**

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| ***Shakk*** (9) | ش-ك-ك | **A state** — being caught between two possibilities |
| ***Imtirāʾ*** (50) | م-ر-ي | **An act** — to keep worrying at the doubt, to pick at the subject |

**The sūra names doubt twice, and the second time it chooses a more active word.**

**I present this as my own reading;** the definitions of the two roots and the wording of the two verses are verifiable.

### هَٰذَا — the demonstrative

**What it points to is not stated.** The nearest referent is the scene described in verses forty-three to forty-nine.

**And that the demonstrative indicates what is *near* is worth recording:** *hādhā* — "this". Not *dhālika* ("that").

**I give this as my own reading:** what was doubted is set before the doubter. What was debated as a distant possibility is shown as **an object close at hand**. **The same demonstrative was used in the eleventh verse too: *hādhā ʿadhābun alīm*.**

| Verse | Expression | What is shown |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | ***hādhā** ʿadhābun alīm* | **The smoke** |
| **50** | ***hādhā** mā kuntum bihi tamtarūn* | **The scene of the Fire** |

**The same pronoun of nearness in both verses.** I record this as a verifiable repetition within the sūra.

## 44/51-52 — إِنَّ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ فِى مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ · فِى جَنَّٰتٍ وَعُيُونٍ

*Inna'l-muttaqīna fī maqāmin amīn · Fī jannātin wa ʿuyūn*

"The muttaqīn are in a safe station. Among gardens and springs."

### ٱلْمُتَّقِين — the root و-ق-ي and the chain with the fifty-sixth verse

**The root was treated in many sections** (`050-kaf.md`, `076-insan.md`, `078-nebe.md`, `091-sems.md` and more); its concrete sense: **to guard oneself by putting something in between.** *Wiqāya* — the thing that guards, a shield. In Form VIII, *ittaqā* — to guard oneself.

**And now we come to one of the cleanest internal chains of the sūra.**

**The same root returns in the fifty-sixth verse — but this time the agent changes:**

| Verse | Word | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| **51** | *al-**muttaqīn*** — those who guard themselves | **They themselves** — Form VIII, reflexive |
| **56** | *wa **waqāhum** ʿadhāba'l-jaḥīm* — He guarded them | **God** — Form I, transitive |

**The same root, two agents, two patterns.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared root of the two verses:** the sūra opens the block with **their guarding themselves** and closes it with **their being guarded**. And in between, in the fifty-seventh verse, this is given its name: *faḍlan min rabbika* — **as a bounty.**

**The chain has three steps, then: they guarded themselves (51) → they were guarded (56) → their being guarded was a bounty (57).**

### فِى مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ — and the comparison with the twenty-sixth verse

**This comparison was established above (at verse 26) and is completed here:**

| Verse | Construct | Who | What the attribute reports |
|---|---|---|---|
| **26** | *wa **maqāmin karīm*** | **The house of Pharaoh** | **Splendour** |
| **51** | *fī **maqāmin amīn*** | **The muttaqīn** | **Safety** |

**Both phrases have the same noun and the same indefiniteness; the only thing that changes is the attribute.**

**And the particles change too:** in the twenty-sixth verse the station is the object of *kam tarakū* — the thing **left**. In the fifty-first it comes with *fī* — the thing one is **inside**.

**I present this as my own reading:** the sūra sets the two stations side by side and builds the difference **not in size but in kind**. **What the first lacked was not splendour; it was security.**

**A note on variant readings:** it is transmitted that the word is also read *muqām* (a place of residence, a noun of place from Form IV). **The difference of meaning is small:** *maqām* is the place where one stands, *muqām* the place where one stays. **I issue no ruling about the readings.**

**أَمِين — the root أ-م-ن.** The sūra's chain of five occurrences was given at the twelfth verse.

**And the use here is worth recording:** *amīn* is used of a **place**. For a place to be "safe" is for the one who is in it to have nothing to fear.

**The Qurʾān uses this phrase elsewhere too: *al-baladu'l-amīn* (al-Tīn 95/3) — and it was treated in `095-tin.md`. The phrase, that is, is settled in the Qurʾān as an attribute of a place.**

### فِى جَنَّٰتٍ وَعُيُونٍ — the plainest repetition in the sūra

**A phrase identical letter for letter with the twenty-fifth verse.** It was recorded above and is completed here:

| Verse | Particle | Who | The relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **25** | ***min*** | **The house of Pharaoh** | The place they **departed** |
| **52** | ***fī*** | **The muttaqīn** | The place they **are in** |

**The only difference between the two verses is a single letter.**

**This is a verifiable datum of the text.** And I recorded its interpretation above; here I add one thing more:

**I give this as my own reading:** that the description of the Garden is built out of **familiar** material is a choice. In `056-vakia.md` the reverse was recorded — there it was said of the zaqqūm that *"the word does not introduce, it estranges"*, and it was set out in a table that the plants of the people of the right hand are **familiar**.

**al-Dukhān makes the same distinction:** on the side of the Fire a **strange** tree (*zaqqūm*) and an unrecognised substance (*muhl*); on the side of the Garden **known** things (a garden, springs) — and precisely the same things the house of Pharaoh possessed.

## 44/53-54 — يَلْبَسُونَ مِن سُندُسٍ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٍ مُّتَقَٰبِلِينَ · كَذَٰلِكَ وَزَوَّجْنَٰهُم بِحُورٍ عِينٍ

*Yalbasūna min sundusin wa istabraqin mutaqābilīn · Kadhālika wa zawwajnāhum bi-ḥūrin ʿīn*

"They wear fine silk and brocade, facing one another. So it is. And We have paired them with wide-eyed companions."

### سُندُس and إِسْتَبْرَق

**The two words were treated at `076-insan.md` 76/21. I do not repeat them.** The record there:

> - **سُندُس** (*sundus*) — fine, soft silk.
> - **إِسْتَبْرَق** (*istabraq*) — thick, glossy, figured silk; brocade.

**And two further things were recorded in `076-insan.md`, and both should be carried over here.**

**One — about the origin of the words:**

> *"Some of the words used in this sūra's description of luxury entered Arabic from outside: *zanjabīl*, *sundus*, *istabraq*."*

**And in the closing record of `076-insan.md`:** *"no ruling was issued as to which language they came from; it was recorded only that their having entered Arabic from outside is the widespread view."* **I keep the same record.**

**Two — about the meaning of silk in that world:**

> *"silk was an imported good in that world. It came from China, passed from hand to hand along the road, and was a luxury of heavy price. The word, that is, is not the name of a fabric but the name of an **unattainability**."*

**I rely on that treatment.**

**There are two things peculiar to al-Dukhān.**

**One — al-Dukhān gives the two silks side by side.** In `076-insan.md` it was recorded that al-Insān 76/21 says *thiyābu sundusin khuḍrun wa istabraq*; **in al-Dukhān there is no colour (*khuḍr* — green), only the two kinds.**

| Sūra | Expression | The difference |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Insān 76/21** | *thiyābu **sundusin khuḍrun** wa istabraq* | **Colour is present** |
| **al-Dukhān 44/53** | *min **sundusin** wa **istabraq*** | **No colour** |

**Two — and here is the real point: the sentence does not end with the clothing.**

### مُّتَقَٰبِلِين — the root ق-ب-ل

**The word is the active participle of Form VI (*tafāʿul*), and the basic contribution of Form VI in Arabic is reciprocity:** two parties doing the same thing to one another.

***Qabila* — to meet, to come face to face. *Qibla* — the direction one faces. *Mutaqābil* — turned towards one another.**

**The word was treated at `056-vakia.md` 56/16** (*muttakiʾīna ʿalayhā mutaqābilīn*) **and this was recorded there:**

> *"when these two states (*muttakiʾīn* and *mutaqābilīn*) come together, what emerges is a picture in which ease and being face to face are present at once. In this world these two mostly come apart: to be at ease one must be alone, and to be together one must be on one's guard."*

**In al-Dukhān the accompanying word is different, and this opens a separate reading.**

**In al-Dukhān *mutaqābilīn* comes at the end of a sentence about clothing.**

**And I give this as my own reading, its ground being the word order of the sentence:** the sentence begins with clothing and ends with a gaze. **Clothing does its work only if there is someone looking.** A man's dressing is not meaningful on its own; dress establishes a relation of **being seen**.

**The verse joins the two in a single sentence: the thing worn, and the facing of one another.**

**And one thing more must be recorded — as my own reading:** *mutaqābilīn* is a state and it is **plural**. The description of the Garden, that is, is not built as a list of pleasures alone; **reciprocity** is added.

**This sharpens when compared with the forty-first verse:**

| Verse | Relation | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **41** | *lā yughnī **mawlan** ʿan **mawlan*** | **Relations do not work** |
| **53** | ***mutaqābilīn*** | **Standing face to face** |

**I present this as my own reading;** the wording of the two verses is verifiable, but it is I who establish a contrast between them. **The forty-first verse described the moment when relations are of no avail; the fifty-third the state in which relation is established.**

### كَذَٰلِكَ وَزَوَّجْنَٰهُم — the shared pattern with the twenty-eighth verse

**It was recorded above (at verse 28) and is completed here:**

| Verse | Expression | Verb | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **28** | *kadhālika **wa awrathnāhā** qawman ākharīn* | **و-ر-ث** | **A passing out of hand** |
| **54** | *kadhālika **wa zawwajnāhum** bi-ḥūrin ʿīn* | **ز-و-ج** | **A joining together** |

**The two sentences are in the same pattern and stand at the same place in the two tableaux.**

**I record this as a verifiable repetition within the sūra.**

### زَوَّجْنَٰهُم بِ — the point of the particle

**The verb is in Form II (*zawwaja*) and has been made transitive with *bāʾ*: *zawwajnāhum bi-ḥūrin ʿīn*.**

**And the lexicographers record this particle, because the verb *zawwaja* is normally directly transitive.**

**Two explanations are transmitted:**

| Explanation | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Its coming with *bāʾ* brings out the sense of "pairing"** | The verb here reports not a contract of marriage but **a bringing near, a joining** |
| **It has the sense of *qarranā* (We brought near)** | Some of the lexicographers explain the verb with this sense |

**I issue no firm ruling.**

**And the same phrase occurs word for word at al-Ṭūr 52/20:** *wa zawwajnāhum bi-ḥūrin ʿīn.* **The expression, that is, is not peculiar to al-Dukhān.**

### حُورٍ عِينٍ

**This phrase was treated in `055-rahman.md`, `052-tur.md` and `056-vakia.md`. I do not repeat it.** The record in `055-rahman.md`:

> *"**The word occurs in several places in the Qurʾān** — at al-Dukhān 44/54, al-Ṭūr 52/20 and al-Wāqiʿa 56/22, in the form *ḥūrun ʿīn* (wide-eyed ḥūrs)."*

**I add here only a record of wording:** *ʿuyūn* (springs, 52) and *ʿīn* (wide-eyed, 54) are **from the same root** — **ع-ي-ن**. In Arabic *ʿayn* means both **eye** and **spring**; the shared idea of the root is **a source and an opening**.

**The sūra uses this root in both its branches two verses apart:**

| Verse | Word | Which branch |
|---|---|---|
| **25, 52** | *ʿuyūn* | **A spring** |
| **54** | *ʿīn* | **An eye** |

**I record this as a verifiable datum of wording; I make no claim of a deliberate play on words.**

## 44/55 — يَدْعُونَ فِيهَا بِكُلِّ فَٰكِهَةٍ ءَامِنِينَ

*Yadʿūna fīhā bi-kulli fākihatin āminīn*

"There they call for every fruit, in safety."

### يَدْعُونَ بِ — to call, to ask for

**The root د-ع-و was treated above (at verse 22).** *Daʿā bi-* — to ask for a thing, to call for it.

**And the two occurrences within the sūra were recorded:** Moses called upon his Lord (22), the muttaqīn call for fruit (55). **The same root, two branches.**

**I add here a note on word order:** the verb is imperfect (*yadʿūna*) — continuity. And *fīhā* comes between: **"there" they call.** The **place** of the asking, that is, is stressed.

### بِكُلِّ فَٰكِهَةٍ — the scope

***Kulli fākihatin* — "every fruit".** *Kull* + an indefinite: comprehensive.

**The root ف-ك-ه was treated above (at verse 27) and `056-vakia.md` was relied on.** The record there: *"In Arabic *ṭaʿām* (food) reports a necessity, *fākiha* a surplus. Fruit is not eaten in order to stay alive."*

**And its counterpart within the sūra was recorded above:**

| Verse | Expression | Who | What is missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **27** | *naʿmatin kānū fīhā **fākihīn*** | **The house of Pharaoh** | **Security** |
| **55** | *bi-kulli **fākihatin** **āminīn*** | **The muttaqīn** | — |

**The difference between the two verses is a single word, and that word comes now.**

### ءَامِنِين — and the missing part of the favour

**The root أ-م-ن; the fifth and last occurrence in the sūra.**

**And the word is a *ḥāl*: "being in safety".**

**Now we come to the real point, and I give it as my own reading.**

**After a list of favours is given, one more item is not added to the list — the *condition* of the list is added.**

It may be seen thus: the list in verses twenty-five to twenty-seven was complete. There was a garden, there was water, there were crops, there was station, there was comfort, there was ease. **The one thing missing was the knowledge of whether they would last.**

**And the greatest enemy of a favour lies not in the favour itself but in the mind of the one who has it: the possibility of its being cut off.**

**The verse puts exactly this at the end of the list of fruits: *āminīn*.**

**And this connects with the fifty-first verse:**

| Verse | Word | What it qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| **51** | *maqāmin **amīn*** | **The place** — the space one is in |
| **55** | ***āminīn*** | **The person** — the state of the one who is in it |

**The one is an attribute of the place, the other of the person.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the two verses:** safety is established both outside and inside. **The place is safe, and the one in it is safe too.** These two are not the same thing — in a safe place there can also be someone who is afraid.

## 44/56 — لَا يَذُوقُونَ فِيهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ إِلَّا ٱلْمَوْتَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ ۖ وَوَقَىٰهُمْ عَذَابَ ٱلْجَحِيمِ

*Lā yadhūqūna fīha'l-mawta illa'l-mawtata'l-ūlā, wa waqāhum ʿadhāba'l-jaḥīm*

"There they do not taste death, save the first death. And He has guarded them from the punishment of the blaze."

### لَا يَذُوقُونَ — and the chain with the forty-ninth verse

**It was recorded above (at verse 49):** *dhuq* (a command) and *lā yadhūqūn* (a denial) are from the same root.

**I add one more record here:** the **objects** of the two sentences differ.

| Verse | Verb | Object (stated or implied) |
|---|---|---|
| **49** | *dhuq* | **The punishment** — the object is not stated, it comes from the context |
| **56** | *lā yadhūqūna* | **Death** — the object is stated explicitly |

**I record this as an observation about word order.**

**And the use of the verb "to taste" of death is settled in the Qurʾān:** *kullu nafsin dhāʾiqatu'l-mawt* — "every soul shall taste death" (Āl ʿImrān 3/185; al-Anbiyāʾ 21/35; al-ʿAnkabūt 29/57).

**The finding recorded in `078-nebe.md` works here too:** *"tasting requires **taking in**."* **Death, that is, is named not as an event coming from outside but as a thing taken in.**

### إِلَّا ٱلْمَوْتَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ — the linguistic debate over the exception

**This exception is one of the places on which the lexicographers dwell most. The reason is plain: with the note *fīhā* (there) in place, how can a death "they do not taste there" be excepted?**

**I give the views in a table and impose no preference.**

| Reading | What it says | Ground | Objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A disjunctive exception** (*istithnāʾ munqaṭiʿ*) | *Illā* here has the sense of **"but, however"**: "There they do not taste death; **but** they had tasted the first death [in this world]" | It is common in Arabic for *illā* to come in the sense of *lākin*, and the thing excepted may be outside the set | — |
| **Reinforcement of the negation** (*taʾkīdu'n-nafy*) | The exception is there to **strengthen** the negation: "they never taste death at all" | In Arabic the excepting of something impossible makes the negation absolute | Whether this usage fits the present example is debated |
| **A connected exception, reading *fīhā* broadly** | *Fīhā* covers not only the Garden but **the whole process**; then the first death is inside the set | It preserves the flow of the sentence | The pronoun *fīhā* refers to the nearest referent (the Garden); to widen it is forced |
| ***Illā* = *baʿda* / *siwā*** | "**After** / **other than** the first death" | Some lexicographers transmit this equivalent | This sense of the particle is not widespread |

**The first reading (disjunctive) is the most widespread in the classical commentaries.** I give this as a record of what is widespread, not as a preference.

### A comparison with al-Ṣāffāt 37/58-59

**And a datum must be added to this debate, because the Qurʾān uses the same phrase in another place, from the mouths of the people of the Garden:**

> *"Are we not to die again — **save our first death** — and are we not to be punished?"* (al-Ṣāffāt 37/58-59 — *illā mawtatana'l-ūlā*)

**Let us set the two places side by side:**

| | **al-Ṣāffāt 37/58-59** | **al-Dukhān 44/56** |
|---|---|---|
| Who speaks | **The people of the Garden** — in the form of a question | **The text** — in the form of a statement |
| The expression | *illā **mawtatana'l-ūlā*** | *illā **al-mawtata'l-ūlā*** |
| Definiteness | **By a genitive** — "our first death" | **By the definite article** — "that first death" |

**The same exceptive pattern, the same phrase, two separate mouths.**

**I give this as a record of wording:** the expression is not a construction peculiar to al-Dukhān, and the occurrence in al-Ṣāffāt may be counted an instance supporting the disjunctive reading — there too those who ask are excepting the death they tasted **in this world**.

### The chain with the thirty-fifth verse

**It was recorded above (at verse 35) and is completed here. It is the most striking repetition in the sūra:**

| Verse | Expression | Who | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **35** | *in hiya illā **mawtatuna'l-ūlā*** | **Those who deny** | "There is one death; nothing after" |
| **56** | *lā yadhūqūna fīha'l-mawta illā **al-mawtata'l-ūlā*** | **The text** | "They tasted one death; nothing after" |

**The shared part of the two sentences is identical letter for letter: مَوْتَة + ٱلْأُولَىٰ, and both have the exceptive particle *illā*.**

**This is a verifiable datum of the text. What is interpretation is this, and I give it as my own reading:**

**The sentence of those who deny turns out true of the muttaqīn.**

They had said: *"There is only one death, that is all."* **And the fifty-sixth verse says the same thing — but adds one qualification: *fīhā*.**

| | **The denier's sentence (35)** | **The text's sentence (56)** |
|---|---|---|
| How many deaths | **One** | **One** |
| What comes after | **Nothing** at all | **There is something** — and there is no death there |
| The qualification | None | ***fīhā*** — "there" |

**The difference, that is, is not in the number of deaths but in whether there is anything after death. The denier counted the number rightly, but stopped counting too soon.**

**This reading is mine;** the wording of the two verses is verifiable.

**And the technique recorded in `050-kaf.md` works here too:** *"The sūra takes the word the objector chooses and spreads it over the whole text. And each time it turns its direction."*

### وَوَقَىٰهُمْ عَذَابَ ٱلْجَحِيمِ — the change of tense

**The whole block had been told in the imperfect (the present/future):** *yalbasūna* (53), *yadʿūna* (55), *lā yadhūqūna* (56).

**And in the last clause the verb turns to the perfect: *waqāhum* — "He guarded".**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and the exegetes record this shift.**

**Its explanation is this — I give it as my own reading:** the states in the Garden continue (imperfect), but **the guarding is a completed work** (perfect). If a man is there, the guarding has already happened; it is no longer a process.

**And this closes the chain established with the fifty-first verse:** *muttaqīn* (those who guarded themselves) → *waqāhum* (God guarded them). **Both verbs are from the same root; the one is their work, the other His.**

**ٱلْجَحِيم — the second and last occurrence in the sūra.** In the forty-seventh verse it was the place into which a man was dragged; here it is the thing guarded against.

## 44/57 — فَضْلًا مِّن رَّبِّكَ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ ٱلْفَوْزُ ٱلْعَظِيمُ

*Faḍlan min rabbika, dhālika huwa'l-fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm*

"As a bounty from your Lord. That is the great triumph."

### فَضْلًا — the case, and the placing

**The word is in the accusative and the lexicographers transmit several readings as to what it attaches to:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| **A causal object** | "**So that it might be a bounty**" — the reason for the guarding |
| **An absolute object** | In place of the verbal noun of the verb *waqāhum*: "…He guarded them, guarding them as a bounty" |
| **A circumstantial accusative** | The state of the guarding: "in the state of a bounty" |

**I do not impose a preference.** In all three the result arrives at the same place.

**And the shared pattern with the sixth verse was recorded above:**

| Verse | Expression | What it qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *raḥmatan **min rabbika*** | **The sending down of the Book** |
| **57** | *faḍlan **min rabbika*** | **Being guarded from the Fire** |

**Both sentences are in the same pattern and stand at the two ends of the sūra.**

### فَضْل — the root ف-ض-ل

**Its concrete sense: a thing's being more than is required, its increasing.** *Faḍl* — what is left over, what remains. *Fuḍūl* — surpluses. *Fāḍil* — one who is superior: who goes beyond what is expected of him.

**And the logic of the root is worth recording:** *faḍl* is the part **above** what is deserved. The word, that is, carries within itself a **gratuitousness** — what is deserved would not be *faḍl* but *ajr* (a wage) or *jazāʾ* (a requital).

**Now let us look at how the verse is placed — I give this as my own reading.**

**Since the fifty-first verse the persons described have been named *al-muttaqīn* — that is, ones who have done something. They guarded themselves, they took care, they held themselves back.**

**And in the fifty-sixth verse it was said that God guarded them.**

**The fifty-seventh verse renames that guarding: *faḍl*.**

| Step | Verse | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 51 — *al-**muttaqīn*** | **They** — they guarded themselves |
| 2 | 56 — *wa **waqāhum*** | **God** — He guarded them |
| 3 | 57 — ***faḍlan** min rabbika* | **And this was a bounty** |

**The sentence, that is, comes to the end of a chain of deserving built over two verses, and takes the outcome outside deserving.**

**This reading is mine;** the order of the three verses and the definition of the word *faḍl* are verifiable.

**And this agrees with the general stance of the sūra:** in the sixth verse the coming of the Book too was named a *raḥma* — that is, not by way of deserving.

### ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ ٱلْفَوْزُ ٱلْعَظِيمُ — the fourth and last occurrence of the pattern

**The pattern repeated in four places in the sūra is completed here** (6, 42, 49, 57).

**And in the last occurrence the subject of the pattern is not a person but a thing: *dhālika* — "this".**

| Verse | Subject | What |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | *innahu* | **God** |
| 42 | *innahu* | **God** |
| 49 | *innaka* | **A human being** — mockery |
| **57** | ***dhālika*** | **The deliverance** |

**I record this as a verifiable datum within the sūra.**

**ٱلْفَوْز — the root ف-و-ز.** On its concrete sense the lexicographers record two branches:

- **فَازَ** — to be delivered, to attain one's desire, to win.
- **مَفَازَة** — a desert, a wide waterless land.

**The link between the two senses is made by the lexicographers thus, and it is striking:** the desert's being called *mafāza* is either **an optimistic naming** (since it is a place of destruction, the opposite is said), or it is because **deliverance from it** is hoped for. **I issue no firm ruling;** I transmit it as a relation established by the lexicographers.

**And the idea the word carries is not *winning* but *being delivered*.** To come out of a danger.

**I record this as my own reading, and it connects with the next verse:** the sentence does not say *"this is the gain"*. **The deliverance is the thing said in the preceding verse: being guarded from the punishment of the blaze.** The content of the *fawz*, that is, is given by the text itself.

**ٱلْعَظِيم — the root ع-ظ-م.** Its concrete sense: **bone** (*ʿaẓm*). *ʿAẓīm* — big-boned, great. The concrete core of the root is an idea of a **skeleton**: the load-bearing, hard part of a thing.

**And the attribute forms one of the last verse-endings of the sūra.**

## 44/58 — فَإِنَّمَا يَسَّرْنَٰهُ بِلِسَانِكَ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ

*Fa-innamā yassarnāhu bi-lisānika laʿallahum yatadhakkarūn*

"We have made it easy only in your tongue — that they may perhaps take heed."

### فَإِنَّمَا — the *fāʾ* and the restriction

**The *fāʾ* at the head attaches to the whole sūra.** After what has been told across fifty-seven verses, a sentence of conclusion is built.

**And *innamā* is the particle of restriction: "only, nothing but".**

**I record an observation as to what is restricted:** the sentence does not say *"We made it easy"*; it says *"We made it easy **only** in your tongue"*. **What is restricted is the *instrument* of the making easy.**

### يَسَّرْنَٰهُ — the root ي-س-ر and the comparison with al-Qamar

**The root was treated in many sections** — as recorded in `054-kamer.md`: `094-insirah.md`, `092-leyl.md`, `087-ala.md`, `084-insikak.md`, `073-muzzemmil.md`, `080-abese.md`, `051-zariyat.md`. **Its concrete sense: ease, suitability, being doable without strain.**

**And this verse was already cited in `054-kamer.md`:**

> *"The Qurʾān uses the same verb of itself once more: *'We have made it easy in your tongue'* (al-Dukhān 44/58; Maryam 19/97 — *fa-innamā **yassarnāhu** bi-lisānika*)."*

**And this was recorded in `054-kamer.md` about the pattern:**

> *"The verb is in the **Form II** (*tafʿīl*) pattern: *yassara*. What this form carries here is **transitivising** and **gradation**: to **make** a thing easy. It does not mean that the Qurʾān was not easy and was made easy afterwards; it means that **it was given as something easy.**"*

**I do not repeat it. Now I compare the two sūras — this shows the difference sought.**

| | **al-Qamar 54/17, 22, 32, 40** | **al-Dukhān 44/58** |
|---|---|---|
| **The sentence** | *wa laqad **yassarna'l-Qurʾāna li'dh-dhikri*** | *fa-innamā **yassarnāhu bi-lisānika*** |
| **The object** | ***al-Qurʾān*** — an explicit noun | ***-hu*** — a pronoun |
| **The qualification** | ***li**'dh-dhikr* — **for what** | ***bi**-lisānika* — **by what / by what route** |
| **The particle** | **ل** — end, purpose | **ب** — means, instrument |
| **How many times** | **Four times** — a refrain | **Once** |
| **What follows** | *fa-hal min muddakir* — **a question** | *laʿallahum yatadhakkarūn* — **hope** |
| **The shared root** | **ذ-ك-ر** | **ذ-ك-ر** |

**The two verses use the same verb and say two separate things.**

**al-Qamar gives the purpose of the ease: *li'dh-dhikr* — for remembrance. This was recorded in `054-kamer.md`: *"The verse does not only say 'We made the Qurʾān easy'; it says 'We made it easy للذِّكْرِ'. The ease, that is, has been given for a particular work**."*

**al-Dukhān gives the instrument of the ease: *bi-lisānika* — in your tongue.**

**I present this as my own reading, and its ground is the work of the two particles:** the one says **why** the ease is, the other **how** it is provided. **And together the two complete each other:** the text is easy because it is given in a language, and that ease is for the sake of remembrance.

**And the difference in the sentences that follow completes this too.** al-Qamar asks a question (*fa-hal min muddakir* — "is there any who will take heed?"); **al-Dukhān reports a hope (*laʿallahum yatadhakkarūn*).** Both sentences are from the root ذ-ك-ر.

**One further record:** the same expression occurs at Maryam 19/97 as well (*fa-innamā yassarnāhu bi-lisānika*) — **almost identical with the sentence in al-Dukhān.** I give this as a record of wording.

### بِلِسَانِكَ — "in your tongue"

**لِسَان — the tongue (the organ) and the tongue (a spoken language).** In Arabic the same word covers both; and the Qurʾān often uses this word in the sense of a language.

**Two readings are transmitted as to what the phrase says:**

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| **In the Arabic language** | The text was sent down in the language the addressees speak |
| **From your mouth** | The text was made easy by your reciting — its recitation and memorising were made easy |

**The two readings do not exclude one another** and both are transmitted in the classical commentaries. **I do not impose a preference.**

**And one record: the chain with the second verse of the sūra closes.**

| Verse | Expression | What |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | *wa'l-**kitāb**i'l-mubīn* | An **oath** by the Book |
| **58** | *fa-innamā **yassarnāhu** bi-lisānika* | The Book **was made easy** |

**The sūra, that is, opened by swearing by the Book and closes by saying how the Book was given.**

**I record this as a verifiable chain within the sūra.** And a note on the referent of the pronoun (*-hu*): the most widespread reading refers it to the Qurʾān; this link is supported by the second verse of the sūra as well.

### لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ — the chain with the thirteenth verse

**This link was recorded above (at verse 13) and is completed here:**

| Verse | Expression | Mood | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13** | *annā lahumu'dh-**dhikrā*** | **A question of denial** | "**How should** taking the reminder be possible for them?" |
| **58** | *laʿallahum **yatadhakkarūn*** | **A particle of hope** | "**Perhaps** they will take heed" |

**The same root, two opposite moods, at the two ends of the sūra.**

**And it is no contradiction — I give this as my own reading:** the thirteenth verse spoke of the moment **inside** the punishment, the fifty-eighth of the time **before** it. **The sūra, that is, says at which time the possibility of taking heed stands open.**

**And the verb is in Form VIII: *tadhakkara* — to make remembering one's own business, to take it upon oneself.**

**A record about the particle *laʿalla*:** in Arabic this particle means "it may be hoped that", and when it is used of God the majority of the lexicographers explain it **from the addressee's side**: the hope reports the position of the addressee — the door is open.

## 44/59 — فَٱرْتَقِبْ إِنَّهُم مُّرْتَقِبُونَ

*Fa'rtaqib innahum murtaqibūn*

"So watch; they too are watching."

### The sūra closing with the same command

**The command of the tenth verse comes back forty-nine verses later in the same wording.**

| Verse | Expression | Object |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | *fa'**rtaqib** yawma taʾti's-samāʾu bi-dukhānin mubīn* | **That day** — there is an object |
| **59** | *fa'**rtaqib*** | **No object** |

**Both commands are in the same pattern: *fāʾ* + a Form VIII imperative.** And both open a division of the sūra: the one the section of events (10-57), the other the closing.

**And the difference is worth recording — I give it as my own reading:** in the tenth verse what was to be watched for **was stated**; in the fifty-ninth it is not. **Because the forty-nine verses between have already stated it.**

**The command is the same, then, but the material accumulated behind it has changed.** The first time a day was pointed to; the second time we return to it with the content of that day already told.

**A similar observation was recorded in `054-kamer.md` for the refrains** (*"the reading that the material accumulated behind the refrain increases"*); in al-Dukhān the repetition is twofold and stands at the two ends of the sūra.

### إِنَّهُم مُّرْتَقِبُونَ — the other party watching too

**And the last sentence makes the command reciprocal: *innahum murtaqibūn* — "they too are watchers".**

**A plural active participle, from the same form.** The verb commanded to the addressee, that is, is reported as a verb the other party performs too.

**What does this mean?** The exegetes explain it in two ways:

| Reading | What it says |
|---|---|
| **They too are waiting** | The other party is waiting for an outcome as well — they hope for something in their own favour |
| **They too are being watched / their end is being watched for** | The waiting continues on both sides; but the outcome will fall out in favour of one |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**And I record an observation about word order:** the sentence does not say "you wait, they will be destroyed". **It joins both parties in the same verb.** The difference will emerge not in the verb but **at the end of the verb**.

**The Qurʾān builds a similar close with another root as well:** *"So turn away from them and wait; they too are waiting"* (al-Sajda 32/30 — *fa-aʿriḍ ʿanhum wa'ntaẓir innahum muntaẓirūn*). **There the root is ن-ظ-ر, here ر-ق-ب.**

**The difference between the two roots is worth recording and was partly recorded at the twenty-ninth verse:**

| Root | Core | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **ن-ظ-ر** | **To look** | A gaze; and in Form IV, **to grant a respite** |
| **ر-ق-ب** | **To watch** | To follow a thing **so as not to miss it**; *raqaba* — the neck |

**As recorded in `050-kaf.md`, the picture in the root ر-ق-ب is of a man stretching out his neck to look.** *Irtiqāb*, that is, is not a passive waiting.

**I present this as my own reading:** the sūra commands its addressee not to sit and wait but **not to take his eyes off**. And the finding recorded in `054-kamer.md` for al-Qamar 54/27 works here too: *"The man does not withdraw, he goes on looking."*

### On the last word of the sūra

**And one further observation.**

**The sūra does not end with a threat.** In the last sentence the other party is named, but no punishment is announced for them. **What is announced is that they too are waiting.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the last word of the sūra is not a verdict but **a statement of a situation.** And the statement puts both parties in the same position: both are waiting.

**The closing observation recorded in `050-kaf.md` works here too:** *"the sūra tells its addressee what he will not do before it tells him what he will do."* **In al-Dukhān this is plainer still: what he will do is a single word, and that word had already been said in the middle of the sūra.**

## What this says to the present

**This section is entirely my own reading and is not binding.**

**The verb the sūra repeats most is a verb of waiting, and this is no accident.**

al-Dukhān does not give its addressee a technique of argument. In the thirteenth verse it says why: **the proof has already been given.** There is no piece of information missing; what is missing is when that information will be of use.

**And the most human place in the sūra is the three sentences between the twelfth and fifteenth verses.**

What is described there is not the portrait of a disbeliever; **it is a very widespread human state.** A man under pressure turns. The turning is sincere — at that moment he really does believe. Then the pressure passes, and the turning passes with it.

**The sūra's diagnosis is this, and it follows from the wording of the verse:** the man is not lying; **what carries his faith is not the faith itself but the pain itself.** When the carrier is lifted, what it carried falls.

**And this is something that can be tested today too:** a person can ask what it is that binds him to a thing. **If the bond depends on the presence of a hardship, then what will become of the bond when the hardship passes is known in advance.**

**A second place: the list between the twenty-fifth and twenty-ninth verses.**

That list is an inventory of wealth, and its last item is startling: **mourning.** After the garden, the water, the crops, the mansion and the comfort have all been enumerated, the trace left behind is asked for, and the answer is given: **there is none.**

**What this says to the present is this:** the hardest thing for a man to give up is not property but **the hope of being remembered.** Everything possessed is carried with the expectation that it will leave a trace behind. The verse measures that expectation — and the unit it uses to measure it is taken not from property but **from the response of others.**

**A third place: the forty-ninth verse.**

In that verse two attributes a man said of himself are given back to him. **And the mockery arises not from the attributes being false but from their being out of place.**

**What this says to the present is plain:** the attributes a man uses of himself hold only within a particular frame. When the frame changes, the attribute does not remain. **And the verse does not say when the frame will change — only that it will.**

**Finally, the sūra's own command.**

*Irtaqib* — watch. And as recorded in `054-kamer.md`: **this is not inactivity.** The picture in the root is of a man stretching out his neck to look.

**The sūra commands its addressee not to produce the outcome but not to let the outcome out of his sight.** And by saying in the last sentence that the other party is waiting too, it does not turn the waiting into a superiority: **both parties are in the same position. The difference is not in the waiting but in what is waited for.**

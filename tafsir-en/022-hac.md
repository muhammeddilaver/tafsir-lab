# 22. Sūrat al-Ḥajj

**Seventy-eight verses.** It takes its name from the word **بِٱلْحَجِّ** in the twenty-seventh verse. It is the only sūra in the Qurʾān named for the rite of the ḥajj.

## A record on the period of revelation

**Whether this sūra was revealed at Mecca or at Medina is disputed in the classical sources.** The views transmitted may be summarised as follows:

| View | What is transmitted as its ground |
|---|---|
| **Entirely Meccan** | The address *yā ayyuha'n-nās*, the scenes of the Resurrection, the proofs of the raising of the dead |
| **Entirely Medinan** | The permission to fight (39-41), the detail of the rulings of the ḥajj, the address *yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū* (77) |
| **Mixed** | That some verses were revealed at Mecca and some at Medina; and in particular that 19-24 and 39-41 belong to separate periods |

**The third view is the one most widely transmitted in the sources.** In this commentary **I impose none of them as a preference and I build no ruling upon them.** Understanding the sūra does not depend on settling this distinction; it stands here only as a record.

The basmala was treated in earlier sections and is not taken up again here; the verse numbers are counted without it.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-7 | The shaking of the Hour and the proof of the raising | *Wa anna'llāha yabʿathu man fi'l-qubūr* (7) |
| **II** | 8-16 | Three portraits of man: the disputer, the one who stands at the edge, the one who rages | *Wa anna'llāha yahdī man yurīd* (16) |
| **III** | 17-24 | The one who will make the separation; a world that prostrates; two adversaries | *Wa hudū ilā ṣirāṭi'l-ḥamīd* (24) |
| **IV** | 25-37 | The Sacred Mosque, the call of Abraham (Ibrāhīm), the *shaʿāʾir* and the sacrifice | *Wa bashshiri'l-muḥsinīn* (37) |
| **V** | 38-41 | The permission, the principle of repelling, and the condition of power | *Wa lillāhi ʿāqibatu'l-umūr* (41) |
| **VI** | 42-51 | The peoples who denied, the respite, the ruins, the blindness of the heart | *Aṣḥābu'l-jaḥīm* (51) |
| **VII** | 52-59 | What Satan casts, two hearts, the emigration | *Wa inna'llāha la-ʿalīmun ḥalīm* (59) |
| **VIII** | 60-66 | The measure of requital and the proofs of power | *Inna'l-insāna la-kafūr* (66) |
| **IX** | 67-76 | The *mansak*, the cutting off of dispute, the parable of the fly | *Wa ila'llāhi turjaʿu'l-umūr* (76) |
| **X** | 77-78 | Bowing, prostration, and *ḥaqqa jihādih* | *Fa-niʿma'l-mawlā wa niʿma'n-naṣīr* (78) |

**The backbone of the sūra is a change of address.** The address *yā ayyuha'n-nās* (O people) occurs **four times** in this sūra: 1, 5, 49, 73. This is one of the densest concentrations within a single sūra in the Qurʾān. **And the sūra closes by changing the address in the seventy-seventh verse:** *yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū.*

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the distribution of the addresses:** the sūra opens with the widest set of addressees — the whole species — and closes by **narrowing**. What passes in between is how that wide set is divided: the one who disputes, the one who stands at the edge, the one who submits.

**The second backbone is a word: ح-ج-ج, and the place that surrounds it.** The sūra opens with the Resurrection, descends in its middle to the oldest place of worship on earth, and then rises again. **The most abstract and the most concrete stand within the same text.**

## 22/1-2 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ إِنَّ زَلْزَلَةَ ٱلسَّاعَةِ شَىْءٌ عَظِيمٌ · يَوْمَ تَرَوْنَهَا تَذْهَلُ كُلُّ مُرْضِعَةٍ عَمَّآ أَرْضَعَتْ وَتَضَعُ كُلُّ ذَاتِ حَمْلٍ حَمْلَهَا وَتَرَى ٱلنَّاسَ سُكَٰرَىٰ وَمَا هُم بِسُكَٰرَىٰ وَلَٰكِنَّ عَذَابَ ٱللَّهِ شَدِيدٌ

*Yā ayyuha'n-nāsu'ttaqū rabbakum inna zalzalata's-sāʿati shayʾun ʿaẓīm · Yawma tarawnahā tadhhalu kullu murḍiʿatin ʿammā arḍaʿat wa taḍaʿu kullu dhāti ḥamlin ḥamlahā wa tara'n-nāsa sukārā wa mā hum bi-sukārā wa lākinna ʿadhāba'llāhi shadīd*

"**O people! Be conscious of your responsibility towards your Lord. The shaking of the Hour is a tremendous thing.** On the day you see it, **every nursing woman will forget the child she nurses and every pregnant woman will set down her burden. You will see people drunk, though they are not drunk; but the punishment of God is severe.**"

### زَلْزَلَة — root: ز-ل-ز-ل

**The root was analysed in detail in `099-zilzal.md`** — on the occasion of that sūra's name: a reduplicated quadriliteral root (*muḍāʿaf rubāʿī*), conveying a repeated, oscillating motion; *ṣalṣala*, *qaʿqaʿa*, *waswasa* have the same structure. **I do not repeat it here.**

**The root was also treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/214** — there the verb was used **of people**: *wa zulzilū* (they were shaken). The note made in al-Baqara holds exactly: **the root describes the shaking of the earth, but what was shaken there was not the earth but people.**

**Setting the three places side by side shows how the word works:**

| Place | What is shaken | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zilzāl 99/1 | **The earth** — *zulzilati'l-arḍ* | The event itself |
| al-Baqara 2/214 | **The believers** — *wa zulzilū* | The measure of the hardship |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/1** | **The Hour** — *zalzalatu's-sāʿa* | The two combined |

**The construct here must be recorded: *zalzalatu's-sāʿa*.** The genitive binds the shaking not to **a place** but to **a time**. It is not "the shaking of the earth" but **"the shaking of that Hour"**.

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the genitive itself:** what is shaken is not bounded as a place. **In Sūrat al-Zilzāl the subject of the shaking was the earth; here it is a time.**

### شَىْءٌ عَظِيمٌ — leaving it undefined

**The sentence ends with *shayʾun ʿaẓīm*: "a great thing".**

**The choice of word is striking, and I record it as an observation about word order:** *shayʾ* is the most general, the most contentless word in Arabic — "a thing". It is not described; it is not **named**. Then comes the adjective: *ʿaẓīm.*

**The first sentence therefore does not describe the event; it reports only its magnitude.** The description is left to the second verse — and that description will not be a description of an event but **a human scene**.

### تَذْهَلُ — root: ذ-ه-ل

**The root occurs here for the first time in the index.** The sense the lexicographers give: **to be parted from something in terror and bewilderment; the mind's being severed from it.**

What distinguishes the root is that it differs from ordinary forgetting (*nisyān*). **The lexicographers generally give the difference thus:** *nisyān* is the erasure of knowledge; *dhuhūl* is the forcible tearing away of attention **while the knowledge stays in place**. The person knows, but can no longer look there.

**The verse does not say *tansā* (she forgets), it says *tadhhalu*.** The mother does not forget her child — **she is severed from it.**

### The scene of the nursing mother

**The burden the mother carries has been treated in two places in this commentary, and this place is their opposite:**

| Place | What is described | The words |
|---|---|---|
| `046-ahkaf.md` 46/15 | **The hardship of carrying and of giving birth** | *Ḥamalathu ummuhu **kurhan** wa waḍaʿathu **kurhā*** |
| `031-lokman.md` 31/14 | **Weakness laid upon weakness** | *Ḥamalathu ummuhu **wahnan ʿalā wahn*** |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/2** | **The snapping of the bond** | *Tadhhalu kullu **murḍiʿatin** ʿammā arḍaʿat* |

**In al-Aḥqāf and Luqmān the mother was named together with the hardship she endured; here the same relationship is named at the moment it breaks.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared subject of the three verses:** in the two sūras motherhood was used as the proof of **man's most durable bond** — that was the ground of the counsel given. **al-Ḥajj makes that bond a unit of measurement:** the magnitude of the shaking, since it cannot be told on its own, is measured by **the moment the strongest bond snaps**.

### مُرْضِعَة — why this pattern

**The choice of word carries a grammatical point, and the lexicographers record it.**

In Arabic a distinction is observed between **مُرْضِع** (*murḍiʿ*, without the *tāʾ*) and **مُرْضِعَة** (*murḍiʿa*, with the *tāʾ*):

| Pattern | The distinction the lexicographers give |
|---|---|
| **مُرْضِع** | **An attribute** — a woman who bears the quality of nursing; she need not actually be nursing at that moment |
| **مُرْضِعَة** | **A woman actually nursing at that moment** — the breast in the mouth |

**The verse chooses the second.** The scene is therefore not "a woman who has a child" but **a woman nursing her child at that very moment**.

**I transmit this distinction as the lexicographers transmit it;** I do not present it as a fixed rule, because the sources themselves debate whether it holds everywhere. **But that it suits the context is plain:** the force of the scene comes from the severing happening **in mid-act**.

### وَتَضَعُ كُلُّ ذَاتِ حَمْلٍ حَمْلَهَا

"Every pregnant woman will set down her burden."

**The repetition of the word must be recorded: *ḥamlin … ḥamlahā*** — the same root twice, one indefinite, one definite.

**And the verb *taḍaʿu*:** the same verb was used of giving birth at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/15 (*wa waḍaʿathu kurhā*). **There birth was a natural outcome arriving at the end of thirty months; here it comes before its time, in terror.**

### سُكَٰرَىٰ وَمَا هُم بِسُكَٰرَىٰ

"You will see people drunk, though they are not drunk."

**This is the most striking movement of word order in the sūra, and it deserves to be dwelt on in detail.**

Simile is frequent in the Qurʾān. **What happens here is different: a simile is built, and then withdrawn inside the same sentence.** First *sukārā* (drunk) is said, and immediately after *wa mā hum bi-sukārā* (they are not drunk).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the sentence:**

- **The simile is necessary**, because the state described has no name in human speech. The nearest counterpart is drunkenness: the loss of balance, the blurring of perception, the failure of self-command.
- **The simile is insufficient**, because drunkenness is the result of a substance and is temporary. **The cause of the state here is not in a substance.**
- **For this reason the verse adds a third sentence:** *wa lākinna ʿadhāba'llāhi shadīd* — the cause is stated.

**There are thus three steps: liken, correct, give the cause.** The simile is not erased — **its boundary is drawn.** Having taken the picture, the reader also learns how far the picture holds.

**This procedure is of the same kind as the stance recorded at `039-zumer.md` 39/67:** there too a depiction is made, and then, **within the same verse**, a note of transcendence is entered. **In both places the text itself enters the qualification upon its own simile.**

### A note on variant readings

It is transmitted that the word **سُكَٰرَىٰ** is also read as **سَكْرَىٰ** (*sakrā*). **I do not name a reader.** No decisive difference of meaning arises between the two readings: one is a plural pattern, the other a singular-adjectival pattern used of a group.

### What this says to the present

The verse's method of measuring teaches something in itself: **the way to convey a vast event is not to line up vast words.**

The verse does not say "it is dreadful, it is terrifying." **It shows the distance between a mother and her infant.** The scale is taken from the place a person knows best.

## 22/3-4 — وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يُجَٰدِلُ فِى ٱللَّهِ بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ وَيَتَّبِعُ كُلَّ شَيْطَٰنٍ مَّرِيدٍ · كُتِبَ عَلَيْهِ أَنَّهُۥ مَن تَوَلَّاهُ فَأَنَّهُۥ يُضِلُّهُۥ وَيَهْدِيهِ إِلَىٰ عَذَابِ ٱلسَّعِيرِ

*Wa mina'n-nāsi man yujādilu fi'llāhi bi-ghayri ʿilmin wa yattabiʿu kulla shayṭānin marīd · Kutiba ʿalayhi annahu man tawallāhu fa-annahu yuḍilluhu wa yahdīhi ilā ʿadhābi's-saʿīr*

"Among the people is one who **disputes about God without knowledge** and follows every **rebellious** satan. Of him it is written: whoever takes him as a friend, **he leads him astray and guides him to the punishment of the blaze.**"

### وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن — a pattern of the sūra

**This pattern occurs three times in the sūra: 3, 8, 11.** Each of the three opens a portrait of a man, and the three are different.

| Verse | The portrait | The distinguishing trait |
|---|---|---|
| **3** | The disputer | *Bi-ghayri ʿilm* — without knowledge; **he follows another** |
| **8** | The disputer | *Bi-ghayri ʿilmin wa lā hudan wa lā kitābin munīr* — groundless three times over; **he leads himself** |
| **11** | The one who serves at the edge | *ʿAlā ḥarf* — **he has taken no side** |

**The sequence itself carries information, and I record it as an observation about word order:** the man in the third verse is a **follower** (*yattabiʿu*), the man in the eighth is one who **leads astray** (*li-yuḍilla*), and the man in the eleventh is one who **calculates his own account**. The same pattern, three separate positions.

**The root ج-د-ل was analysed at `002-bakara.md` 2/197:** **to twist a rope tight**; *jadīl* — a firmly plaited rope; in wrestling, to throw one's opponent to the ground. **I do not repeat it here.** **In al-Baqara this word stood among the things forbidden during the ḥajj** (*wa lā jidāla fi'l-ḥajj*) — and this sūra is the sūra of the ḥajj. **That the word is placed here is worth recording.**

**مَّرِيد** — root **م-ر-د**: the core sense the lexicographers give is **bareness, having nothing left upon one**. *Amrad* — a youth whose beard has not come; *shajaratun mardāʾ* — a leafless tree. From this comes the sense of being **stripped of every good**. *Mārid* and *marīd* are of the same root; so is *mutamarrid* (obstinate).

**The picture in the word must be recorded:** rebelliousness is described here not as an excess but as **a stripping** — having no covering left to hold on to.

### يُضِلُّهُۥ وَيَهْدِيهِ

**The most striking place in the fourth verse is the juxtaposition of two verbs:** *yuḍilluhu* (he leads him astray) and *yahdīhi* (he guides him).

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the opposition of the verbs:** the verb *hidāya* is used here not for something positive but for **conducting to punishment**. The root does not by itself mean "to bring to what is right"; it means **to direct somewhere**. The direction is learned from the end of the sentence: *ilā ʿadhābi's-saʿīr.*

**The sense of "directing" in the same root was recorded in `048-fetih.md` when the word *hady* (the sacrificial animal — the one sent to its place) was treated.** I rely on that treatment. **And the word *hady* will occur in this sūra too** (it will be taken up in the section 22/33-37) — the same root, in two separate places in the same sūra.

## 22/5 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ إِن كُنتُمْ فِى رَيْبٍ مِّنَ ٱلْبَعْثِ فَإِنَّا خَلَقْنَٰكُم مِّن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ مِن نُّطْفَةٍ ثُمَّ مِنْ عَلَقَةٍ ثُمَّ مِن مُّضْغَةٍ مُّخَلَّقَةٍ وَغَيْرِ مُخَلَّقَةٍ … وَتَرَى ٱلْأَرْضَ هَامِدَةً فَإِذَآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَآءَ ٱهْتَزَّتْ وَرَبَتْ وَأَنۢبَتَتْ مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍۭ بَهِيجٍ

*Yā ayyuha'n-nāsu in kuntum fī raybin mina'l-baʿthi fa-innā khalaqnākum min turābin thumma min nuṭfatin thumma min ʿalaqatin thumma min muḍghatin mukhallaqatin wa ghayri mukhallaqatin li-nubayyina lakum · Wa nuqirru fi'l-arḥāmi mā nashāʾu ilā ajalin musamman thumma nukhrijukum ṭiflan thumma li-tablughū ashuddakum · Wa minkum man yutawaffā wa minkum man yuraddu ilā ardhali'l-ʿumuri li-kaylā yaʿlama min baʿdi ʿilmin shayʾā · Wa tara'l-arḍa hāmidatan fa-idhā anzalnā ʿalayha'l-māʾa'htazzat wa rabat wa anbatat min kulli zawjin bahīj*

"**O people!** If you are in doubt about the raising of the dead — **We created you from dust, then from a drop, then from an *ʿalaqa*, then from a *muḍgha* whose form is determined and undetermined; that We might make it plain to you.** We keep in the wombs what We will for an appointed term, then We bring you forth as an infant; then you reach your full strength. **Some of you are taken in death, and some of you are turned back to the most abject age of life — so that after having known he should know nothing.** **You see the earth burnt out; and when We send down water upon it, it stirs and swells and brings forth every delightful kind.**"

### The building of the verse: two proofs for one doubt

**The verse opens with a conditional sentence:** *in kuntum fī raybin mina'l-baʿth* — "if you are in doubt about the raising of the dead".

**And the answer comes from two directions:**

| Proof | Where | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **The first** | Man's own formation | Coming to be not out of nothing, but by passing **through stages** |
| **The second** | Parched ground | The reviving of **what appears to have died** |

**What the two have in common must be recorded, and I give it as my own reading:** both proofs are things **the addressee sees**. The verse does not begin by giving news of the unseen; **it begins by pointing to what is visible.** The question asked was "how can something that has not happened happen?"; the answer takes the form "look at what has already happened."

### The stages, and the limit set by the method

**The verse names four items:** *turāb*, *nuṭfa*, *ʿalaqa*, *muḍgha*. Then three steps of life: *ṭifl*, *ashudd*, and two ends — death, or *ardhalu'l-ʿumur.*

**At `040-gafir.md` 40/67 the same staged forming was treated in six steps, and a limit set by the method was drawn there. At `039-zumer.md` 39/6 (*khalqan min baʿdi khalqin fī ẓulumātin thalāth*) the same limit was repeated. I keep the same limit here, and repeat it with its ground:**

**The dictionary meanings the lexicographers give for the words are these:**

| Word | Root | The sense the lexicographers give |
|---|---|---|
| **نُطْفَة** | ن-ط-ف | **A drop** — a small quantity of water, a seeping fluid |
| **عَلَقَة** | ع-ل-ق | **A thing that hangs and clings**; the root carries "to hang, to attach". *ʿAlaq* — a leech; *taʿlīq* — hanging up |
| **مُضْغَة** | م-ض-غ | **A chewed morsel**; a piece of flesh the size of a mouthful chewed in the mouth |

**These are the meanings the lexicographers record, and that is as much as I transmit. I do not enter into equating them one-to-one with the terms of modern embryology.** The ground: such an equation would load onto the verse a claim the verse does not make; and it would leave the verse to be defended alongside scientific descriptions whenever those change. **The proof the verse builds is not a claim of terminological accuracy.**

**How the proof works is plain in the verse's own sentence: *li-nubayyina lakum* — "that We might make it plain to you".** What is made plain is **the graded character of a process**. The passage from one stage to the next is not in human hands, and man has witnessed none of its stages. **The same line as that treated at `056-vakia.md` 56/58-59: the separation of man's share from the outcome.**

### مُّخَلَّقَةٍ وَغَيْرِ مُخَلَّقَةٍ

**There is disagreement over this construct in the classical commentaries. I transmit the views; I do not impose a preference:**

| View | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Formed / unformed** — whether the forming is completed or not |
| 2 | **Complete / incomplete** — that which reaches the end of the process and that which does not |
| 3 | **Faultless / faulty** — a difference in the creating |

**The common point is this, and it comes out of the verse itself:** not every process that begins ends in the same place. **The verse builds a regular series, then adds the exception to that series itself** — just as in the note *wa minkum man yutawaffā min qabl* at `040-gafir.md` 40/67. **And here the same note comes twice:** in the womb (*mukhallaqatin wa ghayri mukhallaqa*), and after birth (*wa minkum man yutawaffā*).

### أَرْذَلِ ٱلْعُمُرِ — "the most abject age of life"

The root **ر-ذ-ل**: the lowest part of a thing, the part that is picked out and thrown away. *Ardhal* — the most worthless, what is cast aside.

**And the rest of the sentence must be recorded:** *li-kaylā yaʿlama min baʿdi ʿilmin shayʾā* — "so that after having known he should know nothing."

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is where the sentence stands in the verse:** the verse builds an ascending series — a drop, an infant, the age of strength — **and places a descent at the end of the series.** Knowledge is acquired, then taken back.

**And this serves the verse's real proof:** man has no mastery over his own body either at the beginning or at the end. The age of strength in the middle is an exception. **The one who objects to the raising of the dead is the party who has no say at either end of his own life.**

### هَامِدَة — root: ه-م-د

**The root occurs here for the first time in the index.** The sense the lexicographers give: **the going out of a fire, the cooling of ashes; the ending of motion and of sound.** *Hamadati'n-nār* — the fire went out. *Hāmid* — lifeless, motionless.

**The choice of word builds a picture:** dry earth is called not "dead" (*mayyita*) but **"burnt out"**. **That is, the present state of something that once burned.**

**Comparing this word with *khāshiʿa* at `041-fussilet.md` 41/39 shows how two sūras give the same proof in two separate pictures:**

| Place | Word | Root | The picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/39 | **خَاشِعَة** | خ-ش-ع | **Head bowed** — lowered, its voice cut off. In the Qurʾān the word is generally used of **man** |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/5** | **هَامِدَة** | ه-م-د | **Burnt out** — its fire gone, no stirring left in it |
| `039-zumer.md` 39/21 | **حُطَام** | ح-ط-م | **Broken stubble** — snapped and crumbled |

**The three words face in different directions, and I record this as an observation:**

- **Fuṣṣilat** gives the ground a **posture**: bowed.
- **al-Ḥajj** gives the ground a **past**: burnt out, that is, it was burning before.
- **al-Zumar** gives the ground an **end**: crumbled.

**And all three are tied to the same conclusion.** In Fuṣṣilat, *inna'lladhī aḥyāhā la-muḥyi'l-mawtā*; here, at 22/6, *wa annahu yuḥyi'l-mawtā*; in al-Zumar, *dhikrā li-uli'l-albāb.*

### ٱهْتَزَّتْ وَرَبَتْ — identical with Fuṣṣilat

**These two verbs are the same two verbs that occur at `041-fussilet.md` 41/39:** *ihtazzat wa rabat.*

**The only difference between the two verses is the presence of a third verb:**

| | Fuṣṣilat 41/39 | **al-Ḥajj 22/5** |
|---|---|---|
| The state of the ground | *Khāshiʿa* | *Hāmida* |
| The first movement | *Ihtazzat* — **it stirred** | *Ihtazzat* |
| The second movement | *Wa rabat* — **it swelled** | *Wa rabat* |
| The third | — | *Wa anbatat min kulli zawjin **bahīj*** |

**al-Ḥajj thus takes one step beyond where Fuṣṣilat stops: it gives the outcome as well.**

**اِهْتَزَّ** — root **ه-ز-ز**: to shake, to jolt. The root **ر-ب-و** was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/275 in the discussion of *ribā*: **to increase, to swell, to rise.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**And the whole construct is worth recording:** the first verse of the sūra opened with *zalzala* — a shaking. **In the fifth verse the ground is shaken again** (*ihtazzat*) — but this time the outcome of the shaking is vegetation.

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the presence of the two words in the same sūra four verses apart:** the same family of verbs, one for ruin and one for revival. **Being shaken is not in itself an end; it depends on what is shaken.**

### زَوْجٍۭ بَهِيجٍ

The root **ب-ه-ج**: **beauty, a brightness pleasing to the eye, the giving of joy.** *Bahja* — beauty, gladness.

**The choice of word must be recorded as an observation:** the verse speaks not of the plant's usefulness (*nāfiʿ*) but of its **appearance**. The proof is built on **pleasing the eye** before filling the stomach.

## 22/6-7 — ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلْحَقُّ وَأَنَّهُۥ يُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ وَأَنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ · وَأَنَّ ٱلسَّاعَةَ ءَاتِيَةٌ لَّا رَيْبَ فِيهَا وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَبْعَثُ مَن فِى ٱلْقُبُورِ

*Dhālika bi-anna'llāha huwa'l-ḥaqqu wa annahu yuḥyi'l-mawtā wa annahu ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr · Wa anna's-sāʿata ātiyatun lā rayba fīhā wa anna'llāha yabʿathu man fi'l-qubūr*

"**So it is; because God is the truth itself, because it is He who gives life to the dead, and because His power extends over everything. And the Hour is coming, there is no doubt in it; and God will raise those who are in the graves.**"

### A list of five conclusions

**The fifth verse was a long proof; these two verses set out the conclusions that follow from it. Five items, each joined by *anna*:**

| Order | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Anna'llāha huwa'l-ḥaqq* — **God is the truth** |
| 2 | *Wa annahu yuḥyi'l-mawtā* — **He gives life to the dead** |
| 3 | *Wa annahu ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr* — **His power extends over everything** |
| 4 | *Wa anna's-sāʿata ātiya* — **the Hour is coming** |
| 5 | *Wa anna'llāha yabʿathu man fi'l-qubūr* — **He will raise those in the graves** |

**The logic of the order is worth recording, and I give it as an observation about word order:** the list begins with **the agent** (who), moves to **the act** (what He does), attaches to **power** (can He do it), then descends to **time** (when), and ends with **extent** (whom).

**And the note *lā rayba fīhā* in the fifth item is an answer to the first word of the fifth verse:** that verse opened *in kuntum fī **raybin***. **The same root returns two verses later, in the form of a denial.** This is a link that can be verified from the text.

## 22/8-10 — وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يُجَٰدِلُ فِى ٱللَّهِ بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ وَلَا هُدًى وَلَا كِتَٰبٍ مُّنِيرٍ · ثَانِىَ عِطْفِهِۦ لِيُضِلَّ عَن سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ · ذَٰلِكَ بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ يَدَاكَ وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَيْسَ بِظَلَّٰمٍ لِّلْعَبِيدِ

*Wa mina'n-nāsi man yujādilu fi'llāhi bi-ghayri ʿilmin wa lā hudan wa lā kitābin munīr · Thāniya ʿiṭfihi li-yuḍilla ʿan sabīli'llāh · Lahu fi'd-dunyā khizyun wa nudhīquhu yawma'l-qiyāmati ʿadhāba'l-ḥarīq · Dhālika bimā qaddamat yadāka wa anna'llāha laysa bi-ẓallāmin li'l-ʿabīd*

"And among the people is one who disputes about God **without any knowledge, without any guide, without any illuminating book**; **turning his side away**, in order to lead men astray from the way of God. For him there is **disgrace** in this world; and on the Day of Resurrection We shall make him taste the punishment of burning. '**This is for what your hands sent ahead; and God is never one to wrong His servants.**'"

### The refusal of three grounds

**In the third verse there was a single negation: *bi-ghayri ʿilm*. Here it becomes three.**

| The ground refused | What it would have been |
|---|---|
| *ʿIlm* | **His own knowledge** |
| *Hudan* | **Someone who guides** — direction from outside |
| *Kitābin munīr* | **A written, illuminating text** |

**The three close off every source of knowledge, and I record this as an observation about word order:** a man takes a view either by his own investigation, or from a teacher, or from a text. **The verse eliminates all three, one by one.** One thing remains, and it comes in the very next word: **a posture.**

### ثَانِىَ عِطْفِهِۦ — "turning his side away"

**This is the most concrete description of the body in the sūra.**

The root **ث-ن-ي**: **to bend, to fold, to double over.** *Ithnayn* (two), *tathniya* (the dual), *istithnāʾ* (to set a thing apart by bending it) are of the same root.

**عِطْف** — root **ع-ط-ف**: to lean, to bend, to incline to one side. *ʿIṭf* is a man's **flank, his side, the line of shoulder and neck**. *ʿAṭfa* — a bend in a road. From the same root comes *ʿāṭifa* (tenderness — a leaning towards someone).

**The construct is, exactly: "one who bends his side".** That is, to turn the body aside while speaking, to swing the shoulder round, to take up the posture of a man who will not listen.

**Two explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Sheering off in arrogance** — turning the shoulder in pride |
| 2 | **Turning the face away and going** — an avoidance of listening |

**Both describe the same movement of the body;** one looks to the cause, the other to the effect. **I do not impose a preference.**

**And where the construct stands in the verse is worth recording:** it comes immediately after the three sources of knowledge are refused. **A posture is put in the place of a ground.**

**A description of the body of the same kind was treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/51:** *naʾā bi-jānibih* — "he removed his side". **The same region (the side, the flank), the same movement.** I rely on that treatment. **The difference is this:** in Fuṣṣilat the movement was made **when a favour was given**; here it is made **during a dispute**.

### The change of address — *iltifāt*

**There is a movement of word order in the tenth verse, and it must be recorded:** the ninth verse spoke in the third person (*lahu*, *nudhīquhu*). **The tenth verse shifts abruptly to the second person:** *bimā qaddamat **yadāka*** — "your hands".

This is the usage called **iltifāt** (a shift in the direction of address) in Arabic.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the change of pronoun:** throughout the account the man was described from a distance; **at the moment of the verdict, he is faced directly.** A general portrait turns into a single addressee.

### لَيْسَ بِظَلَّٰمٍ لِّلْعَبِيدِ

**ظَلَّام** is an intensive form: "one who wrongs greatly".

**The lexicographers dwell on the pattern, and two explanations are transmitted:**

| Explanation | Ground |
|---|---|
| 1 | The intensive is due to the **plural *ʿabīd*** (servants): wrong done to a great many servants is a great wrong |
| 2 | The intensive widens the scope of the negation: **if even a little is absent, much is absent altogether** |

**I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.** **The same construct also occurred at `041-fussilet.md` 41/46** (*wa mā rabbuka bi-ẓallāmin li'l-ʿabīd*).

## 22/11 — وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَعْبُدُ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ حَرْفٍ فَإِنْ أَصَابَهُۥ خَيْرٌ ٱطْمَأَنَّ بِهِۦ وَإِنْ أَصَابَتْهُ فِتْنَةٌ ٱنقَلَبَ عَلَىٰ وَجْهِهِۦ خَسِرَ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱلْءَاخِرَةَ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ ٱلْخُسْرَانُ ٱلْمُبِينُ

*Wa mina'n-nāsi man yaʿbudu'llāha ʿalā ḥarf · Fa-in aṣābahu khayruni'ṭmaʾanna bih · Wa in aṣābathu fitnatuni'nqalaba ʿalā wajhih · Khasira'd-dunyā wa'l-ākhirata dhālika huwa'l-khusrānu'l-mubīn*

"And among the people is one who serves God **on an edge**. If good befalls him he **settles** with it; if a trial befalls him he **turns about on his face.** He has lost this world and the next. That is the manifest loss."

### عَلَىٰ حَرْفٍ — the concrete picture of the word

The root **ح-ر-ف**: **the edge, the tip, the brink of a thing; a sharp rim.**

**The derivatives of the root complete the picture:**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **حَرْف** | Edge, tip, boundary line |
| **حَرْف** (in language) | **Letter** — the sound-unit at the edge of a word |
| **حِرْفَة** | Profession, craft — the side a man turns towards |
| **إِنْحِرَاف** | Deviation, departure from the straight |
| **تَحْرِيف** | Drawing aside, shifting, distorting |
| **مُحَارِف** | One whose earnings are scant, whose affairs run against him |

**The concrete usages the lexicographers record: *ḥarfu'l-jabal* — the edge of the mountain, the lip of the precipice. *Ḥarfu's-safīna* — the gunwale of the ship. The word therefore conveys being not inside a thing but at its edge.**

**The concrete picture is this:** a soldier standing not in the middle of an army but on its outermost line. **He is there not in order to flee but in order to be able to flee.** If the army holds he takes his share of the spoils; if it breaks he withdraws at once. **Or else:** a man sitting on the very lip of a rock — his weight not fully committed, half of him over the void.

**I give this picture as an observation about the language;** the dictionary sense of the word carries it, and similar images are transmitted in the classical commentaries as well.

### Two conditional clauses — a symmetry

**The verse builds the two conditions in exact symmetry:**

| | The first condition | The second condition |
|---|---|---|
| The verb | *Aṣābahu* | *Aṣābathu* |
| What comes | *Khayrun* — **good** | *Fitnatun* — **a trial** |
| The response | *Iṭmaʾanna bih* — **he settled with it** | *Inqalaba ʿalā wajhih* — **he turned about on his face** |

**The symmetry is worth recording, and I give it as an observation about word order:** the same verb (*aṣāba*), the same pattern, the same length. **The only thing that changes is what arrives — and the man's entire position is determined by it.**

**ٱطْمَأَنَّ** — root **ط-م-ن**: to settle, to come to rest in place. **The root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/260** (*li-yaṭmaʾinna qalbī*) — and there the lexicographers' picture, "a thing settling into a hollow and becoming fixed", was recorded. **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the comparison matters:** in al-Baqara *iṭmiʾnān* was Abraham's **request**, and it was granted. **Here the same word becomes the name of a weakness** — because the cause of the settling is different. **There it was a proof; here it is *khayr*.**

**ٱنقَلَبَ عَلَىٰ وَجْهِهِ** — *inqilāb*: to be turned over, to have one's underside brought up. **The construct means, exactly, "he turned about upon his face"** — a return from the direction he faced, and a fall with it.

**I record this as an observation:** for a man standing at the edge, "turning about on his face" is a natural outcome. **The choice of word agrees with the first word (*ḥarf*):** the turning of one who stands at the edge is not a walk but a fall.

### A comparison of three portraits

**Two portraits of the same kind have been treated in this commentary. Setting the three side by side shows the difference between them:**

| | `029-ankebut.md` 29/10-11 | `041-fussilet.md` 41/49-51 | **al-Ḥajj 22/11** |
|---|---|---|---|
| The man's words | *Āmannā bi'llāh* — **a claim of faith is present** | (No words; conduct) | (No words; **service is actually present**) |
| The trigger | *Ūdhiya fi'llāh* — **harm** | *Massahu'sh-sharr* / *anʿamnā* — **from both directions** | *Khayr* / *fitna* — **from both directions** |
| The error | **A slip of scale** — equating a man's hardship with the punishment of God | **A slip of direction** — the capacity is there, but used only in asking | **A slip of position** — standing at the edge |
| What the verse calls it | *Jaʿala fitnata'n-nāsi ka-ʿadhābi'llāh* | *Yaʾūsun qanūṭ* | *ʿAlā ḥarf* |

**What the three share and where they differ must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:**

**What they share:** in none of the three is there denial. All three carry an attachment of some kind. **The break arises not from the absence of attachment but from what the attachment has been made conditional upon.**

**The difference lies in the kind of measure:** in al-ʿAnkabūt the **weighing** is wrong — two weights have been equated. In Fuṣṣilat the **direction** is wrong — the capacity is there, and is spent on asking. **In al-Ḥajj the place is wrong** — the man has made no choice between the centre and the edge.

**And the portrait in al-Ḥajj parts from the other two at one point:** here the man's **service actually continues** (*yaʿbudu'llāha*). The verb is not negated. **The verse is therefore not disputing the presence of worship but the ground on which the worship rests.**

### What this says to the present

What the verse describes is an **attribute**, not a group. **Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I record this plainly:** whoever bears this attribute is included in the description; no wholesale ruling is issued about any community.

And the test of the attribute is given within the verse itself: **to settle when good comes, to withdraw when a trial comes.** The measure is not what a man does while he is comfortable; **it is where he stands when he is pressed.**

## 22/12-13 — يَدْعُوا۟ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُۥ وَمَا لَا يَنفَعُهُۥ · يَدْعُوا۟ لَمَن ضَرُّهُۥٓ أَقْرَبُ مِن نَّفْعِهِۦ لَبِئْسَ ٱلْمَوْلَىٰ وَلَبِئْسَ ٱلْعَشِيرُ

*Yadʿū min dūni'llāhi mā lā yaḍurruhu wa mā lā yanfaʿuh · Dhālika huwa'ḍ-ḍalālu'l-baʿīd · Yadʿū la-man ḍarruhu aqrabu min nafʿih · La-biʾsa'l-mawlā wa la-biʾsa'l-ʿashīr*

"He calls, besides God, upon what **can neither harm him nor benefit him**. That is the far straying. **He calls upon one whose harm is nearer than his benefit.** **What a wretched protector, what a wretched companion!**"

### Two verses, two separate rulings

**The twelfth and thirteenth verses open with the same verb (*yadʿū*) but say two separate things. The difference must be recorded:**

| Verse | The ruling |
|---|---|
| **12** | What is called upon **can give neither harm nor benefit** — it is wholly without effect |
| **13** | The harm of what is called upon **is nearer than its benefit** — not without effect, but effective in the other direction |

**This is not a contradiction but a gradation, and I record it as an observation about word order:** the first verse describes **the object itself** — it has no power of its own. The second describes **the outcome of the relationship** — the man who enters that relationship is harmed.

**The harm therefore comes not from the object but from the attachment.** The object is without effect; attaching oneself to it is not without effect.

**أَقْرَبُ** — "nearer". **The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say its harm is **greater** than its benefit, it says it is **nearer**. The comparison is made not by quantity but by **distance**.

**مَوْلَىٰ** and **عَشِير** — two names for a relationship:

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **مَوْلَىٰ** | و-ل-ي | **Nearness, guardianship** — one who protects, who sees to a man's affairs |
| **عَشِير** | ع-ش-ر | **One who lives alongside** — *muʿāshara*, *ʿashīra* are of the same root; one who shares the daily round |

**The two are two separate kinds of relation:** one **vertical** (protection, taking charge), the other **horizontal** (companionship, being together). **The verse negates both.**

**And a link to the end of the sūra must be drawn:** the seventy-eighth verse will close *huwa **mawlākum** fa-niʿma'l-**mawlā***. **The same word stands at the two ends of the sūra: here *la-biʾsa'l-mawlā*, there *niʿma'l-mawlā*.** This is a contrast that can be verified from the text.

## 22/14 — إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُدْخِلُ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ جَنَّٰتٍ تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يُرِيدُ

*Inna'llāha yudkhilu'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti jannātin tajrī min taḥtiha'l-anhār · Inna'llāha yafʿalu mā yurīd*

"God brings those who believe and do righteous deeds into **gardens beneath which rivers flow**. God does what He wills."

### Where it is placed

**This verse comes immediately after the three negative portraits (3, 8, 11)** and sums up the other side in a single sentence.

**I record this as an observation about word order:** three verses set out three separate forms of deviation in detail — disputing without knowledge, turning the face away in arrogance, standing at the edge. **The other side is not set out in detail:** two attributes, one sentence. **Faith and deed.**

**Deviation has kinds; the straight course has none.** This is an observation that can be verified from the proportions of the verses.

### إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يُرِيدُ

**That this sentence stands in this verse is striking.** The pattern generally comes in contexts that speak of power; **here it is placed at the end of a sentence about reward.**

**A similar sentence will come in the eighteenth verse of the same sūra:** *inna'llāha yafʿalu mā **yashāʾ***. **There is a difference of word between the two sentences, and the lexicographers record it:** the distinction between *irāda* and *mashīʾa* has been debated in the classical sources, and there is no agreement upon it. **I do not enter into that debate; I record only that both words are used in the same sūra.**

## 22/15 — مَن كَانَ يَظُنُّ أَن لَّن يَنصُرَهُ ٱللَّهُ فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ فَلْيَمْدُدْ بِسَبَبٍ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ ثُمَّ لْيَقْطَعْ فَلْيَنظُرْ هَلْ يُذْهِبَنَّ كَيْدُهُۥ مَا يَغِيظُ

*Man kāna yaẓunnu an lan yanṣurahu'llāhu fi'd-dunyā wa'l-ākhirati fa'l-yamdud bi-sababin ila's-samāʾi thumma'l-yaqṭaʿ · Fa'l-yanẓur hal yudhhibanna kayduhu mā yaghīẓ*

"Whoever supposes that God **will not help him** in this world and the next, **let him stretch a rope to the sky, then let him cut it;** then let him look whether his scheme takes away **what enrages him**."

### The disagreement over the verse

**This is the most disputed verse in the sūra as regards its understanding. The disagreement lies at three separate points, and each must be recorded separately.**

**The first point — to whom does the pronoun in *yanṣuruhu* refer?**

| View | Reading |
|---|---|
| A | **To the Prophet** — "one who supposes God will not help him (the Prophet)" |
| B | **To the man himself** — "one who supposes God will not help him" |

**The second point — what is the *sabab*?**

| View | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | **A rope, a cord** — the concrete sense of the root. *Sabab* is originally "that by which a place is reached"; the lexicographers give the first sense as **rope** |
| B | **A way of ascent to the sky, a means** — figuratively "a cause, an instrument" |

**The third point — what does *fa'l-yaqṭaʿ* (let him cut) cut?**

| View | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | **Let him cut the rope** — let him hang himself, that is, let him finish his rage upon himself |
| B | **Let him cut off the coming of revelation** — let him reach up to the sky and stop what comes down from it |
| C | **Let him cut the distance** — let him traverse the road as far as the sky |

**Several readings follow from the combination of these three points. I set out in a table those most prominent in the sources:**

| Reading | How the verse as a whole is understood |
|---|---|
| **1** | **"If he can, let him climb to the sky and cut off the help."** The man wants to prevent God's help; the verse proposes to him an impossible task. **The proposal is made in order to show the impossibility** |
| **2** | **"If he is bursting with rage, let him hang himself."** The verse says that rage will not change the fact; whatever the man does, the thing that enrages him will remain in place |
| **3** | **"If he does not expect help from God, let him seek it from some other place than the sky."** The verse shows that there is no alternative |

**I impose none of these as a preference. All three are transmitted in the sources, and none of them decisively excludes the others.**

**The one thing that is certain is the last word of the sentence:** *hal yudhhibanna kayduhu mā **yaghīẓ***. **The question is rhetorical and its answer is no.** Whichever reading is taken, then, the verse arrives at the same place: **rage does not remove what it rages at.**

### كَيْد and غَيْظ

The root **ك-ي-د**: **a hidden plan, a cunning scheme**; the lexicographers also record in the root the sense of "being hard pressed, straining with difficulty" (*kāda yakīdu* — to be in the throes of death).

The root **غ-ي-ظ**: **rage that boils within.** The lexicographers observe a difference between *ghayẓ* and *ghaḍab*, and generally give it thus: *ghaḍab* is rage that shows itself, that has an effect; *ghayẓ* is rage that **stays inside and burns the man himself**. **I transmit this distinction as it is transmitted.**

**And the choice of the word carries the whole verse; I give this as my own reading:** if *ghayẓ* is the rage that stays inside, then **the man's stretching a rope to the sky produces no result outside either.** A state that remains within moves to change something without, and the verse leaves the outcome as a question.

**مَا يَغِيظُ** — "what enrages him". **What that thing is is not said.** The verse leaves the object of the rage empty and describes only the mechanism. **The consequence is this:** the description works without being tied to a single event.

## 22/16 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ ءَايَٰتٍۭ بَيِّنَٰتٍ وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَهْدِى مَن يُرِيدُ

*Wa kadhālika anzalnāhu āyātin bayyināt · Wa anna'llāha yahdī man yurīd*

"Thus have We sent it down as **clear signs**. And God guides whom He wills."

**The verse closes the second block.** One of the three grounds refused in the eighth verse was *kitābin munīr* (an illuminating book). **The sixteenth verse reports that that book has been sent down.**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and its ground is the shared vocabulary of the two verses:** *munīr* (illuminating) and *bayyināt* (clear) do the same work. **What was lacking in the eighth verse is therefore supplied in the sixteenth:** the ground is not absent — **it has not been taken up.**

## 22/17 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَٱلَّذِينَ هَادُوا۟ وَٱلصَّٰبِـِٔينَ وَٱلنَّصَٰرَىٰ وَٱلْمَجُوسَ وَٱلَّذِينَ أَشْرَكُوٓا۟ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَفْصِلُ بَيْنَهُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ شَهِيدٌ

*Inna'lladhīna āmanū wa'lladhīna hādū wa'ṣ-ṣābiʾīna wa'n-naṣārā wa'l-majūsa wa'lladhīna ashrakū · Inna'llāha yafṣilu baynahum yawma'l-qiyāma · Inna'llāha ʿalā kulli shayʾin shahīd*

"**Those who believe, the Jews, the Sabians, the Christians, the Magians, and those who associate others with God** — God **will separate between them** on the Day of Resurrection. God is surely witness over everything."

### Six groups, and what the list does

**The verse names six communities. This is the widest list named together in the Qurʾān.**

**And what the list does is understood from what it does not do. I record this carefully:**

- **The verse issues no ruling about any of the groups.** It does not say who is right and who is wrong.
- **The verse says when the judgement will be given and by whom:** *yawma'l-qiyāma*, and the agent is God.
- **The verb is *yafṣilu*** — root **ف-ص-ل**: **to separate, to open a space between two things.** *Faṣl*, *tafṣīl*, *fāṣila* are of the same root. **It does the same work as the root ف-ر-ق treated in `025-furkan.md` on the occasion of *furqān*.**

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I enter a record here, and it is binding:** no wholesale ruling about any religious community may be built on this verse. **The verse itself does not do that; it leaves the judgement to the Resurrection and to God.** This commentary stands at the same limit.

**And this agrees with the general line of the sūra:** what is described throughout the sūra is **attributes** — the one who disputes, the one who stands at the edge, the one who turns his face away. Whoever bears the attribute is included in the description; and those attributes are the monopoly of no community.

### إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ شَهِيدٌ

**The closing attribute matches the subject of the verse exactly.** The one who will make the separation is the one who is **witness over everything**. **The ground of the judgement is therefore not partiality but the completeness of the witnessing.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the choice of the attribute:** the verse does not say *ʿalīm* (knowing) or *ḥakīm* (giving judgement); **it says *shahīd*.** **Witnessing, unlike knowledge, conveys being present at the event.**

## 22/18 — أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَسْجُدُ لَهُۥ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ وَٱلنُّجُومُ وَٱلْجِبَالُ وَٱلشَّجَرُ وَٱلدَّوَآبُّ وَكَثِيرٌ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ وَكَثِيرٌ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ

*A-lam tara anna'llāha yasjudu lahu man fi's-samāwāti wa man fi'l-arḍi wa'sh-shamsu wa'l-qamaru wa'n-nujūmu wa'l-jibālu wa'sh-shajaru wa'd-dawābbu wa kathīrun mina'n-nās · Wa kathīrun ḥaqqa ʿalayhi'l-ʿadhāb · Wa man yuhini'llāhu fa-mā lahu min mukrim · Inna'llāha yafʿalu mā yashāʾ*

"Have you not seen that to God prostrate those in the heavens and those on the earth, **the sun, the moon, the stars, the mountains, the trees, the animals, and many of mankind?** **And for many the punishment has become due.** Whomever God abases, none can honour him. God does what He wills."

### A verse of prostration

**This verse is one of the verses of prostration in recitation.**

**And a record of disagreement is needed here:** **how many verses of prostration this sūra contains** has been debated among the schools of law.

| View | Verse of prostration |
|---|---|
| Some | **18 alone** |
| Some | **18 and 77** — two prostrations in the sūra |

**This is a matter of law; I confine myself to transmitting the views, and I issue no ruling. No record in this commentary is binding.**

### The structure of the list

**The verse names nine items. The order needs to be worked out:**

| Order | Item | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Man fi's-samāwāt* | **Rational beings** — in the heavens |
| 2 | *Man fi'l-arḍ* | **Rational beings** — on the earth |
| 3 | *Al-shams* | **A heavenly body** |
| 4 | *Al-qamar* | **A heavenly body** |
| 5 | *Al-nujūm* | **Heavenly bodies** — plural |
| 6 | *Al-jibāl* | **Inanimate** — on the earth, fixed |
| 7 | *Al-shajar* | **Plant** — on the earth, fixed but alive |
| 8 | *Al-dawābb* | **Animal** — on the earth, moving |
| 9 | *Kathīrun mina'n-nās* | **Man** — some of them |

**The logic of the order must be recorded, and I give it as an observation about word order:**

**First two general categories** (*man fi's-samāwāt*, *man fi'l-arḍ*) — these come with the particle *man*, which is used of rational beings. **Then the list fills in those two categories:** it begins in the heavens and descends to the earth (sun, moon, stars → mountains, trees, animals).

**And the order within the earth carries an arrangement too:** motionless and lifeless (mountain) → motionless and living (tree) → moving and living (*dawābb*). **From the lifeless to the living, from the fixed to the moving.**

**At the end of the series stands man — and man comes with a qualification of his own.**

### Not "most of mankind", but "many of mankind"

**This is the most striking feature of word order in the verse, and it deserves to be dwelt on in detail.**

**There are two separate constructions in Arabic, and their meanings differ:**

| Construction | Its meaning |
|---|---|
| **أَكْثَرُ ٱلنَّاسِ** (*aktharu'n-nās*) | **Most of mankind** — the majority. *Akthar* is an elative: "the more numerous" |
| **كَثِيرٌ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ** (*kathīrun mina'n-nās*) | **Many of mankind** — a numerically large set, but **no proportion is stated** |

**The verse uses the second: *kathīrun mina'n-nās*.**

**And immediately after, the same word returns: *wa kathīrun ḥaqqa ʿalayhi'l-ʿadhāb*.** **Both parties are thus named with the same word: *kathīr* and *kathīr*.**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and its ground is the word itself:**

- **The verse gives no proportion.** It does not say "the majority prostrate", nor "the majority do not".
- **Both sets are qualified by the same adjective: both are *many*.**
- **How the whole is divided is not stated.**

**The Qurʾān uses the pattern *aktharu'n-nās* elsewhere, and there a majority really is reported.** Here that pattern is not chosen.

**As my own reading I add this:** in the list the sun, the moon, the stars, the mountains, the trees and the animals pass **without qualification** — of none of them is "many of them" said. **The qualification appears only at man.** Among all the beings in the list, therefore, **man is the only kind that is divided** — and the verse does not give the proportion of that division, only that it exists.

### يَسْجُدُ — one verb, nine subjects

**The verb is singular and comes first: *yasjudu*.** In Arabic a verb placed before its subjects stays singular; this is the ordinary rule of the language, and it operates here too.

**But the singleness of the verb raises a question: how do a mountain and a man do the same thing?**

**The answers given to this question in the classical commentaries fall under two headings:**

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| A | **Prostration is of two kinds:** prostration without will (a being's conforming to the order of its own creation) and prostration with will (man's choice). The verb is shared, the form differs |
| B | **Prostration here means submission**, and every being submits according to its own nature; man's prostration is in addition a choice |

**The two explanations are close to one another, and both occur in the sources. I do not impose a preference.**

**What can be drawn from the verse itself is this, and I give it as my own reading:** man enters the list **last** and as the **only qualified** item. **Man's prostration is thus positioned as something different from the rest of the list** — because he alone carries the note "many of them".

**At `041-fussilet.md` 41/37 the matter of prostration was treated from another side: there the sun and the moon were named as things not to be prostrated to (*lā tasjudū li'sh-shamsi wa lā li'l-qamar*). Here the same two bodies are on the side that prostrates.**

**Setting the two verses side by side is worth recording:**

| Place | The sun and the moon |
|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/37 | **Not to be prostrated to** — themselves signs, not the target |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/18** | **Prostrating** — inside the list |

**The same two bodies, in two verses, in two separate positions; and the two verses complete one another.** Fuṣṣilat says why they are not to be prostrated to: they are created. al-Ḥajj says the same thing from another side: **they too are among those who prostrate.** This is a link between the two sūras that can be verified from the text.

### وَمَن يُهِنِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِن مُّكْرِمٍ

The root **ه-و-ن**: lightness, worthlessness, abasement. The root **ك-ر-م**: value, generosity, nobility.

**The two roots are set as opposites.** And the structure of the sentence must be recorded: *fa-mā lahu min mukrim* — **"there is none to honour him"**, that is, there is no second authority.

**And where the sentence stands in the verse is meaningful:** it comes immediately after the list of prostration. **Prostration is the most extreme form of lowering — the placing of the face on the ground.** And immediately after describing prostration, the verse speaks of **being abased**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the placing of the two sentences side by side:** there are two lowerings, and they run in opposite directions. **One is bending by one's own choice, the other is being brought low for not bending.**

## 22/19-22 — هَٰذَانِ خَصْمَانِ ٱخْتَصَمُوا۟ فِى رَبِّهِمْ فَٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ قُطِّعَتْ لَهُمْ ثِيَابٌ مِّن نَّارٍ يُصَبُّ مِن فَوْقِ رُءُوسِهِمُ ٱلْحَمِيمُ · يُصْهَرُ بِهِۦ مَا فِى بُطُونِهِمْ وَٱلْجُلُودُ · وَلَهُم مَّقَٰمِعُ مِنْ حَدِيدٍ · كُلَّمَآ أَرَادُوٓا۟ أَن يَخْرُجُوا۟ مِنْهَا مِنْ غَمٍّ أُعِيدُوا۟ فِيهَا

*Hādhāni khaṣmāni'khtaṣamū fī rabbihim · Fa'lladhīna kafarū quṭṭiʿat lahum thiyābun min nār · Yuṣabbu min fawqi ruʾūsihimu'l-ḥamīm · Yuṣharu bihi mā fī buṭūnihim wa'l-julūd · Wa lahum maqāmiʿu min ḥadīd · Kullamā arādū an yakhrujū minhā min ghammin uʿīdū fīhā wa dhūqū ʿadhāba'l-ḥarīq*

"**These are two adversaries, contending about their Lord.** For those who disbelieve, **garments of fire have been cut**; **boiling water** is poured over their heads. By it, what is in their bellies and **their skins are melted.** For them there are **maces of iron**. Whenever they wish to come out of it, out of **anguish**, they are returned into it: '**Taste the punishment of burning!**'"

### هَٰذَانِ خَصْمَانِ ٱخْتَصَمُوا۟ — a grammatical point

**There is a striking shift of number in the sentence, and the lexicographers record it:**

- **هَٰذَانِ خَصْمَانِ** — **dual**: "these two adversaries".
- **ٱخْتَصَمُوا۟** — **plural**: "they contended".

**The subject is dual, the verb plural.**

**The explanation the lexicographers give is this, and I transmit it as a report:** *khaṣm* here conveys not a single person but **a side**. The sides are two, but the persons within each side are many. **Hence the number is dual and the verb is plural.**

**And this is connected with the preceding verse.** The seventeenth verse had named **six groups**. **The nineteenth verse reduces those six to two.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sequence of the two verses:** there were six names in the list; on the day of judgement there are **two sides**. **The separation is therefore not made according to the names of the groups; and the verse does not say how the six names divide into two either.**

**خَصْم** — root **خ-ص-م**: to contend, to go to law. *Khuṣūma*, *mukhāṣama* are of the same root. **The word carries a legal resonance:** a *khaṣm* is the party who stands opposite in a court.

**And the subject of the contention is given: *fī rabbihim* — "about their Lord".** A shared pronoun is used: **the Lord of both parties.** The dispute goes on over a shared subject.

### قُطِّعَتْ لَهُمْ ثِيَابٌ مِّن نَّارٍ

**The verb must be recorded: *quṭṭiʿat* — root ق-ط-ع, and in Form II (*tafʿīl*), the pattern that conveys intensity.** The word belongs to the tailor's craft: **the cutting of cloth.**

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not say "fire wraps them". **It says garments of fire have been cut** — that is, something prepared to measure, to the person. The choice of the verb builds a picture in which the punishment is **not random but measured**.

### ٱلْحَمِيم

The root **ح-م-م**: to grow hot, to grow heated. *Ḥamīm* — **boiling water.**

**The word was treated at `056-vakia.md` 56/42, 54 and 93, and at `038-sad.md` 38/57. I do not repeat it here.** It was recorded there that in al-Wāqiʿa it occurs three times and that all three belong to the same group; and that in Ṣād it is paired with *ghassāq*.

**What differs in the use here is its direction:**

| Place | How *ḥamīm* comes |
|---|---|
| `056-vakia.md` 56/54 | *Fa-shāribūna ʿalayhi mina'l-ḥamīm* — **drunk** |
| `038-sad.md` 38/57 | *Fa'l-yadhūqūhu ḥamīmun wa ghassāq* — **tasted** |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/19** | *Yuṣabbu min fawqi ruʾūsihim* — **poured from above the head** |

**In the other two places *ḥamīm* enters by the mouth; here it is poured from outside.** And the direction of the pouring is specified: *min fawqi ruʾūsihim.*

**This difference prepares the word of the next verse.**

### يُصْهَرُ — root: ص-ه-ر

**The root occurs here for the first time in the index and deserves to be dwelt on in detail.**

**The core sense the lexicographers give: to melt metal or fat in fire.** *Ṣahara'sh-shaḥma* is used of melting fat. **The noun form *ṣihr* is melted fat.**

**The root has another branch as well:** *ṣihr* — **kinship established by marriage** (affinity). The lexicographers generally join the two senses in the idea of "mixing and merging": things that melt run into one another; marriage too mixes two lineages. **I transmit this connection as it is transmitted, and build no interpretation upon it.**

**The word's work here is to complete the picture:**

**In the nineteenth verse the water was poured over the head.** The twentieth verse says where that water reaches: *mā fī buṭūnihim wa'l-julūd* — **what is in their bellies, and the skins.**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and its ground is the juxtaposition of the two words:** *buṭūn* (the inside of the belly) and *julūd* (skin) — **the innermost and the outermost of a man.** The verse binds both to the same verb. **The liquid comes down from above and acts both from within and from without; no place is left between.**

**ٱلْجُلُود** — skin. **Skins were treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/20-22:** there the skins were **bearing witness** (*shahida ʿalayhim … wa julūduhum*). **The same organ, in two sūras, in two separate roles: in one a witness, in the other the place the punishment reaches.**

### مَقَٰمِعُ مِنْ حَدِيدٍ

**مَقَامِع** — its singular is **مِقْمَعَة**. **Root ق-م-ع: to press down, to subdue, to drive back.** *Qamʿ* is the forcing of a thing into its place; *qumʿ* — the bottom of a thing, the place it sits.

**The pattern *mifʿal* is the pattern of instrument nouns in Arabic** (*miftāḥ* — key, *miqaṣṣ* — shears, *minshār* — saw). **The word therefore means, exactly, "an instrument of pressing down".**

**In English it is usually rendered "mace" or "club", and the lexicographers describe the word as a staff with an iron head.**

**And the root of the word prepares the subject of the next verse. I record this as my own reading:** *qamʿ* means "to drive back" — and the twenty-second verse describes exactly a driving back: *kullamā arādū an yakhrujū … uʿīdū fīhā.* **The name of the instrument and the verb of the scene do the same work.**

### كُلَّمَآ أَرَادُوٓا۟ أَن يَخْرُجُوا۟ مِنْهَا مِنْ غَمٍّ

The root **غ-م-م**: **to cover, to overspread.** *Ghamām* — cloud (that which covers the sky); *ghamma* — the distress that overspreads a man within; *maghmūm* — one who is covered over.

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the cause of their wanting to come out is given not as the fire but as **being covered over**. **What is described, then, is less pain than enclosure.**

**كُلَّمَا** — "every time". **The particle conveys repetition:** the wish to come out is not once but continual.

**And I record this as an observation about word order:** the verse builds a cycle — to want, to be sent back, to want again. **The chief element in the scene is that the enclosure becomes continual.**

## 22/23-24 — إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُدْخِلُ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ جَنَّٰتٍ تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ يُحَلَّوْنَ فِيهَا مِنْ أَسَاوِرَ مِن ذَهَبٍ وَلُؤْلُؤًا وَلِبَاسُهُمْ فِيهَا حَرِيرٌ · وَهُدُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلطَّيِّبِ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْلِ وَهُدُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطِ ٱلْحَمِيدِ

*Inna'llāha yudkhilu'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti jannātin tajrī min taḥtiha'l-anhār · Yuḥallawna fīhā min asāwira min dhahabin wa luʾluʾā · Wa libāsuhum fīhā ḥarīr · Wa hudū ila'ṭ-ṭayyibi mina'l-qawl · Wa hudū ilā ṣirāṭi'l-ḥamīd*

"God brings those who believe and do righteous deeds into gardens beneath which rivers flow. There they are adorned with **bracelets of gold and with pearls**; and their **garment there is silk.** **They have been guided to the good of speech; they have been guided to the path of the Praiseworthy.**"

### A table of contrasts

**The two sections are built in exact correspondence. The words match:**

| Subject | 19-22 | **23-24** |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing | *Thiyābun min **nār*** — garments of fire | *Libāsuhum fīhā **ḥarīr*** — silk |
| What comes upon them | *Yuṣabbu … al-ḥamīm* — boiling water is poured | *Yuḥallawna … asāwira min dhahab* — bracelets are put on |
| What is at hand | *Maqāmiʿu min **ḥadīd*** — maces of iron | *Asāwira min **dhahab*** — bracelets of gold |
| Direction | *Kullamā arādū an yakhrujū … uʿīdū* — being sent back | *Wa hudū … wa hudū* — being guided |

**The clearest correspondence is in the two metals: *ḥadīd* and *dhahab*.** Both are metal, both are in the hand. **One is for striking, the other for wearing.**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and its ground is the kinds of the words:** the contrast is built not with abstract concepts but **with objects of the same kind**. Garment against garment, metal against metal, what comes from above against what comes from above.

### وَهُدُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلطَّيِّبِ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْلِ

**This sentence is the most striking place in the section.**

**Everything enumerated is material: bracelet, pearl, silk. Then something of another kind comes abruptly: *aṭ-ṭayyibu mina'l-qawl* — "the good of speech".**

**And the verb is repeated: *wa hudū … wa hudū*.** Twice the same verb, in two separate directions: **speech** and **path**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the breaking of the list:** after objects have been enumerated, the verse stops enumerating objects. **The last two items are not objects but orientations.**

**And that the verb is passive must be recorded: *hudū* — "they were guided".** They did not find; **they were led.** This agrees with the sequence treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/69: **first the effort, then the guiding.** I rely on that treatment.

**صِرَٰطِ ٱلْحَمِيدِ** — "the path of *al-ḥamīd*". **The construct is striking:** the path is annexed to an attribute. *Ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* (the straight path) is the common construct; **here the path is named by the attribute of the station to which it leads.**

**And the attribute *ḥamīd* occurs twice in this sūra: here (24) and in the sixty-fourth verse** (*wa inna'llāha la-huwa'l-ghaniyyu'l-ḥamīd*). The root **ح-م-د** — praise. **The subject of the two occurrences differs: one is the far end of a path travelled, the other is being in need of nothing.**

## 22/25 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ وَيَصُدُّونَ عَن سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْحَرَامِ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلْنَٰهُ لِلنَّاسِ سَوَآءً ٱلْعَٰكِفُ فِيهِ وَٱلْبَادِ

*Inna'lladhīna kafarū wa yaṣuddūna ʿan sabīli'llāhi wa'l-masjidi'l-ḥarāmi'lladhī jaʿalnāhu li'n-nāsi sawāʾani'l-ʿākifu fīhi wa'l-bād · Wa man yurid fīhi bi-ilḥādin bi-ẓulmin nudhiqhu min ʿadhābin alīm*

"Those who disbelieve and who bar others from the way of God and from **the Sacred Mosque, which We appointed for the people — the one who dwells there and the one who comes from outside being equal in it**… And whoever seeks in it **to swerve from the truth** by wrongdoing, We shall make him taste a painful punishment."

### جَعَلْنَٰهُ لِلنَّاسِ — to whom the mosque belongs

**The second term of the construct must be recorded: *li'n-nās* — "for the people".** Not for a tribe, not for a city, not for a community.

**The same record was entered at `002-bakara.md` 2/125:** *wa idh jaʿalna'l-bayta **mathābatan li'n-nāsi** wa amnā*. **I rely on that treatment and repeat the observation made there: "for the people" — not for a particular community.**

**And the observation made in al-Baqara goes one step further here, because the verse of al-Ḥajj defines the equality explicitly:**

### سَوَآءً ٱلْعَٰكِفُ فِيهِ وَٱلْبَادِ

| Word | Root | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلْعَاكِف** | ع-ك-ف | **The one who stays there** — the resident. *Iʿtikāf* is of the same root: to hold oneself in a place |
| **ٱلْبَاد** | ب-د-و | **The one who comes from outside** — from the desert, from the country. *Badawī*, *bādiya* are of the same root |

**The two words answer to two kinds of man around a place: the one who owns and the one who visits.** And the verse sets *sawāʾan* (equal) between them.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pair of words itself:** the equality is established not as an abstract principle but **between two concrete positions**. And one of the parties equated is the man who lives beside the place. **Nearness, therefore, produces no privilege.**

**The same measure was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/199 as well:** *thumma afīḍū min ḥaythu afāḍa'n-nās* — "press on from where the people press on." **There too a claim of privilege was being removed, and there too the word used was *al-nās*.** I rely on that treatment.

**بِإِلْحَادٍۭ بِظُلْمٍ** — the root **ل-ح-د**: **to lean from one side to another, to swerve from the straight.** *Laḥd* — the niche dug into the side of a grave; the word comes from this concrete sense. *Ilḥād* — swerving aside from the straight line.

**The root was treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/40** (*inna'lladhīna yulḥidūna fī āyātinā*). **I rely on that treatment.** There the swerving concerned **the signs**; here it concerns **a place**.

## 22/26-27 — وَإِذْ بَوَّأْنَا لِإِبْرَٰهِيمَ مَكَانَ ٱلْبَيْتِ أَن لَّا تُشْرِكْ بِى شَيْـًٔا وَطَهِّرْ بَيْتِىَ لِلطَّآئِفِينَ وَٱلْقَآئِمِينَ وَٱلرُّكَّعِ ٱلسُّجُودِ · وَأَذِّن فِى ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلْحَجِّ يَأْتُوكَ رِجَالًا وَعَلَىٰ كُلِّ ضَامِرٍ يَأْتِينَ مِن كُلِّ فَجٍّ عَمِيقٍ

*Wa idh bawwaʾnā li-Ibrāhīma makāna'l-bayti an lā tushrik bī shayʾan wa ṭahhir baytiya li'ṭ-ṭāʾifīna wa'l-qāʾimīna wa'r-rukkaʿi's-sujūd · Wa adhdhin fi'n-nāsi bi'l-ḥajji yaʾtūka rijālan wa ʿalā kulli ḍāmirin yaʾtīna min kulli fajjin ʿamīq*

"And when We **made ready** for Abraham the place of the House: '**Associate nothing with Me; and purify My House for those who circle it, those who stand, those who bow and prostrate.** And **proclaim the pilgrimage among the people:** they will come to you **on foot** and **on every lean mount**, coming from every **deep ravine**.'"

### بَوَّأْنَا — root: ب-و-أ

**The concrete sense of the root: to level a place and make it fit to settle in; to prepare a place of lodging.** *Mabāʾa* — a halting place, a place returned to. *Bawwaʾa* — he settled, he made a place ready.

**The choice of word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** the verse does not say "We showed Abraham the House" or "We had him build it". **It says "We made its place ready"** — *makāna'l-bayt.*

**What the verse describes is therefore not the building itself but the place of the building.** The place has priority.

**The same root occurred at `029-ankebut.md` 29/58** (*la-nubawwiʾannahum mina'l-jannati ghurafā* — "We shall settle them in chambers in the Garden"). **The same verb; one a place in this world, the other in the next.**

### وَطَهِّرْ بَيْتِىَ — four attributes

**The command is *ṭahhir* (keep it pure), and those for whom it is given are enumerated. There are four items:**

| Order | Word | Posture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Al-ṭāʾifīn* | **Those who circle** — in motion |
| 2 | *Al-qāʾimīn* | **Those who stand** |
| 3 | *Al-rukkaʿ* | **Those who bow** — half bent |
| 4 | *Al-sujūd* | **Those who prostrate** — fallen to the ground |

**The order itself carries information, and I record this as an observation about word order:** the list descends **from the most mobile to the most still**, and **from the most upright to the most prone**. Circling → standing → bending → falling.

**And the fourth item connects with the eighteenth verse of this sūra:** there all beings were prostrating. **Here prostration is enumerated as the last form the human body takes around a place.**

**ٱلطَّآئِفِين** — root **ط-و-ف**: **to go round a thing.** *Ṭāʾifa* (a group, a section), *ṭawāf*, *ṭūfān* (water that encompasses everything) are of the same root.

**The picture in the root must be recorded:** to circle is to move while remaining bound to a centre. **Neither drawing away nor stopping; the centre fixed, the motion continual.**

### وَأَذِّن فِى ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلْحَجِّ

The root **أ-ذ-ن**: **the ear** (*udhun*). Two branches issue from it: **to give leave** (to lend an ear and accept) and **to proclaim** (to bring to the ears). *Adhān* is of the same root.

**The verb is in Form II: *adhdhin*** — that is, "announce, proclaim".

**And the particle must be recorded: *fi'n-nās* — "among the people".** Not *ila'n-nās* (to the people).

**I record this as a grammatical observation:** the particle *fī* conveys that the call is to be made **in the midst** of the people, from among them. It is not a summons from outside.

**ٱلْحَجّ** — root **ح-ج-ج**: **to make for a place, to intend it; to repeat the intending.**

**The other branch of the root must be recorded:** from the same root comes **حُجَّة** (*ḥujja* — proof). The lexicographers explain the connection thus: a *ḥujja* is what **directs** the hearer to a definite conclusion. **The ḥajj and the ḥujja therefore do the same thing: they direct towards a goal.**

**I give this connection as an observation about the language;** the two branches of the root are recorded in the dictionaries in this way.

### يَأْتُوكَ رِجَالًا وَعَلَىٰ كُلِّ ضَامِرٍ

**Two ways of coming are enumerated, and their order must be recorded.**

| Order | Word | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **رِجَالًا** | **On foot** — from *rijl* (foot); one who comes walking |
| 2 | **عَلَىٰ كُلِّ ضَامِرٍ** | **On a mount** — but what kind of mount? |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order:** **the one on foot is named first.** The man without means has been counted before the man with means.

**ضَامِر** — root **ض-م-ر**: **to grow thin, to grow lean, to waste in flesh.** *Ḍamīr* (conscience) is reckoned to this root too: what is hidden within, what grows fine. *Iḍmār* — to conceal.

**The word is an adjective, and it reports not the kind of the mount but its condition: "grown lean, made thin".**

**I record this as an observation, and its ground is the meaning of the word:** the verse does not say "on a camel". **It says a mount whose flesh has wasted from the length of the road.** **The attribute thus writes the length of the journey onto the body of the animal.** The distance is conveyed not by a separate sentence but by the state of the beast.

**And the construct that follows says the same thing again: *min kulli fajjin ʿamīq*.**

**فَجّ** — root **ف-ج-ج**: **a wide road between two mountains, a pass.** *ʿAmīq* — **deep, far down.**

**The construct means, exactly, "from every deep pass".** And the adjective *ʿamīq* is used in Arabic for distance as well: so far off that its bottom cannot be seen.

**Three separate words thus say the same thing: *ḍāmir* (the road has worn the mount down), *fajj* (a pass), *ʿamīq* (deep/far).** The distance is conveyed three times, by three separate routes.

### The addressee of the command and its outcome

**The command is given to a single person: *adhdhin* — "you, proclaim".** And the outcome is plural: *yaʾtūka* — "they will come to you".

**I record this as an observation about word order:** a single call, a plural answer. **And the verb of the answer is *yaʾtūka* — imperfect, that is, ongoing.** It conveys not a single coming but a repeated movement.

## 22/28-29 — لِّيَشْهَدُوا۟ مَنَٰفِعَ لَهُمْ وَيَذْكُرُوا۟ ٱسْمَ ٱللَّهِ فِىٓ أَيَّامٍ مَّعْلُومَٰتٍ عَلَىٰ مَا رَزَقَهُم مِّنۢ بَهِيمَةِ ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ فَكُلُوا۟ مِنْهَا وَأَطْعِمُوا۟ ٱلْبَآئِسَ ٱلْفَقِيرَ · ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا۟ تَفَثَهُمْ وَلْيُوفُوا۟ نُذُورَهُمْ وَلْيَطَّوَّفُوا۟ بِٱلْبَيْتِ ٱلْعَتِيقِ

*Li-yashhadū manāfiʿa lahum wa yadhkuru'sma'llāhi fī ayyāmin maʿlūmātin ʿalā mā razaqahum min bahīmati'l-anʿām · Fa-kulū minhā wa aṭʿimu'l-bāʾisa'l-faqīr · Thumma'l-yaqḍū tafathahum wa'l-yūfū nudhūrahum wa'l-yaṭṭawwafū bi'l-bayti'l-ʿatīq*

"**That they may witness benefits for themselves** and **on appointed days** may **make mention** of the name of God over the beasts of the herds He has provided them. **Eat of them, and feed the destitute poor as well.** Then let them **be rid of their unkemptness**, **fulfil their vows**, and **circle the Ancient House.**"

### لِّيَشْهَدُوا۟ مَنَٰفِعَ لَهُمْ

**The ground of the coming is given, and two items are enumerated: *manāfiʿ* and *dhikr*.**

**مَنَافِع** — the plural of the root *nafʿ*: **benefits.** **And the word comes indefinite and plural:** which benefits is not said.

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the verse does not define the benefit. No note is entered as to whether it is worldly or of the hereafter. **The classical commentaries dwell on this indefiniteness, and explanations are transmitted in both directions:**

| View | Scope |
|---|---|
| A | **Worldly benefits** — trade, acquaintance, meeting |
| B | **Benefits of the hereafter** — forgiveness, reward |
| C | **Both** — the leaving of the word indefinite requires this |

**I do not impose a preference. But this record follows from the text:** the word is indefinite and plural; **the verse has set no limit.**

**And it is necessary to rely on `002-bakara.md` 2/198:** there the verse *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an tabtaghū faḍlan min rabbikum* was treated, and it was recorded that **trade during the season of the ḥajj was explicitly declared not to spoil the rite**. **The word *manāfiʿ* in al-Ḥajj 22/28 is the positive statement of that permission in al-Baqara.**

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared subject of the two verses:** al-Baqara was lifting a prohibition by saying "there is no sin"; **al-Ḥajj says the same thing by placing it in the list of grounds.** One is a defence, the other a description.

**فِىٓ أَيَّامٍ مَّعْلُومَٰتٍ** — "on appointed days". **No number is given, no name is given.**

**Which days this expression covers has been debated in the sources of law, and its relation to *al-ḥajju ashhurun maʿlūmāt* at `002-bakara.md` 2/197 is also a matter of debate. No legal ruling is issued in this commentary; I confine myself to recording the existence of the disagreement, and no record here is binding.**

### فَكُلُوا۟ مِنْهَا وَأَطْعِمُوا۟

**Two commands side by side: to eat and to feed.**

**The order is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** first *kulū* (eat), then *aṭʿimū* (feed). **The one who slaughters is not excluded from what he has slaughtered.** The rite does not deprive its performer of the produce of his own hand.

**ٱلْبَآئِسَ ٱلْفَقِيرَ** — two attributes side by side:

| Word | Root | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلْبَائِس** | ب-أ-س | **One in hardship** — *baʾs*: severity, difficulty. Treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/214 as *al-baʾsāʾ* |
| **ٱلْفَقِير** | ف-ق-ر | **Poor** — the root carries *faqār* (the spine); the lexicographers explain *faqīr* as "one whose back is broken" |

**The two words say two separate things:** one gives the **state** (being in hardship), the other the **means** (having no property). **And the verse joins the two.**

### ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا۟ تَفَثَهُمْ

**تَفَث** — **root ت-ف-ث. The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

**The explanations the lexicographers give for this word differ, and the sources record this openly:**

| Explanation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | **Dirt, grime, unkemptness** — what gathers over a long journey and during the state of iḥrām |
| B | **The acts performed on leaving iḥrām** — shaving, cutting the nails, washing |
| C | **That the word was rarely used in pre-Islamic Arabic and that its sense could not be firmly established** |

**The third record is important, and I give it as a report: it occurs in the sources that some lexicographers could not establish the meaning of this word with certainty.** I impose no preference in this commentary.

**What is certain is that the verb is *qaḍā*: *l-yaqḍū* — "let them finish, let them complete".** What is at issue, then, is the bringing of a condition to an end.

**وَلْيُوفُوا۟ نُذُورَهُمْ** — "let them fulfil their vows in full". **The root و-ف-ي: completeness, leaving nothing wanting.** **The same root does the same work as the idea of *itmām* treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/196 in the context of *wa atimmu'l-ḥajja wa'l-ʿumrata lillāh*:** that a thing begun should not be left half done.

### ٱلْبَيْتِ ٱلْعَتِيق

**عَتِيق** — root **ع-ت-ق**. **The root has three branches, and the lexicographers record all three:**

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| **Old, ancient** | *ʿAtīq* — worn with age, with time passed over it |
| **Freed, set at liberty** | *Iʿtāq* — the manumission of a slave; *ʿatīq* — one set free |
| **Precious, choice** | *ʿAtīq* — of superior kind (of a horse, of wine) |

**There is disagreement over which sense the adjective bears here:**

| View | Reading |
|---|---|
| A | **Old** — the first place on earth set apart for worship |
| B | **Freed** — never having passed into the ownership of any tyrant, belonging to no one |
| C | **Precious** — honoured, choice |

**All three occur in the sources and none excludes the others. I do not impose a preference.**

**But the second sense makes a link with the twenty-fifth verse, and I give this as my own reading:** verse 25 had said *jaʿalnāhu li'n-nās* — **"for the people".** **If *ʿatīq* means "the property of no one", then the two verses say the same thing in two separate words:** the place is nobody's possession.

**This is not a firm finding** — the sense of the adjective is disputed in any case. **But that the two verses stand side by side is a datum that supports the reading.**

**The construct ٱلْبَيْتِ ٱلْعَتِيق occurs twice in this sūra: 29 and 33.** The verses that lie between the two occurrences form the core of the sūra's section on the ḥajj.

## 22/30-31 — ذَٰلِكَ وَمَن يُعَظِّمْ حُرُمَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّهُۥ عِندَ رَبِّهِۦ … فَٱجْتَنِبُوا۟ ٱلرِّجْسَ مِنَ ٱلْأَوْثَٰنِ وَٱجْتَنِبُوا۟ قَوْلَ ٱلزُّورِ · حُنَفَآءَ لِلَّهِ غَيْرَ مُشْرِكِينَ بِهِۦ وَمَن يُشْرِكْ بِٱللَّهِ فَكَأَنَّمَا خَرَّ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ فَتَخْطَفُهُ ٱلطَّيْرُ أَوْ تَهْوِى بِهِ ٱلرِّيحُ فِى مَكَانٍ سَحِيقٍ

*Dhālika wa man yuʿaẓẓim ḥurumāti'llāhi fa-huwa khayrun lahu ʿinda rabbih · Wa uḥillat lakumu'l-anʿāmu illā mā yutlā ʿalaykum · Fa'jtanibu'r-rijsa mina'l-awthāni wa'jtanibū qawla'z-zūr · Ḥunafāʾa lillāhi ghayra mushrikīna bih · Wa man yushrik bi'llāhi fa-ka-annamā kharra mina's-samāʾi fa-takhṭafuhu'ṭ-ṭayru aw tahwī bihi'r-rīḥu fī makānin saḥīq*

"So it is. Whoever **honours the inviolable things of God**, that is better for him with his Lord… **Shun the filth of idols, and shun false speech.** As **ḥanīf**s to God, associating nothing with Him. **And whoever associates anything with God is as though he had fallen from the sky, and the birds were snatching him away, or the wind were sweeping him off to a far place.**"

### حُرُمَٰت — root: ح-ر-م

**The core sense of the root: to make inviolable, to forbid, to set apart.** *Ḥarām*, *ḥaram*, *iḥrām*, *maḥram*, *ḥurma* are of the same root. **The root occurred in many contexts in `002-bakara.md`.**

**What distinguishes the root must be recorded:** the same word means both "forbidden" and "worthy of reverence". **The two do not contradict:** to make a thing inviolable is both to protect it and to fence it. *Ḥaram* — a place that may not be entered and that is protected.

**حُرُمَات** is plural and is left indefinite: which inviolable things they are is not enumerated.

### ٱلرِّجْسَ مِنَ ٱلْأَوْثَٰنِ — a grammatical point

**The construct is striking and the lexicographers dwell on it: *ar-rijsa mina'l-awthān*.**

**In Arabic the particle *min* is here reckoned explicative (*bayāniyya*):** that is, not "the filth that comes from idols" but **"the filth that is the idols"** — the *min* explains what the *rijs* is.

**I record this as a grammatical observation, and the lexicographers explain the usage in this way.** The difference of sense is this: **idols are counted not as the source of the filth but as the filth itself.**

**وَٱجْتَنِبُوا۟ قَوْلَ ٱلزُّورِ** — "shun false speech."

The root **ز-و-ر**: **crookedness, swerving to one side.** *Zāra* — he visited (he turned towards someone); *azwar* — one whose chest is crooked. **From this comes the sense of "speech that has swerved from the truth".**

**That the two commands are placed side by side is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** one is an **object** (idols), the other a **word**. And both are forbidden with the same verb (*ijtanibū*). **The verse thus counts a stone object and a sentence in the same category.**

**ٱجْتَنِبُوا۟** — root **ج-ن-ب**: side, flank. In Form VIII: **"not to take a thing to one's side, to pass it by".** The command is therefore not "destroy them" but **"do not go near them"**.

**And the root's other occurrence in this sūra must be recorded: at 22/36, *fa-idhā wajabat junūbuhā* — the flanks of the sacrificial animal.** **The same root, one a command and one a scene.**

### حُنَفَآءَ — root: ح-ن-ف

**The lexicographers dwell on the concrete sense of the root: the inward turning of the foot** (*ḥanaf* — a kind of crookedness of the foot). **From this comes the sense of "to incline in one direction".**

**And the Qurʾānic usage of the word turns that inclination in a positive direction:** a *ḥanīf* is **one who turns his face from everything crooked and faces a single direction**.

**The lexicographers record one further point, and it is curious:** the same root also occurs in some usages with exactly the opposite sense (*ḥanīf* — one who swerves from religion). That a root should carry opposite senses (*aḍdād*) is a known phenomenon in Arabic. **I give this as a note on the language.**

### فَكَأَنَّمَا خَرَّ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ — the simile of the fall

**This is the most detailed simile in the sūra, and it is divided into four verbs:**

| Order | Verb | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Kharra mina's-samāʾ* | **He fell from the sky** — loss of height |
| 2 | *Fa-takhṭafuhu'ṭ-ṭayr* | **The birds snatch him** — being torn apart while still in the air |
| 3 | *Aw tahwī bihi'r-rīḥ* | **The wind sweeps him off** — loss of direction |
| 4 | *Fī makānin saḥīq* | **To a far place** — the point of arrival |

**The structure of the simile must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading: the falling man never reaches the ground.**

Two possibilities are given (*fa-takhṭafuhu* or *aw tahwī bihi*), and **neither of them allows the fall to be completed.** Either he is snatched in the air, or he is swept away. **The scene is therefore not a fall but a being held in suspension.**

**خَرَّ** — root **خ-ر-ر**: to fall from a height; and, in falling, **to make a sound** (*ḥarīr* — the murmur of water). The word is used in the Qurʾān of prostration as well (*kharrū sujjadā*).

**This is an overlap worth recording:** the same verb describes both the falling to the ground in prostration and the fall in this verse. **The difference lies in whether the fall is controlled.** Between the list of prostration in the eighteenth verse and this verse stand the two faces of one root.

**سَحِيق** — root **س-ح-ق**: **to crush, to grind fine.** *Saḥq* — to pound in a mortar. **From this comes the sense of "very far";** the lexicographers make the link thus: so far that whoever reaches it counts as crushed.

**تَخْطَفُ** — root **خ-ط-ف**: **to seize swiftly.** **The same root occurred at `029-ankebut.md` 29/67** (*wa yutakhaṭṭafu'n-nāsu min ḥawlihim* — "while people are snatched away all around them"). **And there too the subject was the safety of the Sanctuary.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The comparison of the two verses is worth recording:**

| Place | Who is snatched | Where |
|---|---|---|
| al-ʿAnkabūt 29/67 | **People** — outside the Sanctuary | The **surroundings** of the safe zone |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/31** | **The one who associates** | **Between** sky and earth |

**In al-ʿAnkabūt safety was bound to a place; here insecurity is bound to there being nothing to hold on to.**

## 22/32-33 — ذَٰلِكَ وَمَن يُعَظِّمْ شَعَٰٓئِرَ ٱللَّهِ فَإِنَّهَا مِن تَقْوَى ٱلْقُلُوبِ · لَكُمْ فِيهَا مَنَٰفِعُ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى ثُمَّ مَحِلُّهَآ إِلَى ٱلْبَيْتِ ٱلْعَتِيقِ

*Dhālika wa man yuʿaẓẓim shaʿāʾira'llāhi fa-innahā min taqwa'l-qulūb · Lakum fīhā manāfiʿu ilā ajalin musamman thumma maḥilluhā ila'l-bayti'l-ʿatīq*

"So it is. **Whoever honours the *shaʿāʾir* of God — that is from the taqwā of hearts.** In them there are **benefits for you until an appointed term; then their place of arrival is the Ancient House.**"

### شَعَآئِر — the analysis of the word

**This is one of the key words of the sūra and deserves detailed treatment.**

**Its singular: شَعِيرَة (*shaʿīra*). Root: ش-ع-ر.**

**The root was analysed at `002-bakara.md` 2/154, and what was recorded there was this: the root is to perceive something fine; the lexicographers relate it to *shaʿr* (hair) — to notice what is as fine as a hair. *Shuʿūr* and *shāʿir* are of the same root. I do not repeat it here.**

**What is to be added about the derivative here is this:**

**شَعِيرَة**, by the definition the lexicographers give, is **"a mark by which a thing is made known"**. The root's sense of "perceiving, noticing" turns, in this pattern, into **"that which makes one notice"**.

**The word's relatives complete the picture:**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **شِعَار** (*shiʿār*) | The **mark, the watchword** by which a group makes itself known — the cry used in battle is this too |
| **شَعْر** (*shaʿr*) | Hair — the finest thing that can be noticed |
| **شُعُور** (*shuʿūr*) | Awareness |
| **شَاعِر** (*shāʿir*) | One who perceives what others cannot |
| **مَشْعَر** (*mashʿar*) | A place upon which a mark has been set; *al-Mashʿaru'l-Ḥarām* — the place-name that occurs at al-Baqara 2/198 |

**Thus *shaʿāʾir* means "mark" before it means "symbol"; and a mark is what announces the existence of a thing to another.**

**I give this as an observation about the language, and the pattern of the word supports it.**

### وَمَن يُعَظِّمْ — the choice of verb

**عَظَّمَ** — root **ع-ظ-م**: **to be great.** The concrete counterpart of the root is **عَظْم** (*ʿaẓm* — bone): the hardest, largest structure of the body.

**The verb is in Form II (*tafʿīl*): "to make great, to reckon great".**

**I record this as a grammatical observation:** the pattern conveys not **actually enlarging** a thing but **reckoning it great**. The object itself does not change; **what changes is the attitude that meets it.**

**And this is the same as the verb in the thirtieth verse: *wa man yuʿaẓẓim ḥurumāti'llāh*.** Between the two verses stand two objects:

| Verse | What is honoured |
|---|---|
| **30** | *Ḥurumāti'llāh* — **the inviolable things** (prohibitions, limits) |
| **32** | *Shaʿāʾira'llāh* — **the marks** (places, forms of worship, sacrificial animals) |

**The two complete one another:** one is what is **not to be done**, the other what is **visible**.

### فَإِنَّهَا مِن تَقْوَى ٱلْقُلُوبِ

**This construct is the centre of gravity of the verse.**

**First a question of word order: to what does the pronoun in *innahā* refer?**

**The lexicographers part company at this point, and I transmit their views:**

| View | The referent of the pronoun |
|---|---|
| A | **To the *shaʿāʾir*** — the marks that are honoured |
| B | **To the act of honouring** — to a verbal noun implied in the sentence (*taʿẓīm*) |

**Both carry the meaning to a similar place, and I do not impose a preference.** But reading B is the more favoured among the lexicographers, because the ruling is given not of the object but of **the conduct**.

### تَقْوَى ٱلْقُلُوبِ — the analysis of the construct

**The construct is a genitive: "the taqwā of hearts".**

**The root ت-ق-و / و-ق-ي: to guard, to be guarded, to take cover.** *Wiqāya* — a protective covering; *ittiqāʾ* — to put oneself under protection.

**What is striking in the construct is that taqwā is annexed to an organ.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the genitive itself:**

- **The verse binds the counterpart of an outward act (honouring) to an inward place.** The act is visible — to slaughter a sacrifice, to circle, to show reverence to a place. **But its worth is weighed where it cannot be seen.**
- **And the construct is *taqwa'l-qulūb*, not *qulūbu'l-atqiyāʾ* (the hearts of the God-fearing).** Taqwā is annexed not to the person but **to the heart**.
- **The plural is used: *al-qulūb*.** Not one heart, but hearts.

**And the function of the construct in the verse is this: it sets a criterion.** The verse does not command "honour the *shaʿāʾir*"; **it says where the one who honours them is to bind his act.**

**This criterion will return once more, and more sharply, in the thirty-seventh verse:** *lan yanāla'llāha luḥūmuhā wa lā dimāʾuhā wa lākin yanāluhu't-taqwā minkum.* **The two verses do the same work and use the same word.**

### لَكُمْ فِيهَا مَنَٰفِعُ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى

**The thirty-third verse adds a concrete note to the thirty-second.**

**There is disagreement over the pronoun (*fīhā*):**

| View | The referent of the pronoun |
|---|---|
| A | **The sacrificial animals** — as one part of the *shaʿāʾir* |
| B | **The *shaʿāʾir* as a whole** |

**Reading A appears the better fit for the context, since the rest of the sentence says *maḥilluhā* (their place of arrival), and this requires something that is driven along. But I do not impose a preference.**

**And the structure of the sentence is worth recording:**

| Stage | The expression |
|---|---|
| First | *Lakum fīhā **manāfiʿ*** — **benefits for you** |
| Term | *Ilā ajalin musammā* — **until an appointed time** |
| Then | *Maḥilluhā ila'l-bayti'l-ʿatīq* — **the place they arrive at** |

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the verse does not forbid taking benefit from the sacrificial animal; **it states its term.** The benefit is lawful, but it has a time.

**And the word *manāfiʿ* occurs here for the second time** — it was in the twenty-eighth verse as well (*li-yashhadū manāfiʿa lahum*). **There is a difference between the two occurrences and it must be recorded:** in 28 the benefit was the ground of **the act of pilgrimage**; in 33 it is on **the animal**. **The same word, then, first about the reason for the rite and then about its object.**

**And it is necessary to rely on the section `002-bakara.md` 2/196-203:** the rulings of the ḥajj were treated there, and the measure of that section was recorded thus — **"the time, the place and the form are regulated; but no extra burden is laid on."** **al-Ḥajj 22/33 carries the same measure forward:** the benefit is not removed, **its time is fixed.**

## 22/34-35 — وَلِكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ جَعَلْنَا مَنسَكًا لِّيَذْكُرُوا۟ ٱسْمَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَىٰ مَا رَزَقَهُم مِّنۢ بَهِيمَةِ ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ فَإِلَٰهُكُمْ إِلَٰهٌ وَٰحِدٌ فَلَهُۥٓ أَسْلِمُوا۟ وَبَشِّرِ ٱلْمُخْبِتِينَ · ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِرَ ٱللَّهُ وَجِلَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَٱلصَّٰبِرِينَ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَصَابَهُمْ وَٱلْمُقِيمِى ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ

*Wa li-kulli ummatin jaʿalnā mansakan li-yadhkuru'sma'llāhi ʿalā mā razaqahum min bahīmati'l-anʿām · Fa-ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥidun fa-lahu aslimū · Wa bashshiri'l-mukhbitīn · Alladhīna idhā dhukira'llāhu wajilat qulūbuhum wa'ṣ-ṣābirīna ʿalā mā aṣābahum wa'l-muqīmi'ṣ-ṣalāti wa mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn*

"**For every community We appointed a *mansak***, that they might make mention of the name of God over the beasts of the herds He has provided them. **Your God is one God; so submit to Him.** And give glad tidings to **those who are lowly**: **those whose hearts tremble when God is mentioned**, who are patient under what befalls them, who keep up the prayer, and who spend of what We have provided them."

### مَنسَك — root: ن-س-ك

**The lexicographers dwell on the concrete sense of the root, and two branches are recorded:**

| Branch | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | **To slaughter, to shed blood** — *nasīka*, the sacrificial animal |
| B | **To worship, to give oneself to worship** — *nāsik*, one who worships much |

**The lexicographers enter a third record as well:** the root also carries the sense of **purification** (*nasaka'th-thawba* — he washed the garment). **In the sources these three senses are generally brought together in the idea of "setting a thing apart in order to cleanse it".**

The pattern **مَنْسَك** may be a **noun of place or time** or a **maṣdar mīmī**. The word can therefore mean both "the place or the time of worship" and "the form of worship". **Both readings are transmitted in the classical commentaries.**

**A note on variant readings:** it is transmitted that the word is also read as **مَنْسِك** (*mansik*, with kasra). **I do not name a reader.** The two readings turn on the distinction between the pattern's being a noun of place and its being a verbal noun.

### وَلِكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ — the scope

**The most striking feature of the sentence is its plurality: *li-kulli umma* — "for every community".**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the verse reports that the sacrifice and the form of worship **are not peculiar to this community**. A different *mansak* has been given to different communities.

**And immediately after, the common point is set down: *fa-ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥid*.** The forms are plural, the one turned to is one.

**The same sentence will come once more at 22/67: *li-kulli ummatin jaʿalnā mansakan hum nāsikūh*.** **There is a difference between the two occurrences:**

| Verse | The rest of the sentence | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **34** | *Fa-ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥidun fa-lahu aslimū* | Shows **the common point** |
| **67** | *Fa-lā yunāziʿunnaka fi'l-amr* | Cuts off **the dispute** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that both verses open with the same sentence:** the same piece of information is given twice and tied to two separate conclusions. **In the one, submission; in the other, the abandoning of contention.**

### ٱلْمُخْبِتِين — root: خ-ب-ت

**The root occurs here for the first time in the index.**

**The concrete sense the lexicographers give: *khabt* — low ground, a hollow, level land.** Ground with no elevation, without unevenness. **From this, in Form IV, *ikhbāt* — to be lowered, to submit, to grow still.**

**The picture in the word must be recorded:** a *mukhbit*, by the root of the word, is a man **who has no height**. **The attribute is thus described not by an action but by a form of ground.**

**And the root will occur once more in this sūra: at 22/54, *fa-tukhbita lahu qulūbuhum* — "that their hearts may be lowered before Him".** **What the two occurrences share is the heart.**

### وَجِلَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ — root: و-ج-ل

**The root: to shiver with fear, an inward trembling.** The lexicographers describe *wajal* as "the sudden stirring of the heart".

**And what distinguishes the word must be recorded:** *khawf* (fear) is generally felt of a harm to come; *wajal* is **a momentary, bodily shiver**.

**The trigger given in the verse is worth recording too: *idhā dhukira'llāh* — "when God is mentioned".** The shiver arises, then, not from a threat but **from a name**.

### Four attributes

**The thirty-fifth verse names four attributes, and its order can be compared with the four at `031-lokman.md` 31/17:**

| Order | **al-Ḥajj 22/35** | `031-lokman.md` 31/17 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Wajilat qulūbuhum* — **the trembling of the heart** | *Aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta* — **prayer** |
| 2 | *Al-ṣābirīna ʿalā mā aṣābahum* — **patience** | *Wa'mur bi'l-maʿrūf* — **enjoining what is right** |
| 3 | *Al-muqīmi'ṣ-ṣalāt* — **prayer** | *Wa'nha ʿani'l-munkar* — **forbidding what is wrong** |
| 4 | *Mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn* — **spending** | *Wa'ṣbir ʿalā mā aṣābak* — **patience** |

**The two lists share two items: prayer and patience. And patience comes in both with the same words:** *ʿalā mā aṣābahum* / *ʿalā mā aṣābak*.

**The difference lies in direction, and I record this as my own reading:** Luqmān's list contains two **outward-facing** items (enjoining, forbidding); **al-Ḥajj's list runs from the inside outward** — heart, endurance, prayer, spending. **In Luqmān patience stood at the end of the list, as the price of giving counsel; in al-Ḥajj patience stands in the middle of the list, as a state.**

## 22/36 — وَٱلْبُدْنَ جَعَلْنَٰهَا لَكُم مِّن شَعَٰٓئِرِ ٱللَّهِ لَكُمْ فِيهَا خَيْرٌ فَٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱسْمَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْهَا صَوَآفَّ فَإِذَا وَجَبَتْ جُنُوبُهَا فَكُلُوا۟ مِنْهَا وَأَطْعِمُوا۟ ٱلْقَانِعَ وَٱلْمُعْتَرَّ

*Wa'l-budna jaʿalnāhā lakum min shaʿāʾiri'llāhi lakum fīhā khayr · Fa'dhkuru'sma'llāhi ʿalayhā ṣawāff · Fa-idhā wajabat junūbuhā fa-kulū minhā wa aṭʿimu'l-qāniʿa wa'l-muʿtarr · Kadhālika sakhkharnāhā lakum laʿallakum tashkurūn*

"**The large sacrificial beasts too We have appointed for you among the *shaʿāʾir* of God;** in them there is good for you. **Make mention of the name of God over them as they stand in rows.** When their flanks fall to the ground, eat of them and feed **both the one who is content and the one who asks**. Thus have We made them subservient to you, that you may give thanks."

### ٱلْبُدْن — root: ب-د-ن

**The sense of the root: body, trunk; to be bulky, to grow fat.** *Badan* is of the same root; *bādin* — heavy-bodied.

**بُدْن**, its singular **بَدَنَة**: by the lexicographers' definition, **a large-bodied sacrificial animal.**

**Which animals it covers has been debated in the sources of law:**

| View | Scope |
|---|---|
| A | **The camel alone** |
| B | **Camel and cattle** |

**This is a matter of law; I confine myself to transmitting the views, and I issue no ruling. This record is not binding.**

**The root of the word is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** the animal is named not by its species or its name but **by the bulk of its body**. And the thirty-seventh verse will touch exactly this point: *luḥūmuhā wa lā dimāʾuhā* — **flesh and blood.**

### صَوَآفَّ — root: ص-ف-ف

**The root: to be in a row, to be drawn up.** *Ṣaff*, *ṣufūf* are of the same root; the sūra names `061-saff.md` and `037-saffat.md` come from this root.

**صَوَافّ** is the plural of *ṣāffa*: **those standing in a row.**

**The word's work here is to build a scene, and I record this as an observation:** the animals are described **standing and drawn up in a row**. The verse describes not the moment of slaughter but **the moment before it**.

**A note on variant readings:** it is transmitted that the word is also read as **صَوَافِنَ** (*ṣawāfin* — standing on three legs). **I do not name a reader.** Both readings describe the animal's stance.

### فَإِذَا وَجَبَتْ جُنُوبُهَا

The root **و-ج-ب**: **to fall, to come down to the ground; and to become necessary.** The lexicographers join the two senses: for a thing to be "obligatory" is for it to have settled into its place, to have become unavoidable.

**The construct is, exactly: "when their flanks have fallen".** *Junūb* — flanks, sides.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse does not name death directly. **It conveys it by the standing animal's toppling onto its side.** And this makes an exact contrast with the preceding word (*ṣawāff* — standing drawn up): **upright stance → falling to the side.**

**And the relation of roots must be recorded: *junūb* (flanks) is of the same root (ج-ن-ب) as *ijtanibū* (pass by) in the thirtieth verse.** **The same root, six verses apart, one a command and one a scene.**

### ٱلْقَانِعَ وَٱلْمُعْتَرَّ

**Two words answer to two separate kinds of needy person, and the lexicographers dwell on the difference between them.**

| Word | Root | The distinction the lexicographers give |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلْقَانِع** | ق-ن-ع | **The one who is content** — satisfied with what is given, who does not ask |
| **ٱلْمُعْتَرّ** | ع-ر-ر | **The one who comes and stands by** — who shows himself, who draws near |

**A record is needed about the root ق-ن-ع:** the root can mean both "to be content" and "to ask" (*qaniʿa* — he was content; *qaniʿa* — he asked). **The lexicographers record this distinction, and which of the two is meant here has been debated.**

**The root ع-ر-ر:** *ʿarra* — to come and settle upon a place, to fasten upon. A *muʿtarr* is **one who comes into view without asking openly**.

**The use of the two words together builds a scope, and I give this as my own reading:** one is **the one who does not ask**, the other **the one who cannot ask but is seen.** **The verse therefore covers both types who do not voice their need.**

**And the same type was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/273:** *yaḥsabuhumu'l-jāhilu aghniyāʾa mina't-taʿaffuf* — "one who does not know thinks them rich, because they refrain from asking." **The word differs, the attitude described is the same.**

## 22/37 — لَن يَنَالَ ٱللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا وَلَا دِمَآؤُهَا وَلَٰكِن يَنَالُهُ ٱلتَّقْوَىٰ مِنكُمْ كَذَٰلِكَ سَخَّرَهَا لَكُمْ لِتُكَبِّرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمْ وَبَشِّرِ ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ

*Lan yanāla'llāha luḥūmuhā wa lā dimāʾuhā wa lākin yanāluhu't-taqwā minkum · Kadhālika sakhkharahā lakum li-tukabbiru'llāha ʿalā mā hadākum · Wa bashshiri'l-muḥsinīn*

"**Neither their flesh nor their blood reaches God; what reaches Him is the taqwā that comes from you.** Thus has He made them subservient to you, that you may magnify God for having guided you. And give glad tidings to those who do good."

### What the verse does

**This verse is the ruling of the sūra's section on the ḥajj, and it deserves to be dwelt on in detail.**

**The verse proceeds in three steps:**

| Step | Expression | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Lan yanāla'llāha luḥūmuhā wa lā dimāʾuhā* | **Leaves the material outside** |
| 2 | *Wa lākin yanāluhu't-taqwā minkum* | **Says what takes its place** |
| 3 | *Li-tukabbiru'llāha ʿalā mā hadākum* | **States the purpose of the act** |

### لَن يَنَالَ — the force of the negation

**The verb is negated with *lan*.** In Arabic *lan* negates the future **decisively**. It is stronger than *lā*.

**And the verb is *yanālu* — root ن-و-ل: to reach a thing, to attain it, to arrive at it.** *Nawāl* — what is obtained.

**The word order must be recorded, and this is the most striking feature of the verse:** the subject of the sentence is *luḥūm* and *dimāʾ*, and its object is *Allāh*. **The sentence is not built as "God does not take the flesh"; it is built as "the flesh does not reach God".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order of the elements:** the agents are the flesh and the blood. **The verse makes the material the moving party and reports that its motion cannot arrive.** What is refused is not a taking but a **reaching**.

### لُحُومُهَا وَلَا دِمَآؤُهَا — why these two words

**Two words are chosen: flesh and blood. Both are plural.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the material side of the sacrifice is gathered into two items — **the part that is eaten** (flesh) and **the part that is poured out** (blood).

**And that the two are enumerated separately is meaningful:** flesh is the part distributed among people and producing benefit. Blood is the part that is **not distributed**, only poured out. **The verse places both under the same ruling.**

**What is said, then, is that neither the part that produces benefit nor the part that does not, arrives.** This shows that the distinction is **not built upon benefit**.

### وَلَٰكِن يَنَالُهُ ٱلتَّقْوَىٰ مِنكُمْ

**The particle of exception (*lākin*) does not lift the ruling; it adds a note to it.**

**And what does arrive is named: *at-taqwā* — the same word as in the thirty-second verse.**

**The two verses must be set side by side:**

| Verse | Construct | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **32** | *Fa-innahā min **taqwa'l-qulūb*** | The **source** of the honouring |
| **37** | *Wa lākin yanāluhu't-**taqwā minkum*** | The **arriving side** of the sacrifice |

**And the note in the thirty-seventh verse must be recorded: *minkum* — "from you".** The taqwā does not belong to the animal; **it belongs to the one who slaughters.**

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the contrast of the two annexations:** there are two genitives in the verse — *luḥūmu**hā*** (its flesh) and *taqwā **minkum*** (the taqwā from you). **One is bound to the animal, the other to the man.** What does not arrive belongs to the animal; what arrives belongs to the man.

### The matter of an act of worship and its purpose

**This distinction is the centre of the verse and can be generalised. I record it as my own reading, and its ground is the duality the verse builds itself.**

**Every act of worship has two sides:**

| Side | Its counterpart in the sacrifice | Its character |
|---|---|---|
| **Matter** | Flesh, blood, the animal itself | **Visible, measurable, countable** |
| **Purpose** | *Taqwā*, *takbīr* | **Invisible, immeasurable** |

**What the verse does is not to cancel the material.** This needs stressing: **the verse does not abolish the sacrifice.** The thirty-sixth verse described the slaughter in detail; the thirty-seventh comes immediately after that description.

**The material therefore stands where it is; but it is not counted sufficient on its own.**

**And this measure works exactly as the verse treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/197:** *wa tazawwadū fa-inna khayra'z-zādi't-taqwā* — "take provision; the best provision is taqwā." **The observation recorded in al-Baqara holds here too: the concrete command is not cancelled, a further layer is added upon it.** **And both verses use the same word: *taqwā*.**

**I rely on that treatment and add this:** in al-Baqara the added layer lay upon **provision** (the material of a journey); **in al-Ḥajj the added layer lies upon the sacrifice** (the material of an act of worship). **Both fall within the same section of a sūra — the discussion of the ḥajj.**

### لِتُكَبِّرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمْ

**The purpose clause must be recorded: the verb is *tukabbirū* — "that you may acknowledge as great".**

**The root ك-ب-ر, in Form II.** And this is **in the same pattern and doing the same work** as the verb *yuʿaẓẓim* (to reckon great) in the thirty-second verse. **The two verbs frame the sūra's section on the ḥajj:**

| Verse | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | *Yuʿaẓẓim* | *Ḥurumāti'llāh* |
| **32** | *Yuʿaẓẓim* | *Shaʿāʾira'llāh* |
| **37** | *Tukabbirū* | **God Himself** |

**I record this as an observation about word order:** across the three verses the object of the honouring narrows step by step — the inviolable things, the marks, and finally **the one who is marked**.

**عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمْ** — "for having guided you". **The ground of the *takbīr* is therefore not the sacrifice but the guidance.**

**And this can be compared with the usage in the fourth verse:** there we had *yahdīhi ilā ʿadhābi's-saʿīr* — the same root, the opposite direction. **The two ends of one root within a single sūra.**

### A record that no legal dispute is entered

**The ruling of the sacrifice, its time, upon whom it falls, which animals may be slaughtered, and how the meat is to be distributed have been debated in detail in the sources of law, and there are differences among the schools.**

**This commentary does not enter those debates and issues no ruling.** What the verse treats is not the detail of the ruling but **its measure**; and the verse sets that measure itself.

### What this says to the present

The distinction the verse draws does not stay confined to the sacrifice.

**Every visible work, because it has a measurable side, is open to supposing itself sufficient.** The count comes out right, the quantity comes out right, the form comes out right. **The verse says there is one more thing that has to come out right — and that this thing cannot be counted.**

**And it must be recorded again that in saying this the verse does not belittle the material.** The meat is distributed, the poor are fed; that is commanded immediately before this verse. **The distinction is not the preferring of one to the other; it is a statement of which of them arrives where.**

## 22/38 — إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُدَٰفِعُ عَنِ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ كُلَّ خَوَّانٍ كَفُورٍ

*Inna'llāha yudāfiʿu ʿani'lladhīna āmanū · Inna'llāha lā yuḥibbu kulla khawwānin kafūr*

"**God defends those who believe.** God does not love anyone who is **treacherous and ungrateful**."

### يُدَٰفِعُ — the choice of pattern

**The root د-ف-ع: to push, to drive back, to bar the way of something. The root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/251. I do not repeat it here.**

**The pattern here is Form III (*mufāʿala*): *yudāfiʿu*.**

**This pattern generally conveys reciprocity in Arabic** (*qātala* — he fought, *jādala* — he disputed). **But it is used for one-sided intensity as well.**

**The lexicographers generally take the use in this verse in the second sense: continual and forceful defence.** I give this as a report.

**A note on variant readings:** it is transmitted that the word is also read as **يَدْفَعُ** (*yadfaʿu*, Form I). **I do not name a reader.** The direction of the meaning does not change; the difference of pattern touches the note of intensity.

**And where the verse stands must be recorded:** this is the verse **immediately before** the permission to fight. **Before permission is given, then, it is said who the real agent of the defence is.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the order:** the thirty-eighth verse puts God as the agent; the thirty-ninth gives permission to man. **Had the order been reversed, the meaning would have been reversed too.**

**خَوَّان** and **كَفُور** — both in the intensive pattern. **خ-و-ن**: betrayal of a trust, secret diminishing. **ك-ف-ر**: to cover over, ingratitude.

## 22/39-40 — أُذِنَ لِلَّذِينَ يُقَٰتَلُونَ بِأَنَّهُمْ ظُلِمُوا۟ وَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ نَصْرِهِمْ لَقَدِيرٌ · ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ مِن دِيَٰرِهِم بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ إِلَّآ أَن يَقُولُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍ لَّهُدِّمَتْ صَوَٰمِعُ وَبِيَعٌ وَصَلَوَٰتٌ وَمَسَٰجِدُ يُذْكَرُ فِيهَا ٱسْمُ ٱللَّهِ كَثِيرًا

*Udhina li'lladhīna yuqātalūna bi-annahum ẓulimū · Wa inna'llāha ʿalā naṣrihim la-qadīr · Alladhīna ukhrijū min diyārihim bi-ghayri ḥaqqin illā an yaqūlū rabbuna'llāh · Wa law lā dafʿu'llāhi'n-nāsa baʿḍahum bi-baʿḍin la-huddimat ṣawāmiʿu wa biyaʿun wa ṣalawātun wa masājidu yudhkaru fīha'sma'llāhi kathīrā · Wa la-yanṣuranna'llāhu man yanṣuruh · Inna'llāha la-qawiyyun ʿazīz*

"**Permission has been given to those who are fought against, because they have been wronged.** God is surely able to help them. **They are those who have been driven from their homes without right, only for saying 'Our Lord is God'.** Were it not that God repels some people by means of others, **the cloisters, the churches, the synagogues and the mosques in which the name of God is much mentioned would have been pulled down.** God will surely help whoever helps Him. God is strong, mighty."

### أُذِنَ — a passive verb

**The verb is passive: "permission has been given".** Who gives the permission is not said — **but its not being said is not a vagueness; it is a choice of style:** the rest of the sentence already speaks of God.

**And the verb's being passive says one thing more, and I record this as a grammatical observation:** permission is something **received**, not something that **arises from oneself**. The sentence is not built in the form "they have a right".

**لِلَّذِينَ يُقَٰتَلُونَ** — **this verb too is passive: "those who are fought against".**

**This is the most decisive feature of word order in the verse and it must be recorded:** the permission is given not to **those who fight** but to **those who are fought against**. The passive form of the verb fixes the position of the party.

**A note on variant readings:** it is transmitted that the verb is also read as **يُقَاتِلُونَ** (*yuqātilūna*, active — "those who fight"). **I do not name a reader.** The difference between the two readings is meaningful: the one indicates the party subjected to fighting, the other the party doing the fighting. **I transmit the disagreement; I do not impose a preference.**

**بِأَنَّهُمْ ظُلِمُوا۟** — the ground is given, and this verb too is passive: "they were wronged."

**Three verbs in succession are passive: *udhina*, *yuqātalūna*, *ẓulimū*.** **I record this as an observation about word order:** throughout the sentence the party occupying the position of subject **initiates no action of its own.**

### إِلَّآ أَن يَقُولُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ

**The exceptive construction is striking.** The sentence says "they were driven out without right", and then adds an exception: *illā an yaqūlū rabbuna'llāh.*

**In Arabic this is called *istithnāʾ munqaṭiʿ*: not a real exception but a construction close to irony.** It means, that is, "their only offence was this".

**I record this as a grammatical observation, and the lexicographers explain the construction in this way:** the pattern says **by the form of the sentence** that the ground alleged cannot count as a ground.

### وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍ

**This sentence occurred in the same words at `002-bakara.md` 2/251 and was treated there. I rely on that treatment and repeat the observation recorded there:**

> **The balance is established not by the dominance of a single power but by powers limiting one another.** The verse does not say "God destroys the corrupters"; it says "He repels some people by means of others." **The instrument, that is, is again man.**

**It was also recorded in the al-Baqara section that this verse occurs once more at al-Ḥajj 22/40.** **Now the difference between the two occurrences must be drawn out:**

| | `002-bakara.md` 2/251 | **al-Ḥajj 22/40** |
|---|---|---|
| The condition | *Law lā dafʿu'llāhi'n-nāsa baʿḍahum bi-baʿḍ* | **The same** |
| The consequence (had it not been) | *La-fasadati'l-**arḍ*** — **the earth would be corrupted** | *La-huddimat **ṣawāmiʿu wa biyaʿun wa ṣalawātun wa masājid*** — **the places of worship would be pulled down** |
| The context | The ruling drawn from the story of David and Goliath | **The ground of the permission to fight** |
| The scope | **The whole earth** | **Particular buildings** |

**The relation of the two verses must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:**

**al-Baqara lays down the general ruling: without the repelling, the earth would be corrupted. al-Ḥajj gives one concrete item of the same ruling: without the repelling, the places of worship would be pulled down.**

**al-Ḥajj thus draws out one example from within what al-Baqara calls "corruption".** And the example chosen is striking: **as the index of corruption, it is not crops, or trade, or life that is chosen, but places of worship.**

### The enumeration of four buildings

**The verse names four buildings. This is a list without parallel in the Qurʾān and needs careful treatment.**

| Order | Word | What the sources give as its counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **صَوَامِع** | Its singular *ṣawmaʿa*. **The place of those who withdraw into seclusion** — a cloister, a cell. Root **ص-م-ع**: to taper, to grow thin and rise; *aṣmaʿ* — pointed at the top |
| 2 | **بِيَع** | Its singular *bīʿa*. Explained as **a church** |
| 3 | **صَلَوَات** | The plural of *ṣalāt*. Explained as **the Jews' place of worship (synagogue)**; the use of the word in this sense is recorded by the lexicographers |
| 4 | **مَسَاجِد** | Its singular *masjid*. **The place where prostration is made** |

**A record is needed on the third item:** it has been debated whether the word *ṣalawāt* is used here in the sense of "worship" or "place of worship". **Since the list consists throughout of names of places, the sense of place comes to the fore, but both readings are transmitted. I do not impose a preference.**

**Observations on the structure of the list — I give them so far as they can be drawn from the text:**

**First: the order.** The list runs **from the least crowded to the most crowded**: the single hermit's cell → the church → the synagogue → the mosque. **This is an observation about word order.**

**Second: where the qualification stands.** The verse names four buildings, then attaches a relative clause **to all of them at once**: *yudhkaru fīha'sma'llāhi kathīrā* — "in which the name of God is much mentioned".

**That the qualification is attached to all four buildings has been debated by the lexicographers:** some say the qualification belongs only to the last (*masājid*), others that it belongs to all four. **I transmit both views; I do not impose a preference.**

**Third and most important — per the method (`STYLE.md`) I record it plainly:**

**No wholesale ruling about any religious community may be built on this list.** The verse issues no verdict of truth about the systems of belief to which the named buildings belong; **it neither endorses nor rejects them.** What the verse says is one thing only: **had the destruction not been prevented, all of these would have been pulled down.**

**And this commentary draws no present-day political conclusion from the verse.** The verse states a principle; how the principle is to be applied to which event today is not the subject of this text and is not entered into here.

**What can be drawn from the verse on the basis of the text is this, and I give it as my own reading:** the permission is given not **in order to attack** but **in order to stop a destruction**. The construction of the sentence shows it: the conditional clause depicts a destruction (*la-huddimat*), and the repelling is set against that destruction.

**لَّهُدِّمَتْ** — root **ه-د-م**: to pull down, to bring a building down from its foundations. **The verb is in Form II (*tahdīm*) and is passive:** the pattern conveys intensity — **being pulled down one by one, from end to end.**

## 22/41 — ٱلَّذِينَ إِن مَّكَّنَّٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتَوُا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَأَمَرُوا۟ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَنَهَوْا۟ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ وَلِلَّهِ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْأُمُورِ

*Alladhīna in makkannāhum fi'l-arḍi aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāta wa ātawu'z-zakāta wa amarū bi'l-maʿrūfi wa nahaw ʿani'l-munkar · Wa lillāhi ʿāqibatu'l-umūr*

"**They are those who, if We give them power and place on the earth, keep up the prayer, pay the alms, enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong.** And to God belongs the end of all affairs."

### إِن مَّكَّنَّٰهُمْ — the condition itself

**The verse is built as a conditional sentence, and this is its most striking feature.**

The root **م-ك-ن**: **place, location; to set a thing in its place.** *Makān*, *tamkīn*, *mumkin* are of the same root. **In Form II, *makkana*: to make one possessed of a place, to give means and power.**

**The root was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/26** (*wa laqad makkannāhum fīmā in makkannākum fīh*) — there the means given to the people of ʿĀd were described. **I rely on that treatment.**

**The comparison of the two verses is worth recording:**

| Place | Verb | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/26 | *Makkannāhum* — **given** (past) | *Fa-mā aghnā ʿanhum* — **it availed them nothing** |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/41** | *In makkannāhum* — **were it given** (conditional) | *Aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāta…* — **four verbs** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of the two patterns:** al-Aḥqāf describes how a means already given went to waste; **al-Ḥajj describes how a means yet to be given would be used.** One is a reckoning of the past, the other a condition upon the future.

### The four, and the comparison with Luqmān

**Three of the same four occurred at `031-lokman.md` 31/17. The two lists must be set side by side:**

| Order | **al-Ḥajj 22/41** | `031-lokman.md` 31/17 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāta* | *Aqimi'ṣ-ṣalāta* |
| 2 | *Wa ātawu'z-zakāta* | — |
| 3 | *Wa amarū bi'l-maʿrūf* | *Wa'mur bi'l-maʿrūf* |
| 4 | *Wa nahaw ʿani'l-munkar* | *Wa'nha ʿani'l-munkar* |
| 5 | — | *Wa'ṣbir ʿalā mā aṣābak* |

**There are two differences, and both must be recorded:**

**The first difference: the alms are in the al-Ḥajj list and not in Luqmān.** **I give this as my own reading, and its ground is the context of the lists:** the list in Luqmān is **a father's counsel to his son** — a list of personal responsibility. **The list in al-Ḥajj is the list of one who holds power** — and the alms are the only item that requires a disposal over property.

**The second difference: patience is in Luqmān and not in al-Ḥajj.** **As recorded in `031-lokman.md`, the item of patience there answered to the natural consequence of the second and third commands:** the price of saying something to people. **That item is absent from the al-Ḥajj list** — because the list is not the list of the party who pays the price but of the party **who has the means**.

**I give these two observations as my own reading; their ground is the difference in the addressees of the two verses.**

### The direction of the order

**By the analysis of direction recorded at `031-lokman.md` 31/17, the four verbs line up thus:**

| Order | Verb | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāt* | **Upward** — between the servant and God |
| 2 | *Ātawu'z-zakāt* | **Downward** — between the servant and the needy |
| 3 | *Amarū bi'l-maʿrūf* | **Horizontal, positive** |
| 4 | *Nahaw ʿani'l-munkar* | **Horizontal, negative** |

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the list opens on the vertical axis and then passes to the horizontal. **And the vertical axis is set as the first business of power.**

### وَلِلَّهِ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْأُمُورِ

**The closing of the verse enters a note upon the list as a whole.**

The root **ع-ق-ب**: heel, the back of a thing; what comes after a thing. *ʿĀqiba* — what comes at the end.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is where the sentence stands in the verse:** the verse enumerates what power will do, and then says **to whom the outcome belongs.** **The relation between the two sentences is this: the works to be done belong to man, the outcome does not.**

**And this agrees with a line the sūra has drawn earlier:** in the fifteenth verse *kayd* (the laying of a scheme) produced no outcome; in the thirty-eighth the agent of the defence was God. **In all three places man's share is separated from the outcome.**

## 22/42-45 — وَإِن يُكَذِّبُوكَ فَقَدْ كَذَّبَتْ قَبْلَهُمْ قَوْمُ نُوحٍ وَعَادٌ وَثَمُودُ · وَقَوْمُ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَقَوْمُ لُوطٍ · وَأَصْحَٰبُ مَدْيَنَ وَكُذِّبَ مُوسَىٰ فَأَمْلَيْتُ لِلْكَٰفِرِينَ ثُمَّ أَخَذْتُهُمْ فَكَيْفَ كَانَ نَكِيرِ · فَكَأَيِّن مِّن قَرْيَةٍ أَهْلَكْنَٰهَا وَهِىَ ظَالِمَةٌ فَهِىَ خَاوِيَةٌ عَلَىٰ عُرُوشِهَا وَبِئْرٍ مُّعَطَّلَةٍ وَقَصْرٍ مَّشِيدٍ

*Wa in yukadhdhibūka fa-qad kadhdhabat qablahum qawmu Nūḥin wa ʿĀdun wa Thamūd · Wa qawmu Ibrāhīma wa qawmu Lūṭ · Wa aṣḥābu Madyan · Wa kudhdhiba Mūsā fa-amlaytu li'l-kāfirīna thumma akhadhtuhum · Fa-kayfa kāna nakīr · Fa-ka-ayyin min qaryatin ahlaknāhā wa hiya ẓālimatun fa-hiya khāwiyatun ʿalā ʿurūshihā wa biʾrin muʿaṭṭalatin wa qaṣrin mashīd*

"If they deny you — before them the people of Noah (Nūḥ), ʿĀd, Thamūd, the people of Abraham, the people of Lot (Lūṭ) and the inhabitants of Madyan denied too. Moses (Mūsā) too was denied. **I granted the disbelievers a respite, then I seized them. How was My rejection of them!** **How many a town We destroyed while it was doing wrong: now it stands with its roofs fallen in; and how many an abandoned well, how many a lofty palace!**"

### The structure of the list

**Six communities and one prophet's name are enumerated. The order must be recorded:**

| Order | Name | How it is named |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Qawmu Nūḥ* | By the name of the **people** |
| 2 | *ʿĀd* | **Directly by the name of the people** |
| 3 | *Thamūd* | **Directly by the name of the people** |
| 4 | *Qawmu Ibrāhīm* | By the name of the **people** |
| 5 | *Qawmu Lūṭ* | By the name of the **people** |
| 6 | *Aṣḥābu Madyan* | By the name of a **place** |
| 7 | *Wa kudhdhiba Mūsā* | **By the prophet's name — and in a separate sentence** |

**The seventh item breaks the pattern of the list, and this is an observation about word order:** in the first six the denying party is the subject (*kadhdhabat*); **with Moses the verb is passive and Moses becomes the subject** (*wa kudhdhiba Mūsā*).

**His people are not named.** **In the classical commentaries the reason given is that those who denied Moses were not his own people alone. I transmit this as a report.**

**فَأَمْلَيْتُ** — root **م-ل-و/م-ل-ي**: to draw out, to give time. *Malā* — a long stretch of time. **The verb means "I granted a respite".**

**And the person of the verbs must be recorded:** *amlaytu*, *akhadhtuhum* — **first person singular.** Where "We" is mostly used throughout the sūra, "I" comes here. **This is an *iltifāt*, and it makes the ruling singular.**

**نَكِير** — root **ن-ك-ر**: to refuse to recognise, to reject. **The construct *kayfa kāna nakīr* — "how was their refusal to recognise Me?" or "how was My rejection of them?"** Both readings are possible, and the lexicographers record both.

### خَاوِيَةٌ عَلَىٰ عُرُوشِهَا — a picture

The root **خ-و-ي**: **to be emptied, to be left hollow; to collapse.** *Khawāʾ* — empty, desolate.

**عُرُوش** — its singular *ʿarsh*: **a roof, a covered structure.** The same word is used for "throne" as well.

**The construct is, exactly: "fallen in upon its roofs".** The walls have collapsed, the roof has come down to the ground, and the rubble lies beneath the roof.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse gives **the order** of the ruin. First the wall goes, then the roof falls upon it. **The last thing standing, then, is the thing on top.**

### وَبِئْرٍ مُّعَطَّلَةٍ وَقَصْرٍ مَّشِيدٍ

**Two more structures are enumerated, and the two are opposites:**

| Structure | Attribute | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| **بِئْر** — a well | *Muʿaṭṭala* — **idle, abandoned** | Sunk in the ground, modest, **giving life** |
| **قَصْر** — a palace | *Mashīd* — **raised up, plastered** | Built high, imposing |

The root **ع-ط-ل**: to stand empty, to be without function; to be unadorned (*ʿāṭil* — a woman without ornaments).

The root **ش-ي-د**: to raise up; and *shīd* — lime, plaster. ***Mashīd* therefore means both "raised" and "plastered".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the contrast of the two objects:** the verse has chosen two extremes — **the lowest structure (a well) and the highest structure (a palace).** One carries water, that is, life; the other displays power. **And both are named in the same end.**

**And what is striking is this: of the well it is not said "it was pulled down", but "it was left idle".** The well is still standing — but there is no one to use it. **Desolation, that is, is counted as an item separate from destruction.**

## 22/46 — أَفَلَمْ يَسِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَتَكُونَ لَهُمْ قُلُوبٌ يَعْقِلُونَ بِهَآ أَوْ ءَاذَانٌ يَسْمَعُونَ بِهَا فَإِنَّهَا لَا تَعْمَى ٱلْأَبْصَٰرُ وَلَٰكِن تَعْمَى ٱلْقُلُوبُ ٱلَّتِى فِى ٱلصُّدُورِ

*A-fa-lam yasīrū fi'l-arḍi fa-takūna lahum qulūbun yaʿqilūna bihā aw ādhānun yasmaʿūna bihā · Fa-innahā lā taʿma'l-abṣāru wa lākin taʿma'l-qulūbu'llatī fi'ṣ-ṣudūr*

"**Have they not travelled in the land, so that they might have hearts with which to understand, or ears with which to hear?** **The truth is that it is not the eyes that go blind; it is the hearts within the breasts that go blind.**"

### The building of the sentence

**The logic of the verse is built the other way round, and this is striking.**

The expected construction is: "had they hearts, they would travel and take a lesson." **The verse builds the reverse:** *a-fa-lam yasīrū … **fa-takūna** lahum qulūb* — "have they not travelled, **so that they might have hearts**?"

**Travelling, then, is set as the cause of the heart, not as its effect.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the verb *fa-takūna* is in the subjunctive (conveying result):** the verse says that seeing the ruins would bring an organ into being. **The heart is not the precondition of travelling; it is its product.**

### Two organs, and no third

**The verse names two organs: the heart and the ear.** The eye is not named.

**And this prepares the second half of the sentence:** *lā taʿma'l-abṣār* — **the eyes do not go blind.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the eye is absent from the first list; and in the second sentence it is **exempted from the ruling**. The two moves face the same way: the eye is not the matter the verse is disputing.

**At `046-ahkaf.md` 46/26 three organs were treated together:** *samʿ*, *abṣār*, *afʾida* — hearing, eyes, hearts. **There all three had been given, and all three had been of no use.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The comparison of the two verses:**

| | `046-ahkaf.md` 46/26 | **al-Ḥajj 22/46** |
|---|---|---|
| The organs | **Three:** ear, eye, heart | **Two:** heart, ear |
| The ruling | *Fa-mā aghnā ʿanhum* — **none of them availed** | *Lā taʿma'l-abṣāru wa lākin taʿma'l-qulūb* — **the blindness of one is bound to the other** |
| The order | **From outside in** — ear, eye, heart | **From inside out** — heart, ear |

**The difference of order is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** al-Aḥqāf enumerated the instruments in the order in which they were given. **al-Ḥajj puts the heart first** — and the end of the verse says why: the matter is in the heart.

**At `041-fussilet.md` 41/5 the other party had described its own condition by three obstacles:** *akinna* (coverings on the heart), *waqr* (heaviness in the ear), *ḥijāb* (a veil between). **And at `041-fussilet.md` 41/44 the text itself gave the same verdict about them.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**Setting the three places side by side shows how the matter is handled:**

| Place | Who says it | Organ | Obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/5 | **The other party** — about itself | Heart, ear, the space between | Covering, heaviness, veil |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/44 | **The text** — about them | Ear, eye | *Waqr*, *ʿamā* |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/46** | **The text** — a general ruling | **The heart** | **Blindness — and its place is specified** |

### فَإِنَّهَا لَا تَعْمَى ٱلْأَبْصَٰرُ وَلَٰكِن تَعْمَى ٱلْقُلُوبُ

**This sentence deserves detailed treatment.**

**First a grammatical matter: to what does the pronoun in *fa-innahā* belong?**

**The lexicographers explain this pronoun as a *ḍamīru'sh-shaʾn* (the pronoun of the situation):** a pronoun that refers to nothing in particular but puts the whole sentence forward. Its counterpart in English is the expression "the truth is that".

**I transmit this as a grammatical note in the form in which it is transmitted.** The pattern lays stress on the sentence that follows.

**The second matter: the logic of the sentence.**

**The verse seems to deny an obvious fact: eyes do not go blind.** Yet eyes do go blind, and everyone knows it.

**The resolution of the sentence is this, and I give it as my own reading, its ground being the context:**

**The verse is not disputing the existence of blindness as a medical condition.** The subject is the ruins described in the preceding verse: fallen roofs, abandoned wells, lofty palaces. **These are being seen.** The eye is working — indeed the verse assumes that these people travel in the land.

**What the verse says, then, is this: what is missing here is not the image.** The image is complete, the conclusion drawn is nil. **And to name this state the verse takes the word "blindness" from the eye and gives it to the heart.**

**The word is carried from one organ to another** — and as it is carried, **its ruling in its old place is expressly cancelled** (*lā taʿma'l-abṣār*). This is not a simile; **it is the moving of a word's place.**

**The third matter: why the note "within the breasts"?**

**ٱلَّتِى فِى ٱلصُّدُورِ** — "which are within the breasts".

**This note has been dwelt on, because that the heart is in the breast is already known. Two explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | Ground |
|---|---|
| A | ***Taʾkīd* (emphasis)** — in Arabic the repetition of a known thing is for stress. "The heart in one's own breast" |
| B | **Specification** — the word *qalb* is used in Arabic figuratively for "mind, consciousness"; the note states that the real place is what is meant |

**I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

**As my own reading I add this, its ground being the verse as a whole:** the verse performs a change of place — blindness is carried from the eye to the heart. **And the new address of what is carried is given explicitly.** The function of the note is to keep the transfer from hanging in the air: blindness is loaded not onto an abstract concept but **onto a particular organ in a particular place**.

The root **ق-ل-ب**: **to turn, to bring the underside up.** *Inqilāb*, *taqallub* are of the same root. **And the root occurred in the eleventh verse of this sūra as well:** *inqalaba ʿalā wajhih* — "he turned about on his face". **The same root, one the name of an organ and one a verb.**

### What this says to the present

The distinction the verse draws becomes still more visible in an age of information: **images have never been so abundant, and the abundance of images by itself produces no conclusion.**

The verse does not give this as a list of faults. **It gives it as a diagnosis:** what is lacking is not data. And the address of the diagnosis is specified.

## 22/47-48 — وَيَسْتَعْجِلُونَكَ بِٱلْعَذَابِ وَلَن يُخْلِفَ ٱللَّهُ وَعْدَهُۥ وَإِنَّ يَوْمًا عِندَ رَبِّكَ كَأَلْفِ سَنَةٍ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ · وَكَأَيِّن مِّن قَرْيَةٍ أَمْلَيْتُ لَهَا وَهِىَ ظَالِمَةٌ ثُمَّ أَخَذْتُهَا وَإِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ

*Wa yastaʿjilūnaka bi'l-ʿadhābi wa lan yukhlifa'llāhu waʿdah · Wa inna yawman ʿinda rabbika ka-alfi sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn · Wa ka-ayyin min qaryatin amlaytu lahā wa hiya ẓālimatun thumma akhadhtuhā wa ilayya'l-maṣīr*

"**They ask you to hasten the punishment.** God does not break His promise. **A day with your Lord is like a thousand years of what you count.** How many a town I granted **a respite** while it was doing wrong, and then I seized it. To Me is the journey's end."

### Two scales of time

**What the verse does is a change of scale.**

**The objection is this: the punishment does not come, so it will not come.** The ground of the objection is the time that has passed.

**And the answer opens up time itself to discussion:** *ka-alfi sanatin **mimmā taʿuddūn*** — "like a thousand years of **what you count**."

**The note must be recorded: *mimmā taʿuddūn*.** The counting party is specified. **The verse, in saying "a thousand years", is therefore not reporting an absolute span; it is making a comparison against man's unit of counting.**

**I give this as my own reading, and its ground is that note:** the sentence does not report a length of time, it reports **the difference between two scales**. And the particle of comparison (*kāf*) supports this too: *ka-alfi sanatin* — "like a thousand years", not "a thousand years".

**At `070-mearic.md` 70/4 another number occurs (*fifty thousand years*), and at `032-secde.md` 32/5 a thousand years occurs again. The difference of the numbers has been dwelt on in the classical commentaries and various explanations are transmitted. No calculation is drawn from these numbers in this commentary;** **per the method (`STYLE.md`) no reckoning of numbers is made.** The one thing recorded is that all three verses do the same work: **not to take man's measure of time as absolute.**

### أَمْلَيْتُ — the root's second occurrence

**The verb occurred in the forty-fourth verse as well: *fa-amlaytu li'l-kāfirīn*.** **It is repeated here, and this is a link that can be verified from the text.**

**The difference between the two occurrences is in the object:**

| Verse | To whom the respite is granted |
|---|---|
| **44** | *Li'l-kāfirīn* — **to persons** |
| **48** | *Li-qaryatin* — **to towns** |

**And in both the same sequence of verbs is repeated: granting a respite → seizing.**

**ٱلْمَصِير** — root **ص-ي-ر**: to pass from one state to another; to arrive. *Maṣīr* — the place one comes to.

## 22/49-51 — قُلْ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ لَكُمْ نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ · فَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ وَرِزْقٌ كَرِيمٌ · وَٱلَّذِينَ سَعَوْا۟ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِنَا مُعَٰجِزِينَ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْجَحِيمِ

*Qul yā ayyuha'n-nāsu innamā ana lakum nadhīrun mubīn · Fa'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti lahum maghfiratun wa rizqun karīm · Wa'lladhīna saʿaw fī āyātinā muʿājizīna ulāʾika aṣḥābu'l-jaḥīm*

"Say: '**O people! I am to you only a clear warner.**' Those who believe and do righteous deeds shall have **forgiveness and a noble provision**. But **those who run about among Our signs, seeking to frustrate them** are the companions of the blaze."

### إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ لَكُمْ نَذِيرٌ

**The sūra's third address *yā ayyuha'n-nās* comes here** (1, 5, 49, 73).

**And the pattern of the sentence must be recorded: *innamā* — the particle of restriction (*ḥaṣr*).** "Only, nothing but."

**I record this as a grammatical observation:** in the forty-seventh verse it was said *yastaʿjilūnaka bi'l-ʿadhāb* — "they ask you to hasten the punishment". **The forty-ninth verse corrects the addressee of that demand:** bringing punishment is not the warner's business. **The particle of restriction does exactly this work.**

**The same limit was treated at `025-furkan.md` 25/43** (*a-fa-anta takūnu ʿalayhi wakīlā*), and was recorded there as a "limit of responsibility". **I rely on that treatment.**

### مُعَٰجِزِين — root: ع-ج-ز

**The root: to be powerless, to be unable to do a thing.** *ʿAjz*, *ʿājiz*, *muʿjiza* are of the same root.

**In Form III (*mufāʿala*), *muʿājiz*: "one who strives to render powerless".** The word therefore conveys **not a failure but an effort to make something fail**.

**سَعَوْا۟** — root **س-ع-ي**: **to walk quickly, to run; to strive.**

**The construct is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** the verse does not say "those who deny the signs". **It says "those who run about among Our signs"** — the verb, that is, is a verb of motion. **Denial is described as a work done at a run, not sitting down.**

**And this makes a link with the forty-sixth verse:** there we had *a-fa-lam **yasīrū** fi'l-arḍ* — "have they not travelled in the land?" **Two verbs of motion in two verses: one required, the other censured.** The same energy, in two separate directions.

## 22/52-54 — وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ مِن رَّسُولٍ وَلَا نَبِىٍّ إِلَّآ إِذَا تَمَنَّىٰٓ أَلْقَى ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ فِىٓ أُمْنِيَّتِهِۦ فَيَنسَخُ ٱللَّهُ مَا يُلْقِى ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ ثُمَّ يُحْكِمُ ٱللَّهُ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ · لِّيَجْعَلَ مَا يُلْقِى ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ فِتْنَةً لِّلَّذِينَ فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ وَٱلْقَاسِيَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ · وَلِيَعْلَمَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ أَنَّهُ ٱلْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكَ فَيُؤْمِنُوا۟ بِهِۦ فَتُخْبِتَ لَهُۥ قُلُوبُهُمْ

*Wa mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin wa lā nabiyyin illā idhā tamannā alqa'sh-shayṭānu fī umniyyatih · Fa-yansakhu'llāhu mā yulqi'sh-shayṭānu thumma yuḥkimu'llāhu āyātih · Wa'llāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm · Li-yajʿala mā yulqi'sh-shayṭānu fitnatan li'lladhīna fī qulūbihim maraḍun wa'l-qāsiyati qulūbuhum · Wa inna'ẓ-ẓālimīna la-fī shiqāqin baʿīd · Wa li-yaʿlama'lladhīna ūtu'l-ʿilma annahu'l-ḥaqqu min rabbika fa-yuʾminū bihi fa-tukhbita lahu qulūbuhum*

"We have sent no messenger or prophet before you but that, **when he desired / when he recited, Satan cast something into his desire / into what he recited.** **God abolishes what Satan casts, then makes His signs firm.** God is knowing, wise. **That He may make what Satan casts a trial for those in whose hearts is a sickness and for those whose hearts are hardened;** and **that those who have been given knowledge may know that it is the truth from your Lord, and may believe in it, and that their hearts may be lowered before Him.**"

### First, a note of method

**This verse has been associated in the history of exegesis with a certain report. The matter was treated in `053-necm.md`, and the stance adopted there is kept here exactly. I repeat the records made in al-Najm:**

- **I do not pass over the matter**, because per the method disagreement is not concealed.
- **The report has been widely rejected in the tradition of ḥadīth criticism**; the discontinuity of its chains (their being *mursal*) and the absence of an unbroken line of transmission are given as the grounds. **I give this judgement as a report.**
- **I do not transmit the wording of the sentence that is alleged.**
- **I do not speculate on the details of the report.**

**And I add one thing more here, because this verse is intelligible on its own, apart from that report:**

**The verse itself does not narrate an event.** No name of a person, no time, no place occurs in it. **The sentence is built in the form of a general rule:** *wa mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin wa lā nabiyyin **illā** …* — that is, "it holds for all messengers and prophets without exception".

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the pattern of the sentence is universal. Its being tied to a particular incident arises not from the verse's own wording but from a report coming from outside.

### تَمَنَّىٰ and أُمْنِيَّة — two meanings

**Root: م-ن-ي. The root was treated in `053-necm.md` and in `075-kiyame.md`, and its derivatives were recorded thus:**

> - **مَنَىٰ** (*manā*) — he apportioned, he measured, he determined.
> - **مَنِيَّة** — death; "that which has been apportioned".
> - **أُمْنِيَّة** (*umniyya*), its plural **أَمَانِىّ** — a wish, a fancy.

**It is transmitted that the word is read in two separate senses in this verse, and both readings occur in the classical sources:**

| | **The first reading** | **The second reading** |
|---|---|---|
| *Tamannā* | **He desired, he wished** | **He recited** — he gave voice to a text |
| *Umniyya* | **His desire, his wish** | **What he recited** |
| Ground | The common sense of the root: to apportion, to wish | The second usage recorded by the lexicographers: *tamanna'l-kitāba* — he recited the book |
| The sense of the verse | When the messenger wished for something, Satan mixed something into that wish | When the messenger recited, Satan cast something around what was recited |

**That the second sense (to recite) exists in Arabic is recorded by the lexicographers.** **I transmit both readings and impose no preference.**

**What must be recorded is this:** whichever reading is taken, **the second half of the verse does not change.** *Fa-yansakhu'llāhu mā yulqi'sh-shayṭānu thumma yuḥkimu'llāhu āyātih* — what is cast is removed, and the signs are made firm.

### فَيَنسَخُ and يُحْكِمُ

**Two verbs in succession, and both must be recorded:**

The root **ن-س-خ**: **to remove a thing and put another in its place; and to copy.** The lexicographers record both senses: *naskh* is both "abrogation" and "transcription".

The root **ح-ك-م**: **to make firm, to restrain.** The concrete sense of the root is *ḥakama* — the bit placed in a horse's mouth: the thing that **holds and controls** the animal. From this come *ḥukm*, *ḥikma*, *muḥkam*.

**The order of the two verbs must be recorded, and I give this as an observation about word order:** first **removal**, then **making firm.** And between them stands *thumma* — a particle conveying an interval.

**The verse thus enumerates two separate operations:** the taking out of what was mixed in, and the strengthening of the text. **The second is not a repetition of the first; it is a distinct operation.**

### Two groups, two outcomes

**The fifty-third and fifty-fourth verses give two separate outcomes of the same event:**

| Who | The state of the heart | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| *Alladhīna fī qulūbihim maraḍun* | **Sickness** | *Fitna* — a trial |
| *Al-qāsiyati qulūbuhum* | **Hardness** | *Fitna* — a trial |
| *Alladhīna ūtu'l-ʿilma* | — | *Fa-tukhbita lahu qulūbuhum* — **being lowered** |

**In all three groups the heart occurs.** This is a word repeated four times across three verses.

The root **ق-س-و**: **to harden, to grow stiff.** It is used of stone.

**And two kinds of sickness are distinguished:** *maraḍ* (sickness — working but disordered) and *qaswa* (hardness — not working at all). **The two are enumerated separately.**

**فَتُخْبِتَ لَهُۥ قُلُوبُهُمْ** — **the root خ-ب-ت was treated in the thirty-fourth verse:** *khabt* — low, level ground; *ikhbāt* — to be lowered, to submit. **What the two occurrences share is the heart.**

**And setting the two verses side by side is worth recording:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **34** | *Wa bashshiri'l-**mukhbitīn*** — an attribute of a person |
| **54** | *Fa-**tukhbita** lahu qulūbuhum* — an act of the heart |

**The same root twenty verses apart: one names the person, the other says where that naming takes place.**

**And the order in the fifty-fourth verse must be recorded:** *yaʿlama* (let them know) → *yuʾminū* (let them believe) → *tukhbita* (let it be lowered). **Three stages: knowledge, faith, the settling of the heart.**

**This order agrees with the distinction treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/260:** there **faith and *iṭmiʾnān*** were distinguished and it was recorded that "one is acceptance, the other a settling within". **Here the same distinction is given in three steps.** I rely on that treatment.

## 22/55-57 — وَلَا يَزَالُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ فِى مِرْيَةٍ مِّنْهُ حَتَّىٰ تَأْتِيَهُمُ ٱلسَّاعَةُ بَغْتَةً أَوْ يَأْتِيَهُمْ عَذَابُ يَوْمٍ عَقِيمٍ · ٱلْمُلْكُ يَوْمَئِذٍ لِّلَّهِ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ

*Wa lā yazālu'lladhīna kafarū fī miryatin minhu ḥattā taʾtiyahumu's-sāʿatu baghtatan aw yaʾtiyahum ʿadhābu yawmin ʿaqīm · Al-mulku yawmaʾidhin lillāhi yaḥkumu baynahum · Fa'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti fī jannāti'n-naʿīm · Wa'lladhīna kafarū wa kadhdhabū bi-āyātinā fa-ulāʾika lahum ʿadhābun muhīn*

"Those who disbelieve will not cease to be **in doubt** about it **until the Hour comes upon them suddenly, or the punishment of a barren day comes upon them.** **On that day sovereignty belongs to God; He judges between them.** Those who believe and do righteous deeds are in gardens of bliss. But those who disbelieve and deny Our signs shall have **an abasing punishment**."

### يَوْمٍ عَقِيمٍ — "a barren day"

The root **ع-ق-م**: **barrenness, failure to bear.** *ʿAqīm* — one that bears no child; and the lexicographers record in the root the sense of "closing, stopping up" as well (*ʿuqma* — the closing of the womb).

**The word is used of wind too:** *ar-rīḥa'l-ʿaqīm* (al-Dhāriyāt 51/41) — a wind that brings nothing forth, that has no use.

**The construct here is *yawmun ʿaqīm* — "a barren day".**

**Two explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries:**

| Explanation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | **A day after which no day comes** — one that does not give birth to what follows it |
| B | **A day in which there is no good** — like the barren wind |

**I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

**As my own reading I add this, its ground being the sense of the root:** every day "gives birth" to the next; that is the series of days. **A barren day is the day on which the series is cut.** And this closes the debate about time in the forty-seventh verse: there the term was being stretched, here the term ends.

**مِرْيَة** — root **م-ر-ي**: **doubt, hesitation; and to milk** (*marā* — he milked the udder). The lexicographers make the link thus: one who doubts turns the matter over and over; one who milks draws at the udder again and again. **I transmit this connection as it is transmitted.**

**What distinguishes the word: unlike *shakk*, *mirya* is a doubt that is kept up.** And the verse says so with the verb as well: *lā yazālu* — "unceasingly".

### ٱلْمُلْكُ يَوْمَئِذٍ لِّلَّهِ

**The word order of the sentence must be recorded: *al-mulku* first, *lillāh* last.**

**In Arabic the fronting of the predicate conveys restriction (*ḥaṣr*).** Here the reverse obtains: the subject is in front, but the prepositional phrase *lillāh* stands in the position of the predicate and carries the stress.

**I record this as a grammatical observation:** the sentence reports that on that day sovereignty is with no one else. **And this links with the forty-first verse of the sūra:** there it was said *wa lillāhi ʿāqibatu'l-umūr*. **The two sentences do the same work: to whom power belongs in the end.**

**عَذَابٌ مُّهِين** — **the root ه-و-ن occurred in the eighteenth verse** (*wa man yuhini'llāh*). **The same root, one a verb and one an adjective.**

## 22/58-59 — وَٱلَّذِينَ هَاجَرُوا۟ فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ ثُمَّ قُتِلُوٓا۟ أَوْ مَاتُوا۟ لَيَرْزُقَنَّهُمُ ٱللَّهُ رِزْقًا حَسَنًا · لَيُدْخِلَنَّهُم مُّدْخَلًا يَرْضَوْنَهُۥ وَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلِيمٌ حَلِيمٌ

*Wa'lladhīna hājarū fī sabīli'llāhi thumma qutilū aw mātū la-yarzuqannahumu'llāhu rizqan ḥasanā · Wa inna'llāha la-huwa khayru'r-rāziqīn · La-yudkhilannahum mudkhalan yarḍawnah · Wa inna'llāha la-ʿalīmun ḥalīm*

"**Those who emigrated in the way of God and were then killed or died** — God will surely provide for them with **a goodly provision**. God is the best of providers. **He will surely bring them into a place with which they will be well pleased.** God is knowing, **slow to act in anger.**"

### ثُمَّ قُتِلُوٓا۟ أَوْ مَاتُوا۟

**Two verbs are enumerated separately: to be killed and to die.**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and its ground is the particle *aw* (or):** the verse **equates** the two forms of death. Whatever comes after for the one who emigrates, the promise is the same.

**And the distinction of passive and active is meaningful too:** *qutilū* is passive (they were killed), *mātū* active (they died). **One comes from outside, the other happens of itself.** Both are tied to the same ruling.

**This links with the thirty-ninth verse of the sūra:** there permission was given **to those who are fought against**. **Here the end of those who emigrate is named.** The two verses answer to two states of the same community.

**هَاجَرُوا۟** — root **ه-ج-ر**: **to part, to leave, to sever a tie.** *Hijra*, *hajr*, *mahjūr* are of the same root. **In Form III there is reciprocity:** *hājara* — to part from one another, to leave and go.

### حَلِيم — the choice of the attribute

The root **ح-ل-م**: **not to hasten, not to be carried off by anger, to move slowly.** *Ḥilm* — to meet a thing with patience and gravity.

**Where the attribute stands here is worth recording, and I give this as my own reading:** the verse gives a promise (*la-yarzuqannahum*, *la-yudkhilannahum* — both with emphasis). **And at the close comes the attribute *ḥalīm*.**

**In the forty-seventh verse the objection was *istiʿjāl* — a demand to hasten.** **And *ḥilm* is exactly the refusal to hasten.** This relation of words between the two verses is a link that can be verified within the sūra.

## 22/60 — ذَٰلِكَ وَمَنْ عَاقَبَ بِمِثْلِ مَا عُوقِبَ بِهِۦ ثُمَّ بُغِىَ عَلَيْهِ لَيَنصُرَنَّهُ ٱللَّهُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَعَفُوٌّ غَفُورٌ

*Dhālika wa man ʿāqaba bi-mithli mā ʿūqiba bihi thumma bughiya ʿalayhi la-yanṣurannahu'llāh · Inna'llāha la-ʿafuwwun ghafūr*

"So it is. **Whoever requites with the like of what was done to him and is then assaulted again**, God will surely help him. God is much pardoning, much forgiving."

### The measure: بِمِثْلِ مَا

**At the centre of the sentence stands a note of equivalence: *bi-mithli mā ʿūqiba bih* — "with the like of what was done to him".**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the requital is not left free; **its measure is the thing that came.** And the measure is bound not to the discretion of the one who requites but **to what was done to him**.

**And there is a second step in the conditional clause: *thumma bughiya ʿalayh* — "then he was assaulted again".** **The promise, then, is tied not to the first requital but to the assault continuing after the requital.**

**بَغَىٰ** — root **ب-غ-ي**: **to want, to seek; and to exceed the bound.** Both branches of the sense are recorded by the lexicographers; *baghy* is a wanting that goes past the limit.

### The word order of the closing attributes

**The verse ends, after the promise of help, with two attributes of pardon: *ʿafuwwun ghafūr*.**

**This is a striking pairing, and I record it as my own reading, its ground being that the two sentences stand side by side:**

The sentence acknowledges a right **to requite**. And at the close it names **pardon**. **Two things in one verse:** the acknowledging of a right and the recalling of pardon.

**The verse imposes no preference between the two.** The requital is counted lawful, and pardon is named as well. **And this is of the same kind as the measure recorded at `002-bakara.md` 2/203:** there too two choices (to hasten, to stay behind) ended in an equal ruling.

**عَفُوّ** — root **ع-ف-و**: **to wipe away, to efface the trace of.** It is used of the wind's effacing tracks in the sand. **غَفُور** — root **غ-ف-ر**: **to cover.** *Mighfar* (the head-covering) is of the same root.

**The difference between the two attributes is worth recording, and the lexicographers give it thus:** *ʿafw* **effaces** the trace; *ghufrān* **covers** it over. One destroys, the other conceals. **I transmit this distinction as it is transmitted.**

## 22/61-62 — ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُولِجُ ٱلَّيْلَ فِى ٱلنَّهَارِ وَيُولِجُ ٱلنَّهَارَ فِى ٱلَّيْلِ وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ سَمِيعٌۢ بَصِيرٌ · ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلْحَقُّ وَأَنَّ مَا يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِهِۦ هُوَ ٱلْبَٰطِلُ

*Dhālika bi-anna'llāha yūliju'l-layla fi'n-nahāri wa yūliju'n-nahāra fi'l-layli wa anna'llāha samīʿun baṣīr · Dhālika bi-anna'llāha huwa'l-ḥaqqu wa anna mā yadʿūna min dūnihi huwa'l-bāṭilu wa anna'llāha huwa'l-ʿaliyyu'l-kabīr*

"So it is; because God **makes the night enter into the day and makes the day enter into the night**, and because God is hearing, seeing. So it is; because **God is the truth itself, and what they call upon besides Him is falsehood itself.** And God is the high, the great."

### يُولِجُ — root: و-ل-ج

**The sense of the root: to enter through a narrow place.** *Wilāj* — an entering; *tawlīj* — to insert. **The lexicographers record the root for narrow passages, such as passing through the eye of a needle.**

**The choice of word is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** the relation of night and day is described not as **an exchange of places** but as **a seeping of each into the other**. **The boundary, that is, is not sharp.**

**At `039-zumer.md` 39/5 the same fact was described with another root:** *yukawwiru'l-layla ʿala'n-nahāri wa yukawwiru'n-nahāra ʿala'l-layl* — **ك-و-ر: to wind, to fold.** **I rely on that treatment.**

**The two verbs can be compared:**

| Place | Verb | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/5 | *Yukawwiru* | **To wind** — one is wound **over** the other |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/61** | *Yūliju* | **To insert** — one enters **into** the other |

**Both pictures describe the same fact, and both say that the boundary is not sharp.** **And I keep here the limit set by the method that was drawn in the al-Zumar section:** I draw from these words no firm conclusion about the shape or the motion of the earth. **What the verse describes is the unbroken passage of night into day.**

### ٱلْحَقّ and ٱلْبَٰطِل

The root **ح-ق-ق**: **to be fixed, to stand in place, to be realised.** *Ḥaqq* is that which does not move from its place.

The root **ب-ط-ل**: **to come to nothing, to be void, to perish.** The lexicographers explain *bāṭil* as "that which has no support to hold it up". *Bāṭil* is what falls of itself.

**The opposition of the two roots is built into the meanings of the words themselves, and I record this as an observation about the language:** one **stands**, the other **falls**. The contrast lies not on the axis of true and false but **on the axis of permanence**.

**And the sixty-second verse is a repetition of the sixth:** *dhālika bi-anna'llāha huwa'l-ḥaqq* — the same sentence, fifty-six verses apart. **This is a link that can be verified from the text.**

## 22/63-64 — أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ أَنزَلَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءً فَتُصْبِحُ ٱلْأَرْضُ مُخْضَرَّةً إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَطِيفٌ خَبِيرٌ · لَّهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَهُوَ ٱلْغَنِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ

*A-lam tara anna'llāha anzala mina's-samāʾi māʾan fa-tuṣbiḥu'l-arḍu mukhḍarra · Inna'llāha laṭīfun khabīr · Lahu mā fi's-samāwāti wa mā fi'l-arḍ · Wa inna'llāha la-huwa'l-ghaniyyu'l-ḥamīd*

"Have you not seen that God sends down water from the sky, **and the earth becomes green**? God is **one who reaches the finest thing**, aware. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. God is **the one in need of nothing**, the praiseworthy."

### The fifth verse in abbreviated form

**The same proof was given in the fifth verse with four verbs:** *hāmida* → *ihtazzat* → *rabat* → *anbatat.*

**Here it is given with a single verb: *fa-tuṣbiḥu'l-arḍu mukhḍarra*.**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and its ground is the comparison of the two verses:** the sūra gives the same proof twice. **In the first the process is detailed, in the second the outcome is in a single word.** Long when the proof is being built, short when it is being recalled.

**فَتُصْبِح** — root **ص-ب-ح**: to become morning. **The verb means "to be found in a state in the morning", and from this "to come into a state".**

**And the tenses of the verbs must be recorded:** *anzala* is perfect (past), *tuṣbiḥu* imperfect (present/future). **There is a shift of tense between the two verbs, and this reports that the fact recurs.**

**مُخْضَرَّة** — root **خ-ض-ر**: greenness. **The pattern is Form IX (*ifʿilāl*) — in Arabic the pattern used for verbs of colour and of defect.** *Iḥmarra* (it reddened), *iṣfarra* (it yellowed) are of the same pattern.

**The choice of pattern is an observation:** this form conveys that the colour comes about **of itself and completely**. **The greening, then, is described not as the result of an operation but as a coming into a state.**

**لَطِيف** — root **ل-ط-ف**: **fineness, reaching to the finest place.** **The attribute was treated at `031-lokman.md` 31/16** (*inna'llāha laṭīfun khabīr*) — there it came in the context of the mustard seed, and it was recorded that "the attribute matches the subject of the verse exactly: reaching what is smallest". **I rely on that treatment.**

**And in the present context the same match holds:** the water goes down into the soil and does its work **in a place that cannot be seen**, inside the seed. The attribute *laṭīf* suits that work.

## 22/65 — أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ سَخَّرَ لَكُم مَّا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱلْفُلْكَ تَجْرِى فِى ٱلْبَحْرِ بِأَمْرِهِۦ وَيُمْسِكُ ٱلسَّمَآءَ أَن تَقَعَ عَلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِۦ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ بِٱلنَّاسِ لَرَءُوفٌ رَّحِيمٌ

*A-lam tara anna'llāha sakhkhara lakum mā fi'l-arḍi wa'l-fulka tajrī fi'l-baḥri bi-amrih · Wa yumsiku's-samāʾa an taqaʿa ʿala'l-arḍi illā bi-idhnih · Inna'llāha bi'n-nāsi la-raʾūfun raḥīm*

"Have you not seen that God has made subservient to you whatever is on the earth, and **the ships that run upon the sea by His command**? **And He holds the sky from falling upon the earth except by His leave.** God is most kind to people, most merciful."

### وَيُمْسِكُ ٱلسَّمَآءَ

The root **م-س-ك**: **to hold, not to let go, to grasp.** *Imsāk* — a holding; the lexicographers attach *misk* to this root as well (that which holds a scent).

**And the word order of the verb must be recorded:** *yumsiku's-samāʾa **an taqaʿa*** — literally, "He holds the sky from falling".

**I record this as an observation:** the verse describes the sky's standing not as **a balance** but as **a holding**. **The present state, that is, is not described as continuing of itself; it is described as the result of a continual holding.**

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) I draw a limit:** I derive from this verse no scientific claim about gravitation or the motion of heavenly bodies. **What the verse establishes is not a description of physics but a record of dependence:** the state depends upon a holding from outside.

**إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ** — a note of exception. **Neither its not falling nor its falling, therefore, lies outside the scope of that same leave.** The verse does not report an absolute impossibility.

**رَءُوف** — root **ر-أ-ف**: **a fine-grained compassion.** The lexicographers observe a difference between *raʾfa* and *raḥma* and generally give it thus: *raʾfa* is finer and more particular; *raḥma* is more encompassing. **I transmit this distinction as it is transmitted.**

**And where the two attributes stand in this verse is worth recording:** after speaking of the sky, the sea and the earth, the verse names **man** at the close: *bi'n-nāsi la-raʾūfun raḥīm.* **The scale descends from the largest to the smallest.**

## 22/66 — وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَحْيَاكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَكَفُورٌ

*Wa huwa'lladhī aḥyākum thumma yumītukum thumma yuḥyīkum · Inna'l-insāna la-kafūr*

"**It is He who gave you life, then will cause you to die, then will give you life again.** Man is indeed ungrateful."

### Three verbs, two tenses

**The moods of the verbs must be recorded:**

| Verb | Mood | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Aḥyākum* | **Perfect** — done | **He gave life** |
| *Yumītukum* | **Imperfect** — to come | **He will cause to die** |
| *Yuḥyīkum* | **Imperfect** — to come | **He will give life** |

**I record this as an observation about word order:** **one** of the three is an accomplished fact, and it is the state the addressee is in. **The first item of the series is therefore not in dispute — the addressee is living it.**

**And the second and third are in the same pattern.** The verse builds a series in which, the first being granted, the second and third do not require separate assessment.

**This is a summary of the proof in the fifth verse.** The sūra does the same work a second time: **there the process was detailed, here it is reduced to three verbs.**

**إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَكَفُورٌ** — **the root ك-ف-ر: to cover.** *Kafūr* is in the intensive pattern.

**And the root of the word matches the subject of the verse, and I give this as an observation:** the verse builds a three-stage series of life; and ingratitude is named as **the covering over of that series**. The root conveys exactly this.

## 22/67-69 — لِّكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ جَعَلْنَا مَنسَكًا هُمْ نَاسِكُوهُ فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ وَٱدْعُ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ إِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى مُّسْتَقِيمٍ · وَإِن جَٰدَلُوكَ فَقُلِ ٱللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ · ٱللَّهُ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَكُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ فِيمَا كُنتُمْ فِيهِ تَخْتَلِفُونَ

*Li-kulli ummatin jaʿalnā mansakan hum nāsikūh · Fa-lā yunāziʿunnaka fi'l-amr · Wa'dʿu ilā rabbik · Innaka la-ʿalā hudan mustaqīm · Wa in jādalūka fa-quli'llāhu aʿlamu bimā taʿmalūn · Allāhu yaḥkumu baynakum yawma'l-qiyāmati fīmā kuntum fīhi takhtalifūn*

"**For every community We appointed a *mansak*; they perform it.** So let them **not contend with you** in this matter. Call to your Lord; you are on a straight guidance. **And if they dispute with you, say: 'God knows best what you are doing.' God will judge between you on the Day of Resurrection concerning that in which you differed.**"

### The repetition of the thirty-fourth verse

**The sentence occurred at 22/34. The differences must be recorded:**

| | **22/34** | **22/67** |
|---|---|---|
| The sentence | *Wa li-kulli ummatin jaʿalnā mansakan* | *Li-kulli ummatin jaʿalnā mansakan* |
| The addition | *Li-yadhkuru'sma'llāhi…* — **a ground** | *Hum nāsikūh* — **its performers** |
| The continuation | *Fa-ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥidun fa-lahu aslimū* | *Fa-lā yunāziʿunnaka fi'l-amr* |

**هُمْ نَاسِكُوهُ** — "they are its performers". **A nominal sentence and the pattern of the active participle: it conveys continuity.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two verses:** the thirty-fourth verse bound the *mansak* to **submission**; the sixty-seventh binds it to **the cutting off of dispute**. **The same fact is used for two separate conclusions.**

### فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ

The root **ن-ز-ع**: **to pull out, to tear away.** *Nazʿ* — to wrench a thing from its place; the sūra name `079-naziat.md` is from this root. **In Form III (*munāzaʿa*) there is reciprocity: to pull against one another, to contend.**

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** contention is described as **two parties each pulling a thing towards itself** — like pulling at the two ends of a rope.

**And the verb is in the prohibitive, but its addressee is in the third person: *fa-lā yunāziʿunnaka*.** That is, "let them not contend with you". **The apparent addressee of the command is they, but what is in practice being said is said to the Prophet.**

**The lexicographers explain this pattern as "a command in the form of a prohibition":** to all appearances a prohibition upon the other party, in reality a "do not enter into contention" addressed to the one spoken to. **I transmit this explanation as it is transmitted.**

**And this can be read together with the measure treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/46:** *wa lā tujādilū ahla'l-kitābi illā bi'llatī hiya aḥsan*. **I rely on that treatment.** **The two verses face the same way:** the dispute itself is not the goal.

### فَقُلِ ٱللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ

**The answer given must be recorded, and this is the most striking place in the verse.**

**The answer given to the dispute is not a counter-argument.** *Allāhu aʿlamu bimā taʿmalūn* — "God knows best what you are doing."

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the content of the answer:** the answer does not enter into the subject of the dispute. **It refers the matter to an authority** — and that authority does not judge in this world: the sixty-ninth verse gives its time: *yawma'l-qiyāma.*

**And the choice of verb is meaningful: *bimā taʿmalūn* — "what you do".** Not what you believe, but **what you do.**

**This links with the seventeenth verse of the sūra:** there too six groups were enumerated and the judgement was left to the Resurrection — *inna'llāha yafṣilu baynahum yawma'l-qiyāma.* **The same structure, twice, fifty verses apart.**

**And the verbs are close as well: there *yafṣilu* (He separates), here *yaḥkumu* (He judges).**

## 22/70 — أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ فِى كِتَٰبٍ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ

*A-lam taʿlam anna'llāha yaʿlamu mā fi's-samāʾi wa'l-arḍ · Inna dhālika fī kitāb · Inna dhālika ʿala'llāhi yasīr*

"**Do you not know that God knows what is in the heaven and the earth?** That is in a book. That is easy for God."

### أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ — the change of pattern

**The pattern *a-lam tara* (have you not seen?) occurred three times in the sūra: 18, 63, 65. Here the pattern changes: *a-lam taʿlam* — "do you not know?"**

**I record this as an observation about word order, and its ground is the change of verb:** in the three earlier places something **visible** was being shown to the addressee — a world prostrating, the greening ground, the running ship. **Here there is nothing to show:** the subject is knowledge itself. **And the verb changes accordingly.**

**إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ فِى كِتَٰبٍ** — "that is in a book". **The word is indefinite: "a book".** Which book is not said.

**It is transmitted that in the classical commentaries this expression is taken to indicate the *Lawḥ-i Maḥfūẓ*.** **I transmit this as a report;** the verse itself gives no name, and no name the verse does not give is inserted in this commentary.

**إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ** — **the root ي-س-ر: ease.** **The root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/185** (*yurīdu'llāhu bikumu'l-yusra*). **I rely on that treatment.** **And the root will return indirectly in the last verse of this sūra:** *wa mā jaʿala ʿalaykum fi'd-dīni min ḥaraj.*

## 22/71-72 — وَيَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِۦ سُلْطَٰنًا وَمَا لَيْسَ لَهُم بِهِۦ عِلْمٌ · وَإِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتُنَا بَيِّنَٰتٍ تَعْرِفُ فِى وُجُوهِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ ٱلْمُنكَرَ يَكَادُونَ يَسْطُونَ بِٱلَّذِينَ يَتْلُونَ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتِنَا

*Wa yaʿbudūna min dūni'llāhi mā lam yunazzil bihi sulṭānan wa mā laysa lahum bihi ʿilm · Wa mā li'ẓ-ẓālimīna min naṣīr · Wa idhā tutlā ʿalayhim āyātunā bayyinātin taʿrifu fī wujūhi'lladhīna kafaru'l-munkar · Yakādūna yasṭūna bi'lladhīna yatlūna ʿalayhim āyātinā · Qul a-fa-unabbiʾukum bi-sharrin min dhālikum · An-nāru waʿadaha'llāhu'lladhīna kafarū wa biʾsa'l-maṣīr*

"They serve, besides God, things **for which He has sent down no warrant** and **of which they themselves have no knowledge**. The wrongdoers shall have no helper. **When Our clear signs are recited to them, you recognise displeasure on the faces of those who disbelieve; they all but assault those who recite Our signs to them.** Say: '**Shall I tell you of something worse than that? The Fire!**' God has promised it to those who disbelieve; and what a wretched journey's end!"

### Two groundlessnesses

**The seventy-first verse is the eighth verse in abbreviated form.** There three grounds were refused; here two:

| Verse | What is refused |
|---|---|
| **8** | *ʿIlm* + *hudan* + *kitābin munīr* |
| **71** | *Sulṭān* (a warrant sent down) + *ʿilm* (his own knowledge) |

**سُلْطَان** — root **س-ل-ط**: **mastery, dominion; sharpness.** The lexicographers record the word in the sense both of "authority" and of "proof"; **what the two have in common is a power that overcomes the other party.**

**And the two items answer to the two sources of knowledge:** one **sent down from outside**, the other **found within oneself**. **Neither is present.**

### تَعْرِفُ فِى وُجُوهِهِمُ ٱلْمُنكَرَ

**This is the most striking sentence of observation in the sūra.**

**ٱلْمُنكَر** — root **ن-ك-ر**: **not to recognise, to find strange.** *Munkar* means "unrecognised, rejected"; and in the Qurʾān it is generally used in the sense of **evil** (at 22/41, *nahaw ʿani'l-munkar*).

**And in the sentence two opposed verbs stand side by side: *taʿrifu* (you recognise) and *al-munkar* (the unrecognised).** The two are **not of the same root but exactly opposite in sense:** ع-ر-ف and ن-ك-ر are a known pair of opposites in Arabic (*maʿrūf* / *munkar*).

**I record this as an observation about the language, and here lies the point of the sentence:** *you recognise the unrecognised.* **What appears on the face is the thing whose name is "not being recognised"; and it is plain enough to be read on a face.**

**And the object of the observation must be recorded: *fī wujūhihim* — on their faces.** Not in speech, but on the face. **The verse speaks, that is, not of a spoken objection but of an expression.**

**يَكَادُونَ يَسْطُونَ** — **the root س-ط-و: to fall upon someone, to advance on him; to raise the hand.** The lexicographers explain the word as "to bear down on someone using one's strength".

**And the note *yakādūna* (they all but) is important:** the act does not take place. **The verse describes not an event but a threshold.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the combination of the two verbs:** the scene advances in three degrees — **the expression that appears on the face → almost assaulting → but not assaulting.** The verse stops at the third degree.

**قُلْ أَفَأُنَبِّئُكُم بِشَرٍّ مِّن ذَٰلِكُمْ** — "shall I tell you of something worse than that?"

**The logic of the sentence is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** the other party's face changes because it reckons the signs "evil". **And the answer is given using their own measure:** since you find it evil, shall I tell you of **something worse**? **The reply is built out of the other party's own word.**

## 22/73 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ضُرِبَ مَثَلٌ فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥٓ إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ لَن يَخْلُقُوا۟ ذُبَابًا وَلَوِ ٱجْتَمَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ وَإِن يَسْلُبْهُمُ ٱلذُّبَابُ شَيْـًٔا لَّا يَسْتَنقِذُوهُ مِنْهُ ضَعُفَ ٱلطَّالِبُ وَٱلْمَطْلُوبُ

*Yā ayyuha'n-nāsu ḍuriba mathalun fa'stamiʿū lah · Inna'lladhīna tadʿūna min dūni'llāhi lan yakhluqū dhubāban wa lawi'jtamaʿū lah · Wa in yaslubhumu'dh-dhubābu shayʾan lā yastanqidhūhu minh · Ḍaʿufa'ṭ-ṭālibu wa'l-maṭlūb*

"**O people! A parable has been struck; listen to it.** Those you call upon besides God **could not create a fly** even if they all gathered for it. **And if the fly were to snatch something from them, they could not recover it from it.** **Weak is the seeker, and weak the sought.**"

### فَٱسْتَمِعُوا۟ لَهُۥ — a warning given in advance

**Parables in the Qurʾān are generally given directly. Here a warning comes first: *ḍuriba mathalun fa'stamiʿū lah*.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** a call to attention has been set before the parable. **And the verb is *istamaʿa* — Form VIII: to lend an ear, to listen with attention** (unlike *samiʿa*: not to hear but to listen).

**And this is directly connected with the matter treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/26.** There the example of the gnat (*baʿūḍa*) is given, and it was recorded, with the report that this example was objected to, as follows:

> It is transmitted in the classical commentaries that this verse was revealed upon the mockery of the Qurʾān's giving as examples creatures that look small and insignificant. **The answer is to the effect that the size of the example is of no consequence: the worth of a comparison comes not from the dimensions of the thing compared but from the truth of what it shows.**

**I rely on that treatment. And the two verses must be compared:**

| | `002-bakara.md` 2/26 | **al-Ḥajj 22/73** |
|---|---|---|
| The creature | *Baʿūḍa* — **a gnat** | *Dhubāb* — **a fly** |
| The work of the verse | **To defend** the giving of small examples | **To use** the small example |
| The stress | *Lā yastaḥyī an yaḍriba mathalan* — He does not shrink | *Fa'stamiʿū lah* — **listen** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the function of the two verses:** al-Baqara legitimates the method, **al-Ḥajj applies it.** And al-Ḥajj, by setting a command to listen before it, forestalls from the outset any making light of the smallness of the example.

### The two steps of the parable

**The parable contains two separate claims, and the two must be recorded separately:**

| Step | The claim | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *Lan yakhluqū dhubāban wa lawi'jtamaʿū lah* | **They cannot create** — even if they all combine |
| **2** | *Wa in yaslubhumu'dh-dhubābu shayʾan lā yastanqidhūhu minh* | **They cannot recover what is taken from them** |

**And the second step is heavier than the first. I give this as my own reading, its ground being the comparison of the two claims:**

**The first step refuses a claim to create** — and creating is in any case an extraordinary work. **The second step refuses an ordinary work:** to take back something snatched from oneself. **This is a work any living thing can do.**

**The parable, therefore, does not raise the claim; it lowers it.** And the lower it goes, the more decisive it becomes.

**وَلَوِ ٱجْتَمَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ** — "even if they all gathered for it." **The note also puts number out of play:** multitude does not change the outcome.

**لَّا يَسْتَنقِذُوهُ** — root **ن-ق-ذ**: **to save, to pull free.** In Form X: *istinqādh* — to try to rescue, to get back.

**ذُبَاب** — a fly. **The word occurs only here in the Qurʾān.**

### ضَعُفَ ٱلطَّالِبُ وَٱلْمَطْلُوبُ — the closing sentence

**This sentence is the densest place in the verse and deserves unravelling.**

**The sentence consists of two words, both from the same root (ط-ل-ب), one an active participle and one a passive participle:**

| Word | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **ٱلطَّالِب** | Active participle | **The one who seeks, who searches, who pursues** |
| **ٱلْمَطْلُوب** | Passive participle | **The one sought, searched for, pursued** |

**And the verb is given to both at once: *ḍaʿufa* — "he was powerless, he is weak".**

**The lexicographers differ over whom the sentence intends. I transmit their views:**

| View | *Ṭālib* | *Maṭlūb* |
|---|---|---|
| A | **The idol** — seeking back what the fly took from it | **The fly** |
| B | **The one who serves** — seeking something from the idol | **The idol** |
| C | **Both general** — the sentence covers both parties at once | |

**I do not impose a preference.** All three occur in the sources.

**But the work the sentence does is the same whichever reading is taken, and I give this as my own reading:**

**The sentence builds a symmetry and places the same ruling at both its ends.** The seeker and the sought, the searcher and the searched for — **both are qualified by the same attribute.**

**The consequence is this: there is no power at any side of the relationship.** Usually in a relation of demand one of the parties is strong — either the one who asks (who takes by force) or the one asked (who has the power to give). **The sentence eliminates both.**

**And that the words are chosen from the same root shows this at the level of sound as well: *ṭālib* and *maṭlūb* are built from the same letters.** **The sentence thus says by the very structure of the word that the two parties are of the same stuff.**

**This is of the same kind as the parable of the spider treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/41:** there we had *awhana'l-buyūti la-baytu'l-ʿankabūt* — the frailest of houses. **In both parables a small creature is chosen, and in both the matter is not strength but weakness.** I rely on that treatment.

### What this says to the present

The measure the parable chooses is worth recording: **a fly is a creature a man can drive off but cannot finally master.** It is harmless, but persistent.

**By lowering the claim to this level the verse ends the argument.** It makes no lofty comparison — it does not say the sun, the mountain, the sea. **It takes the smallest thing and shows that even there the outcome does not change.**

## 22/74-76 — مَا قَدَرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ حَقَّ قَدْرِهِۦٓ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَقَوِىٌّ عَزِيزٌ · ٱللَّهُ يَصْطَفِى مِنَ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ رُسُلًا وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ وَإِلَى ٱللَّهِ تُرْجَعُ ٱلْأُمُورُ

*Mā qadaru'llāha ḥaqqa qadrih · Inna'llāha la-qawiyyun ʿazīz · Allāhu yaṣṭafī mina'l-malāʾikati rusulan wa mina'n-nās · Inna'llāha samīʿun baṣīr · Yaʿlamu mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum · Wa ila'llāhi turjaʿu'l-umūr*

"**They have not valued God with the value that is His due.** God is strong, mighty. **God chooses messengers from among the angels and from among men.** God is hearing, seeing. **He knows what is before them and what is behind them.** And to God all affairs are returned."

### مَا قَدَرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ حَقَّ قَدْرِهِ

**This sentence was treated at `039-zumer.md` 39/67, and what was recorded there was this: the core sense of the root ق-د-ر is measure; the sentence reports an error of measurement. I do not repeat it here.**

**The difference between the two occurrences lies in what follows:**

| Place | The rest of the sentence | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| al-Zumar 39/67 | *Wa'l-arḍu jamīʿan qabḍatuhu…* | **Brings a scale** — the earth a handful |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/74** | *Inna'llāha la-qawiyyun ʿazīz* | **Gives an attribute** — strength and might |

**And the difference of context must be recorded, and I give this as my own reading:** in al-Ḥajj the sentence comes **immediately after the parable of the fly**. **The error of measurement, that is, is named after having been shown by way of a fly.**

**The link between the parable and the ruling is this:** to reckon as equal to Him one who is powerless before a thing the size of a fly **is an error of measure.** And the verse names that error in the language of measure: *mā qadarū … ḥaqqa qadrih.*

**قَوِىّ** — root **ق-و-ي**: strength. **The attribute occurs twice in this sūra: 40 and 74.** In the fortieth verse after the principle of repelling, here after the parable. **In both places at the end of a comparison of strength.**

### ٱللَّهُ يَصْطَفِى مِنَ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ رُسُلًا وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ

The root **ص-ف-و**: **purity, clearness.** *Ṣafā*, *ṣafwa* are of the same root. **In Form VIII, *iṣṭifāʾ*: to choose out and purify, to set apart the cleanest.**

**The picture in the word must be recorded, and I give this as an observation:** the act of choosing is named not as a preference but as **a purifying**. **What is chosen, then, is not what is separated out of a crowd but what is strained out of a mixture.**

**And the word order of the sentence is worth recording: *mina'l-malāʾikati rusulan wa mina'n-nās*.**

**Two sources are enumerated: angels and men. And both are tied to the same verb.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** messengership is set down not as the property of a kind of being but as **a choosing**. **And that the choice is made from both kinds is said in the same sentence.**

**This can be read together with the objection treated at `025-furkan.md` 25/7:** there objection was made to the messenger's being human (*mā li-hādha'r-rasūli yaʾkulu'ṭ-ṭaʿām*). **al-Ḥajj 22/75 gives an indirect answer to that objection:** the choice is made from both kinds, and the party who chooses is the same. **I rely on that treatment.**

### يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ

**The construct was treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/42:** *min bayni yadayhi wa lā min khalfih* — and it was recorded there: **"In Arabic this pair is used to cover all the directions of a thing."** **I rely on that treatment.**

**To whom does the pronoun here (*hum*) refer? The lexicographers differ:**

| View | The referent of the pronoun |
|---|---|
| A | **To the chosen messengers** — angels and men |
| B | **To all people** |

**I do not impose a preference.** Reading A appears closer to the context, since the subject of the preceding sentence is the choosing.

**وَإِلَى ٱللَّهِ تُرْجَعُ ٱلْأُمُورُ** — **it does the same work as the sentence in the forty-first verse:** *wa lillāhi ʿāqibatu'l-umūr.* **The same word (*al-umūr*), the same ruling, thirty-five verses apart.**

## 22/77 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱرْكَعُوا۟ وَٱسْجُدُوا۟ وَٱعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ وَٱفْعَلُوا۟ ٱلْخَيْرَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ

*Yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanu'rkaʿū wa'sjudū wa'ʿbudū rabbakum wa'fʿalu'l-khayra laʿallakum tufliḥūn*

"**O you who believe! Bow, prostrate, serve your Lord and do good, that you may prosper.**"

### The change of address

**Four times through the sūra *yā ayyuha'n-nās* has occurred (1, 5, 49, 73). Here, in the last verse but one, the address changes: *yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū*.**

**This is the clearest structural movement in the sūra, and I record it as my own reading, its ground being the distribution of the addresses:**

**The sūra opened by calling to the whole species and repeated that address four times.** The verses between told how that wide set is divided: the disputer, the one who stands at the edge, the one who turns his face away; and two adversaries (19), six groups (17).

**And the last two verses call to one side of that division.** **From the general to the particular, from the crowd to an addressee.**

### Four commands

| Order | Command | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Irkaʿū* | **Bow** — bend |
| 2 | *Wa'sjudū* | **Prostrate** — fall down |
| 3 | *Wa'ʿbudū rabbakum* | **Serve your Lord** — general |
| 4 | *Wa'fʿalu'l-khayra* | **Do good** — outward-facing |

**The logic of the order must be recorded, and I give this as an observation about word order:** the list opens with **two concrete bodily movements**, then rises to **a general concept** (*ʿibāda*), then descends to **the outer world** (*khayr*).

**The order, that is: body → concept → deed.**

**And that bowing and prostration are enumerated separately is striking:** both are parts of the prayer, and the verse could have named the prayer. **The prayer is not named; the two postures are commanded separately.**

**This links with the twenty-sixth verse of the sūra:** there the purifying of the House was asked for the sake of *ar-rukkaʿi's-sujūd*. **The same two words in two places in the sūra: one says for whom the place was made ready, the other says what the addressee is to do.**

**And it links with the eighteenth verse too:** there was a list of a world prostrating, and man passed **with a qualification**. **Here that qualification is closed: the command is given directly.**

**تُفْلِحُونَ** — root **ف-ل-ح**: **to cleave, to split.** *Fallāḥ* — the ploughman, the one who splits the soil. **The lexicographers explain *falāḥ* as "reaching what one wants", and the concrete sense of the root carries this: to reach the crop by splitting the soil.**

**The picture in the root agrees with the order of the commands, and I give this as my own reading:** the list begins with two movements of bending — towards the ground. **And the closing word too has to do with the ground: to split.**

## 22/78 — وَجَٰهِدُوا۟ فِى ٱللَّهِ حَقَّ جِهَادِهِۦ هُوَ ٱجْتَبَىٰكُمْ وَمَا جَعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ مِنْ حَرَجٍ مِّلَّةَ أَبِيكُمْ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ هُوَ سَمَّىٰكُمُ ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ مِن قَبْلُ وَفِى هَٰذَا لِيَكُونَ ٱلرَّسُولُ شَهِيدًا عَلَيْكُمْ وَتَكُونُوا۟ شُهَدَآءَ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ

*Wa jāhidū fi'llāhi ḥaqqa jihādih · Huwa'jtabākum wa mā jaʿala ʿalaykum fi'd-dīni min ḥaraj · Millata abīkum Ibrāhīm · Huwa sammākumu'l-muslimīna min qablu wa fī hādhā li-yakūna'r-rasūlu shahīdan ʿalaykum wa takūnū shuhadāʾa ʿala'n-nās · Fa-aqīmu'ṣ-ṣalāta wa ātu'z-zakāta wa'ʿtaṣimū bi'llāh · Huwa mawlākum · Fa-niʿma'l-mawlā wa niʿma'n-naṣīr*

"**Strive for God as striving for Him is due.** He has chosen you and **has laid upon you no narrowness in the religion.** The way of your father Abraham… **He named you Muslims before, and in this too**, so that **the messenger might be a witness over you and you might be witnesses over the people.** So keep up the prayer, pay the alms, and hold fast to God. **He is your protector: how excellent a protector, how excellent a helper!**"

### وَجَٰهِدُوا۟ فِى ٱللَّهِ حَقَّ جِهَادِهِ

**The root ج-ه-د was treated at `029-ankebut.md` 29/6 and 29/69:** **to expend strength, to go to the end of one's capacity.** *Jahd* — strength, capacity. **And it was treated at `025-furkan.md` 25/52** (*wa jāhidhum bihi jihādan kabīrā*); there it was recorded that the referent of the pronoun is **the Qurʾān** and that the text is presented as the instrument of the striving. **I do not repeat it here; I rely on that treatment.**

**Setting the four occurrences side by side shows how the root is used:**

| Place | Expression | What it conveys |
|---|---|---|
| `029-ankebut.md` 29/6 | *Man jāhada fa-innamā yujāhidu **li-nafsih*** | **To whom the striving belongs** |
| `029-ankebut.md` 29/69 | *Alladhīna jāhadū **fīnā** la-nahdiyannahum subulanā* | **The return for the striving** |
| `025-furkan.md` 25/52 | *Wa jāhidhum **bihi** jihādan kabīrā* | **The instrument of the striving** |
| **al-Ḥajj 22/78** | *Wa jāhidū **fi'llāhi ḥaqqa jihādih*** | **The measure of the striving** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of particles in the four verses:** each verse fixes another side of the root — its owner, its return, its instrument, its measure. **al-Ḥajj's is the measure: *ḥaqqa jihādih* — "as is due to it".**

**حَقَّ جِهَادِهِ** — the construct is the same as a pattern that occurred earlier in the sūra: **at 22/74, *ḥaqqa qadrih*.**

| Verse | Construct | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| **74** | *Ḥaqqa **qadrih*** | **Valuing** — and it has not been done |
| **78** | *Ḥaqqa **jihādih*** | **Striving** — and it is commanded |

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the same pattern twice, four verses apart. **In the one a failure is reported, in the other a command is given.**

**ٱجْتَبَىٰكُمْ** — root **ج-ب-ي**: **to gather, to bring together** (*jibāya* — the collecting of taxes). **In Form VIII, *ijtibāʾ*: to choose out and gather, to set apart for oneself.**

**And the verb does the same work as *yaṣṭafī* (He chooses) in the seventy-fifth verse.** **Two verbs in two consecutive verses: one for the messengers, the other for those addressed.**

### وَمَا جَعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ مِنْ حَرَجٍ

**This sentence is one of the most often cited in the sūra.**

**The root ح-ر-ج was treated in detail at `048-fetih.md` 48/17, and what was recorded there was this:**

> **The concrete sense of the root: narrowness, constriction.** The concrete usage the lexicographers record is livelier still: **حَرَجَة** — a thicket so dense with trees pressed together that it cannot be passed through. **There is thus an image of impassability in the root.**

**I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it.** **In the al-Fatḥ section this verse (al-Ḥajj 22/78) was already cited as an example.**

**The word order of the sentence must be recorded: *min ḥaraj* — the *min* is redundant (added for emphasis).**

**I record this as a grammatical observation:** in Arabic a *min* placed before an indefinite noun in a negative sentence makes the negation **comprehensive**. That is, not "He has not laid a narrowness" but **"He has laid no narrowness at all"**.

**And the sentence should be linked to the note on ease at `002-bakara.md` 2/185.** What was recorded there was this:

> *"God desires ease for you, He does not desire hardship for you."* … **The requirement itself is built upon ease.** … One concerns the structure of life, the other the design of the ruling.

**The two verses must be set side by side:**

| | `002-bakara.md` 2/185 | **al-Ḥajj 22/78** |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | *Yurīdu'llāhu bikumu'l-yusra wa lā yurīdu bikumu'l-ʿusra* | *Wa mā jaʿala ʿalaykum fi'd-dīni min ḥaraj* |
| Form | **A statement of will** — what is desired | **A statement of fact** — what has not been laid down |
| Word | *Yusr* / *ʿusr* — **ease / hardship** | *Ḥaraj* — **narrowness** |
| Scope | Context: **the ruling of the fast** | **The religion** — general |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of scope in the two verses:** the note in al-Baqara was set inside a particular ruling (the fast) and was read there as "the design of the ruling". **The note in al-Ḥajj widens the scope by saying *fi'd-dīn*.**

**And the choice of the word *ḥaraj* makes the note concrete:** al-Baqara said *ʿusr* (hardship) — a word of quantity. **al-Ḥajj says *ḥaraj* (narrowness) — a word of space.** As recorded in the al-Fatḥ section: **there is an image of impassability in the root.**

**The two verses, then, say the same thing on two separate axes: one the weight of the load, the other the width of the space to move in.**

**And that this note comes immediately after the command *ḥaqqa jihādih* is worth recording as my own reading:** the verse first commands that the striving be given **its due**, and then reports that **no narrowness has been laid down**. **The two sentences bound one another:** the utmost effort is asked, but there is no constriction in what is required.

### مِّلَّةَ أَبِيكُمْ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ

The root **م-ل-ل**: one of the senses the lexicographers give is **to have written down, to dictate** (*imlāʾ* is reckoned to the same root). *Milla* means "the way followed, the religion held to".

**The lexicographers record a difference of usage between *milla* and *dīn*:** *milla* is generally used **with reference to a prophet** (*millatu Ibrāhīm*), while *dīn* is used with reference to the servant or to God. **I transmit this distinction as it is transmitted.**

**أَبِيكُمْ** — "your father". **The use of the word here is worth recording, and I give this as an observation:** the link is established through lineage, but **it is not lineage that is meant.** The verse passes immediately to a naming — and the name is not a name of descent but **a name of attribute**: *al-muslimīn.*

**And this agrees with the line treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/130-132:** there *aslim — aslamtu* occurred as a verb, and it was recorded thus: **"*Islām* occurs here not as the name of a religion but as a verb."** **I rely on that treatment.**

### هُوَ سَمَّىٰكُمُ ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ مِن قَبْلُ وَفِى هَٰذَا

**There are two notes of time in the sentence: *min qablu* (before) and *fī hādhā* (in this).**

**The lexicographers differ over the referent of the second:**

| View | What *fī hādhā* points to |
|---|---|
| A | **The Qurʾān** — in this book |
| B | **This time / this situation** |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**And what *min qablu* points to has been debated as well:** to the earlier books, or to Abraham's prayer. **Both explanations are transmitted in the sources. No preference is adopted in this commentary.**

**What is certain is whose act it is: *huwa sammākum* — the subject is God.**

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the community does not give itself the name. **And the name is not a name of lineage or of geography; it is a name of attribute derived from a verb.**

**ٱلْمُسْلِمِين** — root **س-ل-م**: **to be sound, to be without flaw, to be in well-being.** **The root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/130-132. I do not repeat it here.** The summary recorded there: *Islām* is submission, but the submission here is not a defeat — it is **becoming whole and sound**.

### لِيَكُونَ ٱلرَّسُولُ شَهِيدًا عَلَيْكُمْ وَتَكُونُوا۟ شُهَدَآءَ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ

**Two witnessings, two degrees:**

| Who | Over whom |
|---|---|
| *Al-rasūl* | **Over you** |
| *You* | **Over the people** |

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the sentence builds a chain. **And the last link of the chain is *al-nās* — the word the sūra addressed four times.**

**The structure of the sūra closes here, and I give this as my own reading, its ground being the distribution of the addresses:**

**The sūra opened with *yā ayyuha'n-nās*** — the addressee: all people.
**In the seventy-seventh verse the address narrowed** — *yā ayyuha'lladhīna āmanū.*
**And the seventy-eighth verse binds the narrowed party back to the wide set:** *wa takūnū shuhadāʾa **ʿala'n-nās**.*

**The sūra, that is, separates a group out of the crowd it addresses and sets that group back before the same crowd** — this time not as the addressee but **as a witness**.

### فَأَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتُوا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ

**The two commands are the same as the first two items of the list in the forty-first verse.**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **41** | *Aqāmu'ṣ-ṣalāta wa ātawu'z-zakāta* — **in the indicative** | **Those who hold power** — inside a conditional sentence |
| **78** | *Fa-aqīmu'ṣ-ṣalāta wa ātu'z-zakāta* — **in the imperative** | **Those addressed** — directly |

**I record this as an observation about word order:** in the forty-first verse these two verbs described **what would be done if means were given**. **In the seventy-eighth they are commanded without the condition of means.** The difference between the two is the removal of the condition.

### وَٱعْتَصِمُوا۟ بِٱللَّهِ

The root **ع-ص-م**: **to hold on, to cling fast; to protect.** *ʿIṣma* — being kept safe; *iʿtiṣām* — to be kept safe by holding on to something.

**The concrete picture the lexicographers record: a drowning man's clinging to something.**

**And the pattern of the verb must be recorded: Form VIII, that is *ittikhādh* — a work a man does for himself.** The holding on is the act of the one who holds.

### هُوَ مَوْلَىٰكُمْ فَنِعْمَ ٱلْمَوْلَىٰ وَنِعْمَ ٱلنَّصِيرُ

**The sūra closes with the word used in the thirteenth verse.**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| **13** | *La-**biʾsa'l-mawlā** wa la-biʾsa'l-ʿashīr* — **what a wretched protector** |
| **78** | *Fa-**niʿma'l-mawlā** wa niʿma'n-naṣīr* — **how excellent a protector** |

**This is a link that can be verified from the text, and it binds the two ends of the sūra.** **The same word (*mawlā*), with two opposite rulings: one for what is called upon besides God, the other for God.**

**And the second word too comes from within the sūra: *naṣīr*.** **The root ن-ص-ر occurred many times in the sūra:** 15 (*lan yanṣurahu'llāh*), 39 (*ʿalā naṣrihim la-qadīr*), 40 (*wa la-yanṣuranna'llāhu man yanṣuruh*), 60 (*la-yanṣurannahu'llāh*), 71 (*wa mā li'ẓ-ẓālimīna min naṣīr*).

**I record this as an observation about word order:** the root is one of the densest in the sūra, and **it opened in the fifteenth verse as a doubt** — "one who supposes God will not help him". **The seventy-eighth verse ends with the word that closes that doubt: *niʿma'n-naṣīr*.**

### The measure the sūra leaves

**The sūra is a text that binds two extremes together: the most abstract thing (the shaking of the Hour) and the most concrete (an animal toppling onto its side) are inside the same text.**

**And the sentence that stands between them is the thirty-seventh verse:** *lan yanāla'llāha luḥūmuhā wa lā dimāʾuhā wa lākin yanāluhu't-taqwā minkum.*

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the sūra:** the text describes the form of worship in detail — who will come, by what road, how the animal will stand, when it will fall, to whom it will be fed. **And into the middle of all that detail it sets a sentence saying that the detail itself does not arrive.**

**Two things are said at once, and neither is let go:** form matters and is described; **form is not sufficient, and this too is said in the same place.**

**The forty-sixth verse repeats the same measure in another field:** the eye sees, and seeing is not enough. **And the seventy-third verse a third time:** the multitude gathers, and the multitude is not enough.

**In all three places the same structure: what is present is not denied, but it does not count as an outcome on its own.**

# 36. Sūrat Yāsīn

**Eighty-three verses.** It is widely reported that it came down in the Meccan period. It takes its name from the two letters of its first verse: **يس**.

The sūra builds a single claim along three separate roads, and all three converge at the same point.

- **The first road is a story** (13-32): messengers come to a town, they are called liars, a man comes running from the farthest part of the town, speaks, and receives what his speech earns him.
- **The second road is looking** (33-44): dead earth, a palm grove, night, sun, moon, ship.
- **The third road is a person's own body** (68, 77-79): the direction of a life turned back on itself, a man who comes out of a drop and sits down to argue, and a man who takes a crumbled bone in his hand and asks a question.

And the last sentence of the sūra is the same sentence as that of the man who came running. In the twenty-second verse the man says *"…and to Him you will be returned"*; in the eighty-third verse the sūra ends with the same two words: **وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text, and it will be taken up separately below.

### A note on the merits of the sūra — and the stance of this commentary

**A great many reports about the merits of this sūra are in circulation.** A significant portion of them are **disputed** in the tradition of hadith criticism; some have been judged weak, some fabricated (*mawḍūʿ*). In this commentary:

- **Reports of merit are not admitted into the understanding of the sūra.**
- Ascriptions such as "the heart of the Qurʾān" are **not presented as certain knowledge**; that such an ascription is transmitted is recorded, and no ruling is issued about its soundness.
- **The ban on fabricated quotation is applied here with particular strictness:** no saying that is not certain is ascribed to a source.

**What is worth recording is this:** the internal order of the sūra — the repetitions, rings and closures that will be tabulated shortly — shows itself without needing any report of merit at all. This section will concern itself with that alone.

The basmala was treated in earlier sections; the verses are numbered without it.

## The structure of the sūra

The sūra consists of eight blocks, and most of the block boundaries are drawn by the text's own words.

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-6 | Letters, oath, messengership, what is sent down | *fa-hum **ghāfilūn*** (6) |
| **II** | 7-12 | The word coming true, fetters and barriers, where the warning works, the record | *fī imāmin **mubīn*** (12) |
| **III** | 13-32 | The story of the town and the man who came running | *jamīʿun ladaynā **muḥḍarūn*** (32) |
| **IV** | 33-44 | Three signs: the earth, night and sky, the ship | *wa matāʿan ilā **ḥīn*** (44) |
| **V** | 45-50 | Two calls, two responses, and the suddenness | *wa lā ilā ahlihim **yarjiʿūn*** (50) |
| **VI** | 51-67 | The trumpet, the rising and two scenes | *wa lā **yarjiʿūn*** (67) |
| **VII** | 68-76 | Lifespan, the charge of poetry, the animals, the gods taken up | *mā yusirrūna wa mā **yuʿlinūn*** (76) |
| **VIII** | 77-83 | The closing proof: the drop, the bone, the green tree, "Be" | *wa ilayhi **turjaʿūn*** (83) |

**Three of the block boundaries are drawn with the same root: ر-ج-ع.** Block V ends with *lā yarjiʿūn*, block VI with *lā yarjiʿūn*, block VIII with *ilayhi turjaʿūn*. **This is a countable datum** and will be tabulated separately below.

### The spine of the sūra: ر-ج-ع

**The root occurs five times in the sūra and falls into two groups:**

| Verse | Expression | Who / what | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | *wa ilayhi **turjaʿūn*** | The words of the man who came running | **Positive — certain** |
| 31 | *annahum ilayhim lā **yarjiʿūn*** | The generations destroyed | Negative |
| 50 | *wa lā ilā ahlihim **yarjiʿūn*** | Those seized all at once | Negative |
| 67 | *wa lā **yarjiʿūn*** | Those supposed frozen where they stand | Negative |
| 83 | *wa ilayhi **turjaʿūn*** | **The last sentence of the sūra** | **Positive — certain** |

**The pattern can be verified:** three negative uses in the middle, two positive uses at the ends. And the two positive uses are **word for word identical**.

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra takes the verb "to return" out of a person's hands and binds it to a single direction. There is no going back — not for the perished generations to this world, not for the one seized all at once to his household, not for the frozen man from where he stands. The one return left is the *ilayhi* at the head of the sentence.

### "And all at once" — *idhā fujāʾiyya*

**The most conspicuous device of word order in the sūra is the particle *فَإِذَا*, which conveys suddenness.** In Arabic this is called *idhā fujāʾiyya*: it announces the emergence of an unexpected state of affairs. The English that carries it is "and all at once there they are".

| Verse | Expression | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 29 | *fa-**idhā** hum khāmidūn* | A people goes out like a fire |
| 37 | *fa-**idhā** hum muẓlimūn* | The day is stripped away, the dark is left |
| 51 | *fa-**idhā** hum mina'l-ajdāthi… yansilūn* | They stream out of the graves |
| 53 | *fa-**idhā** hum jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn* | All of them are brought present |
| 77 | *fa-**idhā** huwa khaṣīmun mubīn* | The drop becomes an adversary in argument |
| 80 | *fa-**idhā** antum minhu tūqidūn* | Fire comes out of a green branch |

**Six occurrences, in four different blocks.** **I record this as an observation:** every transition the sūra recounts — death, darkness, resurrection, coming-to-be — is given **with no intermediate step shown**. The particle erases the distance in between.

| **ر-ح-م (raḥīm)** | *tanzīla'l-ʿAzīzi'**r-Raḥīm*** (5) | *salāmun qawlan min rabbin **raḥīm*** (58) |

**The three most striking are these, and all three will be taken up again in the closing section:**

1. **ر-س-ل** — In the third verse the Prophet is told *"you are one of the messengers"*. In the fifty-second verse **the same word is spoken by those who denied**: *"the messengers told the truth."*
2. **ض-ر-ب مثل** — In the thirteenth verse the Prophet is told *"strike for them a parable"*. In the seventy-eighth verse **a man strikes a parable for God**: *"and he has struck a parable for Us."*
3. **ع-م-ل الأيدي** — In the thirty-fifth verse it is said *"their hands did not make it"*. In the seventy-first verse it is said *"of what Our hands have made"*. **The same construction, two different hands.**

### Six times *mubīn*

**The adjective that recurs most often in the sūra is *mubīn*** (root **ب-ي-ن**; the root was analysed in `002-bakara.md` and at many points in the index, and **I do not repeat it here**).

| Verse | Construction | What it qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | *imāmin **mubīn*** | The record |
| 24 | *ḍalālin **mubīn*** | The state the man supposes for himself |
| 47 | *ḍalālin **mubīn*** | The state the deniers impute to the believers |
| 60 | *ʿaduwwun **mubīn*** | Satan |
| 69 | *qurʾānun **mubīn*** | The text |
| 77 | *khaṣīmun **mubīn*** | Man |

**I record this as a countable fact.** The adjective is given to six different things in the sūra, and in all six its work is the same: **not hidden, standing in the open.** And two of them (24 and 47) are **the same construction, spoken by opposing sides.**

### On the occasion of revelation

On its being Meccan there is something close to agreement. Individual reports of revelation are transmitted for certain verses; **they will be mentioned only in the discussion of verses 77-78, cautiously and without naming names.**

## 36/1-2 — يسٓ · وَٱلْقُرْءَانِ ٱلْحَكِيمِ

*Yā-Sīn · Wa'l-Qurʾāni'l-Ḥakīm*

"Yā-Sīn. By the Qurʾān full of wisdom."

### يسٓ — two letters

**The disconnected letters were treated at length in the discussion of al-Baqara 2/1; I do not repeat those findings here.** The summary was this: twenty-nine sūras in the Qurʾān open with letters read one by one; the meaning of these letters is **not known with certainty**; one group of scholars refers the meaning to God; and **no numerical reckoning, abjad calculation or letter-counting is carried out on these letters.**

**In the discussion of Qāf 50/1 the same caveat was entered once more, and more heavily.** I enter it here a third time, because **يس** is one of the places in the Qurʾān where claims of that kind have been produced most abundantly. **This commentary does not take that road.**

**The pronunciation should be recorded:** this verse is read not as "yasin" but as **"Yā-Sīn"**, the two letters named separately. That is, these are not a word but the letters themselves.

### The explanations transmitted — and the certainty of none of them

**The table below is not a list of preferences; it is an inventory of what is transmitted.** And this has to be written above the table: **there is no certain knowledge.**

| Explanation | What it says | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Tafwīḍ (referring the meaning to God)** | These letters belong to the ambiguous verses; their manner is not entered into | The stance of the early generations; the choice of silence where the text gives no explanation |
| **"O man"** | *Yā-Sīn* means "O man" in some dialects or in another language | **It is transmitted; it is not certain knowledge.** Its ground is a claim about a dialect, and the lexicographers do not agree on that claim. **It is not adopted in this commentary** |
| **One of the names of the Prophet** | *Yā-Sīn* is a name of the Prophet | **It is transmitted; it is not certain knowledge.** The same method, applied to the other disconnected letters, does not give consistent results |
| **Vocative particle + abbreviation** | *Yā* is the particle of calling, *sīn* the first letter of a word (*insān*, *sayyid*, and so on) | It rests on supposition; it is not supported from the text |
| **An oath** | God is swearing by these letters | It is weakened by the explicit particle of oath (*wa'l-Qurʾān*) coming immediately after: the oath is already made separately |
| **The name of the sūra** | The letters are the name of the sūra | The sūra is indeed known by this name; but the same does not hold consistently for the other disconnected letters |
| **The material of the challenge** | "These are the letters the Qurʾān is made of; go ahead" | The explanation found most reasonable in al-Baqara, because it rests on an order internal to the text |

**I set the explanations "O man" and "a name of the Prophet" apart in particular, because these two reports circulate as though they were certain knowledge.** They are not. **I leave them where they stand in the table and issue no ruling upon them.**

**An observation about word order — an observation, not an explanation:** the third verse begins with *إِنَّكَ* ("you"), that is, it turns to a person addressed directly. **Those who say "Yā-Sīn is a vocative" take their ground from here.** This is the **only ground internal to the text** for the second and fourth explanations in the table, and it must be recorded. **But it is not sufficient by itself:** there are other places in the Qurʾān where direct address follows the disconnected letters, and no claim of a vocative is made there.

### The pattern: letters, then the Book

**The pattern established in the discussion of al-Baqara works here too:** most groups of letters are followed immediately by an expression that speaks of the Book. **In Yāsīn nothing at all comes in between.**

**In the discussion of Qāf 50/1 a table of the three single-letter sūras was given.** Yāsīn has to be set beside that table:

| Sūra | Opening | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Ṣād 38/1 | صٓ | *wa'l-Qurʾāni **dhi'dh-dhikr*** — possessed of the reminder |
| Qāf 50/1 | قٓ | *wa'l-Qurʾāni'**l-Majīd*** — of wide renown |
| **Yāsīn 36/1-2** | **يسٓ** | *wa'l-Qurʾāni'**l-Ḥakīm*** — full of wisdom |
| al-Qalam 68/1 | نٓ | *wa'l-**qalam**i wa mā yasṭurūn* — by the pen |

**In all four sūras the first thing that comes after the letters belongs to the domain of writing.** In three the oath is by the text itself, in one by the instrument that writes it. **This is a verifiable order.**

### ٱلْحَكِيم — root ح-ك-م

**The concrete sense of the root is to hold back.** The core usage the lexicographers give has to do with an animal:

- **حَكَمَة (ḥakama)** — the iron bit put in a horse's mouth, the rein. It keeps the animal from going where it should not.
- **أَحْكَمَ (aḥkama)** — to make firm, to bring to a state in which nothing shifts.
- **حُكْم (ḥukm)** — to bind a matter to a settled place; to bring a dispute to a stop.
- **حِكْمَة (ḥikma)** — from here: **the knowledge that holds one back from doing wrong.**

**The picture beneath the root, then, is not knowledge but a limit.** In Arabic, to be wise is not to know much but **to keep what one knows in its place**. The work of the rein is not to stop the animal but **to hold it on course**.

**A dispute about the pattern — a distinction the lexicographers record:** the *faʿīl* pattern in Arabic can carry both the sense of an active participle and that of a passive participle.

| Reading | What *Ḥakīm* means | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Possessor of wisdom** (active participle: like *ḥākim*) | The common use of the pattern; an attribute given to the Qurʾān |
| 2 | **Made firm, rendered unassailable** (passive participle: like *muḥkam*) | Agreement with Hūd 11/1, *kitābun uḥkimat āyātuh* |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The common point is this:** on either reading the word declares that there is **no slackness** — either in the one who speaks or in what is spoken.

### The answer to the oath — a comparison with Qāf

**In the discussion of Qāf 50/1 the fact that the answer to the oath is not given explicitly was treated as a problem**, and four separate views were tabulated there.

**In Yāsīn there is no such problem: the answer comes immediately after, in the third verse** — *innaka lamina'l-mursalīn*, and **with the *lām* that marks it as the answer clause** (*la-min*).

| Sūra | Oath | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Qāf 50/1-4 | *wa'l-Qurʾāni'l-Majīd* | **Not given explicitly** — the exegetes differ |
| **Yāsīn 36/2-3** | *wa'l-Qurʾāni'l-Ḥakīm* | ***Innaka lamina'l-mursalīn*** — explicit, with the *lām* |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the subject matter of the two sūras:** the subject of Qāf was the resurrection, and the oath pointed to an event that **will come to pass**; the first matter in Yāsīn is **messengership itself**. Because the answer to the oath is not an event but a **standing**, it can be stated.

## 36/3-4 — إِنَّكَ لَمِنَ ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ · عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ

*Innaka lamina'l-mursalīn · ʿAlā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm*

"You are indeed one of those sent. Upon a straight path."

### Three intensifiers, one on top of another

**The sentence carries three separate devices of emphasis, and for a short sentence this is extraordinary:**

| Device | What it does |
|---|---|
| *Inna* | Strengthens the sentence |
| *Lām* (*la-min*) | Marks it as the answer to the oath and strengthens it a second time |
| The oath itself (verse 2) | Placed in front of the sentence |

**This is a verifiable accumulation.** In Arabic the number of devices of emphasis rises with the resistance of the one addressed; the lexicographers grade this as *ibtidāʾī* (without emphasis), *ṭalabī* (a single emphasis) and *inkārī* (multiple emphasis). **The sentence here is of the third grade** — that is, an addressee who **rejects** is presupposed.

**And the rest of the sūra bears this out:** in the fifteenth verse the messengers will be told *"you are only human beings like us"*, and in the sixty-ninth verse the rejection of a charge will come: *"We did not teach him poetry."*

### مِنَ ٱلْمُرْسَلِين — being included in a list

**A point of word order:** the sentence does not say *innaka rasūl* ("you are a messenger"). It says ***lamina'l-mursalīn*** — **"you are one of those sent".**

**That is, what is announced is not a singular attribute but inclusion in a body.** *Min* here is partitive: part of a whole.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra's own story:** thirteen verses later **three messengers** will come to a town, and they too will say *innā ilaykum mursalūn* (14) — **with the same word.** The sūra frames the Prophet's position not as an exception but as **the continuation of an ongoing work**. The place of the story in the sūra is prepared by this sentence.

**The root ر-س-ل occurs in seven places in the sūra** (3, 13, 14, 16, 20, 30, 52) and will be tabulated separately in the closing section.

### عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ

**صِرَاط** — root **ص-ر-ط**. The lexicographers record the word's relation to the verb **to swallow** (*saraṭa* — to swallow): **the wide road that takes the traveller in and swallows him.** The word was treated in `001-fatiha.md`; **I do not repeat it here.**

**مُسْتَقِيم** — root **ق-و-م**, the active participle of Form X: **that which has straightened of itself, that which stands straight.** Form X here conveys not a request but **the acquiring of a state**.

**Word order:** the particle *ʿalā* — "upon". The path is given not as a destination but as **the ground one is standing on**. The sentence has no verb; it is a nominal sentence. **In Arabic the nominal sentence conveys continuity:** not something set out on once but something stood upon.

**A repetition inside the sūra — three times صراط:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | *ʿalā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm* | **The Prophet** — he is upon it |
| 61 | *wa ani'budūnī hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* | **The command itself** — service becomes the name of the path |
| 66 | *fa'stabaqū'ṣ-ṣirāṭ* | **Those who do not see** — they race along the path but cannot see it |

**All three occurrences can be verified.** **My own reading:** the same word is used in the sūra first as a position (4), then as a definition (61), then as a supposition (66). And in the third the path is standing where it was — what is missing is not in the path but in the one looking.

## 36/5-6 — تَنزِيلَ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ · لِتُنذِرَ قَوْمًا مَّآ أُنذِرَ ءَابَآؤُهُمْ فَهُمْ غَٰفِلُونَ

*Tanzīla'l-ʿAzīzi'r-Raḥīm · Li-tundhira qawman mā undhira ābāʾuhum fa-hum ghāfilūn*

"The sending down of the Mighty, the Merciful. That you may warn a people whose fathers were not warned — for they are heedless."

### تَنزِيلَ — word order and case

**The word is in the accusative, and its being in the accusative requires an implied element.** The main explanations the lexicographers give:

| Explanation | Implied | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute object** | *nazzalahu tanzīlan* | "He sent it down with a sending down" — it strengthens the verb |
| **Object of an elided verb** | *aʿnī tanzīla* / *unazzilu tanzīla* | "I mean the sending down of…" |
| **Accusative of praise** | — | Putting in the accusative for the sake of commendation |

**A nominative reading (*tanzīlu*) is also transmitted in the sources on the variant readings; on that reading the word becomes subject or predicate and the sense comes to "this is the sending down of…".** **Since I am not certain of its details or of which authorities it belongs to, I name no names.** The meaning comes out at the same place on either reading.

**The pattern of the word is worth recording:** *tanzīl* is **the verbal noun of Form II.** In Arabic this form can convey **gradualness** (*tadrīj*, doing something piece by piece, stage by stage) — as against *inzāl* (Form IV). **The lexicographers record this distinction; but I do not claim that it operates everywhere, because the Qurʾān uses both patterns.**

### ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ — the changing of the names of the one who sends down

**This matter was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/2, and a table was set up there:** in the *ḥā-mīm* sūras the one who sends down is mostly named *al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm*, whereas in Fuṣṣilat he is named *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*. The finding there was this: **the pair of names changes with the subject of the sūra, and this is a verifiable difference.**

**I add the pair from Yāsīn to that table:**

| Sūra | Names of the one who sends down | What it announces |
|---|---|---|
| al-Jāthiya 45/2 · al-Aḥqāf 46/2 | *al-ʿAzīz* · *al-Ḥakīm* | **Power and aptness** |
| Ghāfir 40/2 | *al-ʿAzīz* · *al-ʿAlīm* | **Power and knowledge** |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/2 | *al-Raḥmān* · *al-Raḥīm* | **Mercy** |
| **Yāsīn 36/5** | ***al-ʿAzīz*** · ***al-Raḥīm*** | **Power and mercy — a mixed pair** |

**What is to be recorded is this: the pair in Yāsīn takes one name from each of the two ends of the table.** *Al-ʿAzīz* from the side of power, *al-Raḥīm* from the side of mercy. **This is a countable datum.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sūra's own arrangement:** in Yāsīn the two sides stand side by side. In the seventh and ninth verses a state of being shut in will be described; in the eleventh verse forgiveness and a **generous** reward will be announced; in the fifty-eighth verse a **peace** will be given, and in the sixty-third a hell will be shown. **From beginning to end the sūra holds the two scenes together, and the names of the one who sends down announce this from the first sentence.**

**One note:** the same pair of names (*al-ʿAzīz al-Raḥīm*) also occurs as a recurring verse-ending in Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ. **The pair, then, is not peculiar to Yāsīn;** what is peculiar to Yāsīn is that the pair comes **inside the sentence about sending down**.

### لِتُنذِرَ قَوْمًا مَّآ أُنذِرَ ءَابَآؤُهُمْ — a dispute over *mā*

**The meaning of the sentence depends on which *mā* the particle is, and this is one of the places where the classical commentaries dwell longest.**

| Reading | What *mā* is | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Negating** | "That you may warn a people **whose fathers were not warned**" |
| 2 | **Relative** | "That you may warn a people with **that of which** their fathers were warned" |
| 3 | **Verbal-noun *mā*** | "That you may warn **as** their fathers were warned" |

**I do not impose a preference.** But one observation can be recorded: **the *fa-hum ghāfilūn* at the end of the sentence appears to support the first reading**, because the *fāʾ* announces a consequence — "they were not warned, **and so** they are heedless." On the second reading the consequence the *fāʾ* attaches is weaker. **This is an observation, not a preference;** the defenders of the second and third readings explain the *fāʾ* otherwise.

**And this must be recorded as well:** the first reading does not mean "no messenger ever came to their fathers". It is widely said that **the near forefathers** are meant — that is, a period in which the warning was interrupted. The thirty-first verse will say *"how many generations before them We destroyed"*; the sūra does not presuppose a past without warning.

### غَٰفِلُون — root غ-ف-ل

**The concrete sense of the root: the absence of any mark upon a thing.** The lexicographers describe the word *ghufl* as "unstamped, unmarked": *arḍun ghuflun* — land with no track or road upon it; *dābbatun ghuflun* — an unbranded animal.

**From here comes the definition of *ghafla*: the state in which there is no mark for the attention to fall upon.**

**I record this as an observation, and it rests on the lexicographers' definition of the root:** heedlessness is not ignorance. In ignorance there is no knowledge; in heedlessness **the knowledge does not strike the attention.** And the next verse of the sūra will describe exactly this: a word has come true, but *"they do not believe"*; then an obstacle to sight will be described.

**The word closes the first block of the sūra** and gives the block its closing sound: *ghāfilūn*.

## 36/7 — لَقَدْ حَقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَىٰٓ أَكْثَرِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ

*Laqad ḥaqqa'l-qawlu ʿalā aktharihim fa-hum lā yuʾminūn*

"The word has surely come true against most of them; so they do not believe."

### حَقَّ — root ح-ق-ق

**The concrete sense of the root: a thing's settling into its place, coming to pass, becoming fixed.** For *ḥaqqa'l-amru* the lexicographers give the definition "the matter became settled, became unavoidable". **The root was analysed at length in `069-hakka.md`;** I do not repeat that record here.

**What belongs here is the subject of the verb: *al-qawl* — the word.**

**It comes definite (*al-*)**, that is, a word whose identity is known. **In the classical commentaries it is said that this is the sentence announced beforehand concerning those who persist in denial.** The Qurʾān uses this pattern elsewhere too: at Fuṣṣilat 41/25, *wa ḥaqqa ʿalayhimu'l-qawl* — treated there in `041-fussilet.md`.

### فَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ — the direction of the *fāʾ*

**A point of word order, and this is the most disputed place in the verse.**

In Arabic the *fāʾ* announces **consequence and sequence**. The sentence is built in this order: **the word came true → they do not believe.**

**Two different meanings have been drawn from this, and the exegetes differ:**

| Reading | Order | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The word is the **cause**, the unbelief the **consequence** | They do not believe because the sentence has been passed |
| 2 | The *fāʾ* here announces **not consequence but explanation** | Their not believing is the **mark** that the word has come true |

**I do not impose a preference.** But two data internal to the text must be recorded, and both can be verified:

1. **The verse says *aktharihim* (most of them) — not all of them.** The sentence, then, has not been passed on the whole body. In the next block (11) the person on whom the warning **does work** will be described. **The door has been left open.**
2. **The same root and the same word return in the seventieth verse:** *wa yaḥiqqa'l-qawlu ʿala'l-kāfirīn* — **and there the verb is not in the perfect but in the imperfect (*muḍāriʿ*, future or ongoing).** The word, then, is named in the seventh verse as having come true and in the seventieth as coming true.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these two data:** the sūra says that a group has closed, but it gives that closing not as **a declaration of destiny** but as **a statement of consequence** — because it names the same word at the end of the sūra as a process still at work, and in between it describes whom the warning reaches.

**And with this verse block II opens.** The next two verses will be the **description** of the sentence "they do not believe".

## 36/8-9 — إِنَّا جَعَلْنَا فِىٓ أَعْنَٰقِهِمْ أَغْلَٰلًا فَهِىَ إِلَى ٱلْأَذْقَانِ فَهُم مُّقْمَحُونَ · وَجَعَلْنَا مِنۢ بَيْنِ أَيْدِيهِمْ سَدًّا وَمِنْ خَلْفِهِمْ سَدًّا فَأَغْشَيْنَٰهُمْ فَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ

*Innā jaʿalnā fī aʿnāqihim aghlālan fa-hiya ila'l-adhqāni fa-hum muqmaḥūn · Wa jaʿalnā min bayni aydīhim saddan wa min khalfihim saddan fa-aghshaynāhum fa-hum lā yubṣirūn*

"We have put collars on their necks, and those collars reach the chins, so their heads are forced up. We have set a barrier before them and a barrier behind them, and We have covered them over; so they do not see."

### أَغْلَٰل — root غ-ل-ل

**The root was analysed in `069-hakka.md`** (*fa-ghullūh* — "bind him"): *ghull* is **the iron ring put on the neck or the hand**, a shackle; its plural is *aghlāl*. **And in `040-gafir.md` at 40/71 the word was treated in this plural form** (*idhi'l-aghlālu fī aʿnāqihim wa's-salāsil*). **I do not repeat it here; I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is a difference in the tense of the verses, and it can be verified:**

| Place | Time | Who puts them on |
|---|---|---|
| al-Ḥāqqa 69/30 | In the hereafter, by command | Those who receive the command |
| Ghāfir 40/71 | In the hereafter, while being dragged | Not stated |
| **Yāsīn 36/8** | ***Now*, with a perfect verb: *jaʿalnā*** | **Stated: "We"** |

**In Yāsīn, then, the bond is not an instrument of punishment but the description of a present state.** The word is the same, the scene different.

### ٱلْأَذْقَان — root ذ-ق-ن

***Dhaqan*** — **chin.** Its plural is *adhqān*. In the Qurʾān the word occurs at al-Isrāʾ 17/107 in the form *yakhirrūna li'l-adhqāni sujjadan* (they fall down upon their chins), and at 109 as *yakhirrūna li'l-adhqāni yabkūn*.

**The description of the verse depends on where the word sits:** the collar is on the neck, but it rises **as far as the chin**. A mass filling the space between neck and chin makes it impossible to bend the head forward.

### مُقْمَحُون — root ق-م-ح

**The concrete use of the word has to do with the camel, and the lexicographers record this plainly:**

- **قَمَحَ البَعِيرُ** — the camel would not drink the water and **raised its head**, drawing its mouth away from the water.
- **أَقْمَحَ** (Form IV) — to bring someone into this state; to hold the head up by force.
- **مُقْمَح** — the passive participle: **one whose head has been forced up, who cannot bend forward.**

**A second branch of the root: *qamḥ* — wheat.** The lexicographers record the two branches separately and do not agree on the relation between them. **I issue no etymological ruling.**

**The whole of the description is this:** a ring on the neck, the ring resting against the chin, the head fixed upward. **Such a person cannot see what is in front of him, cannot look ahead, cannot see the ground he treads on.** He looks upward — but looking upward here is not an ascent, **it is a paralysis.**

**A point of word order:** *fa-hiya ila'l-adhqān* — the pronoun *hiya* is feminine singular and refers back to *aghlāl*. In Arabic, non-rational plurals count as feminine singular. **And two *fāʾ*s come one after the other:** *jaʿalnā … **fa**-hiya … **fa**-hum muqmaḥūn. **Three stages, that is, are chained one to the next: the ring → its resting on the chin → the raising of the head.**

**One absence must be recorded:** in Arabic *aghlāl* is generally used for the ring that **binds the hand to the neck**, and the verse does not mention the hand. **In the classical commentaries it is widely said that the hands are meant as well;** the wording of the verse does not say this. **I transmit it; I issue no ruling.**

### سَدًّا — root س-د-د

**The concrete sense of the root: to close an opening, a gap.** *Sadda'l-khurmata* — he stopped up the hole. And the second branch of the root is worth recording: **سَدَاد / سَدِيد** — **rightness, aptness.** The lexicographers make the connection thus: a right word is a word that **closes** the place left open; an apt act is one that fills the gap.

**Beneath the root, then, lies "closing what is open"** — and the word can convey both praise and obstruction. **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers frame it.**

**A variant reading — it affects the meaning:**

| Reading | The distinction transmitted |
|---|---|
| **سَدًّا** (*sadd*, with fatḥa) | A barrier made by human hands |
| **سُدًّا** (*sudd*, with ḍamma) | A natural barrier created by God |

**Both readings are transmitted.** **Most of the lexicographers do not adopt this distinction and treat the two as synonyms.** **I do not impose a preference;** I record it, because for those who do adopt the distinction the character of the barrier in the verse changes.

**A point of word order, and it matters:** the word *saddan* is spoken **twice, separately** — *min bayni aydīhim **saddan** wa min khalfihim **saddan***. It could have been said with a single *sadd*, "before them and behind them". **The repetition announces that the two barriers are set up separately.**

### فَأَغْشَيْنَٰهُمْ — root غ-ش-و

**To cover, to envelop, to overlay.** *Ghishāwa* — the film over the eye (al-Baqara 2/7, *wa ʿalā abṣārihim ghishāwa*). *Ghāshiya* — that which covers over (al-Ghāshiya 88/1).

**A third layer, then, is added.** The description in full is three obstacles, and the three lie on three separate axes:

| Obstacle | Axis | Its effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Ring + chin** | **Vertical** — the head up | He cannot look ahead |
| **Two barriers** | **Horizontal** — front and back | He cannot advance, cannot look back |
| **Covering** | **The organ of sight itself** | He cannot see |

**And the block ends with *fa-hum lā yubṣirūn*: "they do not see."**

### A comparison with Fuṣṣilat 41/5 — claim and description

**In `041-fussilet.md` at 41/5 it was treated how the addressee counts off three obstacles in his own words:** *"Our hearts are within coverings, in our ears is a heaviness, and between you and us is a veil."* The finding there was this: one of the obstacles is **inside** (the heart), one **at the entrance** (the ear), one **in between** (the veil); and **it is the other side that says this** — announcing its closedness not as a fault but as a statement of position.

**These two places in the two sūras are two accounts of the same condition. A comparison table:**

| | **Fuṣṣilat 41/5** | **Yāsīn 36/8-9** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who speaks** | **The addressee** — about himself | **The narrator** — about them |
| **Kind of speech** | **Claim / statement of position** | **Description** |
| **Verb** | *qālū* — "they said" | *jaʿalnā* — "We made" |
| **Obstacle 1** | *Akinna* — coverings on the heart | *Aghlāl* — rings on the neck |
| **Obstacle 2** | *Waqr* — heaviness in the ear | *Sadd* — two barriers, in front and behind |
| **Obstacle 3** | *Ḥijāb* — a veil in between | *Aghshaynāhum* — a covering over them |
| **The sense that closes** | **Hearing** — the sūra opens with *fa-hum lā yasmaʿūn* (41/4) | **Sight** — *fa-hum lā yubṣirūn* (36/9) |
| **Purpose** | To end the argument: *fa'mal innanā ʿāmilūn* | To show a state |

**Two differences can be verified, and both must be recorded:**

**First: the speaker changes.** In Fuṣṣilat it is the person himself who counts off the obstacles; in Yāsīn it is the narrator. **The same closedness is stated once as a defence and once as a diagnosis.**

**Second: the sense changes.** The axis of Fuṣṣilat is **hearing** — in that sūra the text arrives but does not get through, and in the twenty-sixth verse the words *"do not listen, make noise"* will be spoken. The axis of Yāsīn is **sight** — and the evidential section of the sūra (33-44) will call three times upon **looking**, saying *"a sign for them"*.

**I record this as my own reading:** each sūra has chosen the sense that closes according to its own method of proof. Because Fuṣṣilat comes with a word, hearing closes; because Yāsīn comes with a sign, sight does.

### A third place: "what is before them and what is behind them"

**The construction *mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum* occurs in several places in the sūra and in the index, and its work differs everywhere:**

| Place | Expression | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **Yāsīn 36/9** | *min bayni aydīhim saddan wa min khalfihim saddan* | A **barrier is set** before them and behind them |
| **Yāsīn 36/45** | *ittaqū mā bayna aydīkum wa mā khalfakum* | They are asked to **guard against** what is before them and behind them |
| Fuṣṣilat 41/25 | *fa-zayyanū lahum mā bayna aydīhim wa mā khalfahum* | What is before and behind them is **made to look attractive** |

**All three are verifiable textual data.** **My own reading:** the same two directions become the object of three different operations — they are closed off, guarding against them is asked, they are decked out. And the two occurrences within Yāsīn itself run **in opposite directions:** in the ninth verse those two directions are **closed**, in the forty-fifth verse guarding against those two directions **is asked of them**. What is said to be closed is later named as a field of responsibility.

## 36/10-11 — وَسَوَآءٌ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَأَنذَرْتَهُمْ أَمْ لَمْ تُنذِرْهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ · إِنَّمَا تُنذِرُ مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلذِّكْرَ وَخَشِىَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ بِٱلْغَيْبِ فَبَشِّرْهُ بِمَغْفِرَةٍ وَأَجْرٍ كَرِيمٍ

*Wa sawāʾun ʿalayhim a-andhartahum am lam tundhirhum lā yuʾminūn · Innamā tundhiru mani'ttabaʿa'dh-dhikra wa khashiya'r-Raḥmāna bi'l-ghayb, fa-bashshirhu bi-maghfiratin wa ajrin karīm*

"It is the same for them whether you warn them or do not warn them; they will not believe. You can warn only the one who **follows the reminder and fears the Merciful unseen**. Give him, then, the good news of forgiveness and a generous reward."

### An exact overlap with al-Baqara 2/6

**The sentence of the tenth verse is almost word for word the same as al-Baqara 2/6:**

| Place | Text |
|---|---|
| al-Baqara 2/6 | *inna'lladhīna kafarū **sawāʾun ʿalayhim a-andhartahum am lam tundhirhum lā yuʾminūn*** |
| **Yāsīn 36/10** | ***sawāʾun ʿalayhim a-andhartahum am lam tundhirhum lā yuʾminūn*** |

**The difference lies in one thing alone: in al-Baqara the subject of the sentence is named at the outset** (*inna'lladhīna kafarū*), **in Yāsīn it is not named** — because the subject had already been given in the preceding verse (*aktharihim*).

**This is a verifiable commonality and can be used for the exegesis of the Qurʾān by the Qurʾān:** this sentence was dwelt upon in the discussion of al-Baqara; **I rely on that treatment.** What belongs here is this: in Yāsīn the sentence comes **after a description** (8-9), in al-Baqara **inside a classification**.

**سَوَآء** — root **س-و-ي**: **equality, equivalence.** *Istiwāʾ* is from the same root. The word here does not mean "it makes no difference" but **"the two possibilities carry equal weight"**.

**Word order:** *a-andhartahum **am** lam tundhirhum* — *am* here is the *muʿādila*, the *am* of equivalence: a link between two equal alternatives. It is close to the English "whether … or …".

### إِنَّمَا تُنذِرُ — the addressee of the warning is narrowed

**And now comes the most important work of the verse: a limitation is set up with *innamā* (only).**

**What this sentence says is different from what it appears to say at first glance, and it must be read carefully.** The sentence does not say "you **can** warn only these" — in the sixth verse of the sūra the Prophet was charged with warning a **people**, and in the seventieth verse the warning will still be going on.

**What the sentence says is where the warning *works*.** In Arabic a verb can be named by its outcome: *tundhiru* here means "you bring about the outcome of warning". **The lexicographers and the exegetes record this distinction.**

**And two conditions are counted:**

| Condition | Word | What it announces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ittabaʿa'dh-dhikr* | **Following the reminder** — outward, an act |
| 2 | *Khashiya'r-Raḥmāna bi'l-ghayb* | **Fearing without seeing** — inward, a state |

**A point of word order:** the first condition is an **act** (he followed), the second an **inward state** (he feared). And the order runs from outside inward. **I record this as my own reading:** the verse ties the measure neither to behaviour alone nor to feeling alone; it joins the two with a *wāw* and sets them side by side.

### خَشِىَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ بِٱلْغَيْبِ — exactly the same as Qāf 50/33

**This construction occurs word for word in the same form at `050-kaf.md` 50/33:** *man khashiya'r-Raḥmāna bi'l-ghaybi wa jāʾa bi-qalbin munīb.*

**In both places the same three elements are present:** the verb *khashiya*, the name *al-Raḥmān*, the qualification *bi'l-ghayb*. **This is a verifiable commonality.**

**خ-ش-ي** — the root: **a fear that rests on knowledge.** The lexicographers distinguish *khashya* from *khawf* (fear): *khawf* is the fear felt in the face of danger, whereas *khashya* is the awe that arises **from knowing the greatness of what is feared**. The root has occurred in several places in the index; **I do not repeat it here.**

**بِٱلْغَيْب** — "without seeing". The *bāʾ* here announces a circumstantial state: the condition of the person. The awe, that is, is not towards something he sees.

**And the choice of name is worth recording: *al-Raḥmān*.** The one feared is named **by the name of mercy**. **This was dwelt upon in the discussion of Qāf; I rely on that treatment.**

**A note within the sūra: *al-Raḥmān* occurs four times in Yāsīn**, and the four are in four different mouths:

| Verse | Who / in what context |
|---|---|
| 11 | **The narrator** — the description of the one who fears |
| 15 | **Those who deny** — *"the Merciful has sent down nothing"* |
| 23 | **The man who came running** — *"if the Merciful wills harm for me"* |
| 52 | **Those raised** — *"this is what the Merciful promised"* |

**All four occurrences can be verified.** **My own reading:** the same name occurs throughout the sūra in the mouth of the denier, of the believer and of the one raised alike. And those who deny use it **inside the sentence of denial** (15) — that is, they know the name; what they reject is the sending down.

### فَبَشِّرْهُ بِمَغْفِرَةٍ وَأَجْرٍ كَرِيمٍ

**Word order:** *fa-bashshir**hu*** — the pronoun is **singular.** In the preceding clause *man* (whoever) had occurred; *man* is singular in form. **The warning is directed to a body, the good news to one person.** It is a difference of word order that can be recorded.

**أَجْرٍ كَرِيم** — root **ك-ر-م**. The lexicographers give the core of the root as "being the good of its kind, generosity". The adjective is used here of the **reward**: that what is given in return is **of worth**.

**And it comes indefinite** — *maghfiratin*, *ajrin*. The lexicographers explain this use of the indefinite as **magnification** (*taʿẓīm*): a quantity not stated, a limit not given.

## 36/12 — إِنَّا نَحْنُ نُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ وَنَكْتُبُ مَا قَدَّمُوا۟ وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ وَكُلَّ شَىْءٍ أَحْصَيْنَٰهُ فِىٓ إِمَامٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Innā naḥnu nuḥyi'l-mawtā wa naktubu mā qaddamū wa āthārahum wa kulla shayʾin aḥṣaynāhu fī imāmin mubīn*

"It is We who bring the dead to life, and We write down **what they have sent ahead and the traces they leave behind**. Everything We have counted up in a clear register."

### إِنَّا نَحْنُ — the doubled pronoun

**A point of word order:** *innā* already announces "we"; then comes *naḥnu*. **In Arabic this is called the *ḍamīr al-faṣl***, the separating pronoun that comes between. Its function is **restriction** (*qaṣr*): to assign the act to that agent alone.

**The sentence, then, announces not "we give life" but "giving life belongs to us alone".**

**And this matches the closing of the sūra:** in the seventy-ninth verse it will be said *"the One who brought them into being the first time will give them life"*. **In the twelfth verse the assignment, in the seventy-ninth the ground for it.**

### مَا قَدَّمُوا۟ وَءَاثَٰرَهُمْ — two separate things

**Two things are recorded, kept apart from one another and joined with a *wāw*:**

| Word | Root | What |
|---|---|---|
| *Mā qaddamū* | ق-د-م | **What they have sent ahead** — what they did themselves |
| *Āthārahum* | أ-ث-ر | **Their traces** — what they left behind |

**The root أ-ث-ر has been analysed in two places in the index, and I rely on those treatments:**

- **`046-ahkaf.md` 46/4** — *athāratin min ʿilm* ("a remnant of knowledge"): a crumb of knowledge left over from the past. The finding there: **the demand is presented reduced to its minimum.**
- **`040-gafir.md` 40/21** — *āthāran fi'l-arḍ* ("traces in the earth"): the mark in stone left by past peoples. The finding there: **those who had left marks in stone were destroyed too.**

**The core of the root: a trace, the mark that remains behind.** *Athar* — a footprint; a report handed down; a remnant.

**A table of three uses — all three treated in the index:**

| Place | What kind of trace | Its work |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aḥqāf 46/4 | **A trace of knowledge** | Asked for as proof |
| Ghāfir 40/21 | **A trace in stone** | Shown not to count as proof |
| **Yāsīn 36/12** | **A trace of deeds** | **Entered in the record** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between these three uses:** in al-Aḥqāf a trace was being asked for, in Ghāfir it was being said that a trace does not save, in Yāsīn the trace is **written into the account.** The same word, three different operations.

**And the extent of *āthār* is not explained in the verse.** It is widely transmitted that in the classical commentaries it is understood as **the effects that continue after a person's death**; concrete explanations such as **the steps taken on the way to the mosque** are also transmitted. **I transmit the explanations; I do not add them to the wording of the verse:** the wording says only *āthārahum*.

**My own reading:** the verse does not end the range of what enters the record with the person's lifetime. *Mā qaddamū* comes to a stop while *āthār* continues. And the direction of the two words is opposite: one is sent **ahead**, the other left **behind**.

### فِىٓ إِمَامٍ مُّبِينٍ — a disputed word

**إِمَام** — root **أ-م-م**: **to intend, to head towards**; and **to be in front.** *Umm* — mother (the original, that to which one returns); *umma* — a body turned in one direction; *imām* — **what is placed in front, what is looked to.**

**What the word is here is disputed:**

| Reading | What *imām* is | Its ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Preserved Tablet** — the original record in which everything is written | *Kulla shayʾin aḥṣaynāh* — the breadth of the phrase "everything" |
| 2 | **The book of deeds** | The verb of writing that governs the clause (*naktubu*) |
| 3 | **The master copy to which reference is made** | The lexical sense of the word: what guides, what is set in front |

**I do not impose a preference.** **The common point can be verified:** on all three readings the word announces a document that is **consulted and followed** — not a "ledger" but an **"original"**.

**أَحْصَيْنَٰهُ** — root **ح-ص-ي**. The lexicographers connect the root with the **pebble** (*ḥaṣā*): the Arabs used pebbles in counting, and from this comes *iḥṣāʾ* — **to count through one by one until the count is exhausted.** The word is not "to record" but **"to finish the counting"**.

**I record this as an observation:** the verb of recording (*naktubu*) and the verb of counting (*aḥṣaynā*) come one after the other, and the second is in the past tense. **The writing is going on; the counting is named as finished.**

**And block II closes: *fī imāmin mubīn*** — the first of the sūra's six occurrences of *mubīn*.

## 36/13-14 — وَٱضْرِبْ لَهُم مَّثَلًا أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْقَرْيَةِ إِذْ جَآءَهَا ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · إِذْ أَرْسَلْنَآ إِلَيْهِمُ ٱثْنَيْنِ فَكَذَّبُوهُمَا فَعَزَّزْنَا بِثَالِثٍ فَقَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّآ إِلَيْكُم مُّرْسَلُونَ

*Wa'ḍrib lahum mathalan aṣḥāba'l-qaryati idh jāʾaha'l-mursalūn · Idh arsalnā ilayhimu'thnayni fa-kadhdhabūhumā fa-ʿazzaznā bi-thālithin fa-qālū innā ilaykum mursalūn*

"Strike for them the parable of the people of the town, when the messengers came to it. We sent two to them, and they called them both liars; so We **reinforced** them with a third, and they said: 'We are messengers sent to you.'"

### Which town? — there is no certain knowledge

**This has to be written first and plainly: the verse does not name the town, does not name the messengers, does not give the time.**

**Various names are transmitted in the classical commentaries**, and these reports differ from one another; some rest on narratives regarded as Isrāʾīliyyāt, some on reports from a later period. **In this commentary:**

- **No name is given for the town.**
- **No report is transmitted about who the messengers were.**
- **The names transmitted for the man who came running are not given.**

**The ground is the text itself, and it can be verified:** the verse says *"strike for them a **parable**"* — *mathal*. **The work of the narrative, that is, is not to give a historical record but to show a situation.** When the names are not given the narrative loses nothing; **that it loses nothing shows that the names are not part of the narrative.**

**This is an interpretation, and I record it as my own reading.** But withholding the names is not an interpretation; it is **what the state of the knowledge requires:** there is no certain knowledge.

### مَثَل — root م-ث-ل

**The sense of the root: likeness, being equivalent; and setting a thing up opposite.** *Muthūl* — to stand upright, to stand in someone's presence; *timthāl* — an image. *Mathal* — **a case set in front for comparison.**

**And the construction must be recorded: *ḍaraba mathalan* — "to strike a parable".** In Arabic the verb *ḍarb* in this construction means "to set down, to put forward". The lexicographers explain it by likening it to the striking of coin (*ḍarbu'd-darāhim*): **pressing a die into a material.**

**A repetition inside the sūra — and it has turned around:**

| Verse | Expression | Who gives it | To whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13** | *wa'**ḍrib** lahum **mathalan*** | **The Prophet (by command)** | To them |
| **78** | *wa **ḍaraba** lanā **mathalan*** | **Man** | **To Us** |

**The same verb, the same object, two different agents.** In the thirteenth verse striking a parable is a charge; in the seventy-eighth it is a **matter of accusation** — because there a man sets out from his own crumbled bone and passes judgement about God. **This repetition is a countable datum and will be taken up again in the closing section.**

### ٱلْقَرْيَة and ٱلْمَدِينَة — one place, two names

**The town is called *al-qarya* in the thirteenth verse and *al-madīna* in the twentieth.** **This is a verifiable change of word.**

**ق-ر-ي** — the sense of the root is **to gather, to bring together**. *Qarā'l-māʾa fi'l-ḥawḍ* — he gathered the water in the basin. And from the same root **قِرَى (*qirā*)** — **the receiving of a guest**, that is, gathering the guest in and feeding him. *Qarya* — the place where people are gathered. **The root has occurred in `047-muhammed.md`.**

**م-د-ن** — the relation of the root to *dayn/dīn* (ديـن) is disputed among the lexicographers; some explain *madīna* as "a governed place, a place under rule". **The lexicographers do not agree on this explanation; I issue no etymological ruling.** The common rendering of the word: **a settled, ordered city.**

**Is there a point to the change?** In the classical commentaries this is generally counted as stylistic variation. **I record this as an observation and issue no ruling:** the word changes precisely in the sentence in which **the man comes from a distance** — *min aqṣa'l-**madīna***. To speak of the "farthest part" of a place presupposes that the place has boundaries and a centre. *Madīna* is the word that carries that measure.

### فَعَزَّزْنَا بِثَالِثٍ — a military word

**عَزَّزَ** — root **ع-ز-ز**. The core sense of the root is **hardness and invincibility**: *arḍun ʿazāz* — hard, stone-solid ground. From here *ʿizza* (invincibility) and *ʿazīz* (the one who prevails, who is not overcome).

**Form II, *taʿzīz*: to strengthen a thing, to reinforce it, to make its rear secure.**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "We **sent** a third"; it says **"We reinforced them with a third".** **The third messenger, that is, is not a new beginning but a support set behind the first two.**

**And this matches the first word of the verse:** in the fifth verse the one who sends down had been named ***al-ʿAzīz*** — **the same root.** In the sūra ع-ز-ز occurs twice: at 5 and at 14. **One is an attribute of the one who sends down, the other the reinforcing of the messengers.** It is a verifiable connection; I do not claim that it establishes a point, I record it.

### Word order: the order in which the number is given

**In the thirteenth verse, *al-mursalūn* — definite and plural.** How many they were is not said.
**In the fourteenth verse, *ithnayni* — two people**, then *thālithin* — a third.

**The number, then, is not given first but afterwards.** And the word order follows the order of the event: two went, they were called liars, a third was added. **The narrative begins knowing the outcome but gives the event in sequence.**

**And a change of agent must be recorded:** *arsalnā* — **the sender is "We"**. The messengers did not come on their own account. This will determine what the objection in the fifteenth verse strikes against.

**فَقَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّآ إِلَيْكُم مُّرْسَلُونَ** — and the three speak together: *innā ilaykum mursalūn*. **The very word used of the Prophet in the third verse** (*al-mursalīn* / *mursalūn*). **The sūra builds its own story with the word of its own opening sentence.**

## 36/15-17 — قَالُوا۟ مَآ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُنَا وَمَآ أَنزَلَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ مِن شَىْءٍ إِنْ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا تَكْذِبُونَ · قَالُوا۟ رَبُّنَا يَعْلَمُ إِنَّآ إِلَيْكُمْ لَمُرْسَلُونَ · وَمَا عَلَيْنَآ إِلَّا ٱلْبَلَٰغُ ٱلْمُبِينُ

*Qālū mā antum illā basharun mithlunā wa mā anzala'r-Raḥmānu min shayʾin in antum illā takdhibūn · Qālū rabbunā yaʿlamu innā ilaykum la-mursalūn · Wa mā ʿalaynā illa'l-balāghu'l-mubīn*

"They said: 'You are only human beings like ourselves; the Merciful has sent down nothing. You are doing nothing but lying.' The messengers said: 'Our Lord knows that we are indeed messengers sent to you. Nothing lies upon us but **the plain delivery of the message**.'"

### The three steps of the objection

**The other side says three separate things, and the three are of separate kinds:**

| Step | Expression | Kind of objection |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Mā antum illā basharun mithlunā* | **About the person** — the nature of the messengers |
| 2 | *Wa mā anzala'r-Raḥmānu min shayʾ* | **About the principle** — the sending down itself |
| 3 | *In antum illā takdhibūn* | **A verdict** — the charge of lying |

**A point of word order:** all three sentences are in the pattern of **restriction** (*ḥaṣr*) — *mā … illā*, *mā … min*, *in … illā*. **All three sentences, that is, say "only"; none of them leaves a possibility open.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this threefold pattern:** the objection asks no question. It builds three closed sentences. **And precisely for that reason the argument does not grow in what follows — it turns into a threat** (verse 18).

### مَآ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُنَا — and the messengers' answer

**This objection is recorded many times in the Qurʾān, and the way it is recorded in Yāsīn is worth recording: the messengers do not reject it.**

**The answer given to the same matter at Fuṣṣilat 41/6 was treated there:** *qul innamā anā basharun mithlukum yūḥā ilayya* — "**I too am only a human being like you**; it is revealed to me." **There, that is, the premise of the objection is accepted and the ground is shifted.**

**The same move is made in Yāsīn, but more briefly:** the messengers do not claim not to be human. The only thing they say is that **they have been charged** — *innā ilaykum la-mursalūn.*

| Place | Objection | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/6 | (The same objection, implicitly) | *Innamā anā basharun mithlukum* — **acceptance**, then *yūḥā ilayya* |
| **Yāsīn 36/15-16** | *Mā antum illā basharun mithlunā* | **Silence** at this point, then *innā ilaykum la-mursalūn* |

**My own reading:** both texts take the argument out of the messenger's **kind** and carry it over to his **charge**. For the objection in fact states not a proof but an **expectation**: a messenger ought to have been of another kind. And an expectation has no evidential value — **the very distinction set up for "astonishment" in `050-kaf.md` at 50/2 is at work here.**

### رَبُّنَا يَعْلَمُ — a testimony without an oath

**A point of word order, and it matters:** the messengers do not swear. They say *rabbunā yaʿlamu* — "**our Lord knows**".

**The sentence does the work of an oath but is not one:** it **produces a witness.** And the witness produced is the witness the other side does not accept.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the sentence:** the messengers do not produce a proof. The other side said "you are lying"; the messengers do not say "here is the evidence", they **say who it is that knows.** And immediately afterwards they draw the boundary of their responsibility.

### وَمَا عَلَيْنَآ إِلَّا ٱلْبَلَٰغُ ٱلْمُبِينُ — the sentence that sets the limit

**ب-ل-غ** — the root: **a thing's arriving at the place it is going to.** *Bulūgh* — maturity (the body's arriving at the point it is going to); *balāgha* — a word's arriving fully at its aim. ***Balāgh*** — **the act of delivery itself.**

**The work of the word is worth recording:** *balāgh* is the **delivering** of the word, not the getting of it accepted. Where the act ends is the word's arrival at the one addressed.

**ٱلْمُبِين** — not one of the sūra's six occurrences of *mubīn*; here it comes **definite**, *al-mubīn*, and it is an attribute of *balāgh*. **The delivery, that is, must also be plain:** a delivery that cannot be understood does not count as delivery.

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence limits the responsibility from two sides at once. **From above:** they are not responsible for the outcome. **From below:** the delivery is required to be **plain**. The responsibility is neither widened nor lifted.

## 36/18-19 — قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّا تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ لَئِن لَّمْ تَنتَهُوا۟ لَنَرْجُمَنَّكُمْ وَلَيَمَسَّنَّكُم مِّنَّا عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ · قَالُوا۟ طَٰٓئِرُكُم مَّعَكُمْ أَئِن ذُكِّرْتُم بَلْ أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ مُّسْرِفُونَ

*Qālū innā taṭayyarnā bikum, la-in lam tantahū la-narjumannakum wa la-yamassannakum minnā ʿadhābun alīm · Qālū ṭāʾirukum maʿakum, a-in dhukkirtum, bal antum qawmun musrifūn*

"They said: 'We have **met with ill omen** through you. If you do not stop, we will surely stone you, and a painful punishment from us will surely touch you.' The messengers said: '**Your omen is with you.** Is it because you have been reminded? No — you are a people who go beyond all bounds.'"

### تَطَيَّرْنَا — root ط-ي-ر and the name of a custom

**The concrete sense of the root is to fly.** *Ṭāʾir* — a bird; *ṭayr* — birds. **The root has occurred in `067-mulk.md` and `056-vakia.md`** (the flight of birds, the use *lāgha'ṭ-ṭayru*); **I do not repeat the basic analysis of the root.**

**What belongs here is the word in Form V: *taṭayyara* — to take as an ill omen.** And behind this lies **a documented Arab custom**.

**The custom is this, and the historians and the lexicographers record it:** before setting about a task or setting out on a journey, a bird would be startled up and the direction of its flight watched.

| Term | The bird's direction | How it was read |
|---|---|---|
| **سَانِح (*sāniḥ*)** | To the right | Counted as a good omen |
| **بَارِح (*bāriḥ*)** | To the left | Counted as an ill omen |

**This was called *zajr al-ṭayr* (the startling of the bird).** And this business, born of putting a bird to flight, turned in the end into a word used **with no bird left in it** for taking anything whatever for a bad sign. This is what the English **"ill omen"** answers to.

**The pattern must be recorded: Form V (*tafaʿʿul*).** In Arabic this form most often conveys **a person's taking something upon himself, appropriating it to himself.** ***Taṭayyur*, that is, is not ill fortune coming to a person but a person's *counting* something ill-omened.** The subject of the verb is the one who does the reading.

**And the messengers' answer presses on exactly this point.**

### طَٰٓئِرُكُم مَّعَكُمْ — the structure of the answer

**The answer takes the word of the objection and changes its place.** They said *taṭayyarnā **bikum*** — "we met with ill omen **through you**", that is, the cause is outside. The messengers say *ṭāʾirukum **maʿakum*** — "**your bird is with you**", that is, the cause is inside.

**A point of word order:** in both sentences the same root, but **the particle changes:** *bāʾ* (which announces cause) → *maʿa* (which announces accompaniment). **With the change of one particle the responsibility changes place.** This can be verified from the text.

**And the choice of word must be recorded:** the messengers do not use an abstract word, they use the other side's own word. **The argument is carried on inside the other side's vocabulary.**

### The same charge in the Qurʾān — three places

**The saying to messengers "you have brought ill fortune" is recorded in three separate stories in the Qurʾān:**

| Place | Who says it | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| al-Aʿrāf 7/131 | **Pharaoh's people** — about Moses (Mūsā) | *Yaṭṭayyarū bi-Mūsā wa man maʿah* |
| al-Naml 27/47 | **Thamūd** — about Ṣāliḥ | *Iṭṭayyarnā bika wa bi-man maʿak* |
| **Yāsīn 36/18** | **The people of the town** — about the messengers | *Innā taṭayyarnā bikum* |

**All three are verifiable textual data.** **My own reading:** the same move is repeated in three separate stories, and its structure is always the same: **there is a hardship, and the newcomer is pointed to as the cause of the hardship.** This is a move that produces no proof but ends the argument — because once the person named as the cause has been removed, the claim never gets tested.

### لَنَرْجُمَنَّكُمْ — root ر-ج-م

**The concrete sense: to throw stones, to stone.** *Rajm* — stoning. And the second branch of the root: **to revile, to drive off, to banish.** *Al-Shayṭānu'r-rajīm* — the banished Satan.

**In which sense in the verse?** The lexicographers record both possibilities: **actual stoning** or **stoning with words (grave insult).** **I do not impose a preference.** But an observation: in the continuation of the sentence a *ʿadhābun alīm* (painful punishment) is named separately. **Since the two are counted separately, *rajm* and *ʿadhāb* must be two different things** — this appears to support the first sense. **It is an observation, not a preference.**

**Word order:** *la-in lam tantahū* — the protasis; *la-narjumannakum* — the apodosis, strengthened with the **heavy nūn of emphasis** (*nūn al-tawkīd al-thaqīla*). And the second clause with the same nūn: *la-yamassannakum*. **Both threats are in the heaviest pattern of emphasis.**

**نَتَهُوا۟ / تَنتَهُوا۟** — root **ن-ه-ي**: to reach the last limit of a thing; from here *nahy* (prohibition) and, in Form VIII, *intihāʾ* — **to desist, to bring to an end.** "If you do not stop" — that is, what is asked for is the breaking off of the speech.

### أَئِن ذُكِّرْتُم — a sentence left incomplete

**This expression is the most disputed place in the verse, because the sentence is not completed.**

**The structure: hamza (interrogative) + *in* (conditional) + *dhukkirtum* (the verb of the condition).** The apodosis of the condition is **not stated.**

| Supplied element | The completed form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *A-in dhukkirtum **taṭayyartum**?* | "Is it because you have been reminded that you took it for an ill omen?" |
| 2 | *A-in dhukkirtum **tawaʿʿadtum***? | "Is it because you have been reminded that you threaten?" |
| 3 | The apodosis is understood from what precedes | "Is it because you have been reminded that [all this]?" |

**All three supplied readings are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The common point can be verified:** in all three the condition is the same — *dhukkirtum*, "you have been reminded."

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the messengers name their own work **"reminding"**. The root is **ذ-ك-ر**. What comes, that is, is described not as new information but as **the setting in front** of what has been forgotten. And this is the same root as *ittabaʿa'**dh-dhikra*** in the eleventh verse.

**Different readings of this word are transmitted in the sources on the variant readings** (*dhukkirtum* / *idhdhakkartum* and the like). **Since I am not certain of the details I give no names and no particulars;** the difference in meaning is not fundamental.

### مُّسْرِفُون — root س-ر-ف

**The concrete sense: to pass the limit in something, to overstep the measure.** In English *extravagance* is used only of spending; **in Arabic the root announces overstepping the bound in every domain.**

**And the diagnosis must be recorded:** the messengers do not call the other side "liars" or "ignorant". They say ***musrif*** — **one who goes beyond the bound.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sense of the word:** the diagnosis is not about the other side's **state of knowledge** but about its **state of measure**. The problem is not not knowing but **not stopping where one should stop.** And this agrees with the content of the eighteenth verse: to a word a **threat of stoning** has been given in return — the disproportion is plain.

**Word order:** *bal* — the particle of *iḍrāb*. **This particle was treated in `050-kaf.md` at 50/2:** leaving what precedes and passing to the real matter. So here: the question is broken off midway and the diagnosis is laid down.

## 36/20-21 — وَجَآءَ مِنْ أَقْصَا ٱلْمَدِينَةِ رَجُلٌ يَسْعَىٰ قَالَ يَٰقَوْمِ ٱتَّبِعُوا۟ ٱلْمُرْسَلِينَ · ٱتَّبِعُوا۟ مَن لَّا يَسْـَٔلُكُمْ أَجْرًا وَهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ

*Wa jāʾa min aqṣa'l-madīnati rajulun yasʿā, qāla yā qawmi'ttabiʿu'l-mursalīn · Ittabiʿū man lā yasʾalukum ajran wa hum muhtadūn*

"And from the farthest part of the city a man came **running** and said: 'O my people! Follow the messengers. Follow those who **ask of you no reward** and who are rightly guided.'"

### How the man is introduced

**The verse says four things, and all four are built on indefiniteness:**

| Element | Text | What it gives / withholds |
|---|---|---|
| **From where** | *min aqṣa'l-madīna* | Not a place, but **a distance** |
| **Who** | *rajulun* — **indefinite** | Not a name, but **a kind** |
| **What he is doing** | *yasʿā* — imperfect, circumstantial | Not an attribute, but **a movement** |
| **What he said** | *yā qawmi* | Not a position, but **a belonging** |

**His name is not given. His trade, his age, his standing are not given.** The only information about position that is given is a **distance**: *aqṣā* — farthest.

**أ-ق-ص-ى / ق-ص-و** — the root: **distance.** *Qaṣā* — he went far off; *aqṣā* — the elative: **farthest.** The word also occurs in the Qurʾān in the construction *al-masjid al-aqṣā* (al-Isrāʾ 17/1).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word itself:** the man comes not from the centre but **from the edge**. The part of the city that decides, argues and threatens is in the middle; this person joins it **on foot, from outside.** And after he has said his say he is on his own again — no one joins him.

### يَسْعَىٰ — root س-ع-ي

**The concrete sense of the root: to walk fast, to go at a pace close to running.** The root has occurred in many places in the index (`079-naziat.md`, `062-cuma.md`, `092-leyl.md` and others); **I do not repeat its basic analysis.**

**Two things belong here:**

**First — the second branch of the root: *saʿy* is at the same time "to work, to exert oneself".** *Wa an laysa li'l-insāni illā mā saʿā* (al-Najm 53/39). The word, that is, announces both the speed of the body and the effort of the will. **The lexicographers record this doubleness.**

**Second — word order:** *rajulun **yasʿā*** — an imperfect verb serving as an attribute to an indefinite noun. **In Arabic the imperfect verb announces continuity and liveliness.** The man is introduced not as one who "had come" but as **"a man coming at a run"**. The verb is still going on in the scene.

**My own reading:** running is the sign of being aware of lateness. The verse does not describe the man's intention; it describes **his speed** — and how much a person cares about a matter usually shows in the pace at which he goes to it.

### يَٰقَوْمِ — the choice of address

**The man's first word is *yā qawmi* — "O my people".**

**A point of word order:** not *qawmī* but ***qawmi***; the *yāʾ* at the end (the first-person pronoun) has been dropped and a kasra left in its place. In Arabic this elision is common in the vocative and announces **closeness**.

**And the word itself must be recorded: the man says to them not "O people" but "O my people".** He counts the body he has stepped in front of as his own body. **This is the key to the whole story, and it will return in the twenty-sixth verse** — there too he will say *qawmī*.

### ٱتَّبِعُوا۟ مَن لَّا يَسْـَٔلُكُمْ أَجْرًا — a testable measure

**The argument the man builds is the most striking piece of the story, and its structure should be examined.**

**The man does not prove that the messengers are telling the truth.** He gives a **measure**: *lā yasʾalukum ajran* — **"they ask no reward of you."**

**The property of this measure is this, and it can be verified:** the other side cannot test the messengers' word — because the word concerns the hereafter. But it **can test the messengers' interest**, because that is something visible. **The man puts forward what can be tested.**

**And the word order supports this:** the sentence is built **twice** with the imperative *ittabiʿū* — at 20 *ittabiʿu'l-mursalīn* (follow the messengers), at 21 *ittabiʿū man lā yasʾalukum ajran* (follow those who ask no reward). **The second sentence is not a repetition of the first but its ground.** The same command is given a second time **with an attribute**.

| Sentence | Command | The name of the one addressed |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | *Ittabiʿū* | ***al-mursalīn*** — "the messengers", that is, their claim |
| 21 | *Ittabiʿū* | ***man lā yasʾalukum ajran*** — "those who ask no reward", that is, their testable attribute |

**I record this as my own reading:** the man advances in two steps. First he names them as messengers — he states his own acceptance. Then he names the same people **with an attribute the other side too can accept**. **The argument, that is, comes down to common ground.**

**A point of word order — a shift of number:** *man lā yasʾalu**kum*** (singular verb), then *wa **hum** muhtadūn* (plural pronoun). **In Arabic *man* can be singular in form and plural in sense;** the sentence follows the form first and the sense afterwards. **The lexicographers call this *ḥamlu'l-lafẓ* and *ḥamlu'l-maʿnā*.** This is not an error but a settled usage.

**مُهْتَدُون** — root **ه-د-ي**, the active participle of Form VIII: **one who has found the way.** Form VIII here announces a state **realised in the person himself**. And note: not *hādūn* (those who guide) but ***muhtadūn*** (those who have found the way). **The man names the messengers not as people who direct others but as people who themselves stand in the right place.**

## 36/22-24 — وَمَا لِىَ لَآ أَعْبُدُ ٱلَّذِى فَطَرَنِى وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ · ءَأَتَّخِذُ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ ءَالِهَةً إِن يُرِدْنِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ بِضُرٍّ لَّا تُغْنِ عَنِّى شَفَٰعَتُهُمْ شَيْـًٔا وَلَا يُنقِذُونِ · إِنِّىٓ إِذًا لَّفِى ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Wa mā liya lā aʿbudu'lladhī faṭaranī wa ilayhi turjaʿūn · A-attakhidhu min dūnihī ālihatan in yuridni'r-Raḥmānu bi-ḍurrin lā tughni ʿannī shafāʿatuhum shayʾan wa lā yunqidhūn · Innī idhan lafī ḍalālin mubīn*

"**And why should I not serve the One who brought me into being?** — and to Him you will be returned. Shall I take gods besides Him? If the Merciful wills harm for me, their intercession will avail me nothing and they cannot rescue me. **Then I would indeed be in plain error.**"

### The most striking side of the argument: the first person

**The man does not say to his people "why do you not serve?"** He builds both sentences **upon himself**:

- *Wa mā **liya** lā **aʿbudu*** — "what is it **to me** that I should **not serve**?"
- *A-**attakhidhu*** — "shall **I** take?"
- *Innī idhan lafī ḍalālin mubīn* — "**then I** would be in plain error."

**All three sentences are in the first person singular. This is a verifiable datum of word order.**

**And its consequence is this:** the man does not **accuse** his people. He does not condemn what they do, he does not lay a verdict on them. **He conducts a piece of reasoning upon himself, and they listen to it.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pronouns:** had the same reasoning been built as "why do you do this?", the other side would have gone on the defensive. Built in the first person there is nothing to defend — **the listener does not hear a verdict about himself, he hears a person reckoning his own account.** And if the reasoning holds, the listener draws the conclusion himself.

**And precisely for this reason the twenty-fourth verse is striking:** *"then **I** would be in plain error."* **The man uses the word "error" only of himself** — and this should be compared with the place in the forty-seventh verse where those who deny will use the same word of the believers (taken up below).

### وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ — the turn in the middle of the sentence

**And now comes the most important point of word order in the sūra.**

**The sentence begins in the first person singular** — *faṭara**nī*** ("brought me into being"). **And the same sentence ends in the second person plural** — *wa ilayhi **turjaʿūn**a* ("and **you** will be returned to Him").

**In Arabic the shift in the direction of address is called *iltifāt*.** Here the *iltifāt* is made **inside a single sentence**, with no break at all.

| Half of the sentence | Person | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| *Wa mā liya lā aʿbudu'lladhī **faṭaranī*** | **1st singular** | His own coming into being |
| *wa ilayhi **turjaʿūn*** | **2nd plural** | **Their** return |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this transition:** the man builds the proof upon himself but leaves the conclusion to them. **The subject of the sentence is "I", its target "you".** And he does this not in the language of accusation but with a subordinate clause — what is said is slipped in almost like a reminder.

**And these two words — وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ — are the last two words of the sūra.** The eighty-third verse ends with this clause. **The sentence spoken by the man in the story, that is, becomes the closing sentence of the sūra.** This is the clearest internal repetition in the sūra and will be taken up separately in the closing section.

### فَطَرَنِى — root ف-ط-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to split, to cleave lengthwise.** *Faṭara* — he split. Its derivatives show the direction of the root:

- **فُطُور** — a rift, a crack. al-Mulk 67/3: *hal tarā min fuṭūr* — "do you see any crack?" (**treated in `067-mulk.md`**).
- **إِفْطَار** — to **break** the fast, that is, to split open a closedness.
- **فِطْرَة** — the state in which one is first split out; the original creation.
- **فَاطِر** — the one who splits out, who brings into being for the first time.

**The root has occurred in `082-infitar.md`, `067-mulk.md`, `042-sura.md` and `077-murselat.md`; I do not repeat its basic analysis.**

**What belongs here is this:** the man names God not by a known name but **by the relation He has with him** — *alladhī **faṭaranī***, "the One who split me out / brought me into being." **The proof, that is, begins with a personal datum: with his own existence.**

**And the sūra will repeat this at the close:** in the seventy-seventh verse it will be said *"has man not seen that We created him from a drop?"*, and in the seventy-eighth *"and he has forgotten his own creation"*. **The man in the story is the one who does not forget; the man at the close is the one who forgets. Both are looking at the same datum.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared subject of the two places.**

### ءَأَتَّخِذُ … شَفَٰعَتُهُمْ — a sentence of impossibility

**Word order:** *a-attakhidhu* — an *istifhām inkārī* (a question that conveys denial): no answer is expected, an impossibility is announced.

**And a condition is set up inside the sentence:** *in yuridni'r-Raḥmānu bi-ḍurr* — "if the Merciful wills harm for me."

**The choice of name is worth recording and is striking:** the willing of harm is attached to the name **al-Raḥmān**. The man could have used "God" or some other name. **He uses the name of mercy.**

**I record this as an observation:** the sentence says, without distinguishing them, that mercy and harm belong to the same will. **And this agrees with the pair *al-ʿAzīz al-Raḥīm* in the fifth verse of the sūra** — there too the two sides were named together in a single sentence.

**شَفَٰعَة** — root **ش-ف-ع**: **to make what is odd even.** *Shafʿ* — even; *witr* — odd (the two occur together at al-Fajr 89/3, **treated in `089-fecr.md`**). *Shafāʿa* — to join oneself to someone as a second, not to leave him alone.

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** intercession is **standing beside** a person. And the verse says this will not happen: *lā tughni ʿannī shafāʿatuhum shayʾan* — "their standing beside me will gain me nothing."

**ٱلْغَنِى / تُغْنِ** — root **غ-ن-ي**: a thing's leaving no need for anything else. *Lā tughnī* — "it does not meet the need, it does not suffice."

**وَلَا يُنقِذُونِ** — root **ن-ق-ذ**: **to pull a thing out of danger, to rescue.** *Inqādh* — rescue. And note: the *yāʾ* at the end of the verb has been dropped (*yunqidhūnī* → *yunqidhūn*), for the sake of the verse-ending. **The same elision occurs in the twenty-fifth verse too** (*fa'smaʿūn*).

**A repetition inside the sūra:** the same root returns in the forty-third verse — *wa lā hum **yunqadhūn*** (of those who drown). **Both occurrences can be verified:** one is what the man says of himself, the other what is said of those who drown at sea. **In both, the absence of anyone to rescue is announced.**

## 36/25 — إِنِّىٓ ءَامَنتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ فَٱسْمَعُونِ

*Innī āmantu bi-rabbikum fa'smaʿūn*

"I have believed in your Lord; so hear me."

### بِرَبِّكُمْ — "your Lord"

**The most striking word in the sentence is *rabbikum*, and it is required by the word order.**

**The man does not say "I have believed in my Lord". He says "I have believed in your Lord".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the pronoun:** the man ascribes the being the other side rejects **to them all the same.** What he has believed in, that is, he names not as a choice belonging to himself but as a reality **whose owner they are too.** **The same attitude was there at the beginning of the story:** he had said to them not "O people" but "O **my** people". **In both places the pronoun of belonging shows not separation but a share held in common.**

### Whom is he addressing? — a disagreement

**There are two views about the addressee of the sentence:**

| View | Addressee | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **His people** | "I have believed in your Lord, so hear me" — the declaration of his faith |
| 2 | **The messengers** | "I have believed in you [in what you brought], so bear witness" — the registration of his faith |

**Both views are found in the classical commentaries. I do not impose a preference.**

**But one datum internal to the text can be recorded:** all the speech before this sentence (20-24) is addressed to his people — *yā qawmi*, *ittabiʿū*, *turjaʿūn*. **There is no sign of a change of addressee.** **This appears to support the first view. It is an observation, not a preference.**

### فَٱسْمَعُونِ — word order

**The *yāʾ* at the end of the verb has been dropped:** *ismaʿūnī* → *fa'smaʿūn*. The same elision as *yunqidhūn* in the twenty-third verse. **This elision, made for the sake of agreement with the verse-ending, is seen twice in this part of the sūra** (*faṭaranī* preserved, *yunqidhūn* and *fa'smaʿūn* shortened).

**And the content of the command is worth recording: "hear me."** What is asked for is not acceptance but **hearing.** The man is not asking them to believe — he is asking them **to hear what he says.**

**This matches the messengers' sentence in the seventeenth verse:** *wa mā ʿalaynā illa'l-balāghu'l-mubīn* — "nothing lies upon us but delivery." **The messengers ask for delivery, the man asks to be heard. Neither asks for the outcome.** **I record this overlap as my own reading; its ground is that both sentences ask for the process and not the result.**

### Where the speech ends

**The man's speech ends here, and it lasted six verses** (20-25). **I record a count:**

| Verse | What it does |
|---|---|
| 20 | The call — *ittabiʿu'l-mursalīn* |
| 21 | The ground — they ask no reward |
| 22 | Proof from his own case — *faṭaranī* |
| 23 | The invalidity of the alternative — intercession avails nothing |
| 24 | The conclusion — *then I would be in error* |
| 25 | The declaration — *I have believed, hear me* |

**The order can be verified and carries a design:** call → ground → proof → elimination of the alternative → conclusion → declaration. **The declaration of faith is placed last.** **My own reading:** the man builds the reasoning first and discloses himself last. The weight of what he says, that is, is attached not to who he is but to the reasoning he has built.

## 36/26-27 — قِيلَ ٱدْخُلِ ٱلْجَنَّةَ قَالَ يَٰلَيْتَ قَوْمِى يَعْلَمُونَ · بِمَا غَفَرَ لِى رَبِّى وَجَعَلَنِى مِنَ ٱلْمُكْرَمِينَ

*Qīla'dkhuli'l-jannata qāla yā layta qawmī yaʿlamūn · Bimā ghafara lī rabbī wa jaʿalanī mina'l-mukramīn*

"'Enter the garden,' it was said. He said: '**If only my people knew** — how my Lord has forgiven me and made me one of **those who are honoured**.'"

### The scene that is skipped

**The most striking thing about the verse is what it does not say.**

**The text does not tell what happened to the man.** In the twenty-fifth verse he was speaking; in the twenty-sixth it is said *"enter the garden"*. **The event in between is not in the text.**

**It is widely transmitted in the classical commentaries that the man was killed by his people**, and there are reports differing from one another about the manner of his death. **In this commentary:**

- **That he was killed is a piece of information not present in the wording of the verse.** I record that it is widely transmitted; I do not present it as a datum belonging to the verse.
- **I do not enter into the reports about the manner of his death.**

**In Arabic the deliberate skipping of a part of a narrative is called *ṭayy***, and the lexicographers record it as a device of style.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the text's own gap:** what the story skips shows what the story is not about. **What is recounted is not what befell the man but what the man said.** And the first sentence he says makes no mention at all of what befell him.

### قِيلَ — a passive verb

**Word order:** *qīla* — passive. **The one who speaks is not named.**

**And there is no connective at all between the two clauses:** *qīla'dkhuli'l-jannata* **·** *qāla yā layta qawmī yaʿlamūn*. **In Arabic this is called *faṣl*** — juxtaposition without a connective; it is generally used when the second clause is the direct answer to the first.

**The answer, that is, comes without delay.** "Enter the garden" was said — and the first thing he said was about his people.

### يَٰلَيْتَ قَوْمِى يَعْلَمُونَ — the content of the wish

**لَيْتَ** — the particle of wishing. In Arabic *layta* is mostly used for a wish that is **not expected to be realised** (*laʿalla* for one that is). **The lexicographers record this distinction.**

**And both sides of the wish must be recorded:**

**First — for whom:** *qawmī* — **"my people".** In the twentieth verse he had said *yā qawmi*; here he uses the same word again. **The event that came in between — whatever it was — has not changed the pronoun of belonging.** This can be verified from the text.

**Second — what they are to know:** *bimā ghafara lī rabbī wa jaʿalanī mina'l-mukramīn* — **his being forgiven and his being honoured.**

**Counting what is absent from the wish makes what is present visible:**

| **Absent** from the wish | **Present** in the wish |
|---|---|
| A demand for revenge | Forgiveness |
| A claim to have been in the right | Being honoured |
| The punishment of his people | **That his people should know** |
| Any mention of his own pain | — |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the wish:** the man wants not his people's **punishment** but their **knowing**. And what he wants to make known is not that he was in the right but **the treatment he has received.** What he wants is that they should understand that they too could receive the same treatment — because what he reports is not a victory but **a forgiveness**.

### ٱلْمُكْرَمِين — a passive attribute

**ك-ر-م** — the root: being the good of its kind; generosity, worth.

**And the pattern matters: *mukram* — a passive participle, from Form IV.** **Not "one who is worthy", that is, but "one to whom honour is shown".**

**I record this as an observation:** the man counts no attribute for himself. Both verbs belong to **God**: *ghafara lī* (He forgave me), *jaʿalanī* (He made me). **There is not one verb in the sentence of which the man is the subject.**

**And its link with the eleventh verse can be verified:** there it had been said *fa-bashshirhu bi-**maghfiratin** wa ajrin **karīm*** — **forgiveness and a *karīm* reward.** Here the same two things are named as having come about: *ghafara lī* and *mina'l-**mukram**īn*. **The same two roots, fifteen verses apart, once as promise and once as fulfilment.** This is a countable repetition within the sūra.

### Two men — Yāsīn 36/20-27 and Ghāfir 40/28-44

**In `040-gafir.md` at 40/28-44 the man who gives that sūra its name was treated:** a man of Pharaoh's household, concealing his faith, unnamed, addressing his people as *yā qawmi*, standing alone. The findings there were these: his argument is a dilemma that covers both possibilities; he does not exclude the other side; the longest uninterrupted speech in the sūra belongs to him; and his speech ends with *wa ufawwiḍu amrī ila'llāh*.

**The two stories come out of the same mould and must be compared. Every row of the table below can be verified from the two texts:**

| | **Yāsīn 36/20-27** | **Ghāfir 40/28-44** |
|---|---|---|
| **His name** | Not given | Not given |
| **How he is introduced** | *Rajulun yasʿā* — **a man coming at a run** | *Rajulun muʾminun… yaktumu īmānah* — **a believer concealing his faith** |
| **Where he comes from** | *Min aqṣa'l-madīna* — **from the farthest part of the city** | *Min āli Firʿawn* — **from inside the ruling house** |
| **His position** | **At the edge** | **At the centre** |
| **The state of his faith** | **Open** — he declares it, *innī āmantu* | **Concealed**, opening out as the speech goes on |
| **His form of address** | *Yā qawmi* — **once** | *Yā qawmi* — **five occurrences counted in the discussion of Ghāfir** |
| **The kind of argument** | **A testable measure**: they ask no reward | **A dilemma**: if he is a liar the harm is his own, if he is truthful it will touch you |
| **The person of the argument** | **First singular**: "why should I not serve" | **First plural**: "who will help us" |
| **His attitude to the other side** | **He does not accuse** — he makes himself the example | **He does not accuse** — he grants them their kingdom (*lakum mulku'l-yawm*) |
| **The length of his speech** | **Six verses** (20-25) | **Spread over sixteen verses** (28-35, 38-44) |
| **His last sentence** | *Yā layta qawmī yaʿlamūn* — **"if only my people knew"** | *Wa ufawwiḍu amrī ila'llāh* — **"I commit my affair to God"** |
| **His outcome** | *Qīla'dkhuli'l-janna* — **recompense in the hereafter** | *Fa-waqāhu'llāhu sayyiʾāti mā makarū* — **protection in this world** |

**Three differences must be recorded in particular:**

**First — the places they come from are opposite.** The man of Ghāfir speaks **from within**: he is of Pharaoh's household, his word carries weight, his risk comes from his position. The man of Yāsīn comes **from outside**: he is from the farthest part of the city, his word carries no weight, his risk comes from his defencelessness. **The two stories give the same attitude as it appears in two different positions.**

**Second — their ends are opposite.** The man of Ghāfir is **protected**, and this is written plainly. The man of Yāsīn is not protected; what is given him in return is shown **in another place**. **I record this as my own reading:** read together, the two stories show that the recompense of a true word **has no single form**. The Qurʾān recounts the same attitude with two different outcomes and counts both legitimate.

**Third — the direction of their last sentences is opposite.** The man of Ghāfir commits his affair **upward**: *ufawwiḍu amrī ila'llāh*. The man of Yāsīn turns his word **back**, towards his people: *yā layta qawmī yaʿlamūn*. **One lets the outcome go, the other is still delivering the message.**

**And the shared point is more striking than the differences:** **neither of them has a name.** In both sūras the person who builds the longest and most orderly argument is the person who is not named. **I record this as an observation;** its ground is that neither text gives a name.

## 36/28-29 — وَمَآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ مِنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ مِن جُندٍ مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَمَا كُنَّا مُنزِلِينَ · إِن كَانَتْ إِلَّا صَيْحَةً وَٰحِدَةً فَإِذَا هُمْ خَٰمِدُونَ

*Wa mā anzalnā ʿalā qawmihī min baʿdihī min jundin mina's-samāʾi wa mā kunnā munzilīn · In kānat illā ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan fa-idhā hum khāmidūn*

"After him We sent down upon his people no army from the sky, nor were We going to send one down. **It was but a single cry; and all at once there they were, gone out like a fire.**"

### جُند — army

**Root ج-ن-د.** The lexicographers give the concrete sense of the root as **hard, stony ground** (*janad*); *jund* — a massed, close-packed crowd; from here, **army.**

**A point of word order:** the sentence is built as a **negation**, and it is negated twice:

- *Wa **mā** anzalnā… min jund* — **We did not send down.**
- *Wa **mā** kunnā munzilīn* — **nor were We going to send down.**

**The second clause is not a repetition of the first.** The first announces an event, the second an **attitude**: *mā kunnā* + the active participle negates continuity in Arabic, in the sense "this is not Our practice".

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this two-layered negation:** the verse does not merely say that no army was sent down; it says that **such a thing would be out of proportion.** No army is needed to bring the people of one town to an end. And disproportion was precisely the diagnosis directed at the other side in the nineteenth verse of the story: ***musrifūn*** — those who go beyond the bound. **Where the sūra condemns disproportion it keeps the measure in its own narrative too.** This is an interpretation.

**A repetition inside the sūra:** the word *jund* returns in the seventy-fifth verse — *wa hum lahum **jundun** muḥḍarūn*. **Both occurrences can be verified:** one the army not sent down from the sky, the other the army named in the discussion of the gods that are taken up. **In both the word announces a crowd that is of no use.**

### صَيْحَةً وَٰحِدَةً — a single sound

**ص-ي-ح** — the root: **to shout aloud, to cry out.** **The root was analysed in `050-kaf.md` at 50/42** (*yawma yasmaʿūna'ṣ-ṣayḥata bi'l-ḥaqq*), and what was recorded there was this: **the Qurʾān uses this word in scenes of destruction** — of Thamūd, of Madyan and of other peoples it is said *"the cry seized them"*. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the threefold repetition within the sūra. This is one of the clearest internal repetitions in Yāsīn:**

| Verse | Expression | Of whom | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **29** | *in kānat illā **ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan** fa-idhā hum khāmidūn* | **The people of the town** | **Past** |
| **49** | *mā yanẓurūna illā **ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan** taʾkhudhuhum* | **The present addressee** | **Future** |
| **53** | *in kānat illā **ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan** fa-idhā hum jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn* | **Everyone** | **The resurrection** |

**And this must be recorded as well: the first five words of verses 29 and 53 are exactly the same** — *in kānat illā ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan*. **After that they part:** one ends with *khāmidūn* (gone out), the other with *muḥḍarūn* (brought present).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this exact repetition:** the same sentence once **ends** and once **begins.** The sūra recounts the end and the beginning with the same sound. And the qualification *wāḥida* (a single one) stands in all three — no repetition is needed.

### خَٰمِدُون — root خ-م-د

**The concrete sense of the word has to do with fire:** *khamadati'n-nār* — **the fire went out**, its flame died down, though its ash is still hot. The lexicographers distinguish *khumūd* from *ikhmād*: *khumūd* is the dying down of the flame, *ikhmād* is extinguishing.

**And the choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "they died". It says **"they went out"**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the picture in the root of the word:** going out is not a thing's ceasing to exist — **it is the end of its burning.** The town is standing, the people are standing; the motion has ended. And for the noise of a crowd the simile of fire has been chosen: **what makes a sound while it burns and falls silent when it goes out.**

**And this agrees with the preceding verse:** no army was sent down, because what was to be put out was a flame in any case.

## 36/30 — يَٰحَسْرَةً عَلَى ٱلْعِبَادِ مَا يَأْتِيهِم مِّن رَّسُولٍ إِلَّا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ

*Yā ḥasratan ʿala'l-ʿibād, mā yaʾtīhim min rasūlin illā kānū bihī yastahziʾūn*

"**Alas for the servants!** No messenger ever comes to them but they mock him."

### يَٰحَسْرَةً — calling to something that is not there

**A point of word order, and it has a place of its own in Arabic.**

*Yā* is the particle of calling, and it normally requires **an addressee who can be called**. What is called here is a **feeling**: *ḥasra*.

**The lexicographers call this *nidāʾ majāzī*** and explain it thus: to announce the intensity of a state, that state is called as though it were a being that could be called. What is implied is: **"O regret! If you have a time to come, this is exactly that time."**

**There are similar usages in the Qurʾān** — *yā waylanā* (36/52), *yā ḥasratā ʿalā mā farraṭtu fī janbi'llāh* (al-Zumar 39/56). **The second is directly the same word.**

### حَسْرَة — root ح-س-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to lift a covering, to strip off, to lay bare.**

- **حَاسِر** — one left bare-headed, without a helmet (unprotected in battle).
- **انْحَسَرَ** — it drew back, it receded (of the sea going out).
- **حَسِير** — an animal broken down with exhaustion; whose strength is **spent**.

**The root was treated in `069-hakka.md`** and recorded there as the doubleness of "stripping / regret"; **it has occurred in `067-mulk.md` too** (*yanqalib ilayka'l-baṣaru khāsiʾan wa huwa **ḥasīr***). **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is this: *ḥasra* is the third branch of the root, and it derives from the first two.** Regret is the state that appears when **the lifting of the covering** and **the spending of one's strength** come together: the person sees, but there is nothing left he can do. **The word carries within it both the knowledge side and the power side of the feeling of "if only".**

### To whom does the regret belong? — a disagreement

**The owner of the regret is not stated in the sentence, and the exegetes differ:**

| View | To whom the regret belongs | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To the servants themselves** — the regret they will feel at the resurrection | Understanding *ʿalā* as "upon" rather than "concerning"; agreement with al-Zumar 39/56 |
| 2 | **To the angels, or to the narration itself** | The sentence's coming in the form of a call |
| 3 | **A saying belonging to the style of the narrative** — ascribed to no one | Its being a *nidāʾ majāzī*; the addressee's not being real |

**I do not impose a preference.**

**And a caveat of transcendence must be entered — the caveat kept consistently across the index holds here too:** states such as regret, sorrow and remorse cannot be ascribed to God **in a way that would imply deficiency**. **Two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries for expressions of this kind:** to accept the wording as it stands and leave its manner to God (*tafwīḍ*), or to interpret it as a mode of expression (*taʾwīl*). **I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

### عَلَى ٱلْعِبَاد — the saying of "servants"

**A choice of word that must be recorded:** in speaking of those who mock, the verse does not call them *al-kāfirūn* or *al-mukadhdhibūn*. It says ***al-ʿibād*** — **"the servants".**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word itself:** what is regretted is not their enmity but **their being servants.** The attribute may change, that is, but the belonging does not. And this agrees with the attitude of the man in the story: he too had called them *qawmī*.

### مَا يَأْتِيهِم مِّن رَّسُولٍ إِلَّا … — generalisation

**Word order:** *mā … illā* — **the pattern of restriction.** And the verbs are **imperfect**: *yaʾtīhim*, *yastahziʾūn*. **In Arabic the imperfect announces a repeated and ongoing event.**

**The sentence, then, recounts not an event but a rule.** And *min rasūlin* — indefinite, in a negative context: **"no messenger at all".**

**My own reading:** here the story stops being the history of a single town. In the twenty-ninth verse a people had gone out; the thirtieth verse ties this to **a general rule**; and the thirty-first will open it out onto **history**. The sūra widens the story in three steps: **one town → all messengers → all generations.**

### ٱسْتِهْزَاء — root ه-ز-أ

**The concrete sense: to make light of a thing, to laugh at it with contempt.** Form X (*istihzāʾ*) here announces not a **request** but **a person's counting a thing to be so** — *istahzaʾa bihī*: he counted him worth mocking.

**Worth recording:** the verse gives the reaction the name not of **denial** but of **mockery**. **And the standing of mockery before evidence is the same as in the table set up for "astonishment" in `050-kaf.md` at 50/2:** mockery rests on no premise, cannot be argued with, cannot be falsified. **It makes its subject not what is said but the one who says it.** And precisely for that reason it produces no answer.

## 36/31-32 — أَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا قَبْلَهُم مِّنَ ٱلْقُرُونِ أَنَّهُمْ إِلَيْهِمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ · وَإِن كُلٌّ لَّمَّا جَمِيعٌ لَّدَيْنَا مُحْضَرُونَ

*Alam yaraw kam ahlaknā qablahum mina'l-qurūni annahum ilayhim lā yarjiʿūn · Wa in kullun lammā jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn*

"Have they not seen how many generations before them We destroyed? **They do not return to these.** And all of them, gathered together, will be brought present before Us."

### ٱلْقُرُون — root ق-ر-ن

***Qarn***, root **ق-ر-ن**: **to bind together, to yoke side by side.** *Qirān* — the joining of two things; *qarīn* — a close companion (***qarīn* was treated in `050-kaf.md`**); *qarn* — an animal's horn (what is joined to the head).

**From here comes the sense of *qarn* as a body of people: people who lived at the same time and are bound to one another — a generation.**

**The rendering "century" settled on the word later;** the usage in the Qurʾān runs towards **generation**. **The lexicographers differ over its length; I give no definite figure.**

### أَنَّهُمْ إِلَيْهِمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ — a disputed sentence

**The parsing of the sentence, and therefore its meaning, is disputed:**

| Reading | What *annahum* attaches to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The second object of *alam yaraw* | "**Have they not seen** that these do not return to them?" — that is, that the destruction is permanent |
| 2 | An independent sentence, an explanation | "**They do not return to these**" — the state of those destroyed is announced |
| 3 | An explanation of *kam ahlaknā* | The destroyed generation's not coming back to this world |

**I do not impose a preference.** **The common point can be verified:** on all three readings what is announced is **that there is no return.**

**And the word is the spine-root of the sūra: ر-ج-ع.** A table of the five occurrences was given in the section on structure. **This is the second occurrence of the root and the first negative one.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the distribution within the sūra:** in the twenty-second verse the man had said *"you will be returned to Him"* — **there is a return, but its direction is upward.** In the thirty-first it is said *"they do not return to these"* — **there is no going back.** The sūra sets the two sentences nine verses apart and they complete one another: **there is a return, but not to where it began.**

### وَإِن كُلٌّ لَّمَّا … — a matter of variant reading and parsing

**This sentence is one of the most disputed places of parsing in the sūra, and two readings are transmitted:**

| Reading | The work of *in* and *lammā* | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **لَمَّا** (with shadda) | *In* = negating, *lammā* = *illā* | "All of them **will only** be brought present before Us" |
| **لَمَا** (without shadda) | *In* = the lightened form of *inna*, *lām* = distinguishing, *mā* = otiose | "All of them **will surely** be brought present before Us" |

**Both readings are transmitted, and I record them, because they change the parsing entirely.** **But there is no difference in meaning:** the first reading reaches the same result by way of restriction (*only*), the second by way of emphasis (*surely*). **I do not impose a preference.**

### مُحْضَرُون — root ح-ض-ر

**The root: to be present, to be in a place.** *Ḥāḍir* — present; *ḥaḍar* — settled life (as against bedouin life).

**And the pattern must be recorded: *muḥḍar* — a passive participle, from Form IV.** **Not "one who is present" but "one who is brought present" — that is, one who is brought.** The verb is passive; who brings them is not said.

**A repetition inside the sūra — three occurrences:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **32** | *jamīʿun ladaynā **muḥḍarūn*** | **The destroyed generations and these** |
| **53** | *jamīʿun ladaynā **muḥḍarūn*** | **Those raised** |
| **75** | *wa hum lahum jundun **muḥḍarūn*** | **The gods taken up / those who take them up** |

**The three words in verses 32 and 53 are exactly the same: *jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn*.** This is a verifiable repetition.

**My own reading:** the section of the story (block III) closes with this sentence, and the section on the resurrection is built with the same sentence. **The sūra ties the outcome of the story and the outcome of the resurrection to the same words** — that is, the story is set down not as an account of the past but as **the same outcome seen in advance.**

**And a detail of word order:** *ladaynā* — "with Us". The word announces a narrower nearness than *ʿinda*, and the lexicographers record this: *ladā* covers being in someone's presence; *ʿinda* covers belonging as well. **What is chosen here is the word that announces presence.**

## 36/33 — وَءَايَةٌ لَّهُمُ ٱلْأَرْضُ ٱلْمَيْتَةُ أَحْيَيْنَٰهَا وَأَخْرَجْنَا مِنْهَا حَبًّا فَمِنْهُ يَأْكُلُونَ

*Wa āyatun lahumu'l-arḍu'l-maytatu aḥyaynāhā wa akhrajnā minhā ḥabban fa-minhu yaʾkulūn*

"**A sign for them is the dead earth:** We brought it to life and brought grain out of it, and of that they eat."

### وَءَايَةٌ لَّهُم — the pattern that builds the block

**This construction occurs three times in the sūra and marks the three parts of block IV. This is a verifiable structural datum:**

| Verse | Expression | The sign |
|---|---|---|
| **33** | *Wa **āyatun lahum**u'l-arḍu'l-mayta* | **The earth** |
| **37** | *Wa **āyatun lahum**u'l-layl* | **Night and the heavenly bodies** |
| **41** | *Wa **āyatun lahum** annā ḥamalnā dhurriyyatahum* | **The ship** |

**A point of word order:** in all three sentences **the predicate is brought forward** (*āyatun lahum*) and **the subject comes after** (*al-arḍ*, *al-layl*, *annā ḥamalnā*). In Arabic the bringing forward of an indefinite predicate announces **the drawing of attention to importance** (*ihtimām*), and the lexicographers record this. **Moreover an indefinite word is not normally permitted to be a subject; bringing the predicate forward resolves that difficulty as well.**

**And the word itself must be recorded: *āya* — "sign".** Not a proof, but a **sign.** A sign exists not for itself but for what it points to. **This was dwelt upon in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/37:** there, immediately after the sun and the moon are named as *āya*, prostration to them is forbidden, because *"a sign exists for what it points to."* **I rely on that treatment.**

### ٱلْأَرْضُ ٱلْمَيْتَة — the choice of word

**The same proof was treated in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/39, and the word used there was different:** *annaka tara'l-arḍa **khāshiʿatan** fa-idhā anzalnā ʿalayha'l-māʾa'htazzat wa rabat* — "you see the earth **with its head bowed**; when We send down water upon it, it stirs and swells." The finding there was this: the word *khāshiʿa* is generally used in the Qurʾān of **human beings**, and here it is given to parched ground; three words build a process: **bowed → stirred → swelled.**

**Yāsīn chooses another word: *al-mayta* — dead.**

**The comparison of the two sūras shows that the choice of word depends on the subject of the sūra:**

| | **Fuṣṣilat 41/39** | **Yāsīn 36/33** |
|---|---|---|
| **The attribute of the earth** | *Khāshiʿa* — **head bowed** | *Al-Mayta* — **dead** |
| **Is a process recounted** | **Yes**: *ihtazzat wa rabat* (it stirred, it swelled) | **No**: *aḥyaynāhā* (We brought it to life) |
| **Is the conclusion drawn explicitly** | **Yes**: *inna'lladhī aḥyāhā la-muḥyi'l-mawtā* | **No** — it is not drawn |
| **What the sentence ends with** | The explicit statement of the proof | *Fa-minhu yaʾkulūn* — **"of that they eat"** |

**Two differences can be verified, and both must be recorded.**

**First:** because Yāsīn is a sūra of the resurrection it uses the word for death directly — *al-mayta*, *aḥyaynā*. **The words are the words of the discussion of resurrection.** Fuṣṣilat, whose axis is communication, gives the earth **a human state** (*khāshiʿa*) instead.

**Second, and more striking:** **Fuṣṣilat draws the conclusion explicitly, Yāsīn does not.** Fuṣṣilat says *"the One who brought it to life will surely bring the dead to life"*. Yāsīn says that the dead earth was brought to life and ends the sentence with **bread**: *fa-minhu yaʾkulūn*.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is how the two verses end:** Yāsīn does not state the proof, it **shows** it. The one addressed is already eating the grain that comes out of the dead earth; the verse gives him no information, it names what he already holds. **And the sense that closed at the beginning of the sūra was sight** (*fa-hum lā yubṣirūn*, 9). **The evidential section returns to the same sense.**

### حَبًّا — grain

**Root ح-ب-ب.** *Ḥabba* — a grain, a seed. And from the same root comes *ḥubb* (love); the lexicographers explain the connection by the picture of "the kernel of the heart". **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers frame it, not as a definite etymological ruling.**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "plant" or "crop", it says **"grain"**. **A grain is both the thing eaten and the beginning of the next plant** — that is, it carries its own repetition inside itself. **I record this as an observation;** the verse does not work this detail out, it only uses the word.

**Word order:** *fa-minhu yaʾkulūn* — the *fāʾ* announces a consequence, and *minhu* is brought forward. **The fronting draws attention to "the source of what they eat".**

## 36/34-35 — وَجَعَلْنَا فِيهَا جَنَّٰتٍ مِّن نَّخِيلٍ وَأَعْنَٰبٍ وَفَجَّرْنَا فِيهَا مِنَ ٱلْعُيُونِ · لِيَأْكُلُوا۟ مِن ثَمَرِهِۦ وَمَا عَمِلَتْهُ أَيْدِيهِمْ أَفَلَا يَشْكُرُونَ

*Wa jaʿalnā fīhā jannātin min nakhīlin wa aʿnābin wa fajjarnā fīhā mina'l-ʿuyūn · Li-yaʾkulū min thamarihī wa mā ʿamilat-hu aydīhim, a-fa-lā yashkurūn*

"In it We made gardens of palms and vines, and We made springs gush forth in it — that they might eat of its fruit. **Their hands did not make it.** Will they not then give thanks?"

### جَنَّٰت — root ج-ن-ن

**The sense of the root is to cover** — the verb *janna* means "it covered, it hid"; *janīn* (covered in the womb), *jinn* (what is not seen), *junna* (a shield) and *majnūn* are from the same root. **The root has been analysed in a great many places in the index** (`056-vakia.md`, `050-kaf.md`, `072-cin.md` and others); **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the worldly use of the word.** In saying "garden" the sūra here means **not the garden of the hereafter but a garden in this world** — the place where trees cover the ground. **And in the fifty-fifth verse the same word will be used of the hereafter** (*aṣḥāba'l-janna*). **The sūra uses the word in both senses;** this is a verifiable datum and is the word's natural breadth in Arabic.

### نَخِيل وَأَعْنَٰب — two trees

**Palms and vines are named together, as this pair, many times in the Qurʾān.** Both are plants that grow in the surroundings of the one addressed, that require labour, and that yield seasonal produce.

**And one link within the sūra must be recorded:** *nakhīl* (palms) occurs here; **in the thirty-ninth verse the dried branch of the same tree will be likened to the crescent moon** — *ka'l-**ʿurjūni**'l-qadīm*, the old, dried stalk of a date cluster. **Both occurrences can be verified:** the same tree is named once **with its fruit** and once **with its dried branch**. **I record this as an observation;** I do not claim that the sūra makes the connection deliberately.

### وَفَجَّرْنَا — root ف-ج-ر

**The concrete sense: to split, to split open and make flow.** *Fajara'l-māʾa* — he split the water open, made it gush. And from the same root:

- **فَجْر** — dawn: the light that **splits** the darkness of the night.
- **فُجُور** — the **splitting** of a moral limit.

**The root was treated in `089-fecr.md`, `091-sems.md` and `082-infitar.md`; I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the pattern.** *Fajjarnā* — **Form II**, and this form announces multiplicity and intensity. **Not a single splitting, that is, but in many places.** And the object is *mina'l-ʿuyūn* — with the partitive *min*: "some of the springs", that is, an unstated number.

**عُيُون** — the plural of *ʿayn*. **And the doubleness of the word is worth recording: in Arabic *ʿayn* is both "eye" and "spring".** The lexicographers explain the connection by the picture of **water flowing**. **Since this section of the sūra is built upon sight the word is striking — but I make no point of it, I record the word's doubleness.**

### وَمَا عَمِلَتْهُ أَيْدِيهِمْ — a dispute over *mā*

**The meaning of the sentence again depends on the particle *mā*, and the exegetes differ:**

| Reading | What *mā* is | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Negating** | "**Their hands did not make it**" — the fruit is not the work of man |
| 2 | **Relative** | "And [that they might eat] of **what their hands have worked**" — the share of human labour is named too |

**Both readings are found in the classical commentaries. I do not impose a preference.**

**The upshot of the two readings differs, and this must be recorded:**
- **On the first reading** the verse puts human labour outside the proof: what you eat is not of your making.
- **On the second reading** the verse takes human labour into the favour: both what comes of itself and what comes by the work of the hand.

**One datum internal to the text can be recorded, and this is not to take a side in the disagreement:** **the same construction returns in the seventy-first verse** — *mimmā **ʿamilat aydīnā*** ("of what **Our hands** have made"). **The same verb and the same noun in both verses, but two different hands.**

| Verse | Expression | Whose hand |
|---|---|---|
| **35** | *wa mā **ʿamilat-hu aydīhim*** | **Their** hand |
| **71** | *mimmā **ʿamilat aydīnā*** | **"Our"** hand |

**This repetition is a countable datum.** **And the caveat of transcendence entered in `040-gafir.md` at 40/7 in the discussion of the Throne holds here too:** expressions such as *aydīnā* cannot be understood as ascribing a limb or a body to God; two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries (*tafwīḍ* and *taʾwīl*). **I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

### أَفَلَا يَشْكُرُون — a closure within the block

**This question occurs twice in the sūra, both times after an enumeration of favours:**

| Verse | After what |
|---|---|
| **35** | **Plants and water** — what comes out of the earth |
| **73** | **Animals** — mounts, food, drink |

**Both occurrences can be verified.** And there are two more questions in the same pattern in the sūra, but with different verbs: *a-fa-lam takūnū **taʿqilūn*** (62) and *a-fa-lā **yaʿqilūn*** (68).

**The distribution of the four questions shows a design, and I record this as an observation:**

| Verb | Verses | After what |
|---|---|---|
| ***Yashkurūn*** — to give thanks | **35, 73** | **Things given** |
| ***Yaʿqilūn*** — to reason | **62, 68** | **Things seen** (following Satan; the turning back of a lifespan) |

**The sūra, that is, ties two different verbs to two different subjects:** before a favour, **thanks**; before a fact, **reason**. **This is an interpretation; its ground is where the four questions stand.**

**ش-ك-ر** — the lexicographers dwell on the concrete sense of the root: *shakarati'd-dābba* — the animal grew fat on little fodder. **In the root, that is, there is "making a little look like much, giving back more than was received".** From here, thanksgiving: **the making visible** of what has been received.

## 36/36 — سُبْحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلْأَزْوَٰجَ كُلَّهَا مِمَّا تُنۢبِتُ ٱلْأَرْضُ وَمِنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ وَمِمَّا لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*Subḥāna'lladhī khalaqa'l-azwāja kullahā mimmā tunbitu'l-arḍu wa min anfusihim wa mimmā lā yaʿlamūn*

"Glory be to the One who created all the pairs — of what the earth brings forth, of themselves, **and of what they do not know**."

### سُبْحَٰنَ — the glorification in the middle of the sūra

**The root س-ب-ح has been analysed in many places in the index** (`087-ala.md`, `056-vakia.md`, `059-hasr.md` and others): the core sense is **to swim away in water or in space**, and from there **tanzīh** — to hold God **far** from everything unbefitting. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is the word's place in the sūra. The construction *Subḥāna'lladhī* occurs twice in Yāsīn, and both are turning points:**

| Verse | Construction | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **36** | ***Subḥāna**'lladhī khalaqa'l-azwāja kullahā* | **In the middle of the evidential section** |
| **83** | *Fa-**subḥāna**'lladhī bi-yadihī malakūtu kulli shayʾ* | **In the last verse of the sūra** |

**Both occurrences are in the same pattern: *subḥāna* + *alladhī* + a verbal or adjectival clause.** **And in both the word *kull* is present:** *al-azwāja **kullahā*** (36) and ***kulli** shayʾ* (83). **This is a verifiable overlap.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra says the glorification twice and at two different breadths — once for **all the pairs**, once for **everything**. The second contains the first. And both are sentences of **comprehension**; the glorification comes, in this sūra, after a whole that admits no limit.

**Word order:** *subḥāna* is in the accusative and is counted the absolute object of an elided verb (with *usabbiḥu tasbīḥan* understood). The lexicographers record that the word is frozen in this pattern.

### ٱلْأَزْوَٰج — root ز-و-ج

**A correction is needed about the sense of the word, because the usual English rendering is misleading.**

**In Arabic *zawj* is not "a pair" but one member of a pair.** Each of two spouses, that is, is separately a *zawj*. The two together make *zawjān* (two singles). **The lexicographers record this plainly:** *zawj* — a single thing that has beside it its like or its opposite.

**The root has occurred in many places in the index** (`056-vakia.md`, `050-kaf.md`, `055-rahman.md`, `078-nebe.md` and others); **I do not repeat its basic analysis.**

**What belongs here is the extent of the word in this place.** *Al-Azwāj* is plural and is followed by *kullahā* (all of them) — **that is, the extent is left at its widest.**

**Two readings are transmitted for the extent of the word:**

| Reading | What *azwāj* is | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Male-female pairs** | The commonest use of the word; the qualification *min anfusihim* |
| 2 | **Every kind that exists in opposition and likeness** | The breadth of the qualification *kullahā*; its being used of plants too |

**I do not impose a preference.** **The second reading has a ground internal to the text, and it must be recorded:** *mimmā tunbitu'l-arḍ* — "of what the earth brings forth." That "pair" is said of plants shows that the word cannot be reduced to an animal mating alone.

### وَمِمَّا لَا يَعْلَمُونَ — the verse leaves its own list open

**This is the most striking place in the verse, and it must be handled carefully.**

**The sentence counts three sources and joins them with the particle *min*:**

| Source | Expression | State of knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Mimmā tunbitu'l-arḍ* | **Seen** — what the earth brings forth |
| 2 | *Wa min anfusihim* | **Lived** — their own selves |
| 3 | *Wa mimmā **lā yaʿlamūn*** | **Not known** |

**The third item comes in the same grammatical structure as the first two** — the same *min*, the same *mā*. **It is counted, that is, as a member of the list, with full standing.** But its content is not given.

**This is seen elsewhere in the Qurʾān too:** at al-Naḥl 16/8 it is said *"and He creates what you do not know"*.

**And now a caveat of method must be entered — per the method (`STYLE.md`) and plainly:**

**This verse is one of the places most often used for claims of scientific miracle.** Filling the third item with this or that fact known today is a widespread interpretation. **This commentary does not take that road.** The ground is the text itself:

**The verse has deliberately left the third item unfilled.** To fill it with the knowledge of any period **destroys the work the verse plainly does** — because the moment it is filled, the item ceases to be "what they do not know".

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the sentence:** the verse places the limit of knowledge not **outside** the proof but **inside** it. It does not say "you do not know these things, so be silent"; it says "what you do not know is part of the same order too". **The force of the proof comes not from the content of the third item but from its having been left empty:** the extent of the order is not bounded by the knowledge of the one who knows.

**And this does not happen only here in the sūra. Yāsīn leaves a list open in three separate places:**

| Verse | What is left open | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **36** | The third source of the pairs created | *mimmā **lā yaʿlamūn*** |
| **42** | The things like the ship that they ride | *min mithlihī **mā yarkabūn*** — not named |
| **55** | The occupation of the people of the garden | *fī **shughulin*** — its content not given |

**All three occurrences can be verified.** **My own reading:** in three separate contexts — creation, favour, recompense — the sūra does not close the extent. And in all three, what is not closed is that the extent is **not measured by the knowledge of the one addressed.**

**تُنۢبِتُ** — root **ن-ب-ت**: a plant's coming out of the ground. The verb is in **Form IV** (*anbata*) and its subject is *al-arḍ* — "what the earth brings forth". **The lexicographers record:** the earth is the **means** of the bringing forth; the verse gives the subject to the earth, but the sentence as a whole begins with glorification, so the real agent has already been named.

## 36/37 — وَءَايَةٌ لَّهُمُ ٱلَّيْلُ نَسْلَخُ مِنْهُ ٱلنَّهَارَ فَإِذَا هُم مُّظْلِمُونَ

*Wa āyatun lahumu'l-laylu naslakhu minhu'n-nahāra fa-idhā hum muẓlimūn*

"A sign for them too is the night: We **strip** the day off it; and all at once there they are, left in darkness."

### نَسْلَخُ — root س-ل-خ

**The concrete sense of the root belongs to the butcher's trade, and the lexicographers record this without hesitation:**

- **سَلَخَ الشَّاةَ** — he **flayed** the sheep, separated the skin from the carcass.
- **سَلَّاخ** — a flayer, a butcher.
- **مَسْلُوخ** — flayed.
- **سَلَخَ الشَّهْرُ** — the month came to its end, ran out (the same picture: a husk being drawn off to the last).
- **إِنْسِلَاخ** — a snake's sloughing of its skin.

**The picture beneath the word, then, is this: a covering drawn off a body whole and in one piece.**

**And now the word order of the verse must be recorded, because it is the reverse of the usual.**

### Which is the body, and which is stripped off?

**The sentence is built thus:** *al-**layl**u — the **night** is the sign. *Naslakhu minhu'**n-nahār*** — the **day** is what we strip off it.

| Element | Role |
|---|---|
| **Night** | **The body** — the trunk, the thing stripped |
| **Day** | **What is stripped** — the skin, the thing drawn off |

**This is a verifiable datum of word order, and it is the reverse of the usual picture.** In everyday speech the day is the body and the night is its withdrawal. **The verse builds the opposite: the night is the body, and the day is what has been drawn over it and taken off again.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the direction of the verb:** in the verb *salkh* what is drawn off is the **skin** and what remains is the **body**. If what remains once the day is drawn off is the night, then what is permanent is the night. **And this determines the work of the proof:** the one addressed counts the day as the ordinary case; the verse shows the day as something **added**. To show that what is counted ordinary is temporary is what the sūra has been doing all along.

**A note:** the Qurʾān recounts the relation of night and day elsewhere with other verbs too — covering, winding, bringing one after the other. **The verb Yāsīn chooses is the most concrete of these.** I record this as an observation; I do not build a claim that the sūra has made a deliberate choice.

### فَإِذَا هُم مُّظْلِمُون

***Idhā fujāʾiyya*** — the second occurrence in the sūra. A table of the six occurrences was given in the section on structure.

**مُظْلِمُون** — root **ظ-ل-م**, the active participle of Form IV: **those who have entered into darkness.** The verb is ascribed **to them** — not "it grew dark" but **"they were left in the dark".**

**The root ظ-ل-م has occurred in many places in the index** (`071-nuh.md`, `076-insan.md`, `068-kalem.md` and others), and the second branch of the root — *ẓulm*, wrongdoing — was treated there. **The lexicographers make the connection as "putting a thing somewhere other than where it belongs"; and darkness is light's not being where it belongs.** **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers frame it; I do not repeat it here.**

**And a point of word order must be recorded:** the verb *muẓlimūn* is used of people. **The verse, that is, recounts not the darkening of the sky but people's being left in the dark.** The sign is not what happens in the sky but **what happens to a person**.

## 36/38 — وَٱلشَّمْسُ تَجْرِى لِمُسْتَقَرٍّ لَّهَا ذَٰلِكَ تَقْدِيرُ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْعَلِيمِ

*Wa'sh-shamsu tajrī li-mustaqarrin lahā, dhālika taqdīru'l-ʿAzīzi'l-ʿAlīm*

"And the sun runs on **to a resting place appointed for it.** That is the **measure** set by the Mighty, the Knowing."

### A caveat of method — this first

**This verse is one of the places in the Qurʾān where claims of scientific miracle have been produced most abundantly.** Interpretations that draw this or that finding of modern astronomy out of the verse are widespread.

**Per the method (`STYLE.md`) and plainly: this commentary does not take that road.** The grounds:

1. **The verse's own proof is not a piece of astronomical information.** The second half of the sentence says so: *dhālika **taqdīru**'l-ʿAzīzi'l-ʿAlīm* — **"this is a measure."** The proof is not how the sun moves but that its movement is **measured**.
2. **For the one addressed the sign is the order visible to the eye.** The verse does not report a fact unknown to the one addressed; it names a fact he knows. **In the thirty-third verse the dead earth, in the thirty-seventh the night — all of them were things seen.**
3. **The wording of the verse does not require any particular cosmological model**, and because it does not require one it cannot count as the confirmation of any model.

**Below I give the sense of the word and transmit the classical explanations. Modern correlations are one point of view; they are not the proof of the verse, and they are kept within that caveat.**

### تَجْرِى — root ج-ر-ي

**The concrete sense: to flow.** The flowing of water is expressed with this root. And the root's second use belongs to seafaring: **الجَوَارِي (*al-jawārī*)** — ships that run along. The Qurʾān uses this word of ships.

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "turns" or "walks", it says **"runs".** To run announces a movement that is unbroken and keeps on of itself — **without a halt.**

**And two verses later the same field will come back:** in the fortieth verse *yasbaḥūn* (they swim) will be said of the heavenly bodies, and in the forty-first *fulk* (ship) will come. **Three times the sūra recounts the movement of the heavenly bodies in the language of water: it runs, it swims, and immediately after, the ship.** This is a verifiable lexical field.

### مُسْتَقَرّ — root ق-ر-ر, and three possibilities

**The root: to stay fixed in a place, to settle, to come to rest.** *Qarār* — settledness; *al-qarr* — cold (air that stands still in a place). **The root was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/39 (*dāru'l-qarār*).**

**The pattern of the word here comes from Form X, and in Arabic this pattern can be three different things. This is a grammatical datum:**

| Pattern | What it announces | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun of place** | A place | **The place where it will come to rest** |
| **Noun of time** | A time | **The time when it will come to rest** |
| **Mīmī verbal noun** | The act itself | **Coming to rest, settling** |

**All three are grammatically possible, and the exegetes divide along these three possibilities.**

### The classical explanations

**The table below is an inventory of what is transmitted; it is not a list of preferences.**

| Explanation | What *li-mustaqarrin lahā* means | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **It runs until an appointed time** | The *lām* here has the sense of *ilā* ("until"); the sun's running lasts for a term |
| 2 | **It runs towards a position of its own** | The noun-of-place reading; every body has an appointed place |
| 3 | **It runs to the arrival point of each day** | The daily rising and setting; the reading closest to the observation of the one addressed |
| 4 | **It runs to the annual turning points** | The shifting and turning of the sun's place of rising with the seasons |
| 5 | **It runs for the sake of stability** | The verbal-noun reading: the running secures the fixity of the order |

**All five explanations are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The common point can be verified, and it is what really needs to be recorded: on all five the running is *not aimless*.** All of them announce a limit, a goal or an order. **The second half of the verse already says this.**

### A note on a variant reading

**A reading in the form *lā mustaqarra lahā* is transmitted in the sources on the variant readings** — "there is no resting place for it", that is, it runs without ever stopping.

**This reading is not counted among the readings transmitted by mass transmission.** **I record it, because it reverses the meaning; but I issue no ruling upon it and do not go into the details of whom it is ascribed to.**

### تَقْدِير — root ق-د-ر

**The concrete sense of the root is to measure.** *Qadara'sh-shayʾa* — he measured a thing, determined its quantity. *Qadr* — quantity, measure. *Miqdār* — a measuring instrument, or the quantity measured.

**The Form II verbal noun *taqdīr*: the act of setting the measure, of determining.**

**And a repetition within the sūra must be recorded:** the next verse begins with the same root — *wa'l-qamara **qaddarnā**hu manāzil* (39). **Two verses in a row with the same root, one a noun and one a verb.** This is a verifiable repetition.

**I record this as my own reading:** read together, the two verses say the setting of the measure separately for the sun and for the moon, and with the same word. **The proof is not the movement itself; it is that the movement is bound to a measure.**

### ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْعَلِيم — the second pair of names

**In the fifth verse the sūra had named the one who sends down as *al-ʿAzīz al-Raḥīm*; here the same attribute comes with another partner: *al-ʿAzīz al-ʿAlīm*.**

| Verse | Pair | After what |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | *al-**ʿAzīz*** · *al-Raḥīm* | **The sending down** |
| **38** | *al-**ʿAzīz*** · *al-ʿAlīm* | **The measure** |
| 79 | — | *bi-kulli khalqin **ʿalīm*** |
| 81 | — | *al-Khallāqu'l-**ʿAlīm*** |

**This should be read together with the table set up in `041-fussilet.md` at 41/2:** there the pair of Ghāfir 40/2 was *al-ʿAzīz al-ʿAlīm* — **the same pair as Yāsīn 36/38.**

**My own reading:** *al-ʿAzīz* occurs twice in the sūra, and in each its partner changes. The sending down is paired with mercy, the measure with knowledge. **And *al-ʿAlīm* will return twice more towards the end of the sūra** (79, 81) — both times in the discussion of creation. **The distribution of the attribute is a verifiable datum.**

## 36/39 — وَٱلْقَمَرَ قَدَّرْنَٰهُ مَنَازِلَ حَتَّىٰ عَادَ كَٱلْعُرْجُونِ ٱلْقَدِيمِ

*Wa'l-qamara qaddarnāhu manāzila ḥattā ʿāda ka'l-ʿurjūni'l-qadīm*

"And the moon — We have appointed stations for it, until it comes round again **like an old dried date-stalk**."

### Word order: the sun nominative, the moon accusative

**There is a difference of case between the two verses, and it can be verified:**

| Verse | Word | Case | Its role in the sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 38 | *wa'**sh-shamsu*** | **Nominative** | Subject — the head of the sentence |
| 39 | *wa'l-**qamara*** | **Accusative** | Object — governed by an elided verb |

**On the accusative reading the structure Arabic calls *ishtighāl* is at work:** with *wa qaddarna'l-qamara qaddarnāhu* understood, the verb is present both covertly and overtly. **A nominative reading (*wa'l-qamaru*) is also transmitted in the sources on the variant readings;** on that reading the sentence takes the same structure as the thirty-eighth verse. **I give no names;** the difference in meaning is not fundamental.

**An observation:** on the accusative reading what comes to the fore is that **something is done to** the moon (*qaddarnāhu* — We appointed for it), whereas on the nominative reading the moon **is recounted in its own right.** **This is an observation; it is not a preference.**

### مَنَازِل — root ن-ز-ل

***Manzil*** — a place one comes down to; the halting place where a traveller stops. Its plural is *manāzil*. **The root also occurred in the sūra in the forms *tanzīl* (5) and *munzilīn* (28)** — in the sūra ن-ز-ل appears in three different derivatives.

**The background of the word is concrete and must be recorded:** the Arabs followed the moon's positions in the sky, called them ***manāzil al-qamar***, and reckoned the seasons, the times of rain and the times for travel by these positions. **The verse, that is, uses a word belonging to a calendar system the one addressed himself used.**

**This determines how the verse is to be understood:** *manāzil* is not a concept foreign to the one addressed — **it is the name of his own calendar.** The proof is built not from an unknown fact but **from a measure used every day.**

### كَٱلْعُرْجُونِ ٱلْقَدِيم — the material of the simile

**عُرْجُون** — the **stalk** of a date cluster that remains on the branch after the fruit has been taken off. As it dries it **grows thin, turns yellow and takes on a curve.**

**ٱلْقَدِيم** — root **ق-د-م**: to go before, to grow old. Here: **old, with time gone over it.** That is, drier still and thinner still.

**The picture the simile gives: a thin, yellowish, curved sliver — that is, the crescent.**

**And the material of the simile must be recorded, and this is the most striking side of the verse:** a heavenly body is likened to **the dried branch of a tree in the courtyard of the one addressed.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is what the thing is likened to:** in recounting the sky the sūra reaches not upward but downward. The evidential section had begun with dead earth and continued with a palm grove; now the moon is recounted with a piece of the tree in that grove. **Earth and sky are spoken of with the same vocabulary.**

**And the link inside the sūra can be verified:** in the thirty-fourth verse *nakhīl* (palm trees) was named — **in its fruit-bearing state.** In the thirty-ninth the same tree is named — **with its dried branch.** **Two occurrences five verses apart, in two different ages of the tree.** I record this as an observation.

### حَتَّىٰ عَادَ — the expression of the cycle

**Word order:** *ḥattā* — a particle announcing a goal; *ʿāda* — a perfect verb, **"it returned, it came back".**

**The choice of verb is worth recording: *ʿāda* — not "it became" but "it returned".** The crescent, that is, is not an end the moon arrives at but **a beginning it comes back to.** The word carries the cycle inside itself.

**And the perfect tense is used** — although the event is a recurring one. **In Arabic the perfect is used for recurring events as well, in order to announce certainty;** the lexicographers record this.

## 36/40 — لَا ٱلشَّمْسُ يَنۢبَغِى لَهَآ أَن تُدْرِكَ ٱلْقَمَرَ وَلَا ٱلَّيْلُ سَابِقُ ٱلنَّهَارِ وَكُلٌّ فِى فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ

*La'sh-shamsu yanbaghī lahā an tudrika'l-qamara wa la'l-laylu sābiqu'n-nahār, wa kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn*

"**Neither may the sun overtake the moon, nor can the night outstrip the day.** Each swims in an orbit."

### يَنۢبَغِى — root ب-غ-ي

**The core sense of the root: to want, to seek, to go after.** *Baghā* — he wanted, he sought. And the second branch of the root: **to overstep the bound, insolence** — *baghy*. The lexicographers make the connection thus: to want a thing **too much** leads to crossing the limit.

**Form VII (*inbighāʾ*) turns the direction of the root:** *yanbaghī* — **to be fitting, to be seemly, to lie within the possible.**

**And two readings are transmitted for the sense of the word here:**

| Reading | What *lā yanbaghī* means | Upshot |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **It is not possible** | A physical impossibility: the order does not permit it |
| 2 | **It is not fitting, not seemly** | An overstepping of position: each has its own work |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **And the common point can be verified: on either reading what is announced is a *limit*.**

### A repetition inside the sūra: two occurrences of *yanbaghī*

**This verb occurs twice in the sūra, and both times it announces a limit. This is one of the most striking internal repetitions in Yāsīn:**

| Verse | Expression | Whose limit |
|---|---|---|
| **40** | *la'sh-shamsu **yanbaghī** lahā an tudrika'l-qamar* | **The heavenly bodies'** — the limit of the order |
| **69** | *wa mā **yanbaghī** lah* | **The Prophet's / the text's** — the limit of the word |

**Both occurrences can be verified.** **I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sharing of the verb:** the sūra uses the same verb once for the order of the sky and once for the kind to which the revelation belongs. In the fortieth verse the sun cannot enter the moon's field; in the sixty-ninth the Qurʾān does not enter the field of poetry. **What is said in both sentences is the same: everything has a field of its own, and the fields do not mix.**

### أَن تُدْرِكَ — root د-ر-ك

**The concrete sense: to catch up, to come up from behind and seize.** *Adraka* — he caught up, he reached. And from here **إِدْرَاك (*idrāk*)** — to grasp: **the mind's catching up with a thing.** The lexicographers record this connection. **The root has occurred in `068-kalem.md`.**

**The picture in the sentence, then, is a chase** — and the verse does not say that the chase comes to nothing; it says **that there is no chase.**

### وَلَا ٱلَّيْلُ سَابِقُ ٱلنَّهَارِ — the point of the nominal sentence

**A point of word order, and it must be recorded:** the first clause was built **with a verb** (*yanbaghī lahā an tudrika*), the second is built **with a noun** (*sābiqu'n-nahār* — an active participle in a construct).

| Clause | Structure | What it announces |
|---|---|---|
| *Lā'sh-shamsu yanbaghī lahā an tudrika'l-qamar* | **Verbal sentence** | That an event does not occur |
| *Wa la'l-laylu **sābiqu**'n-nahār* | **Nominal sentence** | That an **attribute** is absent |

**In Arabic the nominal sentence announces continuity and fixity.** **The second clause, that is, does not say "the night does not outstrip the day"; it says "the night is not an outstripper of the day"** — that is, it has no such attribute, and never has had.

**I record this distinction as an observation about word order.**

### What is being recounted? — a disagreement

**The exegetes differ over what the sentence announces:**

| View | What is recounted |
|---|---|
| 1 | **That the times do not mix** — the sun belongs to the day, the moon to the night; neither enters the other's time |
| 2 | **That the order is not broken** — none of them leaves its own orbit and enters another's field |
| 3 | **That the sequence does not change** — night and day follow one another; neither gets in front of the other |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The common point: the keeping of a limit.**

### فَلَك — root ف-ل-ك

**The sense of the root: roundness, turning.**

- **فَلْكَة** — the round weight at the head of a spindle; and the round of the knee.
- **فَلَك** — that which turns, that which is round; the circle a heavenly body follows.
- **فُلْك** — **ship.** The same root.

**The lexicographers record that *fulk* and *falak* come from the same root** and explain the connection by the picture of **floating/turning on water**. **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers frame it, not as a definite etymological ruling.**

**And the link inside the sūra can be verified and is striking:** in the fortieth verse *falak*, **in the forty-first *fulk*.** **Two verses in a row, the same root, two different words** — one in the sky, one at sea. **I record this as an observation;** I do not claim that the sūra sets up a deliberate play on words. What I record is that two derivatives of the root have been used **one after the other**.

**And a caveat of method — per the method (`STYLE.md`):** no modern model of orbits and no particular astronomical structure is drawn out of the word *falak*. **The lexical sense of the word is "that which turns / that which is round", and that is the information the verse gives.** Correlations beyond this are one point of view and are not the proof of the verse.

### يَسْبَحُون — the form used for rational beings

**س-ب-ح** — this root has been analysed in many places in the index: the core sense is **to swim away in water or in space.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**There is a detail of word order that belongs here, and the lexicographers record it:** the verb has come in the form ***yasbaḥūn*** — that is, the **sound masculine plural**, the plural form used in Arabic for **rational beings**. For non-rational plurals *tasbaḥu* would have been expected.

**This usage is seen elsewhere in the Qurʾān too** — for instance at Yūsuf 12/4, where the prostration of the stars is recounted, the form for rational beings is likewise used. **The lexicographers record this as a style in which inanimate things are treated as rational.**

**I record this as a grammatical datum; I issue no ruling upon it.**

**And the choice of verb is worth recording:** *yasbaḥūn* — "they swim." **In the thirty-eighth verse *tajrī* (it runs) had been said of the sun.** Both verbs belong to a liquid element. **This section of the sūra recounts the sky in the language of water**, and in the next verse it will pass to real water, to the ship. **This transition can be verified.**

## 36/41-42 — وَءَايَةٌ لَّهُمْ أَنَّا حَمَلْنَا ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ فِى ٱلْفُلْكِ ٱلْمَشْحُونِ · وَخَلَقْنَا لَهُم مِّن مِّثْلِهِۦ مَا يَرْكَبُونَ

*Wa āyatun lahum annā ḥamalnā dhurriyyatahum fi'l-fulki'l-mashḥūn · Wa khalaqnā lahum min mithlihī mā yarkabūn*

"A sign for them too is that We carried their offspring in **the laden ship**. And We created for them, of the like of it, things that they ride."

### The third *wa āyatun lahum*

**The third and last sign of the block.** The first two were events of nature (the earth, the night); **this third is a human making — or so it appears.** And precisely for that reason the word order changes: in the first two the sign was a **noun** (*al-arḍ*, *al-layl*); here it is a **clause** (*annā ḥamalnā* — "our carrying").

**The sign, that is, is not the ship but the being carried.** **This is a verifiable difference of word order.**

### ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ — who was carried? A disagreement

**This is the most disputed place in the verse, and there is no certain knowledge.**

**ذ-ر-ر / ذ-ر-و** — the lexicographers differ over the sense of the root: *dharr* (a small particle, to scatter) or *dharw/dharā* (to winnow, to spread). **I issue no definite etymological ruling.** *Dhurriyya* — the line that spreads out from someone.

| View | Who was carried | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The forefathers in Noah's (Nūḥ) ark** | The sense of *dhurriyya* as "line of descent"; those carried are the forefathers, those addressed their offspring |
| 2 | **Their own children and families** | The direct sense of the word; everyone who travels by ship |
| 3 | ***Dhurriyya* here means "forefathers"** | The lexicographers record that in some usages the word points backwards as well |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **And I write plainly that there is no certain knowledge:** the verse does not mention Noah, gives no name of an event, states no time.

**One observation can be recorded:** the verse says *ḥamalnā* (We carried) — **the agent is not the ship.** Whichever ship it may be, that is, what the verse says is who the carrier is. **The disagreement does not change the work of the verse.**

### ٱلْمَشْحُون — root ش-ح-ن

**The concrete sense: to fill or load a vessel or a vehicle to the brim.** *Shaḥana's-safīnata* — he loaded the ship. *Mashḥūn* — a passive participle: **filled to bursting.**

**The second branch of the root is worth recording: شَحْنَاء (*shaḥnāʾ*) — rancour, enmity.** The lexicographers explain the connection by the picture of **"being full inside"**: rancour is the state of a breast that has been filled. **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers frame it.**

**And the choice of adjective is worth recording:** the ship is described not as "large" or "sound" but as ***laden***. **My own reading:** being laden announces that the ship's carrying capacity is used to the limit — that is, the weight of the load. And that is exactly the work of the verse: **a heavy load standing on water.**

### مِّن مِّثْلِهِۦ مَا يَرْكَبُون — things that are not named

**The verse says one thing more but does not name it: *min mithlihī mā yarkabūn* — "of the like of it, things that they ride."**

**The exegetes differ over what this is:**

| View | What | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Camels** — "the ships of the land" | This simile is made for the camel in Arabic; the verb *yarkabūn* is used most of camels |
| 2 | **Later ships and boats** | *Min mithlihī* — "of its like", that is, of the same kind |
| 3 | **Every kind of mount** | The generality of the word |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And this must be recorded: the verse does not name.** This is the third open list in the sūra — together with *mimmā lā yaʿlamūn* in the thirty-sixth verse and *fī shughulin* in the fifty-fifth.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is what these three places share:** where the sūra does not close the extent, it does not take the knowledge of the one addressed as its measure. **And in this verse the withholding of a name does work:** the expression "of the like of it" says that the ship is an instance of a **kind**; the members of the kind are not enumerated.

**And a caveat of method:** filling this opening with today's means of transport is a widespread interpretation. **This commentary does not carry out that filling** — the wording of the verse gives no list, and because it gives none it cannot count as the confirmation of any list. **This is the same caveat as the one entered at the thirty-sixth verse.**

## 36/43-44 — وَإِن نَّشَأْ نُغْرِقْهُمْ فَلَا صَرِيخَ لَهُمْ وَلَا هُمْ يُنقَذُونَ · إِلَّا رَحْمَةً مِّنَّا وَمَتَٰعًا إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

*Wa in nashaʾ nughriqhum fa-lā ṣarīkha lahum wa lā hum yunqadhūn · Illā raḥmatan minnā wa matāʿan ilā ḥīn*

"If We willed, We would drown them; then they would have no **one to answer their cry**, nor would they be rescued — **except as a mercy from Us and an enjoyment for a time.**"

### The favour held in suspension

**A point of word order, and it determines the whole work of the block:** across three verses the ship was recounted as a **favour**. The forty-third verse puts that same ship inside a **conditional clause**: *wa in nashaʾ nughriqhum.*

**The verse, that is, does not take the favour back; it says that the favour depends on a permission.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the conditional particle:** in Arabic *in* is for a condition that is possible but not certain. **The sentence is not a threat but a statement of dependence:** to stand upon the water is a permission renewed at every moment.

### صَرِيخ — a word that faces two ways

**ص-ر-خ** — the root: **to shout aloud, to cry out for help.** *Ṣurākh* — a cry.

**And the pattern of the word here (*faʿīl*) is open to two senses at once; the lexicographers record both:**

| Sense | Who the *ṣarīkh* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The one who cries out** — the one calling for help |
| 2 | **The one who runs to the cry** — the one who comes to the rescue, the helper |

**The context requires the second sense:** *fa-lā ṣarīkha lahum* — "there is no *ṣarīkh* **for** them", that is, **there is no one who runs to their aid.** **The lexicographers prefer the second sense in this construction; I record it.**

**And the doubleness of the word is worth recording in itself:** the same word announces both the one who calls and the one who comes. **The lexicographers attribute this to the word's being built on a sound:** the one who shouts and the one who answers the shout are the two ends of one event.

### وَلَا هُمْ يُنقَذُونَ — a repetition inside the sūra

**The root ن-ق-ذ occurs for the second time in the sūra.** **In the twenty-third verse the man who came running had said:** *wa lā **yunqidhūn*** — "[those gods] cannot rescue me." **Here:** *wa lā hum **yunqadhūn*** — "they are not rescued."

| Verse | Expression | Who / from whom |
|---|---|---|
| **23** | *wa lā **yunqidhūn*** | **The gods taken up** cannot rescue — active |
| **43** | *wa lā hum **yunqadhūn*** | **Those who drown** are not rescued — passive |

**Both can be verified.** **And the difference of pattern must be recorded:** the first is **active** (they cannot rescue), the second **passive** (they are not rescued). **My own reading:** in the first sentence the one who cannot rescue is named; in the second the one who might rescue is not named at all. In the story a candidate had been eliminated; in the evidential section there is not even a candidate.

**غ-ر-ق** — the root: to sink into water, to drown. *Ighrāq* — to sink something. The word occurs often in the Qurʾān in the stories of Noah and Pharaoh.

### إِلَّا رَحْمَةً مِّنَّا — the exception

**Word order:** *illā* here is counted an *istithnāʾ munqaṭiʿ*; that is, what is excepted is not of the same kind as that from which it is excepted. *Ṣarīkh* and *inqādh* are one kind, *raḥma* another. **The lexicographers record that in this case *illā* comes to mean "but, only"** — **the same structure was treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/25-26;** I rely on that treatment.

**The sentence says this, then: there is no rescuer who saves — what saves is mercy.**

**My own reading:** the verse does not deny the rescue, **it changes its source.** It says there is no help and then names the help.

### وَمَتَٰعًا إِلَىٰ حِينٍ — the closing of the block

**م-ت-ع** — the root: to **take enjoyment** of a thing, to benefit from it; and a thing's stretching out and lasting. *Matāʿ* — the thing enjoyed; household goods, provisions. **The word is used often in the Qurʾān of the life of this world** — it occurred in `040-gafir.md` at 40/39 in the form *innamā hādhihi'l-ḥayātu'd-dunyā matāʿ* and was treated there. **I rely on that treatment.**

**حِين** — an unspecified term. *Ilā ḥīn* — "until a time." **The length is not stated.**

**And that the block ends with this word is worth recording:** block IV counted three signs — earth, sky, ship — and closed all of them with a **time-limited** qualification. **My own reading:** the evidential section counts the favours and at the end says that all of them are bound to a term. **And the next block begins exactly from here:** in the forty-fifth verse it will be said *"guard against what is before you and what is behind you"* — that is, be aware of the term.

## 36/45-46 — وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ ٱتَّقُوا۟ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيكُمْ وَمَا خَلْفَكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُونَ · وَمَا تَأْتِيهِم مِّنْ ءَايَةٍ مِّنْ ءَايَٰتِ رَبِّهِمْ إِلَّا كَانُوا۟ عَنْهَا مُعْرِضِينَ

*Wa idhā qīla lahumu'ttaqū mā bayna aydīkum wa mā khalfakum laʿallakum turḥamūn · Wa mā taʾtīhim min āyatin min āyāti rabbihim illā kānū ʿanhā muʿriḍīn*

"When they are told, 'Guard against what is before you and what is behind you, that you may be shown mercy'… **No sign of the signs of their Lord comes to them but they turn away from it.**"

### A sentence whose answer is not given

**A point of word order, and this is the most striking place in the verse.**

**The forty-fifth verse begins with a conditional clause:** *wa idhā qīla lahum…* — "when they are told…". **And the answer to the condition is not given.** Without the sentence being completed, the text passes to the forty-sixth verse.

**The exegetes explain this thus:** the answer has been elided and the next verse stands in its place — that is, "they turn away". **In Arabic the dropping of the answer is common where the indication is plain;** the same rule was mentioned in `050-kaf.md` at 50/1 in the discussion of the answer to the oath.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the comparison of two verses — and this comparison can be verified within the sūra:**

| Verse | Call | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **45** | *Ittaqū* — **"guard"** | **Not given** — the sentence breaks off |
| **47** | *Anfiqū* — **"spend"** | **Given** — with a long objection |

**The two verses begin with the same pattern: *wa idhā qīla lahum*.** Both report a command. **But to one no answer at all is given, and to the other a detailed answer is given.**

**This is a countable difference.** **And my own reading is this:** the call to guard concerns an **inward matter**, and what answers it is silence — there is nothing to argue about, one merely turns away. The call to spend concerns **the hand**, and what answers it is objection. **The sūra shows which call produces speech:** the one in which something is asked of a person.

### مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيكُمْ وَمَا خَلْفَكُمْ — a disagreement

**What the construction announces is disputed:**

| View | What is before | What is behind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The hereafter** — what stands ahead | **This world / what befell earlier peoples** |
| 2 | **Past sins** | **Those to be committed in future** |
| 3 | **Dangers seen and known** | **Unseen dangers, coming from behind** |
| 4 | **Every direction** — an all-embracing expression | — |

**All four are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**And a contrast within the sūra must be recorded — this is the verse's most important link:**

**In the ninth verse those same two directions had been closed:** *wa jaʿalnā min bayni aydīhim saddan wa min khalfihim saddan* — **a barrier before them and behind them.**
**In the forty-fifth verse they are asked to guard against those same two directions:** *ittaqū mā bayna aydīkum wa mā khalfakum.*

**These two occurrences can be verified.** **And I record this as my own reading:** the sūra also names as a field of responsibility the very place it says is closed. **Read together, the two verses show that the description in the ninth verse is not a declaration of incapacity** — because a command is still being given for the same direction. A command presupposes that the one addressed can carry it out. **Verses sixty-six and sixty-seven will support this reading further** (taken up below).

### ٱتَّقُوا۟ — root و-ق-ي

**The concrete sense of the root: to put a shield in front of a thing, to protect.** *Wiqāya* — a shield, a protection. Form VIII, *ittiqāʾ*: **to protect oneself, to take something as one's shield.**

**The root was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/21 (*mā kāna lahum mina'llāhi min wāq* — they had no protector) and at 40/45 (*fa-waqāhu'llāh* — God protected him).** **I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here: the object of the verb.** *Ittaqū **mā bayna aydīkum*** — what is to be guarded against is not a thing but a **direction.** The verse does not say "guard against such and such", it says **"guard against what will come from that side"**.

### لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُون — word order

***Laʿalla*** — the particle of hope. And the verb is **passive**: *turḥamūn* — "you are shown mercy."

**Worth recording:** what guarding earns is given not as a right but as a **hope**; and the agent of the mercy is not named. **The verse, that is, does not set up an equation:** guarding does not make mercy necessary, it **opens onto** it.

### مُّعْرِضِين — root ع-ر-ض

**The concrete sense of the root: a thing's turning broadside, standing sideways.** *ʿArḍ* — breadth (as against length). Form IV, *iʿrāḍ*: **to turn the face away** — to turn one's side to someone, not to stand facing him.

**The picture in the word is worth recording, and I give it as the lexicographers record it:** turning away is not opposing. **The one who opposes turns his face and argues; the one who turns away turns his side and does not speak.**

**And the word order generalises:** *mā taʾtīhim **min āyatin min āyāti** rabbihim **illā** kānū ʿanhā muʿriḍīn* — the *mā … illā* pattern of restriction, and *min āyatin* indefinite in a negative: **"no sign at all".** **The sentence, that is, announces not an event but a rule** — the very same pattern as *mā yaʾtīhim min rasūlin illā…* in the thirtieth verse.

| Verse | Pattern | What comes | Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **30** | *mā yaʾtīhim min **rasūlin** illā…* | **A messenger** | *Yastahziʾūn* — **mockery** |
| **46** | *mā taʾtīhim min **āyatin** illā…* | **A sign** | *Muʿriḍīn* — **turning away** |

**The two patterns are verifiably the same.** **My own reading:** the sūra builds the same structure twice — once in the section of the story, once after the evidential section. **In the first what comes is a person and the reaction is mockery; in the second what comes is a sign and the reaction is a silent turn.** Neither reaction produces an argument.

## 36/47 — وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ أَنفِقُوا۟ مِمَّا رَزَقَكُمُ ٱللَّهُ قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَنُطْعِمُ مَن لَّوْ يَشَآءُ ٱللَّهُ أَطْعَمَهُۥٓ إِنْ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا فِى ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ

*Wa idhā qīla lahum anfiqū mimmā razaqakumu'llāhu, qāla'lladhīna kafarū li'lladhīna āmanū: a-nuṭʿimu man law yashāʾu'llāhu aṭʿamah? In antum illā fī ḍalālin mubīn*

"When they are told, '**Spend of what God has provided you**', those who deny say to those who believe: '**Are we to feed one whom God would have fed had He willed? You are in plain error.**'"

### To whom is the objection addressed?

**This must be recorded first, because it determines the character of the objection.**

**Word order:** *qāla'lladhīna kafarū **li'lladhīna āmanū*** — those who deny say it **to those who believe**.

**This, then, is not a question of theology.** No one is putting a question to God, no one is wondering what destiny is. **The sentence is a move in an argument, directed at the other side.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the qualification *li'lladhīna āmanū*:** the verse takes particular care to state to whom the words are said. Without that statement the sense would not have suffered. **That it is stated shows that the words are not a thought but a retort.**

### The qualification inside the command itself

**And now comes the most important point of word order in the verse.**

**The command is this:** *anfiqū **mimmā razaqakumu'llāh*** — "spend **of what God has provided you**."

**The command states within itself the source of what is asked for.** The command, that is, already contains this: what is in your hand is not yours, it has been given to you.

**And the objection takes precisely this principle and turns it against the command:** *law yashāʾu'llāhu aṭʿamah* — "had God willed, He would have fed him."

| | **The command (47a)** | **The objection (47b)** |
|---|---|---|
| The principle it rests on | **God** gives provision | **God** gives provision |
| The conclusion it draws | **So give** | **So do not give** |

**The same premise, two opposite conclusions. This is a verifiable textual datum.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the command:** the objection brings no new information. It repeats **the very principle the command rests on** and draws the opposite conclusion from it.

### Where the objection breaks

**This has to be written carefully and step by step, because the premise of the objection is not false.**

**The verse does not reject the sentence "had God willed, He would have fed him".** The premise is true and the verse does not touch it at all. The break is in the conclusion, and it shows in three places:

**First: the same reasoning collapses when applied to the objector's own provision.** What they eat falls under the same rule. *Mimmā razaqakumu'llāh* — the command had already said so. **If "had God willed, He would have given" is a ground for not giving, the same sentence makes what is in their own hands inexplicable too:** there is no longer any ground for its having been given to them. **The objection is a sentence that cuts away its own position as well.**

**Second: the sentence puts a piece of information in the place of an obligation.** "Had God willed, it would have been" is a piece of **information**; "so I will not do it" is a **decision**. **There is no logical link between the two** — because the same sentence can be built for everything that has been done. A man eating a meal does not say "had God willed, He would have fed me" and give up eating.

**Third: the objection uses the authority that issues the command against that authority.** The one who commands spending and the one who gives provision are the same. **The objection sets "the will of the giver" against "the command of the giver"** — whereas the command itself is a part of that will.

**I record these three points as my own reading, and their grounds are the wording of the verse** — in particular the qualification *mimmā razaqakumu'llāh*.

### Using destiny to escape responsibility — a pattern within the Qurʾān

**This move is recorded in several places in the Qurʾān, and the structure is the same in all of them. The following three places can be verified:**

| Place | Saying | To escape what |
|---|---|---|
| al-Anʿām 6/148 | *Law shāʾa'llāhu mā ashraknā* — "had God willed, we would not have associated" | **The responsibility of belief** |
| al-Naḥl 16/35 | *Law shāʾa'llāhu mā ʿabadnā min dūnihī min shayʾ* — "had God willed, we would not have served anything besides Him" | **The responsibility of worship** |
| **Yāsīn 36/47** | *Law yashāʾu'llāhu aṭʿamah* — "had God willed, He would have fed him" | **The responsibility of wealth** |

**All three sentences are in the same pattern: *law* + *shāʾa'llāh* + a negative conclusion.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the shared structure of these three places:** every time, the pattern moves **from a true premise to a false conclusion**, and every time the work the move does is the same: **to take the person out of the sentence.** The subject of the sentence is God, its addressee no one; the speaker has placed himself outside the account.

**And a limit is drawn in this commentary:** the verses do not criticise **belief in destiny**. What is criticised is the use of a true belief **to escape a task**. **When this distinction is not kept the verse is read wrongly:** the verse does not say that the sentence "had God willed, it would have been" is false; it says that that sentence **cannot be the ground for not spending**.

### إِنْ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا فِى ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ — a repetition inside the sūra

**The objection ends with a verdict, and that verdict had occurred earlier in the sūra — but in another mouth.**

| Verse | Expression | Who says it | About whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **24** | *Innī idhan la-fī **ḍalālin mubīn*** | **The man who came running** | **About himself** — as a supposition |
| **47** | *In antum illā fī **ḍalālin mubīn*** | **Those who deny** | **About the believers** — as a verdict |

**The same construction, two different mouths.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**And the difference must be recorded:** the man uses the word **of himself** and **conditionally** — "if I take another, **I** would be in error." Those who deny use the word **of another** and **absolutely** — "**you** are in plain error."

**I record this as my own reading:** the same word is once a measure a person applies to himself and once a label stuck on someone else. **The sūra sets the two side by side twenty-three verses apart, and there is no sentence of comment between them.**

### أَنُطْعِم — root ط-ع-م

**The root: to eat, to taste.** *Ṭaʿām* — food; *iṭʿām* (Form IV) — **to feed, to satisfy.**

**The choice of word is worth recording and is required by the word order:** the command was *anfiqū* (spend) — a general word, "lay out, give." **The objection slides to a narrower word: *nuṭʿimu* — "are we to feed".**

**My own reading:** the objection reduces the command to its **most concrete and most necessary** form and rejects it in that form. And precisely for that reason the weight of the refusal increases: what is refused is not a gift but a **feeding**. **This can be verified by the verse's own choice of word.**

**ن-ف-ق** — the root: to be used up, to run out; and *nafaq* — an underground passage (one that is entered at one end and left at the other). The lexicographers describe *infāq* as "wealth going out of the hand". **In the root of the word there is a diminishing** — and the verse commands that diminishing while recalling its source.

### What this says to the present

**The pattern of this verse requires no religious dress.** The structure is this: **to escape a task by making a truth that lies outside the task into a ground.**

The same structure is seen in sentences like "that is just how the system works", "what would change if I helped", "whoever deserves it will find it anyway". **The common point is that such sentences mostly rest on a true observation** — and precisely for that reason they are persuasive.

**The measure the verse sets is worth recording, and it comes out of the command itself:** the question is not "how does the system work" but **"what do you have in your hand"**. *Mimmā razaqakumu'llāh* — the scope of the command is limited to what is in the person's hand. **A verdict about the system does not lift the responsibility for what is in the hand.**

**This is an interpretation and I record it as my own reading; its ground is the qualification in the wording of the command.** **And I take no side in any current dispute:** what the verse describes is not a group of people but **a sentence pattern**; whoever builds that pattern falls under it.

## 36/48-50 — وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ · مَا يَنظُرُونَ إِلَّا صَيْحَةً وَٰحِدَةً تَأْخُذُهُمْ وَهُمْ يَخِصِّمُونَ · فَلَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ تَوْصِيَةً وَلَآ إِلَىٰٓ أَهْلِهِمْ يَرْجِعُونَ

*Wa yaqūlūna matā hādha'l-waʿdu in kuntum ṣādiqīn · Mā yanẓurūna illā ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan taʾkhudhuhum wa hum yakhiṣṣimūn · Fa-lā yastaṭīʿūna tawṣiyatan wa lā ilā ahlihim yarjiʿūn*

"They say: 'When is this promise, if you are truthful?' **They are waiting for nothing but a single cry** — which will seize them while they are **wrangling**. **They will be able to leave no bequest, nor to return to their households.**"

### مَا يَنظُرُون — the second branch of the root

**ن-ظ-ر** — the known sense of the root is to look. **But the second branch of the root is to wait:** *intiẓār* — waiting; *naẓira* — he waited, he granted a respite. *Anẓara* — he granted a respite (*fa-naẓiratun ilā maysara*, al-Baqara 2/280).

**The lexicographers make the connection thus: to look is to watch for what is to come.** **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers record it.**

**And the sense here runs towards waiting:** *mā yanẓurūna illā ṣayḥatan wāḥida* — "they wait for nothing but a single cry."

**A point of word order:** the sentence ascribes to them a **waiting** — whereas they are not waiting, they are **asking in mockery** (*matā hādha'l-waʿd*). **The verse, that is, names the real state of affairs beneath the mocking question:** something really does stand in front of the one who asks "when".

### وَهُمْ يَخِصِّمُون — the moment of the seizing

**Word order:** *wa hum yakhiṣṣimūn* — **a circumstantial clause.** The cry, that is, seizes them while they are at some occupation, and that occupation is named: **wrangling.**

**خ-ص-م** — the root: **to be an adversary, to wrangle, to go to law.** *Khaṣm* — the opposing party in a suit; *khuṣūma* — contention. **The root has occurred in `050-kaf.md`.**

**The form of the word here is striking:** its original is *yakhtaṣimūn* (Form VIII), and the *tāʾ* has been turned into a *ṣād* and assimilated (*idghām*). **Several different readings of this word are transmitted in the sources on the variant readings** (*yakhiṣṣimūn*, *yakhaṣṣimūn*, *yakhṣimūn* and the like). **Since I am not certain of their details or of their ascriptions I give no names.** **On one reading the sense slides towards "they wrangle with one another", on another towards "they are at enmity"; the difference is not fundamental.**

**And a repetition within the sūra can be verified:**

| Verse | Expression | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **49** | *wa hum **yakhiṣṣimūn*** | **Those seized all at once** — in a state of wrangling |
| **77** | *fa-idhā huwa **khaṣīmun** mubīn* | **Man** — who comes out of a drop and becomes a plain adversary |

**The same root in two different places.** **My own reading:** in the forty-ninth verse wrangling is a **moment**, in the seventy-seventh an **attribute**. The sūra first catches man in the act of arguing, then names him an arguer.

### تَوْصِيَة — root و-ص-ي

**The concrete sense of the root belongs to plants, and the lexicographers record it:** *waṣā'n-nabtu* — **the plant grew thick, its branches ran into one another.** It is close to *waṣala*: **to join, to attach one thing to another.**

**From here *waṣiyya*: a person's *attaching* his word to what comes after him.** A bequest, that is, is an attempt to continue by words the bond that death cuts.

**The picture in the word determines the work of the verse:** *fa-lā yastaṭīʿūna **tawṣiyatan*** — no word can be attached. **The cut is complete.**

**The pattern must be recorded: *tawṣiya* — the verbal noun of Form II**, that is, it announces the performing of the act. And it comes **indefinite**: "not even a single bequest."

### Two things prevented at once

**The verse counts two things, and both belong to "afterwards":**

| What is prevented | Its direction |
|---|---|
| *Tawṣiya* | **Forward** — the word's reaching beyond oneself |
| *Ilā ahlihim yarjiʿūn* | **Backward** — the person's returning to his own house |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the direction of the two verbs:** suddenness is the absence of an interval. And when there is no interval two things become impossible at once — **leaving something behind and returning somewhere.** All of a person's preparation with regard to death is these two; the verse removes both in a single sentence.

**And the word *ahl* is worth recording:** the verse does not say "to their houses", it says **"to their households"**. What is prevented is returning not to a place but **to people**.

**The third occurrence of the root ر-ج-ع in the sūra**, and it closes block V. **A table of the five occurrences was given in the section on structure.**

## 36/51-52 — وَنُفِخَ فِى ٱلصُّورِ فَإِذَا هُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَجْدَاثِ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَنسِلُونَ · قَالُوا۟ يَٰوَيْلَنَا مَنۢ بَعَثَنَا مِن مَّرْقَدِنَا هَٰذَا مَا وَعَدَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ وَصَدَقَ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ

*Wa nufikha fi'ṣ-ṣūri fa-idhā hum mina'l-ajdāthi ilā rabbihim yansilūn · Qālū yā waylanā man baʿathanā min marqadinā? Hādhā mā waʿada'r-Raḥmānu wa ṣadaqa'l-mursalūn*

"The trumpet is blown; and all at once there they are, **streaming out of the graves** towards their Lord. They say: '**Woe to us! Who has raised us from where we lay?** — This is what the Merciful promised; **the messengers told the truth.**'"

### ٱلصُّور — a disagreement

**This word was treated in `069-hakka.md` at 69/13, and two readings were tabulated there.** The finding there was this: the first reading (**horn/trumpet**) is the reading of the majority and agrees better with the structure of the verb — *nufikha **fī**'ṣ-ṣūr*, "it was blown **into** the *ṣūr*"; for blowing into a form a different word order would be expected. **I rely on that treatment and do not repeat it here.**

**And as was recorded there, the Qurʾān speaks of two blowings** (al-Zumar 39/68). **In Yāsīn how many blowings there are is not said**, and this must be recorded: the verse recounts a single blowing but gives no number. **It is widely said in the classical commentaries that this is the second blowing; I transmit that, I do not add it to the wording of the verse.**

### ٱلْأَجْدَاث — root ج-د-ث

***Jadath*** — a grave, a tomb; its plural is *ajdāth*. **The root was analysed in `054-kamer.md` at 54/7**, and the word's sense of "the pit itself, the dug place" was recorded there. **It has occurred in `070-mearic.md` at 70/43 too.**

**And in `054-kamer.md` a comparison table of three verses was given — Yāsīn 36/51 was already named there.** I do not repeat the table here; **I rely on that treatment.** The only thing that needs recording is that all three verses use the same noun and three different verbs: *yansilūn* (Yāsīn), *yakhrujūn* (al-Qamar), *yakhrujūna sirāʿan* (al-Maʿārij).

### يَنسِلُون — root ن-س-ل

**The concrete sense of the root: to separate from something and come out, to be shed.**

- **نَسَلَ الشَّعْرُ / الرِّيشُ** — the hair fell out, the feathers were shed (separating from the body).
- **نَسَل** — **offspring, descent**: what **separates and comes out** from someone.
- **نَسَلَ فِى المَشْيِ** — he walked fast and without pause.

**The lexicographers tie these branches to the same core: to separate from an origin and come out one after another.**

**The verb occurs in the same form in one other place in the Qurʾān:** *wa hum min kulli ḥadabin **yansilūn*** (al-Anbiyāʾ 21/96).

**And the choice of word must be recorded:** the verse does not say "they rise" or "they come to life". It says **"they stream out"** — fast, one after another, without pause.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the two branches of the root:** the same word recounts both **coming out by birth** (*nasl*) and **coming out of the grave**. **The lexicographers record this shared ground; I make no point of it, I note that the two branches of the root come together in the same word.** And the word's place within the sūra supports this: in the seventy-seventh verse man's coming out **of a drop** will be recounted. **Two comings-out, standing at the two ends of the same sūra.**

### يَٰوَيْلَنَا — word order

***Yā waylanā*** — the same structure as *yā ḥasratan* in the thirtieth verse: **calling to an addressee that is not there** (*nidāʾ majāzī*). *Wayl* — perdition, woe.

**There are two instances of *nidāʾ majāzī* in the sūra** (30 and 52), **and both call upon a word of regret.** It is a verifiable repetition. **And the mouths are different:** the one in the thirtieth verse belongs to the narration, the one in the fifty-second to those raised.

### مَّرْقَد — root ر-ق-د

**The root: to sleep.** *Raqada* — he slept; *ruqūd* — the state of sleep. **The root has occurred in `051-zariyat.md`.**

**The pattern: *marqad* — a noun of place. "The place where one sleeps, a bed."**

**And the choice of word is the most striking side of the verse:** those raised do not call the grave "a grave". They say **"where we lay"**.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is whose mouth the word is in:** this word belongs not to **the narrator** but to **those raised**. The verse gives no definition of the state of the *barzakh*; **it reports the perception of the one speaking.** For one who wakes there is no elapsed time — sleep is named as sleep only on waking.

**And a note — the same line was kept in `040-gafir.md` at 40/46:** it is transmitted that in the classical commentaries inferences about the state of the **barzakh** have been drawn from this verse. **I content myself with transmitting that discussion and issue no ruling.**

### هَٰذَا مَا وَعَدَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ — who is speaking? A disagreement

**The exegetes differ over who speaks the sentence:**

| View | Who speaks | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The same people** — the continuation of their question | That no connective and no *qāla* has come between |
| 2 | **The angels or the believers** — an answer to the question | The content of the sentence: a confirmation, an informing |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**One datum internal to the text can be recorded:** there is no new *qālū* in the sentence — it is joined by **faṣl**. **This appears to support the first view.** **It is an observation, not a preference.**

### وَصَدَقَ ٱلْمُرْسَلُون — the longest ring of the sūra closes

**And now comes the most striking internal link of the sūra.**

**In the third verse the sūra had said this to the Prophet:** *innaka lamina'**l-mursalīn*** — "you are one of the messengers." This was a sentence strengthened **with an oath** and built **with three devices of emphasis** — that is, an addressee who **rejects** was presupposed.

**In the fifty-second verse the same word returns, and this time it is spoken by those who denied it:** *wa ṣadaqa'**l-mursalūn*** — "**the messengers told the truth.**"

| Verse | Expression | Who says it | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3** | *innaka lamina'l-**mursalīn*** | **The narrator** — to the Prophet | **In this world**, with three emphases |
| **52** | *wa ṣadaqa'l-**mursalūn*** | **Those raised** | **When they rise**, with no emphasis |

**This is a correspondence that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the word the two sentences share:** the claim put forward with emphasis at the beginning of the sūra is spoken in the middle of the sūra **by the other side** and with no emphasis at all. **And the falling away of the devices of emphasis is a datum of word order:** in the third verse there were *inna* and the *lām* because there was resistance opposite; in the fifty-second there is none of them, because no resistance is left.

**The root ر-س-ل occurs in seven places in the sūra** (3, 13, 14, 16, 20, 30, 52) **and will be tabulated separately in the closing section.**

## 36/53-54 — إِن كَانَتْ إِلَّا صَيْحَةً وَٰحِدَةً فَإِذَا هُمْ جَمِيعٌ لَّدَيْنَا مُحْضَرُونَ · فَٱلْيَوْمَ لَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْـًٔا وَلَا تُجْزَوْنَ إِلَّا مَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

*In kānat illā ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan fa-idhā hum jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn · Fa'l-yawma lā tuẓlamu nafsun shayʾan wa lā tujzawna illā mā kuntum taʿmalūn*

"It is but a single cry; and all at once there they all are, brought present before Us. **Today no soul is wronged in the least, and you are recompensed only for what you used to do.**"

### The verse in which the exact repetitions are knotted

**This single verse carries two separate internal repetitions of the sūra at once, and both can be verified:**

| Piece | Where it had occurred |
|---|---|
| *In kānat illā **ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan*** | **36/29** — exactly the same five words |
| *jamīʿun ladaynā **muḥḍarūn*** | **36/32** — exactly the same three words |

**The fifty-third verse, that is, joins the first half of the twenty-ninth verse to the second half of the thirty-second.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these two exact repetitions:** in the section of the story the end of the people of the town (*ṣayḥa* → *khāmidūn*) and the end of the generations (*muḥḍarūn*) had been given separately. **The scene of the resurrection joins the two in a single sentence.** The sūra, that is, frames the resurrection not as a new event but as **the gathering up of what has already been recounted piece by piece.**

**And the endings of the two sentences should be compared:**

| Verse | How the sentence ends |
|---|---|
| **29** | *fa-idhā hum **khāmidūn*** — **gone out** |
| **53** | *fa-idhā hum jamīʿun ladaynā **muḥḍarūn*** — **brought present** |

**The same sound, two opposite outcomes: one an extinguishing, the other a being brought into the presence.**

### فَٱلْيَوْمَ — the time of the scene

***Al-Yawma*** — "today", definite. **The word occurs five times in this section of the sūra, and this is a countable density:**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| 54 | *fa'**l-yawma** lā tuẓlamu nafsun* |
| 55 | *inna aṣḥāba'l-jannati'**l-yawma** fī shughulin* |
| 59 | *wa'mtāzu'**l-yawma** ayyuha'l-mujrimūn* |
| 64 | *iṣlawha'**l-yawma** bimā kuntum takfurūn* |
| 65 | *a**l-yawma** nakhtimu ʿalā afwāhihim* |

**Five occurrences within twelve verses.** **My own reading:** the sūra frames the resurrection not as an account of the future but as **a day one is inside of**. Each time the word comes at or near the head of the sentence; **the scene is recounted not as "that day" but as "today".**

### لَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْـًٔا وَلَا تُجْزَوْنَ — an *iltifāt*

**A point of word order, and it can be verified:**

| Clause | Person | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| *Lā **tuẓlamu nafsun** shayʾan* | **3rd person** (*nafsun*) | Passive |
| *Wa lā **tujzawna** illā mā kuntum taʿmalūn* | **2nd plural** (*you*) | Passive |

**In Arabic the shift in the direction of address is called *iltifāt*.** **Here the general rule is given in the third person and its application in the second.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the first clause lays down a **principle** and points at no one — *nafsun*, indefinite: "no soul at all." The second clause applies that principle **to the one addressed**. **The principle first general, then personal.**

**شَيْـًٔا** — in the accusative as an *absolute object*: "not even by a hair's breadth." **The indefinite-in-a-negative pattern covers the smallest quantity.**

**ج-ز-ي** — the root: to give the equivalent of a thing, to match. In English *penalty* is used only of a negative requital; **in Arabic the root is neutral** and covers a good requital too.

### تَعْمَلُون and يَكْسِبُون — two verbs

**There is a difference of word that must be recorded, and it shows itself eleven verses apart:**

| Verse | Verb | Root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **54** | *mā kuntum **taʿmalūn*** | ع-م-ل | **What you did** — the act itself |
| **65** | *bimā kānū **yaksibūn*** | ك-س-ب | **What they earned** — what is left over from the act |

**ك-س-ب** — the core of this root is **to obtain, to earn**; it announces what **remains** with a person as the result of an act. **ع-م-ل** announces the **performing** of the act.

**I record this as an observation:** in the fifty-fourth verse the recompense is tied to **what is done**, in the sixty-fifth the testimony to **what is earned**. Both verbs name the same event from two different sides.

## 36/55-57 — إِنَّ أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ ٱلْيَوْمَ فِى شُغُلٍ فَٰكِهُونَ · هُمْ وَأَزْوَٰجُهُمْ فِى ظِلَٰلٍ عَلَى ٱلْأَرَآئِكِ مُتَّكِـُٔونَ · لَهُمْ فِيهَا فَٰكِهَةٌ وَلَهُم مَّا يَدَّعُونَ

*Inna aṣḥāba'l-jannati'l-yawma fī shughulin fākihūn · Hum wa azwājuhum fī ẓilālin ʿala'l-arāʾiki muttakiʾūn · Lahum fīhā fākihatun wa lahum mā yaddaʿūn*

"The people of the garden are today in an **occupation**, **taking their ease.** They and their spouses are in shade, reclining upon **couches**. There they have fruit, and they have **whatever they call for**."

### The language of the description of the garden

**The language of the descriptions of the garden was treated in detail in `056-vakia.md`:** the ordering of the senses, the choice of objects, the distinction between *ṭaʿām* and *fākiha* (necessity and surplus), whether the tables are joined by *wāw* or by a chain, and the table's closing with a sound. **I do not repeat it here; I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is the brevity of the table in Yāsīn.** In al-Wāqiʿa there were long tables for two groups; **in Yāsīn the table is four verses** (55-58) **and the fourth is not an object but a word.**

### فِى شُغُلٍ — an occupation that is not named

**ش-غ-ل** — the root: a task's **taking hold** of a person, leaving him no time for anything else. *Shughl/shughul* — occupation, the work that takes a person up.

**It is transmitted in the sources on the variant readings that the word is read in the forms *shughl* and *shughul*; there is no difference in meaning.**

**And the most striking side of the verse is this: what the occupation is, is not said.**

**Various explanations are transmitted in the classical commentaries** — being occupied with the favours, being occupied with one another, being so full as to remain unaware of the state of those in the fire. **I transmit these as explanations; I do not add them to the wording of the verse.** **There is no certain knowledge.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the word comes indefinite:** *fī **shughulin*** — unspecified. **This is the third open list in the sūra** (together with 36 and 42; tabulated above).

**And the word itself is worth recording:** the recompense is recounted not as a **condition** but as an **occupation**. Not a motionless repose, that is, but **a time that is filled.** I give this as an observation.

### فَٰكِهُون — the state-side of the root

**The root ف-ك-ه was analysed in detail in `056-vakia.md` at 56/20.** The finding there was this: the root has two branches — ***fākiha*** (fruit) and ***fukāha*** (pleasant talk, jesting, banter); and *ṭaʿām* announces necessity, *fākiha* surplus. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here is this, and it can be verified: Yāsīn uses both branches of the root two verses apart.**

| Verse | Word | Pattern | Which branch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **55** | *fākihūn* | **Active participle / adjective** | **State** — at ease, content, in good cheer |
| **57** | *fākiha* | **Noun** | **Object** — fruit |

**In al-Wāqiʿa the root occurred three times** (20, 32, 65) **and in all three as an object or a verb** (*fākiha*, *fākiha*, *tafakkahūn*). **In Yāsīn, by contrast, the person's state is named first and the object in his hand afterwards.** **This difference is a countable datum.**

**A note on the variant readings:** it is transmitted that the word is read in the forms *fākihūn* and *fakihūn*. **The second is the assimilate-adjective pattern and announces a settled state;** the first is an active participle. **The difference does not change the meaning fundamentally;** I record it.

**My own reading:** the first word of the table is not an object but a **state**. The sūra begins its description of the garden not with what is possessed but **with the condition of the person**.

### هُمْ وَأَزْوَٰجُهُمْ — spouses named together

**In `040-gafir.md` at 40/8 the naming of spouses together in the prayer of those who bear the Throne was treated** (*wa man ṣalaḥa min ābāʾihim wa azwājihim wa dhurriyyātihim*), **and a reference to `056-vakia.md` was made there.** **I rely on that line.**

**There is a lexical link that belongs here and can be verified within the sūra:** the word ***azwāj*** occurs twice in Yāsīn.

| Verse | Expression | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **36** | *khalaqa'l-**azwāja** kullahā* | **Creation** — all the pairs |
| **56** | *hum wa **azwājuhum*** | **Recompense** — together with their spouses |

**The same word, one in the evidential section and one in the section on recompense.** I record this as an observation.

### ٱلْأَرَآئِك — root أ-ر-ك

***Arīka*** — its plural is *arāʾik*. The lexicographers describe the word as **"a couch with a canopy, a covered divan"**: a shaded seat with a curtain or covering drawn over it.

**The root has occurred in `076-insan.md` and `083-mutaffifin.md`;** in both together with the same verb: *muttakiʾīna fīhā ʿala'l-arāʾik*.

**And the choice of the word agrees with another word of the verse:** *fī **ẓilālin*** — in shade. **A covered couch and shade are two words belonging to the same field.**

**مُتَّكِـُٔون** — root **و-ك-أ**, Form VIII: **to lean on something, to recline.** The lexicographers record: to recline is, in Arab culture, the posture of **one who is in no hurry**; the traveller and the labourer do not recline.

### مَّا يَدَّعُون — a disputed verb

**The root د-ع-و, Form VIII: *iddaʿā*.** **Two readings are transmitted for the sense of the word here:**

| Reading | Meaning | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What they ask for, what they call for** | The "to ask, to call" branch of the verb *iddiʿāʾ* |
| 2 | **What they claim as their own** | The "to attribute a thing to oneself" branch of *iddiʿāʾ* |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The common point can be verified:** on either reading what is given is what **the person himself determines** — not what is assigned from outside.

**A point of word order:** *lahum* is repeated **twice** — *lahum fīhā fākihatun **wa lahum** mā yaddaʿūn*. Two things could have been joined with a single *lahum*. **The repetition shows that two possessions are announced separately:** one a definite object (fruit), the other an extent whose limit is not given (what they call for).

## 36/58 — سَلَٰمٌ قَوْلًا مِّن رَّبٍّ رَّحِيمٍ

*Salāmun qawlan min rabbin raḥīm*

"**Peace** — **as a word** from a Lord who is merciful."

### The table closes with a sound

**This was treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/25-26, and a table was set up there:** in the description of the *sābiqūn* the objects are counted off sense by sense — touch, sight, an inward state, taste, sight — **and the table closed with hearing:** *illā qīlan salāman salāmā.* The finding there was this: *"all the objects counted fill a place; but what makes living somewhere bearable or unbearable is most often not the objects but the speech that is spoken there. The table has put this last."*

**Yāsīn follows the same order, and this can be verified:**

| Verse | What is given | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| 55 | Occupation, ease | **State** |
| 56 | Shade, couch, spouses | **Object and place** |
| 57 | Fruit, everything called for | **Object** |
| **58** | ***Salām*** | **Word** |

**Yāsīn's table is far shorter than that of al-Wāqiʿa — four verses — but it ends in the same place.** **This is a countable overlap between the two sūras.**

### The source of the peace — a comparison of three places

**In `056-vakia.md` at 56/91 the sentence *fa-salāmun laka min aṣḥābi'l-yamīn* was treated**, and three readings were tabulated there; **I do not repeat them.** One of the findings there was this: **"the content of the recompense is not an object but a word."**

**What belongs here is the naming of the source. A comparison of three places:**

| Place | Expression | The source of the peace |
|---|---|---|
| al-Wāqiʿa 56/26 | *illā qīlan **salāman salāmā*** | **Not named** — only the word heard |
| al-Wāqiʿa 56/91 | *fa-**salāmun** laka min aṣḥābi'l-yamīn* | **A group** — the people of the right (on one reading) |
| **Yāsīn 36/58** | ***Salāmun** qawlan min rabbin raḥīm* | ***The Lord* — named plainly** |

**All three places can be verified.** **I record this as my own reading:** al-Wāqiʿa gives the peace as an **atmosphere** — it is the only thing heard there. Yāsīn ties the peace to a **source**. The two sūras recount the same recompense from two different sides: one does not say where it comes from, the other does.

### قَوْلًا — a dispute over the parsing

**The word is in the accusative, and why it is in the accusative is disputed:**

| Explanation | Accusative as what | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The strengthening of *salām* with a verbal noun** (absolute object) | "Peace — and that as a real word" |
| 2 | **A circumstantial accusative** | "A peace in the form of a word" |
| 3 | **The object of an elided verb** (*yuqālu qawlan*) | "It is spoken to them as a word" |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **The common point:** the word stresses that the peace has been **spoken**.

**My own reading:** the sentence would have been intelligible without the word *qawlan* — *salāmun min rabbin raḥīm* would have sufficed. **That it has been added announces separately that the peace is not a condition but an *address*.** What is given, that is, is not a feeling of safety but **a word that has been spoken**.

### مِّن رَّبٍّ رَّحِيمٍ — indefinite

**A point of word order, and it is striking:** *rabbin* and *raḥīmin* come **indefinite** — "from a Lord", "merciful". It could have been said definitely, *mina'r-rabbi'r-raḥīm*.

**The lexicographers explain this use of the indefinite as *taʿẓīm* (exaltation):** to leave the attribute indefinite makes it indescribable. **I transmit it as a note.**

### A repetition inside the sūra: two occurrences of *raḥīm*

**The form *raḥīm* of the root ر-ح-م occurs twice in the sūra, and both times at the end of a sentence:**

| Verse | Expression | What is given |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | *tanzīla'l-ʿAzīzi'**r-Raḥīm*** | **The Book** — what is sent down |
| **58** | *salāmun qawlan min rabbin **raḥīm*** | **The peace** — what is spoken |

**Both occurrences can be verified.** **I record this as my own reading:** at the beginning of the sūra the name *raḥīm* is named as the source of a **text**, at the end as the source of a **peace**. **Both are speech.** The sūra uses the same name at the source of two different addresses — one that comes in this world, one that is given there.

## 36/59-62 — وَٱمْتَٰزُوا۟ ٱلْيَوْمَ أَيُّهَا ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · أَلَمْ أَعْهَدْ إِلَيْكُمْ يَٰبَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ أَن لَّا تَعْبُدُوا۟ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنَ إِنَّهُۥ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ · وَأَنِ ٱعْبُدُونِى هَٰذَا صِرَٰطٌ مُّسْتَقِيمٌ · وَلَقَدْ أَضَلَّ مِنكُمْ جِبِلًّا كَثِيرًا أَفَلَمْ تَكُونُوا۟ تَعْقِلُونَ

*Wa'mtāzu'l-yawma ayyuha'l-mujrimūn · Alam aʿhad ilaykum yā banī Ādama al lā taʿbudu'sh-shayṭān? Innahū lakum ʿaduwwun mubīn · Wa ani'budūnī, hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm · Wa laqad aḍalla minkum jibillan kathīran, a-fa-lam takūnū taʿqilūn*

"'**Stand apart today, you who are guilty!** O children of Adam! Did I not lay a covenant upon you, **that you should not serve Satan** — he is a plain enemy to you — **and that you should serve Me? This is a straight path.** He has led astray **a great multitude** of you. Were you not reasoning at all?'"

### وَٱمْتَٰزُوا۟ — root م-ي-ز

**The root: to separate two things from one another, to distinguish.** *Mayz* — separating; *tamyīz* — the power of discrimination. **Form VIII (*imtiyāz*): to become separate of oneself, to stand apart.**

**The choice of form is worth recording:** it does not say *umīzū* (be separated — passive) or *fāriqū*. ***Imtāzū*** — a Form VIII imperative: **separate yourselves.**

**The same root occurs in the Qurʾān in the context of sorting out:** *li-yamīza'llāhu'l-khabītha mina'ṭ-ṭayyib* (al-Anfāl 8/37).

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sense of the form:** the command is not an act of separating but a command **to separate oneself**. And this has a placing within the sūra: in verses fifty-five to fifty-eight the people of the garden were recounted, and in the fifty-ninth the command to separate comes. **The separation, that is, comes after the tables, not before them.** The two groups are first described separately, and then they actually separate.

**ٱلْمُجْرِمُون** — root **ج-ر-م**. The core the lexicographers give: *jarm* — **to pluck fruit from its branch, to cut off.** From here *jurm* — cutting off and taking, taking what is not deserved; **a crime.** **The picture in the word is a separating** — and the verb of the command is likewise a separating. **I record this as an observation.**

### أَلَمْ أَعْهَدْ إِلَيْكُمْ — root ع-ه-د

**The lexicographers dwell on the concrete sense of the root:** *ʿahd* is **to watch over a thing again and again, to keep coming back to it.** *Taʿahhada'sh-shayʾa* — he kept watch over a thing continually and did not neglect it. *Al-ʿAhd* — the rain's coming again and again to a place.

**From here comes the sense of a covenant: a thing that must be watched over continually.** **The root has occurred in `048-fetih.md` and `070-mearic.md`.**

**And this must be recorded: the verb is in the first person singular** — *alam **aʿhad***, "did I not lay a covenant?" **This is the only place in the sūra where the speech is directly in the first person singular** (and it continues at 61 with *iʿbudūnī*). **This is a verifiable datum of word order.**

**My own reading:** the closest address of the scene is here. Throughout the sūra the verbs were mostly in the "we" form (*jaʿalnā*, *arsalnā*, *aḥyaynā*, *khalaqnā*); at the moment of the reckoning the form passes to the singular.

### What is the covenant? — a disagreement

| View | What the covenant is | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The primordial pledge of the *fiṭra*** | The taking recounted at al-Aʿrāf 7/172; the breadth of the address *yā banī Ādam* |
| 2 | **The command given by way of the prophets** | That a covenant can only be known through a message delivered |
| 3 | **Reason itself** | The verse's ending with *a-fa-lam takūnū taʿqilūn* |

**All three are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

### يَٰبَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ — the widening of the address

**A datum of word order that must be recorded:** throughout the sūra the one addressed was *qawm* (6, 20, 26, 28), *al-ʿibād* (30), *hum*. **Here the address becomes *banī Ādam* — the whole human line.**

**And after the sixty-ninth verse *al-insān* will come** (77). **Towards its last block, that is, the sūra widens the addressee three times: a people → the children of Adam → man.** This is a countable progression.

### أَن لَّا تَعْبُدُوا۟ ٱلشَّيْطَٰن — "serving Satan"

**This expression must be read carefully, because no one says that he prostrates to Satan.**

**ع-ب-د** — the core of the root is **submission and obedience**. The lexicographers give the example *ṭarīqun muʿabbad* (a road levelled by being trodden underfoot): a road much trampled, much pressed down. **In the root of service, then, there is "leaving oneself to the one opposite"** — not the form of a rite.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sense of the root:** the verse ties service not to **intention** but to **obedience**. **Whoever's call is followed is the one served** — even if the person does not call it service.

**And this reading is completed together with the seventy-fourth verse:** there those who take up *āliha* (gods) will be spoken of, and it will be said that those gods cannot help. **Together the two verses describe two different errors: in one the one followed is an enemy, in the other the one followed is powerless.**

**إِنَّهُۥ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِين** — the fourth of the sūra's six occurrences of *mubīn*. **And it is worth recording:** the enmity is not **hidden** but *mubīn* — plain. The verse, that is, does not disclose a secret; it recalls something known.

### وَأَنِ ٱعْبُدُونِى هَٰذَا صِرَٰطٌ مُّسْتَقِيم

**The covenant has two clauses, and both are joined with the particle *an*:**

| Clause | Expression | Its direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Al lā taʿbudu'sh-shayṭān* | **Negative** — a prohibition |
| 2 | *Wa ani'budūnī* | **Positive** — a command |

**And the second clause ends with a sentence: *hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm*.**

**A repetition inside the sūra — the sūra's second occurrence of صراط:**

**In the fourth verse *ʿalā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm* was said of the Prophet — a position.** **In the sixty-first verse *hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* is said — a definition.**

**This is a correspondence that can be verified from the text.** **My own reading:** in the fourth verse the person **standing upon** the path was named; in the sixty-first the path **itself** is named. And the naming is done with a command: *iʿbudūnī* — "serve Me." **The path, that is, is described not as a route but as a turning.**

**The demonstrative must be recorded: *hādhā* — "this here."** It points to the command. **The name of the path becomes the command itself.**

### جِبِلًّا كَثِيرًا — root ج-ب-ل

**The root: *jabal* — mountain.** And from the same root:

- **جِبِلَّة (*jibilla*)** — make-up, nature, the original upon which a thing is created. *Jabala'llāhu'l-khalqa* — God shaped the creation.
- **جِبِلّ (*jibill*)** — **a great multitude, a people heaped up in a mass.**

**The lexicographers make the connection thus: people heaped up in great numbers like a mountain.** **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers record it.**

**It is transmitted in the sources on the variant readings that the word is read with several different vowellings** (*jibillan*, *jubulan* and the like). **I do not go into the details;** there is no difference in meaning.

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "many people", it says **"a great heap"**. **My own reading:** the word announces that the straying was not one by one but **in a mass** — and this will return in the next block of the sūra: in the seventy-fifth verse *jund* (army) will be said of the gods that are taken up. **Both words announce a crowd.**

**أَفَلَمْ تَكُونُوا۟ تَعْقِلُون** — the first of the sūra's two questions about reason (the other at 68). **Four questions were tabulated in the section on structure.**

**Word order:** *a-fa-lam **takūnū** taʿqilūn* — "were you not reasoning", in the past and in the durative form (*kāna* + imperfect). **The question, that is, looks not at a moment but at a lifetime.**

## 36/63-64 — هَٰذِهِۦ جَهَنَّمُ ٱلَّتِى كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ · ٱصْلَوْهَا ٱلْيَوْمَ بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَكْفُرُونَ

*Hādhihī jahannamu'llatī kuntum tūʿadūn · Iṣlawha'l-yawma bimā kuntum takfurūn*

"**This is the Hell you were promised.** Enter it today, for the denial you persisted in."

### The work of the demonstrative

**A point of word order:** *hādhihī* — "this here." **And in the sixty-first verse *hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* had been said — a demonstrative there too.**

| Verse | Demonstrative | To what |
|---|---|---|
| **61** | *Hādhā* | **The path** — the command to serve |
| **63** | *Hādhihī* | **Hell** — what was promised |

**Two demonstratives three verses apart.** **My own reading:** both are sentences of **pointing**, and both point to something already spoken of. **In the scene there is no longer any describing; there is pointing.** The same attitude runs through this whole section of the sūra: *al-yawma* five times, the demonstrative twice.

### كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُون — "what was promised"

**The verb is passive and in the durative form: *kuntum tūʿadūn* — "you were being promised."**

**Worth recording:** the word is *tūʿadūn* — from the root و-ع-د. **In Arabic *waʿd* is neutral**: it is used both for good and for ill (*waʿīd*, by contrast, is only for a threat). **Here *waʿd* is used of Hell.**

**And a link inside the sūra can be verified:** in the fifty-second verse those raised had said *hādhā mā **waʿada**'r-Raḥmān* — **the same root.** **Two occurrences: one for the resurrection itself, the other for Hell.** In both, what is said is the coming to pass of what was announced beforehand.

### ٱصْلَوْهَا — root ص-ل-ي

**The root: to enter fire, to be scorched in fire, to be exposed to burning fire.** **In `056-vakia.md` *taṣliya* (56/94) was treated** — a Form II verbal noun, announcing the performing of the act. **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here: here it is a Form I imperative** (*iṣlaw*) — that is, the act of **entering** the fire is commanded to the persons themselves. **In al-Wāqiʿa the act was something done to them (*taṣliya*); in Yāsīn it is commanded of them.** **This difference can be verified.**

### بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَكْفُرُون — the name of the ground

**A point of word order, and it sets up a pattern within the sūra. In this section the same pattern comes three times:**

| Verse | Pattern | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| 54 | *illā mā **kuntum taʿmalūn*** | To do |
| 64 | *bimā **kuntum takfurūn*** | To deny |
| 65 | *bimā **kānū yaksibūn*** | To earn |

**All three have the pattern *kāna* + imperfect — that is, an ongoing, repeated act.** **My own reading:** the recompense is tied not to a single moment but to **a state that has lasted.** The pattern announces not a momentary decision but a habit.

**ك-ف-ر** — the core of the root is **to cover**. *Kāfir* is explained by the lexicographers with the examples of "the farmer who covers the seed with earth" and "the darkness that covers the night". **The root has been treated in many places in the index; I do not repeat it here.**

**And the placing of the root here is worth recording:** in the ninth verse of the sūra a covering had been drawn over them (*fa-aghshaynāhum*). **Here, as the ground, a root of covering is named once more.** **I give this as an observation; I make no point of it.**

## 36/65 — ٱلْيَوْمَ نَخْتِمُ عَلَىٰٓ أَفْوَٰهِهِمْ وَتُكَلِّمُنَآ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَتَشْهَدُ أَرْجُلُهُم بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَكْسِبُونَ

*Al-yawma nakhtimu ʿalā afwāhihim wa tukallimunā aydīhim wa tashhadu arjuluhum bimā kānū yaksibūn*

"**Today We seal their mouths.** Their hands **speak to Us**, and their feet **bear witness** to what they earned."

### نَخْتِم — root خ-ت-م

**The concrete sense of the root: to close the mouth of a vessel or of a writing with a seal.** *Khātam* — a signet ring; *khitām* — the end of a thing, its closing (because the seal is pressed last of all).

**The root has occurred in `083-mutaffifin.md` (*raḥīqin makhtūm*) and in `042-sura.md` and `063-munafikun.md`.**

**And the picture in the root here must be recorded:** a seal does not merely **close** something, it is pressed in order to **certify** that it has remained closed. A sealed vessel is a vessel one can see has not been opened.

**Word order:** *nakhtimu **ʿalā** afwāhihim* — with the particle *ʿalā*. In Arabic *khatama ʿalā* announces a covering over. **The same structure is used at al-Baqara 2/7 of the heart and the ear** (*khatama'llāhu ʿalā qulūbihim wa ʿalā samʿihim*). **In Yāsīn the place sealed is *al-afwāh* — the mouths.**

### A comparison with Fuṣṣilat 41/20-21

**In `041-fussilet.md` at 41/20-21 the witness of the limbs was treated in detail.** The findings there were these: three witnesses are counted — **the ear, the eye, the skin**; in third place stands not the *heart* but the *jild*, because **the skin is the boundary — the face that touches the world**; and their owners protest to their skins: *lima shahidtum ʿalaynā*, and the answer is *anṭaqana'llāhu'lladhī anṭaqa kulla shayʾ*.

**Yāsīn recounts the same scene but parts from it at three points. A comparison table — every row can be verified from the two texts:**

| | **Fuṣṣilat 41/20-21** | **Yāsīn 36/65** |
|---|---|---|
| **The witnesses** | **Ear, eye, skin** | **Hands, feet** |
| **The state of the mouth** | **Not mentioned — free** | ***Sealed*** |
| **The verb of the witnessing** | *Shahida* — **all three with the same verb** | *Tukallimu* (hands) + *tashhadu* (feet) — **two different verbs** |
| **To whom the speaking is** | Not stated | ***Tukallimu-nā*** — **"to Us"** |
| **Is there a protest** | **Yes** — *lima shahidtum ʿalaynā* | **No** |
| **What the witness concerns** | *Bimā kānū **yaʿmalūn*** | *Bimā kānū **yaksibūn*** |

**Three differences must be recorded in particular.**

**First and most decisive: the mouth.**

**In Fuṣṣilat the mouth is free — indeed its owners speak and protest to their skins.** **In Yāsīn the mouth is sealed and there is no protest.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two texts:** the sealing of the mouth is not the removal of the defence — it is the removal of **the telling.** The mouth is the organ with which a person **gives an account** of what he has done; the hand and the foot are the organs that **do** it. **Yāsīn silences the one that tells and makes the one that does speak.**

**Second: two different verbs.**

**Yāsīn says *tukallimu* (speak) of the hands and *tashhadu* (bear witness) of the feet.** The two verbs could have been the same; they are not.

**My own reading:** the hand is the organ that **works**, the foot the organ that **carries**. The hand can tell what was done — hence "it speaks". The foot bears witness only to where one was — hence "it bears witness". **I give this distinction as a reading; its ground is that two separate verbs have been chosen.**

**Third: the addressee.**

*Tukallimu-**nā*** — the hands speak **"to Us"**. **Not to their owner.**

**This is a verifiable datum of word order and must be recorded:** the limb does not join its owner's defence; **it gives its statement to another addressee.** **In Fuṣṣilat there was a conversation between the skins and their owners** (*qālū li-julūdihim*); **in Yāsīn there is no such conversation.**

### A third list: al-Nūr 24/24

**The same scene is recounted in a third place in the Qurʾān, with a third list:**

| Place | Witnesses |
|---|---|
| Fuṣṣilat 41/20 | **Ear · eye · skin** |
| **Yāsīn 36/65** | **Hand · foot** (the mouth sealed) |
| al-Nūr 24/24 | ***Tongue* · hand · foot** |

**All three lists are verifiable textual data.** **And in al-Nūr the tongue is among the witnesses** — that is, the area sealed in Yāsīn is there on the side that speaks.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the three lists:** the lists change, and **the one thing that does not change is that the witness comes from the person's own body.** The variability of the lists shows that the verses are set down not to give an inventory of organs but **to describe a situation**: **the evidence is not brought from outside.**

**And this agrees with the twelfth verse of the sūra:** there it had been said *wa naktubu mā qaddamū wa āthārahum* — a record was being kept. **Here the bearer of the record is the body.** **I give the link between the two verses as an observation.**

### What this says to the present

**The distinction the verse sets up — the organ that tells and the organ that does — is a distinction with a counterpart in everyday life.**

The information a person gives about himself and the information his conduct gives often do not agree; and the most reliable datum about someone is usually not what he says but **what he does**.

**The verse does not say this as a piece of moral advice; it builds it as a scene.** **I give this connection as my own reading and do not force it:** the work of the verse is to recount a scene of the resurrection; the counterpart here follows from the logic of the scene itself.

## 36/66-67 — وَلَوْ نَشَآءُ لَطَمَسْنَا عَلَىٰٓ أَعْيُنِهِمْ فَٱسْتَبَقُوا۟ ٱلصِّرَٰطَ فَأَنَّىٰ يُبْصِرُونَ · وَلَوْ نَشَآءُ لَمَسَخْنَٰهُمْ عَلَىٰ مَكَانَتِهِمْ فَمَا ٱسْتَطَٰعُوا۟ مُضِيًّا وَلَا يَرْجِعُونَ

*Wa law nashāʾu la-ṭamasnā ʿalā aʿyunihim fa'stabaqu'ṣ-ṣirāṭa fa-annā yubṣirūn · Wa law nashāʾu la-masakhnāhum ʿalā makānatihim fa-ma'staṭāʿū muḍiyyan wa lā yarjiʿūn*

"**Had We willed, We would have wiped out their eyes**; they would race along the path, but how would they see? **Had We willed, We would have transformed them where they stand**; they could go neither forward nor back."

### Two occurrences of *law* — two conditions that were not fulfilled

**A point of word order, and it establishes a very important caveat within the sūra.**

**Both verses begin with *law*.** **In Arabic *law* differs from *in*:** *in* is for a condition whose fulfilment is possible; ***law* is for a condition unfulfilled, impossible, or not carried out** (*ḥarf imtināʿ li-imtināʿ*). **The lexicographers record this distinction.**

**Both verses, that is, recount something that was not done:** the eyes were not wiped out, the forms were not changed.

### A comparison with verses eight and nine — the limit the sūra sets for itself

**And now comes one of the most important contrasts within the sūra. The table below can be verified entirely from the text:**

| | **36/8-9** | **36/66-67** |
|---|---|---|
| **Verb form** | *Jaʿalnā* — **perfect**, done | *Law nashāʾu la-ṭamasnā* — **not done** |
| **What is done** | Ring, barrier, covering | The wiping out of the eyes, the changing of the form |
| **Result** | *Fa-hum lā **yubṣirūn*** | *Fa-annā **yubṣirūn*** |
| **Status** | **Realised** | **Not realised** |

**The same root is used in both places: ب-ص-ر — sight.** This is a verifiable repetition.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference of verb form between the two places:**

**Verses eight and nine were describing a state.** Verses sixty-six and sixty-seven say that **the physical counterpart of that state was not carried out**: the organ of sight was not annulled, the capacity to move was not taken away — **and these are named separately and with the qualification "had We willed".**

**The sūra, that is, sets a limit internal to the text on how its own first description is to be understood.** The ring in the eighth verse is not an iron ring; the barrier in the ninth is not a stone wall. **We know this because the sūra recounts the real physical annulment separately and as something that did not happen.**

**And this reading agrees with the forty-fifth verse too:** there they were being asked **to guard against** those same two directions (*mā bayna aydīkum wa mā khalfakum*). **For the direction said to be closed a command is still being given.** A command presupposes that it can be carried out.

**Reading the three places together is the ground of this reading**, and all three are countable textual data: **8-9 (the description), 45 (the command), 66-67 (what was not done).**

### طَمَسْنَا — root ط-م-س

**The concrete sense of the root: to wipe out the trace of a thing, to level its surface and make it indistinct.** *Ṭamasa'l-athar* — he wiped out the track; *ṭamasa'l-kitāb* — he blotted out the writing, made it illegible.

**The *ṭams* of an eye:** the **levelling** of the place of the eye, so that no socket and no shape remain.

**The root occurs elsewhere in the Qurʾān too:** *min qabli an naṭmisa wujūhan* (al-Nisāʾ 4/47), *rabbana'ṭmis ʿalā amwālihim* (Yūnus 10/88).

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** not to blind, but **to wipe out.** The organ is not damaged — **its place is not left.**

### فَٱسْتَبَقُوا۟ ٱلصِّرَٰطَ — the third صراط

**س-ب-ق** — the root: to get ahead, to race. **Form VIII (*istibāq*): to race, to try to arrive before one another.**

**And this is the sūra's third and last occurrence of صراط:**

| Verse | Expression | The state of the path |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | *ʿalā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm* | **It is stood upon** |
| **61** | *hādhā ṣirāṭun mustaqīm* | **It is named** |
| **66** | *fa'stabaqu'ṣ-ṣirāṭ* | **It is raced along — but without being seen** |

**All three occurrences can be verified.** **My own reading:** in the third occurrence the path is standing where it was and is being raced along. **What is missing is not in the path but in the one looking.** And the question at the end of the sentence says this: *fa-annā yubṣirūn* — "how would they see?"

**Word order:** *annā* — a particle asking manner and place; here it is **a question conveying denial**: no answer is expected, an impossibility is announced.

### لَمَسَخْنَٰهُمْ — root م-س-خ

**The sense of the root: to take a thing out of its own form and put it into another, to deform it.**

**And the second branch of the root must be recorded:** *masīkh* — **food that is insipid, that has lost its savour.** The lexicographers make the connection thus: a thing's losing the quality proper to it. **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers record it.**

**عَلَىٰ مَكَانَتِهِمْ** — root **م-ك-ن**: place, position, capacity. *Makāna* — the place and the condition one is in. **The change, that is, was to have happened on the spot — with no time even to flee, to go, to prepare.**

**مُضِيًّا** — root **م-ض-ي**: to pass on, to advance, to keep going. A verbal noun.

**And the sentence closes two directions at once:** *fa-ma'staṭāʿū **muḍiyyan** wa lā **yarjiʿūn*** — **neither forward nor back.**

**This is the same as the structure in verses forty-nine and fifty, and it can be verified:**

| Verse | The two directions closed |
|---|---|
| **50** | *Tawṣiya* (a word forward) · *ilā ahlihim yarjiʿūn* (a return backward) |
| **67** | *Muḍiyy* (going forward) · *yarjiʿūn* (going back) |

**In both verses the same second verb: *yarjiʿūn*.** **My own reading:** the sūra closes the same pair in two separate scenes — once for those seized all at once, once as a supposition. **And in both, what closes is "afterwards".**

**The fourth occurrence of the root ر-ج-ع in the sūra**, and the closing of block VI. **A table of the five occurrences was given in the section on structure.**

## 36/68 — وَمَن نُّعَمِّرْهُ نُنَكِّسْهُ فِى ٱلْخَلْقِ أَفَلَا يَعْقِلُونَ

*Wa man nuʿammirhu nunakkis-hu fi'l-khalq, a-fa-lā yaʿqilūn*

"**Whomever We give long life, We reverse in his make.** Will they not then reason?"

### نُّعَمِّرْ — root ع-م-ر

**The concrete sense of the root: to keep a place inhabited and full.** *ʿAmara'l-makāna* — he lived there, he made it flourish; *ʿimāra* — building up; *maʿmūr* — made to flourish, full.

**And *ʿumr* (lifetime) comes from here: the span in which the body is full and in working order.** **The root has occurred in `052-tur.md` (*al-bayti'l-maʿmūr*).**

**The choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say "whomever We keep alive", it says **"whomever We give *taʿmīr*"** — **Form II**, that is, the **lengthening** of a life. And within the root there is "keeping full". **What is lengthened is a span that is kept full.**

### نُنَكِّسْ — root ن-ك-س

**The concrete sense of the root: to turn upside down, to reverse a direction.**

- **نَكَسَ الشَّيْءَ** — he turned a thing over, he upended it.
- **نُكِسَ فِى مَرَضِهِ** — the illness **came back**: on the way to recovery it **turned round again.** (In Turkish the borrowed verb *nüksetmek*, "to relapse", comes from this root.)
- **مَنْكُوس** — turned upside down.
- **نَاكِسُو رُءُوسِهِمْ** — those who hang their heads (al-Sajda 32/12).

**The picture beneath the word, then, is the reversal of the direction of a movement** — not something that stops, but something that **comes back.**

**It is transmitted in the sources on the variant readings that the word is read in the forms *nunakkis-hu* (Form II) and *nankus-hu* (Form I).** **I give no names;** the difference in meaning is not fundamental — Form II announces the intensity of the act.

**فِى ٱلْخَلْق** — "in his make." **What is reversed, that is, is the body and the body's strength**, not position or standing.

### A comparison with Ghāfir 40/67 — the ascent and the descent

**In `040-gafir.md` at 40/67 the stages of a human life were treated**, and a table was given there: **dust → a drop → a clot (*ʿalaqa*) → an infant → the age of full strength (*ashudd*) → old age (*shuyūkh*)**, all joined with *thumma* — **that is, there is time between the stages.** And the second thing recorded there was this: the verse builds an orderly series and **adds the exception itself** (*wa minkum man yutawaffā min qabl*).

**Yāsīn gives the same fact in an entirely different way, and the difference can be verified:**

| | **Ghāfir 40/67** | **Yāsīn 36/68** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it gives** | **A series of stages** — six phases counted | **A direction** — a single sentence |
| **Connective** | *Thumma* (five times) — with intervals | **None** — a conditional sentence |
| **Where it ends** | *Shuyūkh* — old age, the end of the series | *Nunakkis-hu* — **the reversal of the series** |
| **The work of the sentence** | **Description** | **Proof** — it ends with *a-fa-lā yaʿqilūn* |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the structure of the two verses:** Ghāfir counts the ascent, Yāsīn names the descent. **Together the two draw an arc.** And Yāsīn uses this not as a piece of information but as a **proof** — the sentence ends with a question about reason.

**The structure of the proof is this, and it must be recorded:** the verse does not only say that a person **loses** his strength; it says that the **direction** of the strength **is turned round** — and the agent of the verb is "We" (*nunakkis-hu*). **Both the increase and the decrease, that is, are in the same hand.**

**My own reading:** one who sees the direction turn round in his own body sees that the strength was not his own. For a thing that is owned does not go backwards without its owner's leave. **The sūra builds this proof from the most concrete place of all: the arm of the one addressed.**

### أَفَلَا يَعْقِلُون

**The second of the sūra's two questions about reason (the other at 62), and one of the four rhetorical questions.** **It was tabulated in the section on structure.**

**ع-ق-ل** — the concrete sense of the root is **to bind**: *ʿiqāl*, the rope that binds a camel's knee; *ʿaqala'l-baʿīra* — he tethered the camel. From here *ʿaql*: **the bond that holds a person back from acting without thought.**

**Worth recording:** the root carries **the same picture** as *ḥakīm* in the second verse of the sūra (ح-ك-م — rein, bit). **Both words mean "what binds, what holds on course", and both occur in this sūra.** **I record this as an observation; I make no point of it.**

## 36/69-70 — وَمَا عَلَّمْنَٰهُ ٱلشِّعْرَ وَمَا يَنۢبَغِى لَهُۥٓ إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا ذِكْرٌ وَقُرْءَانٌ مُّبِينٌ · لِّيُنذِرَ مَن كَانَ حَيًّا وَيَحِقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَى ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ

*Wa mā ʿallamnāhu'sh-shiʿra wa mā yanbaghī lah · In huwa illā dhikrun wa qurʾānun mubīn · Li-yundhira man kāna ḥayyan wa yaḥiqqa'l-qawlu ʿala'l-kāfirīn*

"**We did not teach him poetry; nor would it befit him.** It is only a reminder and a plain Qurʾān — **that it may warn whoever is alive** and that the word may come true against the deniers."

### ٱلشِّعْر — root ش-ع-ر

**This root was analysed in detail in `069-hakka.md` at 69/41** (*wa mā huwa bi-qawli shāʿir*), **and Yāsīn 36/69 was already named there.** The findings there were these:

- **The original sense of the root is not to write poetry:** *shaʿara* — **to feel, to perceive**, to arrive at a fine awareness. *Shāʿir* — **one who feels, who perceives.**
- **In the Arabia of the Jāhiliyya the poet** was not an entertainer who spoke ornate words: **he was the spokesman of the tribe, its memory, an instrument of war.** And in the common understanding it was accepted that a poet had **a jinn helper**.
- **The charge of being a poet said two things:** that the word comes out of the man himself (intuition/inspiration), and that behind him stands an unseen being.
- **And the main finding established there:** *"the distinguishing property of poetry is that it makes no claim to be true… to count a word as poetry is to lift the obligation of faith towards it."*

**I do not repeat these; I rely on that treatment.**

**What belongs here is the difference between the two verses, and it can be verified:**

| | **al-Ḥāqqa 69/41** | **Yāsīn 36/69** |
|---|---|---|
| **What is rejected** | ***The word***: *wa mā huwa bi-qawli shāʿir* | ***The equipment***: *wa mā **ʿallamnāhu**'sh-shiʿr* |
| **The subject of the sentence** | **The Qurʾān** — "it is not the word of a poet" | **God** — "**We did not teach him**" |
| **Where the objection strikes** | The **kind** of the text | The **person's** capacity |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two verbs:** al-Ḥāqqa rejects the genre of the text; Yāsīn says that the person received no such training. **The two cut the charge at two different places:** one says "this is not poetry", the other "there is no poetry in him."

**And the verb *ʿallamnā* must be recorded:** the verse does not say "he does not know poetry"; it says **"We did not teach him poetry"**. **The agent of the verb of teaching is "We"** — that is, the verse already ties the source of everything the Prophet knows to one place and leaves poetry outside that list.

### وَمَا يَنۢبَغِى لَهُۥ — a repetition inside the sūra

**This verb occurs for the second time in the sūra.** **Its first occurrence was in the fortieth verse:** *la'sh-shamsu **yanbaghī** lahā an tudrika'l-qamar.*

| Verse | Expression | Whose limit |
|---|---|---|
| **40** | *la'sh-shamsu **yanbaghī** lahā* | **The limit of the order of the sky** — the sun cannot enter the moon's field |
| **69** | *wa mā **yanbaghī** lah* | **The limit of the word** — poetry cannot enter this field |

**Both occurrences can be verified.** **I record this as my own reading:** the sūra uses the same verb twice, for two different orders. **In the fortieth verse the fields of the heavenly bodies do not mix; in the sixty-ninth the fields of the kinds of speech do not mix.** What is said in both sentences is the same: **everything has a field of its own.**

**And a disagreement about the pronoun must be recorded:**

| Reading | To whom the pronoun *lah* refers | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To the Prophet** | "Poetry does not befit him / does not come from him" |
| 2 | **To the Qurʾān** | "Poetry does not befit the Qurʾān" |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **An observation:** the pronoun in the first half of the sentence (*ʿallamnā-hu*) refers to the Prophet; **this appears to support the first reading.** It is an observation, not a preference.

### إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا ذِكْرٌ وَقُرْءَانٌ مُّبِين — two names

**Two names are given to the text, and the two are of separate kinds:**

| Name | Root | What it announces |
|---|---|---|
| ***Dhikr*** | ذ-ك-ر | **Its function** — reminding |
| ***Qurʾān*** | ق-ر-أ | **Its form** — what is recited |

**And the word *dhikr* occurs for the second time in the sūra:** in the eleventh verse *mani'ttabaʿa'**dh-dhikra*** had been said. **Both occurrences can be verified:** the first described the one who follows the text, the second names the text.

**مُّبِين** — the fifth of the sūra's six occurrences of *mubīn*. **And note: the adjective is given to *qurʾān*.**

**ب-ي-ن** — *mubīn*, the active participle of Form IV of this root, can be used in Arabic both **intransitively** (that which is itself plain) and **transitively** (that which makes something else plain). **The lexicographers record this doubleness.** **Both senses are possible here, and I do not impose a preference.**

### لِّيُنذِرَ مَن كَانَ حَيًّا — the addressee narrows for the third time

**There is a progression that must be recorded, and it is countable:**

| Verse | The addressee of the warning | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *qawman mā undhira ābāʾuhum* | **A people** — a measure of condition |
| **11** | *mani'ttabaʿa'dh-dhikra wa khashiya'r-Raḥmāna bi'l-ghayb* | **One who follows and fears** — conduct and inward state |
| **70** | *man kāna **ḥayyan*** | **One who is alive** — a measure of a single word |

**All three occurrences can be verified.** **And in all three the measure lies not in the warning but in the hearer.**

**حَيًّا** — "alive". **Two readings are transmitted for the sense of the word here:**

| Reading | Who the *ḥayy* is | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One whose heart is alive** — who understands, who responds | The use of death and life in this sense in the Qurʾān |
| 2 | **One who is still living** — whose time has not yet passed | The direct sense of the word |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **One datum internal to the text can be recorded:** the eleventh verse of the sūra had described the person on whom the warning works **by an inward state** (*khashiya*). **This agrees with the first reading.** **It is an observation, not a preference.**

### وَيَحِقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَى ٱلْكَٰفِرِين — the ring closes

**And one of the clearest rings of the sūra is completed here.**

**The seventh verse had said:** *laqad **ḥaqqa'l-qawlu** ʿalā aktharihim fa-hum lā yuʾminūn.*
**The seventieth verse says:** *wa **yaḥiqqa'l-qawlu** ʿala'l-kāfirīn.*

| Verse | Expression | Form | About whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7** | *laqad **ḥaqqa**'l-qawl* | **Perfect** — realised | *Aktharihim* — **most of them** |
| **70** | *wa **yaḥiqqa**'l-qawl* | **Imperfect** — coming true | *Al-Kāfirīn* — **those who persist in denial** |

**Both occurrences can be verified, and they carry two differences: form and addressee.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is these two differences:** in the seventh verse the word is named as **having come about**, in the seventieth as **coming about**. And the addressee turns from a numerical majority (*aktharihim*) into **an attribute** (*al-kāfirīn*). **The verdict, that is, is tied not to a group but to a description** — and as is set down in `STYLE.md`, **what the verse describes are attributes; whoever bears the attribute falls under it.**

**And a point of word order:** the seventieth verse joins two purposes with a *wāw* — *li-yundhira* (that it may warn) **and** *yaḥiqqa'l-qawl* (that the word may come true). **Two different outcomes of the same text, side by side in a single sentence.**

## 36/71-73 — أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ أَنَّا خَلَقْنَا لَهُم مِّمَّا عَمِلَتْ أَيْدِينَآ أَنْعَٰمًا فَهُمْ لَهَا مَٰلِكُونَ · وَذَلَّلْنَٰهَا لَهُمْ فَمِنْهَا رَكُوبُهُمْ وَمِنْهَا يَأْكُلُونَ · وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا مَنَٰفِعُ وَمَشَارِبُ أَفَلَا يَشْكُرُونَ

*Awalam yaraw annā khalaqnā lahum mimmā ʿamilat aydīnā anʿāman fa-hum lahā mālikūn · Wa dhallalnāhā lahum fa-minhā rakūbuhum wa minhā yaʾkulūn · Wa lahum fīhā manāfiʿu wa mashāribu, a-fa-lā yashkurūn*

"Have they not seen that of **what Our hands have made** We created for them livestock, and they own them? We have **made them submissive** to them: some of them they ride, and of some they eat. In them they have other benefits, and drinks. Will they not then give thanks?"

### مِّمَّا عَمِلَتْ أَيْدِينَا — a contrast within the sūra

**This construction has the same structure as the one in the thirty-fifth verse, and it runs in the opposite direction. The table was given above in the discussion of 36/35; I do not repeat it from here.** Its summary was this:

| Verse | Expression | Whose hand |
|---|---|---|
| **35** | *wa mā **ʿamilat-hu aydīhim*** | **Their** hand |
| **71** | *mimmā **ʿamilat aydīnā*** | **"Our"** hand |

**This is a verifiable textual datum.**

**And the caveat of transcendence must be repeated here:** expressions such as *aydīnā* cannot be understood **as ascribing a limb or a body** to God. **Two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries:** to accept the wording as it stands and leave its manner to God (*tafwīḍ*), or to interpret it in the sense of power and of direct making (*taʾwīl*). **The same caveat was entered in `040-gafir.md` at 40/7 in the discussion of the Throne.** **I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the work of the construction must be recorded — this is a separate matter from the question of transcendence:** the expression announces that the making was done **without an intermediary**. In the thirty-fifth verse the share of human labour was in dispute; here, with the same pattern, it is said that the animals are not of human making.

### أَنْعَٰم — root ن-ع-م

***Anʿām*** — the plural of *naʿam*: **domestic animals** of the camel, cattle, sheep and goat kind. And from the same root comes **نِعْمَة (*niʿma*)**.

**The lexicographers record the connection:** the core of the root *naʿam* is **softness and ease** (*nāʿim* — soft); a favour is what makes life soft. **And because the Arabs counted these animals the most valuable of property, they named them directly with this word.**

**The word used in the verse, then, comes from the same root as "favour"** — and the verse ends with *a-fa-lā yashkurūn*. **I record this as an observation.**

### فَهُمْ لَهَا مَٰلِكُون — and its link with the last verse of the sūra

**م-ل-ك** — the root: to have power of disposal over a thing. *Mulk* — ownership and sovereignty; *malik* — a sovereign.

**And the link inside the sūra can be verified and is striking:**

| Verse | Word | Whose | Its extent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **71** | *fa-hum lahā **mālikūn*** | **Human beings'** | **Animals** — limited |
| **83** | *bi-yadihī **malakūtu** kulli shayʾ* | — | **Everything** — unlimited |

**The same root twice in the last block of the sūra.** **I record this as my own reading:** the seventy-first verse **grants** man an ownership — it does not deny it, it says *fa-hum lahā mālikūn*. But it states in the same sentence how that ownership came about: *khalaqnā **lahum***. **And the last verse of the sūra gathers the same root, in its unlimited extent, into a single hand.**

**The sūra, that is, does not deny man's ownership; it places it inside a higher ownership.**

### وَذَلَّلْنَٰهَا — root ذ-ل-ل

**The root has two branches, and the lexicographers keep them apart:**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ذُلّ (*dhull*)** | **Abasement, humiliation** — negative |
| **ذِلّ / ذَلُول (*dhill* / *dhalūl*)** | **Docility, submissiveness** — of an animal; it carries no negative sense |

**And the concrete use of *dhalūl* must be recorded:** *dābbatun dhalūl* — **an animal accustomed to being ridden, that does not shy, that is easily handled.** And *ṭarīqun dhalūl* — a much-walked, easy road.

**Form II, *tadhlīl*, is therefore the domestication of an animal: its coming out of the wild state and becoming able to work with a human being.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the second branch of the word:** the verse does not say that the animal is humiliated but that it is **made docile**. And this is a fact open to the eye: a camel's or an ox's working with a human being cannot be explained by the animal's strength — **the strength is in the animal; the submission has been given separately.** The proof is built from here.

### رَكُوبُهُمْ — a note on the pattern

***Rakūb*** — **the *faʿūl* pattern.** In Arabic this pattern usually has the sense of an **active participle** (*ṣabūr* — one who endures much), but in some words it takes the sense of a **passive participle**. **The lexicographers place *rakūb* in this second group:** *ar-rakūba* — **the animal that is ridden**, not the one that rides.

**A few more words are counted in the same pattern** (*ḥalūb* — the animal that is milked, and the like). **I transmit this note as the lexicographers give it.**

**And a point of word order:** *fa-**minhā** rakūbuhum wa **minhā** yaʾkulūn* — *min* partitive, twice over: **"some of them they ride, of some they eat."** Not all the animals, that is, are for both tasks; **the verse makes the distinction itself.**

### مَنَٰفِعُ وَمَشَارِبُ

***Manāfiʿ*** — the plural of *manfaʿa*; root **ن-ف-ع**. **And the extent of the word is left open:** which benefits they are is not counted. **This is like the thing the sūra has already done three times** (36, 42, 55).

***Mashārib*** — root **ش-ر-ب**. **Two readings are transmitted:**

| Reading | What *mashārib* is |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The plural of *mashrab*: drinks** — milk and the like |
| 2 | **A plural noun of place: drinking places** |

**The context requires the first reading** (a drink obtained from the animals); **the lexicographers record both.**

**And the word order: four benefits are counted** — riding, eating, benefits, drinks. **The first two are definite, the extent of the last two is left open.**

### أَفَلَا يَشْكُرُون — the second occurrence in the sūra

**This question comes for the second and last time in the sūra** (the first at 35). **It was tabulated in the section on structure and in the discussion of 36/35.**

**And what the two occurrences share can be verified:** both come after **things eaten and drunk** — one plants, the other animals. **The sūra asks about thanksgiving twice, and only in these two places.**

## 36/74-76 — وَٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ ءَالِهَةً لَّعَلَّهُمْ يُنصَرُونَ · لَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ نَصْرَهُمْ وَهُمْ لَهُمْ جُندٌ مُّحْضَرُونَ · فَلَا يَحْزُنكَ قَوْلُهُمْ إِنَّا نَعْلَمُ مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ

*Wa'ttakhadhū min dūni'llāhi ālihatan laʿallahum yunṣarūn · Lā yastaṭīʿūna naṣrahum wa hum lahum jundun muḥḍarūn · Fa-lā yaḥzunka qawluhum, innā naʿlamu mā yusirrūna wa mā yuʿlinūn*

"They have taken gods besides God — **in the hope that they might be helped.** Yet they cannot help them; **and they are an army brought out ready for them.** So let their speech not grieve you. **We know what they hide and what they make public.**"

### لَّعَلَّهُمْ يُنصَرُون — the name of the reason

**A point of word order:** the ground for taking gods is given in the verse, and it is given with a word of **hope**: *laʿallahum yunṣarūn* — "in the hope that they might be helped."

**And the verb is passive:** *yunṣarūn*. **By whom they will be helped is not said** — because the one who is to help is the very thing they have taken up.

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle *laʿalla*:** the verse names the taking of gods not as a matter of belief but as an **expectation.** **What is sought is not a truth but a support.** And the next verse cuts off exactly this expectation: *lā yastaṭīʿūna naṣrahum.*

**And this should be compared with the sixty-first verse:** there *wa ani'budūnī* — service was called for **with a command**, and no promise was counted out in return. **Here service is built upon an expectation.** There is a difference of word order between the two sentences, and it can be verified.

### وَهُمْ لَهُمْ جُندٌ مُّحْضَرُونَ — disputed pronouns

**This sentence is one of the most disputed places in the sūra, because it is not clear to whom the pronouns *hum* and *lahum* refer.**

| Reading | Who *hum* is | Who *lahum* is | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The people** | **The gods** | "**They** are an army standing ready for those gods" — the one who awaited help has fallen into the position of the helper |
| 2 | **The gods** | **The people** | "**Those gods** are an army brought out ready for them" — but a useless one |
| 3 | Both, in the hereafter | — | They will be brought present together at the resurrection |

**All three are found in the classical commentaries. I do not impose a preference.**

**The reversal the first reading carries is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** on that reading, the people who expect help from their gods are in fact the party that **defends** its gods. **The direction of service, that is, has been turned round:** what was taken up in order to be protected has turned into a thing that must be protected.

**And two internal repetitions of the sūra come together in this one sentence:**

| Word | Its earlier occurrence | Here |
|---|---|---|
| ***Jund*** | **28** — *min jundin mina's-samāʾ* (the army not sent down from the sky) | **75** — *jundun muḥḍarūn* |
| ***Muḥḍarūn*** | **32, 53** — *jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn* | **75** — *jundun muḥḍarūn* |

**Both can be verified.** **My own reading:** *jund* occurs twice in the sūra, and in both the word announces **a crowd that is of no use** — one that is not thought worth sending down, one that cannot defend. **And *muḥḍarūn*, in all three of its occurrences, announces being brought without one's own will.**

### فَلَا يَحْزُنكَ قَوْلُهُمْ — a return to the Prophet

**A point of word order, and it matters for the structure of the sūra:** the sūra had begun in the third verse with direct address to the Prophet (*innaka lamina'l-mursalīn*). **In the seventy-sixth verse it turns to him again** — and this is the closing of block VII.

**I record this as my own reading:** both sentences concern the same matter. In the third verse there was an addressee opposite who **rejects**, and the sentence was built with three devices of emphasis. In the seventy-sixth verse the same rejection is still going on — **but what is now given him is not a proof but a consolation.**

**ح-ز-ن** — the lexicographers dwell on the concrete sense of the root: ***ḥazn*** — **hard, stony, broken ground**; a place difficult to walk on. And *ḥuzn* is derived from here: **the heart's becoming rough, losing its evenness.**

**The root has occurred in `058-mucadele.md`.**

**The picture in the word is worth recording, and I give it as an observation:** grief is expressed in Arabic with a word for **ground** — the difference between walking on a level road and walking on stones. **And the answer the verse gives is not to forbid the grief but to show what it rests on:** *innā naʿlamu…*

### مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُون — a contrast

**Word order: two opposite verbs side by side. In Arabic this is called *ṭibāq*.**

| Verb | Root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Yusirrūn* | س-ر-ر | **To hide** — to keep within |
| *Yuʿlinūn* | ع-ل-ن | **To make public** — to bring out |

**س-ر-ر** — the core of the root is **secrecy**: *sirr* — a secret; *sarīra* — what is kept within. **And from the same root comes *surūr* (joy);** the lexicographers explain the connection by the picture of **"what is born inside the heart"**. **I transmit this relation as the lexicographers frame it.**

**ع-ل-ن** — the core of the root is **coming out into the open**: *ʿalana'l-amru* — the matter came out; *ʿalāniya* — openness.

**And the two verbs coming together announce a comprehension:** hidden and open — there is no third state in between. **The pattern is used often in the Qurʾān to express knowledge comprehensively.**

**My own reading:** the consolation is not given by saying that what is said is false. **It is given by saying that it is known how much of what he says the speaker is saying.** What the other side says openly, that is, is not all it has to say — and this agrees with the three closed sentences of the fifteenth verse: *mā antum illā… wa mā anzala… in antum illā…* **Closed sentences are most often sentences behind which something unsaid is standing.** This is an interpretation.

## 36/77-78 — أَوَلَمْ يَرَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ أَنَّا خَلَقْنَٰهُ مِن نُّطْفَةٍ فَإِذَا هُوَ خَصِيمٌ مُّبِينٌ · وَضَرَبَ لَنَا مَثَلًا وَنَسِىَ خَلْقَهُۥ قَالَ مَن يُحْىِ ٱلْعِظَٰمَ وَهِىَ رَمِيمٌ

*Awalam yara'l-insānu annā khalaqnāhu min nuṭfatin fa-idhā huwa khaṣīmun mubīn · Wa ḍaraba lanā mathalan wa nasiya khalqahū, qāla man yuḥyi'l-ʿiẓāma wa hiya ramīm*

"**Has man not seen that We created him from a drop?** And all at once there he is, a plain adversary in argument. **He has struck a parable for Us and forgotten his own creation.** He says: '**Who will bring the bones to life when they have crumbled?**'"

### ٱلْإِنسَٰن — the last widening of the addressee

**Here the sūra widens the addressee for the third and last time:** *qawm* → *banī Ādam* (60) → ***al-insān*** (77). **This progression was tabulated above.**

**And the word is a generic noun** — with the definite *al-*: **the human kind.** The sentence, that is, recounts not a person but a **condition**.

### فَإِذَا هُوَ خَصِيمٌ مُّبِينٌ — the distance that is skipped

***Idhā fujāʾiyya*** — **the sixth and last occurrence in the sūra.** A table of the six occurrences was given in the section on structure.

**And this occurrence stands over the widest interval of the six:**

| The beginning of the sentence | The end of the sentence |
|---|---|
| *Min **nuṭfatin*** — a drop | ***Khaṣīmun mubīn*** — a plain adversary |

**Not a single stage in between is counted.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the particle itself:** *fa-idhā* erases the whole distance in between. **And what is erased is exactly what the next verse calls "forgotten".** The word order of the sentence, that is, imitates the fault it diagnoses: **the road between the drop and the arguer does not appear in the verse either — because it does not appear in a person's mind either.**

**A comparison must be recorded:** in `040-gafir.md` at 40/67 the same road had been recounted **with six stages and five occurrences of *thumma*** — that is, the intervals were deliberately shown. **In Yāsīn there is a single *fāʾ*.** **The two verses give the same distance in two opposite ways:** one by opening it out, the other by closing it up.

### نُّطْفَة — a limit of method

**The lexical sense of the word: a small quantity of water, a drop.** *Nuṭfa* — among the lexicographers it is used also for "the little water left in a bucket or a vessel". **The root has occurred in `075-kiyame.md`, `076-insan.md`, `080-abese.md` and `053-necm.md`.**

**And the limit of method set in `040-gafir.md` at 40/67 holds here too, and I repeat it:** words such as *nuṭfa* **are not equated one-to-one with the terms of modern embryology.** **The work of the verse is not to give a description of stages** — indeed no stage is counted here at all. **The proof is that the beginning of a human being is not in his own hands.** It is the same line as the one treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/58-59.

### خَصِيم — a repetition inside the sūra

**The root خ-ص-م occurs for the second time in the sūra.** **In the forty-ninth verse *wa hum yakhiṣṣimūn* (while they wrangle) had been said.**

| Verse | Word | What |
|---|---|---|
| **49** | *yakhiṣṣimūn* — **a verb** | The **state** of wrangling — a moment |
| **77** | *khaṣīmun* — **an assimilate adjective** | The **attribute** of being an arguer — settled |

**Both occurrences can be verified.** **And the difference of pattern must be recorded:** in Arabic the *faʿīl* pattern announces a **settled and fixed** quality. **Man, that is, is named not as someone who is arguing but as an arguer.**

**مُّبِين** — the **last** of the sūra's six occurrences of *mubīn*. **And the first occurrence of the adjective, in the twelfth verse, was used of a record** (*imāmin mubīn*); the last is used of man himself. **The six occurrences of the adjective were tabulated in the section on structure.**

### وَضَرَبَ لَنَا مَثَلًا — the ring closes

**And one of the clearest rings of the sūra is completed here.**

**In the thirteenth verse the Prophet had been commanded:** *wa'**ḍrib** lahum **mathalan*** — "strike a parable for them."
**In the seventy-eighth it is said of man:** *wa **ḍaraba** lanā **mathalan*** — "he has struck a parable **for Us**."

| Verse | Verb | Who strikes it | For whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13** | *Iḍrib* — **imperative** | **The Prophet** | *Lahum* — for them |
| **78** | *Ḍaraba* — **perfect** | **Man** | ***Lanā*** — **for Us** |

**The same verb, the same object, the reverse direction.** **This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the identity of the two constructions:** in the thirteenth verse to strike a parable is a bridge built so that the other side may understand. In the seventy-eighth, to strike a parable becomes **setting out from what is in one's own hand and passing judgement about the whole.** **The same verb is used once to explain and once to set a limit.**

### وَنَسِىَ خَلْقَهُۥ — the closing proof of the sūra

**And now come the three words that carry the whole closing section of the sūra.**

**ن-س-ي** — the root has two branches, and the lexicographers record both:

| Branch | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **To forget** — knowledge going out of the mind |
| 2 | **To abandon** — to leave knowingly, to leave out of account |

**The second branch is used plainly in the Qurʾān** (*nasu'llāha fa-nasiyahum*, al-Tawba 9/67). **The root has occurred in `058-mucadele.md`, `045-casiye.md` and `059-hasr.md`.**

**Which branch in the verse? A disagreement:**

| Reading | What is happening |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The knowledge has been lost** — his own beginning is not remembered |
| 2 | **The knowledge has been abandoned** — it is known but not taken into account |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

### A comparison with al-Wāqiʿa 56/62 — the same proof, two namings

**The same proof was treated in `056-vakia.md` at 56/62:** *wa laqad **ʿalimtum**u'n-nashʾata'l-ūlā fa-lawlā tadhakkarūn* — "you have **known** the first bringing forth; will you not then reflect?" **The finding there was this: the proof rests not on a lack of knowledge but on a knowledge already present** — *ʿalimtum*, "you know." **I rely on that treatment.**

**And the two sūras name the same gap with two different words. A comparison table:**

| | **al-Wāqiʿa 56/62** | **Yāsīn 36/78** |
|---|---|---|
| **Verb** | *ʿAlimtum* — **"you have known"** | *Nasiya* — **"he has forgotten"** |
| **Person** | **2nd plural** — to the one addressed | **3rd singular** — about man |
| **What is said** | **The knowledge is there** | **The knowledge is not being used** |
| **The call** | *Fa-lawlā **tadhakkarūn*** — remember | **No call** — a diagnosis |
| **The name of the proof** | *Al-Nashʾatu'l-ūlā* — **the first bringing forth** | *Khalqahū* — **his own creation** |

**The two verses look at the same datum and do two different pieces of work.** **al-Wāqiʿa calls to remembrance, Yāsīn diagnoses the forgetting.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the difference between the two verbs:** in neither sūra does the proof bring new information. **What it brings is the taking into account of something already known.** And this determines the force of the proof: no acceptance is asked of the other side — **he is asked to draw the conclusion from what he already accepts.**

**And the link within Yāsīn can be verified:** in the twenty-second verse the man who came running had said *alladhī **faṭaranī*** — "the one who brought me into being." **In the seventy-eighth verse man is spoken of as *wa nasiya khalqahū* — "he has forgotten his own creation."**

**The same datum, in two different persons.** **My own reading:** the man in the sūra's story is the person who remembers what the man in the closing section forgets. **Both are looking from the same place; one takes his own beginning as proof, the other forgets it and takes his own end as proof.**

### مَن يُحْىِ ٱلْعِظَٰمَ وَهِىَ رَمِيمٌ

**ٱلْعِظَٰم** — the plural of *ʿaẓm*, root **ع-ظ-م**. **The root was treated in `069-hakka.md` and in `056-vakia.md` at 56/96:** the lexicographers record the relation between *ʿaẓm* (bone) and *ʿaẓīm* (great) — **the bone is the hardest and load-bearing part of the body.** **I do not repeat it here.**

**What belongs here: the material of the objection.** The objector chooses the **most durable** part of the body. **My own reading:** if the hardest part rots away, the rest has already ceased to be. **The objection is built in its strongest form** — and the answer will be given at exactly this point.

**رَمِيم** — root **ر-م-م**. **The root has two branches, and the lexicographers record both:**

| Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **رِمَّة (*rimma*)** | **A rotted bone**, a crumbled remnant |
| **رَمَّ / مَرَمَّة (*ramma* / *maramma*)** | **To repair, to mend** |

**The same root announces both decay and repair.** **The lexicographers record this doubleness; I make no point of it, I note the existence of the root's two branches.** **And this can be verified: the objection uses the first branch of the root, and the answer recounts the work of the second.**

**A note on word order:** *wa hiya **ramīm*** — the pronoun is feminine (*hiya*, for the bones) while the adjective is masculine in form (*ramīm*, not *ramīma*). **The lexicographers attribute this to the *faʿīl* pattern's having the sense of a passive participle** (like *marmūm*); **in this pattern the masculine-feminine distinction falls away.** It is a settled usage.

### On the occasion of revelation

**It is transmitted in the classical commentaries that a man brought a rotted bone and crumbled it in his hand as he put this objection.**

**In this commentary:** **the reports transmitted about the man's name differ from one another; I give no name.** I content myself with recording that the incident is transmitted.

**And the understanding of the verse does not depend on that report** — the verse says *al-insān*, it gives no personal name.

## 36/79 — قُلْ يُحْيِيهَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنشَأَهَآ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ خَلْقٍ عَلِيمٌ

*Qul yuḥyīha'lladhī anshaʾahā awwala marratin, wa huwa bi-kulli khalqin ʿalīm*

"Say: '**The One who brought them into being the first time will give them life.** He knows every act of creation.'"

### قُلْ — the way the answer is given

**A point of word order, and it must be recorded:** the answer is not given directly. **The Prophet is commanded: *qul* — "say".**

**It is not he who produces the answer; he is the one who conveys it.** **And this agrees with the seventeenth verse of the sūra:** there the messengers had said *wa mā ʿalaynā illa'l-balāghu'l-mubīn*. **The same limit is kept for the Prophet too at the end of the sūra.** I record this link as an observation.

### The structure of the answer: an answer with the verb of the question

**The question was:** *man **yuḥyi**'l-ʿiẓāma* — "**who will give life** to the bones?"
**The answer is:** ***yuḥyīhā***'lladhī anshaʾahā — "the One who … **will give them life**."

**The answer takes the verb of the question exactly as it stands and fills in only its subject.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verb the two sentences share:** the question had been built as a claim of impossibility (*man* — "who could do it?"), but **in its wording it is a question about an agent.** The answer follows the wording: it states the agent. **The answer, that is, does not argue with the objection — it answers its question.**

**And the agent of the answer is given not with a name but with a relative clause:** *alladhī **anshaʾahā awwala marratin*** — "the One who brought them into being the first time."

### أَنشَأَهَا — root ن-ش-أ

**The root was analysed in detail in `056-vakia.md`**, and, as was recorded there, in al-Wāqiʿa the root occurred **four times**: the bringing into being of the spouses in the garden (35), the bringing of man into being again (61), the first bringing forth (62), and the bringing forth of the tree of the fire (72). **I do not repeat it here.**

**The core of the root: to begin a thing from nothing and raise it, grow it, little by little.** *Nashaʾa* — he grew, he was brought up; *nāshiʾ* — a growing youth. **The word announces, that is, not a single act but a beginning and a developing.**

**And the choice of word is worth recording:** the verse does not say *khalaqahā* (created them), it says ***anshaʾahā*** (brought them into being). **My own reading:** the objection rested on rotting, that is, on a **dissolution**. The answer chooses the word that announces the act of building. **Against what dissolves is set the name of the one who builds.**

### أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ — the form of the proof

**And now it is necessary to record what the proof does not say.**

**The verse does not say "repeating is easier".** Such a sentence is found elsewhere in the Qurʾān (at al-Rūm 30/27 there occurs *wa huwa ahwanu ʿalayh*), **but it is not here.**

**The verse says only this: the same agent.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the wording of the sentence:** the proof is not a **comparison of power** but an **identification.** The objector says "this thing cannot be done"; the answer says "this thing has already been done once, and here is the name of the one who did it." **The question of ease is never opened at all** — because the moment it is opened, the party measuring the work would be the human being.

### وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ خَلْقٍ عَلِيمٌ

**Two readings are transmitted for what the word خَلْق is here:**

| Reading | What *khalq* is | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A verbal noun: creating** | "He knows every act of creating" |
| 2 | **In the sense of a passive participle: what is created** | "He knows every created thing" |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.**

**ٱلْعَلِيم** — *ʿalīm* occurs three times in the sūra, and the table was given in the discussion of 36/38: **38 (*al-ʿAzīzu'l-ʿAlīm*), 79 (*bi-kulli khalqin ʿalīm*), 81 (*al-Khallāqu'l-ʿAlīm*).** **Two of the three occurrences are in the closing section, and both are in the discussion of creation.**

**And the work of the sentence must be recorded:** the objection contained the assumption that a thing scattered and mixed **cannot be told apart**. **The sentence answers that assumption:** knowing does not disappear with scattering.

## 36/80 — ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلشَّجَرِ ٱلْأَخْضَرِ نَارًا فَإِذَآ أَنتُم مِّنْهُ تُوقِدُونَ

*Alladhī jaʿala lakum mina'sh-shajari'l-akhḍari nāran fa-idhā antum minhu tūqidūn*

"**He who made fire for you out of the green tree** — and all at once there you are, kindling from it."

### Where the sentence attaches

**A point of word order, and it determines the work of the verse:** the sentence begins with *alladhī* and **attaches to the *alladhī anshaʾahā* of the preceding verse.**

**This, then, is not a separate proof — it is a second attribute of the one who gives life.**

| Verse | Relative clause |
|---|---|
| **79** | *alladhī **anshaʾahā** awwala marratin* — who brought them into being the first time |
| **80** | *alladhī **jaʿala** lakum mina'sh-shajari'l-akhḍari nāran* — who made fire out of the green tree |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the attachment of the *alladhī*:** the proof from fire is given here not as an independent proof but as **an attribute of the one who gives life.** **And this differs from the use in al-Wāqiʿa.**

### A comparison with al-Wāqiʿa 56/71-72

**In `056-vakia.md` at 56/71-74 the tree of the fire was treated.** The findings there were these: the question comes in the form *a-antum anshaʾtum **shajaratahā***, that is, **the distance of the question's stepping back** is being measured — man strikes the fire, the branch gives the fire, the tree gives the branch; and **in the desert fire was struck by rubbing two dry branches together.** A table was also given there: of the four trees in the sūra, the fourth **is in this world and belongs to everyone.** **I do not repeat it here; I rely on that treatment.**

**Yāsīn recounts the same fact but parts from it at three points, and all three can be verified:**

| | **al-Wāqiʿa 56/71-72** | **Yāsīn 36/80** |
|---|---|---|
| **The form of the sentence** | **A question**: *a-antum anshaʾtum shajaratahā* | **A relative clause**: *alladhī jaʿala lakum* |
| **The attribute of the tree** | **None** — *shajaratahā* | ***Al-Akhḍar*** — **green** |
| **The proof it attaches to** | **Independent** — the third in a series of proofs from provision | **Attached to the proof of giving life** — the continuation of *alladhī anshaʾahā* |
| **Where it leads** | *Tadhkiratan wa matāʿan li'l-muqwīn* — a reminder and a benefit | **Directly to the resurrection** — to verse 81 |

**And the most important difference is the second row: the adding of the attribute.**

### ٱلشَّجَرِ ٱلْأَخْضَر — the attribute carries the proof

**خ-ض-ر** — the root: **greenness, freshness.** *Khaḍir* — fresh growth; *khuḍra* — greenstuff; *akhḍar* — green.

**And green here is not a piece of information about colour but about a state: it announces that the tree is moist and alive.**

**This is the structure of the proof, and it must be recorded:**

| What comes out | Its source |
|---|---|
| **Fire** — dry, burning | **A green tree** — moist, alive |

**The opposite, that is, comes out of its opposite.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that the attribute has been added:** what al-Wāqiʿa recounted was the **source** of the fire; what Yāsīn recounts is that the fire's source is **its opposite.** And this is the exact counterpart of the objection: the objection said *"the living does not come out of what has rotted"*; the answer says *"the dry comes out of the moist."* **In both cases what comes out does not resemble what it comes out of** — and the one addressed does one of these every day.

**And the background is concrete:** in the desert fire was struck by rubbing two branches together, and there were trees known to be suitable for the work. **The lexicographers name these trees.** **The verse, that is, looks at a technique the one addressed has in his own hands** — as was recorded in the discussion of al-Wāqiʿa.

### A caveat of method — per the method (`STYLE.md`)

**This verse is one of the places often used for claims of scientific miracle:** correlations are made with the storing of energy in plants, the chemistry of combustion and the like.

**This commentary does not take that road.** The grounds:

1. **The proof of the verse is not a piece of chemical knowledge.** The proof is **that the coming of the opposite out of its opposite is visible to the eye** — and this is something the one addressed knows.
2. **The verse does not recount a mechanism;** it recounts a fact and its agent.
3. **Such a correlation does not strengthen the proof of the verse;** because the force of the proof comes from its resting on something **in the hand of the one addressed**.

**Modern correlations are one point of view and are kept within that caveat.**

### فَإِذَآ أَنتُم مِّنْهُ تُوقِدُونَ

***Idhā fujāʾiyya*** — and this is **the last of the six occurrences** in the sūra.

**And a point of word order must be recorded:** five of the six occurrences were in the **third person** (*fa-idhā **hum***, *fa-idhā **huwa***). **This last is in the second person: *fa-idhā antum*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the change of pronoun:** what has been recounted throughout the sūra as "they" becomes **"you"** in the last proof. **And this runs in the same direction as the *turjaʿūn* of the sūra's last verse:** the closing section takes the one addressed inside the sentence.

**و-ق-د** — the root: a fire's catching and burning. *Waqūd* — fuel; *īqād* (Form IV) — to kindle. **The verb is ascribed to man:** *tūqidūn* — **"you kindle."**

**Worth recording, and the same line was kept in `056-vakia.md`:** the verse **grants** man his share — it is you who strike the fire. Then it steps back one pace: **but it is not you who put the fire in the tree.**

## 36/81-83 — أَوَلَيْسَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ بِقَٰدِرٍ عَلَىٰٓ أَن يَخْلُقَ مِثْلَهُم بَلَىٰ وَهُوَ ٱلْخَلَّٰقُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · إِنَّمَآ أَمْرُهُۥٓ إِذَآ أَرَادَ شَيْـًٔا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ · فَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِى بِيَدِهِۦ مَلَكُوتُ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ

*Awa-laysa'lladhī khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa bi-qādirin ʿalā an yakhluqa mithlahum? Balā, wa huwa'l-Khallāqu'l-ʿAlīm · Innamā amruhū idhā arāda shayʾan an yaqūla lahū kun fa-yakūn · Fa-subḥāna'lladhī bi-yadihī malakūtu kulli shayʾin wa ilayhi turjaʿūn*

"**Is the One who created the heavens and the earth not able to create the like of them?** Surely — He is the great Creator, the Knowing. **When He wills a thing, His command to it is only to say 'Be'; and it is.** **Glory be to the One in whose hand is the dominion of everything; and to Him you will be returned.**"

### The last step of the proof

**The three proofs of the closing section build a stair, and this is a verifiable sequence:**

| Verse | Proof | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **79** | *Alladhī anshaʾahā awwala marratin* | **His own body** — man's first creation |
| **80** | *Mina'sh-shajari'l-akhḍari nāran* | **The technique in his hand** — fire from the moist |
| **81** | *Khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ* | **The heavens and the earth** — the largest scale |

**The proof, that is, begins with the human being, rises to the tree, and from there to the heavens.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the objection had been built on the smallest scale — a handful of rotted bone. The answer enlarges the scale in three steps. **And at every step something the objector accepts is used:** his own birth, the fire he himself kindles, the sky he sees.

**The Qurʾān sets up this comparison elsewhere too** — it was treated in `040-gafir.md` at 40/57: *la-khalqu's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍi akbaru min khalqi'n-nās*. **I rely on that treatment.**

### بَلَىٰ — a point about a particle

**The question is negative:** *awa-laysa … bi-qādirin* — "is He **not** able?"

**And the answer is *balā*. In Arabic this is the particle used specifically in answer to a negative question, and the lexicographers record its difference from *naʿam* plainly:**

| Particle | As an answer to a negative question | Upshot |
|---|---|---|
| ***Naʿam*** | **Confirms the negative** | "Yes, He is not able" |
| ***Balā*** | **Breaks the negative** | "**No, He is able**" |

***Balā*, that is, is the particle that annuls the negation.** **This is a grammatical datum and is needed for the verse to be understood rightly.**

**And it is worth recording:** the answer is not given by the Prophet; the interrogative sentence itself gives it. **The question is asked and answered in the same verse** — that is, the question is not a request for information but a means of **drawing attention** (*istifhām taqrīrī*).

### مِثْلَهُم — a disagreement

| Reading | What *mithl* is | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Themselves** | In Arabic, in usages such as *mithluka lā yafʿalu hādhā*, *mithl* announces the person himself |
| 2 | **Others like them** | The direct sense of the word: others of the same kind |

**Both are transmitted; I do not impose a preference.** **An observation:** the first reading overlaps more directly with the subject of the objection (the bringing to life of those same bones). **It is an observation, not a preference.**

### ٱلْخَلَّٰق — the intensive form

***Khallāq*** — **the *faʿʿāl* pattern**, which in Arabic announces **multiplicity and continuity.** Not *khāliq* (creator) but *khallāq* (**one who creates much and continually**).

**The choice of pattern is worth recording:** the objection was questioning whether a single act could be done. **The answer names the doer of the act not with the attribute of an agent who acts once but with that of one who acts continually.**

### إِنَّمَآ أَمْرُهُۥ … كُن فَيَكُونُ

**This construction occurs in many places in the Qurʾān and was treated in `002-bakara.md` at 2/117.** **It was also handled in `040-gafir.md` at 40/68**, and the note there was this: **the expression is not a description of speech but a way of announcing that no intermediary comes in between.** **I do not repeat it here; I rely on both places.**

**What belongs here is the construction of the sentence, and there is a verifiable difference between the two sūras:**

| Place | Sentence | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Ghāfir 40/68 | *fa-**idhā qaḍā amran** fa-innamā **yaqūlu** lahū kun fa-yakūn* | ***Amr* the object, *yaqūlu* the verb** — the **course** of the act |
| **Yāsīn 36/82** | *innamā **amruhū** idhā arāda shayʾan **an yaqūla** lahū kun fa-yakūn* | ***Amruhū* the subject, *an yaqūla* the predicate** — the **definition** of the act |

**In Ghāfir the sentence recounts an event: when He decrees a matter, He says thus.** **In Yāsīn the sentence gives a definition: His command is only to say…**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the parsing of the two sentences:** with *innamā* (the particle of restriction) Yāsīn says that the act **is not otherwise**. Ghāfir recounts a sequence. **The two verses use the same construction in two different sentence types.**

**And a note on the variant readings:** it is transmitted that in some places the word *fa-yakūnu* is read in the accusative (*fa-yakūna*). **I do not go into the details or into whether it applies to this verse;** the difference in meaning is not fundamental.

**And the choice of verb is worth recording:** in Ghāfir *qaḍā* (He decreed), in Yāsīn ***arāda*** (He willed). **The second announces an earlier stage:** not a decree but a **will.** And the sentence passes directly from will to coming-to-be.

### فَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِى — the second glorification

**In the thirty-sixth verse *subḥāna'lladhī khalaqa'l-azwāja kullahā* had been said. The table was given in the discussion of 36/36.** **Both occurrences are in the same pattern, and both contain the word *kull*.**

**And the *fāʾ* here must be recorded:** *fa-subḥāna* — **a *fāʾ* announcing a consequence.** The glorification, that is, comes as the **result** of the three preceding verses. **In the thirty-sixth verse there was no *fāʾ*.**

### مَلَكُوت — a point about a suffix

**Root م-ل-ك.** And the lexicographers dwell on the **ـُوت** suffix at the end of the word: in Arabic this suffix announces **magnification and extension.** Words such as *raḥamūt* (the wide reach of mercy), *jabarūt* and *rahabūt* are in this pattern.

***Malakūt*, that is, is the widest and most comprehensive form of *mulk*.**

**And the link inside the sūra can be verified:** in the seventy-first verse *mālikūn* was said of human beings — **a limited ownership.** Here *malakūtu **kulli shayʾ*** — **unlimited.** **The table was given in the discussion of 36/71.**

### بِيَدِهِۦ — a caveat of transcendence

**The caveat kept consistently across the index holds here too:** this expression cannot be understood **as ascribing a limb, a body or a place** to God. **Two attitudes are transmitted in the classical commentaries:** to accept the wording as it stands and leave its manner to God (*tafwīḍ*), or to interpret it in the sense of **power and disposal** (*taʾwīl*). **The same caveat was entered in `040-gafir.md` at 40/7.** **I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

**And the work of the construction must be recorded:** *bi-yadihī* is brought forward (before *malakūt*). **In Arabic the bringing forward of the predicate announces restriction:** in His hand alone.

### وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ — the last two words of the sūra

**And the sūra ends with the last two words of the twenty-second verse.**

| Verse | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **22** | *Wa mā liya lā aʿbudu **alladhī faṭaranī** — **wa ilayhi turjaʿūn*** |
| **83** | *Fa-subḥāna **alladhī bi-yadihī malakūtu kulli shayʾin** — **wa ilayhi turjaʿūn*** |

**The overlap between the two verses is not only in the last two words but in the whole pattern of the sentence, and this can be verified:**

| Element | 36/22 | 36/83 |
|---|---|---|
| **Connector** | *alladhī* | *alladhī* |
| **What follows** | *faṭaranī* — a verb | *bi-yadihī malakūt* — an attribute |
| **Closing** | ***wa ilayhi turjaʿūn*** | ***wa ilayhi turjaʿūn*** |
| **Shift of person** | 1st singular → **2nd plural** | 3rd singular → **2nd plural** |

**There is *iltifāt* in both sentences, and both go in the same direction: towards the one addressed.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is this exact repetition:** in the story of the sūra an unnamed man who came running from the farthest part of the city had built a sentence. **Sixty-one verses later the sūra closes with the same sentence.**

**What the man said, that is, becomes the last word of the sūra.** All the proofs in between — dead earth, night, sun, moon, ship, drop, bone, green tree — turn out to be arrayed as the ground of that sentence.

**And a detail of word order:** the verb is in the **second person** — *turjaʿūn*, "you will be returned." **The preceding clause was in the third person** (*alladhī bi-yadihī*). **The *iltifāt* takes the reader inside the sentence:** the sūra ends not with "they will be returned" but with **"you will be returned"**.

### The method of the sūra — a closing observation

**What follows is a gathering up of the data recorded across the sūra, and it is an interpretation; I give it as my own reading.**

**Yāsīn does not handle the resurrection as a subject of debate.** There is not a single abstract proof built in favour of the resurrection in the sūra. Instead, three things are done:

1. **A person is shown** (20-27) — his argument testable, his language free of accusation, his end visible.
2. **A turn is made to things that are seen** (33-44) — and all three are named *wa āyatun lahum*: **not a proof, a sign.**
3. **A turn is made to a person's own body** (68, 77-79) — the direction of a life, the drop, the bone.

**And the most repeated structure of the sūra confirms this method:** six times *fa-idhā* (and all at once), five times *al-yawma* (today), twice a demonstrative (*hādhā*, *hādhihī*). **The sūra does not prove something; it shows something.**

**And that the sense which closes in the ninth verse is sight** (*fa-hum lā yubṣirūn*) **agrees with this.** The sūra advances by calling upon the sense it says is closed. **And in the sixty-sixth verse it says, within its own text, that that closing is not a physical annulment.**

**Finally:** the last sentence of the sūra is the sentence of the man in the story. **This can be verified from the text.** And what I add as an interpretation is this: **the sūra gives its longest section to a person who is not named, and makes its closing with his words too.** What remains is not who the speaker was but what he said.

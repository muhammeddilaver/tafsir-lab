# 40. Sūrat Ghāfir (al-Muʾmin)

**Eighty-five verses.** It is widely reported that it was revealed in the Meccan period. It is known by two names: **Ghāfir**, from the construct **غَافِرِ ٱلذَّنۢبِ** (the forgiver of sin) in the third verse, and **al-Muʾmin**, from the **believing** man described in verse 28.

It is the first of the seven sūras that open with *Ḥā-mīm*.

## The structure of the sūra

| Block | Verses | Subject | What closes the block |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1-9 | Five attributes, those who dispute, the prayer of the bearers of the Throne | *Wa dhālika huwa'l-fawzu'l-ʿaẓīm* (9) |
| **II** | 10-20 | The day of reckoning, and what is not missed | *Inna'llāha huwa's-samīʿu'l-baṣīr* (20) |
| **III** | 21-46 | Moses (Mūsā), Pharaoh, and **the man who concealed his faith** | *Adkhilū āla Firʿawna ashadda'l-ʿadhāb* (46) |
| **IV** | 47-56 | The dispute in the Fire | *Innahū huwa's-samīʿu'l-baṣīr* (56) |
| **V** | 57-85 | Proofs, prayer, and the close | *Wa khasira hunālika'l-kāfirūn* (85) |

### The backbone of the sūra: ج-د-ل

**One root keeps returning across the sūra: ج-د-ل — to dispute, to wrangle.**

| Verse | Expression |
|---|---|
| 4 | *Mā yujādilu fī āyāti'llāhi illa'lladhīna kafarū* |
| 5 | *Wa jādalū bi'l-bāṭili li-yudḥiḍū bihi'l-ḥaqq* |
| 35 | *Alladhīna yujādilūna fī āyāti'llāhi bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum* |
| 56 | *Inna'lladhīna yujādilūna fī āyāti'llāhi bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum* |
| 69 | *Alam tara ila'lladhīna yujādilūna fī āyāti'llāh* |

**Five occurrences, distributed across the five blocks of the sūra.** And the expression in verses 35 and 56 is **word for word the same.** This is a repetition that can be verified from the text.

The concrete sense of the root **ج-د-ل** — **to twist a rope tight**, to throw an opponent in wrestling — was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/197. **I do not repeat it here.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra takes up denial not as an opinion but as **a posture of dispute**. And in the thirty-fifth verse the measure of it is given: *bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum* — **without any proof having come into their hands.** What is condemned, then, is not the disputing itself but its being **carried on with nothing to stand on.**

## 40/1-3 — حمٓ · تَنزِيلُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْعَلِيمِ · غَافِرِ ٱلذَّنۢبِ وَقَابِلِ ٱلتَّوْبِ شَدِيدِ ٱلْعِقَابِ ذِى ٱلطَّوْلِ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ إِلَيْهِ ٱلْمَصِيرُ

*Ḥā-mīm · Tanzīlu'l-kitābi mina'llāhi'l-ʿazīzi'l-ʿalīm · Ghāfiri'dh-dhanbi wa qābili't-tawbi shadīdi'l-ʿiqābi dhi'ṭ-ṭawl · Lā ilāha illā hū · Ilayhi'l-maṣīr*

"Ḥā-mīm. The sending down of this Book is from God, the Mighty, the Knowing: **the forgiver of sin, the accepter of repentance, severe in punishment, the possessor of bounty.** There is no god but He; to Him is the journey's end."

### The five attributes and their arrangement

**Five attributes are set out one after another in the third verse, and the order is worth recording:**

| Order | Attribute | What it states |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ghāfiri'dh-dhanb* | **Forgiveness** |
| 2 | *Qābili't-tawb* | **Acceptance of the return** |
| 3 | *Shadīdi'l-ʿiqāb* | **The severity of the punishment** |
| 4 | *Dhi'ṭ-ṭawl* | **Bounty, breadth of means** |
| 5 | *Lā ilāha illā hū* | **Oneness** |

**The third attribute stands after two attributes of mercy and before an attribute of bounty** — that is, in the middle. **This is a placement that can be verified.**

**I record this as my own reading:** this is a sūra that will take dispute as its subject, and in its very first verses it leaves both sides open. **Neither forgiveness alone nor punishment alone is named;** the two are given in a single breath, and the last of them is an attribute that states breadth.

**غَافِر and قَابِل** — **both are active participles**, that is, they state not an act but an **attribute**. Not a one-off deed but a continuing state.

**ٱلتَّوْب** — the verbal-noun form of *tawba*. Root **ت-و-ب**: to turn, to come back. **What is accepted is named not a feeling of remorse but a *return*.**

### ذِى ٱلطَّوْل

**Root ط-و-ل: length.** The root has occurred in `073-muzzemmil.md` and `057-hadid.md`.

**The lexicographers dwell on what the word amounts to in this construct:** *ṭawl* means **breadth of means, the power to do a thing, the surplus one has in hand.** The lexicographers explain it by the picture of "having a long arm" — close to the English idiom "open-handed".

**I record this as an observation:** the attribute states not the thing given but **the breadth of the capacity to give.** And it has been placed immediately after the attribute "severe in punishment".

## 40/4-6 — مَا يُجَٰدِلُ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ فَلَا يَغْرُرْكَ تَقَلُّبُهُمْ فِى ٱلْبِلَٰدِ

"None dispute about the signs of God but those who deny. **Do not let their moving about in the cities deceive you.**"

**تَقَلُّبُهُمْ فِى ٱلْبِلَٰدِ** — *taqallub*: root **ق-ل-ب** (to turn, to be turned about); **Form V: to keep turning from one place to another.** It describes going from city to city for trade, and affairs running smoothly.

**What the sentence does is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the verse closes off an error of measure. **That the other side's affairs are going well cannot be counted as proof that their position is sound.** This is the logic treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/11 — "had it been good, they would not have reached it before us" — built from exactly the opposite end: **there prosperity was being counted as proof; here it is said to be no proof at all.**

**وَجَٰدَلُوا۟ بِٱلْبَٰطِلِ لِيُدْحِضُوا۟ بِهِ ٱلْحَقَّ** — "they disputed with falsehood, to make the truth **slip** by means of it."

**إِدْحَاض** — root **د-ح-ض**: **the slipping of the foot, losing one's hold and sliding away.** *Makānun daḥḍun* — slippery ground.

**The picture in the word is worth recording:** the aim is not to refute the truth — but to **make it slip.** That is, to shift it from its place, to turn the ground it stands on slippery. **The target of the dispute is described not as showing the true to be false, but as dislodging it from where it sits.**

## 40/7-9 — ٱلَّذِينَ يَحْمِلُونَ ٱلْعَرْشَ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهُۥ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّهِمْ وَيُؤْمِنُونَ بِهِۦ وَيَسْتَغْفِرُونَ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ رَبَّنَا وَسِعْتَ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ رَّحْمَةً وَعِلْمًا

"**Those who bear the Throne and those around it** glorify their Lord with praise, believe in Him, and **ask forgiveness for those who believe**: 'Our Lord! You have encompassed everything in mercy and knowledge…'"

### A note of tanzīh

**The note kept consistently across this series concerning the Throne and the expressions bound up with it holds here too:** these expressions cannot be understood in a way that ascribes place, body or being carried to God. **For verses of this kind two main positions are transmitted in the classical commentaries:** to take the wording as it stands and leave its modality to God (*tafwīḍ*), or to interpret it in the sense of sovereignty and disposal (*taʾwīl*). **I transmit both; I do not impose a preference.**

### The direction of the prayer

**And what really has to be recorded is for whom the prayer is made:** *wa yastaghfirūna li'lladhīna āmanū* — **for those who believe.**

**The text of the prayer is given in the verse, and two words within it stand out:** *wasiʿta kulla shayʾin **raḥmatan wa ʿilmā*** — "You have encompassed everything in mercy and knowledge."

**The order is worth recording: mercy before knowledge.** And this can be set beside the second verse of the sūra: there the one who sends down was named *al-ʿazīzu'l-**ʿalīm***. **In the prayer that same knowledge is placed after mercy.**

**رَبَّنَا وَأَدْخِلْهُمْ جَنَّٰتِ عَدْنٍ ٱلَّتِى وَعَدتَّهُمْ وَمَن صَلَحَ مِنْ ءَابَآئِهِمْ وَأَزْوَٰجِهِمْ وَذُرِّيَّٰتِهِمْ** — the prayer does not stop with the person: **fathers, spouses, children.**

**When the language of the descriptions of the Garden was treated in `056-vakia.md`, this verse (Ghāfir 40/8) was named as one of the places where spouses are mentioned together.** I rely on the note made there.

## 40/10-15 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ يُنَادَوْنَ لَمَقْتُ ٱللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ مِن مَّقْتِكُمْ أَنفُسَكُمْ إِذْ تُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلْإِيمَٰنِ فَتَكْفُرُونَ

"Those who deny will be called to: '**God's loathing is greater than the loathing you feel for yourselves** — you were being called to faith, and you kept denying.'"

### The loathing felt for oneself

**There is a comparison in the sentence, and the second term of the comparison is worth recording: *maqtikum anfusakum* — the loathing you feel for yourselves.**

**That is, people's revulsion at themselves on that day is taken as a given and used as a measure.**

**I record this as my own reading:** before it describes the punishment as something coming from outside, the verse names its counterpart **inside** the person. **And the measure of magnitude is taken from there.**

**قَالُوا۟ رَبَّنَآ أَمَتَّنَا ٱثْنَتَيْنِ وَأَحْيَيْتَنَا ٱثْنَتَيْنِ فَٱعْتَرَفْنَا بِذُنُوبِنَا فَهَلْ إِلَىٰ خُرُوجٍ مِّن سَبِيلٍ**

"'Our Lord! **You made us die twice and gave us life twice.** We confess our sins. **Is there any way out?**'"

**How the "two deaths and two lives" are counted is debated in the classical commentaries, and the main explanation is this:** the state of non-being counts as one "death", coming into the world is the first life, departing from the world is the second death, and rising in the hereafter is the second life. **I transmit this as an explanation that is transmitted; other reckonings are also mentioned, and I do not impose a preference.**

**فَهَلْ إِلَىٰ خُرُوجٍ مِّن سَبِيلٍ** — **what is asked for is not pardon but a *way out*.** I record this as an observation: they confess (*fa'ʿtarafnā*), but the thing requested is not forgiveness, it is **a change of place.**

**ٱلَّذِى يُرِيكُمْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ وَيُنَزِّلُ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ رِزْقًا وَمَا يَتَذَكَّرُ إِلَّا مَن يُنِيبُ** — "**only he who turns takes heed.**"

The root **أ-ن-ب**: to turn, to turn towards. **It was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/8 (*ʿabdin munīb*); I rely on that treatment.**

**The condition must be recorded:** the sign is shown to everyone (*yurīkum āyātih*), **but taking heed is made to depend on turning.** This is the same measure treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/44: the same text — a road for one, a weight for another.

**رَفِيعُ ٱلدَّرَجَٰتِ ذُو ٱلْعَرْشِ يُلْقِى ٱلرُّوحَ مِنْ أَمْرِهِۦ عَلَىٰ مَن يَشَآءُ مِنْ عِبَادِهِۦ لِيُنذِرَ يَوْمَ ٱلتَّلَاقِ** — *yawmu't-talāq*: **"the day of meeting".** **The sūra will use three names in succession for the resurrection:** *yawmu't-talāq* (15), *yawmu't-tanād* (32), *yawmu'l-āzifa* (18). **All three name that day by way of a *relation*: meeting, calling to one another, drawing near.**

## 40/16-17 — يَوْمَ هُم بَٰرِزُونَ لَا يَخْفَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ مِنْهُمْ شَىْءٌ لِّمَنِ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ لِلَّهِ ٱلْوَٰحِدِ ٱلْقَهَّارِ

"On that day they will be **out in the open**; nothing of theirs will be hidden from God. '**Whose is the dominion today?**' — 'God's, the One, the **Qahhār**.'"

### Who asks the question, who answers it

**There is a question in the verse and its answer comes immediately after — but neither side is identified.**

**The most widespread explanation in the classical commentaries is that both the question and the answer come from the same place:** since no one is left to answer, the question receives its own answer. **I transmit this as an explanation that is transmitted.**

**بَٰرِزُون** — root **ب-ر-ز**: to come out into the open, to emerge into view. *Barāz* — open ground, empty flat land. **The word carries the fact that no place is left to hide in.**

**ٱلْقَهَّار** — root **ق-ه-ر**: to overcome and make submit. **An intensive form (*faʿʿāl*).**

**The choice of attribute is worth recording:** in the answer to the question "whose is the dominion?" **two attributes** are given — *al-wāḥid* (the One) and *al-qahhār*. **That is, the question of whose the dominion is gets answered both by oneness and by overpowering.**

## 40/18-20 — وَأَنذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْءَازِفَةِ إِذِ ٱلْقُلُوبُ لَدَى ٱلْحَنَاجِرِ كَٰظِمِينَ · يَعْلَمُ خَآئِنَةَ ٱلْأَعْيُنِ وَمَا تُخْفِى ٱلصُّدُورُ

"Warn them of **the day that draws near**: **hearts pressed up to the throats**, choking them back… **God knows the treachery of the eyes and what the breasts conceal.**"

### ٱلْءَازِفَة

**Root أ-ز-ف: to draw near; and to become straitened.** **The root was analysed at `053-necm.md` 53/57 (*azifati'l-āzifa*); I rely on that treatment.**

**That the word is used as the name of a day is worth recording:** that day is named not by what happens on it but by **its nearness.**

### إِذِ ٱلْقُلُوبُ لَدَى ٱلْحَنَاجِرِ كَٰظِمِينَ

**حَنَاجِر** — singular *ḥunjura*: **the gullet.**

**The scene is bodily, and it can be set beside the scene treated at `056-vakia.md` 56/83:** there the soul was pressed up to the *ḥulqūm* (the throat). **Here the hearts are pressed up to the *ḥanjara*.**

| Place | What is pressed up | To where |
|---|---|---|
| al-Wāqiʿa 56/83 | **The soul** (not named as such) | *Al-Ḥulqūm* |
| **Ghāfir 40/18** | **The hearts** | *Al-Ḥanājir* |

**Both scenes point to the same region: the place where voice and breath pass.** One describes the moment of death, the other the waiting.

**كَٰظِمِين** — root **ك-ظ-م**: **to tie the mouth of a waterskin**, to let nothing inside it out. From this comes the sense "to bury one's anger or pain inside". **The word was treated in `049-hucurat.md`.**

**So the scene is this: the heart has come up, but no sound comes out.** A skin filling, and a mouth tied shut.

### خَآئِنَةَ ٱلْأَعْيُن

*"The treachery of the eyes."*

**The construct occurs only here in the Qurʾān, and it is striking.**

The root **خ-و-ن**: to act against a trust, to betray in secret. **And the lexicographers dwell on the word's coming in the form *khāʾina* (a feminine active participle):** on one reading it means "the treacherous glance", on another "the treachery of the eyes".

**What the word describes is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the eye is a person's **fastest and most deniable** organ. A glance passes and is over; its owner can easily disown it. **The verse takes as its measure precisely what can be denied.**

**And the second half of the sentence completes this:** *wa mā tukhfi'ṣ-ṣudūr* — what the breasts conceal. **That is, the one is the smallest trace that leaks out, the other what never leaks at all.** Both extremes are covered at once.

**The supposition treated at `041-fussilet.md` 41/22 is directly connected with this:** there it was said, "you supposed that God did not know much of what you were doing". **This verse brings the reach of that knowledge down to the level of a glance.**

## 40/21-22 — أَوَلَمْ يَسِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَيَنظُرُوا۟ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَانُوا۟ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ كَانُوا۟ هُمْ أَشَدَّ مِنْهُمْ قُوَّةً وَءَاثَارًا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ

"Have they not travelled the earth and seen how those before them ended? **They were mightier than these in strength and left more traces on the earth.**"

**ءَاثَارًا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ** — "traces on the earth". Root **أ-ث-ر**: a trace, a mark left behind. **It was treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/4 (*athārati min ʿilm* — a remnant of knowledge); I rely on that treatment.**

**The two uses can be compared:** there what was asked for was a **trace of knowledge**, here what is named is a **trace in stone.** And the verse says that those who left traces in stone were destroyed too.

**فَأَخَذَهُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِذُنُوبِهِمْ وَمَا كَانَ لَهُم مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ مِن وَاقٍ** — *wāq*: one who protects, one who stands as a shield. Root **و-ق-ي** — the same root as *taqwā*. **The absence of a protector is stated with the very word for protection.**

---

## 40/23-25 — وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا مُوسَىٰ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا وَسُلْطَٰنٍ مُّبِينٍ · إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ وَهَٰمَٰنَ وَقَٰرُونَ فَقَالُوا۟ سَٰحِرٌ كَذَّابٌ

"We did indeed send Moses with Our signs and **a clear proof**: to Pharaoh, to Hāmān and to Qārūn. They said: '**A sorcerer, a liar.**'"

### Three names, three separate powers

**The one addressed is not a single man but three. And it is recorded in the classical commentaries that this trio represents three separate powers:** rule, execution and wealth.

**I transmit this as an explanation that is transmitted;** the verse itself draws no such classification. **But the sequence of the sūra is amenable to this reading:** in the thirty-sixth verse Pharaoh will give the order for the tower to **Hāmān** — that is, he is the one who builds.

**وَسُلْطَٰنٍ مُّبِين** — the word *sulṭān* here has the sense of **proof**. **In verses 35 and 56 of the sūra it was said that those who dispute are *bi-ghayri sulṭānin* (without proof).** **The same word occurs on the side of the one sent with the attribute *mubīn* (clear).**

| Verse | Who | *Sulṭān* |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | **Moses** | *Sulṭānin **mubīn*** — a clear proof |
| 35, 56 | **Those who dispute** | *Bi-**ghayri** sulṭānin* — without proof |

**This is a contrast that can be verified within the sūra** and it shows how the narrative is fastened to the backbone of the sūra.

**فَلَمَّا جَآءَهُم بِٱلْحَقِّ مِنْ عِندِنَا قَالُوا۟ ٱقْتُلُوٓا۟ أَبْنَآءَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ مَعَهُۥ** — and the response is not a dispute but an **order**.

**I record this as an observation:** the verse shows what is done where no answer can be given in words. **And the outcome of the decision is given in the same sentence:** *wa mā kaydu'l-kāfirīna illā fī ḍalāl* — the trap they set comes to nothing.

## 40/26-27 — وَقَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ ذَرُونِىٓ أَقْتُلْ مُوسَىٰ وَلْيَدْعُ رَبَّهُۥٓ إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ أَن يُبَدِّلَ دِينَكُمْ أَوْ أَن يُظْهِرَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلْفَسَادَ

"Pharaoh said: '**Leave me to kill Moses; and let him call upon his Lord!** For I fear that he will change your religion, or that he will bring corruption out into the land.'"

### A justification in two sentences

**Pharaoh's speech is made of two parts, and both are worth recording.**

**The first: *dharūnī aqtul Mūsā* — "leave me, let me kill him."**

**The construction of the sentence is odd, and the lexicographers dwell on it:** the most powerful man in the country speaks **as though asking someone's leave.** **I record this as an observation and transmit both readings:** either there is an opinion around him that is holding him back, or this is for show — a sentence framed to display his resolve to those around him.

**The second: the justification.** *Innī akhāfu an yubaddila dīnakum aw an yuẓhira fi'l-arḍi'l-fasād* — **"I fear."**

**And the subject of the fear has two items: the changing of the order, and corruption.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the words themselves:** the decision to kill is presented not with a claim to power but **with a justification of protection.** **And the word in the justification is *fasād* — that is, the crime the other side is said to be about to commit is exactly the name of what is being committed here.** The sūra does not interpret this; **it transmits the sentence as it stands and leaves it there.**

## 40/28 — وَقَالَ رَجُلٌ مُّؤْمِنٌ مِّنْ ءَالِ فِرْعَوْنَ يَكْتُمُ إِيمَٰنَهُۥٓ أَتَقْتُلُونَ رَجُلًا أَن يَقُولَ رَبِّىَ ٱللَّهُ وَقَدْ جَآءَكُم بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ مِن رَّبِّكُمْ وَإِن يَكُ كَٰذِبًا فَعَلَيْهِ كَذِبُهُۥ وَإِن يَكُ صَادِقًا يُصِبْكُم بَعْضُ ٱلَّذِى يَعِدُكُمْ

*Wa qāla rajulun muʾminun min āli Firʿawna yaktumu īmānahū a-taqtulūna rajulan an yaqūla rabbiya'llāhu wa qad jāʾakum bi'l-bayyināti min rabbikum wa in yaku kādhiban fa-ʿalayhi kadhibuh wa in yaku ṣādiqan yuṣibkum baʿḍu'lladhī yaʿidukum*

"**A believing man from the family of Pharaoh, who was concealing his faith,** said: 'Will you kill a man for saying "My Lord is God", when he has come to you with clear proofs from your Lord? **If he is a liar, his lie is against himself; and if he is truthful, some part of what he threatens you with will befall you.**'"

### The man who gives the sūra its name

**His name is not given. His attributes are: a believer, from the family of Pharaoh, and *yaktumu īmānahū* — concealing his faith.**

The root **ك-ت-م** — **analysed here for the first time in this series.** Its meaning: **to hold a thing within and not let it out, to keep it hidden.** The lexicographers record the root's picture of "keeping something in a closed vessel".

**That the three attributes stand together is worth recording:** the man is at once **from within** the other side, **a believer**, and **concealing it.** And he speaks from exactly that position.

### How the dilemma is built

**The argument the man builds is one of the clearest examples of reasoning in the Qurʾān, and it covers both possibilities:**

| Possibility | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **If he is a liar** | *Fa-ʿalayhi kadhibuh* — the harm is his own |
| **If he is truthful** | *Yuṣibkum baʿḍu'lladhī yaʿidukum* — some part of the threat will touch you |

**The strength of the argument lies in its not debating which possibility is the true one.** The man does not claim that Moses is right — **he shows that on either possibility the killing is unnecessary.**

**And the word in the second branch is worth recording: *baʿḍu* — "some part".** Not the whole of the threat, a part of it. **I give this as my own reading:** the expression looks **deliberately softened** so as not to corner the other side. The argument is built on the lowest possible claim: **"even if only some part of it comes" is enough.**

**This comes from the same place as the form of the demand for proof treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/4:** there too what was asked for had been reduced to something as small as **a remnant of a book.** **To leave the other side the widest possible margin and still reach the conclusion.**

**And the man's speech does not end here:** it continues in verses 29-35 and 38-44. **The longest uninterrupted speech in the sūra belongs to this person, whose name is not even given.**

## 40/29-33 — يَٰقَوْمِ لَكُمُ ٱلْمُلْكُ ٱلْيَوْمَ ظَٰهِرِينَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَمَن يَنصُرُنَا مِنۢ بَأْسِ ٱللَّهِ إِن جَآءَنَا قَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ مَآ أُرِيكُمْ إِلَّا مَآ أَرَىٰ وَمَآ أَهْدِيكُمْ إِلَّا سَبِيلَ ٱلرَّشَادِ

"'O my people! **Today the dominion is yours, you have the upper hand in the land.** But who will help us against God's might if it comes upon us?' Pharaoh said: '**I show you only what I myself see, and I guide you only to the path of right direction.**'"

### Two sentences, two separate claims

**The believing man's sentence begins with an admission:** *lakumu'l-mulku'l-yawma ẓāhirīna fi'l-arḍ* — **"today the dominion is yours, you have the upper hand."** He does not object; he concedes it.

**Then he asks:** *fa-man yanṣurunā min baʾsi'llāh* — **"and after that?"**

And the pronoun is worth recording: *yanṣuru**nā*** — "who will help **us**." **He does not place himself outside.** The address *yā qawmi*, which he has kept up since the twenty-eighth verse, passes here into the pronoun as well.

### مَآ أُرِيكُمْ إِلَّا مَآ أَرَىٰ

**Pharaoh's answer is made of two claims:**

| Claim | What it says |
|---|---|
| *Mā urīkum illā mā arā* | **I show you nothing but what I see** |
| *Wa mā ahdīkum illā sabīla'r-rashād* | **I guide you only to the path of right direction** |

**The second claim must be recorded, because the same construct will occur nine verses later in the mouth of the believing man:** *ittabiʿūnī ahdikum **sabīla'r-rashād*** (38).

| Verse | Who says it | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| 29 | **Pharaoh** | *Wa mā ahdīkum illā sabīla'r-rashād* |
| 38 | **The believing man** | *Ittabiʿūnī ahdikum sabīla'r-rashād* |

**The same construct, in two mouths.** This is a repetition that can be verified within the sūra.

**I record this as my own reading:** both sides promise the same thing and use the same word. **The difference does not lie in the wording of the claim.** And the measure given in the thirty-fifth verse of the sūra works precisely here: *bi-ghayri sulṭānin* — **is there anything to stand on?** The only thing offered as a ground in Pharaoh's sentence is **his own view** (*mā arā*).

**وَيَٰقَوْمِ إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ عَلَيْكُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلتَّنَادِ** — *yawmu't-tanād*: **"the day of calling to one another"** — Form VI, reciprocity: calling out to each other.

**It should be read together with *yawmu't-talāq* (the day of meeting) in the fifteenth verse.** **And the fourth block of the sūra (47-56) confirms this name: there they really will call out to one another.**

**And the subject of the believing man's fear is worth recording:** *akhāfu **ʿalaykum*** — "I fear **for you**." **In the twenty-sixth verse Pharaoh too had been afraid:** *innī akhāfu an yubaddila dīnakum* — "I fear **that he will change your religion**." **The same verb, two different anxieties.**

## 40/34-35 — ٱلَّذِينَ يُجَٰدِلُونَ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ بِغَيْرِ سُلْطَٰنٍ أَتَىٰهُمْ كَبُرَ مَقْتًا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ وَعِندَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟

"Those who dispute about the signs of God **without any proof having come to them**… This is **a cause of great loathing** both with God and with those who believe."

**سُلْطَٰن** — root **س-ل-ط**: to prevail, to hold sway. **The word is used both in the sense of "authority" and in the sense of "proof"** — the lexicographers make the connection thus: **a proof is what secures the upper hand in a dispute.**

**The measure must be recorded, and it is the most important record in the sūra:** what is condemned is not disputing but **disputing with nothing in hand to stand on.** *Bi-ghayri sulṭānin atāhum* — and the verb *atāhum* (having come to them): **the ground has to have come from outside; it is not something they produced themselves.**

**مَقْت** — root **م-ق-ت**: **the most violent hatred, an anger that reaches disgust.** The word had also occurred in the tenth verse: *la-maqtu'llāhi akbaru min maqtikum anfusakum* — "God's loathing is greater than the loathing you feel for yourselves."

| Verse | Whose *maqt* |
|---|---|
| 10 | God's — and what people feel **for themselves** |
| 35 | God's **and the believers'** |

**The expression in the tenth verse is worth recording:** people's revulsion **at themselves** is named as a part of the reckoning.

**كَذَٰلِكَ يَطْبَعُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ قَلْبِ مُتَكَبِّرٍ جَبَّارٍ** — "Thus does God seal the heart of every arrogant tyrant."

**That the sealing is made conditional must be recorded:** the sentence does not say "every heart" — it says ***qalbi mutakabbirin jabbār***. **That is, the seal comes after two attributes.** The word *jabbār* was treated at `050-kaf.md` 50/45 and in `059-hasr.md`.

## 40/36-37 — وَقَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ يَٰهَٰمَٰنُ ٱبْنِ لِى صَرْحًا لَّعَلِّىٓ أَبْلُغُ ٱلْأَسْبَٰبَ · أَسْبَٰبَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ فَأَطَّلِعَ إِلَىٰٓ إِلَٰهِ مُوسَىٰ

"Pharaoh said: '**O Hāmān! Build me a lofty tower, that I may reach the ways — the ways of the heavens — and look upon the god of Moses.**'"

**صَرْح** — root **ص-ر-ح**: **to be open, unmixed** (*ṣarīḥ* — plain speech). **The word is used of a building that is tall and stands out in the open.** The root occurs here for the first time in this series.

**أَسْبَاب** — singular *sabab*: **a rope, a cable; the tie that lets one reach a place.** **The "rope" sense of the root was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/166** (*wa taqaṭṭaʿat bihimu'l-asbāb* — the snapping of the ties).

**The structure of the request is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** Pharaoh wants to test what Moses says **on his own plane** — by going up high. **That is, he does not reject the claim; he turns it into a problem of architecture.**

**And the verse gives the outcome of this in a single sentence:** *wa kadhālika zuyyina li-Firʿawna sūʾu ʿamalih* — **"thus was his evil deed made to seem fair to him."** The verb is passive; the one who made it seem fair is not named.

## 40/38-44 — وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِىٓ ءَامَنَ يَٰقَوْمِ ٱتَّبِعُونِ أَهْدِكُمْ سَبِيلَ ٱلرَّشَادِ · يَٰقَوْمِ إِنَّمَا هَٰذِهِ ٱلْحَيَوٰةُ ٱلدُّنْيَا مَتَٰعٌ وَإِنَّ ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ هِىَ دَارُ ٱلْقَرَارِ

"The man who believed said: '**O my people! Follow me, and I will guide you to the path of right direction.** O my people! This life of the world is only an enjoyment; **the hereafter is the abode of settlement.**'"

### The choice of address

**In this section the man begins three times by saying *yā qawmi* (O my people)** (38, 39, 41); the same address had occurred earlier as well. **That is, he does not shut the other side out; he speaks reckoning himself among them.**

**I record this as an observation:** in the twenty-eighth verse he was introduced as someone **concealing his faith.** **As his speech goes on the openness increases, but the address never changes.**

**دَارُ ٱلْقَرَار** — *qarār*: root **ق-ر-ر**, to settle and stay fixed. **The world is called *matāʿ* (a passing thing one makes use of), the hereafter *dāru'l-qarār* (the abode one settles in).** Both words are chosen on the axis of permanence.

**وَأُفَوِّضُ أَمْرِىٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ** — "I **commit** my affair to God."

The root **ف-و-ض**: to leave a matter wholly to another, to hand over authority. **The word has a legal resonance: to grant power of representation.**

**That the speech ends with this sentence is worth recording:** the man has built his argument, given his warning, and at the end **has let go of taking the outcome on himself.** And the next verse states the outcome: *fa-waqāhu'llāhu sayyiʾāti mā makarū* — **"God protected him from the evils of what they plotted."**

## 40/45-46 — فَوَقَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِ مَا مَكَرُوا۟ وَحَاقَ بِـَٔالِ فِرْعَوْنَ سُوٓءُ ٱلْعَذَابِ · ٱلنَّارُ يُعْرَضُونَ عَلَيْهَا غُدُوًّا وَعَشِيًّا

"God protected him from **the evils of what they plotted**; and the evil of the punishment closed in upon the family of Pharaoh: **morning and evening they are exposed to the Fire.**"

**فَوَقَىٰهُ** — root **و-ق-ي**: to protect, to shield. **In the twenty-first verse it was said *mā kāna lahum mina'llāhi min wāq* (they had no protector); here the same root is used of someone who is protected.**

**And what the protection was against is worth recording:** *sayyiʾāti mā makarū* — **the evils of what they plotted.** The plot was laid; **the verse does not say the plot was not laid, it says its outcome did not touch him.**

**وَحَاقَ** — root **ح-ي-ق**: a thing's turning back upon its author and closing in on him. **The lexicographers record that the word is used particularly in the context of "a plot enveloping the one who laid it".**

**غُدُوًّا وَعَشِيًّا** — morning and evening. **In the fifty-fifth verse the Prophet will be commanded to glorify *bi'l-ʿashiyyi wa'l-ibkār* (in the late afternoon and at daybreak).** **The same two times, in two separate scenes: one an exposure, the other a glorification.** This is a contrast that can be verified within the sūra.

**It is reported in the classical commentaries that inferences about the state of the *barzakh* have been drawn from this expression of the verse. I confine myself to transmitting the debate; no ruling is issued.**

## 40/47-50 — وَإِذْ يَتَحَآجُّونَ فِى ٱلنَّارِ فَيَقُولُ ٱلضُّعَفَٰٓؤُا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَكْبَرُوٓا۟ إِنَّا كُنَّا لَكُمْ تَبَعًا فَهَلْ أَنتُم مُّغْنُونَ عَنَّا نَصِيبًا مِّنَ ٱلنَّارِ

"When they **dispute with one another** in the Fire, the weak will say to those who were arrogant: '**We followed you; can you ward off from us any share of the Fire?**'"

### The dispute goes on there too

**The verb is *yataḥājjūn* — root ح-ج-ج, Form VI: to bring proofs against one another, to press arguments back and forth.** **The root was treated at `045-casiye.md` 45/25 (*mā kāna ḥujjatahum*).**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the backbone of the sūra:** *jidāl* has been condemned throughout the sūra. **What is being done in the Fire is once again disputing** — but this time the sides have changed: those who now face one another are people who had followed one another.

**And the dispute yields no result:** *qāla'lladhīna'stakbarū innā kullun fīhā* — "we are all of us in it." **That is, the answer does not resolve the objection; it merely states the situation.**

**There is a third stage as well (49-50):** those in the Fire now turn to the keepers of Hell and ask for **the punishment to be lightened.** **The answer again comes in the form of a question:** *a-wa-lam taku taʾtīkum rusulukum bi'l-bayyināt* — "did your messengers not come to you with clear proofs?"

**A chain in three stages is worth recording:**

| Who to whom | What is asked | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| The weak → those who were arrogant | To share the load | "We are all in it" |
| All of them → the keepers | A lightening | "Did no proof come?" |
| The keepers → all of them | — | *Fa'dʿū wa mā duʿāʾu'l-kāfirīna illā fī ḍalāl* |

## 40/51-55 — إِنَّا لَنَنصُرُ رُسُلَنَا وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ فِى ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَيَوْمَ يَقُومُ ٱلْأَشْهَٰدُ

"We shall surely help Our messengers and those who believe, **both in the life of this world** and **on the day the witnesses will stand.**"

**ٱلْأَشْهَٰد** — the plural of *shāhid*. **The matter of witnessing was treated in verse 19 of the sūra** (*yaʿlamu khāʾinata'l-aʿyun*); here the witnesses are named as the name of a day.

**That the help is said to be "in the life of this world" as well is worth recording** and it is confirmed by the sūra's own example: **in the forty-fifth verse it was stated that the man who concealed his faith was protected** (*fa-waqāhu'llāhu sayyiʾāti mā makarū*). **That is, the promise stands as having been fulfilled once inside the sūra.**

**فَٱصْبِرْ إِنَّ وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ حَقٌّ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لِذَنۢبِكَ وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِبْكَٰرِ** — three commands: **be patient, ask forgiveness, glorify.**

**بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِبْكَٰرِ** — in the late afternoon and at daybreak. **The root ب-ك-ر was treated at `076-insan.md` 76/25 and at `056-vakia.md` 56/36 (*abkār*).** **By naming the two extreme times, everything between them is covered.**

## 40/56 — إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يُجَٰدِلُونَ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ بِغَيْرِ سُلْطَٰنٍ أَتَىٰهُمْ إِن فِى صُدُورِهِمْ إِلَّا كِبْرٌ مَّا هُم بِبَٰلِغِيهِ

"In the breasts of those who dispute about the signs of God without any proof having come to them there is nothing but a craving for **a greatness they will never reach**."

### The sūra's diagnosis is completed here

**The first half of the verse is word for word the same as verse 35.** I tabulated this repetition in the section on structure.

**What is new is the second half:** *in fī ṣudūrihim illā kibrun mā hum bi-bālighīh.*

**The sentence says two things:**

| Expression | What it says |
|---|---|
| *In fī ṣudūrihim illā kibr* | **What lies beneath** the dispute is a craving for greatness |
| *Mā hum bi-bālighīh* | And **they will not reach it** |

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the sentence itself:** the verse names not the **visible subject** of the dispute but **the desire behind it.** And it adds that the desire goes **unanswered**: a greatness that cannot be reached.

**And this diagnosis coincides with the third block of the sūra:** Pharaoh's wish to have a tower built (36-37) was exactly an attempt **to climb to a height that cannot be reached.** **The same verb (*bulūgh* — to reach) had been used there too:** *laʿallī **ablughu**'l-asbāb.*

| Verse | Who | What he wants to reach | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 36 | Pharaoh | *Al-Asbāb* — the ways of the heavens | By means of a tower |
| 56 | Those who dispute | *Kibr* — greatness | *Mā hum bi-bālighīh* |

**This is a link that can be verified within the sūra** and it shows why the narrative is told in precisely this sūra.

**فَٱسْتَعِذْ بِٱللَّهِ** — and the command: take refuge. **The same command had occurred in the same form at `041-fussilet.md` 41/36** (*fa'staʿidh bi'llāh*) and was treated there.

## 40/57-59 — لَخَلْقُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ أَكْبَرُ مِنْ خَلْقِ ٱلنَّاسِ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

"**The creation of the heavens and the earth is surely greater than the creation of mankind**; but most people do not know."

**The verse sets up a comparison, and the work of the comparison is to furnish a proof of the resurrection:** can the one who made the greater not make the lesser?

**The same proof was built with the same structure at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/33** (*a-wa-lam yaraw anna'llāha'lladhī khalaqa's-samāwāti wa'l-arḍa wa lam yaʿya bi-khalqihinna bi-qādirin ʿalā an yuḥyiya'l-mawtā*). **I rely on that treatment.**

**The difference here must be recorded:** in al-Aḥqāf the sentence was in the form of a **question**, here it is a **statement.** And a note is added at the end: *wa lākinna akthara'n-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn* — **most do not know.**

**وَمَا يَسْتَوِى ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ وَٱلْبَصِيرُ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَلَا ٱلْمُسِىٓءُ** — "the blind and the seeing are not equal…"

**The same inequality was built with different words at `045-casiye.md` 45/21** (*am ḥasiba'lladhīna'jtaraḥu's-sayyiʾāti an najʿalahum ka'lladhīna āmanū*). **The two sūras make the same refusal by two different comparisons: one the equating of life and death, the other of seeing and not seeing.**

## 40/60 — وَقَالَ رَبُّكُمُ ٱدْعُونِىٓ أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِى سَيَدْخُلُونَ جَهَنَّمَ دَاخِرِينَ

"Your Lord has said: '**Call upon Me, and I shall answer you.** Those who are too proud to serve Me will enter Hell **with their necks bowed**.'"

### Prayer counted as service

**The transition between the two halves of the sentence must be recorded:** in the first half **prayer** is spoken of; in the second half the same thing is named **service**.

| Half | Word |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Udʿūnī* — **call upon Me** |
| 2 | *ʿAn ʿibādatī* — **from serving Me** |

**That is, the verse counts prayer as service itself.** This follows directly from the construction of the sentence.

**And the object of the pride is worth recording:** *yastakbirūna **ʿan ʿibādatī*** — to be too proud for service. **In verse 56 of the sūra the thing lying beneath the dispute was named *kibr*; here the same root occurs as the reason for not praying.**

**I record this as my own reading:** the sūra positions asking not as a weakness but as **the sign of not being proud.** To ask is to admit a need.

**دَاخِرِين** — root **د-خ-ر**: to be made small, to bow the neck, to be brought low. **The word is the exact opposite of *kibr*, and it has been set against it in the same sentence.**

**The same matter was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/186** (*fa-innī qarīb ujību daʿwata'd-dāʿi idhā daʿān*), and there the absence of the "say" formula was recorded. **The difference here: there it was the nearness of the answer, here it is the commanding of the prayer.**

## 40/61-65 — ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلَّيْلَ لِتَسْكُنُوا۟ فِيهِ وَٱلنَّهَارَ مُبْصِرًا … ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلْأَرْضَ قَرَارًا وَٱلسَّمَآءَ بِنَآءً وَصَوَّرَكُمْ فَأَحْسَنَ صُوَرَكُمْ

"It is God who made the night for you **to rest in**, and the day **giving light**… It is He who made the earth for you **a place to stay** and the sky **a building**, and who **gave you form and made your forms beautiful**."

**وَٱلنَّهَارَ مُبْصِرًا** — "He made the day *seeing*". **The lexicographers count this usage a figure:** it is not the day that sees, but the one who sees in the day. **Transfers of this kind (*isnād majāzī*) are common in Arabic and lend the word life.**

**قَرَارًا** and **بِنَآء** — the earth **a place to stay**, the sky **a building.** **Both words are architectural: one the floor, the other the structure.** The word *qarār* had also been used of the hereafter in verse 39 (*dāru'l-qarār*) — **the same root, for two abodes.**

**وَصَوَّرَكُمْ فَأَحْسَنَ صُوَرَكُمْ** — *taṣwīr*: to give form, to shape. **The like of it was treated in `082-infitar.md`** (*fī ayyi ṣūratin mā shāʾa rakkabak*).

**The order is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** night, day, earth, sky — all of them are outside. **And the list comes from the outside inward and ends *in the human being's own form*.** This is one instance of the pairing named at `041-fussilet.md` 41/53, *āfāq* and *anfus*.

## 40/66 — قُلْ إِنِّى نُهِيتُ أَنْ أَعْبُدَ ٱلَّذِينَ تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ لَمَّا جَآءَنِىَ ٱلْبَيِّنَٰتُ مِن رَّبِّى

"Say: '**When the clear proofs came to me from my Lord**, I was forbidden to serve those you call upon besides God.'"

**The sentence's note of time must be recorded: *lammā jāʾaniya'l-bayyināt* — "when the clear proofs came".**

That is, the refusal is described not as a rejection from the outset but as a position taken **after the proof had come.**

**This coincides exactly with the measure of the sūra:** in the thirty-fifth verse those who dispute were characterised as *bi-ghayri sulṭānin* (with nothing to stand on). **Here the side that is speaking states its own ground.**

**نُهِيتُ** — passive: **"I was forbidden".** The verb does not belong to him. **The same line as that treated at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/9** (*in attabiʿu illā mā yūḥā ilayy*): the source is not in himself.

## 40/67 — هُوَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُم مِّن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ مِن نُّطْفَةٍ ثُمَّ مِنْ عَلَقَةٍ ثُمَّ يُخْرِجُكُمْ طِفْلًا ثُمَّ لِتَبْلُغُوٓا۟ أَشُدَّكُمْ ثُمَّ لِتَكُونُوا۟ شُيُوخًا وَمِنكُم مَّن يُتَوَفَّىٰ مِن قَبْلُ

"It is He who created you from earth, then from a drop, then from an *ʿalaqa*. Then He brings you forth as an infant; then you reach **your full strength**, then you **become old** — and some of you are taken before that."

### The stages and *thumma*

**The verse counts six stages and joins them all with *thumma*** — the Arabic particle that states an interval. **That is, there is time between the stages.**

| Stage | Word |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Turāb* — earth |
| 2 | *Nuṭfa* — a drop |
| 3 | *ʿAlaqa* |
| 4 | *Ṭifl* — an infant |
| 5 | *Ashuddakum* — the age of full strength |
| 6 | *Shuyūkh* — old age |

**And the sequence is cut with a note:** *wa minkum man yutawaffā min qabl* — **"some of you are taken before that."**

**This note is worth recording, and I give it as my own reading:** the verse sets up an orderly sequence of stages and **adds the exception itself.** That is, the sequence is not offered as a rule; **that not everyone goes to the end of it is said in the same sentence.**

**A limit, per the method (`STYLE.md`):** the dictionary meanings of the words *nuṭfa* and *ʿalaqa* (a drop; a thing that hangs and clings, something leech-like) are given by the lexicographers. **I do not enter into equating these one-to-one with the terms of modern embryology;** the work of the verse is to give a description of stages, and the proof lies in these stages **not being in human hands** — the same line as that treated at `056-vakia.md` 56/58-59.

**The same stages of life had occurred in the context of parents at `046-ahkaf.md` 46/15** (*ḥattā idhā balagha ashuddahū wa balagha arbaʿīna sana*). **The two sūras use the same word (*ashudd*).**

## 40/68 — هُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُحْىِۦ وَيُمِيتُ فَإِذَا قَضَىٰٓ أَمْرًا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

"**It is He who gives life and gives death.** When He decrees a matter, He only says to it '**Be**', and it is."

**كُن فَيَكُون** — this construct occurs in many places in the Qurʾān and **was treated at `002-bakara.md` 2/117.** **I do not repeat it here;** the note made there was this: the expression is not a **description of speech** but a way of stating that **no intermediary comes in between.**

**What belongs here is the placing of the verse.** The preceding verse (67) had counted the six stages of the human being: earth, a drop, *ʿalaqa*, an infant, the age of full strength, old age — **and all of them were joined with *thumma*, that is, with intervals.**

**The sixty-eighth verse comes immediately after this and leaves no interval at all:** *fa-innamā yaqūlu lahū kun fa-yakūn* — **two verbs, and between them a *fāʾ*.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the connectives of the two verses:**

| Verse | Connective | What it states |
|---|---|---|
| 67 | *Thumma* (five times) | **With intervals** — the forming of the human being is spread over time |
| 68 | *Fāʾ* | **Without interval** — no distance between the decree and the coming to be |

**That is, immediately after describing the slow construction of the human being, the sūra says that the decree on which that construction depends is instantaneous.** Time is on the side of what comes to be.

## 40/69-76 — أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى ٱلَّذِينَ يُجَٰدِلُونَ فِىٓ ءَايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ أَنَّىٰ يُصْرَفُونَ · إِذِ ٱلْأَغْلَٰلُ فِىٓ أَعْنَٰقِهِمْ وَٱلسَّلَٰسِلُ يُسْحَبُونَ · فِى ٱلْحَمِيمِ ثُمَّ فِى ٱلنَّارِ يُسْجَرُونَ

"Have you not seen those who dispute about the signs of God? **How they are turned away!** When, with fetters on their necks and with chains, they are dragged: **into the boiling water, then thrust into the Fire…**"

### The last occurrence of the root

**This is the fifth and last occurrence of *jidāl* in the sūra.** A table of all five was given in the section on structure.

**And in this occurrence the verb changes: *annā yuṣrafūn* — "how are they turned away?"** Root **ص-ر-ف**: to turn, to make change direction. **The verb is passive: the one who turns them is not named.**

**ٱلْأَغْلَٰل** — the plural of *ghull*: the ring put round the neck. **The root was treated in `069-hakka.md` (*fa-ghullūh*); I rely on that treatment.**

**يُسْجَرُون** — root **س-ج-ر**: **to fill a hearth and set it ablaze; to fire an oven.** The lexicographers also record the word's use for "the sea being made to boil" (*al-baḥru'l-masjūr*) — **this occurred at `052-tur.md` 52/6.**

### ذَٰلِكُم بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَفْرَحُونَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ ٱلْحَقِّ وَبِمَا كُنتُمْ تَمْرَحُونَ

*"This is because of **your rejoicing in the earth without right** and because of **your swaggering**."*

**Two verbs one after another, and both state gladness — but they differ:**

| Verb | Root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Tafraḥūn* | ف-ر-ح | **To be glad, to feel relief** |
| *Tamraḥūn* | م-ر-ح | **To exult, to walk with a swagger** |

The root **م-ر-ح** is described by the lexicographers as **"gladness overstepping its measure, giving oneself airs"**; the word is also used of a strut in one's walk.

**And a qualification is put on the first verb: *bi-ghayri'l-ḥaqq* — "without right".** **None is put on the second.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is that qualification:** gladness is not condemned in itself — **gladness without right** is what is condemned. **The second verb comes unqualified, because the word *maraḥ* already carries the overstepping of measure within it.**

**ٱدْخُلُوٓا۟ أَبْوَٰبَ جَهَنَّمَ خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا فَبِئْسَ مَثْوَى ٱلْمُتَكَبِّرِينَ** — and the block closes with the sūra's word of diagnosis: *al-mutakabbirīn.* **In the fifty-sixth verse it was said that beneath the dispute lies *kibr*; here the same root becomes the closing attribute.**

## 40/77-85 — فَلَمَّا رَأَوْا۟ بَأْسَنَا قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَحْدَهُۥ وَكَفَرْنَا بِمَا كُنَّا بِهِۦ مُشْرِكِينَ · فَلَمْ يَكُ يَنفَعُهُمْ إِيمَٰنُهُمْ لَمَّا رَأَوْا۟ بَأْسَنَا سُنَّتَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلَّتِى قَدْ خَلَتْ فِى عِبَادِهِۦ

"**When they saw Our might** they said: 'We believe in God alone, and we deny what we used to associate with Him.' **But their faith, once they had seen Our might, was of no use to them.** This is God's **way**, which has passed among His servants."

### The close of the sūra

**The close sets in place the measure the sūra has been building from the start.**

**In the thirty-fifth verse the measure of those who dispute was asked after:** *bi-ghayri sulṭānin* — with nothing to stand on. **In the twenty-eighth verse the believing man built an argument with two branches.** **In the fifty-sixth verse the thing lying beneath the dispute was named: *kibr*.**

**And here it is told how the dispute ends: when the punishment is seen.**

**I record this as my own reading, and its ground is the verse's own expression:** *lammā raʾaw baʾsanā* — **the moment of seeing.** Faith comes at the end, but **not by proof, by sight.** And it is not accepted.

**The reason is given too, and it is named as a rule: *sunnata'llāhi'llatī qad khalat fī ʿibādih.***

The root **س-ن-ن**: **to open a way, to make a thing smooth and flowing.** *Sunna* — the way followed, the manner of working that continues. **Here the word states not an exception but *continuity*:** *qad khalat* — it has been so in the past as well.

**وَخَسِرَ هُنَالِكَ ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ** — "and there the deniers were lost."

**هُنَالِكَ** — "there". **An adverb of place, used for time.** The sūra closes an eighty-five-verse narrative of dispute with a marker of place: **the point at which the dispute ends.**
